[P1.1] A purely private business has every right to pack up and move. If a billionaire builds a stadium with private capital, buys the land at market rates, and funds the surrounding infrastructure, they should be free to relocate. That is free enterprise.
[P1.2] But modern professional sports franchises rarely operate as private businesses; they operate as publicly subsidized monopolies. When a team accepts major public subsidies—often hundreds of millions of dollars in municipal bonds, tax breaks, and public land—it alters the nature of the enterprise. Taxpayers cease to be mere customers; they become foundational co-investors. In no fair arrangement does a major investor hand over capital while allowing their partner to walk away unilaterally with the entire appreciating asset.
[P1.3] Currently, this system is profoundly rigged. Cities take on decades of debt and redesign urban districts around stadiums, diverting critical funds from schools and public safety. Meanwhile, owners socialize the costs, privatize the soaring franchise valuations, and eventually use relocation threats to extort the next round of handouts. Look at St. Louis or Oakland: communities that poured public wealth into teams, only to be abandoned for richer markets and left with stranded assets and lingering debt.
[P1.4] Simple financial buyouts cannot fix this. Because leagues artificially restrict the supply of teams to manufacture scarcity, desperate rival cities or wealthy buyers gladly subsidize an owner’s exit fee just to poach a franchise. A financial penalty merely gets priced into the move. Moreover, a standard buyout never truly compensates a city for re-zoned neighborhoods, disrupted local businesses, and decades of lost opportunity costs.
[P1.5] Requiring binding community approval is the only way to end this municipal extortion racket. Only the community can decide if an exit package truly makes them whole. The choice for owners remains entirely voluntary: if you want the absolute freedom to leave, fund your own stadium. If you take the public’s money, you answer to the public’s vote.
