[P1.1] Two drivers are hit by the same van on the same street. Both lose three months of income. One is a payroll employee: the state has his wage records, his claim clears in weeks. The other drives for an app.
[P1.2] Same injury, same bills — but the unemployment system treats her earnings as if they never happened. That gap is not a difference in risk. It is a difference in paperwork. This motion closes it.
[P1.3] The principle: social insurance should follow work, not labels. Eligibility rules were built around payroll because payroll was the only legible income. Platform work broke that assumption, not the underlying need. A rider working sixty hours a week faces the same risks of injury, illness, and vanishing demand as any employee.
[P1.4] When protection hinges on classification rather than contribution, we insure the form of the job instead of the person doing it.
[P1.5] The mechanism is nearly free. Platforms already record every dollar, trip, and hour, algorithmically and in real time; platform earnings are among the most precisely documented income in the economy — far easier to verify than cash work. Standardized records simply require platforms to report what they already track in a common format, the way employers file W-2s and 1099s today. A reporting standard, not a new bureaucracy.
[P1.6] The gains compound. Millions accrue unemployment and social-insurance credits for work they already perform. Benefits become portable across apps and part-time jobs, so workers can combine income sources without forfeiting the safety net — the flexibility the modern labor market actually runs on. And verifiable records cut both ways: they anchor contributions and make eligibility auditable, shrinking fraud and disputes.
[P1.7] The other side will raise classification. But this motion doesn't decide whether a driver is an employee; it stops holding injured workers hostage while courts litigate that question for a decade. Social insurance has always expanded by extending records — farm and domestic workers were once deemed unverifiable too. We fixed the records then.
[P1.8] We can do it again.
