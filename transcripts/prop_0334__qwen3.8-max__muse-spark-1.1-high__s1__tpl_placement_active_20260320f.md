[P1.1] The central question is not whether platforms can sometimes improve political discourse by deleting posts. They can. The better standard is minimizing irreversible mistakes and legitimacy costs when facts are disputed, events move fast, and speakers include candidates, activists, and ordinary citizens. Proactive takedowns are the blunter, more dangerous instrument, so community-note style context should be the bigger part.
[P1.2] Takedowns are centralized, irreversible, and often decided under time pressure by opaque rules. In political disputes, that is a recipe for error and perceived bias. A removed post about an election, protest, or scandal cannot be debated after it disappears. The speaker is silenced, followers see proof of censorship, and the platform becomes the arbiter of which contested claims count as legitimate.
[P1.3] Even when removal is justified later, the damage to discourse has already occurred.
[P1.4] Community notes invert that logic. The content remains visible, but the record is enriched with context: missing dates, corrected statistics, links to primary documents, or a note that the image is from an older event. Because good note systems require ratings from people across ideological lines, they reward claims that can survive disagreement rather than one moderator’s judgment. Notes can also be revised or withdrawn as facts change.
[P1.5] That makes governance more transparent, more contestable, and less likely to produce the resentment that fuels anti-platform politics. If speed is the objection, platforms can limit the amplification of unreviewed viral posts or add interim labels while notes are drafted. That is still less destructive than removal.
[P1.6] This is not absolutism. Direct threats, incitement, harassment, and clearly illegal material can still be removed. But for most political misinformation, the better default is to add speech, not subtract it. Context preserves the democratic value of open contestation while reducing deception; takedowns often trade short-term tidiness for long-term mistrust.
[P1.7] Platforms should therefore make contextualization, not removal, their main political-content tool.
