[P1.1] An employer can require a claims examiner to meet a deadline and explain a decision. It should not be able to make keeping that job conditional on accepting an automated coach for the entire shift. The distinction is not between accountability and freedom from scrutiny. It is between supervision tied to a task and supervision that workers have no right to switch off when the task calls for their own judgment.
[P1.2] Consider an examiner who notices that two medical bills look alike but may cover separate visits. Checking takes time; closing the file looks efficient. A companion calibrated to keep cases moving can prompt precisely when the examiner ought to slow down. One bad suggestion is easy to dismiss.
[P1.3] A daily succession of suggestions, delivered by a system the boss insists on, teaches a different lesson: following the prompt is easier to defend than departing from it. [P1.4] The proper comparison is not a mandatory companion versus leaving workers unaided. Let a novice keep the coach on all day; let an experienced colleague summon it for an unfamiliar case. [P1.5] That is why a prohibition on compulsion is the right line. Workers need not prove that a prompt was wrong, disclose a disability, or persuade a manager that quiet helps them concentrate.
