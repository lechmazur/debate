[P1.1] Copyright in America has always answered a single question: what did a human author contribute? Not "who paid," not "who pressed the button," but who made the creative choices that produced this expression. That is why the Constitution's clause speaks of "Authors," why the Copyright Office has long refused registration to works lacking human authorship, and why a photograph is protected not because a camera made an image but because a person chose the lens, the framing, the light, the moment.
[P1.2] The proposition simply keeps that rule honest in a world where the button is now a prompt box. It does not ban AI-assisted work from copyright. It says: if you want the state to grant you a twenty-plus-year monopoly enforceable against strangers, you must be able to show a human shaped the final expression. Substantial creative control, documented.
[P1.3] That is a modest ask of anyone claiming to be an author.
[P1.4] Consider the alternative concretely. A single operator can generate a hundred thousand images or ten thousand songs in a weekend. If each output vests copyright automatically, we have created an enclosure machine: vast thickets of rights over expression no human ever conceived, held by whoever ran the script fastest, and weaponized against human artists whose independent work happens to resemble the output. Copyright's bargain — limited monopoly in exchange for human creativity we want to encourage — collapses when one side of the trade contributes nothing that needed encouraging.
[P1.5] Machines do not require incentives. They require electricity.
[P1.6] The documentation requirement is the practical heart of the case. Rights that cannot be examined cannot be limited, and courts and registrars need something better than an applicant's assertion that they "guided" the model. Prompts, iterations, selections, edits, arrangement — these are recordable, and creators who genuinely exercise control already produce them as a matter of workflow.
[P1.7] So the standard is this: contribute expression, get protection. Contribute a request, get an output — a useful one, freely usable, but not a monopoly.

[C1.1] The proposition asks American copyright to invent a gate it has never required: document “substantial creative control” over the final expression, or the work is free for the taking. That is not fidelity to authorship. It is a special disability pinned on creators who use a new tool.
[C1.2] Copyright has long protected the human who causes original expression to be fixed—the photographer who times the shutter, the designer who accepts a procedural render and releases it, the musician who keeps the take. The machine never authors. The human who initiates, curates, and publishes does. Elevating an audit trail of “substantial control” confuses creativity with bureaucracy.
[C1.3] Ordinary originality and fixation already supply the human contribution the Constitution cares about. A prompt-and-select workflow is no more automatic than a camera on aperture priority; both channel human choices into fixed expression.
[C1.4] The documentation mandate itself is the damage. A working artist iterating until an image or track is right will not generate litigation-grade logs of every rejected seed, parameter, and discard. A well-lawyered studio will. The rule therefore does not safeguard human creators; it rations protection by compliance capacity and turns the Copyright Office into a paperwork tribunal.
[C1.5] Vague “substantiality” invites arbitrary refusals and years of threshold litigation before anyone ever reaches copying.
[C1.6] The enclosure scare does not salvage this. Existing doctrine already cabins overreach: no protection for ideas, styles, or stock tropes; independent creation remains a full defense; thin copyright and fair use defeat bare resemblance claims. Flooding the system with weak title is a reason for ordinary infringement analysis, not for stripping title from the humans who actually deploy the tools.
[C1.7] Deny the monopoly unless creators submit to a novel control dossier and you do not defend the bargain. You chill the next generation of human work made with machines and hand copyists a free ride. Keep the familiar rule: original works of human authorship qualify. No special AI confession.
[C1.8] Reject the proposition.
