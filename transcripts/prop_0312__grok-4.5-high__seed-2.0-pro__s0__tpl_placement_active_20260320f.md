[P1.1] Professional sports leagues thrive on public passion, not locked-in subscribers. Keeping a meaningful share of marquee games on free-to-air television preserves that passion. When championships, derbies, and season-ending clashes vanish entirely behind streaming paywalls, they cease to be national rituals and become gated private events.
[P1.2] Free-to-air access lets children from working-class homes, retirees on fixed incomes, and families who cannot stack yet another subscription still witness the biggest moments. This is not sentimental charity; it is the growth engine. New fans are not born with credit cards and reliable broadband. They discover the game on a television anyone can turn on.
[P1.3] Once hooked they become lifelong buyers of merchandise, tickets, and later packages. Starve that open pipeline and the future market contracts.
[P1.4] Full migration to streaming deepens inequality. Gaps in high-speed internet and disposable income already leave millions on the wrong side of the digital divide. Sports paywalls compound the exclusion, turning shared cultural moments into luxuries. A title-clinching goal means less to a city when large parts of it never saw the play that decided it.
[P1.5] Leagues chase short-term rights fees by selling everything to the highest bidder. Yet those fees derive value from the very public fervor free-to-air sustains. Keep a real share of the marquee slate free, and the sport remains a broad conversation rather than an exclusive commodity. That balance protects the fans who give the games meaning and the long-term health of the leagues themselves.
