Generative models erase that friction, producing thousands of polished songs, designs, or articles in minutes at near-zero cost. If lightly prompted outputs win copyright, opportunistic actors can flood markets with mass-generated works and weaponize infringement claims against working artists who independently create something similar.
[P1.4] Copyright exists to incentivize human creativity, not to subsidize automated land grabs that starve the public domain.

Documenting that control is both practical and indispensable. Because a finished digital file looks identical whether a human painstakingly shaped it or generated it in one click, ordinary creative records like layered files, drafts, and edit histories are the only way to tell authorship from automation.
[P1.6] Exclusive monopolies belong to documented human expression; machine output belongs in the public domain.
