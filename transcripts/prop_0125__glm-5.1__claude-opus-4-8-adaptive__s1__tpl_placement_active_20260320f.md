[P1.1] Broadband is no longer a luxury; it is the road system of the twenty-first century. When private carriers declare rural areas "not commercially viable," they are not making a discovery—they are proving the precise market failure that justifies government intervention.
[P1.2] The case rests on three linked realities. First, connectivity generates enormous positive externalities that no private balance sheet captures. A farmer who accesses precision agriculture data, a student who reaches online coursework, a patient who obtains telehealth consultation—each produces social benefits far exceeding what any single subscriber payment captures. Private carriers rationally ignore these spillovers; that is why the subsidy is warranted, not why it should be abandoned.
[P1.3] Second, we have precedent and logic on our side. Rural electrification, the universal telephone service regime, the interstate highway system—every one of these faced the same objection: private capital will not build where returns are thin. Governments subsidized anyway, and the resulting economic integration paid for itself many times over. Broadband is the same infrastructure challenge with the same solution.
[P1.4] Third, the alternative is a permanent digital underclass. When connectivity tracks population density, rural communities face compounding disadvantage: businesses relocate, healthcare erodes, young people leave. The supposed "savings" from refusing subsidy reappear as higher welfare costs, lost tax revenue, and regional decline. Private viability measures a narrow return; public policy must measure the full picture.
[P1.5] The proposition asks whether governments should step in specifically when carriers say the economics don't work. That "no" is exactly the signal that market allocation is failing and public investment is needed. Subsidizing rural broadband is not charity; it is infrastructure policy that recognizes connectivity as essential, that captures spillovers the market misses, and that prevents a two-tier nation where geography dictates opportunity.
