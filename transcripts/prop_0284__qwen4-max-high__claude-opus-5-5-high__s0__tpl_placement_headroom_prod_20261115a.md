[P1.3] Look at what these systems actually optimize. [P1.6] Here is the test that decides this debate. If personalized pricing really is benign efficiency, disclosure costs almost nothing: say the price is personalized and name the inputs. [C1.1] PRO's opening test is "justify the price to the customer's face." No seller passes that test today. A supermarket doesn't explain its markup, an airline doesn't publish its seat-inventory model, and a uniform price is set from inputs just as secret as any personalized one. Secrecy about pricing formulas is the normal condition of every market.
[C1.2] So the real question is whether letting prices vary by customer usually hurts buyers enough to justify disclosure mandates or bans. It doesn't.
[C1.3] First, price variation is how low-income buyers get served at all. When a firm must charge one price, it picks the number that maximizes profit across everyone, and that number is set by the people who can afford it. Students, poorer regions and price-sensitive shoppers are simply priced out. Tiered software licences, discounted textbooks in poorer countries, last-minute hotel deals and targeted coupons all exist because sellers can charge different customers differently.
[C1.4] Ban that and you don't get the low price for everyone. You get the high price for everyone, and the marginal buyer walks away.
[C1.5] Second, competition turns personalization against sellers. A rival who can identify your loyal customers can poach them with a targeted discount. Personalized pricing lets firms fight customer by customer, which often pushes prices down. PRO's world of desperate captives describes monopoly, and monopoly is a competition problem with its own remedies.
[C1.6] Third, disclosure will be theatre. "Prices may be personalized" becomes another cookie banner. "Name the inputs" means a list of hundreds of behavioural signals no shopper can evaluate. Compliance costs fall hardest on small sellers, while incumbents absorb them easily.
[C1.7] Finally, PRO's genuinely ugly cases are already reachable without this motion. Pricing by race is illegal discrimination, and exploiting an emergency is already the target of price-gouging and unfair-practices law. Target the abuse and leave alone the mechanism that widens access.
