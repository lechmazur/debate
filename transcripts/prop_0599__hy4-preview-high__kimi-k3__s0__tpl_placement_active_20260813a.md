[P1.1] Start with the failure we already live with: invisibility, not surveillance. Tens of millions of Africans—displaced people, rural women, informal workers—have no reliable proof of identity. Without it, drought relief misses the herder, a girl cannot sit a national exam, a migrant loses her vaccination record at a checkpoint, and a trader cannot open a bank account. A continent-wide digital ID framework is the plumbing of service delivery: one trusted authentication can unlock clinics, schools, cash transfers, and cross-border trade.
[P1.2] Because Africa’s people are mobile, a Ghanaian nurse or Somali trader should not rebuild identity in every country; a common framework makes benefits portable and cuts the duplication and ghost beneficiaries that drain budgets.
[P1.3] The gains are concrete. Governments can target food-crisis subsidies in days, enroll children at birth, and bring informal workers into insurance and credit. Better records also mean less leakage and stronger tax collection, funding the very services people need every day.
[P1.4] The proposition’s own “even if” is a real cost: surveillance risks rise, especially for dissidents and minorities. Accept that. But compare baselines. Tracking already happens through telecom metadata, mobile-money trails, donor biometrics and border databases, often in fragmented systems with weak oversight.
[P1.5] A unified AU framework can impose data minimisation, purpose limitation, independent audits and penalties as continent-wide defaults. That is not perfect privacy; it is accountable identity. And the alternative is worse. Exclusion is a rights harm: the unregistered cannot vote, claim pensions, or seek protection.
[P1.6] Rejecting ID leaves people to informal brokers and unregulated platforms with even less accountability.
[P1.7] Decide on the greater failure. The greater failure is a population locked out of services, not a risk we can constrain by law and design. The AU should build now, with privacy guardrails in its foundation. Waiting for perfect protection means importing someone else’s architecture and inheriting their surveillance anyway.
