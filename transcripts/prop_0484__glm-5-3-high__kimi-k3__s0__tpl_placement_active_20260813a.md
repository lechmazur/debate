[P1.1] When the negotiating table decides whether your country will still exist in eighty years, the size of your population is not a measure of your stake—it is a measure of how much you stand to lose, all at once, in one place, forever.
[P1.2] That is the situation of small island developing states. For Tuvalu, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, the Maldives, sea-level rise is not a policy problem to be balanced against growth. It is the possible erasure of territory, statehood, and a people's entire national existence. Yet the machinery of climate diplomacy is built around the opposite weighting: the states that emit the most, finance the most, and trade the most set the agenda, control the draft texts, and decide what counts as "realistic."
[P1.3] Three arguments carry the case.
[P1.4] First, affected interests. Every serious theory of legitimate decision-making holds that those who bear the consequences should have a voice proportional to what they bear. A SIDS delegate isn't negotiating about a marginal cost curve; they are negotiating about whether their grandchildren have a homeland. Weighted voting by existential risk simply makes the voice match the stake.
[P1.5] Second, misaligned incentives. The states with the most formal and informal power at these talks are the ones whose economies depend on delaying ambition. We would never let the defendant outvote the plaintiff on whether the harm occurred. Procedurally, climate talks do something close to that every year.
[P1.6] Third, precedent. International law already recognizes differentiated stakes—special treatment for riparian states on shared rivers, unique status for Arctic states, the Security Council's permanent seats justified by outsized responsibility. Weighting by existential exposure is not exotic; it is the logic already running underneath the system, applied honestly for once.
[P1.7] The objection will be that sovereignty is already equal—one state, one vote. But formal equality that masks real domination by emitters and funders is not equality; it is decoration. This motion makes the decoration load-bearing.
