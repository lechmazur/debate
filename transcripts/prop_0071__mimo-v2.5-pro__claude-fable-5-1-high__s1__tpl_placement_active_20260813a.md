[P1.1] The proposition before us is straightforward: where nations built substantial wealth through slavery or colonial extraction, the moral and practical obligation runs to direct reparations for descendant communities, not merely symbolic gestures.
[P1.2] Consider the core logic. When one party extracts wealth from another through coercion over centuries, and that extracted wealth compounds into national prosperity while the exploited communities are left structurally impoverished, a "sorry" does not balance the ledger. This is not abstract philosophy. The transatlantic slave trade generated enormous capital for European colonial powers and settler colonies.
[P1.3] Plantation economies in the Caribbean, the American South, and Brazil were engines of national wealth. It is the direct downstream consequence of centuries of coerced labor, stolen resources, and rigged trade. When Britain abolished slavery in 1833, it paid compensation not to the enslaved but to slaveholders—the equivalent of billions in modern currency. The people who actually built the wealth received nothing.
[P1.5] Symbolic apologies cost nothing. They require no redistribution, no institutional reform, no sacrifice. They allow the beneficiary nation to feel moral closure while the material consequences of extraction continue to shape who lives in poverty and who does not.
