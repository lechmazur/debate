[P1.1] The question is not whether history should be taught, but whether the state should be allowed to treat it as campaign material. On war, colonialism, and national identity, political control too often means that the story changes with the party in power: one election produces patriotic triumph, the next produces embarrassed silence, and students receive whatever flatters the current coalition.
[P1.2] Stronger independent scholarly review is the better rule because it subordinates school narratives to evidence rather than officeholding. Historians and subject-matter experts are not morally perfect, but their discipline has correction mechanisms that politics lacks: primary sources, peer critique, revision in light of new evidence, and professional costs for distortion. A school board or ministry may want a clean national myth because it fears electoral punishment; a scholarly review process can insist that the textbook still explain conquest, slavery, occupation, or wartime violence as they actually occurred.
[P1.3] This matters most where history is most painful. If a student’s ancestors were colonized, enslaved, displaced, or targeted in war, the classroom should not force them to absorb a state-approved denial of that experience. And if a student belongs to the dominant group, he should not be taught that his nation never did wrong. Both distortions are failures of education.
[P1.4] Independent review gives victims’ histories a claim based on evidence, not on whether they have enough votes in the district.
[P1.5] The obvious objection is that democratic accountability requires politicians to decide. But accountability does not mean unlimited power. Citizens can still choose curricula, set broad civic goals, and remove officials who mismanage schools. What they should not hold is the right to rewrite the past whenever inconvenient.
[P1.6] A republic that fears evidence is teaching loyalty to power, not history.
[P1.7] Public schools exist to prepare informed citizens, not to manage national memory. For war, colonialism, and identity, that requires independent scholarly review.
