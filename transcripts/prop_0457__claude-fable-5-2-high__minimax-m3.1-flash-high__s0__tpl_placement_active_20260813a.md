[C1.2] Local review and litigation are not merely “veto points.” Before approval, review can reveal cumulative wildlife impacts, hydrological changes, erosion, wildfire risks, and practicable alternatives that project developers may have no incentive to disclose. Litigation also allows communities to enforce standards when agencies fail. Removing those pathways would shift authority toward fast executive decisions with less information and weaker accountability. Jurisdictions competing for projects could create a race to the lowest environmental standard.
[C1.3] Concerns about wealthy litigants do not justify abolishing legal safeguards. The better response is procedural discipline: consolidated review, firm deadlines, transparent evidence, limits on serial lawsuits, and penalties for abusive filings. Those tools can reduce delay while preserving meaningful participation. Stripping review and litigation is treating a governance failure as an environmental solution.
[C1.4] Nor does delay in permitting establish that delay is the principal cause of continued fossil generation. Grid planning, interconnection queues, financing, construction costs, transmission bottlenecks, and public opposition also matter. Accelerating a permit does not guarantee that transmission will be built or that clean energy will displace fossil generation within any meaningful timeframe. PRO’s comparison between an unrecallable decade of emissions and a “misplaced solar farm” is rhetorically powerful but analytically incomplete: projects can impose decades-long habitat disruption, irreversible landscape change, and burdens on host communities.
[C1.5] The proposition’s concession is therefore decisive. It asks governments to significantly reduce local protections before demonstrating that benefits will materialize and harms will be mitigated. Governments should streamline procedures, not environmental accountability. Any reform that weakens substantive review or access to courts leaves a subsidy for whoever can exploit the fastest approval process.
[C1.6] The defensible policy is faster, coordinated, and disciplined permitting—with environmental protections intact, not fewer.
