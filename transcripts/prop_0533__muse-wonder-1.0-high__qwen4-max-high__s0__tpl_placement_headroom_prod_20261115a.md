[P1.1] Ethiopia should fill and operate the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on its own timeline because sovereignty over a shared river does not give the downstream state that developed first a permanent veto.
[P1.2] The Blue Nile originates overwhelmingly in Ethiopia, yet for generations Ethiopia used almost none of it while Egypt built its economy around an assumed permanent share. Colonial-era arrangements and later Egypt-Sudan deals excluded Ethiopia entirely. A country cannot be bound forever by allocations it never consented to, especially to preserve another state's historic use while its own people remain in energy poverty.
[P1.3] That poverty is urgent. A huge share of Ethiopians lack reliable electricity, forcing reliance on wood and charcoal and limiting clinics, schools, pumps, and industry. The GERD is primarily hydropower, not consumptive irrigation. Water passes through turbines and continues downstream.
[P1.4] Filling is temporary retention phased over years, not the permanent diversion Egypt's objections imply.
[P1.5] Downstream harm is manageable and overstated. Phased filling in wet seasons, drought releases, and normal operation can smooth the Blue Nile's seasonality, reduce Sudanese flooding, cut evaporation compared with storage in hotter lowland deserts, and make flows more predictable. Egypt also has Aswan storage, groundwater, and room to improve inefficient flood irrigation and stop wasting water on low-value desert crops.
[P1.6] International river practice favors equitable and reasonable use, not absolute downstream protection. Avoiding significant harm does not mean avoiding any reduction, any change in timing, or any loss of a claimed historic entitlement. If Egypt's objection alone could stall filling indefinitely, equitable use becomes meaningless: the first developer wins forever, and upstream development becomes permission-based.
[P1.7] Ethiopia should share data and manage drought prudently, but Egyptian consent cannot be the price of Ethiopian development. Otherwise over one hundred million Ethiopians are told to remain dark so Egypt can avoid adapting at all.

[C1.3] The physical asymmetry is decisive. [C1.4] And Egypt has no rain-fed agriculture to fall back on — the alternative to Nile water is not efficiency, it is fallow land and displaced farmers.

[C1.6] Ethiopia already signed a Declaration of Principles with Egypt and Sudan committing to cooperate on filling and operation and to avoid significant harm. Honoring your own signature is not subordination. It is sovereignty used responsibly.
