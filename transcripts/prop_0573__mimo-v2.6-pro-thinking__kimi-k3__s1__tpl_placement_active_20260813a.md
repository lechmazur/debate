[P1.1] A songwriter gets a royalty statement from a streaming platform and sees a fraction of a cent per play, multiplied across a pool she cannot see, divided by totals she cannot verify, adjusted by terms she did not negotiate. She cannot answer the most basic questions in any commercial relationship: what was played, how much did it earn, and who decided how it was split. That is not a market with a minor friction. It is a market where one side sets the price, controls the meter, and grades its own homework.
[P1.2] Take the mechanism seriously. Most payouts flow through pro-rata pools: all subscription revenue goes into a pot, divided by total streams, then filtered through contracts with labels, distributors, and aggregators. Every link in that chain is a place where value can leak, be reclassified, or simply go unexplained. Without standardized data — streams by territory and time window, pool totals, per-stream rates, and the payout path to each rights holder — no artist, indie label, or regulator can distinguish a bad deal from an error from a theft.
[P1.3] Information asymmetry on this scale is precisely what disclosure requirements exist to fix. Securities markets do not let issuers say "trust us, the dividends are fair"; they mandate reporting so capital can be priced honestly. Creative labor deserves the same baseline.
[P1.4] Note what the proposition does not require. It does not force open recommendation algorithms or hand over user-level viewing histories. It requires standardized *viewing and payout* data — the meter and the money. Aggregated, privacy-preserving reporting satisfies the rule.
[P1.5] Trade secrecy is not a serious objection here: the identity of the denominator and the arithmetic of the split are not the moat that makes a streaming service competitive. A platform that can only compete by hiding how it pays is competing on opacity.
[P1.6] Nor is this a heavy mandate. Platforms already compute these figures internally to settle with major labels; standardization turns an existing ledger into an auditable one. The cost is formatting, not invention.

[C1.5] That is where tacit coordination begins, and the majors' leverage does the rest.
[C1.6] Finally, notice what changes if PRO wins completely: the songwriter receives a prettier statement showing, with perfect clarity, the same fraction of a cent.
