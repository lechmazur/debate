[C1.2] A rail corridor is a permanent capacity increase that can take cars and buses off the road entirely. Transport emissions depend on vehicle-kilometres and mode share, not fuel type alone.
[C1.3] Yes, tunnelling is slow and expensive. But urban rail expansion is not synonymous with underground mega-projects. Surface light rail, commuter rail on existing rights-of-way, and at-grade extensions can be delivered far faster and cheaper than the metro strawman. They can begin service within the same decade as bus fleet turnover, and they create dedicated zero-carbon capacity that buses cannot match.
[C1.4] Electrifying buses also runs into a capacity ceiling. As cities grow, the bus strategy means buying more buses, hiring more drivers, and competing for the same congested road space. [C1.5] It also anchors compact development around stations, reducing trip lengths in ways that a depot full of chargers never will.

[C1.7] The proposition's urgency trades structural decarbonization for a one-time tailpipe swap. The main near-term strategy must add zero-carbon capacity, not merely clean the existing queue. Latin America should electrify buses, but as a complement to rail, not a substitute for the main near-term strategy.
