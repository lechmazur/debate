[P1.1] The hinge of this motion is one word: outsized. We concede freely that IQ-style tests measure something real — abstract pattern recognition, working memory, processing speed under time pressure. What we deny is that this narrow slice of cognition can carry the weight currently piled on it: streaming children before adolescence, filtering résumés at industrial scale, deciding who ever gets a first interview.
[P1.2] Start with what the tests actually sample: a timed session of decontextualized symbolic puzzles. That is one room in a very large house. Practical judgment, creative synthesis, social perception, learned expertise, the ability to persist through a months-long problem — none of these appear on the answer sheet, and all of them drive real-world performance. The mechanic diagnosing an engine by ear, the nurse who senses a patient deteriorating before the monitor does — these are cognitive acts the test cannot see.
[P1.3] When a company screens on general aptitude, it is selecting for the ability to sit the test plus whatever loosely correlates with it.
[P1.4] Second, even the predictive case is weaker than the usage. The honest finding from decades of psychometrics is that these scores correlate modestly with academic and job outcomes — genuinely, but modestly. The large majority of what determines whether someone thrives remains unexplained by the score. Using a modest predictor as a near-absolute gatekeeper is not rigor; it is overconfidence with a number attached.
[P1.5] Third, the errors compound invisibly. The rejected candidate's lost career is never measured, so the system never confronts its false negatives. Meanwhile a prep industry lets money buy points, which means the score increasingly reflects access as much as aptitude — a sorting machine that mislabels privilege as intelligence.
[P1.6] A thermometer is a precise instrument. It still cannot diagnose pneumonia. IQ-style tests are thermometers being used as full diagnostic machines. The motion asks only that we shrink the tool's role to match what it actually measures.
[P1.7] That is not anti-measurement; it is measurement done honestly.
