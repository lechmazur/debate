[C1.1] Relevance for a public broadcaster does not mean winning a scroll. It means being the one place an entire country can turn to, trust, and share — regardless of age, income, postcode, or algorithm. Prioritising youth short-form video and creator partnerships inverts that mandate. It chases the audience least committed to the institution, on platforms the broadcaster does not control, in a format least suited to public service.
[C1.2] Short-form rewards speed, personality, and simplification. Public service requires the opposite: context, impartiality, and patience. A 40-second clip can tease a story; it cannot explain a budget, investigate a hospital, teach a curriculum, or hold power to account without stripping away the nuance that justifies public funding in the first place. Creator partnerships make that problem worse.
[C1.3] A creator's value is a personal brand — partisan cues, commercial deals, and parasocial loyalty included. Attach that brand to a newsroom and trust transfers in the wrong direction: audiences follow the influencer when they leave, while the broadcaster inherits their controversies and loses editorial control. Worse, distribution is rented. TikTok or YouTube can change a feed, throttle news, or ban a category overnight, and the future-proof strategy vanishes with an algorithm update.
[C1.4] Traditional channel expansion, for all its unfashionable hardware, protects universality. A broadcast signal reaches rural homes, older viewers, disabled audiences who rely on established accessibility services, and households that cannot afford fast broadband or multiple subscriptions. It sustains the shared moments — national emergencies, elections, major cultural and sporting events, local reporting — that make a broadcaster public rather than merely popular. Young people have not permanently rejected that value; they encounter serious news, drama, and live events in depth when stakes are high, and they age into fuller use.
[C1.5] Gutting the core service to fund ephemeral clips fractures the coalition that pays for it. A broadcaster watched a little by everyone is publicly defensible. One desperately chasing teenagers on someone else's app is relevant to neither.

[P2.1] The opposition mistakes preservation for relevance. A public broadcaster that maintains universal reach only among the aging is not universal—it is a reservation for the nostalgic. True universality means meeting every generation where it actually lives, and for under-thirties, that is the algorithmic feed, not the channel lineup. The claim of "one place an entire country can turn to" becomes hollow if half the country never opens it, because the institution failed to follow them into their media environment.
[P2.2] The assertion that short-form inherently strips nuance confuses format with function. A sixty-second explainer can distill a complex budget crisis into accessible civic literacy without sacrificing accuracy; it serves as the doorway to depth, not a replacement for it. Young viewers discover long-form investigations through short-form hooks, building the habit of seeking verified information. Rejecting this funnel because it begins with brevity is like rejecting a library because its entrance has a welcome desk.
[P2.3] Regarding creators, the opposition overstates the risk of editorial capture. Partnerships are contractual collaborations, not franchises. A public broadcaster retains final cut, fact-checking authority, and brand standards; the creator gains credibility from association with trusted journalism, while the broadcaster gains the algorithmic fluency it cannot manufacture internally. The alternative is not neutrality but institutional silence in the spaces where civic discourse now happens.
[P2.4] Finally, the "rented distribution" argument proves too much: it applies equally to linear carriage agreements, which are also negotiated, throttled, and rapidly declining. TikTok is rented, but the cable bundle is expiring. Investing in platforms where youth attention actually concentrates is not ephemeral trend-chasing; it is the only sustainable path to the shared civic moments the opposition values. Channel expansion locks in sunk costs in infrastructure youth have abandoned, while creator partnerships build the viewing habits that will fund the core service for decades.
