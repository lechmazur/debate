[P1.1] Housing outlives the physical prime of any single occupant. When multi-unit housing is constructed, it locks in structural realities—doorway widths, entry steps, corridor geometry, and bathroom dimensions—for fifty to a hundred years. Erecting buildings that ignore universal design guarantees that as our population ages, our physical environment will systematically exclude, isolate, and displace residents.
[P1.2] Universal design is not a niche accommodation for an isolated demographic; it is a baseline necessity for the entire population. Human mobility is fluid: virtually everyone experiences physical limitation at some point, whether through aging, chronic illness, or temporary injury. Standardizing zero-step entries, wider clearances, and adaptable bathrooms ensures people can age in place, raise families, and welcome neighbors without physical barriers. Without these standards, a routine change in physical ability renders a home instantly uninhabitable, needlessly forcing seniors and individuals with disabilities into strained, expensive institutional care.
[P1.3] The economic calculus firmly supports the proposition. The motion explicitly accounts for a modest rise in upfront construction costs. Yet that marginal increase pales against the alternative: the exorbitant cost of post-construction retrofits. Widening framed doors, modifying concrete foundations for level access, or restructuring interior walls after completion costs exponentially more and is often structurally impossible.
[P1.4] Incorporating accessible geometry at the blueprint stage is an efficient, one-time investment that permanently enriches the usable housing stock across generations.
[P1.5] Opposing these standards to preserve a negligible upfront margin represents a short-sighted false economy. It exports massive lifelong costs onto public healthcare, families, and tenants simply to optimize initial development spreadsheets. We must build multi-unit housing for the full reality of the human lifespan, not just the temporarily able-bodied.
