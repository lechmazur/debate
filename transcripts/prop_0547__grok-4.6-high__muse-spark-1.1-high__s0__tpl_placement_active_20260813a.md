[P1.1] Engagement-optimized short-form feeds are ranking machines. Their job is dwell time, replays, and return visits. That objective privileges whatever spikes arousal fastest: social comparison, filtered bodies, humiliation, outrage, and the sense that everyone else is living a fuller life. Adolescents absorb that more deeply because reward systems are highly sensitive and identity is still being assembled through peers.
[P1.2] A chronological feed mostly shows, in time order, what a teenager actually chose to follow. An engagement feed searches a vast inventory for the clip most likely to hold them. The winners are rarely the calmest. They are the most scroll-stopping: beauty extremes, “you’re falling behind” montages, public-shaming loops, escalating drama.
[P1.3] The system need not intend harm. It only has to notice that envious rewatching and anxious checking keep people on the app.
[P1.4] That difference compounds. Infinite variable rewards push later bedtimes. Rapid ranking invites rumination. Lifestyle and appearance extremes set targets no real day can meet.
[P1.5] A chronological or non-personalized feed can still contain some of this material, but it does not hunt for it, amplify it, or interleave it at the moment a user is most likely to keep watching. Optimization is the extra dose.
[P1.6] Picture the after-school scroll. Followed friends produce ordinary, mixed videos. The engagement machine replaces that mix with a curated emotional gauntlet because the gauntlet wins the metric. Anxiety and low mood follow from repeated social threat, failed comparison, and lost rest—not from video as such, but from a feed engineered to maximize the reactions that make those conditions more likely.
[P1.7] Change the ranking target and you change how often a developing mind is served the most destabilizing material. That increment is why engagement-optimized feeds increase adolescent anxiety and depression relative to chronological alternatives.

[C1.3] An engagement-ranked For You feed dilutes that proximal threat with interest-based content that is not about your school hierarchy. The skate kid gets tutorials, not the party photos he missed.
