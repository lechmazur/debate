[P1.1] Funding universities based strictly on enrollment creates a perverse incentive: colleges are rewarded for getting students through the front door, regardless of whether they ever walk across the graduation stage with a viable career. Under an enrollment-only model, a university collects full public subsidies for a student who drops out after two semesters saddled with non-dischargeable debt. That is not public service; it is an enrollment mill operating without institutional accountability.
[P1.2] Tying public subsidies to demonstrated improvements in graduation rates and post-graduation earnings fundamentally realigns institutional priorities with student welfare. The worst outcome in higher education is not skipping college altogether—it is attending college, taking on debt, and leaving without a credential or the earning power to service that debt. When state appropriations depend on completion and career outcomes, universities are compelled to shift resources away from administrative bloat and aggressive marketing and toward advising, tutoring, mental health support, and career-aligned curricula.
[P1.3] Crucially, the proposition demands funding based on *demonstrated improvements*, not static, elitist benchmarks. Regional colleges and access-oriented institutions that enroll historically underrepresented or academically underprepared students are not penalized for their baseline demographics. Instead, they are rewarded precisely when they elevate those students and generate upward mobility. This design prevents institutions from gaming admissions to cream-skim wealthy applicants, focusing institutional energy where it belongs: value addition.
[P1.4] Public higher education is funded because it is supposed to be an engine of social mobility. When universities ask taxpayers and students for billions of dollars, the metric of success cannot merely be headcount on the first day of class. It must be whether students finish their degrees and gain the financial independence promised by higher education. Anchoring funding to completion and earnings growth ensures public investment delivers genuine, lasting opportunity.
