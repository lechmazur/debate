[P1.1] Every large financial crime of the past two decades has needed the same tool: a company whose paper owner is a nominee and whose real owner is nobody's business. The corrupt minister buying a London townhouse, the sanctioned oligarch moving a yacht, the fentanyl network washing cash through real estate, the fraudster who vanishes with pensioners' savings—none of them need a brilliant scheme. They need a box in a filing cabinet that answers no questions. The Panama and Pandora leaks did not reveal exotic financial engineering; they revealed that the engineering was ordinary and the anonymity did all the work.
[P1.2] That is why the motion is narrow and deliberately so. It does not ban shell companies, holding structures, special-purpose vehicles, or privacy from the public. A business may still own assets through subsidiaries, keep competitors from reading its cap table, and shield a founder from nosy neighbours. What it may not do is refuse to tell the regulator which human being ultimately controls it.
[P1.3] The ask is a name, an identity document, and an obligation to keep it current. That costs less than a company's annual filing fee. Any argument that this burden is intolerable is really an argument that hiding from the state is a legitimate business need.
[P1.4] Universality is the point, not an add-on. Beneficial-ownership rules already exist in Britain, the EU, and now the United States, and the result has been predictable: the business migrated to whichever jurisdiction was last to move. Anonymity is a product, and the seller is always the weakest link. A ban that stops at one border is a referral service for the next one.
[P1.5] So the question becomes simple. One side defends registers that let an investigator, a bank, or a sanctions officer trace a company to a person. The other side must defend the right to a corporate mask that the law itself cannot lift. Every honest use survives disclosure.

[C1.1] The case for a universal ban rests on a seductive equation: anonymity equals impunity. But a shell company is a legal container, and anonymity is a feature some use for crime and many use for privacy, safety, or commercial confidentiality. The proposition would criminalize that container in every jurisdiction unless the beneficial owner is unmasked to the state. That is not a scalpel; it is a worldwide prohibition on lawful confidentiality, and it will fail where enforcement matters most.
[C1.2] Consider the honest users. A domestic-violence survivor holding property through a company; a dissident entrepreneur; a small investment vehicle negotiating a takeover. They may have compelling reasons to keep ownership out of public view. The reply is that a confidential regulator file solves this.
[C1.3] It does not. Confidential databases are still databases; they can be breached or misused. And “the regulator knows” is not the same as “no one may form a company without state approval.” A universal ban converts a privacy interest into a licensing hurdle, hitting those least able to navigate it.
[C1.4] Second, the ban mistakes the criminal’s paperwork for the crime. A launderer does not need a legally anonymous company; he can use a nominee with a forged passport, a corrupt agent, or a jurisdiction that does not verify. Requiring a name does not produce a true name. The proposition’s migration argument proves too much: if business flees to the weakest registry, a universal ban needs universal enforcement, which no institution can deliver.
[C1.5] In practice it pushes activity to noncooperative havens and informal structures, making investigation harder.
[C1.6] The better regime is targeted: risk-based beneficial-ownership disclosure in high-risk sectors, verified identity at banks and gatekeepers, law-enforcement access with oversight, and pressure on refusing jurisdictions. That can catch the corrupt minister’s townhouse without criminalizing the abuse survivor’s holding company. The proposition must show a universal ban is necessary and proportionate. It cannot, because it attacks the mask while leaving the face untouched.
