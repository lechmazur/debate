[C1.1] State-level bans on synthetic food dyes are the wrong instrument for an interstate problem. The very evidence cited as “disputed but accumulating” is a reason to deepen centralized scientific review, not to let one state convert uncertainty into prohibition. Assessing dye safety requires aggregate dietary-exposure data, subpopulation sensitivity, dose-response uncertainty, and interactions across the whole food supply. State legislatures are not equipped to do that.
[C1.2] The remedy for a slow federal regulator is faster federal review, more resources, or legal pressure—not 50 separate risk verdicts built from the same immature evidence.
[C1.3] The spillover problem makes state bans affirmatively unjust. Packaged food is manufactured and distributed nationally, so a ban in one large state rarely stays local. Firms must either maintain separate inventories and supply chains—raising costs for everyone—or reformulate everywhere to satisfy the most restrictive state. That may be sold as national progress, but it is a single state’s contested judgment becoming de facto national law without national consensus.
[C1.4] This is not a laboratory of democracy; it is one lab writing the protocol for all the others. Local restaurant trans-fat rules did not travel with a packaged product; a food-dye ban does.
[C1.5] Banning particular dyes also invites substitution, not safety. A manufacturer pushed away from one synthetic colorant can switch to another with a thinner safety record, or to natural alternatives with their own allergenic, stability, or sourcing problems. Without a comprehensive evaluation, a state ban may simply relabel the risk rather than remove it.
[C1.6] Finally, “purely cosmetic” does not suspend normal evidentiary standards. Consistent appearance and consumer enjoyment are real, if modest, values, and uncertain risk is not demonstrated harm. If the evidence were clear, federal action would be the proportionate response. While it remains genuinely disputed, state bans are premature, fragmented, and more likely to deliver compliance theater than safer children.
