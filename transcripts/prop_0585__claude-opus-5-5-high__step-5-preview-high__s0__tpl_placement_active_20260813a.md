[P1.1] The cheapest, most reliable battery on a continental grid is geography. When the wind dies in Iowa, it is often blowing in Oklahoma. When clouds cover California, Arizona is sunny. When a cold snap hits one region, the neighboring one usually has spare capacity.
[P1.2] Transmission and regional markets turn that diversity into a resource. Long-duration storage tries to manufacture, at great expense, what a wider grid gets largely for free.
[P1.3] My case rests on three points.
[P1.4] First, transmission unlocks the decarbonization we have already paid for. Across the United States, hundreds of gigawatts of wind, solar and storage projects sit stalled in interconnection queues, mostly waiting on grid capacity. Wind farms are routinely curtailed because the wires cannot carry their output to cities. Subsidizing storage does not fix that bottleneck.
[P1.5] Building lines does, and every new line serves every clean resource on it, including storage.
[P1.6] Second, integration delivers reliability now, with proven tools. Texas in 2021 shows the cost of isolation: a grid deliberately walled off from its neighbors could import only a trickle while millions froze. Europe's interconnected system, by contrast, routinely leans on neighbors during shortfalls. In the American West, a voluntary real-time market has let utilities trade surplus power across states, cutting costs and curtailment without building a single new power plant.
[P1.7] That is reliability bought with coordination and copper, not speculative hardware.
[P1.8] Third, long-duration storage is the wrong primary bet. Most technologies capable of multi-day discharge, such as iron-air batteries, hydrogen and compressed air, remain early-stage, costly and unproven at grid scale. Subsidizing them primarily means governments pick winners before the market knows which chemistry works. Worse, storage is only as clean as the surplus that charges it, and that surplus has to be delivered over transmission anyway.
[P1.9] Storage moves energy across hours. Transmission moves it across weather systems, time zones and entire regional shortfalls.
[P1.10] The motion says "primarily," not "exclusively." Storage has a role. But the foundation comes first, and the foundation is the grid itself.
