[P1.2] The proposition calls for a temporary moratorium, not a permanent ban. The distinction matters. A moratorium creates focused pressure to build the one thing the field lacks: a binding framework for consent, liability, and dispute resolution. [P1.3] That is not science policy; it is unilateral improvisation with the commons.
[P1.4] Consider the logic of "just a small experiment." Field trials are rarely endpoints. They generate data that justify larger trials, which justify deployment. [P1.7] Ocean iron fertilization offers a concrete parallel: states flagged governance gaps, and international bodies moved toward oversight rather than letting unilateral experimentation set precedents. The lesson is not that research stops; it is that the order matters.
