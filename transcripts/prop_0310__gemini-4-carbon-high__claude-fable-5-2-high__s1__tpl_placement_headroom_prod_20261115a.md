[P1.1] Every high school graduate eventually steps into the same immediate reality: they must feed themselves, maintain a living space, and sign financial contracts. Not every student will calculate derivatives or analyze Victorian poetry as an adult, yet every single one will confront grocery prices, credit terms, and a clogged drain or tripped breaker. Treating cooking, basic home repair, and personal finance as optional extras confuses niche enrichment with foundational survival. When schools graduate eighteen-year-olds who can factor polynomials yet stumble blindly into predatory debt, subsist on expensive ultra-processed food, or lose security deposits over trivial repairs, compulsory education has missed its plainest purpose: building capable, independent adults.
[P1.2] Relegating these skills to electives or family instruction ensures they miss the very students who need them most. Outsourcing the job to households assumes every caregiver already possesses the time, tools, and know-how to teach budgeting, safe cooking, and household maintenance, an assumption that collapses when parents work exhausting hours or face debt themselves, locking disadvantage in place across generations. Optional school courses fail just as predictably. College-focused teenagers bypass practical electives to chase transcript prestige, while less-supported students rarely self-select into forward-looking preparation at fifteen.
[P1.3] Only a core requirement removes that guesswork and guarantees an equitable, universal floor.
[P1.4] Making these competencies core does not mean turning high schools into full-time trade academies or displacing literacy and science. Foundational proficiency is compact and achievable: understanding compound interest, taxes, and leases; cooking affordable, nutritious staples safely; shutting off a leaking water valve, using basic hand tools, and patching drywall. Far from diluting academics, personal finance gives concrete stakes to algebra, cooking grounds health science, and repair cultivates physical problem-solving. Because no graduate can opt out of eating, housing, or money, schools cannot responsibly treat mastering them as optional.
