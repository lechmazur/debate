[P1.2] The case rests on three claims that cannot be answered with a "but it's safer now" reply.
[P1.3] First, the harms of non-disease screening are structural, not technical. Even a flawless test for height or IQ does nothing to address the social cost of parents shopping in a genetic marketplace. [P1.4] Making the science better only makes the sorting cleaner.
[P1.5] Second, access will be unequal by definition. Wealthy buyers will purchase tall, light-skinned, high-"cognitive" embryos; everyone else gets whatever the natural lottery hands them. [P1.7] Third, non-disease screening corrupts the medical mission and the child's right to an open future. A child born from such a selection has been reduced to a designed product, denied the chance to author her own life.
[P1.8] Restriction is not anti-science. It is the minimal, proportionate response to a technology whose predictable use is discrimination, stratification, and the corruption of childhood.
