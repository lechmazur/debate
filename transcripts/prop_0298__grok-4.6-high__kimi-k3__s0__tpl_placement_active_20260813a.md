[P1.5] This is not generic open-data cheerleading. These four histories share a structure: official power separated people from names, land, parents, and citizenship, then stored the evidence of that separation. Restoring access is part of repairing the record, not an optional consumer service. Markets ration repair to those who can pay.
[P1.6] A public archive does not.
[P1.7] When a land claim, a citizenship case, or a family reunion depends on a faded ledger, the person who needs it should not have to rent their history from a firm that did not suffer the rupture and does not answer to the community that did.
