[P1.1] Adult friendship is not collapsing because people stopped caring. It is collapsing because the conditions that make friendship possible—repeated, unhurried, low-cost contact—have been quietly dismantled. That is a policy outcome, and policy can respond.
[P1.2] Friendship needs time. Unpredictable and on-call work schedules consume it. It needs place. Car-dependent sprawl, shuttered third places, and scarce public space make a casual meetup a logistical project.
[P1.3] It needs continuity. High turnover, long commutes, and caregiving without support break it. Two nurses on rotating shifts can live in the same city and still never manage a weekly dinner. A new parent with a long commute has affection for old friends and no realistic way to sustain it.
[P1.4] This is not sentimental. Weak friendship networks are public load-bearing walls. They are where people get rides to surgery, help after a layoff, eyes on a struggling neighbor, and the ordinary trust that makes democracy less brittle. When they thin, the costs do not vanish; they move to emergency rooms, crisis lines, employers, and underfunded services.
[P1.5] Treating friendship as a social-policy concern does not mean state-mandated best friends. It means the decisions we already make collectively should be judged for whether they enable or erode connection: predictable scheduling, paid leave, transit and walkable mixed-use zoning, libraries and parks that function as third places, and workplace norms that stop treating every social hour as wasted productivity.
[P1.6] The opposing side will call friendship private. But the private sphere is not self-maintaining. A city that defunds libraries and zones away gathering spaces has made a policy choice. A workplace with rotating shifts has made one too.
[P1.7] Calling friendship private does not protect it from those choices; it hides them.
[P1.8] The question is not whether government can manufacture intimacy. It cannot. The question is whether public institutions should keep shaping friendship by neglect. We already shape it.
[P1.9] The only real choice is whether we do so blindly or deliberately.
