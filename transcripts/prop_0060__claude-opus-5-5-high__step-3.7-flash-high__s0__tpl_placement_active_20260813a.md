[P1.1] The question is not whether every worker thrives at home. It is whether, for many knowledge workers, letting them work from home beats forcing them into the office five days a week. That is a deliberately modest bar. To win, the other side must show that full-time attendance is the superior arrangement almost across the board.
[P1.2] It isn't, for three reasons.
[P1.3] First, focus. Knowledge work is paid for in concentrated thought: writing code, drafting contracts, analyzing data, designing systems. The open-plan office is built to destroy exactly that. A developer interrupted by a desk-side "quick question" can lose far more than the minute it takes to answer, because rebuilding a complex mental model takes real time.
[P1.4] At home, the same developer controls interruptions, batches messages, and protects long blocks for deep work. For the core output of knowledge jobs, fewer interruptions mean more finished work.
[P1.5] Second, time and energy. A commute of an hour or more a day is common in major cities. That is several hours a week of unpaid, exhausted dead time. Remote work returns some of those hours to work and the rest to rest, and rested people make fewer errors.
[P1.6] Add fewer sick days spent spreading illness around the office, and the productive hours stack up quickly.
[P1.7] Third, the evidence and the incentives point the same way. One of the best-known randomized trials, at a large Chinese travel company, found that call-center staff working from home were more productive and quit less often. Follow-up research on hybrid arrangements has generally found productivity holding steady or improving while attrition fell. Attrition matters enormously, because every resignation costs months of recruiting and onboarding.
[P1.8] Companies also gain access to talent beyond commuting distance.
[P1.9] Expect the other side to talk about mentorship, spontaneity, and culture. Those concerns argue for some deliberate in-person time, not for mandatory full-time attendance. Hybrid work is still working from home, and it still beats the five-day mandate.
[P1.10] The burden is clear. If remote or hybrid work makes many knowledge workers more productive, the motion stands.
