[P1.1] Social media platforms are engineered to exploit adolescent neurodevelopment, prioritizing engagement over safety, and half-measures have failed for over a decade. The alternative of parental controls and softer design tweaks is a failed strategy: controls are easily bypassed, require unequal levels of parental time and tech literacy that low-income families cannot afford, and platforms have zero incentive to enforce them when underage users are a massive, profitable demographic. [P1.2] A blanket ban on accounts for children under 16 is a proportionate, enforceable solution that closes the core loophole of the current system: kids lie about their age to access platforms that profit off their data and attention, and platforms have no reason to verify age when more users mean more ad revenue. [P1.3] Social media is no different. Its algorithmic amplification of harmful content, from extreme dieting to political extremism to relentless cyberbullying, poses unique, irreversible harm to developing brains that voluntary design changes have proven incapable of stopping—platforms will always roll back safety tweaks when they dent engagement, as Instagram did when it tested hiding like counts for teens and scrapped the feature after metrics dropped.
