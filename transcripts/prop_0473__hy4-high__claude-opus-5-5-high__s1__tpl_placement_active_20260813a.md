[P1.1] A matching engine is not a racetrack concession; it is the auctioneer’s clock. Ultra-low-latency co-location sells a shorter path to that clock. The product is not merely a rack, cable, or power supply; it is priority in time: the ability to learn, quote, cancel, and hit before slower orders arrive and public data are seen. An exchange that sells that advantage becomes a referee renting a head start.
[P1.2] The decisive harm is not envy, but wasteful transfer. Shaving microseconds off a fiber path does not make issuers more transparent, fund factories, or improve a pension fund’s allocation. It lets private firms win queue position, cross-venue latency arbitrage, and last-second cancellation races against slower liquidity. Those gains come from someone else’s execution: worse fills, more adverse selection for slower market makers, and a reason for investors to distrust displayed prices.
[P1.3] A market can survive disagreement about value; it cannot easily survive the belief that the venue sells unequal time.
[P1.4] The liquidity defense is weaker than it sounds. Co-location is formally open only in the sense that a scarce fastest tier can be bought; proximity, ports, and feed design are valuable precisely because not everyone can have them. Faster quotes are not resilient liquidity either. A quote whose edge is measured in microseconds can vanish when risk appears, with no public duty to remain.
[P1.5] Buying a deep-looking book in calm periods does not justify a permanent two-tier auction.
[P1.6] The rule should be structural, not anti-technology: no sale of ultra-low-latency server co-location next to matching engines to private trading firms. Exchanges may still build fast systems, charge ordinary connection fees, and provide standardized, delay-equalized gateways, synchronized data, and transparent rules. What they may not sell is a private toll lane to the auction. Co-location bought as differential speed turns a common marketplace into a hierarchy measured in microseconds; prohibition keeps the price-forming mechanism public.
