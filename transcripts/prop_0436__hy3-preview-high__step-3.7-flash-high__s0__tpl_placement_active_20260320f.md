[P1.1] The current model of food-delivery and reservation apps creates a digital pay-to-play environment that systematically hides affordable, independent establishments from the very consumers who need them most. This directly harms the user’s right to transparent price comparison and choice.
[P1.2] This heavy-handed privileging acts as a regressive tax on the consumer. Sponsored placements are often secured by well-capitalized chains that can absorb the customer acquisition costs, while local independent restaurants—which typically offer more competitive pricing and unique cultural value—are buried under layers of paid results. For a user searching for a casual weeknight meal, the app’s interface effectively functions as a paywall against affordability. If a cheaper, high-quality independent option is located two blocks away but remains invisible because it didn't "sponsor" its way to the top, the platform has failed its primary function of connecting supply with local demand.
[P1.3] Furthermore, this practice distorts the local economy. By forcing independent restaurants to sacrifice already thin margins on expensive sponsored slots just to maintain visibility, these apps accelerate the consolidation of the food industry into a few national brands. This reduces the diversity of the local culinary landscape and ultimately leads to higher prices across the board as competition is stifled. The proposition simply demands that the digital marketplace remain a level playing field where a restaurant’s merit and proximity—not its marketing budget—dictate its visibility.
[P1.4] Ensuring that users can easily find cheaper nearby independent options is not an attack on the app’s business model; it is a necessary correction to prevent these platforms from becoming predatory gatekeepers that prioritize corporate profit over consumer welfare and community health.
