[P1.1] A ride is a locked door, a stranger, and your destination. For many women that is not a neutral errand. Women-only ride-hail is justified because it cuts a repeated risk, restores trips that fear cancels, and does so at a cost that is optional and smaller than the harm it prevents.
[P1.2] The danger is structural. At night, after a drink, or in an unfamiliar city, the rider cannot easily exit or verify intent. Harassment and sexual assault in ride-hail are real enough that major platforms have published safety reports about them. Background checks should stay.
[P1.3] They do not solve the problem, because the person who harms a passenger was still cleared to sit in that car. A women-only option removes the pairing women most often fear, for the riders who want that protection, without pretending an app can read intentions.

[P1.5] An opt-in product turns avoided trips into completed ones. It also lets women drive under safer conditions, thickening the supply the feature needs. Safety people believe in is what fills the car.
[P1.6] Complexity objections pretend a voluntary lane replaces the network. It does not. Mixed matching stays the default. Only those who opt in face the narrower pool, and they accept that trade because they prefer it.
[P1.7] A longer wait is a minutes problem. Assault, stalking, and trips never taken are not. Platforms already run shared, premium, and accessible products. Extra dispatch logic has never made those categories illegitimate.
[P1.8] Single-sex cars, coaches, and hostels exist for the same reason: privacy and safety in a confined space, chosen rather than imposed. This is that principle at the curb. The proposition does not demand perfection. It demands that safety and trust outweigh a bounded matching cost.
