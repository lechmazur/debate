[P1.1] A passport is not a product. It is the promise a country makes about who belongs, and who it will stand behind. Europe has spent a decade quietly breaking that promise for a fee, and the record of what that fee bought is now clear enough to close the shop.
[P1.2] Start with what these programs actually are. A golden visa lets a wealthy person skip the queue everyone else stands in, usually by buying property. Citizenship-by-investment goes further: it sells the vote, the passport, and the right to live and work in twenty-seven countries. Because of Schengen and EU citizenship, a passport sold in Valletta or Nicosia is a passport into Berlin and Paris.
[P1.3] The seller keeps the money; the other twenty-six carry the risk. That alone should end the debate, because no single member state has the moral standing to sell what belongs to the whole.

[P1.5] Due diligence promises were made every time, and every time they failed, because the incentive of the seller is to sell.
[P1.6] Third, the economics are thin and the social costs are real. The money flows overwhelmingly into housing, not factories or startups. Portugal scrapped its real-estate route as Lisbon rents pushed locals out; Spain, Ireland, the Netherlands, and Britain have all ended or gutted their schemes. Countries that ran the experiment are the ones abandoning it.
[P1.7] The wealthy can still move to Europe through work, entrepreneurship, or ordinary residence rules. What should end is the sale. If CON wants to keep it, they must show benefits worth exposing an entire continent's borders and housing markets to the judgment of the least scrupulous seller.
