[P1.1] Dating apps don't just host chatter; they arrange meetings between strangers who would otherwise never meet. That power to create physical risk brings a basic obligation: the protection needed to use the product without getting scammed, stalked, or assaulted cannot be an upsell.
[P1.2] When identity verification, robust blocking, and fast scam response sit behind a paywall, safety becomes a luxury good. The people most likely to need it — young users, low-income users, single parents stretching every dollar — are left in the least protected pool. That is both unjust and dangerous, because predators don't stay in the free tier. A scammer with a fake profile can message dozens of unpaid users, harvest money or images, then vanish and remake the account.
[P1.3] Charging the victim for the shield while the attacker roams free gets the economics backwards.
[P1.4] Safety is also collective. One unverified catfisher harms paying and nonpaying users alike. One harasser who can dodge a weak free block by making a new account will simply find another target. Like bouncers in a bar or lights in a parking lot, verification and blocking only work when they cover everyone by default.
[P1.5] The cost excuse collapses on inspection. A block button, an unmatch that deletes chat history, in-app warnings about money requests, selfie-based liveness checks — these are scalable software features, not one-on-one bodyguards. Apps already afford sophisticated systems to promote profiles and process payments. They can afford to make baseline protection universal and still charge for genuine extras: boosts, extra filters, read receipts.
[P1.6] No one should have to pay extra to prove they are real, to stop someone harassing them, or to get help when a crypto investor soulmate asks for gift cards.
