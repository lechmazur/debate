[P1.1] The claim that full-time office attendance boosts knowledge worker productivity relies on an outdated assumption that physical presence equals output, a model that fails for roles where value comes from focused, uninterrupted cognitive work, not face time.
[P1.2] For knowledge workers, the single biggest drain on office productivity is unplanned, low-value interruption. Open-plan layouts, mandatory standups, and casual colleague drop-ins force constant context-switching: even a brief interruption can derail deep focus for 20 minutes or more, cutting high-value output by 30% or more for roles like coding, writing, or data analysis. This was confirmed empirically during the 2020 shift to remote work, when major tech and professional services firms reported sustained or increased productivity as employees moved out of open-plan offices.
[P1.3] Working from home eliminates nearly all of these unplanned disruptions, letting workers protect blocks of time for the deep, complex work that drives most knowledge output.
[P1.4] WFH also grants workers autonomy to align their schedules with their individual cognitive peaks, rather than conforming to a rigid 9-to-5 that wastes prime focus hours on commuting. A worker who does their best analytical work at 7am can log on immediately from home instead of spending 45 minutes each way in traffic, translating that saved time directly into output, or into rest that reduces burnout and sustains long-term productivity, rather than burning out from constant office context-switching and long commutes.
[P1.5] Critics will cite in-person collaboration as an office benefit, but for most knowledge workers, 70-80% of their output comes from independent deep work, not casual office interaction. Scheduled video calls can handle necessary collaboration without sacrificing the focused time that drives most results, and the small loss of spontaneous office chat is far outweighed by the gains from eliminated distractions and schedule autonomy. For many knowledge workers, the net productivity gain from remote work is clear and significant.
