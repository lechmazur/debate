[P1.1] The core flaw of DOGE-style efficiency drives is their reliance on "shock therapy"—a blunt-instrument approach that mistakes rapid headcount reduction for systemic optimization. These initiatives prioritize visible, short-term fiscal savings while ignoring the invisible, long-term erosion of state capacity. When you slash budgets and personnel by arbitrary percentages, you do not just cut "waste"; you cut the institutional memory and specialized expertise required to deliver complex services.
[P1.2] State capacity is not a luxury; it is the "software" that runs the "hardware" of government. For example, when efficiency drives force the mass retirement of senior engineers or regulatory experts, the immediate "savings" on payroll are quickly dwarfed by the catastrophic costs of infrastructure failure, botched contract management, or prolonged permit backlogs. The "damage" in this equation is the compounding interest of neglected maintenance and lost competency. A drive might save $10 million in salaries this year, but if that leads to a $500 million bridge collapse or a decade-long delay in a major energy project, the drive has failed the public.
[P1.3] Furthermore, these drives often damage the very services they claim to streamline. By treating public service delivery like a startup's quarterly optimization, they ignore the non-linear nature of government work. Removing layers of oversight or frontline staff to meet a "waste" quota creates a "service desert" for the most vulnerable citizens. The resulting administrative "brain drain" makes the state less able to respond to crises, from pandemics to climate disasters.
[P1.4] Ultimately, DOGE-style drives are a false economy. They trade the slow, steady accumulation of state capacity for a temporary, headline-grabbing surplus. The proposition stands because the "savings" are a one-time accounting trick, whereas the "damage" is a permanent impairment of the state’s ability to function. You cannot "efficiency" your way out of a hollowed-out government; you can only dismantle it.
