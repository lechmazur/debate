[P1.2] The question is whether a couple who chooses it deserves suspicion. They don't, for three reasons.

Most couples who marry today lived together first. A norm that labels the majority of future spouses as "weakly committed" isn't describing reality.
[P1.4] It is moralizing against it. When a social signal applies to nearly everyone, it stops carrying information about anyone.
[P1.5] Second, it is reasonable. Marriage is meant to be a lifelong legal, financial, and emotional merger. [P1.8] Selection was.
[P1.9] So the burden on CON is heavy. They must show that cohabitation itself signals weaker commitment, not that some couples drift. Otherwise they are asking society to stigmatize a prudent, common choice and to push people toward marrying blind.

[C1.4] Cohabitation invites what researchers call "sliding, not deciding"—inertia replaces intention.
