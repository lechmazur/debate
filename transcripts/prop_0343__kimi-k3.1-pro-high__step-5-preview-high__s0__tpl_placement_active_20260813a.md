[P1.1] The choice is not between AI and learning. It is between disclosed, accountable use that instructors can examine and an unenforceable prohibition that rewards concealment. [C1.1] Disclosure and accountability do not cure the central defect of this motion: it substitutes audit for apprenticeship. A degree certifies that a graduate can reason and write under conditions of genuine intellectual effort. When AI generates the first draft, the student is not practicing that effort; they are practicing quality control. That distinction is not a technicality—it is the entire educational transaction.
[C1.2] The proposition’s strongest claim is that ownership of a verified submission produces learning [P1.3]. But requiring a student to correct a fluent, deceptive AI draft is remediation, not formation. Consider a philosophy essay. The value is not the final argument; it is the struggle to frame a thesis, notice a counterexample, and reconstruct a paragraph when it collapses.
[C1.3] AI removes exactly that friction. A student who verifies citations has learned verification; they have not learned the disciplined thinking that makes citation necessary. Accountability checks the product but cannot guarantee the process was internalized.
[C1.4] Nor does disclosure level the playing field [P1.7]. It advantages those who already possess enough judgment to prompt well, spot errors, and synthesize revisions, while allowing underprepared students to skip the developmental work they most need. The integrity problem PRO raises [P1.6] is real, but the solution is better assessment design and pedagogy, not legitimizing the very shortcut that erodes the skills being assessed.
[C1.5] Finally, “most coursework” [P1.8] is not a small carve-out. In the contemporary university, drafting is the foundational reasoning exercise across nearly every discipline. Exempt a few exams and language labs, but permit AI-assisted drafting everywhere else, and the rule swallows the mission.
[C1.6] Universities should not certify graduates merely capable of supervising a machine. They should certify graduates who can do the work. The proposition gets that backward.
