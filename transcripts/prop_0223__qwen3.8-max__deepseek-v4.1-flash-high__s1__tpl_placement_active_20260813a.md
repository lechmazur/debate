[P1.1] Automated speed enforcement wins because it attacks the fatal-crash problem where it lives: pervasive, repetitive speeding. A police stop can deter only one driver, in one place, at the moment an officer happens to be present. Increasing stops also means more overtime, more patrol deployment, and more unpredictable roadside exposure, while cameras can operate continuously at fixed, documented danger points. They make the risk of being caught steadier, more predictable, and less dependent on who is behind the wheel.
[P1.2] That is what changes behavior at scale. Because crash energy rises steeply with speed, even modest, sustained reductions in mean speed translate into large reductions in deaths and serious injuries. When the same corridor is covered every day, drivers adjust not just once, but habitually.
[P1.3] Traditional stops are expensive, episodic, and carry externalities cameras avoid. They depend on scarce officer time, pull police away from violent crime and emergency response, and too often devolve into discretionary contact that can escalate. Traffic stops are also among the most common flashpoints for racial-profiling complaints and public mistrust. Chases and roadside confrontations create their own danger to officers, motorists, pedestrians, and bystanders.
[P1.4] Automated enforcement, by contrast, can be targeted transparently using crash and speed data, with signage, warnings, and calibrated penalties rather than suspicion-based encounters. It can also shift enforcement away from identity and toward the dangerous act itself.
[P1.5] The proposition does not claim cameras are perfect. It claims they produce larger safety gains with fewer harms than adding more stops. Cameras can be audited, limited to high-risk zones, and designed to prioritize safety over revenue. Police stops remain necessary for drunk driving, reckless conduct, and violent offenses, but as a scalable fatality-reduction tool, they are the blunter instrument.
[P1.6] If the goal is to save lives without multiplying invasive, uneven, and risky police contacts, automated speed enforcement is the safer path.

[C1.7] The proposition treats stops as frozen at their worst while assuming cameras are audited and benevolent.
