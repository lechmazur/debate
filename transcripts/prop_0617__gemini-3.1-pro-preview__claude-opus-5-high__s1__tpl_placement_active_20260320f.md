[P1.1] Digital markets are defined by immense network effects, data hoarding, and winner-take-all dynamics. Once a platform becomes dominant, its primary threat is rarely an established peer; it is nascent competition. It is the innovative startup that could one day pivot and disrupt the incumbent’s core monopoly.
[P1.2] The current antitrust framework is fundamentally broken for this digital reality. It places the burden of proof on under-resourced regulators to gaze into a crystal ball and definitively prove that a fledgling, often pre-revenue startup would eventually grow into a formidable rival. Because this counterfactual is nearly impossible to prove in court, dominant platforms operate with impunity. They create a "kill zone," routinely swallowing up emerging apps, adjacent services, and potential competitors before they can mature.
[P1.3] By the time the anti-competitive harm becomes undeniable, the market has already tipped, and it is impossible to unscramble the egg.
[P1.4] Shifting the presumption of anti-competitiveness to the dominant platform corrects a massive structural information asymmetry. Tech giants possess granular data on user engagement, backend analytics, and market trajectories. They know exactly why they are willing to pay astronomical premiums for startups with negligible revenue. If an acquisition genuinely unlocks substantial consumer benefits—such as integrating a novel technology that dramatically lowers costs or improves user experience—the acquiring firm is perfectly positioned to provide the evidence.
[P1.5] If they cannot prove that benefit, the acquisition is a defensive maneuver designed to kill a threat or hoard talent, and it should be blocked.
[P1.6] This policy does not ban acquisitions; it simply raises the bar for recognized monopolies. By requiring proof of substantial consumer benefit, we redirect the broader tech ecosystem. Venture capital will be incentivized to fund startups building sustainable, standalone products, rather than companies whose only viable exit strategy is auditioning for a buyout by a tech titan. Reversing the burden of proof is the only practical way to prevent defensive monopolies and restore the competitive churn that drives true technological innovation.
