[P1.1] When an animal model cannot predict whether a vaccine will work, the default alternative is not a perfect human study with zero risk — it is a gamble where the real experiment runs on thousands of unprotected people in an outbreak, without consent, without monitoring, and without early stopping rules. [C1.2] When we infect a volunteer, we create illness that would not otherwise exist for that person. Consent forms and rescue protocols do not convert a healthy subject into a patient who stands to benefit clinically. Payment further clouds voluntariness, inviting selection of those who need money more than they can fully assess long-term sequelae or residual risk of severe disease. Imperfect animal models for tuberculosis, influenza, or novel viruses are a scientific problem.
[C1.3] They do not authorize turning human beings into assay systems.
[C1.4] “More broadly” is the real danger. Restricted malaria or other challenge models already press limits; normalizing intentional infection whenever preclinical data are murky would erode the post-war consensus that research subjects must not be sacrificed for collective benefit. Field trials and observational work still require ethics review, monitoring, and early-stopping rules. They do not manufacture disease in the healthy.
[C1.5] Even sophisticated challenge models leave residual uncertainty about dosing, host variability, and rare catastrophic outcomes. An early efficacy signal can mislead if the artificial infection fails to match natural transmission. Accelerating a candidate that later fails still leaves volunteers harmed for a false positive. [C1.6] Ethics constrain science; science does not redefine ethics by claiming necessity. Broader permission normalizes deliberate harm under urgency. That bargain should be refused.
