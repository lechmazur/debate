[P1.1] Loneliness is not a bandwidth problem; it is a place problem. People become less lonely through repeated, unplanned, low-stakes contact — the familiar face at the chess table, the other parent at the rec center, the librarian who learns your name. Apps try to manufacture that intimacy through a screen; social infrastructure creates the conditions where it happens on its own. Cities should therefore put the next dollar into libraries, rec centers, and late-hour public spaces rather than app-based interventions.
[P1.2] First, the mechanism matches the disease. Urban research and common experience agree: casual "weak-tie" encounters are the scaffolding of belonging. After Chicago's 1995 heat wave, researchers found that neighborhoods with lively sidewalks, libraries, and gathering spots saw residents survive at notably higher rates than demographically similar areas without them — connection quite literally kept people alive. No push notification replicates a neighbor knocking on a door.
[P1.3] Second, the economics favor brick over code. A library branch is a durable, multi-use asset: cooling center in July, homework help in October, tax clinic in April — loneliness reduction rides along free on top of all of it. An app is a recurring line item with brutal churn; loneliness tools struggle to retain the very users they target, because isolated people are the least likely to keep initiating contact through a product that reminds them they are alone.
[P1.4] Third, equity. The loneliest residents — seniors, teens, low-income and night-shift workers — are precisely those least served by another download. A widowed 75-year-old will not swipe her way to friendship, but she will come to a Tuesday knitting circle. Free-at-the-door public space is the only intervention that charges no admission to belonging.
[P1.5] CON will praise apps for scale and low cost per user. But cheap-per-download is not cheap-per-friendship; scale without retention is just a bigger leaky bucket. The motion is comparative, and on every margin — durability, stacked returns, reach to those most isolated — physical social infrastructure wins.
