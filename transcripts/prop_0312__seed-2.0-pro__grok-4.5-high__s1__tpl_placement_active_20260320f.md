[P1.1] Professional sports leagues only exist as viable institutions because the public built them. Every major league operates on billions in unacknowledged public support: taxpayer funded stadiums, property tax exemptions, dedicated public safety for events, all paid by every resident, not just people who buy streaming subscriptions. It is fundamentally indefensible to lock the defining marquee games of the season behind paywalls when the public already financed the entire enterprise.
[P1.2] This is not an argument against streaming. It is an argument that a meaningful free-to-air share preserves the core value of the sport itself. When a championship, rivalry game, or historic moment goes exclusively behind a paywall, you do not just charge people to watch—you break the shared cultural ritual that makes sports matter. A child whose household cannot afford an extra monthly subscription does not become a lifelong fan that day.
[P1.3] They disengage. Leagues trading these casual, young, future fans for short-term streaming contract bonuses are burning the foundation of their own audience.
[P1.4] Critics will claim this just forces leagues to leave money on the table. That misses what we are actually weighing: sports are not just a private product. They are one of the last remaining mass shared cultural experiences. Locking them away does not just raise prices for viewers.
[P1.5] It erodes the common public life that makes professional sports worth having in the first place. That cost is far larger than any extra revenue stream might gain.

[C1.3] Public support for bricks does not create a moral lien on the signal.

Revenue that would otherwise be left on free television instead funds denser schedules, better athletes, and wider international discovery.
[C1.5] Artificial free tiers shrink that resource base and leave a thinner product for everyone.
[C1.6] Premium entertainment is already priced. Treating sports as uniquely sacred does not make free mandates efficient. Markets, not legislative nostalgia, decide the optimal mix of free and paid access.
