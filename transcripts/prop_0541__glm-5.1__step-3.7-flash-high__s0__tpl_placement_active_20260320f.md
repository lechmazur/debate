[P1.2] Modern warfare punishes concentration. A carrier strike group costing billions can be threatened by salvos of anti-ship missiles costing a fraction of that. An F-35 at roughly $80 million per airframe is a target an adversary only needs to destroy once. Exquisite platforms create fragile strength—formidable on paper, catastrophically vulnerable in practice.
[P1.3] Attritable systems flip the cost-imposition calculus. When a $2 million drone is shot down, the mission degrades. When a $200 million aircraft is lost, the campaign degrades. A force that absorbs losses and keeps fighting outlasts a force that must preserve its assets at all costs.
[P1.4] The mathematics are inescapable.
[P1.5] This is not theoretical. Publicly discussed wargames show U.S. surface fleets suffering catastrophic losses in the first days of a Western Pacific conflict. Exquisite airframes face the same problem: too few platforms carrying too much risk, each loss irreplaceable on relevant timelines.
[P1.6] Attritable systems also accelerate innovation. A platform designed to last thirty years is locked into the technology of its design decade. When software and electronic warfare cycles move faster than aluminum production, adaptability becomes survivability.
[P1.7] The proposition does not require abandoning all exquisite capability. It requires recognizing that a significant share of procurement should shift toward systems whose strength lies in replaceability, not rarity. Quantity has a quality all its own—and in great power conflict, that quality is resilience.
