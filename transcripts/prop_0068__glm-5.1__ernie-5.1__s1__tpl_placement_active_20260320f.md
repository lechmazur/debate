[P1.1] When a political campaign or commercial advertiser uses AI to profile your psychological vulnerabilities and serve you a persuasion message crafted specifically to exploit them, you face two distinct problems. The content might be AI-generated, and the targeting itself is AI-optimized. Labeling only the first misses the more dangerous half.
[P1.2] AI-optimized persuasion creates an asymmetry that democratic societies have never tolerated. The persuader knows your fears, your biases, your scroll patterns—but you don't even know you're being targeted. Traditional advertising is broadcast: everyone sees the same pitch. Micro-targeted AI campaigns mean your neighbor sees a different argument than you do, designed to hit where *they* are weakest.
[P1.3] No shared debate, no public accountability.
[P1.4] We already enforce disclosure when the stakes demand it. Political ads must identify their sponsors. Telemarketers must respect do-not-call lists. Targeted digital ads already carry "why am I seeing this" labels in the EU.
[P1.5] Requiring platforms to flag AI-optimized persuasion campaigns extends this principle, not invents it.
[P1.6] The objection that labeling is technically infeasible collapses on inspection. Platforms *host* these campaigns—their own ad systems perform the micro-targeting. If they can optimize delivery to a 500-person cohort glued to their phones between 10 p.m. and midnight, they can append a disclosure tag. The information asymmetry exists because platforms enable it; transparency reverses it.
[P1.7] What matters is *why* the label matters. Without it, citizens cannot evaluate whether an argument reached them on its merits or because an algorithm identified them as susceptible. That distinction is the difference between persuasion and manipulation. A healthy public sphere requires that people know when they are being singled out for psychological profiling so they can engage their critical faculties instead of scrolling past unaware.
[P1.8] Labeling AI-generated content alone addresses authorship. Labeling AI-optimized targeting addresses agency—the power to select who sees what and why. Both are essential. Half-transparency is no transparency at all.
