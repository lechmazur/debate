[P1.1] The question is who should control the factual backbone of history education: temporary political majorities or a process disciplined by evidence. Public schools teach children what happened, why it mattered, and how to evaluate contested accounts. On war, colonialism, and national identity, those accounts are especially vulnerable to being flattened into propaganda because politicians have direct incentives to celebrate the nation, excuse violence, and silence victims. Stronger independent scholarly review is the only practical way to make textbooks answer to evidence before they answer to the election cycle.
[P1.2] Independent review does not mean rule by an unaccountable clique. It means that textbooks are evaluated by historians, archivists, teachers, and subject-matter experts using transparent standards: primary sources are weighed, interpretations are checked against competing scholarship, minority experiences are not erased, and revisions occur when evidence changes. That process is not perfect, but it has a self-correcting logic political control lacks. A board or ministry can replace an uncomfortable chapter because it is inconvenient; scholarly review makes that replacement costly by demanding proof, citations, and peer scrutiny.
[P1.3] Leaving these narratives mainly to shifting political control produces predictable harms. One election can turn colonial conquest into a civilizing mission, wartime atrocities into noble necessity, or national identity into an ethnic myth. Students then receive history as loyalty training rather than civic education. That weakens democratic defense: citizens cannot judge their country’s present wars, reparations claims, immigration debates, or international disputes if their schooling has been sanitized.
[P1.4] The proposition wins because it chooses the standard that best serves the purpose of public schooling: teaching history as an accountable discipline, not as the current ruling party’s memory project. Political communities may still set broad curricular goals, fund schools, and debate values, but the core narrative content should be filtered through independent scholarly review before it reaches children.
