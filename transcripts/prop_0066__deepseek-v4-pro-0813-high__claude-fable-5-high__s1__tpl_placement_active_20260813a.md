[P1.1] A stranger’s face is not an ID badge. In public, you may be seen, but you are not searchable by name, address, employer, or social profile. AR glasses with live face recognition destroy that boundary. They turn a casual glance into a nonconsensual database lookup, silently and continuously.
[P1.2] The wearer chooses the device; the person identified chooses nothing.
[P1.3] This is the central injury. The face is matched against photos scraped from social media or sold by data brokers, then joined to whatever the app reveals. Unlike a phone camera, AR glasses can identify while the wearer seems merely to be looking around. The target cannot detect it, consent to it, or escape it except by leaving public life.
[P1.4] The harms are predictable. A stalker can confirm a person’s name, address, and social connections. An abuser can find someone living anonymously. Protesters, religious minorities, union organizers, and people visiting clinics or support groups can be identified by strangers.
[P1.5] Even without a specific crime, the possibility of being unmasked chills lawful activity. People will avoid protests, sensitive appointments, and support groups because public anonymity is no longer reliable. That burden falls hardest on the vulnerable.
[P1.6] Consent cannot fix it. The wearer consents, but the passersby never do. There is no realistic way to opt out of being recognized when the sensor is hidden in ordinary eyeglasses and works in real time. The ban should therefore be structural, not an after-the-fact punishment once someone has already been harmed.
[P1.7] A narrow professional exception is sufficient. Police acting under a warrant, or licensed security in a contained area with audit and retention rules, can address specific safety needs without turning every sidewalk, cafe, and train car into an identification dragnet. Ordinary consumers, employers, landlords, and hobbyists should not have that power. AR can still navigate, translate, message, and display useful information; it should not identify strangers by default.
[P1.8] The proposition bans the one feature that converts public space from relative anonymity into a panopticon.
