[P1.1] Public funding for youth sports, arts, and clubs provides the tangible, physical infrastructure necessary to combat the epidemics of youth loneliness and violence. In contrast, social media literacy campaigns are merely defensive measures that leave children isolated behind their screens.
[P1.2] First, consider the mechanics of loneliness. Loneliness is a structural deficit of meaningful connection. Teaching a teenager how to spot a toxic algorithm or block a cyberbully is useful, but it does not give them a friend. Extracurricular programs force face-to-face interaction, shared goals, and community belonging.
[P1.3] A theater production or a local soccer team builds organic peer networks and breaks digital isolation by giving youths a compelling reason to log off. Literacy campaigns teach kids how to survive being alone online; extracurriculars ensure they are not alone to begin with.
[P1.4] Second, to prevent violence, we must address physical realities. The peak hours for juvenile violent crime and victimization are the unstructured hours immediately following the school day. Social media literacy campaigns do nothing to alter a child's environment during this high-risk window. Publicly funded clubs and sports physically place youth into structured, safe spaces.
[P1.5] Crucially, they provide consistent access to adult mentors—coaches, directors, and community leaders—who can identify warning signs of distress or aggression before violence occurs.
[P1.6] Finally, sports and arts offer experiential learning in conflict resolution. Violence often stems from an inability to manage interpersonal friction. Collaborative activities inherently require daily practice in navigating disputes, managing frustration, and cooperating with diverse peers. This hands-on socialization builds emotional regulation far more effectively than a passive classroom module on digital etiquette.
[P1.7] To meaningfully reduce youth loneliness and violence, we must change where kids spend their time and who they spend it with. Social media literacy is a vital band-aid for digital harm, but publicly funded extracurriculars are the cure for the physical isolation and unstructured time that actually drive these societal crises.

[C1.5] A literacy campaign addresses the on-ramp; a club meeting often arrives after the crash.
[C1.6] Finally, scale and discipline matter.
