[P1.1] The United States manufactures dependency by law. An asylum seeker can file on day one, but cannot even apply for a work permit for 150 days, then waits months more for approval. During that forced idleness they cannot legally support themselves and end up in city shelters, emergency rooms, food pantries — not by choice, but because we forbid the alternative.
[P1.2] Granting authorization at 60 days breaks that pipeline. When people can work legally within two months, they leave shelter systems faster and buy groceries with wages, not aid. Cities like New York and Chicago have strained emergency housing budgets precisely because new arrivals could not work. Self-sufficiency is cheaper than forced dependency.
[P1.3] Work also moves people from underground cash jobs where exploitation is rampant to on-the-books jobs where they pay payroll taxes and labor law applies.
[P1.4] The fiscal effect is straightforward: fewer shelter nights, more contributors. Asylum seekers are generally ineligible for federal means-tested benefits anyway; the public assistance at issue is the emergency net cities provide when people are barred from working. Remove the work bar and you remove the need.
[P1.5] Critics say this invites fraudulent claims, but work authorization is not asylum. It confers no green card and no right to remain if the claim fails. It is temporary and revocable. The incentive to file a meritless case is the years-long chance to stay, not 60 days of legal work.
[P1.6] Someone willing to fabricate a claim, pay a smuggler, and risk removal will not be deterred by a 150-day wait. Someone with a real fear still needs to feed their kids in the meantime.
[P1.7] Security vetting already happens at the start with biometrics and credible fear screening. We can check identity and safety threats within 60 days without granting final relief. That preserves integrity while letting honest claimants support themselves. Trading costly, state-funded idleness for self-reliance reduces public costs without materially changing the fraud calculus.
