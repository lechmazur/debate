[C1.1] The motion asks which tool is "stronger," and PRO answers with a picture of a warm room on a Tuesday evening. But a picture is not a comparison. Three problems sink the claim.
[C1.2] First, reach. Literacy programming scales: one curriculum touches every kid in a school, including the rural, disabled, undocumented, poor, or simply uncoached kid who will never make a team. Youth programs scale only through buildings, coaches, transport, and fees — the exact costs public budgets are worst at. And the kids most at risk of violence are systematically the ones who never sign up.
[C1.3] Selective attendance means programs deliver their benefits to the already-connected. A campaign delivers to the isolated kid scrolling at 1 a.m., which is precisely where PRO's lonely teenager now lives.
[C1.4] Second, mechanism. PRO calls online harm a "symptom of offline emptiness." That is backwards for much of today's conflict. Humiliation spreads through group chats, feuds escalate through screenshots, harassment follows kids home from the pitch, and status is fought for in comment sections.
[C1.5] A football team does not teach a fourteen-year-old how to disengage from a pile-on, spot manipulation, or resist retaliating to an insult at midnight. Literacy interventions directly target the environment where modern peer conflict is ignited, escalated, and recorded. Programs do not.
[C1.6] Third, PRO's evidence is a category error. The Iceland example bundled programs with parental agreements, community coordination, and changed norms — so it cannot isolate funding for clubs as the active ingredient. Meanwhile, drama clubs and competitive teams can reproduce the very dynamics we care about: hazing, exclusion, hierarchy, coaches modeling aggression. PRO assumes programs build belonging; they also build in-groups.
[C1.7] So the honest comparison is not "belonging versus information." It is a targeted, universal, cheap intervention that reaches the actual drivers of adolescent isolation and conflict versus an expensive infrastructure that mostly serves the kids who least need it. Until PRO can show programs reach the disconnected, the motion fails.
