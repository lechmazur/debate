[P1.1] Minimum wage law exists to do one thing: guarantee that when a worker makes themselves available to an employer, their hour is worth at least a floor. Gig platforms have engineered a clever workaround. They tell a driver to log on, sit in a designated zone, keep the app open, accept rides within seconds or face penalties — and then claim that the forty minutes between pings is somehow the worker's leisure time. It isn't.
[P1.2] It's unpaid labor dressed up as freedom.
[P1.3] Consider what waiting time actually is on these apps. The platform algorithmically controls how many workers can log in, where they should position themselves, how quickly they must accept, and what happens to their rating if they decline. That is not a worker browsing Netflix who might pick up a job; that is a worker on call, constrained, and economically captive. Every classical test of "engaged to wait" — control, restriction of movement, inability to use the time effectively for oneself — is satisfied.
[P1.4] If a warehouse made employees stand by a conveyor for an hour with no tasks coming through, no one would seriously argue those minutes are free.
[P1.5] The task-only metric creates a perverse incentive the proposition fixes. Platforms currently profit by oversupplying labor: more idle drivers means faster pickup times, which means happier customers, while the cost of that idleness is dumped entirely on workers. Counting active waiting time internalizes that cost. Suddenly the platform has a reason to match supply to demand rather than warehouse human beings in parking lots at their own expense.
[P1.6] Two clarifications matter for the rest of the debate. First, "active waiting" means logged in and available to that platform — not multi-apping idle time or hours off-shift. Second, compliance is calculated across the pay period, not per minute, so good-earning hours can offset slow ones; the floor only bites when the platform has truly underpaid for time it controlled.
[P1.7] That floor is exactly what minimum wage was invented to be.
