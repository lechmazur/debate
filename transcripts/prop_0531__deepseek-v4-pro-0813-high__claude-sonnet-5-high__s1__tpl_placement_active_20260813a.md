[C1.1] Every "shared civic account" produced immediately after a civil war has to be written by somebody, and that somebody is whichever side controls the ministries, the school curricula, and the memorial commissions when the guns fall silent. That is rarely a neutral committee of citizens. It is usually the victorious faction, the demographic majority, or whoever negotiated from strength. So PRO's proposal does not describe a neutral civic frame descending from above the conflict; it describes handing the losing or minority side's memory to the winners, dressed in the language of unity.
[C1.2] Rwanda is the clearest case. The post-genocide state built a single official narrative around national unity and reconciliation, and backed it with "divisionism" and "genocide ideology" laws that criminalize public mention of RPF killings of Hutu civilians during and after 1994. That is not reconciliation; it is one community's grief being nationalized while another's is silenced by statute. A civic account imposed before political trust exists becomes a permanent gag order, not a bridge.
[C1.3] Spain shows the opposite failure mode. After Franco, the country adopted a "pact of forgetting," effectively a shared civic silence rather than a shared civic account, so that Francoist victims went undocumented and unmemorialized for decades. Only recently, through slow, contested, group-specific memory work, has Spain begun exhuming mass graves and naming perpetrators. The lesson holds for PRO's framework too: premature unity, even framed as citizenship rather than silence, forecloses the truth-telling that has to happen before real shared meaning is possible.
[C1.4] Meanwhile Bosnia demonstrates that civic peace does not require civic story. Republika Srpska and the Federation maintain starkly separate memorials and school narratives, yet the country has not returned to war in thirty years. What sustained peace was power-sharing institutions, not a unified memorial text. CON's case is simple: sequencing and pluralism protect truth and prevent domination; forced unity protects whoever already holds the pen.
