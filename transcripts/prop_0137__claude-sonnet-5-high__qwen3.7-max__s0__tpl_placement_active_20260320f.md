[P1.1] Egg freezing sounds like liberation. In practice, when it is marketed alone, it is a message: solve your fertility problem on your own time, so we don't have to solve our workplace problem on ours. That substitution is the core of this debate, and it is why PRO wins.
[P1.2] Start with the mechanism. Egg freezing is expensive, invasive, and statistically uncertain—success rates for live births from frozen eggs, especially those retrieved after 35, are far lower than the marketing implies. It is a hedge, not a guarantee. When a company puts it on a benefits page next to unlimited vacation and a nice 401k match, it is choosing the cheapest possible gesture toward "supporting women" while avoiding the expensive, structural fixes: real parental leave, subsidized childcare, predictable schedules, and a culture where taking leave doesn't tank your promotion track.
[P1.3] That asymmetry matters because the two policies send opposite signals. Fertility, leave, and caregiving support say: have your family whenever it makes sense for you, and we'll absorb some of the cost. Egg-freezing-as-flagship-benefit says: delay having your family so it doesn't cost us anything now. A famous example from a decade ago—major tech firms rolling out egg freezing coverage—drew exactly this criticism at the time: employees and commentators asked why the same companies weren't equally aggressive about paid leave or on-site childcare.
[P1.4] The optics were not paranoid; they reflected an actual incentive structure. Freezing eggs keeps women working full tilt through their most promotable years, at the woman's medical and financial risk, while childcare and leave cost the employer real money every year, not a one-time perk.
[P1.5] This isn't an argument against egg freezing existing as a benefit. It's an argument against it existing as the flagship, the headline, the thing gestured at in recruiting slides while the harder supports are thin or absent. If a company won't also fix leave and caregiving, promoting egg freezing as their signature "women's benefit" isn't empowerment. It's outsourcing a structural problem onto individual women's bodies and calling it generosity.
