[P1.1] Marketing egg freezing as the flagship women's benefit, without real support for starting a family now, is not empowerment. It is a message: thrive here by postponing motherhood.
[P1.2] A flagship benefit is not just a line item. It is branding. When a firm puts future fertility on the billboard while offering thin parental leave, rigid schedules, no childcare help, and no coverage for trying to conceive today, it signals which kind of mother it prefers — the one who waits. That shapes promotions, planning, and pressure far more than any brochure disclaimer about choice.
[P1.3] The medicine makes that branding more troubling. Egg freezing is invasive, hormonal stimulation and retrieval, with ongoing storage costs and no guarantee of a baby later. Success falls sharply with age at freezing, exactly when busy professionals finally get around to it. Selling an uncertain, medicalized delay as the crown jewel of women's health while neglecting proven supports — paid leave, flexible return, affordable caregiving, fertility treatment now — trades real help today for a gamble tomorrow.
[P1.4] It also divides women. Highly paid professionals who can afford storage, take time for procedures, and absorb failed cycles get celebrated as planners. Younger, lower-paid, shift or hourly workers who need leave and childcare now get nothing usable. A flagship that helps managers delay and abandons assistants who cannot is not closing a gender gap; it is widening a class gap.
[P1.5] Genuine choice requires viable alternatives. Add strong leave, caregiving, pregnancy accommodation, and coverage for conceiving earlier, and egg freezing becomes one option among many. Without them it becomes the company-preferred option, turning a private reproductive decision into a career strategy underwritten by the boss. Employers should not buy that influence unless they first fund the freedom to say no to delay.
