[P1.1] The fundamental purpose of criminal justice is not to balance an emotional ledger of past suffering, but to protect human life and prevent future harm. Past crimes cannot be undone, but future victimization can be stopped. For that reason, rehabilitation must stand as the primary aim of criminal punishment.
[P1.2] Retribution is entirely backward-looking. It reduces justice to an arithmetic of state-sanctioned retaliation: answering harm with calculated pain. Yet inflicting suffering in isolation solves nothing. The overwhelming majority of incarcerated individuals eventually return to society.
[P1.3] An offender subjected purely to punitive confinement is released more alienated, deskilled, and traumatized than when they entered. By treating punishment as an end in itself, retributive systems generate a revolving door of recidivism, manufacturing the very crime they claim to condemn. Retribution purchases temporary catharsis at the expense of lasting public safety.
[P1.4] Rehabilitation, by contrast, is forward-looking and constructive. It addresses the structural and psychological drivers of criminal behavior—such as substance dependency, untreated trauma, and lack of vocational skills. When correctional institutions invest in education, cognitive therapy, and job training, they do not excuse wrongdoing; they dismantle the mechanisms that cause reoffending. Jurisdictions that structure punishment around reintegration achieve drastically lower recidivism rates precisely because they treat offenders as future neighbors rather than permanent outcasts.
[P1.5] Furthermore, rehabilitation demands far greater accountability than retribution. Passively enduring a prison sentence requires nothing from an offender. True rehabilitation forces individuals to confront the reality of their actions, develop genuine empathy for victims, and undertake the demanding work of personal transformation and restitution.
[P1.6] A justice system governed by retribution produces more victims tomorrow to satisfy vengeance today. A system anchored in rehabilitation prioritizes durable community safety, social cohesion, and the active prevention of future harm. Transforming human behavior is the only path that truly protects society.
