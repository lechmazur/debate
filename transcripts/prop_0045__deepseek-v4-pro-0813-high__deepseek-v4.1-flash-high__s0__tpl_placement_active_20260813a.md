[P1.1] The case turns on one practical question: do these tests forecast something an employer actually needs to know—performance, turnover, team effectiveness, or maladaptive behavior? If not, they are not simply weak evidence; they are decision noise dressed as science.
[P1.2] MBTI fails that test at the classification stage. It sorts people into 16 types using four forced choices, treating continuous preferences as either/or types. Most people sit near the midpoint on at least one dimension, so small wording changes or mood shifts can flip a label. A tool that cannot reliably assign the same person the same type across a few weeks cannot reliably predict that person’s future work.
[P1.3] DISC has the same flaw in a four-quadrant form: it measures self-reported preferred style, not capability. The modest predictive value sometimes found in continuous trait measures does not transfer to forced-choice type labels; these tests are not built to measure job capabilities. Hiring decisions, however, require capability, skill, and observable behavior. Saying someone is a “High I” tells an employer almost nothing about whether that person handles customers, deadlines, or conflict well.
[P1.4] Predictive validity is the proper threshold precisely because employment decisions are high-stakes. When employers use MBTI or DISC to screen or group people, they turn weak self-descriptions into consequential outcomes. That creates false precision and can magnify bias: type labels such as introvert, feeling, or cautious can act as proxies for race, gender, neurodivergence, or cultural style while failing to measure the job itself.
[P1.5] Team-building use is no safer. Assigning “ENTJ” or “High C” labels encourages teammates to treat one another as fixed categories rather than observe actual behavior, which can harden conflicts and self-fulfilling roles. Employers already have better alternatives: structured interviews, work samples, trial tasks, and behavior-based references. These target actual job behavior, not a personality type.
[P1.6] If an employer would reject a medical test with this little predictive power, it should reject MBTI and DISC for hiring and team-building. That is the rule the motion asks for, and it fits.
