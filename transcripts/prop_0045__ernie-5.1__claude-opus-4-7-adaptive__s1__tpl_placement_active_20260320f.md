[P1.1] Standardized personality tests like MBTI and DISC are marketed as tools for hiring and team‑building, but the core claim that they predict job performance or team effectiveness is simply not supported by the evidence. Their predictive validity—the correlation between test scores and actual work outcomes—is consistently near zero. Large‑scale meta‑analyses of personality measures show that only the Big Five traits, and even those modestly, relate to performance; MBTI’s four dichotomies and DISC’s four styles explain less than 5 % of variance in job success, a figure indistinguishable from chance.
[P1.2] When employers rely on such weak signals they make costly mistakes. A candidate labeled “INFP” may be passed over for a sales role, while an “ESTJ” is pushed into leadership despite having no demonstrated competence in those areas. The result is higher turnover, lower morale, and legal exposure: personality tests can be challenged as disparate‑impact discrimination because they filter out protected groups without a business‑necessary link to performance.
[P1.3] The opportunity cost is equally stark. Time spent administering, scoring, and interpreting MBTI or DISC could be invested in tools with proven validity—structured behavioral interviews, work‑sample simulations, or validated cognitive ability tests—all of which predict performance two to three times better than any MBTI or DISC score.
[P1.4] Even the original developers of MBTI warned against using it for hiring, yet companies treat the results as gospel. The veneer of scientific objectivity masks a tool that merely sorts preferences, not competencies. Relying on it is no better than flipping a coin, yet firms pay for the privilege of a false sense of certainty.
[P1.5] Because the tests add noise rather than signal, they undermine the very goals of hiring and team‑building: placing the right people in the right roles and creating coherent teams. The rational response is to discontinue their use and replace them with evidence‑based alternatives. Employers should stop using MBTI and DISC now.
