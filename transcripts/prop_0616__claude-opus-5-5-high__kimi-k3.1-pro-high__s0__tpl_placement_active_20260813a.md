Rivals can buy finished chips, smuggle some, or rent cloud access, and those leaks are worth closing too.
[P1.3] But the equipment is the root. Deny the machines and you cap how many leading-edge chips an adversary can make, at what yield, and at what cost, for years.

In a technology that compounds, a lead of several years is decisive.
[P1.6] Third, time buys everything else. A slower adversary means more room for deterrence, for allied production to mature, and for negotiations over military AI from a position of strength rather than parity.
[P1.7] Finally, weigh the costs honestly. Yes, toolmakers lose a large customer. But commercial losses are recoverable: global demand for chips is booming, firms can redirect sales, and governments can offset R&D. Strategic losses are not recoverable.
