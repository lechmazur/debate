[P1.1] Knife and gang violence is not a courtroom problem; it is a problem of the ten seconds before a blade is pulled. A retaliation cycle can start with a threat, a humiliating video, a drug debt, or a group moving through a station after school. If police can act only on concrete signs of a weapon, the blade travels with the person most fearful and most ready to use it. The state arrives with tape, not prevention.
[P1.2] Wider preventive powers change that timing. Time-limited stop-and-search in authorised hotspots, grounded in intelligence, behaviour, or risk patterns, lets officers recover knives before confrontation and map the small networks moving weapons between districts. It also deters carriage: gangs rely on a blade in a waistband being invisible and costless. Frequent, lawful, visible stops make that gamble uncertain.
[P1.3] One recovered knife can break a retaliatory chain.
[P1.4] Rights are not a trump for the person carrying the weapon; they are the reason the state must protect the route from school to the bus stop. A brief, recorded search under clear rules, body-worn cameras, supervisor review, published data, and a complaints route is proportionate to preventing death or life-changing injury. The child walking home, the shop worker closing late, and the witness who wants to speak without fear have claims on the state, too. A status quo that leaves armed groups confident until after bloodshed is not liberty; it is abandonment.
[P1.5] The opposition will warn about abuse and discriminatory use. That risk demands design, not paralysis. Freezing proactive powers does not abolish discretion; it drives it into vague conversations, informal intelligence, and late arrests, with less documentation. Formal, audited stops are easier to challenge than invisible suspicion.
[P1.6] The choice is not freedom or policing: it is whether a known violence hotspot gets short, reviewable interruptions before a stabbing, or ambulances and a homicide inquiry afterward. European countries should choose the interruption.
