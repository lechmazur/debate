[P1.1] Your phone is yours, but Apple and Google retain landlord rights. Two companies control over 95% of the US mobile OS market and dictate how nearly every consumer app is distributed and monetized. That is textbook gatekeeper power, and their mandated toll - up to 30% on in-app purchases - is not payment for service but rent extracted from a captive market.
[P1.2] Forcing app stores to allow independent payment systems fixes that abuse directly. If Spotify, Netflix, or a small fitness startup can use Stripe or their own billing, they keep margin to lower prices or reinvest. Consumers see lower prices, developers keep businesses alive. Right now Amazon won't sell Kindle books inside its iOS app, not because it can't, but because the fee makes it unviable.
[P1.3] That is deliberately broken user experience imposed to protect a cut.
[P1.4] Sideloading and alternative stores are the second necessary release valve. Android has allowed sideloading for years without collapse. Macs allow direct downloads and remain secure and profitable. Sideloading enables whole categories Apple rejects or taxes away: game streaming services, adult content, experimental AI tools, open-source repositories like F-Droid.
[P1.5] It restores the basic principle that if you own hardware, you decide what code runs on it.
[P1.6] The security objection is a pretext for control. We regulate safety without mandating monopoly. Require disclosure, sandboxing, automated notarization and malware scanning, like macOS does, and let users assume informed risk for most consumer apps. We don't ban side streets because highways have guardrails.
[P1.7] The EU's Digital Markets Act already requires this and iPhones haven't become virus dens. Mandating payment choice and sideloading doesn't destroy the App Store - it forces it to compete.
