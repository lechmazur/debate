[P1.1] The modern live-event ticketing market is fundamentally broken, captured by professional scalpers and automated bots that treat concert and sports tickets as financial derivatives rather than access passes. When platforms allow unlimited resale markups, they structurally incentivize hoarding. Bots sweep up inventory in milliseconds, artificially creating scarcity to extract maximum rent from desperate fans. This is not a functioning free market; it is a parasitic extraction of wealth from consumers and artists by middlemen who add zero value to the live experience.
[P1.2] Requiring major platforms to cap resale markups or provide face-value exchange channels directly neutralizes this exploitation. A mandated face-value exchange ensures that if a fan cannot attend, the ticket returns to the public pool at its original price. Capping markups—say, at a modest ten percent to cover basic transaction fees—eliminates the profit motive for professional scalpers. If there is no massive arbitrage to capture, the financial incentive to deploy expensive botnets simply vanishes.
[P1.3] Opponents will argue this disrupts free-market price discovery, but live event ticketing is not a standard commodity market. The supply of a specific tour is strictly fixed, and the primary market already sets a baseline price reflecting the creator's valuation. Allowing secondary markups to soar into the thousands does not benefit the artists; it merely transfers wealth from everyday fans to offshore ticket brokers.
[P1.4] Furthermore, major ticketing platforms operate as entrenched monopolies. They control both primary issuance and secondary resale, actively profiting from the scalping dynamics they claim to oppose. Forcing these dominant platforms to implement face-value exchanges or strict caps strips away their ability to double-dip on secondary fees. Mandating these reforms restores the basic social contract of live entertainment: tickets are meant to be experienced by actual fans, not hoarded and flipped by algorithms for unearned profit.
