[P1.1] Unauthorized workers remain in the United States primarily because they can obtain employment. Remove that access, and the incentive structure driving illegal migration changes fundamentally. Mandatory nationwide E-Verify does exactly this by requiring every employer to verify new hires against federal Social Security and Homeland Security databases within days of hire. This is not a bureaucratic formality; it is a real-time electronic check that catches fraudulent documents, stolen identities, and mismatched data that the current paper-based I-9 system routinely misses.
[P1.2] Critics warn of increased discrimination, but the design of E-Verify actually reduces it. Today, employers reviewing physical documents must make subjective judgments about authenticity and, inevitably, about who "looks" foreign or sounds accented when presenting papers. That discretion creates exactly the bias the opposition fears. A standardized electronic check applied uniformly to every new hire—native-born citizen or legal immigrant—removes human discretion from the equation.
[P1.3] You cannot discriminate based on appearance when the system simply validates a Social Security number against a federal database. States with mandatory E-Verify show no spike in discrimination complaints.
[P1.4] On labor shortages, the claim confuses exploitation with scarcity. The sectors that rely heavily on unauthorized labor—agriculture, construction, hospitality—are not facing a shortage of willing workers; they are facing a shortage of workers willing to accept poverty wages paid under the table without legal protections. When employers must hire only authorized workers, they must raise wages and improve conditions to attract legal labor, including citizens, permanent residents, and guest workers. Arizona, which mandated E-Verify for all employers in 2008, saw its unauthorized population decline significantly while the state economy continued to grow, showing labor markets adjust.
[P1.5] Mandatory E-Verify levels the playing field. It protects American workers from wage suppression, shields compliant employers from unfair competition from those who cheat, and removes the primary magnet that draws unauthorized migration. The alternative is a permanent two-tier labor market where millions work in the shadows, vulnerable to abuse and paid less than their legal counterparts.
