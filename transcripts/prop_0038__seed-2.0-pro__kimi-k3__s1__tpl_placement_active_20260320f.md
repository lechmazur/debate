[C1.1] Nobody disputes that chronic noise is miserable. But notice what this motion actually demands: not that cities regulate noise — they already do, through ordinances, decibel limits, and quiet hours — but that noise receive the *same urgency* as air quality, with *binding limits* on traffic, construction, and commerce. That parity claim fails three ways.
[C1.2] First, the harms are not equivalent. Air pollution is invisible, cumulative, inescapable, and toxic at any dose: particulates drift for miles into lungs that never consented. Noise harm is real but mostly indirect — stress, lost sleep — and radically context-dependent. PRO cites population studies linking road noise to heart attacks [P1.3], but those studies carry an obvious confound: the same traffic making the noise makes the exhaust.
[C1.3] Separating the two cleanly is notoriously difficult, which is why "equivalent permanent harm" [P1.5] is an assertion, not a finding.
[C1.4] Second, the enforcement model does not transfer. Air regulation works because measurement is objective and regional: a sensor reads the same PM2.5 no matter who complains, and a handful of major sources drive most pollution. Noise is transient, hyperlocal, and multi-source — was it the truck, the bar, the jackhammer, or the siren? Binding limits on something you cannot reliably attribute will not produce air-quality-style enforcement; they produce complaint-driven, selective enforcement — law by grievance, weaponized by the loudest resident against venues, builders, and newcomers.
[C1.5] Third, the trade-offs are severe. Binding construction limits extend building timelines and raise housing costs amid a housing shortage. Binding traffic limits push freight into congested daylight hours. Binding commercial limits strangle hospitality jobs and nightlife.
[C1.6] Air quality rules purchase large, quantified health gains per dollar of disruption; noise parity purchases marginal quiet at outsized cost.
[C1.7] Cities have finite enforcement capacity. Every inspector chasing decibel readings is one not inspecting air, water, or lead. Noise merits proportionate, targeted rules — which already exist. Parity is not public health; it is misallocation dressed up as principle.
