[P1.1] Academic independence fails in small, practical ways: a topic dropped from a syllabus, a speaker uninvited, a finding softened so a renewal stays likely. Foreign government-linked funding makes those choices rational. A foreign state does not fund universities as a disinterested patron. It funds them as part of a political strategy.
[P1.2] Countries should require disclosure and impose limits, because independence cannot be left to the hope that money with a foreign policy purpose will behave like a neutral grant.
[P1.3] The mechanism is dependency, not a cartoon of bribery. When a center's budget, graduate stipends, or building depend on a government-linked donor, criticism of that government becomes a financial risk. Researchers learn the boundary without being ordered to observe it. Hiring tilts toward scholars who will not jeopardize the relationship.
[P1.4] The public then receives research that looks independent and is not. Disclosure ends the pretense. If the support is legitimate, it can survive daylight. If naming the source would embarrass the university, that embarrassment is evidence, not a reason for secrecy.
[P1.5] Limits matter because disclosure alone does not cure capture. A fully disclosed dependency is still a dependency. A university can publish the donor's name and still need the donor's check. A ceiling forces diversification and preserves the real option to refuse.
[P1.6] Without that option, academic freedom becomes the freedom to say what the largest foreign patron will tolerate.
[P1.7] The alternative is not open global science. Private collaboration, unclassified research partnerships, and non-state gifts remain available. The line is government linkage, because states can reward and punish across borders in ways individuals cannot. Domestic government funding is politically accountable to the same society the campus belongs to.
[P1.8] A foreign government's university budget answers to another capital. Letting that budget set the outer edge of inquiry is not internationalism. It is subcontracting independence. Countries should require the disclosure and the limit before the quiet edits become the culture of the institution.
