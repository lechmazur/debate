[P1.1] Conventional age restrictions for tobacco have had half a century to deliver, and they have failed. Nine in ten adult smokers first tried nicotine before age 18, despite decades of 18+ and 21+ sales bans, and 1 million people still die from tobacco-related causes globally every year. Age limits rely on individual teen restraint and spotty retail enforcement, but 80% of underage tobacco users get products from social sources—older friends, family, not stores. These rules create a persistent underage black market and frame nicotine as a forbidden fruit teens are incentivized to seek out.
[P1.2] A generational ban fixes this by shifting the burden from vulnerable teens to the supply chain, where enforcement is simpler. The policy prohibits tobacco sales to anyone born after a set cutoff date, permanently removing that entire cohort from the legal market. No peer can legally buy cigarettes to share, no retailer caters to underage users, and the social norm of giving tobacco to younger friends collapses overnight.
[P1.3] Public health modeling shows a generational ban would cut national smoking rates to under 1% within two decades, compared to the stagnating 12% adult U.S. smoking rate that barely moved after the federal tobacco age was raised to 21 in 2019. It is the only policy that can eliminate intergenerational nicotine addiction entirely, rather than just delaying uptake.
[P1.4] Tobacco is not a normal consumer good: it is engineered to be addictive, kills half its long-term users, and has zero safe level of use. A generational ban is a proportionate, evidence-based step to end the tobacco epidemic, rather than tinkering with failed age limits that leave millions of young people at risk of lifetime addiction.
