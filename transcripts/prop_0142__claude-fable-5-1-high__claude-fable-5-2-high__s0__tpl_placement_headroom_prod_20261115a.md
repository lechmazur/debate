[P1.5] Three reasons this is right.
[P1.6] First, it is ordinary consumer protection. We already forbid false claims in advertising. A digitally manufactured body attached to a product is a false claim about what that product delivers. [P1.7] Second, minors are the right population to protect. Adolescence is precisely when body image and self-worth are being wired. [P1.8] Beauty manipulation belongs in that category.
[P1.9] Third, disclosure works and is already feasible. Sponsored-content labels became routine once required. Platforms already detect synthetic media; flagging it is a design choice, not a technical impossibility.
[P1.10] The alternative is a market where children are sold insecurity by images engineered to be unattainable, and told nothing. A label costs almost nothing. The silence costs children their sense of what a normal body looks like.
