[P1.1] When a diner opens a menu, a concert‑goer picks seats, or a shopper scrolls a delivery app, the number they see should be the number they pay. Mandatory service charges that appear only at the final checkout are not a nuance of pricing; they are a hidden tax on the decision‑making process. By forcing the true cost to surface early, we restore the basic premise of a market transaction: informed consent.
[P1.2] Consumers routinely abandon carts when a surprise fee appears at the last click. Data from e‑commerce platforms show that unexpected surcharges can increase abandonment rates by double‑digit percentages, a loss that hurts both buyers and sellers. Restaurants that hide a 10 % service charge until the bill arrives erode trust; diners who discover the extra cost after eating feel cheated, and repeat visits drop. The same pattern plays out in ticketing, where “facility fees” disclosed only after seat selection push fans toward secondary markets that advertise all‑in prices.
[P1.3] Transparency also sharpens competition. When every player must display the full price up front, firms can no longer win by burying fees; they must compete on the actual value of food, entertainment, or delivery speed. This drives down unnecessary charges and rewards businesses that keep costs honest.
[P1.4] Regulators have already recognized the logic. Airline and hotel industries in many jurisdictions must show mandatory taxes and resort fees before purchase, and the result is not chaos but clearer choices for travelers. Extending that standard to restaurants, ticketing services, and delivery apps is a straightforward consumer‑protection step, not a bureaucratic burden. Modern checkout interfaces can list a single line‑item total or a clear breakdown without slowing the flow.
[P1.5] Opponents will claim that upfront fees complicate pricing or hurt small operators. But the cost of a modest UI tweak is trivial compared with the reputational damage and lost sales that hidden fees generate. The real burden is on consumers who are forced to pay more than they thought. Requiring disclosure before checkout simply aligns price with expectation, protects wallets, and strengthens markets.
