[P1.1] When you buy a phone, you should own it. Right now two gatekeepers tax and filter almost everything you do on it. They decide which apps can reach you and take a cut of nearly every digital sale inside them. That is not a marketplace.
[P1.2] It is a toll booth.
[P1.3] The payment lock-in is pure rent-seeking. With commissions around a third, developers must raise prices, cut features, or abandon mobile altogether. Consumers pay more for music, ebooks, and subscriptions while small studios lose the margin that funds innovation. Allowing independent payments fixes this without destroying the store.
[P1.4] Apple and Google could still offer convenient checkout for a reasonable fee — they just could not force it. When developers can use rivals or link to their own sites, fees fall and savings flow to users.
[P1.5] Sideloading extends the same choice to distribution. Warnings of catastrophe ignore how computers already work. Macs, Windows PCs, and even Android allow software from outside the main store using permissions, scanning, and clear warnings. Users who want maximum lockdown can stay inside the official store.
[P1.6] Others can try rival stores that compete on curation or price. Security through captivity is not safety, it is control.
[P1.7] The phrase for most consumer apps matters. No one needs unvetted code on every sensitive device. Policymakers can keep stricter rules for banking, health, kids’ products, and government use while requiring transparency, malware screening, and easy uninstall elsewhere. What cannot be justified is forcing a game, dating app, or streaming service to pay the toll and obey editorial whims to reach its own customers.
[P1.8] Force the gates open and stores must compete on quality and trust rather than coercion. Developers gain leverage, prices fall, and censored or niche apps find users. Apple and Google stay highly profitable, just not untouchable. Ownership, lower costs, real competition — that is why the mandate is overdue.

[C1.1] Your phone is not your laptop. Your laptop doesn't track your location 24 hours, hold your FaceID, hold your banking keys, your kid's messages, and pay for your groceries with a tap. Treating it like a Windows PC in 2008 is nostalgia masquerading as ownership.
[C1.2] That difference makes the app store model a safety product, not a toll booth. Apple and Google's forced checkout is what gives you one card on file, one place to cancel subscriptions, one tap for refunds, mandatory parental approvals, and a receipt engine that blocks the classic internet scam of "free trial that steals your card." Open that to any independent payment iframe and you don't get lower prices, you get the same dirty tricks that plague the open web - fake Spotify paywalls harvesting credit cards, game currencies with no refund button, and children's games pushing them to an external browser to pay.
[C1.3] Sideloading fails the choice test PRO promises in [P1.5]. He says users who want lockdown can stay inside. In reality dominant apps will force you out. When Epic, Match Group, or Meta can save 30% by steering you to a sideloaded APK or outside link, they will - with dark patterns, 'save 10% if you download here' popups and features locked unless you leave.
[C1.4] The Android data already shows this: sideloaded apps carry an order of magnitude more malware because no vetting, no sandbox enforcement, no automatic revocation. Parents can't manage that complexity, and most users will click through.
[C1.5] And 'most consumer apps' [P1.7] collapses. Games, dating and streaming are exactly where kids spend, predators message, and piracy and fraud explode. The commission - largely not even 30% anymore for small developers and subscriptions - funds human review, hosting, malware scanning, and discovery that a small indie could never afford alone. Break the integration and you don't punish Apple; you punish user trust.
