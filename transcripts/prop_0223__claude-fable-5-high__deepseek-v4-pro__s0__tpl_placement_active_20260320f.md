[P1.2] First, the safety mechanism. Deterrence runs on certainty, not severity. A police officer can stop perhaps a dozen speeders in a shift, in one spot, while thousands pass unobserved. Drivers learn the odds and speed accordingly.

[P1.4] Second, the externalities. [P1.5] It cannot profile, cannot escalate, cannot shoot, and cannot be assaulted. It also doesn't consume officers' time—freeing police for crimes that actually require human judgment.
[P1.6] Third, scale and cost. Cameras enforce continuously at a fraction of the cost per violation, meaning coverage of high-fatality corridors that no realistic staffing increase could match.
[P1.7] The honest concerns about cameras—revenue abuse, fines burdening the poor—are design problems with known fixes: income-scaled fines, warning thresholds, transparent placement based on crash data.
