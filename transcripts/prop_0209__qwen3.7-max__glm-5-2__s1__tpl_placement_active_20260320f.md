[P1.1] The current EU asylum framework, anchored by the Dublin Regulation’s "first country of entry" rule, is a structural failure. It dictates that frontline states like Greece, Italy, and Spain must manage most arrivals simply due to geography. This is neither fair nor sustainable. We must implement binding burden-sharing quotas to save human lives and the European project.
[P1.2] First, the status quo guarantees humanitarian crises. Frontline states lack the infrastructure to process massive influxes. The result is overcrowded, dangerous camps and severe human rights violations. By distributing arrivals proportionally based on member states' administrative capacity, quotas ensure asylum seekers are processed humanely and adjudicated faster, rather than languishing in legal limbo for years.
[P1.3] Second, leaving frontline states alone destroys EU political cohesion. When peripheral nations shoulder disproportionate burdens, domestic backlash inevitably follows. We see this in the rise of anti-immigrant populism and unilateral border closures, which directly threaten the Schengen zone. Mandatory quotas replace chaotic fracturing with institutional solidarity, preventing border states from resorting to illegal pushbacks or deliberately allowing migrants to travel north irregularly just to relieve local pressure.
[P1.4] Finally, quotas vastly improve integration. Bottlenecking asylum seekers in peripheral economies struggling with high youth unemployment and strained public services hampers their ability to find work. Distributing them across the EU’s diverse labor markets and housing stocks maximizes their economic contribution and long-term social integration.
[P1.5] The opposition will defend national sovereignty or claim forced relocation is unworkable. But sovereignty is already an illusion under the current chaos, where irregular secondary movements dictate where migrants actually end up. Quotas replace undocumented drift with managed, legal distribution across the continent. A union that cannot manage its external borders collectively will eventually lose its internal ones.
[P1.6] We must choose orderly solidarity over a geographic lottery.
