[C1.1] The PRO’s case rests on two fatal, unproven assumptions: first, that a blunt across-the-board meat tax will only curb overconsumption by the wealthy, and second, that governments will reliably redirect all new tax revenue to offset harm to low-income households. [C1.2] A tax that raises the price of staple meats by 30% doesn’t make a family buy more lentils—it makes them cut back on essentials like rent, childcare, or heating, or skip meals entirely, deepening existing food insecurity. [C1.3] Even if revenue were fully redirected, the immediate harm would still outweigh theoretical long-term gains. The environmental benefits of reduced meat consumption do not justify trading the health of vulnerable people for unenforceable promises of future government support.
