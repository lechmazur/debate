[P1.2] Replacing this system with heterogeneous classes paired with intensive tutoring solves the sorting problem while actively addressing the learning gap. Heterogeneous classrooms maintain high expectations and rigorous, grade-level curricula for everyone. Instead of pulling struggling students into a dead-end remedial track, schools provide high-dosage, just-in-time tutoring. This targeted, small-group support allows students to master foundational gaps during the school day without being segregated from their peers or denied access to complex, grade-level mathematical concepts that drive critical thinking.
[P1.3] Furthermore, detracking measurably improves the learning environment for all students. In heterogeneous classes, positive peer effects elevate the academic discourse. Advanced students solidify their own understanding by engaging with diverse problem-solving approaches, while struggling students benefit from a classroom culture of high expectations. Intensive tutoring ensures that the pace of the heterogeneous class doesn't leave anyone behind, providing a genuine academic safety net that tracking falsely promises but structurally denies.
[P1.4] The core clash today is whether we prioritize the illusion of tailored pacing through permanent segregation, or actual mastery through rigorous inclusion and targeted intervention. Tracking sacrifices long-term equity, collaborative peer benefits, and late-blooming potential for short-term administrative convenience. By replacing arbitrary sorting with universal access and targeted help, we finally align our school structures with the reality that mathematical potential is widely distributed, even if early opportunity is not.
