[P1.1] The principle here is simple and right: rules should protect the people who actually live with the consequences. If an AI system decides who gets a loan, who gets hired, or who gets flagged by police in Lyon or Lisbon, the citizen affected does not care where the company's headquarters sit. They care that the system touched their life. Protection should follow the harm, not the corporate address.
[P1.2] Consider the alternative, which is what CON must defend. If high-risk rules attached to a provider's headquarters, every serious AI company would simply incorporate its risky operations in a lenient jurisdiction outside the EU and beam the outputs back in. A hiring algorithm built and headquartered in a country with no safeguards could screen European job applicants all day long. That is not regulation; that is an open invitation to evade it.
[P1.3] Location-of-impact rules close that door. Location-of-headquarters rules build the door and hang a "this way out" sign above it.
[P1.4] This is also the only standard that treats European and foreign companies equally. If you regulate by headquarters, a French firm faces the full weight of the rules while its American or offshore competitor selling into the same French market faces nothing. That punishes the companies that stay home and rewards those that flee. Impact-based rules level the field: anyone affecting Europeans plays by the same European rules.
[P1.5] There is strong precedent. The GDPR already works on this logic, applying to the processing of EU residents' data regardless of where the processor is based. That extraterritorial-by-impact approach has become the global template precisely because the alternative was unworkable. The same reasoning applies with even greater force to AI, where outputs cross borders instantly and invisibly.
[P1.6] The core question is this: should European protection depend on a citizen's exposure to harm, or on a corporate filing address she will never see? Only one of those answers actually protects people. I urge you to follow the impact. The harm is where the rule belongs.
