[P1.1] A fourteen-year-old gets arrested for shoplifting. The case is diverted, he does community service, and the judge tells him it won't follow him. Then he turns twenty-three, applies for a nursing program, and a $19 background-check vendor pulls the docket entry that was digitized in 2016 and mirrored on four data-broker servers. The judge's promise was sincere.
[P1.2] It was also, in the digital era, a lie.
[P1.3] That gap is the whole case. Every state already accepts the principle that juvenile records deserve confidentiality — the rehabilitative theory of juvenile court is built on it. What has changed is not our values but our storage technology. Paper records were sealed by obscurity: buried in a courthouse basement, functionally invisible.
[P1.4] Digital records are sealed by nothing at all unless a court affirmatively acts. So the proposition isn't a new indulgence; it's the minimum required to keep an existing commitment from being quietly repealed by search indexing.
[P1.5] Notice the word "presumptively." This is a default rule, not an absolute one. Prosecutors, victims, researchers, and the press can still make a case for access in a particular file. All the presumption does is shift who bears the burden of a doubtful case — and that allocation is the real clash.
[P1.6] Today the burden falls on a twenty-two-year-old who must hire a lawyer, navigate a petition process he's never heard of, and pay fees, to undo consequences of something he did before he could drive. Under the proposition, the burden falls on the party who wants ongoing public access to a child's record and can articulate why.
[P1.7] And the beneficiary isn't only the young adult. Employment and housing are the two strongest known stabilizers against reoffending. A regime that makes a nineteen-year-old permanently unhireable is not a public-safety measure; it manufactures the risk it claims to monitor.
[P1.8] Adolescents are, by law and by neuroscience, treated as less culpable and more redeemable. A record that is permanent, searchable, and free contradicts both. Fix the default.
