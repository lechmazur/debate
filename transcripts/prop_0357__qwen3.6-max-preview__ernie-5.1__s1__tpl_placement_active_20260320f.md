[P1.1] When you purchase a vehicle, you are buying a machine, not leasing a software license. Yet automakers are increasingly wiring core repair functions—diagnostic access, module calibration, part pairing—to connectivity subscriptions. When those subscriptions lapse, the car doesn’t just lose streaming music or live traffic; it loses the ability to be properly maintained. This converts outright ownership into a conditional arrangement where basic functionality is held hostage to recurring fees.
[P1.2] A requirement to preserve core repair access after subscription expiration is the only way to keep ownership meaningful.
[P1.3] The harm is structural, not incidental. Modern vehicles rely on software handshakes to verify replacement parts, reset maintenance alerts, and run safety calibrations. If an automaker gates those handshakes behind a paid connection, a lapsed subscription instantly blocks independent mechanics and owners from performing routine work. The result is artificial obsolescence: mechanically sound vehicles become stranded by code, repair costs spike, and consumers are forced into dealer captivity or premature replacement.
[P1.4] This isn’t innovation; it’s a monopoly toll on hardware you already bought. Independent repair networks get starved out, competition collapses, and the manufacturer captures a permanent rent stream from maintenance that should belong to the owner.
[P1.5] The mandate is narrow and technically straightforward. It leaves premium connected services, remote convenience features, and proprietary data streams entirely untouched. It only requires that the diagnostic and calibration pathways necessary for safe, functional repair remain active regardless of subscription status. Automakers already engineer these pathways; the rule simply bars them from weaponizing software to override property rights.
[P1.6] Without this floor, there is no market check on digital lockouts, and ownership degrades into a revocable license. The clash here is simple: companies can absolutely monetize connectivity, but they cannot use it as a kill switch for basic repair. This requirement draws that line and preserves what ownership actually means.
