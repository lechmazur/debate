[P1.7] Recidivism stayed high because a record closes more doors than it opens.
[P1.8] This is not legalization of sales or a free pass for trafficking. It is a narrow change in how small amounts for personal use are handled. [C1.5] PRO says police can focus on supply, but without possession as a tool, officers lose a lawful point of contact to interrupt use, identify dealers, and route people to services.
[C1.6] Portugal is the test case, but it bundled decriminalization with a major treatment and social-service expansion. That undercuts the idea that decriminalization itself does the work. Where treatment capacity is thin, decriminalization may mainly reduce deterrence while leaving addiction untreated.
[C1.7] The real choice is not punishment versus compassion. It is a targeted, treatment-backed enforcement model versus a policy change whose independent effects on deaths and crime are uncertain and context-dependent.
