[P1.1] Fishing nations are not choosing between livelihoods and conservation. They are choosing whether their exclusive economic zones remain fisheries at all.
[P1.2] No-take protection at 30 percent of an EEZ is the minimum scale that actually rebuilds fish. Small, scattered closures leave most spawning biomass exposed. Continuous fishing skims off the large, old females that produce most of the eggs. Without a large, connected fraction of habitat left untouched, recruitment stays weak, food webs stay simplified, and climate shocks have nowhere to buffer.
[P1.3] The remaining 70 percent then has to carry the entire harvest. That is how nations fish themselves into thinner nets and longer trips.
[P1.4] Dependence on fishing income does not cancel this. It is the reason to act. A community that lives on catch is already living on a declining asset if effort is high and biomass is low. Closing water does not create that pain; overfishing already has.
[P1.5] What a large no-take network does is convert part of the EEZ into a factory for fish. Catch per unit effort on the open side can rise even as total area fished falls. Keeping nearly all water open is not a jobs policy. It is open access until the stock no longer pays.
[P1.6] Thirty percent is large because oceans are large and connected. Token parks cannot hold predators, rebuild habitats, or keep effort from simply shifting. At that coverage, representative reefs, shelves, and upwellings can sit inside the line together. That is how recovery compounds instead of leaking.
[P1.7] Refusing this is not solidarity with coastal people. It is betting their next decade on a harvest the water cannot replace. The hardship of transition is real. Collapse is worse, longer, and less reversible.
[P1.8] Nations that wait for painless conservation will discover that the communities they meant to protect have already lost the fish.
