Class size reduction is the prime example: politically popular, extraordinarily expensive, and structurally inefficient. By contrast, high-dosage tutoring—defined as three or more small-group sessions weekly—is the most potent, cost-effective engine of academic growth available to K–8 schools.
[P1.2] On gains per dollar, tutoring wins decisively across both the numerator and the denominator.
[P1.3] First, consider the numerator: instructional impact. Shaving four or five students off a class does not alter pedagogy. Teachers still rely on whole-group instruction, where struggling students slip through the cracks and advanced students wait. High-dosage tutoring fundamentally changes the learning dynamic.
[P1.4] In a 1:2 or 1:3 ratio, instruction is deeply diagnostic. A tutor immediately identifies whether a third-grader struggles with phonemic decoding or fractions, forcing active cognitive engagement and correcting misconceptions in real time. Decades of empirical evaluations confirm that high-dosage tutoring produces effect sizes multiple times larger than class size reductions, driving dramatic, accelerated growth in foundational reading and math.
[P1.5] Second, consider the denominator: cost and structural efficiency. Reducing class sizes across K–8 demands astronomical outlays. Districts must construct physical classrooms and hire thousands of fully certified teachers amidst severe labor shortages. That massive hiring surge historically forces schools to lower credentialing thresholds, diluting baseline teacher quality.
[P1.6] Moreover, class size reduction is an untargeted, blunt instrument, spending millions lowering headcount for students who are already proficient.
[P1.7] Tutoring avoids these traps. It is surgically targeted to students behind grade level and can be scaled flexibly through trained paraprofessionals, service fellows, or recent graduates operating under teacher supervision.
[P1.8] Because high-dosage tutoring delivers vastly larger academic gains without locking districts into permanent, unsustainable payroll expansions, it decisively outperforms class size reduction on a per-dollar basis.
