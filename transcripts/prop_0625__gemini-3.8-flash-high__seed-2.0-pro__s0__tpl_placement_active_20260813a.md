[P1.1] The atmosphere is an indivisible global commons. When a technology is engineered to alter the Earth’s radiative balance—potentially disrupting monsoon patterns, shifting precipitation zones, and redistributing agricultural risk—it cannot be treated as a routine academic exercise. Imposing a temporary moratorium on solar geoengineering field experiments is not an abandonment of science; it is an essential safeguard against unilateralism, technological lock-in, and planetary conflict.
[P1.2] Crucially, this moratorium does not ban research. Computer modeling, laboratory simulation, and passive atmospheric monitoring can and should proceed. What must pause is open-air experimentation—the deliberate physical release of reflective particles or cloud-brightening agents into the sky.
[P1.3] Open-air trials cross an indelible line. They do not merely generate data; they build the industrial, political, and financial infrastructure of deployment. Once proprietary hardware is built and private capital is invested, research programs develop self-sustaining momentum. This technological lock-in lowers the barrier to deployment long before the global public has consented to the risks.
[P1.4] Without a binding framework, field experiments pave an unregulated runway for a single powerful nation, or even a rogue private actor, to present the world with a dangerous fait accompli.
[P1.5] The geopolitical stakes are immediate. If outdoor testing proceeds in the absence of enforceable international law, any subsequent regional drought, crop failure, or extreme weather event can and will be blamed on foreign atmospheric tampering. In a fragmented world, uncoordinated field trials are an engine for international suspicion and escalation. There is currently no treaty governing liability, no mechanism for informed consent from vulnerable nations, and no protocol to resolve transboundary disputes.
[P1.6] Allowing outdoor testing to outpace regulation guarantees that governance will be written retroactively by the powerful actors who act first. A moratorium provides the indispensable diplomatic leverage to compel the world to establish binding, democratic rules before anyone attempts to seize the planetary thermostat.
