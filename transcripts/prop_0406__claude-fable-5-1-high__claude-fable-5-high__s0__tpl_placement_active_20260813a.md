[P1.1] A car parked overnight with a family asleep inside is not a public nuisance. It is the last thing standing between that family and a sidewalk. The question is whether government should knock that last thing away.
[P1.2] Notice how narrow this motion is. It covers people with no safe alternative shelter, and it opposes routine fines, towing, and harassment. It does not prevent a city from responding to actual misconduct: dumping waste, blocking a driveway, threatening neighbors. Those are conduct, and conduct laws already exist.
[P1.3] What the motion forbids is punishing the act of sleeping itself when the only other option is the ground.
[P1.4] Punishment works by deterrence, and deterrence only works when the person can choose differently. A woman working nights at a warehouse who lost her apartment cannot be fined into a lease. Ticket her, and she has less money for a deposit. Tow her car, and the impound fee, often several hundred dollars plus daily storage, exceeds anything she can pay, so the car is auctioned.
[P1.5] In one stroke she loses her shelter, her transportation to work, her belongings, and her children's stability. Then she loses the job. The city has not solved homelessness; it has manufactured a harder, more visible, more expensive case of it.
[P1.6] That is why enforcement is bad policy even for people who care nothing about her. Unsheltered street homelessness costs far more in emergency rooms, jail nights, and cleanup than a person quietly sleeping in a sedan. Cities that tried the alternative, sanctioned safe-parking lots with restrooms and case managers, in places like Santa Barbara, San Diego, and Seattle, found they could address sanitation and safety without a tow truck, and often moved people back into housing.
[P1.7] So the clash is this. CON must show that fines and towing produce a better outcome than leaving the person in the car. Not that vehicle dwelling is unpleasant, not that neighbors would rather not see it, but that taking away the last rung makes anyone, including the neighbors, better off.
