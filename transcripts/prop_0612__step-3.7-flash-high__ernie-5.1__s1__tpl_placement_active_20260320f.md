[P1.1] The core mission of universities is unconstrained, truth-seeking inquiry—a public good that requires insulation from covert external political control. Foreign government-linked funding poses a unique, well-documented threat to that independence, making mandatory disclosure and reasonable limits a necessary, proportionate safeguard, not an attack on global academic collaboration. Foreign state funding is never neutral philanthropy. Donor governments routinely attach explicit or implicit conditions: research agendas aligned with their strategic interests, suppression of findings that expose their abuses, or preferential access to sensitive dual-use research outputs.
[P1.2] Investigations across the U.S., EU, and Australia have uncovered widespread undisclosed foreign government funding to universities, including grants to engineering departments that diverted research on critical technologies to directly benefit the donor state’s military and economic goals, while faculty who raised concerns faced marginalization. Voluntary transparency has failed. Universities have long hidden these funding streams to avoid scrutiny, secure additional grants, and maintain international partnerships, leaving students, faculty, and the public unable to assess potential bias in research they rely on for public health, climate policy, and national security decisions.

They only require transparency about state-linked funding sources and prevent any single foreign government from exerting disproportionate control over a university’s research priorities, hiring, or curriculum.
[P1.4] This policy does not punish global engagement. It protects the integrity of the academic enterprise itself. Without these guardrails, universities risk becoming unwitting extensions of foreign state influence, eroding public trust in research and undermining the very purpose of higher education.
