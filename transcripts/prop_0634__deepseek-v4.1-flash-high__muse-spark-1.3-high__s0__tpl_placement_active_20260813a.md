[P1.1] The line is not editing genes. It is editing the people who do not yet exist. Somatic gene therapy treats a consenting patient; risks end with that patient. Germline embryo editing writes changes into every cell, including sperm or eggs, so the modification is part of the child and their descendants.
[P1.2] That is why a ban can be narrow and still decisive: leave non-heritable therapy legal, ban only the creation of heritable edited embryos for reproduction.
[P1.3] Three reasons make that ban necessary. First, consent is impossible. The future child cannot authorize a permanent experiment written into their body; neither can their children. We do not allow parents to consent to irreversible experiments on behalf of a person who has no voice, especially when the benefit is speculative.
[P1.4] Second, the safety case is not a one-generation question. Off-target edits, mosaicism, and unexpected interactions among genes may not show up immediately. A somatic error is tragic for one patient; a germline error can be replicated across a family line. There is no recall.
[P1.5] Third, the medical need is weak. Embryo selection and somatic therapies can address many conditions without altering the germline. Where they cannot, the answer is more research and ethical alternatives, not crossing a boundary that would predictably be used for enhancement. Once heritable editing is legal for disease, the distinction between therapy and enhancement becomes a regulatory line drawn under commercial and competitive pressure.
[P1.6] The result is not equal access; it is a market in designed children, with the wealthy first in line.
[P1.7] The “reproductive freedom” objection inverts the principle. Freedom protects the right to have a child, not the right to specify that child’s genome and impose that choice on generations who cannot refuse. A country that permits this does not merely tolerate a private risk; it authorizes a permanent public one. The ban is the conservative, precautionary choice: permit therapy that heals a patient, forbid editing that manufactures a lineage.
