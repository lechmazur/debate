[P1.1] Picture a hallway between classes. A teacher who bans phones spends the whole period fighting a losing battle the moment students step into their next room, where a different teacher allows them. That inconsistency is not a minor wrinkle — it is the whole problem, and it is why this policy has to be set at the school level, not teacher by teacher.
[P1.2] Start with the mechanism of harm. Smartphones are engineered by some of the most sophisticated behavioral design teams on earth to capture attention through notifications, social validation loops, and infinite scroll. A fifteen-year-old is not equipped to self-regulate against that, and no individual teacher, however strict, can out-design Silicon Valley's engagement algorithms one classroom at a time. A schoolwide, default-off policy removes the temptation from the environment entirely, rather than asking a teenager to exercise willpower every forty minutes against a device built to defeat willpower.
[P1.3] Second, teacher-by-teacher rules create a fairness and enforcement nightmare. Students learn instantly which teachers enforce the rule and which don't, and treat the strict ones as obstacles to route around rather than authorities to respect. This undermines classroom authority generally: a teacher who bans phones looks arbitrary next to a colleague who doesn't, and students exploit that gap. A single default policy removes the negotiation entirely — there's nothing to argue about, because it isn't that teacher's personal rule.
[P1.4] Third, think about the students most at risk of harm — those prone to anxiety, social comparison, or bullying via group chats that follow them from class to class. A patchwork policy still lets those dynamics operate in every "phones allowed" period. Only a schoolwide default meaningfully interrupts the harm for the whole day, not just during the classes of teachers who happen to care enough to fight it.
[P1.5] Consistency, protection, and enforceability all point the same direction: this decision is too important to leave to individual discretion.
