[P1.1] Every legitimate law rests on a simple premise: compliance must be physically possible. Every human being must sleep, stay warm, and occupy space. When a city bans tents and outdoor sleeping while offering nowhere lawful to go, it does not regulate conduct; it outlaws biological survival for anyone unable to afford housing. Cities need no anti-encampment ordinances to stop violence, theft, open flames, or blocked emergency lanes, because ordinary criminal and safety codes already cover those acts.
[P1.2] Anti-encampment laws specifically ban sheltering oneself in public, and punishing people for existing outdoors is indefensible without a real way inside.
[P1.3] That alternative is real only when genuinely available shelter, storage, and services come together. First, shelter must be genuinely available: an accessible, safe bed open that night, not a theoretical cot on a full waitlist, a facility closed to wheelchairs, or a curfew that costs a night-shift worker their job.
[P1.4] Second, storage is indispensable because a tent holds a person’s lifeline back to stability, including photo IDs, insulin, work tools, and winter coats. When shelters ban bags and sanitation crews trash belongings in sweeps, enforcement destroys the exact documents and medicine needed to survive and regain housing.
[P1.5] Third, service options, from mental health and addiction care to housing navigation, are what turn an emergency cot into an exit rather than a revolving door.
[P1.6] Without those three pillars, criminalizing visible poverty fails even on pragmatic grounds. Fining people who have no money and arresting people who must pass landlord and employer background checks actively locks them onto the street. Sweeping one block with nowhere for residents to go never restores public order; it merely pushes exhausted people two blocks away, stripped of survival gear, severed from outreach workers, and deeper in crisis. A city earns the authority to clear an encampment only when it provides an open door, safekeeping for essentials, and real help getting housed.

[C2.2] PRO’s purported contradiction between predictable engagement and isolated, dangerous camps is backwards. [C2.3] Nor is the “genuinely available” standard as neat as PRO claims. There is no universal set of options that eliminates every individual objection, meaning this standard functionally gives a permanent veto over basic public space rules to the most hazardous encampment conditions.
[C2.4] Existing individual safety codes cannot fix cumulative risk from dozens of packed tents. The glib claim that cities can instantly trade sweep budgets for perfect, universal shelter ignores that trained clinicians, accessible facilities, and tailored support take months or years to build out, and waiting for perfect shelter to address those risks lets preventable deaths mount for no tangible gain.
