[C1.1] A wealth tax fails on its own terms: it raises little money, at enormous administrative cost, while damaging the economy it claims to fund. Europe ran this experiment for decades. A dozen-plus countries had annual wealth taxes in the 1990s; nearly all repealed them—not out of love for billionaires, but because the taxes leaked revenue, triggered capital flight, and cost a fortune to enforce. France famously abandoned its version after watching thousands of wealthy households relocate, taking their tax bases, businesses, and investment with them.
[C1.2] Consider the practical core. An annual net worth tax requires government to value everything a wealthy household owns, every year: private companies with no market price, art, farmland, stakes in startups worth either billions or nothing. Each valuation is a lawsuit waiting to happen, and the gap between paper value and reality creates a liquidity trap. A founder whose company is "worth" two billion on paper may have almost no cash.
[C1.3] Forcing annual sales of stakes in private firms to pay the tax bill destroys businesses, depresses asset prices, and hands ownership to whoever can absorb the fire sale—often foreign capital or large incumbents.
[C1.4] The deeper flaw is that the proposition picks the wrong instrument. Everything PRO wants—taxing buy-borrow-die strategies, taxing unrealized appreciation that escapes income tax—is better achieved inside the existing system: taxing capital gains at death rather than wiping them clean, treating large loans against portfolios as realization events, raising rates on capital income, strengthening estate taxation. These reforms hit the same loopholes without annual valuation circuses, and they hit gains once, accurately, instead of guessing every April.
[C1.5] A wealth tax is the policy equivalent of demolishing the house to fix a leaky faucet. The faucet is real. But the remedy on offer is administratively unworkable, historically discredited, and economically self-defeating—while superior tools sit untouched. That is the case to beat.
