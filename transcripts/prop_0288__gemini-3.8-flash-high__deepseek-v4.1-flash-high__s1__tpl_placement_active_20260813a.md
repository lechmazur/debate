[P1.1] When the state uses its coercive power to strip away someone’s livelihood, lawful status, or legal remedies, it must answer for that decision. Suspending food assistance, disability payments, or a visa is not an administrative inconvenience; it is a life-altering disruption. A family plunged into food insecurity or an immigrant facing sudden detention cannot survive on the promise of a bureaucratic appeal months down the line. Outsourcing that deprivation entirely to an algorithm without meaningful human review violates the core bargain of public administration: that deprivation of rights requires human judgment, accountability, and justification.
[P1.2] First, algorithmic risk models do not find facts; they generate statistical probabilities. They rely on historical data, flawed proxies, and blunt correlations that routinely misidentify innocent behavior as fraud. When agencies treat a high-risk score as self-executing justification for suspension, false positives cease to be mere statistical anomalies—they become immediate human catastrophes. We have seen this repeatedly when automated fraud systems wrongfully cut off thousands of blameless citizens, triggering evictions and debt traps, simply because no caseworker ever examined the underlying records before pulling the plug.
[P1.3] Second, automated suspension reverses the constitutional and moral burden of proof. It traps individuals in a Kafkaesque reality where they are declared fraudulent by an opaque black box. An impoverished recipient or visa holder rarely possesses the institutional power or technical expertise to disprove a proprietary statistical model while their lifelines are frozen.
[P1.4] Crucially, the motion does not forbid agencies from using AI to flag cases for review. It simply draws an indispensable line: an algorithm can prompt an inquiry, but it must never unilaterally trigger the punishment. Before the state cuts off survival resources or legal status, a human being must verify the facts, consider context, and take personal responsibility for the decision. Anything less reduces governance to unaccountable automated violence.
