[P1.1] We should ban the creation of novel synthetic viruses with clear pandemic potential because this research manufactures the disaster it pretends to prevent. A natural pandemic might emerge someday; a lab-made pandemic-capable virus exists certainly, today, in a freezer, in a database, in the hands of fallible people.
[P1.2] Leaks and thefts are not hypothetical. High-containment laboratories around the world have suffered infections, equipment failures, and escapes involving dangerous pathogens. Human error cannot be regulated away, and once a novel transmissible virus is engineered, its genetic blueprint can be copied, emailed, or stolen far more easily than any natural outbreak can be contained. Defensive intent at the moment of creation offers no control over the next lab, the next government, or the next disgruntled employee.
[P1.3] Nor does the payoff justify the gamble. Engineers cannot reliably predict how viruses will evolve in nature, so pre-made vaccines and threat lists seldom match the next real spillover. What actually saves lives in a pandemic — rapid sequencing, broad diagnostics, flexible vaccine platforms, stockpiled protective equipment, strengthened hospitals — does not require building a super-virus first. Safer tools like weakened strains, non-replicating pseudoviruses, and computer models can test the same defenses without creating a transmissible killer.
[P1.4] Publishing methods for enhanced transmissibility and immune escape teaches offense far more than it aids defense.
[P1.5] Only a global prohibition breaks the arms-race logic. If one country claims a defensive exception, every rival will claim the same while suspecting the worst. Like bans on bioweapons and nuclear testing, a clear line against creating pandemic-capable pathogens deters reckless ambition, stigmatizes cheating, and lets scientists collaborate openly on protection without handing every actor a recipe for catastrophe. We can study nature without improving it into a weapon.

[C1.1] The proposition asks us to ban the very science that tells us what a pandemic-capable virus actually is. That's the core problem: you cannot reliably prepare for, detect, or defend against a threat you have forbidden yourself from understanding.
[C1.2] Start with enforcement. A global ban on synthesizing novel pathogens is uniquely unverifiable. The equipment is dual-use to its core — the same DNA synthesizers, cell cultures, and sequencing machines that fill every legitimate virology lab can produce a novel virus. There's no reactor to inspect, no test-ban-style seismic signature.
[C1.3] A ban wouldn't eliminate this research; it would move it to states with the weakest oversight and the fewest scruples, while responsible nations blind themselves. The Biological Weapons Convention is instructive: it has no verification mechanism precisely because biology resists it. PRO promises that prohibition "breaks the arms-race logic," but unverifiable prohibitions feed arms-race logic — everyone assumes everyone else is cheating, because they might be.
[C1.4] Second, PRO's claim that safer substitutes suffice is empirically shaky. Pseudoviruses and non-replicating constructs cannot answer questions that only live, replicating, transmissible viruses can: how a pathogen spreads between hosts, how virulence evolves, whether a vaccine platform works against actual infection. The reconstruction of the 1918 influenza virus, for instance, taught us which genetic features made it historically lethal — knowledge that now informs surveillance of circulating avian strains. When H5N1 transmissibility research was published, it triggered real changes in how we monitor flu in birds.
[C1.5] That knowledge came from making the thing, not from modeling it.
[C1.6] Third, "clear pandemic potential" is a definitional mirage. Our understanding of what makes a virus pandemic-capable comes from exactly this research. Ban it, and the category itself becomes unknowable — enforced against nothing, or against everything, at the whim of whoever defines it.
[C1.7] Finally, nature is already running the experiment. Spillovers will keep happening whether we ban research or not. Unilaterally refusing to build defensive knowledge against engineered threats — while offenders face no effective barrier — is disarmament without inspection, safety without a safety net.
