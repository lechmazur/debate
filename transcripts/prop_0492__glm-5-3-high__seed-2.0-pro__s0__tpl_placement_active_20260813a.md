[P1.1] Automation is about to generate enormous gains for capital while displacing workers who never see a penny of them. An automation tax captures a slice of those gains at the moment they are realized and redirects them to the people bearing the costs. That is not punishment for progress — it is the logical financing mechanism for progress that currently sends all its winners to one side of the ledger.
[P1.2] Start with the asymmetry of displacement. When a warehouse replaces two hundred pickers with robots, the firm books higher margins, shareholders gain, and managers get bonuses. The displaced workers get unemployment, depleted savings, and communities get shrinking tax bases. Economists have long recognized that labor-market transitions generate costs the workers don't choose and can't price.
[P1.3] Every serious policy tradition — from trade adjustment programs to carbon pricing — accepts that when private actors capture a benefit while externalizing a cost onto others, a targeted levy is legitimate. Automation is exactly that case.
[P1.4] Second, the tax funds something that otherwise goes unfunded. Retraining works only when it is real: paid courses, income support during retraining, placement services, wage insurance for those who land lower-paying jobs. That costs money, and the natural source is the activity creating the need. A tax tied to automation-linked layoffs makes the adjustment self-financing instead of leaving displaced workers competing for thin general-revenue funding.
[P1.5] Third, the design problem is manageable. Any new tax raises definitional questions — the income tax did, the carbon tax did. You can base the levy on measurable proxies: reductions in payroll headcount alongside capital investment in robotics and automated systems, or reduced payroll-tax contributions per unit of output. Perfect precision is not required; a workable band is.
[P1.6] The alternative my opponent must defend is doing nothing targeted — letting firms automate at full private gain while the social bill lands on workers and public budgets. That status quo is not neutral. It is a subsidy, paid by the people least able to afford it.
