In customer-facing gig work, a rating is often less like feedback and more like a leash. [P1.3] If refusing that system means losing access, the platform effectively says: endure biased judgment or lose your rent. That is compelled exposure to harassment, not a free market.
[P1.4] The proposition’s threshold is important: “strongly shaped by abuse, bias, or harassment.” Not every negative rating, not every criticism. Platforms can still protect customers through completion rates, safety reports, fraud checks, direct complaints, and audits. [P1.5] Worker refusal removes one tainted metric from access decisions; it does not force platforms to retain unsafe workers.
[P1.6] The likely objection is that ratings build trust. But trust cannot rest on forcing workers to accept biased judgment. [C1.6] Most critically, the motion creates a two-tier workforce.
