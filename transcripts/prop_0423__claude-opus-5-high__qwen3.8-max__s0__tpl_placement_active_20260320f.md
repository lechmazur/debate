It is built by a city that closed streets, floated bonds, absorbed the noise, the traffic, and the rent pressure — and by members who paid dues in the lean years when nobody wanted a ticket. [P1.2] Three reasons this should be the default.

[C1.1] Reserving a meaningful share of tickets for locals or members sounds like fairness, but it is status rationing dressed as community. The proposition replaces an imperfect allocator—price—with a more arbitrary one: zip code, seniority, or the ability to prove membership. That matters because the people who most deserve access are not reliably the people living closest or holding the longest card; they are the fans who value the event most, wherever they are.
[C1.2] PRO’s local-investor story collapses on inspection. If residents have subsidized a venue through taxes or traffic, the remedy is direct compensation: public ticket lotteries, community rebates, or transit benefits. It is not handing a scarce seat to whichever nearby resident happens to win the reservation queue. A local who never watches the sport and may resell the ticket is not repaying civic contribution; he is receiving a windfall.
[C1.3] Members are no safer category. In many events, members are already the privileged core: season-ticket holders, donors, fan-club insiders. Guaranteeing them a meaningful share can crowd out newer, younger, or distant fans whose attachment is just as real.
[C1.4] The practical damage is worse. A mandated hold before dynamic sale creates deadweight: blocks that do not match actual local demand, verification bureaucracy, higher administration costs, and stronger incentives for fraudulent residency or membership. If the reserved share is large, it can push prices up for the remaining general tickets, because organizers still need to cover fixed costs. If it is small, it becomes a token gesture that does not deliver PRO’s justice but still distorts the sale.
[C1.5] And for touring shows, festivals, or one-off finals, “local” is often meaningless: the event exists because people travel to it.
[C1.6] Dynamic markets can be abused, but the answer is targeted repair—verified queues, purchase limits, anti-bot enforcement, transparent resale rules—not an entitlement layer for insiders. Vote CON.
