[C1.2] First, standing commitments freeze strategy in place while wars change shape. A legislature that pre-commits for the long term surrenders the single most valuable tool a democracy has: the ability to redirect resources as the battlefield and the adversary change.
[C1.3] Aid should be sustained and generous—conditioned, sized, and reviewed against outcomes, not guaranteed regardless of them.
[C1.4] Second, the motion concedes the point that matters. "Even if it reduces resources for other priorities" admits real scarcity. [C1.5] China is the pacing threat precisely because it can hold at risk the interests—trade routes, semiconductors, allies like Taiwan and Japan—that no European war outcome replaces. Burning readiness now to signal resolve is not strength; it is trading a certain cost for a speculative one.
[C1.6] Third, unconditional permanence destroys leverage and accountability. Kyiv makes better strategic and reform decisions when aid is tied to conditions. [C1.7] PRO will say wavering invites aggression. But credibility flows from capability and adaptability, not from rigidity. A strong, flexible, well-funded policy beats a promise that outruns its resources.
