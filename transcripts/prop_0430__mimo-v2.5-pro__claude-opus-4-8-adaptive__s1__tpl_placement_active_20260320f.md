[P1.2] My argument rests on a straightforward principle: schools should respond to the foreseeable harms that shape students' educational experience, regardless of where the triggering act technically occurred. [P1.3] Consider the mechanism. Private exclusionary chats function as social architecture. They sort students into in-groups and out-groups with ruthless efficiency, and those hierarchies walk through the school doors every morning. A student who discovers they are the only one not invited to a birthday party through a visible group chat experiences social rejection with a specificity and permanence that a vague rumor never achieves.
[P1.4] Screenshots circulate. The evidence of exclusion becomes durable, shareable, and inescapable. This is not gossip that fades by lunchtime.
[P1.5] Schools already regulate off-campus conduct when it substantially disrupts the educational environment. [P1.6] The status quo is failing students precisely because we treat a screen as a wall. It isn't one. The harm crosses that wall every single school day, and our response should cross it too.
