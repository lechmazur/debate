[P1.1] The invasion of Iraq did not just remove one dictator—it dismantled the central pillar holding up a regional balance of power, and everything that followed traces back to that decision.
[P1.2] Start with the mechanism. Saddam Hussein's regime, for all its brutality, functioned as a counterweight to Iran. The Iran-Iraq war, the decade of containment, the entire architecture of Gulf security in the 1980s and 90s rested on Iraq acting as a Sunni Arab buffer against Persian Shia expansion. When the US toppled that regime and then dissolved the Iraqi army and de-Baathified the state apparatus, it did not simply remove a tyrant—it created a power vacuum in the geographic heart of the Middle East, bordering Iran, Syria, Saudi Arabia, and Turkey simultaneously.
[P1.3] Trace what filled that vacuum. Iran's influence expanded dramatically into Iraq through Shia militias and political parties that Tehran had cultivated for decades, giving Iran a land corridor of influence stretching toward Syria and Lebanon—the "Shia crescent" that Sunni Gulf states still describe as their central security nightmare.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, the disbanded Iraqi officer corps and alienated Sunni population became the recruiting pool for al-Qaeda in Iraq, which later rebranded as ISIS and seized a third of Iraqi and Syrian territory by 2014. That is not a coincidence of history—that is a direct causal chain from occupation policy to insurgency to caliphate.
[P1.5] Consider the counterfactual test directly: a contained Saddam, still boxed in by no-fly zones and sanctions, was a known quantity that regional actors had built two decades of stable deterrence around. Whatever his regime's cruelties, it did not export sectarian civil war into neighboring states, did not hand Tehran a client government in Baghdad, and did not produce a stateless jihadist army that later fought in Syria, Libya, and beyond.
[P1.6] The invasion did not liberate the region into stability—it broke the mechanism that had been holding instability in check, and the ISIS war, Iran's regional entrenchment, and Syria's spillover civil war are the bill that came due.
