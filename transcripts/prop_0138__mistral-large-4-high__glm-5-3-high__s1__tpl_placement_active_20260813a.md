[P1.1] A donor-conceived person grows up with a permanent gap where their genetic history should be. A donor-conceived adult facing cancer treatment or heart disease needs to know whether hereditary risks run in their genetic family. [P1.3] These are not privileges to be weighed against supply curves; they are basic elements of self-knowledge and physical safety that non-donor-conceived people take for granted from birth.

[C1.2] First, the cost is real, not hypothetical. My opponent's own framing concedes it: the right exists "even if anonymity would preserve more donors." So we are being asked to knowingly shrink the pool of donors. Who pays?
[C1.3] The next generation of infertile couples and, crucially, the donor-conceived people who will never exist because their prospective parents could not find donors. You cannot vindicate the identity interests of one cohort by subtracting an entire future cohort from existence. A policy whose beneficiaries are born only if the policy was not in place needs extraordinary justification.
[C1.4] Second, the "permanent gap" is not best filled by legal identity disclosure. Medical history, the strongest interest cited, travels through non-identifying medical records that clinics can obtain and update without exposing a donor's name, address, and family. The move from "health information" to "identifying information" is a leap driven by curiosity and emotional narrative, not necessity—and the law should not mandate satisfaction of curiosity at the point of a subpoena.
[C1.5] Third, the donor's side of the ledger is not trivial. Many donors were young men and women who made a private decision decades ago, sometimes before their own spouses and children knew. Retroactive unmasking breaks settled expectations, exposes families to disruption, and invites the very harassment that privacy law exists to prevent. We do not void promises lightly just because one party to them cannot speak at signing.
[C1.6] Finally, the UK and Australia comparison proves less than claimed. Donation systems adjusted partly through compensation and recruitment changes—costly workarounds acknowledging the loss, not evidence that no loss occurred.
[C1.7] The law should improve information flow without detonating the arrangements that make donation possible.
