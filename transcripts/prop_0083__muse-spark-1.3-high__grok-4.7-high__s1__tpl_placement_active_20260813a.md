[P1.1] Dating is not browsing pictures; it is deciding who to trust with your time, body, and safety. Photos and early messages are the entire basis for that decision. When they are AI-generated, the user is not meeting a person but evaluating a fiction.
[P1.2] A profile photo carries an implicit promise: this is roughly how I look now. An AI-generated or heavily AI-resynthesized photo breaks that promise. It can shave years, reshape a face, add hair or muscle that does not exist. The harm is not vanity.
[P1.3] Someone agrees to a coffee, a walk to a car, a kiss, based on recognition and attraction that were manufactured. Showing up unrecognizable is not a bad angle; it is bait.
[P1.4] AI-written conversation is more intimate deception. On dating apps people do not fall for profiles, they fall for banter, attentiveness, emotional fluency. If the witty opener, the thoughtful follow-up, the perfectly calibrated flirty empathy were generated by a model, the other user is misled about exactly the traits that predict compatibility: humor, effort, social intelligence. A typo fix is not the issue.
[P1.5] When AI drafts the thoughts themselves, it counterfeits character.
[P1.6] None of this requires a ban, only honesty. A clear label — AI photo, AI-assisted chat — costs the sender almost nothing and restores the choice that makes romance legitimate: to pursue this real person as they are. Shy users can still get help; they just cannot pass its output off as their own charm. Without disclosure we create a race to the bottom where honest, awkward humans lose to polished avatars and prompt engineers.
[P1.7] Dating platforms profit by selling trust to strangers. If they let users be wooed by faces that never existed and words never thought, they are not neutral hosts. They are running a deception market.

[C1.7] Friends have rewritten bios for decades without a disclosure duty. The motion indicts the software, not the lie.
[C1.8] Detectors mislabel human writing and miss careful synthetic text.
