[P1.1] The American transmission grid is not aging gracefully—it is strangling the energy transition. Thousands of gigawatts of clean generation projects sit in interconnection queues across the country, unable to reach customers because there is no adequate wire to carry the power. [P1.2] And within permitting, the decisive friction is local veto power over siting.
[P1.3] This is not a minor procedural inconvenience. [P1.4] The costs of delay are enormous and rising. Every year that high-capacity transmission remains unbuilt means more fossil generation running, higher wholesale prices in constrained regions, and clean energy investments stranded or abandoned. [P1.5] They are prices paid by households and businesses across entire regions so that a narrow local process can run its course.
[P1.6] We already accept this logic for other backbone infrastructure. Interstate highways, natural gas pipelines, and fiber backbones do not grant every municipality along the route a veto. [P1.7] Permitting reform that prioritizes faster buildout, even at the cost of reducing local veto power, restores a proportionate balance. The status quo is not working for anyone except those wielding the veto. It is time to weigh the millions who need the grid against the few who can block it.
