[P1.1] Boys are falling behind at a scale that already warps educational outcomes and life chances. Across grades, females earn higher marks in nearly every subject, dominate advanced courses and graduation rates, and now form majorities on college campuses. Reading and writing gaps open early and widen; boys fill special-education rolls, suspension lists, and dropout statistics far out of proportion to their numbers. [P1.2] This is structural, not residual. Schools reward early verbal fluency, sustained quiet attention, and organizational compliance—developmental edges that favor girls. Curricula that cut recess, demand long sedentary blocks, and rely on a heavily female teaching force leave many boys bored, restless, and labeled disruptive by middle school. The mismatch compounds: weaker literacy feeds weaker engagement; weaker engagement feeds weaker credentials.
[P1.3] What begins as a classroom style problem ends as a pipeline of fewer male skilled workers, higher male disconnection, and delayed family formation.
[P1.4] Policy already treats female STEM gaps and racial achievement gaps as major structural priorities. The male lag is at least as large, more consistent across subjects, and more predictive of long-term non-employment. Treating it as marginal guarantees continued under-investment: no systemic push for male teachers, boy-engaging pedagogy, early reading rescue, or routine gender-disaggregated accountability. Either education policy names male underperformance a core structural problem and retools accordingly, or it accepts a permanent second-class academic trajectory for half the student population.
