[P1.1] Undersea cables carry well over ninety percent of global data, including military communications, financial transactions, and power-grid signaling. They are the central nervous system of modern states, yet they remain absurdly vulnerable: concentrated in a handful of narrow chokepoints, lying unmarked on the seabed, and defended largely by private consortia rather than national forces. A few simultaneous cuts or taps could blind a country more thoroughly than a conventional missile barrage, freezing markets, degrading command-and-control, and severing allied coordination within hours.
[P1.2] The proposition is not to abandon visible defense but to recognize that deterrence is hollow if an adversary can collapse your economy and situational awareness without ever crossing a border. Tanks and jets project power, yet power projection requires functioning networks. A squadron of advanced fighters costs billions and deters a narrow class of symmetrical threats; mapping seabed routes, patrolling chokepoints, and funding redundant landing stations costs a fraction of that and secures the substrate beneath every other domain—cyber, economic, kinetic.
[P1.3] Opponents will call this a misallocation, defending a hierarchy of threats drawn from the last century. In reality, the risk is already manifest. Accidental anchor strikes sever links every year, while suspicious naval activity near coastal landing stations shows that hostile interference is a matter of capability, not imagination. The economic damage from even a days-long transoceanic outage would instantly dwarf the price of preventive investment.
[P1.4] Investing in cable protection and redundancy is therefore the most efficient reallocation in defense. Surveillance, rapid-repair fleets, and geographically diverse alternate routes transform a brittle monoculture into a resilient mesh. This does not replace visible force; it makes every other instrument of national power usable. We should shift resources now, before a single quiet morning on the ocean floor proves how catastrophically we have miscalculated.
