[C1.1] The Amsterdam rider example is designed to make you feel the injustice, not think about the mechanism. So look at the mechanism: what actually happens when you flip the default to "employee" for millions of workers at once?
[C1.2] California tried exactly this with AB5 in 2019. The result was not dignified employment for grateful workers—it was freelance journalists losing publications that could no longer justify the compliance risk of using them, translators and musicians cut loose overnight, and Uber and Lyft threatening to leave the state entirely. Voters then passed Prop 22 to carve gig platforms back out, because the blunt default did more harm than the status quo. Spain's rider law pushed Deliveroo out of the Spanish market altogether—workers didn't get upgraded contracts, they got zero contracts.
[C1.3] That is the structural problem with "reclassify by default, exempt the genuine freelancers." Platforms don't respond to a reclassification mandate by keeping the same workforce and just paying more. They respond by shrinking the workforce, tightening acceptance algorithms even further to control labor costs under employment law, or exiting markets. The workers with the least leverage—part-timers, students, second-job holders, migrants without other options—are the first cut, precisely because they're the ones platforms can most easily replace with a smaller core of full-time "employees."
[C1.4] The exemption for "genuine freelancers" doesn't fix this; it's the whole problem in miniature. Every control-based test—schedule autonomy, multiple clients, rate-setting—is manipulable and litigable. Regulators end up running years of case-by-case tribunals, as the UK has with Uber, while millions of workers sit in legal limbo not knowing which category they're in. That uncertainty itself kills flexible work, because platforms won't build products around a classification they can't predict.
[C1.5] And surveys of actual gig workers consistently show most choose the arrangement for the flexibility—not because they're tricked, but because it fits lives that a 9-to-5 employment relationship cannot. Overriding that preference by default is not correcting exploitation. It's imposing a different exploitation, dressed as protection.
