[P1.2] Matching that subsidy scale for regenerative practices like cover cropping, no-till planting, rotational grazing, and diverse rotations is essential precisely because long-term data is still maturing. When states heavily subsidize conventional inputs alone, farmers cannot afford to switch.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, breeders optimize seeds for synthetic nitrogen, not living soils. Equal-scale public support bridges that transition gap and unlocks the agronomic innovation needed to raise regenerative yields in the first place.
[P1.4] Delaying action also misreads the true asymmetry of risk. Judging farming solely by peak single-season yield in ideal weather is reckless in an era of climate extremes. Carbon-rich regenerative soils act like a sponge, absorbing floodwaters and holding moisture through severe droughts that devastate brittle monocultures, while insulating farmers from global fertilizer price spikes and curbing costly disaster bailouts and water cleanup.
[P1.5] Crucially, matching subsidies creates voluntary financial parity rather than an overnight ban on synthetic inputs, scaling adoption safely without sudden food shortages.
