[P1.1] Safe-storage laws are justified because they target the precise moment youth firearm harm happens: easy access to a gun that is not under an adult’s control. They do not ban firearms, confiscate them, or disarm responsible owners. They require that when a gun is not being carried or supervised, it be locked, disabled, or stored separately from ammunition. The test is comparative: large protection for young lives versus small burden on lawful ownership—and safe storage delivers exactly that imbalance.
[P1.2] Youth firearm deaths are usually access events. Two scenarios dominate: a young child finds an unsecured, loaded gun and shoots himself, a sibling, or a friend; and an adolescent in a suicidal crisis reaches for a household firearm because it is instantly lethal and instantly available. Both hinge on seconds. Suicidal urges often pass.
[P1.3] A locked box, a trigger lock, or stored ammunition adds friction and time: the difference between a permanent death and a survivable delay. A curious child can pull a trigger; he cannot open a combination safe. Safe storage attacks the mechanism directly.
[P1.4] The burden on lawful owners is ordinary and modest. A lockbox or cable lock costs far less than the firearms it protects; it is no more intrusive than locking up opioids, alcohol, or a backyard pool. Many responsible owners already store safely, so the law mostly changes the negligent minority whose unsecured guns become neighborhood hazards. Home-defense worries are manageable: quick-access safes and immediate-control exemptions preserve armed self-defense while denying unsupervised access to minors.
[P1.5] Weigh the trade asymmetry. Compliance is a small, reversible inconvenience. A youth firearm death or catastrophic injury is final. Even partial reductions in access produce fewer preventable funerals, fewer paralyzed children, and fewer grieving families.
[P1.6] The benefit is measured in lives and futures that cannot be recovered; the burden is routine and adjustable. Safe-storage laws reduce youth firearm deaths and injuries more than they burden lawful gun ownership.
