[P1.2] The mechanism is straightforward. In weight-class sports — combat sports, lightweight rowing, horse racing — making weight is a limiter. A drug that suppresses appetite and accelerates fat loss lets an athlete hold a lighter frame with a smaller performance tax.
[P1.3] That is not just a health intervention; it shifts the cost-benefit curve of competing in a lighter category. In endurance sports, climbing speed and sustained power depend heavily on power-to-weight. Shedding fat mass without shedding muscle raises that ratio directly. [P1.4] Now ask what scrutiny these drugs currently face in sport. Mostly none. They are not universally prohibited, there is no meaningful testing regime, and off-label use for body composition rather than diabetes flies entirely under the radar. [P1.5] Compare that to the apparatus we build for a cold-medicine stimulant.
[P1.6] "Stricter scrutiny" here is a moderate ask: systematic monitoring of use, mandatory disclosure and therapeutic-use-style review when prescribed, research into prevalence and performance effect, and authority for sport bodies to restrict in disciplines where weight manipulation is intrinsic to the format.
