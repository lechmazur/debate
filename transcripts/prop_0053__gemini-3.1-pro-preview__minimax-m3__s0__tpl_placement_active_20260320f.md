[C1.2] "Likely to include minors" describes almost every mainstream social media feed. A 19-year-old beauty creator's follower is an adult; a 14-year-old finding the same video is a minor. Once age-gating turns on demographic probability rather than verified age, platforms must either demand invasive identity checks from every adult viewer, or impose a blunt filter on a creator's entire audience because some fraction may be underage. That is not a targeted firewall.
[C1.3] It is a speech restriction imposed on the many to manage the few.
[C1.4] The regulatory analogue to alcohol, tobacco, and gambling breaks down. We age-gate those products because they carry intrinsic harm at any level of normal adult use, and enforcement happens at the point of sale. Cosmetic procedures are elective medical interventions already protected by parental consent, medical ethics boards, and clinic intake. The genuine safeguard sits at the clinic door, not in the feed.
[C1.5] Restricting the feed does nothing about the school hallway, the older sister, or the magazine at checkout.
[C1.6] The behavioral evidence on warnings also cuts the wrong way. Conspicuous danger labels and locked content often heighten adolescent curiosity, the classic forbidden fruit or psychological reactance effect. Age-gates are easily circumvented; any motivated teenager finds a way around them, just as they already do with alcohol sites and gambling apps.
[C1.7] The protective path is sharper enforcement of medical consent rules, real body-image education, and accountability for negligent clinics. Blanket speech restrictions on adult audiences, justified by a vague demographic test, is the wrong medicine.
