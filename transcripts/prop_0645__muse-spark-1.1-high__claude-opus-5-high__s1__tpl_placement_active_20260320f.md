[P1.1] When a 14 year old stole from a store in 1985, the file gathered dust in a clerk's basement. Today that same kid is tagged forever - her name indexed, mugshot scraped by data brokers, docket appearing on the first page of a background check at 22. We kept the law's language about second chances while technology quietly abolished them.
[P1.2] Presumptively sealing digital records at adulthood restores the juvenile system's foundational bargain: children are different. Their culpability is diminished, their judgment is immature, their capacity for change is greatest. That is why juvenile courts were created as confidential, rehabilitative, not punitive. Sealing does not destroy records or blind police and judges.
[P1.3] It closes the public web portal. Police, prosecutors, and courts retain access for sentencing and investigation. For the rare case where public access truly matters - serious violent felonies or ongoing safety risk - a prosecutor can move to keep it open. The default is forgiveness; the exception must be justified.
[P1.4] The harm of open digital records is not abstract. Landlords, employers, colleges, and cheap automated background apps run a search in seconds. A juvenile adjudication for shoplifting, vandalism, or a schoolyard fight becomes a lifetime credential of criminality. That dooms the exact anchors that prevent reoffending: a job, housing, education.
[P1.5] We manufacture recidivism by branding kids for life, then act surprised when they cannot leave the system. Current opt-in sealing fails because the kids who need it most don't have lawyers and never file the petition.
[P1.6] This does not hurt public safety, it serves it. The public was never entitled to real-time tracking of children's mistakes. The old system relied on practical obscurity for good reason. Court transparency can be satisfied with anonymized data and aggregate reporting without permanently doxxing a 15-year-old.
[P1.7] If adulthood means anything, it must mean a chance to start adulthood without a search engine that already decided who you are. Seal by default.
