[C1.1] A guarantee is a promise, not a doctor. That distinction decides this debate. Canada guarantees universal primary care by law, yet millions of Canadians have no family physician and wait months for one; Britain guarantees GP access and delivers 8 a.m. phone lotteries for same-day slots. The motion asks governments to sign a universal IOU for a service whose scarce input — clinicians' time — no legislature can conjure.
[C1.2] When demand is made infinite at price zero and supply stays fixed, you don't get access; you get rationing by queue, which punishes exactly the working poor who can't take a Tuesday morning off to wait.
[C1.3] Second, the motion's "even if" clause is its fatal design flaw, not its strength. Keep elective and supplementary services in private markets and you build a permanent pay differential next door to the public tier. Physicians and nurses migrate to dermatology suites and private clinics where fees float freely, while the guaranteed tier — the one with capped budgets — bleeds staff. The structure the motion mandates actively hollows out the very floor it promises.
[C1.4] And politically, once the affluent get their real care privately, the public tier becomes a poor person's service, and poor people's services get poor funding. That's the observed trajectory, not a hypothetical.
[C1.5] Third, universality is regressive waste. The diabetic who skips a sixty-dollar visit needs targeted, means-tested coverage — a solvable, bounded problem. A universal guarantee instead spends scarce fiscal capacity subsidizing checkups for lawyers and executives who were paying anyway, while displacing cheaper innovations already expanding access: retail clinics, direct primary care subscriptions, telemedicine at prices below a phone bill. Government monopsony freezes primary care into whatever model the ministry codifies.
[C1.6] Judge this on care actually delivered to the vulnerable, not entitlements printed in statute books. Targeted subsidy plus competitive supply delivers more visits, sooner, than a universal promise the system cannot keep.
