[P1.1] Start with what the algorithm is actually told to do. An engagement-optimized feed has one objective: keep the viewer watching. It is not asked whether the viewer feels better afterward, only whether they stayed. For an adolescent brain, the content that most reliably keeps someone watching is content that provokes arousal: outrage, envy, fear, and the sting of social comparison.
[P1.2] The system does not need to intend harm. It only needs to notice that the girl who lingered half a second longer on a thin-body video will linger again, and serve her a hundred more.
[P1.3] That is the decisive difference from a chronological feed. A chronological feed is bounded by who you chose to follow and when they posted. It cannot learn your weaknesses. It cannot discover that a fourteen-year-old watches sad content at midnight and then build him a midnight of sadness.
[P1.4] Engagement optimization does exactly that, and the pattern is well documented: researchers and regulators who created test accounts signaling low mood or body concerns have repeatedly found the feed narrowing toward depressive, self-harm, or extreme-dieting content within a short span of use. Leaked internal research from a major platform acknowledged, in roughly these terms, that its product made body-image issues worse for a substantial share of teen girls.
[P1.5] Three mechanisms connect this design to anxiety and depression. First, amplified social comparison, among the most consistent predictors of adolescent depressive symptoms. Second, the variable-reward loop of endless personalized novelty, which drives compulsive use, displaces sleep, and sleep loss is itself a direct pathway to both conditions.
[P1.6] Third, rabbit-holing, where a passing low mood is reflected back and intensified rather than allowed to pass.
[P1.7] The motion is comparative, and the comparison is lopsided. Replace the recommender with a timeline and every one of those mechanisms weakens, because the feed stops studying the child. That is why personalization, not screens, is the variable that matters.
