[P1.1] Obesity is not a knowledge problem. People know soda isn't health food. Yet we built a food environment where soda is cheaper than water, where ultra-processed calories are available everywhere, 24 hours a day, where the supermarket checkout, the gas station, the hospital vending machine all push the same high-sugar, high-fat products. If the environment is engineered for overconsumption, policy must engineer it back.
[P1.2] Decades of individual behavior campaigns - eat less, move more, count calories, make better choices - have been tried and have failed to bend the curve. They assume people act in a vacuum. They don't. Willpower is a weak tool against price, convenience, cravings formulated in labs, and billions of dollars of targeted marketing.
[P1.3] Those campaigns also backfire: they stigmatize, they create shame, and they are inevitably followed least by those with the least time, money and health literacy to act on them.
[P1.4] Environmental and marketing restrictions target the cause. Make the default healthier: tax sugary drinks, restrict junk food advertising to children, set nutrition standards for schools, limit prime placement and supersized portions. We know this playbook works because we used it for tobacco: smoke-free spaces, ad bans, and taxes did what "just say no" posters never could. Cities that taxed sugary drinks saw purchases drop; countries that restricted child advertising saw exposure fall, without requiring anyone to attend a workshop.
[P1.5] This is also the equitable and efficient approach. Behavior campaigns reward the privileged who can afford time to cook, safe places to exercise, and gym memberships. Environmental rules help everyone without demanding individual bandwidth, especially low-income neighborhoods flooded with fast food outlets and targeted billboards.
[P1.6] And it preserves real choice. There is no free choice when children are profiled online and sold addiction before they can read.
[P1.7] We should stop spending most of the budget telling individuals to swim upstream and start turning down the current.
