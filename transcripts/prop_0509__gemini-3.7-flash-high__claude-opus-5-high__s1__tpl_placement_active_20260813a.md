[P1.1] Central banking is not a moral crusade to punish demand; it is a mandate to maximize sustainable economic welfare. When an economy faces elevated inflation—frequently sparked by supply shocks, energy transitions, or structural realignments—central banks face a critical choice: tolerate a gradual, orderly disinflation, or aggressively crush demand to engineer an immediate return to two percent. Choosing an engineered recession is an act of economic self-harm where the cure is vastly worse than the disease.
[P1.2] First, engineered recessions inflict permanent scars through economic hysteresis. Deliberately suffocating growth forces viable businesses into bankruptcy, halts capital investment, and drives workers into prolonged unemployment. This erodes skills, permanently shrinks productive capacity, and lowers potential GDP for years to come. In contrast, running the economy slightly warm while inflation gradually recedes preserves the labor market and protects household balance sheets from catastrophic collapse.
[P1.3] Second, blunt demand destruction worsens the underlying supply deficiencies that often drive inflation. Crushing aggregate demand does not build new power grids, manufacture semiconductors, or expand housing stock. Instead, punitive interest rates dramatically raise the cost of capital, deterring the very private investments needed to expand capacity and resolve price pressures at their root.
[P1.4] Third, the two percent target is a policy heuristic, not a sacred constant. Sacrificing millions of livelihoods simply to reach a precise number a few quarters sooner represents a destructive dogmatism. As long as long-term inflation expectations remain broadly anchored, central banks retain credibility by demonstrating flexibility and protecting the real economy, not by proving their willingness to inflict pain.
[P1.5] Tolerating inflation above target for longer allows the economy to adjust structurally without burning down the house to cool the room. A patient, balanced glide path protects productive capacity and delivers far superior human and economic outcomes.
