[P1.6] A cap on markups or a required face-value exchange does not ban resale. If you get sick or have to work, you can still list your ticket and get your money back plus a reasonable service fee. [C1.1] Price controls have a well-documented failure mode, and resale caps are a price control. When you legally force a market price down below what buyers are actually willing to pay, you do not make the extra demand disappear. You just decide who captures it. Cap resale at face value and the difference between what a ticket is "worth" on paper and what a desperate fan will actually pay does not vanish — it moves into whoever can get the ticket first: bots, insiders, box-office friends, and black-market sellers operating off any regulated platform entirely.
[C1.2] You have not closed the gouging gap. You have just moved it somewhere with zero verification, zero refund protection, and zero fraud recourse. Ask any fan who bought a fake ticket off a random resale site how that "fair lane" worked out.
[C1.3] That is the enforcement problem PRO never solves. A "required face-value channel" only works if you can force every transaction onto it. You cannot. The moment a platform caps markup, the transaction simply migrates to Craigslist, Discord, Instagram DMs, or a friend's cousin's Venmo.
[C1.4] Regulating one company's terms of service does not regulate human behavior; it just chases legitimate, traceable transactions into untraceable ones, stripping away the buyer protections platforms currently provide — identity verification, refund guarantees, chargeback rights — precisely because those cost money that capped resale fees can no longer fund.
[C1.5] Notice too that platforms are already solving parts of this voluntarily — verified-fan presales, paperless and non-transferable tickets, dynamic pricing that captures value at the primary sale instead of leaving it on the table for scalpers. That is the actual lever: price discovery at the artist's own sale, not a government-mandated ceiling on a secondary market. PRO's real complaint is that tickets are underpriced at face value. The fix for underpricing is better primary pricing, not a blanket resale mandate that recreates the same shortage one step downstream and hands the surplus to whoever games the rules best.
