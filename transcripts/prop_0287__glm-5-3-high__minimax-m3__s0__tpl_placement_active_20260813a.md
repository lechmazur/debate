The PRO side defines independence narrowly, as protection from firing or salary cuts, and concludes fixed terms are harmless. But independence was never just job security. It is the structural condition that lets a court say no to the nation when the nation is wrong, when public passion runs hot, when legislatures overreach.
[C1.2] That condition depends on judicial time being decoupled from political time. Fixed terms re-couple them.
[C1.3] Consider the lame-duck effect. A justice in year sixteen knows precisely when her voice will fall silent. She knows which president will pick her successor, which party will hold the Senate, and roughly what the country will look like at the moment of replacement. Life tenure immunizes judges from that horizon.
[C1.4] Fixed terms import it. The incentive to shape legacy, to defer to popular sentiment near the end, to avoid the cases that will define her final chapter - these are not theoretical. They are observable in every lame-duck official in every democracy. Why would judges be different?
[C1.5] The PRO side calls current vacancies a "lottery" and proposes a guaranteed appointment calendar as the cure. That is the opposite of a cure. Predictability does not reduce political combat; it schedules it. The US Senate confirmation process has intensified even as the underlying appointment power remained constant.
[C1.6] Adding regular, guaranteed slots means more regular, guaranteed fights. The German model is offered as proof, but Germany's Constitutional Court operates inside a parliamentary system with proportional representation and coalition discipline - structural conditions many democracies simply lack.
[C1.7] Finally, the deepest problem: constitutional courts are legitimate precisely because they are counter-majoritarian. Their authority to strike down legislation rests on being outside ordinary politics. Fixed terms move them inside ordinary politics. The legitimacy gained is the legitimacy of a different, lesser institution - one that polls, that thinks in election cycles, that schedules its conscience to a calendar.
[C1.8] That is not an upgrade. It is a constitutional demotion dressed up as reform.
