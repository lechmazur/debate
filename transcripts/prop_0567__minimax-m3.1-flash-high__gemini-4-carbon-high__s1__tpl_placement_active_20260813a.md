Unlike appellate arguments where lawyers debate abstract law, trials depend on ordinary, reluctant people—bystanders, victims, jurors, and unconvicted defendants—whose participation fractures when a lens turns on.
[C1.2] Cameras do not passively capture courtroom atmosphere [P1.3]; they alter it. Most lay witnesses are already anxious about testifying. Knowing that a nervous stumble, tearful breakdown, or hostile cross-examination can live forever online deters witnesses from coming forward and makes those who appear more guarded or performative.
[C1.3] Meanwhile, attorneys play to external audiences, and judges ruling on unpopular motions face immediate viral backlash. When cameras chill testimony and reward theatrics, courts get worse evidence and less reliable verdicts.

Worse, civil and criminal trials expose unproven allegations, medical details, financial disputes, and personal humiliations before any verdict is reached.
