[P1.1] Consider a physics lecture in which the teacher draws two diagrams and explains why only one is right. A student who looks down to finish sketching the first can miss the sentence that distinguishes them. Friday’s test is supposed to measure whether she understands forces, not whether she can draw and listen at the same speed.
[P1.2] Captions let a student catch an explanation she cannot hear clearly; a recording lets her replay it alongside the diagram; a handout with a text description lets a screen reader convey what the arrows mean. These are ways into the same intellectual task, not substitutes for doing it.
[P1.3] Why make them the default? Because the teacher knows what the class is meant to learn but cannot know who missed what, who is waiting for a diagnosis, or who would rather fall behind than disclose a disability. Under a request system, silence looks exactly like success. And some losses cannot be repaired on request: nobody can go back on Thursday and record Monday’s lecture.
[P1.4] Schools should supply the tools, staff time and accessible templates rather than leave each teacher to improvise. Providing a recording to enrolled students need not mean publishing it, keeping it forever or pointing a camera at students. If a discussion depends on students speaking candidly about their families, the teacher has a strong pedagogical reason to stop recording when the discussion starts. That is not a reason to withhold the preceding explanation or hand out an unreadable scan.
[P1.5] The presumption puts the question to the person equipped to answer it: would providing this material in another form defeat something I am trying to teach? If not, no student should have to make a personal disclosure to get the lesson intact.
