[P1.1] Cities face a loneliness epidemic, but the answer is not a slick download—it is bricks, benches, and bookshelves. Low‑cost social infrastructure—libraries, recreation centers, late‑hour plazas—delivers the kind of contact that apps can only simulate. Those encounters are spontaneous, unmediated, and they reach people who never open a phone app: the elderly, the low‑income, the undocumented.
[P1.2] App‑based loneliness tools are cheap to launch, but cheap precisely because they stay on the screen. They rely on self‑selection, data plans, and treat isolation as a problem solvable with a notification. The result is a narrow, temporary fix that often deepens the digital divide. [P1.4] Investing in physical infrastructure also builds community resilience. [P1.5] Therefore the proposition is clear: allocate the bulk of new spending to low‑cost social infrastructure, not to app‑based loneliness interventions. The former creates inclusive, durable connection; the latter offers a digital band‑aid that never reaches the people who need it most.
