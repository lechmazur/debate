[P1.1] Teenagers rarely open an app intending to lose two hours of sleep to an endless stream of strangers' videos. They log on to message a friend or check an update, and an algorithm engineered for maximum retention hijacks that intent. During adolescence, the brain's reward systems mature much faster than the prefrontal cortex responsible for impulse control. Major platforms exploit this exact biological window.
[P1.2] Using infinite scroll, autoplay, and variable-ratio reward schedules—the same psychological mechanics that make slot machines compelling—recommendation algorithms serve unpredictable hits of novelty to keep developing brains hooked.
[P1.3] Defaulting minors into less addictive feeds corrects this asymmetry without resorting to censorship. A safer default means chronological feeds from accounts a teen actually chooses to follow, deliberate discovery rather than passive algorithmic bombardment, and natural stopping cues. No topic is banned, and no community is walled off. The platform simply stops deploying coercive retention architecture as the baseline experience.
[P1.4] Defaults matter because human behavior follows the path of least resistance; we should not require every parent or fourteen-year-old to manually dismantle a billion-dollar behavioral engineering trap just to participate in modern social life.
[P1.5] Crucially, a drop in engagement is a feature, not a flaw. When screen time falls simply because an algorithm stops force-feeding unrequested videos at midnight, that lost metric represents reclaimed sleep, focus, and autonomy. Corporate engagement numbers measure captured attention, not human flourishing. Across the economy, we recognize that products used by minors require safe defaults even when safety trims profit margins.
[P1.6] Tech giants have no right to treat adolescent impulse control as a resource to be strip-mined for ad inventory. When a teenager puts their phone down because the artificial dopamine loop was switched off, the platform records a dip in engagement—and the teenager gets their night back.
