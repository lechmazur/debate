[P1.1] India’s labor market currently offers the worst bargain: security on paper for a minority of protected insiders, and insecurity in practice for the vast informal workforce. When dismissal, restructuring, and compliance are unpredictable or costly, firms respond rationally: stay small, outsource permanently, automate early, or hire off the books. The law then protects a job category rather than the worker. India should replace that bargain with simple, predictable employment contracts and protection that follows every person.
[P1.2] Flexibility should mean firms can use transparent fixed-term contracts, adjust staffing as demand changes, and make redundancies under clear notice and severance rules. It does not mean abolishing minimum wages, workplace safety, anti-discrimination, collective bargaining, or penalties for wage theft. Lowering the risk of a formal hire makes employers more willing to create one—especially young firms and labor-intensive manufacturers competing with imports and other production locations. A growing textile exporter should not fear that one uncertain order creates a permanent liability; workers are better served by ten lawful hires than by ten disguised contractors.
[P1.3] In exchange, universal social protection must make losing a particular job survivable: portable health coverage, pensions and injury insurance; temporary income support; maternity protection; and accessible retraining and job-matching. Coverage should attach to the individual, not the employer or contract label, with financing spread across employers, workers where feasible, and general revenue. That closes the loophole through which casualization currently strips workers of benefits.
[P1.4] This is also more pro-worker than rigid contracts. Real bargaining power is the ability to leave a bad employer without losing healthcare, accumulated benefits, or household survival. Portable protection expands workers’ choices, while clear severance and enforceable notice constrain arbitrary dismissal. It also lets labor move from declining sectors to expanding ones without turning economic change into household catastrophe.
[P1.5] The decisive choice is not rigidity versus security; it is brittle insider protection versus dynamic job creation backed by genuine universal security.
