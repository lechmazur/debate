[P1.2] A right to disconnect fixes a market failure, not a bad habit. No individual employee can bargain against the always-on culture alone: the first person to ignore the midnight email looks uncommitted while everyone else replies. We did not leave the weekend, child labor, or the eight-hour day to individual negotiation.
[P1.3] The boundary between work and life deserves the same treatment — updated for the smartphone era.
[P1.4] The costs of the status quo are concrete. After-hours messaging is unpaid overtime laundered as responsiveness. [P1.5] Constant interruption corrodes the deep work that actually creates value.

[P1.7] This is a default rule with flexibility, not a straitjacket.

[P1.9] The criterion for this debate is simple: does the current arrangement respect the basic bargain of employment — that workers sell their hours, not their lives? It does not.
