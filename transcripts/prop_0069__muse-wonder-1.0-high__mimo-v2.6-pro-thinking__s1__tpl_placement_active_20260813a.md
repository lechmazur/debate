[P1.1] Imagine losing an apartment not because of your rent history, but because you were a mediocre food-delivery driver years ago. Or losing a job interview because a platform flagged you as “low trust” after a disputed refund, an arrest that never led to conviction, or a landlord review you never saw. Portable algorithmic reputation creates one opaque score, built for one context, silently judging you in every other.
[P1.2] A legal right to disconnect means people can refuse, delete, and block transfer of that cross-context reputation, and employers, schools, landlords, and platforms cannot penalize them for it. It does not erase courts, licenses, or relevant references. It restores a boundary we already accept elsewhere: bankruptcy gives a fresh start, old debts fall off credit reports, juvenile records can be sealed. A lifelong, unappealable algorithmic dossier is the exception, not the norm.
[P1.3] That boundary is necessary for three reasons. First, autonomy. People change, contexts differ, and a good tenant is not reducible to gig ratings, social-media sentiment, or school discipline data. When every mistake travels, punishment never ends and reinvention becomes impossible.
[P1.4] Second, accuracy. These systems scrape mismatched names, outdated disputes, and biased customer ratings, then hide the logic behind trade secrecy. Without disconnect, there is no real due process — you cannot correct what you cannot see or escape. Third, power.
[P1.5] Consent is fiction when applying for work or housing requires surrendering your score. Only a legal right, not a settings toggle, prevents retaliation for opting out.
[P1.6] Opponents will invoke safety and trust, but relevance is the answer, not portability. A school can run a lawful background check for working with children; a landlord can check rental payment history. What neither needs is an imported, universal trustworthiness number. Disconnect forces decision-makers to justify what they use, for how long, and for what purpose — and gives everyone else the chance to outgrow their worst data point.
