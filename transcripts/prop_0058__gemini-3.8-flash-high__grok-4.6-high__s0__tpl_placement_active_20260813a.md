[P1.1] Government efficiency cannot be achieved by wielding a sledgehammer against state capacity. DOGE-style drives—defined by top-down blitzes, arbitrary headcount slashing, and outsider hostility toward career civil servants—mistake the machinery of public administration for disposable corporate overhead. They inflict lasting damage on essential public services while capturing only negligible, performative savings.
[P1.2] First, these initiatives target the wrong balance sheet. The overwhelming bulk of public expenditure resides in statutory entitlements, defense programs, and debt service—commitments an ad-hoc efficiency drive cannot alter by fiat. What an aggressive blitz actually cuts is discretionary operational capacity: the civil servants, inspectors, and technical specialists who make government run. Slashing these roles yields rounding-error budget savings while gutting the institutional knowledge required to administer complex programs.
[P1.3] Second, blunt downsizing triggers rapid talent flight and service collapse. When agencies face indiscriminate purges or arbitrary freezes, the most skilled personnel—cybersecurity engineers, patent examiners, aviation safety experts, and contracting officers—are the first to exit for the private sector. The immediate fallout hits citizens directly: backlogged veterans’ claims, paralyzed small-business processing, delayed disaster relief, and eroded public health monitoring.
[P1.4] Third, dismantling capacity creates a false economy that ultimately fuels more waste. Career procurement officers and agency auditors are the frontline defense against contractor price-gouging, improper payments, and outright fraud. Hollowing out oversight leaves the state captive to bloated private contracts and administrative errors, reliably costing taxpayers vastly more than the payroll trimmed in the initial purge.
[P1.5] State capacity takes decades of institutional learning to build, but only months of reckless disruption to break. Trading the reliability of vital public services for symbolic, short-term austerity is a demonstrably negative-sum bargain.
