[P1.1] A CBDC intended to replace cash must keep cash’s most basic freedom: buying a coffee without generating a permanent, state-readable record. If it cannot, it is not a true cash replacement; it is a compulsory surveillance ledger dressed in the language of convenience.
[P1.2] Cash already settles this balance. Physical money is anonymous for everyday sums and can be misused, yet every serious legal order preserves it because privacy, autonomy, and financial inclusion outweigh the investigative convenience of total traceability. Regulators do not ban small cash purchases; they police large movements through reporting thresholds. The motion simply translates that settled, risk-based balance into digital form: transfers below a fixed threshold stay private, while larger or cashed-out value enters the identified, reportable system.
[P1.3] That design blunts the usual fear. A threshold CBDC is not blanket secrecy. Tiered wallets can let small balances and peer-to-peer payments function like digital notes, while conversion into bank deposits above the line, or patterns of frantic micro-transferring, remain visible. Illicit actors cannot finance serious crime in tiny anonymous sips; to use the money they must eventually aggregate, cross the threshold, or cash out, and there they meet scrutiny.
[P1.4] Privacy at the bottom does not mean blindness at the boundaries.
[P1.5] The alternative is far worse. If a CBDC replaces cash but traces every snack, every tip, every donation, it exposes abuse survivors, journalists, dissidents, and ordinary privacy-conscious people to permanent monitoring, hacking, and discrimination. It also breeds resistance: people flee to volatile private crypto and informal markets, or they distrust the currency entirely. Mandating anonymous small-value transfers protects the vulnerable, preserves the trust a central bank needs, and ensures the CBDC truly replaces cash rather than abolishing the last neutral medium of exchange.
[P1.6] A CBDC that remembers everything is not modern money; it is memory with a balance sheet. The fixed threshold is the firewall.
