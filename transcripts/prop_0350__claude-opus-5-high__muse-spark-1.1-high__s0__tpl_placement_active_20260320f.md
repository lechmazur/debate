[P1.1] Obesity did not arrive because willpower collapsed sometime around 1980. Human nature is the constant here. What changed is the environment: calorie-dense food got cheaper, faster, more available, more heavily engineered for repeat consumption, and marketed with budgets no health ministry can match. When the constant is biology and the variable is the food supply, the variable is where policy belongs.
[P1.2] Start with the arithmetic of persuasion. A food company spends billions annually to make its product the default choice at every gas station, stadium, screen and checkout aisle. A public health campaign gets a fraction of that, for a few months, telling people to do the harder thing. Asking a parent to out-argue an industry that has optimized every touchpoint of her child's day is not a strategy; it's a hope.
[P1.3] Marketing restrictions and environmental rules change the odds she's playing against, rather than lecturing her about the odds.
[P1.4] Second, the mechanism actually works where it's been tried. Sugary drink taxes reliably shift purchasing, because price is a lever that operates on everyone at once, without requiring anyone to attend a class. Reformulation rules did more to cut trans fats and salt out of diets than a decade of asking consumers to check labels. Nobody had to be convinced; the product simply changed.
[P1.5] That's the signature of environmental policy: it works on the inattentive, the busy, the exhausted, the six-year-old.
[P1.6] Third, equity. Information campaigns are absorbed best by people with time, money, literacy and choice — the least affected group. So the campaign-heavy approach can widen the very gap it claims to close, while a family living in a neighborhood with four fried-chicken outlets and no supermarket gains nothing from a slogan.
[P1.7] Finally, note what "focus more" concedes. Nobody proposes banning education. The claim is about where the marginal dollar and marginal political effort should go. Given that individual-focused campaigns have been the dominant approach for forty years while obesity rose steadily, the burden of proof is not on the side asking to try changing the environment.
