[P1.1] No referee should be allowed to suit up and score goals for one of the teams. Yet in mobile computing, major app-store operators control the bottleneck gateway to billions of users while simultaneously selling competing music services, digital wallets, trackers, and productivity tools inside that same marketplace. When one firm is both gatekeeper and competitor, self-preferencing is not an occasional abuse; it is a rational business imperative.
[P1.2] That conflict distorts competition through three potent levers. First, asymmetric economics: independent developers must surrender steep store commissions on digital sales and subscriptions, inflating their costs, while the operator’s own apps face mere internal accounting transfers. Second, technical and discovery bias: operators preinstall their own software, seize system defaults, skew search rankings, and hoard deep hardware and operating-system interfaces while locking out rivals.
[P1.3] Third, informational predation: running the store yields unmatched visibility into download trends and user engagement, letting gatekeepers spot breakout independent apps early and clone them. Unsurprisingly, innovators and investors routinely avoid building software where the platform owner competes.
[P1.4] Behavioral rules and fines cannot fix this because digital self-preferencing is uniquely easy to disguise. A tweaked ranking algorithm, a sudden review delay before a rival’s launch, or a restrictive rule framed as a privacy or security update can cripple a competitor years before regulators finish investigating. Asking watchdogs to police shifting codebases and subjective design choices in real time guarantees endless evasion and regulatory whack-a-mole.
[P1.5] Structural separation solves the problem at its root. Splitting platform and store operations from first-party app development permanently realigns incentives. Standardized interfaces and simple setup choices ensure consumers still get secure, seamless devices, while spun-off app teams must win users on price and quality rather than gatekeeper privilege.
[P1.6] Meanwhile, a standalone platform operator profits only by making the marketplace safe, reliable, and fair for every developer. When the conflict of interest is structural, the remedy must be structural too.
