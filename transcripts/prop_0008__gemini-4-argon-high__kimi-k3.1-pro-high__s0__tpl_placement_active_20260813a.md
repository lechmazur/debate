[P1.2] First, a generational phase-out dismantles the peer supply chain. As the cutoff moves up every year, the social gap between teenagers and the youngest legal buyers steadily widens. When a sixteen-year-old cannot simply ask a slightly older classmate to buy tobacco, youth initiation drops sharply. Because nicotine dependency is overwhelmingly established in adolescence and early adulthood, closing this window prevents lifetime addiction before it starts.

Stopping corporations from trapping new cohorts in chemical dependency protects future freedom rather than restricting it.
[P1.4] Finally, a generational ban is uniquely pragmatic compared to immediate prohibition. No current legal smoker loses access or faces forced withdrawal.
