[P1.1] A federal or state-wide minimum wage is a single blunt lever pulled across every industry, every region, and every business model at once. A sectoral wage board is a scalpel. That difference — precision versus bluntness — is the whole debate.
[P1.2] Consider the mechanism. When you raise the minimum wage sharply across the board, you hit the rural convenience store in Mississippi with the same floor as the Manhattan hotel chain. Low-productivity, low-margin employers in low-cost regions absorb the shock through hours cuts, automation, or closure. That is where the disemployment literature keeps finding its sharpest effects: at the tail, where the bite is largest relative to local wages.
[P1.3] A wage board avoids this by design. It sets pay industry-by-industry, often region-by-region, with employers, workers, and the public at the table. Fast food in California can be pushed toward $20 because the sector can bear it; home care in rural counties can be raised on a different schedule. Because the floor covers the whole sector, no employer can undercut a competitor by paying less — the classic "race to the bottom" objection to unionization at a single firm simply doesn't apply.
[P1.4] Every McDonald's, every Chick-fil-A, every mom-and-pop burger shop pays the same rate, so the raise passes through to prices roughly uniformly and competitive position is preserved.
[P1.5] Empirically, the countries that consistently deliver high wage floors with strong employment — Australia, the Nordics, much of continental Europe — do it through sectoral or award systems, not through one enormous statutory number. And within the U.S., the New York fast-food wage board of the mid-2010s raised pay substantially without the employment collapse critics predicted, precisely because it was targeted.
[P1.6] The proposition isn't that minimum wages are bad. It's that if the goal is more money in low-wage workers' pockets with fewer jobs destroyed, a tailored floor beats a uniform sledgehammer. That is what sectoral bargaining delivers.
