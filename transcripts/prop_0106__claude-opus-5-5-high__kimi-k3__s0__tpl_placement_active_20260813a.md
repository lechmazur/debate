[P1.2] Start with how we treat every other difficult pediatric decision. [P1.3] Gender dysphoria deserves the same framework, not a special exception carved out by legislators who will never meet the patient.
[P1.4] Second, the "irreversibility" objection cuts toward PRO. [P1.5] Natural puberty permanently deepens voices, reshapes bones, and develops breasts. Picture a fourteen-year-old who has been consistently distressed for years, whose parents have sought therapy, and whose clinicians agree. A ban does not give that teenager more time to decide. [P1.6] Third, uncertainty is a reason for careful access, not prohibition. Yes, systematic reviews have rated much of this evidence as low certainty. The proper response is rigorous assessment, better data collection, and cautious prescribing.
[P1.7] Many clinicians report meaningful relief of distress, and the regret rates observed in treated cohorts appear low. A ban ends the research, drives families to unregulated sources, and abandons the young people with the most severe distress.
