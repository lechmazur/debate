[P1.1] Historically, wedding traditions existed to bind communities around a new household without bankrupting it. Today, commercial markups and social media spectacle have hijacked those rituals. Because modern "tradition" routinely demands steep financial sacrifice for curated performance, the underlying social contract has broken. [P1.2] Start with the punishing economics. Attach the word "wedding" to a venue, meal, or bouquet, and prices jump dramatically. Fulfilling standard traditional expectations, such as accommodating distant relatives, serving a formal dinner, and outfitting a bridal party, now rivals the cost of a home down payment or years of debt repayment. Enforcing this spending through family guilt inverts the entire purpose of communal support.
[P1.3] Because financial strain directly threatens marital stability, relatives cannot reasonably obligate young couples to subsidize a six-hour party with their future security.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, sacred ritual has decayed into exhausting theater. Contemporary wedding culture demands months of aestheticized milestones, from destination pre-wedding trips to choreographed entrances staged for cameras and social feeds. Brides and grooms routinely spend their wedding day managing timelines, appeasing parental status anxieties, and greeting obligatory acquaintances rather than experiencing their own vows. When customs prioritize visual perfection and public display over genuine intimacy, compliance stops honoring heritage and becomes unpaid event production for an audience.
[P1.5] Crucially, shedding obligation does not mean abandoning community or meaning. A courthouse vow followed by a backyard potluck, an intimate dinner, or a quiet elopement honors commitment just as seriously as a ballroom banquet. Once the baseline script morphed into an overpriced, performative gauntlet, walking away from traditional expectations ceased to be selfish and became common sense.
