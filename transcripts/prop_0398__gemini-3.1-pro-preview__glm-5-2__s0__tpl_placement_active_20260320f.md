[P1.1] Compensation has always been a function of both a role's baseline value and the local labor market's economic realities. When an employee moves from an expensive headquarters region to a significantly lower-cost area, expecting to keep an HQ salary distorts the fundamental premise of equitable compensation: purchasing power. A dollar goes much further in rural Ohio than in San Francisco. Location-adjusted pay is not a penalty for moving; it is the mechanism that maintains fairness in living standards across a distributed workforce.
[P1.2] Retaining an inflated salary in a low-cost area is effectively demanding an unearned windfall.
[P1.3] Crucially, failing to adjust pay creates a toxic two-tier system of internal inequity. Imagine two remote developers living in the same affordable town, doing the exact same work. One is a local hire paid the regional market rate, while the other relocated from headquarters and kept their coastal salary. Paying the relocated worker vastly more for the same output in the very same geographic market is fundamentally unjust to the local hire.
[P1.4] Location-based pay bands prevent this by ensuring equal pay for equal work within the same economic environment.
[P1.5] Furthermore, tying compensation to local markets makes widespread remote work sustainable and expands broader economic opportunity. If companies are forced to pay premium HQ rates universally, the incentive to offer remote roles plummets. Businesses would logically mandate return-to-office policies to justify those premiums, or they would simply hire fewer people. Location-adjusted pay allows organizations to distribute capital efficiently, opening up job opportunities to diverse regions rather than funneling outsized resources to a few privileged employees engaging in geo-arbitrage.
[P1.6] Remote workers successfully trade the crushing expenses and geographic constraints of HQ cities for unprecedented freedom, better homeownership prospects, and lower day-to-day living costs. Accepting a salary aligned with that newly chosen reality is a mathematically sound, fair, and necessary part of the modern employment bargain.
