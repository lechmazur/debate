[P1.2] A production tax that tracks environmental and health externalities puts the missing numbers on the invoice.

[C1.3] "Full externalities" is not one honest number. Damages vary enormously by system and place. A tax that treats a cow grazing stubble on marginal land like a steer fattened on Amazon soy overcharges sustainable producers and cheap proteins such as poultry, while the worst industrial operators can pay, pass costs along, or shift production abroad. Health externalities are also tangled with poverty, smoking, processed food, and healthcare access; they are not a clean per-kilo meat number a revenue officer can collect.
[C1.4] If the tax is truly matched to each system’s damage, it becomes case-by-case regulation, not a simple production levy. If it stays blunt, it punishes the small farmer and the poor consumer far more than the concentrated feedlot.

We can cut real damage directly: ban deforestation-linked feed, enforce manure and antibiotic rules, and fund nutrition for low-income families.
