[P1.5] Approval is the only moment regulators hold real leverage; spend it or lose it.
[P1.6] And "proportionate" means proportionate to who bears the disease, not census quotas. A sickle cell trial owes no seats to white patients. That is a demand for evidence, not demographics — and evidence is what we claim to be approving.

[C1.2] That is the core flaw. If a group is ten percent of patients, proportionate enrollment gives it ten percent of the trial. A trial powered to detect one overall effect cannot detect a different effect in a slice that small. [C1.3] The mandate buys months or years of delay and returns statistical noise. PRO calls this "a demand for evidence, not demographics," but proportionality is demographics. [C1.4] Now weigh what the delay costs. PRO calls it "bounded, one-time." It is neither. Recruiting hard-to-reach populations to fixed quotas means every site waits on the slowest stratum, and the delay recurs on every drug in every disease.
[C1.5] Each month an effective therapy sits unapproved, patients with no alternative get sicker or die, and the patients waiting longest are often the same underserved groups PRO wants to protect. When AIDS activists faced a lethal disease, they fought for faster access, not slower trials. Higher development costs also kill marginal drugs outright, especially for small markets.
[C1.6] The better path is precise: targeted pharmacology studies, oversampled subgroups where biology suggests differences, enforced post-market requirements, and honest labeling. PRO wants slower approvals for equity. CON delivers better answers, sooner, for everyone.
