[P1.1] Imagine a candidate who scores ENTJ one week and INFP a few weeks later. Nothing about her skills changed; only the questionnaire’s mood did. That is not personality science; it is a horoscope with HR letterhead. It is the core reason employers should retire MBTI, DISC, and similar type tests from hiring and team-building.
[P1.2] A selection or development tool earns its place only if it improves predictions: who will perform, who will collaborate, which team will deliver. These instruments do not.
[P1.3] They fail first on reliability. Traits like extraversion or assertiveness are continuous, yet MBTI and DISC slice them into forced categories. Masses of people sit near the midpoint, so tiny shifts—fatigue, framing, recent conflict—flip their label. Test–retest inconsistency is well documented in ordinary psychometrics: a measure that cannot classify the same person consistently cannot forecast that person’s behavior on the job.
[P1.4] That weak reliability caps validity. The letters do not predict job performance, retention, or team effectiveness. Where personality matters at all, its influence is broad, modest, and context-dependent; it is far better captured by structured interviews, work samples, and job-knowledge tests. Bolting on a four-letter type or color adds almost no predictive power while creating dangerous false precision.
[P1.5] In hiring, it screens out qualified people for reasons unrelated to the work. A quiet engineer tagged “I” can be steered away from client work she would master; a “D” tag can excuse behavior that damages a team. In team-building, it boxes colleagues into roles—“the feeler,” “the Dominant”—and licenses stereotypes instead of building coordination.
[P1.6] The pleasure of recognizing oneself in a profile is entertainment, not evidence. Team exercises built on these labels may feel insightful, but they do not reliably change behavior or raise performance. The fix is not more cautious label use; it is to stop using the instruments for decisions. Use assessments tied to actual tasks and observe real collaboration.
