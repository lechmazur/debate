A campaign of genuine accounts about unsafe working conditions does not need anyone's home address, stolen images, or a brigade of sockpuppet accounts filing fabricated complaints. The features that make a campaign foreseeable as harmful—coordinated inauthentic behavior, mass-posting of private identifying data, rapid swarming of one target's employer or school—are conduct signals, not viewpoint signals. A duty aimed at those features reaches the abuser's swarm and leaves the whistleblower's cause untouched.
[P2.2] CON has not produced a legitimate cause that requires doxxing to function.
[P2.3] Second, CON misstates the legal standard. A duty of care is not liability for outcomes; it asks whether the platform took reasonable steps against a risk it could anticipate, judged by the signals visible at the time. That is ordinary negligence, applied everywhere else. "Foreseeable becomes hindsight dressed as predictability" is a criticism of negligence law itself, not of this motion, and CON gives no reason platforms should be uniquely exempt.
[P2.4] Third, weigh the errors against each other. CON warns of over-throttling. The status quo is a domestic abuse survivor whose address is published and whose report sits unanswered while the campaign runs for days. Slowing a mass-targeting burst delays a campaign; a lost job, a forced move, or a police response to a false emergency cannot be undone.
[P2.5] Asymmetric caution favors the side where the harm is irreversible.
[P2.6] Finally, CON's fallback—criminal law and targeted orders—proves my case. Those tools require a victim to identify anonymous harassers and litigate after the damage lands. Ex post remedies for a harm that unfolds in hours are not prevention. And the concessions about orders to remove exposed addresses and preserve evidence only work if platforms are legally obliged to comply; without a duty, they remain favors granted to the well-known and withheld from everyone else.
[P2.7] Mitigation need not mean surveillance: friction, rate limits, and delayed amplification are cheap, light-touch tools already within reach. What is missing is not capability. It is obligation.
