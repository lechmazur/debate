[P1.1] Relying almost entirely on income and consumption taxes rests on an outdated fiction: that economic capacity looks like a paycheck spent at a store. For ordinary workers, that holds true; nearly every dollar earned faces payroll or income tax, and most of what remains faces consumption taxes when spent. For ultra-wealthy households, however, taxable income is largely optional. Great fortunes grow primarily through unrealized appreciation in corporate stock, private equity, and real estate.
[P1.2] Instead of selling assets and triggering tax, wealthy owners routinely borrow against appreciating portfolios to fund lavish lifestyles free of income tax. Under a system anchored to realization and spending, top fortunes compound untouched while wage earners fund public life.
[P1.3] Consumption taxes cannot fix this structural blind spot. Even an extravagant billionaire spends only a sliver of an immense fortune; taxing luxury purchases leaves the overwhelming bulk of compounding capital unreached. Worse, most tax systems already levy an annual wealth tax, just on the wrong households. Middle-class families pay yearly property taxes on the assessed value of their primary asset, their home, whether or not they sell it.
[P1.4] Exempting huge financial portfolios from any comparable annual charge privileges dynastic capital over ordinary labor and homeownership.
[P1.5] Introducing an annual tax on large net worth restores fiscal fairness by taxing true ability to pay. Because it applies only above a high threshold, revenue agencies oversee a narrow, manageable pool of taxpayers rather than policing the general public. Furthermore, a modest annual levy rewards productive enterprise over passive hoarding: idle assets feel the charge, whereas growing investments easily outpace a small percentage rate.
[P1.6] Ultimately, economic command resides in accumulated net worth, not just annual cash flows. Only taxing large fortunes directly prevents runaway wealth concentration from eroding the tax base and distorting democracy.
