[P1.1] The climate crisis demands urgent action, and onshore wind—among the cheapest and most proven power sources—must be central to Europe’s response. Every delayed turbine means more coal and gas, locking in emissions for decades. Local opposition, often rooted in misinformation or pure NIMBYism, cannot be allowed to veto the collective survival project that is rapid decarbonization. The scale of the challenge is stark: to meet 2030 targets, Europe must dramatically accelerate onshore wind deployment, yet permitting and local objections currently strangle projects for years.
[P1.2] Grid expansion faces identical bottlenecks—without new transmission lines, renewable power can’t reach demand centers, forcing continued reliance on fossil backups and crippling the entire energy transition.

[P1.4] The cost of inaction is already visible. The UK’s onshore wind restrictions have added significantly to energy bills while stalling substantial ready-to-build capacity. When the price of delay is measured in emissions and economic harm, local objections cannot retain absolute power. Democracy doesn’t require giving every community a veto over national survival.
