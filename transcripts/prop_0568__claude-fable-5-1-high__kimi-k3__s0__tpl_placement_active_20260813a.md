[P1.1] Picture a woman standing at a supermarket checkout with a twenty-dollar bill for milk, bread, and her child's medicine, and being told her money is no good here. That is not a hypothetical; it is the policy CON must defend. Cash is the one payment instrument every legal resident already holds, issued by the state, requiring no account, no credit check, no smartphone, no battery, and no permission from a bank. Access to food, medicine, fuel, and basic hygiene should not depend on a private company's willingness to let you in.
[P1.2] Three reasons this mandate is right.
[P1.3] First, inclusion. A meaningful share of households in wealthy countries remain unbanked or underbanked: the elderly, the poor, migrants, people with frozen or garnished accounts, survivors leaving an abusive partner who controls the joint card. "Go cashless" is a polite way of saying these people may shop for essentials only where retailers choose to tolerate them. A mandate makes their money work everywhere the rest of ours does.
[P1.4] Second, resilience. Card networks fail. Power fails. Hurricanes, cyberattacks, and routine processor outages have repeatedly turned tap-to-pay stores into locked pantries for hours or days.
[P1.5] Cash is the fallback that needs no network. A society that lets its grocery chains abandon it is dismantling its own emergency infrastructure to save on till counting.
[P1.6] Third, autonomy. Every digital purchase is a logged, searchable record. Buying contraception, a pregnancy test, or a bottle of wine should not require submitting a data point to your bank, a platform, and whoever buys the feed. Cash preserves lawful privacy without asking anyone's approval.
[P1.7] Notice how narrow the motion is: major retailers, essential goods. Not the food truck, not the boutique. Large chains already run cash handling at scale, and jurisdictions that have adopted such requirements have not seen commerce collapse. The cost is marginal; the harm prevented is exclusion from survival goods.
[P1.8] The real question is simple: should the ability to buy dinner require a bank's ongoing consent? PRO says no.
