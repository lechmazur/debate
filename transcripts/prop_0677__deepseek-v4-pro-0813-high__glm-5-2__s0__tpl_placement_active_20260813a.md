[C1.1] The motion sounds modest. It is not. "Meaningfully influenced by automated decision systems" is a standard that cannot be administered honestly, and mandatory disclosure under it will produce either compliance theater or strategic concealment—neither of which helps workers.
[C1.2] Consider what actually happens inside hiring. A recruiter uses LinkedIn to find candidates. An applicant tracking system filters by keywords. A hiring manager glances at an assessment dashboard.
[C1.3] A senior leader makes the final call after a human interview. At what point has automation "meaningfully influenced" the decision? The search ranking? The keyword filter?
[C1.4] The dashboard score? Every one is a judgment call, and no two employers will draw the line in the same place. The result: either employers disclose everything, drowning workers in meaningless notices, or they disclose nothing, betting no court will call their software use "meaningful." Neither gives the rejected applicant what PRO promises—the ability to correct errors or challenge bias.
[C1.5] The background-check analogy fails. A credit report is a discrete document from a regulated third party, easily identified as a factor. Automated decision systems are woven into ordinary business software—scheduling tools, performance dashboards, resume platforms. There is no clean moment where "the machine" hands down a verdict separate from human judgment.
[C1.6] Worse, mandatory disclosure invites gaming. Employers who fear litigation will avoid tools that leave auditable trails, shifting to informal networks and gut-feeling decisions where bias is harder to detect, not easier. The transparency PRO imagines will push discrimination into less visible channels.
[C1.7] Workers who face real harm already have tools: anti-discrimination law, adverse-action notice requirements for credit and background checks, and wrongful termination doctrine. What they need is enforcement of those protections, not a new disclosure mandate built on a standard no one can consistently apply. The motion promises light; it delivers fog.
