[P1.1] Publicly funded science is built on a fundamental social contract: taxpayers finance research to discover empirical truth, not to generate flattering headlines. Yet under the status quo, this contract is broken by publication bias and distorted academic incentives. When prestige and career advancement depend on novel, statistically significant findings, researchers are systematically incentivized to bury null results—the notorious file-drawer problem—or engage in post-hoc data dredging, p-hacking, and outcome switching. In clinical medicine, suppressing negative or inconclusive trials directly jeopardizes human lives by exaggerating drug efficacy and masking harms.
[P1.2] In the social sciences, it leads governments to waste billions on unreplicable public policies.
[P1.3] The motion establishes a decisive, two-part remedy to restore scientific integrity.
[P1.4] First, mandatory preregistration enforces transparency. By locking in hypotheses, sample sizes, and analytical pipelines before data collection, it draws an unambiguous line between confirmatory hypothesis testing and exploratory discovery. Preregistration does not restrict scientific curiosity; it simply ensures researchers cannot retroactively rewrite their questions to match accidental statistical noise.
[P1.5] Second, outcome-neutral publishing commitments dismantle the engine of publication bias. When journals accept studies based on the importance of the research question and the methodological rigor of the design—before results are known—scientific quality is judged on sound execution rather than sensational findings. A rigorously designed trial showing that a targeted therapy or educational program fails is just as vital to the scientific record as one showing success.
[P1.6] Public research funding is a fiduciary investment. We cannot permit half the scientific record to disappear into file drawers while distorted artifacts dictate medical guidelines and social spending. Mandatory preregistration paired with guaranteed, outcome-blind publication aligns scientific incentives with objective truth, ensuring that public science truly serves the public good.
