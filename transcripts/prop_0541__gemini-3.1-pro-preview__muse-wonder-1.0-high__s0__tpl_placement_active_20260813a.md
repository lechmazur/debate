[P1.1] The current United States defense strategy relies dangerously on a shrinking number of hyper-advanced, "exquisite" platforms. Aircraft carriers and fifth-generation fighters are technological marvels, but their exorbitant costs mean we can afford fewer of them. In a great-power conflict, particularly across the vast expanse of the Indo-Pacific, mass matters. Concentrating our combat power in a handful of multi-billion-dollar assets creates crippling vulnerabilities.
[P1.2] We are fundamentally deterred from using these assets aggressively because they are too expensive to lose, highly vulnerable to modern anti-access/area-denial missile networks, and incredibly slow to replace.
[P1.3] Shifting a significant share of procurement toward cheaper, attritable systems—such as unmanned aerial vehicles, autonomous naval surface vessels, and loitering munitions—directly solves this capacity crisis. Quantity has a quality all its own. A networked swarm of inexpensive drones can overwhelm sophisticated air defenses through sheer numbers and dispersed lethality, achieving objectives that a single stealth bomber cannot guarantee.
[P1.4] Crucially, attritable systems are expendable by design. Commanders can deploy them into highly contested environments without risking American lives or inviting strategic financial ruin.
[P1.5] Furthermore, the development cycle of exquisite platforms takes decades, often locking the military into outdated paradigms by the time they are actually fielded. Conversely, the technology driving attritable systems evolves at the speed of the commercial tech sector. We can iterate, upgrade, and mass-produce these systems in months. If an adversary develops a countermeasure, we simply adjust the software, swap the payload, and deploy the next cheap iteration.
[P1.6] Finally, our traditional defense industrial base currently lacks the surge capacity to replace a lost destroyer or stealth fighter during a major conflict. Attritable systems, which lean heavily on commercially available components, can be manufactured rapidly across a much broader industrial base. Transitioning away from an exclusive reliance on exquisite platforms toward a mix prioritizing attritable mass ensures the United States possesses the resilience, adaptability, and overwhelming volume necessary to outlast and defeat modern peer adversaries.
