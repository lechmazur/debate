[P1.1] A smart device carries a hidden clock. The hardware may last a decade, but if the manufacturer stops issuing security patches after two years, that phone, thermostat, or doorbell becomes a liability—open to known exploits with no fix coming. Today, consumers learn this only after purchase, if at all, often buried in support forums or quietly announced years later. That is a market failure, and the fix is simple: put the guaranteed support lifespan on the box before the money changes hands.
[P1.2] We have watched this play out repeatedly. Smart TVs lose app support within a few seasons. Security cameras stop getting firmware updates while still sitting on store shelves. Phones branded as current receive their last security patch months after a buyer unwraps them.
[P1.3] The manufacturer knows the support schedule at launch. The buyer does not. That information asymmetry is the entire problem, and packaging disclosure dissolves it in one stroke.
[P1.4] The word "guaranteed" carries the weight. A manufacturer who prints "supported through 2028" has made a binding claim. If they abandon the device early, consumers have false-advertising recourse. That shifts risk from the buyer—who currently bears it entirely—to the party who actually controls the support decision.
[P1.5] Today, a company can sell a device it fully intends to orphan in eighteen months and face no consequence. Mandatory disclosure turns that into a liability.
[P1.6] Packaging, specifically. Not a webpage or a manual, because the purchase decision happens at the shelf. Online shoppers see the box image. In-store buyers hold the product.
[P1.7] A URL or QR code lets the manufacturer bury the truth behind a site redesign or a dead link. Physical print on physical packaging survives.
[P1.8] Smart devices are not toasters. Their value depends on software infrastructure the manufacturer alone controls. When that infrastructure disappears, the device is not merely diminished—it is compromised. Consumers deserve to know when that clock runs out before they buy, not after.

[C1.1] PRO’s proposal trades the illusion of consumer protection for real, tangible harms that hurt the very buyers it claims to help. A mandatory printed “guaranteed support lifespan” on packaging imposes rigid, one-size-fits-all requirements that ignore the variable, unpredictable nature of software support, while adding unnecessary costs that hit low-income shoppers hardest.
[C1.2] First, fixed printed timelines create perverse incentives and unenforceable obligations. If a manufacturer prints a 5-year support window but faces an unforeseen supply chain crisis, abrupt market exit, or a critical zero-day vulnerability that emerges after the printed end date requiring a patch, the guarantee becomes either a lie that further erodes trust, or a legal trap that forces the company to divert resources from supporting newer, more widely used devices to meet an arbitrary deadline for older units. The “false advertising recourse” PRO touts is also useless in practice: a small settlement years after a device is orphaned does not magically restore its security or functionality.
[C1.3] Second, the mandate imposes real costs that get passed directly to consumers. Redesigning packaging, updating compliance documentation for every support timeline adjustment, and managing the legal risk of printed claims adds overhead that budget device makers cannot absorb. For a $100 smart speaker sold at a 5% margin, that extra cost pushes the retail price up, pricing out the exact low-income buyers PRO claims to champion.
[C1.4] Finally, the requirement is redundant for any shopper who does even basic pre-purchase research. Most major manufacturers already publish support timelines on their websites, and major retailers list support details on product pages. The mandate only punishes transparent companies to benefit the tiny share of buyers who never research a device before purchase.
