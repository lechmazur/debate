[P1.1] Restitution that ends at the loading dock is not restitution; it is relocation of a problem. The proposition says former colonial powers should fund conservation, provenance research, and museum capacity in receiving countries rather than treat restitution as complete once objects are shipped back. I defend it on three grounds: causation, practicality, and the meaning of the act itself.
[P1.2] First, causation. The artifacts in question were removed under conditions—war, occupation, extraction economies—that also stripped the receiving societies of the institutions needed to care for them. The same colonial history that emptied the palaces emptied the workshops, the training pipelines, and the funding bases. When a state caused compound harm, repair covers the compound, not the final symptom.
[P1.3] A thief who returns a stolen car with the engine removed has not made the owner whole.
[P1.4] Second, practicality. Objects that have spent a century in climate-controlled European vaults often arrive into tropical humidity, unstable power grids, and underfunded staffs. If restitution makes preservation worse, it betrays the very heritage it claims to restore.
[P1.5] Funding is not charity appended to restitution; it is the mechanism that makes restitution function.

Those records sit overwhelmingly in European archives, expedition ledgers, and museum registries. [C1.1] Return means return. When a museum holds a looted mask, plaque, or manuscript, the wrong is possession without title. That wrong ends the moment ownership and control pass to the rightful community. To insist the former holder must also pay for storage, labs, and staff afterward confuses restitution with foreign aid and converts a duty of non-interference into a license for continued interference.
[C1.2] Worse, the premise insults the very people it claims to help. It pictures receiving countries as helpless warehouses with leaky roofs and no expertise, waiting for European rescue. That picture is false. Bronze casters still work in Benin City, manuscript custodians in Timbuktu preserved texts for generations without Parisian climate control, and national museums across Africa and Asia already conserve, exhibit, and research.
[C1.3] To make European money the condition of African stewardship tells those custodians their sovereignty is conditional on European standards.
[C1.4] Linking returns to funding also gives reluctant holders the perfect excuse to delay. Every return can be postponed until another grant cycle, another training program, another capacity assessment written in London or Berlin. Justice that waits for a budget line is not justice. Unconditional return is faster, cleaner, and harder to evade.
[C1.5] If research collaboration or development aid is desirable, let it be negotiated state to state, not smuggled in as a surcharge on stolen property.
[C1.6] The stolen-car analogy collapses on inspection. A thief who returns a car does not owe lifetime insurance and garage fees; ownership includes the burden and dignity of care. European vaults did not strip engines, in many cases they stabilized objects for a century. Handing them back intact discharges the debt.
[C1.7] Stretching causation to cover all colonial underdevelopment turns every plaque into a blank check for national museum systems, a burden no legal or moral principle of property supports.
[C1.8] True decolonization is a clean break: give it back, recognize full authority, and stop dictating what happens next.

[C2.3] On delay, the logic is backwards. Add a price tag to every return and finance ministries get a veto. A director who fears restitution can now say, "we support return in principle, pending the funding package." Bureaucracy's easiest hiding place is an unfunded mandate.
[C2.4] Unconditional return — sign title, ship it — has no budget line to hide behind, which is why it is harder to evade.
[C2.5] The logbook point concedes my case. If European files hold the provenance, then copy them, digitize them openly, send the archives home with the object. That is part of returning what was stolen, not a separate research program to be funded and managed from London. Paid provenance keeps the keys in European hands while charging the owner for a copy.
[C2.6] And "bounded" is fiction. Conservation of returned objects plus capacity to hold them has no natural endpoint. Who declares humidity low enough, staff trained enough, obligation discharged? The former power, forever.
