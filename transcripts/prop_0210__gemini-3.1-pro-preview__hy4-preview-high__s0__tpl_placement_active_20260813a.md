[P1.1] Medical decisions must be based on the totality of scientific evidence, not a corporate highlight reel. When pharmaceutical companies suppress negative clinical trial results, they distort the medical literature, directly endangering patients and wasting immense public resources. Requiring public disclosure of all trial results as a condition of market access is the only mechanism strong enough to enforce scientific integrity.
[P1.2] First, selective publication harms patients by manufacturing a false illusion of efficacy. When negative trials—those showing a drug is ineffective or causes severe side effects—are buried, doctors are forced to prescribe blindly. If a drug fails three trials but succeeds in two, publishing only the successes creates a dangerously skewed profile. Doctors unwittingly prescribe treatments that are useless or actively harmful, believing they are following evidence-based medicine.
[P1.3] Patients bear the physical cost of this deception.
[P1.4] Second, hiding negative results drives massive scientific waste and unethical human experimentation. When independent researchers do not know a specific chemical pathway has already failed, they unknowingly duplicate doomed studies. This squanders finite research funding. Worse, it exposes new cohorts of human volunteers to ineffective experimental treatments and their associated toxicities, violating the fundamental ethical obligation to minimize harm in medical research.
[P1.5] Finally, market access is the only leverage point that actually works. Historically, regulatory fines for non-disclosure have failed; multi-billion-dollar pharmaceutical corporations simply absorb these penalties as a routine cost of doing business. Market access, however, is their lifeblood. By making the disclosure of all trial data—positive and negative—an absolute prerequisite for selling any product in the country, governments neutralize the financial incentive to hide bad data.
[P1.6] If a company wants the privilege of profiting from a nation's patients, it must first prove it is not lying to their doctors.
[P1.7] Transparency is not a mere administrative preference; it is a life-or-death requirement for modern medicine. Without mandatory disclosure tied to market access, the pharmaceutical industry will continue to privatize its successes and bury its failures at the public’s expense.
