[P1.1] Essential insurance exists for one reason: to stop ordinary bad luck from destroying someone's life. Pure unconstrained actuarial pricing breaks that promise completely. This is not about car insurance for weekend racing. This is basic health cover, home fire insurance, income protection: things no person can safely opt out of, things that make the difference between staying part of the community and falling through the cracks.
[P1.2] Actuarial fairness charges a baby more for health insurance before it draws its first breath, because it was born with a genetic marker. It charges a single mother twice as much for home insurance, not because of anything she has done, but because her postcode has a higher historical theft rate. It does not reward good choices. It punishes people for circumstances they never chose.
[P1.3] That is the opposite of social solidarity. Solidarity means we agree, ahead of time, that we will not abandon each other to bad luck. It means we accept that sometimes you will pay a little more today, so that when your own bad luck comes, you will not be priced out and abandoned. You can pretend this is about fairness on a spreadsheet.
[P1.4] But in the real world, unconstrained actuarial pricing does not produce a fair system. It produces people priced out of coverage entirely. Then the rest of us still end up paying for their emergency hospital visits, their burned out home left abandoned on the street, just in the cruellest, most wasteful possible way. Limiting actuarial pricing does not take away choice for luxuries.
[P1.5] It keeps essential insurance the mutual protection pact it was always meant to be, rather than a betting shop where only the lucky are allowed to play.
