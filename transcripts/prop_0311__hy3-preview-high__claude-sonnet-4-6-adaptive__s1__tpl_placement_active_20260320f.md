[P1.1] Treating cohabitation as a "sign of weak commitment" is a relic of a bygone era that ignores how modern relationships actually succeed. [P1.2] First, cohabitation is the ultimate stress test for compatibility. Marriage is not just a romantic vow; it is a shared life involving finances, chores, and daily habits. By living together first, couples move past the "best behavior" phase of dating and see how a partner handles real-world stress, conflict resolution, and domestic labor. To call this "weak commitment" is absurd; it actually requires a higher degree of honesty.
[P1.3] It is far more committed to work through a sink full of dirty dishes together than to simply sign a legal document based on a courtship that existed only in a vacuum.
[P1.4] Second, the "weak commitment" narrative relies on outdated studies that confused correlation with causation. Earlier data suggested cohabitation led to divorce, but we now understand that was because of "sliding" into marriage without a plan. Today, when cohabitation is a deliberate choice, it leads to more stable marriages because couples enter the union with their eyes wide open. A couple that has navigated the complexities of a shared lease and a joint budget is often better equipped for the long-term rigors of marriage than those who waited for a "perfect" traditional timeline.
[P1.5] Finally, socially, we must acknowledge that the "marriage first" model is increasingly a luxury or a matter of specific religious tradition rather than a universal standard. It is a reasonable, pragmatic step that strengthens the foundation of a future marriage, making the commitment itself more durable.
