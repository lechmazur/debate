[P1.1] Latin American cities need fast, equitable transport decarbonization: the sector generates 15% of regional greenhouse gas emissions, and 80% of urban public transit riders rely on bus services, not rail. Prioritizing rapid bus fleet electrification over near-term rail expansion delivers faster, cheaper, more inclusive emissions cuts that meet 2030 climate goals rail cannot match.
[P1.2] Deployment speed is the decisive factor. Electrifying an existing bus fleet takes 3 to 5 years: operators only need to build charging depots and swap diesel buses for electric models, using existing road infrastructure already in place. Even a 10-kilometer light rail line requires a decade of planning, land acquisition, and construction before it carries a single passenger. That 5-year headstart avoids millions of tons of diesel emissions far sooner, critical for hitting the region’s Paris Agreement commitments.
[P1.3] Cost efficiency amplifies this impact. A kilometer of new urban rail costs 40 to 80 million USD in Latin American contexts, while electrifying a bus fleet to serve the same passenger volume costs less than 10% of that per passenger-kilometer. Saved funds can scale electrification across entire cities at once, rather than funding a single rail corridor that only serves a fraction of commuters.
[P1.4] Equity and public health gains also favor buses. Low-income residents, who make up 60% of urban transit users, almost never live near planned rail lines, but rely on bus routes that run through every neighborhood. Electrifying these fleets eliminates diesel soot and NOx pollution in communities bearing the worst air quality impacts today, cutting childhood asthma rates immediately, while rail expansion almost always prioritizes commercial and middle-class districts first.
[P1.5] Bogotá’s 2022 rollout of 1,200 electric buses to its TransMilenio BRT system cut local air pollution by 12% in corridor neighborhoods within two years— a result no planned rail extension in the city can replicate in the near term. This is a proven, replicable model for the entire region.
