[P1.1] Self-custody cryptocurrency wallets must remain legal by default, with restrictions applied only when a specific user ties the wallet to confirmed criminal conduct. This standard protects fundamental financial privacy and property rights, consistent with centuries of legal tradition that treats neutral tools as lawful unless misused.
[P1.2] We do not ban physical safes, cash, or end-to-end encryption even though bad actors use all three for illicit activity, because punishing innocent users for the crimes of a tiny minority is unjust and counterproductive. The vast majority of self-custody wallet users are law-abiding investors, expats sending low-cost remittances, or people in economies with collapsed fiat systems like Nigeria or Argentina, where crypto is a lifeline to avoid hyperinflation. Criminals primarily use physical cash, offshore shell companies, and unregulated hawala networks for illicit activity—channels far harder to trace than public blockchain transactions tied to self-custody wallets.
[P1.3] Presumptive bans or heavy regulation of self-custody wallets also create severe, avoidable harms. They erase the only meaningful check against arbitrary financial censorship: in 2022, the Canadian government froze the bank accounts of hundreds of trucker protestors and their supporters without individual criminal charges, cutting off their access to the financial system entirely. Self-custody wallets were the only way many of those people could receive donations or pay for basic needs during that crackdown.
[P1.4] They also force users to hand over sensitive personal data to centralized providers, creating massive hacker honeypots. The 2022 Ledger data breach exposed the home addresses, purchase histories, and emails of 1 million self-custody wallet users, a direct result of regulatory pressure forcing the company to collect identifying information from customers. Law enforcement already has robust tools to trace illicit crypto flows on public blockchains and seize assets tied to confirmed crimes, no presumptive ban required.
[P1.5] The proposition’s standard is simple, fair, and effective: punish the criminal conduct, not the tool. CON’s approach sacrifices the rights of millions of innocent people to chase a security benefit that does not exist.
