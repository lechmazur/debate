[P1.1] When a police officer violates someone's rights, the officer almost never pays. The city does. Taxpayers do. Neighborhoods that are most over-policed end up being taxed twice — once through abusive policing, again through the settlement check that follows.
[P1.2] That's not just unfair. It destroys deterrence.
[P1.3] We already know how to fix this kind of moral hazard in other high-stakes professions. Doctors buy malpractice insurance. Drivers buy auto insurance. We don't do that to punish every doctor or driver; we do it to link risk to cost.
[P1.4] Good record, low premium. Bad record, high premium. Uninsurable, you shouldn't be practicing.
[P1.5] Police work is the ultimate high-risk profession for other people. Officers are entrusted to use force, to detain, to kill. Yet internal discipline is famously weak — complaints buried, unions blocking firings, qualified immunity shielding officers from civil liability. So the financial signal defaults to zero for the officer and 100% for the taxpayer.
[P1.6] Requiring personal professional liability insurance reverses that.
[P1.7] It does not mean an officer writes a personal check for a ten-million-dollar verdict. Insurance pays. It means the officer carries a policy, as professionals do. The city can cover a base premium, but surcharges follow misconduct.
[P1.8] An officer with repeated excessive-force complaints sees his premium climb. A chronically violent officer becomes uninsurable and therefore unemployable — without waiting ten years for a department to finally act.
[P1.9] Insurers, unlike city halls, have a direct financial incentive to reduce risk. They will demand de-escalation training, body cameras on, data on stops and complaints, early intervention — because it saves them money. That protects good officers, too. The vast majority who do the job well would pay very little, and would have documentation proving their professionalism.
[P1.10] If you carry the power of the state on your hip, you should carry the financial responsibility that goes with it. Municipal taxpayers should not be the default insurer for misconduct.
