[P1.1] Broad tariffs on strategic clean-tech imports will backfire because they impose immediate costs on deployment while delivering slow, uncertain benefits to manufacturing. Clean-tech supply chains are globally integrated: US solar projects still rely on Chinese panels for the majority of modules, EV assembly depends on Korean and Japanese batteries, and the most advanced chips come from Taiwan. Tariffs don’t instantiate domestic alternatives overnight. The 2018 US Section 201 solar tariffs proved this: 30% duties raised module prices by roughly 20% within months, and new solar installations fell by about 11% that year.
[P1.2] For EVs, tariffs on batteries or critical minerals add thousands of dollars to vehicle costs, directly delaying the price parity needed for mainstream adoption. These deployment slowdowns have permanent climate consequences. Each year of reduced clean-tech growth locks in gigatons of additional CO2 emissions for decades, as older fossil infrastructure continues operating.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, manufacturing gains are neither fast nor guaranteed. A new solar panel factory takes 2–3 years and over a billion dollars to build; a cutting-edge semiconductor fab takes 5+ years and over $10 billion. Even then, scale and cost advantages may remain with Asian producers, as the EU’s battery initiatives demonstrate—local production still struggles to match Asian cost curves. The fundamental mismatch is temporal and economic: tariffs immediately make all clean tech more expensive for domestic buyers, but only slowly and partially create domestic supply.
[P1.4] They force a direct trade-off between certain near-term deployment slowdowns and uncertain long-term manufacturing gains. Climate science demands rapid clean-tech scaling now to meet 2030 targets. Energy security improves with deployed solar farms and EV fleets, not hypothetical future factory capacity. The immediate drag on deployment—fewer solar farms, slower EV uptake, delayed grid modernization—outweighs speculative manufacturing benefits that may never materialize at competitive scale.
[P1.5] Tariffs sacrifice the urgent and certain clean-tech deployment for distant and uncertain manufacturing gains.
