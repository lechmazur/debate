Cash is the only payment method that works without a bank’s permission, a smartphone, a credit check, or a live network connection. Requiring major retailers to accept cash for essential goods ensures that access to daily necessities remains a baseline public guarantee rather than a privilege gated by private financial intermediaries.
[P1.2] First, cashless policies lock vulnerable people out of basic survival. Millions of people remain unbanked or underbanked because minimum-balance fees, overdraft penalties, or identification barriers shut them out of traditional banking. When a dominant supermarket or pharmacy chain drops cash, excluded residents in low-income or rural areas with few alternatives have nowhere else to turn.
[P1.3] Second, cash is irreplaceable emergency infrastructure. Payment networks, telecom grids, and banking servers fail during cyberattacks, software outages, and severe storms. Precisely when communities urgently need water, groceries, and prescriptions, card terminals go dark. A retailer that has eliminated cash drawers, change floats, and handling procedures cannot improvise them mid-crisis.
[P1.4] Requiring cash acceptance keeps vital offline payment capacity intact before disaster strikes.
[P1.5] Finally, this mandate is surgically tailored. It does not burden a corner coffee cart, a small boutique, or a stadium vendor. It applies strictly to major retailers selling essential goods: high-volume corporations with the scale and security to keep at least one cash lane or sealed cash-accepting kiosk open, while still running fast digital checkouts for everyone else. Minor operational convenience for retail giants never outweighs universal, crisis-proof access to food and medicine.
