[P1.1] Requiring airlines to bundle a reasonable carry‑on allowance in the base fare ends the era of hidden fees that erode trust and distort competition. Today a traveler booking a $200 ticket may discover at the gate that a simple roller bag costs another $45, sometimes more if the airline decides the bag is “oversized.” Those add‑ons are not optional luxuries; they are the price of basic mobility. When airlines fragment baggage into a la carte charges, they shift revenue from transparent fare competition to opaque surcharge extraction, rewarding the carrier that can squeeze the most out of each passenger rather than the one that offers the best service.
[P1.2] The solution is simple: mandate a baseline carry‑on – a bag that fits in the overhead bin – included in every ticket. This does not cripple low‑cost carriers. Southwest, for example, has long bundled a free carry‑on and still thrives on a no‑frills model. What changes is that legacy carriers can no longer hide a $30‑$60 fee behind a “basic economy” fare that looks cheap on the search screen but becomes expensive once the traveler adds a bag.
[P1.3] A baseline requirement restores price transparency, lets consumers compare fares on a like‑for‑like basis, and forces airlines to compete on schedule, reliability, and cabin experience instead of on how many ancillary fees they can invent. It also reduces the administrative burden on passengers who currently must navigate a maze of size limits, weight caps, and per‑flight variations.
[P1.4] Critics will claim it limits airline flexibility, but flexibility should not be a synonym for exploiting information asymmetry. A mandated carry‑on baseline protects consumers without dictating how airlines price checked luggage or premium services. It simply draws a line where the ticket must cover what any reasonable traveler needs to bring on board.
