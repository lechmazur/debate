[P1.1] Burnout and isolation are not knowledge problems. They are structural problems. People aren't lonely because they lack a meditation library on their phone, and they aren't exhausted because nobody taught them box breathing. They're lonely because they don't see their colleagues as humans, and they're exhausted because the work never stops.
[P1.2] Spending matters where it actually changes those two facts.
[P1.3] Predictable time off does. When leave is genuinely protected — the laptop closed, the Slack silent, the inbox covered — the nervous system gets what no app can deliver: real recovery. The word "predictable" is doing heavy lifting here. A vacation you spend anxiously monitoring email is not rest.
[P1.4] A four-day weekend everyone in your unit also takes is rest, because the work pressure pauses with you. That requires the employer to spend on coverage, staffing slack, and enforced norms. That is a budget line, not a wallpaper.
[P1.5] In-person gathering does the other half. Weak ties — the hallway chat, the shared lunch, the offsite where you learn your teammate has a kid the same age as yours — are what convert "co-workers" into "people who would notice if I disappeared." That noticing is the actual antidote to isolation. You cannot generate it through a chatbot check-in.
[P1.6] It requires travel budget, food budget, and time that isn't billed to a project.
[P1.7] Now compare the wellness app. At best, it offers individual coping tools for a structural problem, which quietly relocates responsibility onto the burned-out worker: your sleep score is low, your mindfulness streak broke, try harder. At worst, it's a compliance artifact — leadership can point to the subscription while the actual drivers of burnout, understaffing and always-on culture, go untouched. Engagement rates for these apps are notoriously thin after the first weeks.
[P1.8] So the comparison is simple: one category of spending changes the conditions that produce burnout and isolation. The other gives people a nicer interface for enduring those conditions. If the goal is the outcome stated, the money goes to time and presence.
