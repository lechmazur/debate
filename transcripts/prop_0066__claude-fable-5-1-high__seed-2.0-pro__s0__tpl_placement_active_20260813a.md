[P1.1] Public life has always run on a quiet bargain: strangers can see you, but they cannot know you. You can ride a bus, attend a protest, walk into a clinic or a synagogue, and remain a face in a crowd. Live face recognition in AR glasses ends that bargain silently, at the glance of anyone who happens to be wearing them.
[P1.2] Notice what makes this different from a camera or a phone. A phone search takes effort, is visible, and leaves the target a chance to notice and object. Glasses running live recognition are frictionless, covert, and continuous. The wearer does nothing that looks like surveillance.
[P1.3] Students have already shown, with off-the-shelf glasses and a public face search engine, that you can look at a stranger and within seconds see her name, employer, and neighborhood pulled from scraped profiles. Now put that capability on the man who has been staring at a woman on the late train. She cannot opt out. She cannot even know it happened.
[P1.4] That asymmetry is the whole issue. Every other privacy protection we rely on assumes the observed person has some notice or some choice. Here the person being looked at has neither, while the person looking has everything. A rule that lets identification happen by default protects the looker; a ban protects the looked-at.
[P1.5] The looked-at are the many, and they include the vulnerable: domestic violence survivors, undercover officers, people at addiction meetings, dissidents at rallies.
[P1.6] The proposition is deliberately modest. It does not ban AR glasses, navigation, translation, or captions. It bans one function in public and carves out narrow professional uses with accountability attached, such as licensed security or law enforcement operating under a legal framework, with logs and oversight, rather than any curious pedestrian.
[P1.7] The alternative offered will be to regulate data rather than the device. But data flows are invisible; glasses on a face are the one enforceable choke point. If we cannot draw the line where the eye meets the crowd, we will not draw it anywhere.
