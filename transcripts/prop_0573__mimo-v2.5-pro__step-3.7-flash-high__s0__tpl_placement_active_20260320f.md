[P1.1] Every major music and entertainment industry shift has followed the same pattern: new distributors capture value by controlling information, and creators suffer until transparency is mandated. Streaming is simply the latest chapter.
[P1.2] The core problem is straightforward. Spotify, Netflix, Apple Music, and their peers pay creators based on metrics only the platform can see. An artist receives a royalty statement showing a total payment, but cannot verify how that number was calculated, what play counts it rests on, how many free-tier versus premium streams were counted, or whether the platform's reported payout pool matches actual subscription revenue. The creator is told to trust the very entity with a financial incentive to minimize their payment.
[P1.3] No other industry tolerates this arrangement. Radio stations report to performing rights organizations. Cable networks report to content distributors. Film studios have audited box office numbers.

Creators cannot make rational business decisions—whether to self-release or sign with a label, whether to invest in a particular platform strategy—without understanding how money flows.
[P1.6] Standardization is the critical word in this motion. Individual creators lack the negotiating leverage to demand bespoke data access. Voluntary transparency efforts, where they exist, are inconsistent, incomparable across platforms, and revocable at the platform's discretion. A standardized requirement levels the playing field.
[P1.7] It means every artist sees the same categories of data, in comparable formats, enabling cross-platform evaluation and meaningful accountability.
[P1.8] This is not a radical demand. It is the minimum condition for a functioning creative marketplace. When one party controls both the distribution channel and the accounting, mandatory transparency is the only safeguard against self-dealing.
