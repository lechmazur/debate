[P1.1] The core logic of early closing times is intuitive but wrong. Forcing venues to shut at midnight doesn't reduce how much people drink — it compresses drinking into a shorter window, creates a simultaneous exodus of intoxicated people onto streets with nowhere safe to go, and eliminates the marginal hours when sober late arrivals and moderate drinkers would normally dilute the crowd. The disorder doesn't vanish; it concentrates.
[P1.2] Transit extensions attack a different and more mechanically decisive problem: the moment a drunk person decides to get behind a wheel is almost always a transport availability problem, not a time-of-night problem. When a last train runs at midnight and the bar closes at two, that person has no legitimate option. Extend service to three, and the calculation changes immediately. The alternative exists, it's cheap, and crucially, it doesn't require any individual to make the harder choice of calling a taxi or admitting they shouldn't drive.
[P1.3] It lowers the friction on the safe choice to near zero.
[P1.4] Cities that have extended weekend transit — London's Night Tube, various North American late-night bus expansions — have observed reductions in weekend drunk-driving incidents. The mechanism is transparent: more people move through the city by transit, fewer move by private car.
[P1.5] Early closings also generate a second-order harm that transit extensions avoid entirely: displacement. When regulated, staffed, and licensed venues close early, demand doesn't dissolve — it migrates to house parties, unlicensed gatherings, and locations with no security, no bar staff trained to cut people off, and no CCTV. Disorder doesn't fall; it just moves somewhere harder to manage.
[P1.6] The comparison the motion asks us to make is ultimately about which lever is more effective. Earlier closing is a blunt restriction that compresses harm and displaces it. Transit extension is an infrastructural intervention that directly reduces the most dangerous consequence — impaired driving — while letting regulated venues do their job of containing and managing nightlife. One removes a safety valve; the other creates one.
