[C1.1] Treating gene editing like an HIV pill—where handing a generic factory a patent license makes cheap cures roll off the line [P1.7]—fundamentally misunderstands the science.
[C1.2] A gene-editing cure for sickle cell disease [P1.1] is not a chemical tablet. It is an intricate biological procedure. Patient stem cells must be harvested, edited in ultra-specialized cleanrooms, screened for dangerous off-target mutations, and re-infused after chemotherapy inside an advanced hospital. Even for emerging direct-injection therapies, the bottleneck is complex biologics manufacturing and quality control.
[C1.3] You cannot replicate a gene therapy simply by reading a patent. A compulsory license waives a legal barrier, but it cannot transfer unwritten manufacturing know-how, build cold-chain supply networks, or create transplant infrastructure in sub-Saharan Africa. Safe delivery requires voluntary technology transfer and the innovator’s hands-on cooperation—which legal coercion actively destroys.
[C1.4] Worse, this mandate breaks the fragile economics of one-time cures. Universities may fund basic discovery [P1.6], but translating a laboratory enzyme into a safe human therapy takes hundreds of millions of dollars in high-risk private capital across years of failing clinical trials. Unlike daily pills that generate decades of repeat prescriptions, a genetic cure treats a patient once and shrinks its own market.
[C1.5] Crucially, this proposition applies to countries broadly, including wealthy nations. Handing solvent governments a statutory club to force down prices [P1.4, P1.5] tells investors that on the rare occasion a risky biotech moonshot succeeds, the state will cap the return.
[C1.6] That does not turn zero sales into helpful royalties [P1.9]; it guts revenue in paying markets and drives capital away from genetic medicine. Thousands of lethal genetic diseases still have no cure in the display case at all. Compulsory licensing cannot manufacture a single complex therapy where clinical infrastructure is lacking, but it will guarantee that countless future cures are never invented.
