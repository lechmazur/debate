[P1.1] Critical minerals are not ordinary commercial commodities; they are the foundational inputs for modern defense systems, advanced semiconductors, and the green energy transition. When a firm closely tied to a rival state acquires these assets, it is not merely a routine financial transaction—it is a strategic maneuver to control the chokepoints of our industrial base. We must presumptively block these acquisitions because the cost of a false negative in national security is catastrophic.
[P1.2] First, the opacity of rival-state corporate structures makes case-by-case approval inherently dangerous. Firms tied to adversarial governments are rarely independent actors. They are subject to national intelligence laws and state directives that compel them to prioritize geopolitical leverage over shareholder returns. If a state-backed entity acquires a lithium or cobalt mine, they can subtly manipulate output, restrict refining capacity, or embed vulnerabilities into the supply chain.
[P1.3] By the time a standard review detects this weaponization, the asset is already gone, and our defense industrial base is compromised.
[P1.4] Second, a presumptive block correctly shifts the burden of proof. Under a standard review, the government must definitively prove an acquisition will cause harm—a nearly impossible task given the long-term, diffuse nature of supply chain vulnerabilities. A presumptive block forces the acquiring firm to conclusively demonstrate that the asset will remain insulated from state interference and that the host nation’s supply chain will not be degraded.
[P1.5] Finally, this policy deters predatory investment. Rival states currently use the openness of our markets to systematically buy up upstream resources, effectively outsourcing our strategic vulnerabilities. A presumptive block signals that critical mineral infrastructure is strictly off-limits to geopolitical adversaries, forcing them to seek resources elsewhere while we actively secure our own supply chains. We cannot rely on the goodwill of rivals to secure our technological future; we must secure the physical assets themselves.

National security is not measured by how many transactions we veto; it is measured by whether our defense and technology sectors can reliably source materials at scale.
[C1.5] PRO trades a speculative future vulnerability for a guaranteed present shortage. Case-by-case scrutiny with binding safeguards keeps capital flowing, diversifies sources, and builds the resilient networks that true security requires.
