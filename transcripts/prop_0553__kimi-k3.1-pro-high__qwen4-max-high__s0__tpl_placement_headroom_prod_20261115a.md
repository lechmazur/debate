[P1.1] A phone whose battery can be replaced only by its maker is not merely inconvenient; it is a private veto over ownership. India should require manufacturers of consumer electronics to supply parts, tools, and documentation on fair, reasonable terms. Voluntary promises leave repair dependent on the firm profiting from restriction.
[P1.2] Repair lock-in is a market failure. After purchase, consumers cannot bargain over a diagnostic code, compatible screen, or service manual. Withholding these inputs lets sellers control a product’s afterlife, steering owners toward costly authorised service or premature replacement. The burden falls hardest where replacement is least affordable and authorised centres are distant: rural households, students, small shops, and families extending a laptop’s life.
[P1.3] Competition at sale cannot discipline a restriction discovered years later.
[P1.4] Repair also creates public value. Longer device life means fewer discarded boards, batteries, and plastics; independent repair spreads skilled work through local workshops rather than concentrating transactions in corporate networks. A national rule gives workshops predictable access instead of voluntary portals, incomplete manuals, and changing eligibility rules.
[P1.5] CON will invoke safety, cybersecurity, and intellectual property. Those concerns justify targeted safeguards, not blanket manufacturer control. Access can exclude protected source code and sensitive security credentials, require competent handling of hazardous components, and preserve liability for botched repairs. But a battery schematic is not a trade-secret recipe, and withholding every service manual because some repairs are dangerous is prohibition disguised as caution.
[P1.6] The status quo asks consumers to trust the firm that profits when repair fails. This law reverses that incentive without guaranteeing successful repairs or abolishing warranties. India should make ownership real after checkout: devices should be discarded because they are worn out, not because their maker declares their useful life over.
