[P1.1] Toxicity on social media is not mostly a story about bad people. It is a story about what gets amplified. Ranked feeds made one editorial decision at planetary scale: show people whatever they cannot stop reacting to. That decision is the engine of today's toxicity, and it did not exist when the feed was a clock.
[P1.2] Consider the mechanism. Engagement-ranking scores a post by replies, shares, quote-posts, and time spent staring. Which posts win that contest? The ones that provoke.
[P1.3] Anger, indignation, and moral disgust produce more reaction per view than anything else humans make. So the ranking system doesn't merely tolerate outrage; it selects for it, the way a breeder selects for a trait. Facebook's own internal research, widely reported, found that its "meaningful interactions" reweighting effectively rewarded angry reactions and pushed publishers toward divisive content. Nobody planned that.
[P1.4] The optimization target did it.
[P1.5] Chronological feeds have no such selection pressure. Your feed is the people you chose, in the order they spoke. A stranger's rage-bait cannot be injected into ten million feeds because it "performs." The ceiling on any post's reach is the audience that consented to hear from the author.
[P1.6] That single constraint kills the pile-on economy: the quote-post dunk that recruits an army, the deliberately offensive take posted precisely because backlash is distribution.
[P1.7] It also changes the creators. Users learn what the machine rewards and become it. Under a clock, the winning strategy is to be worth following. Under a ranking system, the winning strategy is to be impossible to scroll past.
[P1.8] Much of the sneering, bad-faith, context-collapsed style now called "posting" is an adaptation to that incentive, and it persists across platforms because the incentive does.
[P1.9] The motion is comparative. Chronological feeds had spam, trolls, and volume problems. Those are noise. Ranked feeds took the noise and built a machine that finds the cruelest signal and broadcasts it.
[P1.10] Less amplification of provocation means less toxicity. That is the debate.
