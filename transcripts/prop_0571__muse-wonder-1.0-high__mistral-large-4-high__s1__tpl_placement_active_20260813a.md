[P1.1] Sport promises that the better team, not the luckier official, decides the result. That promise fails when a human is asked to judge, in a split second and from one angle, whether a ball crossed a line, a foot was offside, or a shot clipped the paint. Those are not matters of interpretation. They have a right answer, and leagues should use the tools that get it right.
[P1.2] Semi-automated officiating means sensors, ball-tracking, and limb-tracking make objective calls first — goal-line decisions, in-or-out, offside positions, shot-clock and boundary violations — while officials retain a limited override for malfunction, occlusion, or an anomalous play. Judgment calls like foul severity, intent, and game management stay human. This is not replacing referees; it is relieving them of tasks human eyes are worst at.
[P1.3] First, accuracy. A winger at full sprint, a 100-mph serve, or a crowded goalmouth hides the decisive inch from even an elite official, whose view can be blocked. Tracking systems sample positions many times a second from multiple angles and apply the same geometry every time. They will not be perfect, but perfection is the wrong test — the question is whether they err less than a person guessing through bodies.
[P1.4] Second, fairness. Human calls drift with fatigue, crowd pressure, player reputation, and inconsistency between crews. One match gets a tight offside standard, the next gets a loose one. Automated measurement applies identical criteria to home and away, star and substitute, first minute and last.
[P1.5] That consistency is not sterile; it is equal treatment.
[P1.6] Third, this improves, rather than interrupts, the spectacle. Instant line calls in tennis replaced argument and challenge delays with a quick, accepted answer. Semi-automated offside can similarly cut the long video freeze that fans hate: detect, verify, play on.
[P1.7] Fans can accept defeat by an opponent. They should not have to accept defeat by an avoidable misperception when the correct answer was measurable all along.
