[P1.1] Living together before marriage is not a retreat from commitment; it is commitment practiced in its most demanding form. When two people share a home, they share rent, chores, bad moods, and the unglamorous logistics of daily existence. That is a far deeper test of dedication than any ceremony conducted before the lease is signed. To treat cohabitation as a sign of weak commitment is to confuse the container with the contents—to assume that a marriage license adds loyalty that daily shared life cannot.
[P1.2] In practice, cohabitation functions as a rational compatibility check. Couples who live together learn how they fight, how they handle money under stress, and whether their visions of home actually align before making a lifelong legal vow. This is not hesitation; it is prudence. Partners who cohabit thoughtfully enter marriage with their eyes open, having already navigated the practical realities that break so many unions, which tends to produce more stable marriages rather than weaker ones.
[P1.3] Moreover, cohabitation has already become the socially ordinary path. In many societies, most couples now live together before marrying, if they marry at all. Treating this majority arrangement as morally suspect or commitment-averse imposes an arbitrary stigma on relationships that are often deeply serious. The relevant question is not whether the couple shares an address, but whether they are building a life together with mutual respect, shared finances, and aligned futures.
[P1.4] The alternative view clings to a purely symbolic definition of commitment—one that locates devotion in a ring or a legal document rather than in the daily choice to share a life. But commitment is demonstrated in the mundane: in whose name is on the utility bill, in who cares for the other during illness, in the thousand small negotiations of living under one roof. Far from signaling weak commitment, cohabitation is one of its most honest and tested expressions, and treating it as ordinary simply acknowledges how people actually build lasting partnerships.
