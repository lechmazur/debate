[P1.2] Those three are not lifestyle extras. Sleep is how a developing brain consolidates memory, mood, and growth. Boredom is the condition in which imagination and self-control appear. Free play is how children practice risk, motor skill, and social repair without a feed.
[P1.3] Digital wellbeing is not a rotating list of approved platforms. It is whether a child’s day still contains enough of those three things.
[P1.4] App-by-app advice fails for structural reasons. Products change faster than handouts. Children migrate. Parents become the night-shift moderator of software designed to evade them.
[P1.5] The fight then mutates into control versus autonomy, which is the wrong conflict. “No this app” sounds like a power struggle. “Your brain needs sleep and an afternoon without a screen” sounds like health, the same category as food and vaccines.

[P1.7] They cannot police every bedroom app, and they should stop pretending they can.
[P1.8] The alternative keeps families in infinite skirmishes over moving targets. Our frame gives them a finite scoreboard: Did they sleep? Were they bored? Did they play?
