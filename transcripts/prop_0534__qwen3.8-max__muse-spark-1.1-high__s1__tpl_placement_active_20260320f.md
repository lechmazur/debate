[P1.1] The question is not whether crypto crime should be punished; it is whether a person must ask permission to control their own property. A self-custody wallet is not a bank, exchange, or payment processor. It is a way for an individual to hold cryptographic keys and therefore control assets without relying on a gatekeeper. That makes it closer to carrying cash, keeping a safe, or holding bearer instruments than to operating a money-transmission business.
[P1.2] The default legal position should be: people may own and use lawful tools until their own conduct creates a concrete criminal nexus.
[P1.3] This default matters because self-custody protects ordinary autonomy in ways opponents routinely undervalue. An abuse survivor may need savings an abuser cannot freeze. A dissident, journalist, or minority group may need funds outside a system that can be abused by local officials or hostile institutions. A freelancer in a country with failing banks may need stable-value assets without opening a foreign bank account.
[P1.4] These are not exotic cases; they are the reason permissionless control over value exists. A blanket restriction would punish all of them for the acts of a few.
[P1.5] The opposing case cannot win by pointing generally to “crime.” Every tool with privacy—cash, encrypted messaging, shell companies, even postal mail—can be abused. The law’s answer is normally to investigate specific wrongdoing, not to outlaw the tool itself. Self-custody wallets do not become criminal merely because a criminal might use one. If a wallet is used to launder money, pay ransomware, evade sanctions, or finance violence, prosecutors can target that conduct, seize the illicit assets, and pursue the people involved.
[P1.6] But if the state can restrict self-custody on vague risk-based categories, mere transaction patterns, or generalized suspicion, it replaces due process with preemptive control. The decisive line is direct tie to specific criminal conduct. That line protects lawful users while still allowing enforcement.
