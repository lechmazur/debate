[P1.1] Always-on AI productivity companions—tools that monitor, prompt, or coach workers nonstop through the workday—are a fundamental threat to fair, humane work. Prohibiting their mandatory use is not anti-innovation; it is a necessary guardrail against employer overreach that erodes worker autonomy, distorts work quality, enables invasive, unaccountable surveillance, and strips workers of any voice in how their performance is evaluated, even when that evaluation is flawed or biased.
[P1.2] These tools eliminate the small, necessary gaps in the workday that let workers think, adapt, and recover. A software developer forced to use an AI companion that nags them to cut corners when they stop to troubleshoot a complex bug, or a warehouse worker disciplined for "time off task" when their AI scanner glitches, loses the ability to do thoughtful, careful work. Constant prompting disrupts deep, focused work that requires sustained concentration, as workers are pulled away from complex tasks every time the AI issues a nudge.
[P1.3] The AI optimizes for speed and surface-level metrics, not actual output quality or worker well-being.
[P1.4] The always-on monitoring these companions require collects deeply invasive, often biased data: keystroke logs, tone of voice, even facial expressions, all used to score workers without human context. AI coaching systems, trained on historical employer performance data that often reflects past discriminatory practices, amplify existing biases, penalizing workers who take time to assist customers, accommodate disabled colleagues, or follow safety protocols that slow down output—without any way for workers to push back on unfair, inaccurate assessments.
[P1.5] Mandatory use removes any meaningful worker choice. Refusal to opt in is almost always grounds for termination or demotion, making the coercion structural, not voluntary. A prohibition restores a basic baseline: workers should not be forced to surrender their discretion, privacy, and ability to do good work to an algorithm that only answers to their employer’s bottom line, with no accountability for harm caused by its errors.
