[P1.1] Two facts should anchor everything that follows. First: Japan and South Korea are energy islands. Neither has meaningful domestic fossil resources, neither can plug into a continental grid, and both import the overwhelming majority of their energy by ship—much of it through chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz and past the Taiwan Strait.
[P1.2] Second: when Japan shut its reactors after Fukushima, the replacement was not wind and solar. It was imported coal and LNG, at ruinous cost to both the climate and the trade balance. That is the real-world counterfactual, and it decides this debate.
[P1.3] The case for restart and expansion rests on three pillars.
[P1.4] Security. Uranium is energy-dense, easy to stockpile for years, and sourced from stable partners like Canada and Australia. A country running nuclear plants can ride out a shipping crisis; a country running LNG turbines cannot. After Russia invaded Ukraine, both nations watched gas prices spike and competed with Europe for cargoes.
[P1.5] Nuclear is the only scalable technology that converts that structural vulnerability into resilience.
[P1.6] Emissions. Nuclear's lifecycle carbon footprint is comparable to wind and far below gas. Japan's emissions rose measurably when its fleet went offline. Every idle reactor restarted displaces fossil generation almost one-for-one—the cheapest, fastest decarbonization available anywhere, because the plants already exist.
[P1.7] Feasibility. This is not speculative technology. South Korea builds reactors on time and on budget—its APR-1400 units in the UAE are running today. Japan's restarted reactors operate under post-Fukushima standards among the strictest in the world.
[P1.8] Meanwhile, both countries face hard geographic limits on renewables: mountainous terrain, scarce flat land, typhoons, and no neighbors to balance intermittency.
[P1.9] To win, the opposition must show that leaving safe, already-built reactors idle—while burning imported gas instead—makes these nations safer and cleaner. That position defies both arithmetic and experience. Restart what exists; build what's proven.
