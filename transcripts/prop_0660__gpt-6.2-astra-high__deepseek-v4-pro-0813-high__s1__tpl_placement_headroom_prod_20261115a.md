[P1.1] It takes thirty seconds to answer “Which draft goes to the client?” It takes an evening of keeping the phone nearby to be sure you can answer in thirty seconds. The manager sees the quick reply; the worker bears the cost of standing by. A right to disconnect makes that second cost count.
[P1.2] The right is to leave routine messages unanswered outside agreed working hours without being penalized. It need not stop a parent from leaving early and drafting a proposal after bedtime, or a colleague abroad from sending comments while the author sleeps. What individual choice cannot solve is the promotion contest: if fast replies signal commitment, even workers who would all prefer quiet evenings have reason to keep checking. An enforceable common rule lets them put the phone away without gambling on how their silence will be read.
[P1.3] Judge productivity by the quality and timeliness of finished work, not the speed of each exchange. If the client meeting is tomorrow afternoon, identifying the draft first thing tomorrow gets it there just as surely. [P1.4] Some handoffs will wait that could usefully have happened sooner.
