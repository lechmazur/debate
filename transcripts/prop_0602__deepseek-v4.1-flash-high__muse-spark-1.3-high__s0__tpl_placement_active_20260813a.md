[P1.1] The proposition is modest: mobile computing should not be a permissioned kingdom owned by two gatekeepers. Under baseline security standards—code signing, malware scanning, sandboxing, permission prompts, and rapid revocation—governments should require app stores and mobile operating systems to permit third-party payment systems and sideloading. That is the choice: regulated openness versus private control.
[P1.2] First, the status quo is not a security model; it is a toll booth. Apple and Google take a commission on many digital transactions, restrict external payment links, and can reject apps that compete with their own services. When the same company writes the rules, runs the store, and sells rival products, security becomes a convenient shield for exclusion. Consumers pay through higher prices; developers pay through forced fees.
[P1.3] Second, security and openness are not opposites. General-purpose computers, banks, browsers, and businesses run third-party software every day. Android already permits sideloading; desktop operating systems have for decades. Risks are real but manageable with baseline standards: signed code, sandboxing, user consent, malware review, and revocation.
[P1.4] If a specific app is dangerous, regulate that app—not an entire channel of distribution.
[P1.5] Third, the benefits are concrete: lower fees, competitive payment options, cheaper subscriptions, independent stores, and apps gatekeepers reject for business rather than safety reasons—cloud gaming, alternative browsers, wallets, and niche tools. Competition pressures Apple and Google to improve privacy, price, and service.
[P1.6] The opponent will warn of chaos and malware. But the motion does not demand a lawless open web; it demands baseline protection while ending an unaccountable chokehold. This is not a mandate to abolish vetting; it is a mandate to stop vetting from becoming a veto over competitors. The real security threat is concentrated control over what billions can install and pay.
[P1.7] Open the gate, keep the guardrails.
