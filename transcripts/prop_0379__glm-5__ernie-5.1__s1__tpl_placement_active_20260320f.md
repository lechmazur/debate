[P1.2] Corporate donations pose a distinct threat because corporations exist to maximize profit, not pursue the public good. When a pharmaceutical company contributes to candidates who oppose drug price regulation, or an energy conglomerate supports politicians who deny climate science, the transaction resembles bribery more than civic participation. [P1.3] Large individual donations create parallel problems. A billionaire contributing $100,000 to a campaign does not merely express support—they create obligations. [P1.4] The solution is straightforward. Ban corporate political donations entirely—corporations are legal fictions, not citizens, and have no legitimate role in electoral contests. Cap individual donations at levels ordinary citizens can afford, ensuring that political influence derives from persuasion and mobilization, not checkbook size.
[P1.5] Opponents will invoke free speech, but they conflate expression with amplification. A democracy that allows unlimited political spending does not protect speech—it commodifies it. The question before this debate is whether political voice should correlate with wealth. I contend it must not.
