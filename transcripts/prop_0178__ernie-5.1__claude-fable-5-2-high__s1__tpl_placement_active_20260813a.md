[P1.1] Population health is shaped more by what people eat than by what they know about what they eat. That distinction is the entire case. Reformulation mandates restructure the food supply so that every loaf of bread, every can of soup, every breakfast cereal contains less salt, less sugar, less harmful fat — regardless of who buys it. Labeling and education campaigns, by contrast, ask millions of individuals to read, interpret, and act on information at the point of purchase, every single day, against the full force of marketing budgets engineered to override that information.
[P1.2] The evidence on labeling is sobering. Front-of-pack traffic-light schemes and calorie counts have existed for years in multiple countries, yet obesity and diet-related disease rates continue climbing. Why? Because knowing a product is high in sugar does not stop someone buying it when it is cheap, convenient, and heavily promoted.
[P1.3] Knowledge is necessary but catastrophically insufficient when the obesogenic environment remains unchanged.
[P1.4] Reformulation flips the logic. The UK's voluntary and then mandatory salt reduction program cut average population salt intake by roughly a fifth over a decade — not because people chose to eat less salt, but because manufacturers were required to put less in. Trans fat bans produced measurable drops in cardiovascular events. These are passive, population-wide gains that education campaigns simply cannot replicate because they depend on active, sustained individual compliance.
[P1.5] The critical point is equity. Education campaigns disproportionately fail among low-income, low-literacy, and time-pressed populations — precisely the groups bearing the greatest disease burden. Mandates reach everyone equally, including those who never read a label, never see a campaign, and have the least power to alter their food choices.
[P1.6] The question is not whether education has value. It is whether it can outperform a structural intervention that removes the hazard from the food itself. It cannot.
