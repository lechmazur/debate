[P1.1] A seven-year-old needs constant supervision because they cannot assess danger. A sixteen-year-old must learn to assess danger, and that learning is impossible without room to practice. A teenager who knows every text is read does not become more innocent, only more censored and more secretive — second accounts, deleted chats, coded language. Surveillance does not remove risk; it removes the parent from the conversation where risk could be discussed.
[P1.3] Freedom of movement works the same way. Navigating a neighborhood, taking the bus, meeting friends without an escort, managing time and return, is how judgment, street sense, and social competence are built. A teen kept under constant tracking until 18 does not arrive at adulthood safer; they arrive inexperienced, then face full freedom with zero practice.
[P1.4] Constant tracking promises safety but delivers false security. Location dots do not teach a teen to leave a bad party, resist pressure, or call home without fear. [P1.5] The proposal is not abandonment, but a growing right. Early adolescence: more check-ins, known whereabouts, guidance on phones.
