[P1.1] Sea level rise is not a storm to be weathered; it is a permanent change in the boundary conditions of human settlement. That distinction decides this debate. Hard defenses are an answer to episodic threats. Against a sea that rises every year for centuries, a wall is not protection—it is a subscription with escalating fees and a catastrophic cancellation clause.
[P1.2] The core comparison is simple: safety per dollar over time. Managed retreat is a one-time cost that eliminates risk permanently. A seawall is a recurring cost that merely defers risk—and concentrates it. Every levee invites development behind it, so when it fails (and engineers concede every defense has a design exceedance), the losses are vastly larger than if people had never been encouraged to stay.
[P1.3] New Orleans is the textbook case: decades of flood walls created a bowl of vulnerability that Katrina converted into mass death and over a hundred billion dollars in damage. Walls don't remove risk; they store it, with interest.
[P1.4] Wealthy countries are precisely the ones that can do retreat well: compensate homeowners fairly, rebuild communities inland, and phase the transition over decades rather than fleeing after disaster. Buyout programs in the U.S. and planned relocation efforts from Wales to Louisiana show the model exists. The alternative—armoring thousands of kilometers indefinitely—is fiscally absurd even for rich states, which is why in practice "hard defenses" means protecting wealthy enclaves while everyone else gets neither walls nor exit money. Retreat is the more equitable policy, not the crueler one.
[P1.5] Finally, hard defenses destroy the coast they claim to save. Seawalls cause "coastal squeeze," drowning the wetlands and beaches that are themselves the best natural buffers. Retreat restores those buffers.
[P1.6] Note what the motion asks: which should we *prioritize*. Even the Dutch, the world's best engineers of water, now make room for rivers and the sea. When the masters of the dike are stepping back, the burden falls on those insisting we wall forward forever.
