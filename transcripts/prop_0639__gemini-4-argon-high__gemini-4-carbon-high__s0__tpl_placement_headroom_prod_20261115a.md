[P1.1] Advisory citizens' assemblies have become the ultimate exercise in democratic theater. Governments convene a representative cross-section of ordinary people, ask them to spend months deliberating with experts, praise their civic dedication, and then quietly shelve every recommendation that inconveniences party donors or short-term electoral math. We have seen this repeatedly in national climate assemblies, where hard-won citizen consensus was systematically watered down or ignored by ministries. Without binding authority, sortition is merely a decorative fig leaf that lets politicians stage consultation while hoarding actual power.
[P1.2] Binding decision-making on specific policy questions is essential because electoral politics suffers from fatal conflicts of interest and structural myopia. Elected officials cannot be trusted to draw their own electoral maps, write their own campaign finance laws, or police their own ethics. Likewise, the relentless pressure of the next election paralyzes parliaments on generational challenges like decarbonization or long-term fiscal sustainability, where necessary upfront costs are politically toxic. Randomly selected citizens do not answer to party whips, court wealthy lobbyists, or fear losing office.
[P1.3] Freed from the pathology of permanent campaigning, a deliberative microcosm of the public can actually follow the evidence and make tough, public-spirited trade-offs.
[P1.4] Furthermore, binding power transforms both the rigor of deliberation and public trust. When assembly members know their final vote carries the force of law, they confront legal and budgetary realities with immense gravity. Conversely, asking citizens to sacrifice months of weekends for a glorified focus group, only to have a minister cherry-pick the easy ideas and bury the rest, breeds deep cynicism.
[P1.5] Granting binding authority on specific questions does not abolish legislatures; it rescues democracy precisely where partisan incentives break down. Democratic legitimacy requires trusting informed citizens with real sovereign power, not handing them a suggestion box.
