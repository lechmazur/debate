[P1.1] Climate policy only works when it makes pollution cost. A high carbon price does exactly that: every ton of CO2 costs everywhere, for everyone. That unleashes thousands of cheapest-first cuts the government could never list - the commuter who carpools, the landlord who insulates, the airline that cuts empty flights, the factory that reoptimizes heat. Green industrial subsidies do the opposite.
[P1.2] They make one politically chosen product cheaper - batteries this year, hydrogen next - while leaving the price of polluting at zero. So you push clean energy uphill while dirty energy rolls downhill for free. The SUV still drives more because gas is cheap.
[P1.3] Returning all the revenue as an equal per-capita dividend solves the only serious objection to pricing and makes it an equity win. Carbon pricing looks regressive only if you keep the money. If you divide it equally, it becomes strongly progressive, because emissions rise with income. A low-income family with a below-average footprint pays a little more in prices and gets back far more in the dividend.
[P1.4] The wealthy frequent flyers pay net, the majority gains net. Compare that to industrial subsidies. Who gets a check for a new electric car or a home solar array? Homeowners with high tax liability.
[P1.5] Who gets the factory retrofit grant? Shareholders. It's upward redistribution in green packaging.
[P1.6] And subsidy pots are a lobbyist magnet. Government has to pick winners, technologies, firms, and regions under intense political pressure. It will inevitably overfund the connected and miss the disruptive. Dividend needs no committee, no application, no champion.
[P1.7] It's an automatic price signal and an automatic check, like Alaska's oil dividend. That creates a durable majority who see money each quarter and will defend the policy. Subsidies are invisible to voters and first on the chopping block.
[P1.8] Only a price covers the whole economy - including conservation and efficiency - without pretending bureaucrats know the future.
