[P1.1] Corporate net-zero claims built primarily on carbon offsets are not climate action — they are climate accounting. The proposition is straightforward: if a company claims to be "net-zero," the bulk of that claim must come from cutting emissions inside its own value chain, not from paying someone else to plant trees or preserve forest elsewhere.
[P1.2] The core problem is incentives. Offsets are cheap relative to real operational transformation. When a company can neutralize a ton of CO₂ for a few dollars by purchasing credits rather than investing millions to retool supply chains, electrify fleets, or switch to clean process heat, the rational financial choice is to keep emitting and buy paper. A legally limited offset share forces the expensive, structurally important work to actually happen.
[P1.3] Then there is quality. The offset market has persistent, well-documented problems with additionality, permanence, and leakage. Many forestry credits have been issued for forests that were never at real risk of clearing. Projects over-claim baselines.
[P1.4] Carbon stored in trees is reversible in a single wildfire season. When a net-zero claim rests on these instruments, it communicates certainty that the underlying credits cannot support. An in-value-chain ton eliminated is permanent and additional by definition; an offset ton rarely is.
[P1.5] Allowing a small share preserves flexibility for genuinely hard-to-abate emissions — residual process emissions in cement or agriculture where no viable elimination technology yet exists. The proposition does not ban offsets; it disciplines them. It treats them as a complement to transformation, not a substitute for it.
[P1.6] Without legal limits, voluntary frameworks have not worked. Companies face enormous pressure to publish impressive climate pledges, and almost no pressure to ensure those pledges reflect physical emissions reductions. Regulation is the mechanism that closes the gap between the claim and the reality. The proposition forces net-zero to mean what it says.
