[P1.1] Standardized IQ-style tests operate as the silent gatekeepers of modern meritocracy. From university admissions to corporate screening, these assessments hold an outsized influence over human opportunity. Yet, the foundational premise of this reliance is broken: the cognitive band these tests measure is far too narrow to justify their role as our primary sorting mechanisms.
[P1.2] IQ-style tests—and their structural proxies in academic and employment screening—are optimized to measure a highly specific, sterile subset of mental processing. They evaluate abstract pattern recognition, rote mathematical reasoning, and verbal recall under artificial time constraints. While these are cognitive skills, they are not the sum total of human intelligence.
[P1.3] Crucially, they fail to capture the exact cognitive faculties required for real-world excellence: divergent thinking, adaptive problem-solving, cognitive flexibility, and executive functioning. A multiple-choice exam cannot measure a person’s ability to synthesize conflicting data, navigate complex collaborative environments, or persevere through long-term, ambiguous projects.
[P1.4] This narrow focus creates a destructive mismatch. By treating a single, narrow dimension of cognitive ability as a proxy for general capability, institutions systematically filter out invaluable talent. Excellent innovators, resilient managers, and deep, non-linear thinkers are sorted out before they ever reach the door because they did not optimize for a timed puzzle.
[P1.5] Meanwhile, we over-select for individuals who excel at standardized test-taking but may lack the practical intelligence to execute complex tasks.
[P1.6] The current system relies on IQ-style testing not because it is accurate, but because it is cheap and easy to scale. We have sacrificed human potential on the altar of administrative convenience. Because these tests capture only a sliver of cognitive capacity, using them to decide who gets educated and who gets hired is both intellectually indefensible and economically self-defeating.
