[P1.1] A university enriched by slavery, segregation, or dispossession is not a bystander to history; it is a live beneficiary. Its endowment did not vanish when the injustice ended. Georgetown stayed solvent by selling enslaved people; land-grant colleges were endowed with western land seized from Native nations; Southern public universities took tax dollars while excluding Black students. That wealth was invested and compounded.
[P1.2] The modern institution still lives on income from assets it should never have held.
[P1.3] Acknowledgment leaves that balance sheet untouched. A plaque or renamed building costs warmth, not wealth; it lets a university feel absolved while keeping the full financial upside. It asks the harmed to accept recognition instead of capacity. Apologizing after taking your land and keeping the rent does not restore you.
[P1.4] Truth telling is necessary, but without material redress it is reputation management, not repair.
[P1.5] The remedy must match the injury. Long-term scholarships convert diverted opportunity into durable opportunity: not a one-time check, but permanent pathways for descendants and for students from communities the university impoverished. Community investment returns a share of the land's value to neighborhoods the campus displaced or neglected—housing, hiring, health, schools. This is not punishment of today's students or faculty.
[P1.6] It is spending income from tainted principal on the public goods a university exists to provide.
[P1.7] The objection will be that the past is past and current students are innocent. But this is not inherited guilt; it is beneficiary responsibility. A university that knowingly retains and invests ill-gotten assets owes disgorgement. The past is not past while its proceeds still earn.
[P1.8] The test is not whether an institution feels sorry, but whether people still locked out by that history gain real access. If acknowledgment does not change who can study, heal, and build wealth in the university's shadow, it is insufficient; only long-term scholarships and community investment begin to close the gap.

[C1.6] Today's students did not extract the wealth. A university's live duty is present-tense: need-based access, open research, fair local partnership. Those already return public goods. They do not require a genealogical surcharge.
[C1.7] If compounded proceeds from old injustice still "earn," so does every city, church, and hospital. Justice that cannot draw a line is not disgorgement. It is an unbounded claim on the living.
