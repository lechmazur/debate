[P1.2] Climate talks determine survival itself. For Tuvalu or the Maldives, centimeters of sea-level rise mean permanent loss of land, culture, and sovereignty. For larger emitters the same rise means managed retreat or engineering. Existential risk is unequal.
[P1.3] Treating states as if population or economic output captures the stake is a category error: it confuses how many people live somewhere with how close that place stands to vanishing.
[P1.4] Existing rules amplify high-emission economies that created the crisis while muting the communities extinguished first. [P1.5] This is not charity or special pleading. It is the only decision rule that aligns power with the stakes climate governance claims to address. Without it, consensus stays hostage to actors who have time, space, and money to wait. With it, urgency enters the procedure itself.
[P1.6] Justice requires that those whose existence hangs in the balance control a weight matching that hanging. Equal formal status never equaled equal skin in the game. Risk does. Grant the islands voting power proportional to what they stand to lose forever.
