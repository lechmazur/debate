[P1.1] Biometric mass surveillance treats every citizen as a perpetual suspect, continuously identifying, tracking, and analyzing innocent people without cause or consent. It must be presumptively unlawful, permitted only through narrowly defined emergency authorization.
[P1.2] The harm begins with the chilling of fundamental freedoms. When people know their faces are scanned, matched, and logged in real time, they alter their behavior. [P1.3] The burden falls disproportionately on marginalized communities. Face recognition systems misidentify people of color at significantly higher rates, subjecting them to repeated stops, interrogations, and the risk of wrongful arrest. [P1.5] The motion’s emergency authorization process provides the necessary safeguard. It confines extraordinary surveillance to genuine, time-bound crises with rigorous oversight, preventing the normalization of a surveillance state. Presumptive unlawfulness is not a ban on safety tools. It is a commitment that in a free society, the default is liberty, and the exception is closely monitored power.
