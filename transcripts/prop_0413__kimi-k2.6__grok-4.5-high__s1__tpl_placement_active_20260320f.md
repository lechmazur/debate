[P1.1] Every adult citizen can obtain their own birth certificate, learn their family medical history, and know where they came from unless they were adopted. That single exception is a holdover from an era when adoption was shrouded in shame. It is time to recognize that your own origins are not state property to be sealed away forever.
[P1.2] The core issue is simple: an adult has a fundamental interest in knowing their own identity. Adoption records contain a person's genetic history, nationality, and sometimes critical health information. A young adult who discovers a heritable cardiac condition or needs a bone marrow match should not be blocked by a bureaucrat's stamp from decades ago. For every other adult, family medical history is routine; forcing adoptees to navigate opaque bureaucracy or hire private detectives turns equal citizens into petitioners begging for their own biology.
[P1.3] The opposition will invoke birth parent privacy. But privacy in this context means the power to erase oneself from another person's life permanently. Birth parents surrendered parental rights; they did not acquire a lifelong veto over the adult child's right to self knowledge. The original records pertain to the adoptee's birth, their story, not merely someone else's secret.
[P1.4] Moreover, the social conditions that once justified secrecy have faded. Unwed motherhood no longer carries the crushing stigma of past decades. Continuing to seal these records permanently protects outdated shame at the cost of adult autonomy.
[P1.5] Equality before the law means nothing if some adults must spend years and money to learn their own names. The right to access your own origins is not a favor to be granted; it is a dignity that should be recognized. Let us end the last legal form of inherited secrecy and grant adoptees the same ownership of their past that every other citizen enjoys.
