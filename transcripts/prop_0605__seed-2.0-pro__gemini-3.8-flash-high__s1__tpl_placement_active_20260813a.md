[C1.1] PRO envisions a frictionless transition to an open labour market, but ignores the foundational reality of Gulf economies. In these states, foreign nationals comprise the vast majority of the private workforce, and statutory employer obligations—including funding cross-border recruitment, visas, airfare, healthcare, and housing—constitute the primary social safety net. Replacing this system with entirely portable, worker-held visas that permit unilateral job switching without employer consent triggers catastrophic structural fallout.
[C1.2] First, it destroys recruitment economics. Sponsoring an overseas worker requires substantial upfront investment. If workers can abandon their posts immediately upon arrival without employer permission or cost restitution, it creates an acute free-rider crisis. Well-capitalized conglomerates will simply poach workers whose flights, training, and bureaucratic fees were financed by small and medium businesses.
[C1.3] To hedge against this poaching risk, employers will inevitably slash base wages, halt formal hiring, or covertly shift recruitment fees onto workers, deepening the exact financial desperation PRO decries.
[C1.4] Second, eliminating sponsorship dismantles institutional accountability. Under the sponsorship framework, employers remain legally and financially responsible for worker welfare, municipal registration, and repatriation. If visas become entirely unanchored and worker-held, who provides healthcare, shelter, and return flights during economic downturns? In demographically fragile states where citizens are a minority, severing residency from employer sponsorship will not birth a thriving workforce; it will generate an unprotected, informal precariat of day-labourers vulnerable to severe underemployment and municipal neglect.
[C1.5] Abuses like wage withholding and passport confiscation must be eradicated, but the solution is aggressive state enforcement—such as mandatory digital Wage Protection Systems and swift legal release mechanisms when contracts are breached. We do not protect workers by incentivizing employers to abandon all duty of care, nor can the Gulf build sustainable, modern economies on the foundation of unregulated labour instability.
