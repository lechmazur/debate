[P1.1] When a downturn hits, private sector demand evaporates overnight. Automatic fiscal stabilizers—progressive tax adjustments, unemployment insurance, means-tested safety net payouts—activate immediately, with zero legislative lag, to replace lost household income. Even when public debt ratios are elevated, expanding these stabilizers is the only way to prevent a temporary downturn from becoming a self-reinforcing depression. Austerity-driven cuts to stabilizers shrink GDP faster than they reduce debt stocks, driving debt-to-GDP ratios higher, not lower.
[P1.2] This dynamic is not theoretical. Economies that slashed stabilizer spending during past downturns saw extensions of jobless spells, sharper falls in consumption, and ultimately larger debt burdens than peers that maintained or expanded automatic support. Discretionary stimulus packages cannot match the speed or targeting of built-in stabilizers: they are bogged down by legislative gridlock, pork-barrel spending, and delayed rollout. Cutting stabilizers to "protect" debt ratios is a false economy that destroys the very GDP base that makes debt sustainable.
[P1.3] Elevated debt is a stock problem; downturns are a flow crisis. When interest rates on sovereign debt are near historic lows during recessions, the cost of funding expanded stabilizer spending is negligible compared to the cost of a collapsed tax base. Stabilizers preserve household purchasing power, keep small businesses afloat, and ensure that when recovery comes, the workforce and tax base are intact to support debt reduction. Surrendering stabilizers to debt fears only guarantees that debt ratios will climb further as the economy contracts.
