[P1.1] Democratic resilience rests on citizens who can separate a valid inference from a seductive one and a real signal from noise. That capacity is built by formal logic, probability, and statistical reasoning begun in primary school—not by any curriculum that mainly trains people to scrutinize messengers, formats, and bylines.
[P1.2] Media literacy, however carefully designed, directs attention to the channel. It can mark a dubious outlet or a missing caveat. It cannot show whether the claim follows, how far new evidence should shift belief, or whether a striking number is an artifact of base rates, sampling, or selective presentation. Those are the ordinary judgments of democratic life: a policy effect, a health warning, an election allegation, an economic forecast.
[P1.3] Logic and probability make them possible. Media literacy presupposes them and cannot stand in for them. Without those tools, source-checking collapses into tribal credentialism or blanket cynicism—both corrosive to self-government.
[P1.4] Primary school is the right starting point because reasoning habits compound and motivated cognition arrives early. A child who grasps that “some” is not “all,” that coincidence is not causation, and that rare events occur routinely in large populations acquires protections no later checklist can retrofit. Waiting for a secondary “fake news” unit leaves the architecture of judgment unbuilt, then asks slogans to do the work of structure.
[P1.5] The comparison is decisive. Even the best media-literacy program stays narrow, reactive, and readily politicized—more apt to teach which voices to discount than how to test a claim. Instruction in logic, probability, and statistics is general, cumulative, and harder to capture: it applies equally to a viral clip, a ministry report, a scientific paper, and a campaign speech. A public that can reason can survive bad media.
[P1.6] A public fluent only in media criticism, yet unable to handle valid argument or uncertainty, cannot survive complexity. Early, rigorous teaching of these subjects therefore does more for democratic resilience than any media-literacy curriculum.

Democratic fragility today is not mainly a deficit of syllogistic hygiene; it is the predictable product of motivated reasoning, identity threat, attention platforms engineered for arousal, and collapsed trust in mediating institutions. [P1.2] complains that media literacy "directs attention to the channel," but that is precisely where modern manipulation happens, in feeds, shares, and amplification incentives, not just inside a proposition.
[C1.2] Early formal training also transfers weakly; classroom mastery of quantifier scope or Bayes' theorem rarely survives contact with a partisan clip at 11 p.m. Worse, the tools can be weaponized. "Correlation is not causation" or "some, not all" often becomes a rhetorical squirt gun: a way to look rigorous while dismissing inconvenient facts. Give people sharper deduction and many will construct sturdier defenses for conclusions they reached for tribal reasons.
[C1.3] [P1.5] concedes media literacy is "readily politicized"; so is logic once people believe their side owns reason.
[C1.4] Media literacy matters more because it meets the failure point. A truncated chart, a decontextualized clip, a bot-amplified rumor, or a conflict-of-interest op-ed are caught by asking who benefits, what is missing, where the original lives, and whether independent sources corroborate. Those checks are actionable across ages, even before fractions are solid, and they map onto how misinformation actually spreads. A curriculum can later absorb base-rate and sampling ideas inside those checks.
[C1.5] Because the motion claims "more than any media-literacy curriculum," it fails. Reason training is one narrow input; applied media literacy can include it while also addressing platforms, evidence contexts, verification habits, and the social incentives that make bad claims circulate. A child drilled in argument form can still share a hoax she emotionally wants. A child taught to trace provenance and pause before amplifying is doing resilience's daily work.
