[P1.1] Always-on location tracking and reading a teenager's private messages as the default household rule treats every teenager as a suspect first and a developing person second. That presumption is backwards, and it makes families less safe, not more.
[P1.2] Adolescence is when young people learn judgment by exercising it — deciding where to go, who to trust, and how to handle a painful conversation without an adult silently auditing every word. Privacy is not a reward for perfection; it is the space where identity, conscience, and independent problem-solving form. Continuous surveillance replaces internal judgment with external compliance. The teen behaves not because she understands risk, but because she knows she is being watched — a lesson that evaporates the moment she gets a second account, a friend's phone, or leaves for college.
[P1.3] It also corrodes the channel that actually protects teenagers: voluntary disclosure. A teen who expects every message to be inspected will not stop having risky conversations; she will move them, delete them, or stop having honest ones at home. Friends will stop confiding in her, and she will stop bringing problems to parents who have signaled that curiosity outranks trust. Parents gain a stream of location dots and out-of-context texts while losing the early, candid warning — “something felt weird tonight” — that prevents real harm.
[P1.4] None of this denies danger. It denies that blanket surveillance is a proportionate answer to unspecified danger. A check-in when plans change, location sharing for a late drive or unfamiliar trip, agreed rules about meeting online contacts, and direct conversation target risk without confiscating privacy. Default monitoring also sweeps in other children's private disclosures and creates stored records that can be misread, leaked, or held over a teen in conflict.
[P1.5] That is why the exception matters: with a specific safety concern — credible self-harm signals, exploitation, a pattern of disappearances — targeted monitoring can be justified. Without one, the default should be trust built through expectation and communication, not surveillance built on suspicion.
