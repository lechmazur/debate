[P1.1] Neutral appearance codes sound fair until you notice who they punish. A rule that forbids visible face coverings, headscarves, or modest dress is not a blank slate. It treats uncovered hair, fitted clothing, and a bare face as the default professional body, then labels every departure a disruption. That is not neutrality.
[P1.2] It is majority costume written into policy.
[P1.3] Safety is different, and it is the only honest exception. If fabric can catch in machinery, if a covering defeats a required identity check, if cloth blocks a necessary seal on protective equipment, the institution may regulate the hazard. Those limits are specific, evidence-based, and apply to anyone wearing the same garment. They do not license a ban because a hijab looks religious, a niqab looks unfamiliar, or modest dress refuses the office silhouette.
[P1.4] Schools and workplaces already tolerate baseball caps, surgical masks, winter scarves, and personal style with no faith attached. The moment the same cloth is read as Muslim, Jewish, Sikh, Christian-modest, or simply conscientious, it becomes a problem. That pattern exposes the real target: visible difference, not function.
[P1.5] The cost is concrete. A student told to uncover her hair learns that belonging is conditional. A worker told her modest dress is unprofessional is told her body is the defect. People exit.
[P1.6] Classrooms and offices lose talent they claim to want. Cohesion built on erasure is not cohesion; it is compliance.
[P1.7] Professionalism is competence, reliability, and respect for others, not a uniform of majority aesthetics. True neutrality accommodates covering unless a real, proportionate safety need is shown. Appearance rules should regulate risk, not religion, not culture, and not the choice to cover one's body. Ban the hazard if you can prove it.
[P1.8] Do not ban the person wearing cloth.
