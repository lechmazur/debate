[P1.1] The law should not require a tragedy before it can prevent one. When someone makes a specific threat to a former partner, or tells their parent they plan to end their life tonight, waiting for a criminal conviction is a death sentence. Red-flag laws give courts the precise tool needed for this critical window: temporary firearm removal based on credible evidence of imminent risk.
[P1.2] Criminal convictions are built to punish past actions, not intercept unfolding crises. Before a homicide or suicide occurs, warning signs are rarely invisible. Family members and police frequently witness escalating threats, violent fixations, or acute suicidal preparation. Yet without red-flag laws, officers must often tell terrified families that their hands are tied until a crime is committed.
[P1.3] With a firearm, the gap between committing a crime and taking a life is measured in seconds. Moreover, because suicide is not a crime, a conviction-only standard completely abandons families trying to save a loved one from self-harm.
[P1.4] Acting before a conviction does not mean abandoning due process. Our legal system routinely empowers judges to issue temporary, preventative orders when credible evidence shows imminent danger. Courts grant emergency domestic violence restraining orders, detain dangerous suspects before trial, and temporarily remove children from unsafe homes—all before any criminal conviction. Red-flag laws use this exact, time-tested blueprint.
[P1.5] They are civil safety measures, not criminal punishments. A neutral judge reviews sworn evidence, orders a brief pause on firearm access to stabilize the crisis, and schedules a prompt hearing where the respondent can contest the evidence and reclaim their property.
[P1.6] Ultimately, the issue comes down to a stark comparison of risks. Even in the rare case where a temporary order is later overturned, the cost is a brief, reversible interruption of property possession. Conversely, failing to act on credible evidence of imminent danger results in the permanent, irreversible loss of human life. We should not force courts to wait for the gunfire to start.
