[C1.2] The proposition also mistakes a legitimate pricing tool for the cause of social exclusion. By contrast, blanket legal limits on risk-based premiums force insurers to charge everyone as if they faced average risk. That does not eliminate high costs; it hides them.

Those measures target genuine unfairness without banning the information needed to price insurance responsibly.
[C1.5] Thus, legal limits should protect people from unjust discrimination and catastrophic financial exposure, not prohibit actuarially fair pricing as such. When solidarity is funded openly and progressively, actuarial fairness can coexist with it; when it is imposed through distorted premiums, solidarity is weakened, not strengthened.
