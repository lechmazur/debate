By producing clinically meaningful weight loss, they reduce the incidence and severity of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular events, obstructive sleep apnea, and osteoarthritis—the very conditions that consume a disproportionate share of senior health dollars.

We are not talking about treating a symptom; we are talking about avoiding catastrophic events that routinely cost tens of thousands of dollars each.

[P1.4] Within five years, reduced hospitalizations, fewer complications, and better chronic disease control will outweigh the line-item drug expenditure. The question is not whether these drugs cost money; it is whether Medicare prefers to keep paying for the consequences or finally pay to prevent them. Prevention is the cheaper path.
