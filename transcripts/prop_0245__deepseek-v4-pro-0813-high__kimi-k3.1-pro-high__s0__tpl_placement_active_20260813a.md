[P1.1] Trust in consumer AI is lower today because the public learned about AI harms through preventable, widely shared failures. The main reason is not that AI is risky in the abstract; it is that major firms raced to ship and treated safety as an opt-in afterthought. Slower deployment with stronger default safeguards would have prevented many of the most visible trust-destroying incidents while signaling that user welfare came before market share.
[P1.2] Public trust is asymmetric: one viral case of a chatbot giving dangerous advice, an image model producing nonconsensual deepfakes, or a voice-cloning scam can erase years of goodwill. Many of these were predictable. Slower red-teaming, narrower release, watermarking, age verification, and default content restrictions could have reduced their frequency or severity.
[P1.3] Instead, companies shipped broadly with permissive defaults, making ordinary users the safety testers.
[P1.4] The word “default” matters. When safeguards are opt-in, the least sophisticated users and those most likely to be harmed are left exposed. Strong defaults protect people who never open a settings menu. They also make safety the baseline, not a compliance checkbox added after a scandal.
[P1.5] Slower shipping would not have meant no innovation. It would have meant fewer self-inflicted crises. If the first wave of consumer AI had arrived with fewer hallucinations presented as confident truth, fewer impersonation tools, fewer manipulated sexual images, and fewer manipulative chatbot episodes, the public would have had less reason to treat AI as a threat rather than a tool. The counterfactual is not perfect trust; it is clearly higher trust.
[P1.6] Adoption is not trust. People use AI because employers, schools, or convenience demand it, while widespread wariness about privacy, deception, and misuse persists. The race to ship did not buy trust; it bought ubiquity at the cost of confidence. Slower, safer defaults would have made AI feel like a product with accountable boundaries rather than an uncontrolled experiment.
