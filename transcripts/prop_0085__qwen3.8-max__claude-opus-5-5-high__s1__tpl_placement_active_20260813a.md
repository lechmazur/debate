[P1.1] The decisive question is not whether a smartphone can sometimes be useful in class, but who should control the environment in which students learn. A schoolwide default ban is better because it treats attention as a shared resource, removes enforcement from individual teacher discretion, and protects students from a device designed to interrupt them.
[P1.2] Smartphones are not ordinary school supplies. Their notifications, feeds, and social pressures compete directly with instruction. A student who receives a message can disengage in seconds; the cost is not just that student’s lost focus but the teacher’s interruption and the classmates’ distraction. When schools leave rules to individual teachers, they create a patchwork: one classroom allows phones, another does not, one teacher enforces tightly while another cannot.
[P1.3] Students quickly learn where the weak spots are. That inconsistency breeds conflict, bargaining, and resentment, while teachers waste instructional time policing pockets and bags.
[P1.4] A default ban solves this by setting a clear, predictable norm: phones stay off or stored during the school day. That clarity helps teachers, because they no longer have to negotiate the rule case by case. It also helps students, because it removes the constant social temptation without singling anyone out. It reduces the likelihood of cyberbullying during class, protects privacy in hallways and bathrooms, and lowers the pressure to perform online availability.
[P1.5] For students who need a phone for medical reasons, translation, or an approved lesson, the default can include exceptions without making exceptions the rule.
[P1.6] The strongest objection is that teachers know their rooms best. But a classroom-level rule places the burden of managing an attention economy on the person with the least institutional power. A schoolwide default shares that burden, preserves instructional time, and makes the school day calmer and fairer. If the goal is learning, the default should be phones away, not phones negotiable.
