[P1.1] Doctors, structural engineers, and attorneys genuinely need advanced degrees to perform safely. Nobody disputes keeping credentials where academic mastery is the job. The problem lies across the vast middle of the modern economy—office managers, sales representatives, IT support specialists, logistics coordinators—where employers routinely mandate a bachelor's degree for work mastered through practical experience or targeted training.
[P1.2] When a degree is unnecessary for job performance, requiring it anyway is corporate self-sabotage. By treating a four-year diploma as an automatic cutoff, employers instantly blind themselves to most of the working-age population. They manufacture artificial talent shortages, pay inflated wage premiums for credentials they never actually use, and suffer higher turnover from graduates who treat middle-skill roles as temporary stepping stones.
[P1.3] Defenders of degree mandates usually invoke screening convenience, claiming a diploma signals grit, intelligence, or reliability. In reality, it is a blunt and distorted proxy. A four-year degree primarily measures whether someone had the money, time, and family cushion to pause full-time work in their early twenties. Relying on that filter systematically discards military veterans, community-college graduates, self-taught technologists, and steady workers with years of proven output.
[P1.4] Employers do not need lazy shortcuts when practical tools—brief work-sample tasks, industry certifications, portfolio reviews, and structured interviews—directly test the exact capabilities a role demands.
[P1.5] Using universities as outsourced HR sorting machines also distorts the wider economy, pressuring young adults into heavy tuition debt merely to buy an entry ticket to ordinary jobs. Crucially, dropping degree requirements never penalizes college graduates. Graduates can still apply, and whenever higher education genuinely sharpened their judgment or communication, they will win on merit. Removing arbitrary mandates simply stops confusing pedigree with competence—forcing hiring to measure what applicants can actually do, rather than which tollbooth they could afford to pass.
