[P1.1] Default face-slimming, skin-smoothing, and age-shifting filters on minor-facing social platforms and beauty apps impose unasked-for, developmentally harmful body standards on kids during their most vulnerable window for self-esteem and identity formation. When a 13-year-old opens their camera app or social feed, only to see their natural, blemished, developing face automatically erased, sharpened, and smoothed into an unrealistic, polished ideal, they receive a silent but persistent message: their unaltered appearance is defective, a problem to be fixed rather than a normal part of growing up.
[P1.2] The default effect is a well-documented behavioral nudge that hits minors hardest: most users, especially those with limited digital literacy, never change pre-enabled settings, meaning the vast majority of underage users will view their filtered reflection as their baseline "real" face without ever opting in to that distortion. This passive, involuntary exposure is directly tied to the surge in teen body dissatisfaction, body dysmorphic disorder symptoms, and requests for cosmetic procedures to match the filtered versions of themselves they see online—harms that show up daily in school counseling offices and pediatric clinics across the country.
[P1.3] Critics will claim minors can simply turn filters off, but this ignores the social pressure of digital spaces: if all of a minor’s peers are posting filtered faces, opting out means standing out as the only person with "flaws," amplifying shame rather than relieving it. The fix is simple, low-cost, and already within platforms’ capabilities: they already verify user ages to enforce other content restrictions, so they can easily set all face-altering filters to opt-in for users under 18, rather than on by default.
[P1.4] This preserves full choice for users who want to use filters, while eliminating the involuntary, harmful exposure that drives the worst outcomes for minors. The default should protect kids, not push distorted beauty standards on them before they have the critical framework to push back.
