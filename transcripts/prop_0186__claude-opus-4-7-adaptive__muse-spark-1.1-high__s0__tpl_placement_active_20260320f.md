[P1.1] Recognition is not a mirror; it is a handshake. When a state extends recognition, it does more than acknowledge a fact on the ground—it confers legitimacy, unlocks treaties, sovereign immunity, seats at multilateral tables, access to reserves at central banks, and the presumption that this government speaks for its people. A handshake is a moral act. It should not be automatic just because someone has their hand on the throat of a territory.
[P1.2] My case rests on three claims.
[P1.3] First, incentives. Pure control-based recognition rewards the fastest path to monopoly violence. It tells every colonel contemplating a coup, every militia storming a capital, every regime disappearing dissidents: hold the ministries for a season and the world will call you legitimate. A minimum floor—no mass atrocities, some baseline political rights, no seizure of power by force—flips that incentive.
[P1.4] The Taliban's post-2021 quest for recognition is instructive: the leverage the world retains over girls' education exists precisely because recognition is being withheld.
[P1.5] Second, coherence. States already claim to care about human rights in their charters, their aid conditionality, their sanctions regimes, their war crimes tribunals. Treating recognition as the one domain where cruelty is irrelevant is incoherent. If we can condition IMF loans on governance, we can condition the far more symbolic act of recognition on not gassing your citizens.
[P1.6] Third, the CON alternative is worse than it sounds. "Effective control" seems neutral and factual, but it is a normative choice dressed as realism—a choice to bless whoever wins. It offers no principled way to refuse recognition to a genocidal junta that has, in fact, secured the capital. It converts international society into a ratification machine for force.
[P1.7] The reasonable version of my proposition is a floor, not a purity test: no recognition for regimes born of coups or maintained by mass atrocity, and clear standards for restoration. That floor does not paralyze diplomacy—engagement, consulates, humanitarian channels all continue. It simply refuses to call brutality a government.
