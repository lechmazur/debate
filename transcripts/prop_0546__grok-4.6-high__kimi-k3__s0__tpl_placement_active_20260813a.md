[P1.1] The collapse of local journalism did not leave towns with a freer marketplace of ideas. [P1.2] Polarization feeds on the loss of a shared local map. When the last reporter covering the school board is gone, a zoning dispute or overtime scandal arrives already packaged as a national morality play. [P1.3] Restore them and neighbors argue about the same object instead of two imported identities.
[P1.4] Corruption feeds on the same darkness. Officials steer deals, bury misconduct, and treat public records as optional when nobody is paid to sit through the meeting. [P1.5] The capture objection treats public money as uniquely dangerous and private ownership as innocent. That is backwards. Hedge funds already strip newsrooms. Partisan chains already turn local airtime into talking points.
[P1.6] The status quo is captured; it is just captured by owners, not voters. Other democracies keep public newsrooms at arm’s length from the minister and the advertiser without turning the weather into a party broadcast.
[P1.7] Compare the two worlds. In one, local reality is replaced by national rage and unwatched government. In the other, the public buys reporting capacity the market will no longer supply, insulated from both. The first world is the one we have.
