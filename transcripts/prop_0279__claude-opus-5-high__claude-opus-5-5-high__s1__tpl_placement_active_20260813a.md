[P1.1] Start with the word that does the most work in this debate: selfish. Selfishness means gaining at someone else's expense. So name the victim. It cannot be the child, because the child who is never conceived is not a person waiting in a queue, deprived of a turn.
[P1.2] There is no one standing on the other side of that decision with an interest that was overridden. Every other charge of selfishness in ordinary life — cheating, freeloading, neglect — points at a face. This one points at an empty chair.
[P1.3] What's left is the claim that the childless owe a debt to society: pensions, workforces, the future. But notice how unevenly that debt is ever collected. We don't call the childless selfish when they fund schools through taxes they'll never personally use, or when they're the ones with flexible evenings to care for aging parents, or when they teach, nurse, foster, and adopt. A society that wanted contribution would count contribution.
[P1.4] What it actually polices is reproduction — and overwhelmingly, it polices women's reproduction. The nosy question at the family dinner is not an economic policy.
[P1.5] Second, look at what parenthood actually is: two decades of irreversible, non-delegable obligation to a person who did not consent to their own existence. That is precisely the kind of undertaking that should require an affirmative yes. The child of a reluctant parent — one who complied with pressure rather than wanting the job — pays the price in a way no spreadsheet captures. Someone who honestly concludes "I would do this badly, or resentfully, or not at all" and declines has performed an act of moral seriousness, not evasion.
[P1.6] Third, treating childlessness as a problem to solve gets the diagnosis backwards. People delay and forgo children mostly because housing is expensive, work is insecure, childcare costs a second rent, and partners are hard to find. Those are fixable. Shame is not a fertility policy; it's just a tax on people who already made a defensible choice.
