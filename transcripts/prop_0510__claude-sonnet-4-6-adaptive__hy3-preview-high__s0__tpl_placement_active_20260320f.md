[P1.1] The entire purpose of automatic stabilizers is to be countercyclical. They are designed to expand when the economy contracts and retreat when growth returns. Telling them not to expand during a downturn because debt is elevated is like disabling a car's brakes because the brake fluid is expensive. The tool exists precisely for the difficult moment.
[P1.2] Start with the debt dynamics themselves. When a recession deepens—when unemployment spikes, incomes collapse, and demand implodes—the denominator of the debt ratio shrinks alongside the numerator. Suppressing stabilizers to protect the ratio often worsens it. The eurozone periphery in the early 2010s is the most instructive case: governments cut spending and trimmed benefit programs under pressure from elevated debt ratios, the recessions deepened dramatically, and debt-to-GDP ratios rose anyway.
[P1.3] The medicine accelerated the disease.
[P1.4] Automatic stabilizers are categorically different from permanent discretionary spending. Unemployment insurance contracts as employment recovers. Tax revenues rebound as incomes rise. The expansion is inherently self-reversing, which means the feared permanent debt escalation simply does not follow from letting stabilizers do their job.
[P1.5] CON's argument essentially demands that we treat a temporary, responsive mechanism as if it were an irreversible structural commitment.
[P1.6] The multiplier argument matters here too. In a downturn—especially when monetary policy is constrained by low interest rates or the zero lower bound—fiscal multipliers are at their highest. Every unit of stabilizer spending does more work preserving output, employment, and ultimately tax capacity than it would in normal times. Suppressing stabilizers in precisely the environment where they are most effective is not fiscal prudence; it is pro-cyclical self-harm.
[P1.7] Finally, debt sustainability is not just an arithmetic problem—it is a function of growth expectations. A government that defends employment and income stability during a downturn is maintaining the productive base that makes its debt serviceable. The proposition isn't asking for recklessness; it is asking that well-designed, temporary, growth-protecting mechanisms be allowed to function.
