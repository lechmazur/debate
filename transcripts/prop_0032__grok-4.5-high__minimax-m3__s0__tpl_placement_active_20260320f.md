[P1.1] Imagine opening an email announcing a BRCA-class mutation that multiplies your lifetime cancer risk several times over. No clinician present. No explanation of penetrance, absolute versus relative risk, or the real meaning of a variant of uncertain significance. Only raw probability and a marketing dashboard.
[P1.2] That experience is now routine. Direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies should be required to provide access to certified genetic counseling before customers receive high-impact predictive health results.
[P1.3] These results are medical information, not lifestyle entertainment. They recalibrate screening, surgery decisions, family planning, and psychological well-being. Genetic risk is dense with caveats: incomplete penetrance, ancestry effects, gene-environment interactions, and false positives that mimic certainty. Without guided interpretation, people overreact into unnecessary interventions or underreact by dismissing genuine high-risk findings.
[P1.4] Either error imposes lasting harm—on the individual and on relatives who may also carry the same variant.
[P1.5] Certified genetic counselors exist to close that gap. They assess readiness, translate probabilities into actionable clinical pathways, place findings against personal and family history, and support cascade testing. Requiring companies to furnish access before release simply aligns the commercial delivery pipeline with the actual stakes of what they sell. The firm already collects the sample, runs the assay, and generates the report; adding a counseling gateway is a modest process change that prevents high-consequence mistakes made in isolation and shock.
[P1.6] True autonomy is informed autonomy. Dumping unmediated high-impact results creates the illusion of empowerment while externalizing emotional fallout and downstream clinical costs onto individuals and public systems. Market incentives favor volume and dramatic headlines, not careful stewardship of life-altering data. The safer default is clear: no high-impact predictive results without access to certified counseling first.

That is a serious restriction of choice, and it deserves a serious justification PRO has not yet supplied.
[C1.2] First, scope. "High-impact predictive health results" is a phrase with elastic boundaries. Does it mean BRCA1/2? APOE and Alzheimer's?
[C1.3] Lynch syndrome? Polygenic risk scores for heart disease? Each carries different penetrance, different actionability, and different psychological weight. A blanket mandate treats all of these as if they require the same clinical mediation, and that uniformity is unjustified.
[C1.4] Some results are genuinely actionable; many are probabilistic hints that an informed adult can interpret without a counselor in the room. The motion asks us to regulate the loud outliers and the quiet probabilities with the same blunt instrument.
[C1.5] Second, "access" is doing hidden work. In practice, mandated access becomes a gate. The counselor appointment becomes the condition on which the report is released. That converts a service into a checkpoint and strips competent adults of the right to decline a clinical conversation about their own data.
[C1.6] PRO is asking us to accept paternalism as the default for the entire category, treating every consumer as incapable of reading a results page until a certified professional approves it.
[C1.7] Third, the bottleneck. The certified genetic counselor workforce is finite. A mandate does not conjure new counselors; it queues the existing ones. Prices rise, turnaround slows, and consumers migrate to unregulated overseas services or skip testing entirely.
[C1.8] That is not safety; it is displacement.
[C1.9] Fourth, less restrictive alternatives exist: better report design, layered educational content, on-demand counseling for those who want it, and clear flagging of actionability. The proposition is not the only path to informed customers. It is simply the most coercive one.
[C1.10] The motion trades adult autonomy for an imagined clinical safety net. The case for that trade has not been made.
