[P1.1] The core question of this motion is simple: should employers be allowed to profit from free labor, locking low-income workers out of entire career pathways? The answer is no. Governments should heavily restrict unpaid internships outside genuinely educational placements, because these roles are not learning opportunities—they are exploitative, unregulated make-work that displaces paid jobs and entrenches class inequality.
[P1.2] We fully support genuinely educational internships: those tied to academic credit, with structured learning objectives, faculty oversight, and clear pedagogical goals. The motion’s restriction only targets the vast majority of unpaid internships that have no educational value, exist solely to cut labor costs, and are tied to no formal training program.
[P1.3] First, these unrestricted internships are a barrier to equal opportunity. Only candidates with independent financial support—typically from wealthy families—can afford to work full-time for no pay for months on end. Surveys consistently find that low-income, first-generation, and working-class students are drastically underrepresented in these roles, locking them out of career fields like media, politics, and non-profit management that have long relied on unpaid intern labor.
[P1.4] Second, these roles displace paid entry-level jobs. Employers use unpaid interns to perform the same core work as entry-level staff: administrative support, research, client outreach, production tasks. This allows companies to eliminate or never create paid entry-level positions, shrinking the pool of stable, paying jobs for new workers. For example, the U.S. media industry has replaced most entry-level paid reporter and production assistant roles with stacks of unpaid internships, a shift that has disproportionately excluded low-income candidates from journalism careers entirely.
[P1.5] The burden for the CON side is to explain why we should tolerate a system that enriches employers at the expense of low-income workers, reduces paid job availability, and perpetuates intergenerational inequality. That is the case I will advance over the coming rounds.
