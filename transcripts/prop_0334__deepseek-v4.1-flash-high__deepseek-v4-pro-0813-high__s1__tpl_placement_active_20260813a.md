[P1.1] Political content is not a spam problem; it is a legitimacy problem. Platforms referee disputed claims about elections, war, and public health—domains where facts are contested and often mixed with true-but-misleading framing. The decisive question is not whether platforms should govern political content. It is whether the default tool should be a deletion or a visible, contestable layer of context.
[P1.2] Community-note systems win on the dimensions that matter most: accuracy, legitimacy, and resilience. Proactive takedowns are binary. They work for illegal threats, incitement, doxxing, and non-consensual imagery. But most political harm is not a clean category; it is a true statistic stripped of context, a selectively edited video, a claim that is misleading but not falsifiable in a headline.
[P1.3] Takedown systems either miss it or overreach. A context note can say: this video is edited; this number omits mail ballots; this claim conflicts with the official count. That is more precise than removal because it treats users as adults capable of judgment rather than subjects of a silent veto.
[P1.4] Legitimacy matters too. When a platform deletes a politician’s post, it converts a disputed factual question into a platform-versus-public fight. That fuels the censorship narrative, drives audiences to alternative platforms, and often increases the very spread the platform wanted to stop. Context systems keep the speech visible while attaching correction.
[P1.5] They are harder to demonize because the reasoning is public and appealable. Cross-ideological note approval—where a note becomes visible only after people with different viewpoints rate it helpful—creates a rough public check against partisan capture. No system is immune to manipulation, but takedowns are equally gameable by mass reporting, political pressure, and error, with far less transparency.
[P1.6] The proposition does not demand abolishing takedowns. It demands making context the primary tool for disputed political speech and reserving removal for clear illegality and immediate violence. That is how governance survives value pluralism: not by pretending platforms can be truth referees, but by making correction visible, contestable, and less destructive.

[C1.3] The platform that chooses a label over a takedown remains an amplifier of the harmful post to everyone who sees the headline without the note.

[C1.5] The legitimacy argument is also backward. Leaving a false claim up with a small label may feel less censorious, but it still distributes the underlying falsehood to millions. Proactive removal may be more visible, but it draws a clearer line: dangerous political falsehoods should not be amplified, even with context.
[C1.6] Takedowns must remain the primary defense, with notes as a fallback for the narrow set of ambiguous claims where delay is not itself harmful.
