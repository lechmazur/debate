[P1.1] Presumed consent is not permission to take organs over a patient’s objection. Adults are treated as willing donors unless they opt out, while clinicians verify status, consult relatives, and stop when evidence shows objection. This attacks a loss under opt-in: people who would accept donation never register, leaving families to guess during a crisis. Silence becomes preventable shortage, not respect for choice.
[P1.2] Changing the default increases transplants. More eligible donors enter the pathway because inertia no longer counts as refusal. Relatives also receive clearer guidance: donation reflects the patient’s presumed wish rather than an unfamiliar request requiring a fresh decision. Legislation alone is no miracle, but opt-out reforms paired with public registration, trained coordinators, and hospital investment explain why authorization can rise.
[P1.3] More authorizations mean more organs reaching waiting patients; that is the measurable pathway CON must break.
[P1.4] The ethical test is not whether families feel no grief; donation discussions occur amid bereavement under either system. It is whether reform adds violations or distress beyond that baseline. Soft opt-out limits that risk through easy, durable refusal, accessible information, registry checks, family involvement, and professional discretion when relatives supply evidence of objection. It also reduces the distress of asking families to invent an answer for someone whose wishes were never recorded.
[P1.5] Choice remains real; uncertainty does not.
[P1.6] The motion says “for most patients,” not “regardless of implementation.” Exceptions for children, adults lacking capacity, or groups inadequately reached by public information can remain under explicit consent without surrendering the central gain. CON cannot win with hypothetical mistrust or isolated failures, because opt-in systems also fail. They must show that safeguarded opt-out produces no measurable net increase, or systematically creates more violations and distress. When the default converts passive goodwill into transplants while an objection still stops retrieval, neither claim survives.
