[P1.1] Children thrive when they have stable, committed caregivers who meet their emotional, educational, and material needs. The sex of those caregivers does not predict those outcomes. Same-sex parents, on average, raise children who do as well as children of different-sex parents on school performance, emotional adjustment, and social competence.
[P1.2] The mechanism is straightforward. Warmth, monitoring, low conflict, and resources travel with parents whether the household is two women, two men, or one of each. Same-sex couples who become parents often do so through adoption, fostering, or assisted reproduction, which selects for high investment. The fair comparison is therefore two kinds of committed two-parent homes, not a nuclear ideal versus a leftover category.
[P1.3] Studies that appear to show harm usually smuggle in instability. They count a child whose parent later came out after a heterosexual divorce as a same-sex family, while ignoring how many different-sex households also fail. Once you compare stable same-sex two-parent families with stable different-sex two-parent families, the alleged gap collapses.
[P1.4] That is the evidence public policy should use. If the average child is not worse off, there is no child-protection rationale for barring same-sex couples from marriage, adoption, or equal parental recognition. Forms that assume every family has a mother and a father, or custody rules that treat a same-sex partner as a stranger, impose real costs on kids for no demonstrated average benefit.
[P1.5] A child with two fathers does not need the state to manufacture a mother. A child with two mothers does not need the state to manufacture a father. What they need is the law to stop treating those families as experimental. The children are doing fine.
[P1.6] Policy should catch up.

[C1.1] The 'no differences' consensus collapses once you look at how it was built. Most studies recruited tiny, self-selected samples of affluent lesbian mothers through clinics and advocacy networks, then compared their children to average American kids. With a few dozen families and sympathetic volunteers reporting on their own children, you lack statistical power to detect real harm and invite social-desirability bias. Absence of evidence became evidence of absence.
[C1.2] When researchers use large, representative data that do not recruit on identity — census records, national youth surveys that track household history — the gap does not collapse, it appears. Children living with same-sex parents show on average more anxiety and depression, more school disruption, more instability in living arrangements. That is exactly what you would expect when family form guarantees a missing biological mother or father plus extra transitions into the household. That representative data still find disadvantages despite that wealth filter is telling.
[C1.3] Mothers and fathers are not interchangeable providers of 'warmth and monitoring.' Children learn sex-specific development from living with both sexes: how men and women love, argue, parent and reconcile, how to understand their own bodies and future relationships. A boy raised from birth with no father in the home, or a girl with no mother, loses daily modeling no amount of commitment can replace, and donor-conceived children increasingly voice that loss.
[C1.4] Policy must reflect that asymmetry. Placing a foster child who already lost one family into a home that by design denies a mother or a father is not neutral. Preference for stable mother-father homes in adoption, preserving biological ties when safe, and honest birth records do not punish gay adults — they protect children's distinct interest in both maternal and paternal care. Equality of adults is not sameness of family structures for children.
