[P1.2] Start with unionization. Once the algorithm produces a ranked list, retaliation no longer needs an overt anti-union memo; it only needs a plausible pretext. Legal safeguards that demand proof of motive after the fact arrive too late.
[P1.3] Prediction itself becomes the chilling mechanism.
[P1.4] Productivity scores are equally corrosive. The prediction becomes self-fulfilling; the person never receives a fair chance to refute it, and black-box opacity blocks meaningful challenge.
[P1.5] Quit-risk scoring finishes the capture. Firms can side-line mobile talent before departure, blunt wage pressure, and punish the very exit option that disciplines bad employers. Labor-market fluidity, a real equalizer, is treated as a corporate threat to be neutralized.

What these systems add is opaque, anticipatory control over private intentions and life choices. The power imbalance is already extreme; automating foresight turns it into something closer to engineered destiny.
[P1.7] Aggregate workforce analytics remain available; individual predictive targeting does not. Dignity, freedom of association, and equal opportunity all require that companies stop treating workers’ unchosen futures as algorithmic leverage.
