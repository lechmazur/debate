[P1.1] Sea-level rise and extreme heat are not metaphors. They are physical processes that can destroy the conditions for human habitation. When saltwater intrudes into a coastal aquifer, when recurrent flooding erases homes and farmland, and when humid heat makes ordinary outdoor work unsafe, people are not choosing to migrate for a better wage. They are being displaced by an environment that can no longer sustain them.
[P1.2] The law should recognize that difference.
[P1.3] Current asylum categories fail these people because they were built around persecution, war, and state violence. But the severity of harm is not reduced by the absence of a persecutor. A limited climate asylum category would be exactly that: limited. It need not cover everyone affected by weather.
[P1.4] It should require clear evidence that the applicant’s former home has become effectively unlivable, that the displacement is durable, and that no safe internal relocation exists. That excludes temporary storms, ordinary poverty, and preference migration.
[P1.5] Recognizing this right would not throw borders open. It would create a legal channel through which states can screen, cap, and share responsibility. The realistic alternative is not fewer arrivals; it is more people crossing irregularly, undocumented, and outside any managed process. A limited right is therefore a tool of sovereignty, not a surrender of it.
[P1.6] It is also consistent with existing non-refoulement principles. States already refuse to return people to torture or inhuman treatment. Returning a family to land that can no longer support life, or to heat conditions that foreseeably kill, follows the same protective logic. If the right to life means anything across borders, it is least satisfied when a state knowingly sends someone back into a slow-motion disaster.
[P1.7] The strongest objection is floodgates, but the limited design answers that concern directly. The choice is not between climate asylum and uncontrolled borders. It is between a narrow, legal, managed mechanism and a protection gap that punishes some of the people least responsible for the emissions causing their displacement. Countries can and should close that gap.
