Israelis will not accept becoming a minority in a single state, and Palestinians will not accept permanent statelessness under someone else's army. [P1.5] Two states has failed repeatedly because it was never seriously implemented, not because geography changed. Borders, land swaps, and security arrangements have been mapped in past negotiations; no rival plan has that scaffolding. Flawed, distant, mistrusted — and still less bloody than every other future.

[C1.2] A state is not a slogan. It requires contiguous territory, a legitimate monopoly on force, and a political leadership able to sign and deliver. The West Bank has been carved by settlements, bypass roads, military zones, and separate legal regimes into disconnected cantons. East Jerusalem is governed as part of Israel, and Gaza remains severed from the West Bank politically and physically.
[C1.3] No Israeli coalition can evacuate hundreds of thousands of settlers without a crisis that threatens civil conflict; no Palestinian authority can claim sovereignty while its territory is punctured and its institutions divided. The proposition’s map has already been voted down by facts on the ground.
[C1.4] PRO claims self-determination requires partition. Yet self-determination need not mean ethno-national separation. It can mean equal citizenship, local autonomy, and shared sovereignty within one political space. The demand is for control over life, not for a flag over a shrinking archipelago.
[C1.5] This matters because an unachievable ideal is not neutral. It gives cover to permanent domination. If the world insists partition is coming, every settlement expansion, raid, and blockade can be treated as temporary bargaining noise rather than consolidation of one unequal regime. The two-state formula becomes a diplomatic waiting room, not a path to justice or security.
[C1.6] PRO’s alternatives are not only a democratic single state imposed overnight. The realistic comparison is between an ethnically defined rule over fragmented populations and arrangements based on equal rights, federation, or confederation. Those are hard, but they address the actual space: one land, one governing power, two peoples. Partition, by contrast, promises a clean divorce where there is no clean geography, encourages transfers and maximalist fears, and leaves behind a smaller, angrier, less viable Palestine.
[C1.7] The least-bad achievable outcome must begin with reality, not with a 1990s map that no longer exists.
