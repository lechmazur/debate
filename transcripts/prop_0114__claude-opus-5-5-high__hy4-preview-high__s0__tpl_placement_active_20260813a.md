[C1.1] The moral instinct behind this motion is right; the instrument is wrong. Pensions and social insurance are not medals for socially valuable activity. They are contributory insurance against the loss of earned income, funded by earnings-linked contributions. Paid work earns credit because it generates taxable earnings and employer matching.
[C1.2] Simulated credits, by contrast, must be financed by other people's real contributions, making them a transfer, not insurance. Once governments simulate credits for unpaid care because it “adds value,” the contributory principle dissolves. If saving the state money or raising future workers earns credit, why not volunteering, homemaking, or preventive health habits? The line becomes political and arbitrary.
[C1.3] The Treasury-saving argument is weakest. Much home care would be given anyway; credits would reward behavior that is largely inframarginal while adding certain long-term liabilities. They also require an intrusive bureaucracy to verify hours, disability thresholds, and co-residence—and they invite fraud. Britain already offers carer and child-rearing credits, yet the gender pension gap persists, which should temper claims that such credits are the missing fix.
[C1.4] Above all, this misdiagnoses the injury. A carer’s loss is immediate: wages, savings, promotions, and current security. Consider a nurse who leaves at fifty to care for her mother; she loses decades of compound earnings, not just a contribution record. A pension credit decades later does not pay today’s rent, buy current care, or restore a career.
[C1.5] Worse, by recording care as “covered,” credits reduce pressure for real remedies—affordable care services, paid family leave, and flexible work—that let people combine work and care rather than disappear from the workforce.
[C1.6] So the real choice is not visibility versus invisibility. It is whether to distort insurance, expand future liabilities, and police family life, or to fund care infrastructure and workplace protection now. The credit looks compassionate but leaves carers exposed and the system weaker. Oppose the motion.
