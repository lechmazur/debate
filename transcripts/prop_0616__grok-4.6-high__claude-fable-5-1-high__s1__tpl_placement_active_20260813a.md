[P1.1] Advanced semiconductor manufacturing equipment is not a normal export. It is the physical bottleneck that turns military AI from theory into deployed systems. Restricting it is a security decision first and a commercial one second, even when the commercial bill is large.
[P1.2] The machines that print leading-edge chips sit in a tiny allied cluster: lithography, deposition, etch, inspection. Designs can be stolen, talent hired, software leaked. [P1.4] Military AI does not need the entire consumer market. It needs enough high-end compute under sovereign control for targeting, electronic warfare, intelligence fusion, and autonomous platforms. [P1.7] Openness does not buy reciprocity. Adversaries already fuse civilian and military industry and treat our tools as a shortcut.
