A woman leaving a late shift, a student crossing campus at night, or someone fleeing a controlling partner may have a ride available and still not take it, because the risk feels concentrated in the driver-passenger encounter. [P1.2] The safety gain is not the fantasy of zero risk. It is reducing exposure to a high-fear, high-vulnerability setting and replacing it with a service women can choose, verify, and recommend. [P1.5] The relevant comparison is not “perfect matching versus women-only matching.” It is a modest increase in waiting time or fare versus a barrier that keeps women out of public life.

[C1.2] The matching problem is also not a mere “ten-minute wait.” Women-only supply must be available at the right place, time, and price. Where women drivers are scarce, restricting eligible demand means longer waits, more empty repositioning, higher costs, or no match. Those failures concentrate precisely in PRO’s central examples: late nights, isolated areas, and urgent departures. A woman waiting alone for a restricted match may face more exposure than one taking the next well-screened car.
[C1.3] PRO’s fallback to mixed service does not solve this; it concedes that the promised trusted option disappears when most needed. Prebooking, waitlists, and geofencing similarly protect the business model by excluding difficult trips, not by protecting the women making them.
[C1.4] There is a further supply trap. Women drivers can lose ride opportunities and flexibility when their eligible demand is narrowed, making recruitment and retention harder just as the option requires more of them. The promised mobility gains—late shifts, classes, appointments—therefore cannot be inferred from favorable intentions or initial sign-ups. An option women cannot reliably obtain does not restore access to public life.
[C1.5] The real comparison is not women-only matching versus indifference. It is gender restriction versus investing in safeguards that address conduct directly: robust screening, verified pickups, trip sharing, anomaly detection, responsive emergency support, and serious enforcement against offenders. Those measures preserve the largest possible matching pool and protect riders who cannot or do not wish to use a women-only category.
[C1.6] Some reassurance is plausible; sufficient incremental safety is not established. When the distinctive benefit is unreliable and the distinctive costs strike hardest during vulnerability, the operational complexity and inefficiency are not justified.
