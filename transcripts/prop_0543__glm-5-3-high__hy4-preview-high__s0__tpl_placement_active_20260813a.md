[P1.1] Platforms already know defaults are destiny. That's why they ship teen accounts with location sharing on, profiles public, contact from anyone enabled, and personalized ads tuned to every click. They do it because the overwhelming majority of users, adults and minors alike, never touch a settings menu. Behavioral research has shown for decades that defaults are among the most powerful tools in product design precisely because inertia does the work.
[P1.2] So when a platform sets a harmful default for a fifteen-year-old, that isn't neutrality—it's a design choice with predictable consequences, made by the party with all the information and all the leverage.
[P1.3] Requiring privacy-by-design defaults for minors corrects that asymmetry at its root. It means private accounts by default, location off by default, no contact from unknown adults by default, no sensitive-data ad targeting by default. Mechanically, this shrinks the attack surface for the harms we already worry about: stranger grooming depends on open contact and discoverability; exploitative ad targeting depends on the data firehose; viral pile-ons depend on public-by-default profiles. These aren't speculative links—the causal chain runs directly from the setting to the harm.
[P1.4] Notice what this policy does not do. It does not remove a single post, ban a single topic, or empower an official to decide what minors may say or read. Privacy defaults control who sees *your* information, not what *content* exists. The user—or a parent—can still change the setting.
[P1.5] Compare that to the alternatives: content moderation escalates toward censorship; age verification schemes threaten everyone's anonymity. Defaults are the narrowest instrument available, which is why regulators from Europe's GDPR framework to the UK's children's design code converged on exactly this approach.
[P1.6] The burden falls on the opposing side to show that flipping a default toggle to "private" is censorship. It isn't. It's the state telling platforms: stop monetizing children's data through engineered inertia. The harm reduction is direct; the speech cost is essentially zero.
