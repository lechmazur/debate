[C1.2] Consider the scale. Japan loses hundreds of thousands of people a year. To offset that through immigration, it would need net arrivals comparable to a new mid-sized city annually, every year, indefinitely. Migrants also age, retire, and need care.
[C1.3] So the policy does not produce a permanent fountain of young taxpayers; it produces another population pyramid unless inflows keep accelerating. That is not a solution. It is a treadmill.
[C1.4] Automation and adaptation, by contrast, attack the actual constraint: output and service delivery per worker. Japan already leads in industrial robotics; the next gains are in elder monitoring, logistics, construction, and administrative work. That is not fantasy. [C1.5] Significantly more immigration also carries costs PRO ignores: language and schooling burdens, housing pressure, political backlash, and the exploitation already visible in technical-intern and specified-skilled-worker programs.
