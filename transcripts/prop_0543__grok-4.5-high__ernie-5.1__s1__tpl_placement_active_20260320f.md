[P1.1] Large social media platforms are built for maximum engagement and data extraction, not for the developmental realities of minors. The result is predictable harm: children receive unsolicited messages from strangers, algorithms push them toward content that intensifies anxiety and self-harm, and intimate personal data is harvested to shape their preferences and monetize their attention before they can meaningfully consent.
[P1.2] Privacy-by-design defaults stop this at the architecture level. Require large platforms to make accounts for minors private by default, disable public searchability and broad discovery features for under-18s, block location sharing with third parties, and sharply restrict behavioral advertising that uses kids’ activity data. These are not content rules. They are safer defaults.
[P1.3] The mechanism is straightforward and measurable in outcomes. Private defaults cut the volume of stranger contact that enables grooming. Reduced public visibility and discovery limit the algorithmic pathways that accelerate body-image spirals or contagion effects around disordered behavior. Less data collection for advertising removes a vector for commercial manipulation of still-maturing brains.
[P1.4] Because millions of minors use these platforms, even moderate drops in default exposure produce large absolute reductions in harmful incidents. Parents and children already struggle with buried menus and dark patterns; a legal default produces lower exposure rates without perfect supervision.
[P1.5] This does not significantly increase censorship. Censorship silences expression or limits what can be said. Under privacy-by-design, every minor can still create and share content within chosen circles; every post remains accessible to approved contacts; speech is neither removed nor throttled by viewpoint. The only shift is the starting privacy posture for a uniquely vulnerable group.
[P1.6] Openness remains an available option, not the forced entry point.
[P1.7] Platforms will not adopt this voluntarily at scale. Their business models reward open networks and data wealth. Governments must therefore mandate the requirement for large platforms precisely because the market fails children. The policy delivers measurable harm reduction while leaving the core of free expression intact.
