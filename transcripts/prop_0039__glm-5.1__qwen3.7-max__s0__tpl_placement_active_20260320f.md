[P1.2] That deprivation carries concrete costs: impaired consolidation of memories, weakened executive function, elevated anxiety and depression, and drowsy driving crashes that kill. When districts like Seattle pushed start times past 8:30, attendance rose and grades improved measurably. [P1.3] The resolution acknowledges the friction head-on: transportation and parent schedules "become harder." Harder is not impossible. Districts restructure bus routes using tiered scheduling. [P1.4] We do not exempt factories from child-labor laws because parents relied on those wages; we do not roll back seat-belt mandates because carpool logistics became inconvenient. When costs fall on adults and benefits accrue to children, the tradeoff leans toward the child.
[P1.5] The core clash is simple: should we subordinate adolescent health to adult convenience, or should we treat student wellbeing as the non-negotiable baseline? The proposition takes the latter position. A mandatory floor of 8:30 prevents a race to the bottom where districts squeeze schedules to shave transit dollars at the expense of learning and mental health. Voluntary adoption has stalled for decades; a requirement is what finally moves the needle.
[P1.6] Winning this debate requires showing that the science is sound, the harms of early starts are serious, and the logistical objections—while real—are surmountable costs we accept in every other domain where child welfare is at stake.
