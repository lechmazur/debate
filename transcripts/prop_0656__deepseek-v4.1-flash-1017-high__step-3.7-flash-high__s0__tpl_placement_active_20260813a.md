[P1.1] A CBDC that replaces cash inherits cash’s obligations, not just its convenience. Cash is not merely a payment rail; it is the only widely available way ordinary people can transact without asking a bank, a platform, or the state for permission and without creating a permanent record. If a central bank designs a digital replacement and leaves no anonymous peer-to-peer option, it does not modernize cash—it abolishes the privacy cash provides. That is the core reason the proposition is right.
[P1.2] The motion is deliberately bounded: anonymity below a fixed legal threshold, not universal anonymity. That boundary is the whole point. Small transactions are where privacy is most ordinary and most socially valuable: a teenager buying medicine, a person giving to a cause the government dislikes, a family sending money to a relative, a customer buying a sensitive service. None of these needs to be invisible to law enforcement above the threshold; they simply should not be automatically legible to every intermediary and agency.
[P1.3] A fixed threshold lets democratic legislatures draw the line, while larger flows remain traceable and subject to existing anti-money-laundering controls.
[P1.4] The predictable objection is crime. But the proposition does not create a crime haven; it preserves the privacy baseline cash already provides. A dealer moving serious money must still cross the threshold or use other channels that law enforcement can target. The real comparison is not perfect anonymity versus perfect safety.
[P1.5] It is a cash-like private option for small payments versus a mandatory surveillance ledger for everyone.
[P1.6] If CBDCs are meant to replace cash, the burden is on their designers to preserve what makes cash legitimate. A digital currency that cannot be used anonymously for a cup of coffee is not a replacement; it is a control system with a payment app. Require threshold anonymity, or admit cash is being abolished rather than upgraded.

Set it too high, and it enables massive untraceable illicit flows.
[C1.4] There is no calibrated middle ground that avoids either useless privacy or widespread harm. CBDCs are meant to improve on cash’s flaws, not replicate its unaccountable anonymity with digital efficiency. Requiring threshold anonymity is a step backward, not an upgrade.
