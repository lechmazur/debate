[P1.1] A city can ban camping in its parks. The question is when that ban is justified, and the answer is simple: when the person being told to leave has somewhere real to go. Without that, an anti-encampment law stops being a rule about public space and becomes a penalty for having no private space.
[P1.2] First, the principle. A legitimate law asks people to do something they can actually do. "Don't sleep here" is a fair command only if "sleep there instead" is a real option. Everyone must sleep.
[P1.3] When every shelter bed is full, or no shelter exists, the person is not choosing to break the law; the city has made their survival the offense. The Supreme Court's Grants Pass decision said the Constitution permits this. Permission is not justification. Plenty of things are legal and still unjust, and punishing people for an unavoidable bodily necessity is one of them.
[P1.4] Second, "genuinely available" has teeth. A bed that turns away couples, bans pets, forces people to abandon everything they own, or vanishes once the line fills up is a press-release bed, not a real option. That is why storage belongs in the motion. When a sweep throws away someone's tent, ID, phone, and medication, it destroys exactly what they need to get a job, keep a caseworker appointment, or qualify for housing.
[P1.5] Many people "refuse shelter" because accepting means losing their belongings. Fix that, and refusals fall.
[P1.6] Third, criminalization without options fails on its own terms. Sweeps don't end homelessness; they relocate it a few blocks away. Fines that can't be paid become warrants. Warrants become jail stays and records that later block apartments and employment.
[P1.7] The city pays for police, courts, and emergency rooms, and gets the same encampment back next month.
[P1.8] Notice what this case does not claim. It does not say tolerate encampments forever. Once real shelter, storage, and services are offered, enforcement is fair, and those who refuse can be moved. That is a city that wants order earned, not imposed on people with nowhere else [clipped]
