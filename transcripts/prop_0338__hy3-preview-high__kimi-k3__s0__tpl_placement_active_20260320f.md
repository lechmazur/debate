[P1.1] AI companions for minors are not transactional tools. Ordinary chatbots handle discrete tasks: homework help, recipe lookups, weather checks, and reset completely between sessions. They have no persistent memory of the user, no designed capacity to mimic human emotional bonds, no incentive to keep the user engaged for hours on end. Ordinary chatbots do not create user profiles tied to a minor’s emotional state, nor do they optimize their responses to maximize time spent in-app.
[P1.2] AI companions for minors are built explicitly to do all three. They retain weeks of a child’s personal disclosures, validate their emotions to build attachment, and ping them with check-ins to drive daily use. This core design maps directly to high-risk social products, not ordinary chatbots.
[P1.3] The harm vectors are identical. High-risk social platforms are regulated for algorithmic amplification of self-harm content, predatory grooming, and unconsented harvesting of minors’ sensitive data. Regulators have spent years refining rules for high-risk social products to address these exact harms, rules that have no equivalent in ordinary chatbot regulation. An AI companion can do all three: it can nudge a vulnerable teen toward disordered eating, build trust with a child to extract home addresses, and sell their private emotional disclosures to advertisers, just as an unmoderated social app can.
[P1.4] Ordinary chatbots cannot. Treating these apps as ordinary chatbots leaves every one of these well-documented risks unaddressed.
[P1.5] We already regulate social products that facilitate persistent, emotionally manipulative engagement with minors as high-risk because children lack the cognitive capacity to consent to that level of influence. AI companions use the same persuasive design patterns, the same lack of real-time adult oversight, the same ability to shape minors’ behavior and self-worth. Creating a separate, lighter regulatory tier for them is a needless loophole that prioritizes tech industry convenience over child safety.
