[P1.1] Every year malaria kills several hundred thousand people, most of them children under five in sub-Saharan Africa. That is not a risk projection. It is a certainty, repeating annually, while we debate. The question in this motion is not whether gene drives carry ecological uncertainty—they do.
[P1.2] The question is whether speculative, manageable ecological risk justifies guaranteeing those deaths continue. It does not.
[P1.3] Start with the standard CON must defend: blocking trials until long-term ecological certainty exists. That standard is a polite word for "never." Long-term certainty about any novel intervention is impossible before you gather long-term data, and long-term data requires trials. We did not wait for ecological certainty before deploying insecticide-treated nets, indoor spraying, or antimalarial drugs—each reshaped ecosystems and selected for resistance, and each was justified because the alternative was mass death.
[P1.4] Demanding a standard for gene drives that no accepted malaria tool ever met is not caution; it is an asymmetric trap that freezes the status quo and calls it safety.
[P1.5] Second, the motion is not a call for reckless release. It says tightly regulated: phased containment trials first, small releases in isolated sites, continuous monitoring, self-limiting drive designs that fade from populations rather than persisting indefinitely, and the capacity to halt and counter if something goes wrong. Field releases of modified mosquitoes have already occurred in Brazil, the Cayman Islands, and elsewhere under regulation, without the predicted ecological collapse. Regulation converts unknowable risk into bounded, testable risk—which is exactly what trials are for.
[P1.6] Third, notice whose risk gets weighed. The people demanding perfect certainty are largely not the ones burying children. Burkina Faso and other high-burden countries have invested years building the scientific capacity for these trials because they want tools beyond nets and failing drugs. Blocking them from outside says: tolerate a familiar catastrophe so distant observers can feel virtuous about an unfamiliar risk.
[P1.7] The precaution that truly protects is a controlled trial that can be stopped. The recklessness is a decade of delay that cannot. Permit the trials.
