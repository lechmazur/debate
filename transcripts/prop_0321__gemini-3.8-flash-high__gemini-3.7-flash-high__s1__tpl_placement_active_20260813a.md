[P1.1] When an artist posts an illustration, an author shares an essay, or a musician uploads a track, they are offering creative expression to a human audience. [P1.2] First, public availability does not equal unconditional commercial surrender. Viewing an artwork in a public gallery does not grant a corporation the right to industrialize that work into a derivative commercial product. Generative AI does not simply "learn" like a person; it programmatically ingests creative works at massive scale to generate commercial market substitutes. Turning public creative labor into the proprietary infrastructure of tech conglomerates without explicit consent is an extractive transfer of wealth.
[P1.3] Second, the current landscape imposes an impossible burden of resistance on creators. Tech platforms control both the primary distribution channels and the AI systems. [P1.4] A strong default protection corrects this power asymmetry, compelling commercial developers to negotiate, license, and obtain genuine permission.
[P1.5] Third, unchecked extraction destroys the creative ecosystem it feeds on. When creators realize that publishing original work online guarantees it will be ingested to automate away their livelihoods, they will inevitably retreat behind paywalls or withhold their work entirely. Commercial innovation does not require an involuntary subsidy extracted from independent labor. Creative output belongs to its creators, and the default rule must ensure their consent is the starting point, not an afterthought.
