[P1.1] In millions of homes, a single tablet or phone is shared by the whole family. A parent uses it to pay bills, then hands it to an eight-year-old to play a game. Yet the moment that child opens the app store, they are one tap away from sportsbooks, explicit material, and anonymous chat apps notorious for predation. Governments must require app stores to provide a simple way to block or hide these high-risk categories on shared devices.
[P1.2] Current parental controls fail because they are built for a privileged fantasy where every child owns a dedicated device tied to a separate child account. In reality, mobile systems handle multi-user profiles poorly, and families constantly pass one screen back and forth. If a parent enables rigid age locks on a shared device, they cripple their own access to everyday tools; if they leave them off, the child is exposed. Families do not need a maze of sub-accounts.
[P1.3] They need a simple toggle that hides gambling, pornography, and high-risk social platforms.
[P1.4] Tech giants will never build this voluntarily because their financial incentives are compromised. App stores take a lucrative cut of in-app purchases, making digital casinos and hyper-engaging social apps massive revenue drivers. It is no accident that downloading a betting app takes one tap, while restricting it requires an expedition through buried menus. Regulation is necessary to strip away this deliberate friction.
[P1.5] Finally, this mandate is consumer empowerment, not censorship. It bans nothing; every legal app remains available to adults who want it. Nor does it create an unworkable classification burden. App stores already require developers to disclose gambling mechanics, mature content, and unmoderated user interaction before listing an app.
[P1.6] The taxonomy already exists. Requiring gatekeepers to connect those existing labels to a simple, user-controlled switch costs monopolies next to nothing while giving families the control they deserve.
