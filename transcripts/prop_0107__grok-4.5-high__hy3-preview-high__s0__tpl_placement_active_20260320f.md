[P1.1] Careful screening for persistent gender dysphoria isolates adolescents for whom gender-affirming medical care—puberty blockers and hormones—delivers substantial mental-health improvements that far outweigh rare long-term regret or harm.
[P1.2] Without intervention, these young people face ongoing body incongruence that fuels depression, anxiety, and elevated suicide risk. Access to care interrupts that trajectory. By preventing irreversible puberty that conflicts with identity, blockers reduce acute distress; hormones then promote congruence, supporting better peer relationships, school functioning, and self-worth. Clinical trajectories and observational follow-up consistently show drops in dysphoria and concurrent psychiatric symptoms once treatment proceeds under multidisciplinary oversight.
[P1.3] Regret remains exceptional under the proposition’s conditions. Screening requires documented persistence, psychological evaluation that rules out confounding conditions, and informed consent with capacity assessment. These filters exclude most transient explorers and those with unresolved comorbidities. Consequently, desistance after eligibility is low, and true detransition driven by regret is reported only in a tiny fraction of cases.
[P1.4] Medical risks such as fertility impact or bone-density effects are real but mitigable through counseling, monitoring, and options like gamete banking; they do not erase the net mental-health gain for those who proceed.
[P1.5] The decisive comparison is therefore straightforward: the certain and severe mental-health costs of withholding care from this screened group exceed the infrequent and often partial harms of providing it. Denial leaves the mismatch unaddressed and the distress compounding; careful affirmation largely resolves the driver of that distress. Access improves outcomes more than it increases harm.
