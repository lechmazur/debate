[C1.1] Treating AI companion apps as "high-risk social products" is a fundamental category error. It misdiagnoses a single-user conversational tool as if it were a toxic social media network, imposing an intrusive, ill-fitting regulatory apparatus while ignoring that ordinary chatbot governance already possesses the exact mechanisms needed to keep minors safe.
[C1.2] The "high-risk social product" framework was designed to address multi-user network hazards: viral outrage, social comparison, peer-to-peer cyberbullying, and predatory adult users. AI companions possess none of these network dynamics. There is no public square, no social hierarchy, and no audience. Stripped of rhetoric, an AI companion is simply a conversational model equipped with persistent memory and a specific system prompt.
[C1.3] The genuine concerns raised about these apps—such as inappropriate sexual content, crisis mishandling, or excessive user dependency—are software alignment challenges, not network externalities. They are solved directly through standard chatbot-level interventions: robust input-output filters, mandatory refusal protocols for self-harm and mature themes, persistent non-human disclaimers, and limits on proactive retention pings.
[C1.4] Moreover, importing the regulatory regime of high-risk social platforms creates severe collateral harm. High-risk classifications inevitably trigger invasive age-assurance mandates—like biometric facial analysis or government ID collection—and third-party monitoring that destroys user confidentiality. For millions of isolated, neurodivergent, or socially anxious minors, private conversational software offers a valuable, low-stakes sandbox to practice social skills, process thoughts, and find judgment-free emotional equilibrium. Forcing these products into restrictive high-risk categories either violates youth privacy or drives teenagers toward unvetted, unmonitored open-source models with zero safety features.
[C1.5] We should not misapply social network bureaucracy to a single-user computational tool. Regulating companion apps under standard chatbot frameworks—strengthening algorithmic guardrails, data minimization, and safety filters—directly addresses every behavioral risk without breaking the technology or harming the minors who use it safely.
