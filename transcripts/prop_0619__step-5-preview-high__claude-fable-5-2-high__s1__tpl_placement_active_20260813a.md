[P1.1] A schoolteacher posts a comment on a local zoning dispute. Within hours, a coordinated campaign descends: her home address is posted, threats pile up in her mentions, calls are made to her employer demanding her firing. She is forced from her job. The platform amplifies the campaign to thousands and monetizes the attention, then tells her it is "just speech."
[P1.2] That is the gap this proposition closes. Coordinated harassment is not an exchange of ideas; it is organized conduct with foreseeable offline consequences — swatting, doxxing, employment retaliation, psychological devastation, and silence extracted from ordinary citizens. When the harm is foreseeable and the coordination is clear, a duty of care is an ordinary, well-worn legal tool, not a novel experiment. Employers owe it to workers; common-law negligence imposes it across dozens of domains.
[P1.3] The duty proposed here is narrow by design. It attaches to coordinated campaigns, not unpopular opinions, and it demands mitigation — rate-limiting, rapid escalation of surge reports, verified protections for high-risk targets — not flawless prevention. Satisfaction can be built on due process — notice, appeal, audit — so the duty does not become a censorship machine. Platforms already enforce takedowns against the conduct most threatening to their revenue, like copyright infringement.
[P1.4] Harassment victims simply lack the leverage that advertisers and rights-holders possess.
[P1.5] The market failure is structural. Virality pays; the engagement generated by pile-ons enriches the platform while the victim absorbs the cost. Voluntary pledges have proven toothless because there is no consequence for under-enforcement. The targets are predictable: journalists, activists, domestic-abuse survivors, and minority voices whose absence from public discourse is itself an offline harm.
[P1.6] Finally, this duty constrains, rather than threatens, speech. It channels platform discretion through enforceable standards and transparency instead of leaving censorship decisions to arbitrary corporate calculus. A legal duty makes power accountable. Intimidation is not the price of liberty; it is the refusal of it.
