[C1.2] First, sectoral mandates accelerate job destruction through capital substitution and incumbent bias. When wage hikes target a specific low-margin industry, large corporate chains absorb the shock by automating—deploying self-service kiosks, automated kitchens, and algorithmic scheduling. While an economy-wide minimum wage establishes a uniform floor that applies to all competitors, a sectoral board paints a bullseye on specific labor-intensive industries, rapidly pricing vulnerable workers out of jobs.
[C1.3] Second, sectoral wage setting triggers damaging boundary distortions. Slicing the labor market into arbitrary industrial silos invites legal classification wars and corrupting carve-outs. [C1.4] Concentrated wage gains for a protected tier come at the direct expense of disemployment and wage stagnation for everyone else.
[C1.5] Finally, the US completely lacks the institutional infrastructure of Europe or Australia. Sectoral wage boards substitute statutory certainty for administrative micromanagement, producing deeper job losses where workers can least afford them.
