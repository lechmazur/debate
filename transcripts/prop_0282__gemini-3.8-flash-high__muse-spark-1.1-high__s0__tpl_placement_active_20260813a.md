Beyond emotional retraumatization, this entertainment ecosystem incites amateur internet sleuths to harass surviving relatives, trespass on private property, and publicly accuse innocent people in pursuit of digital clout.

This dynamic creates a grotesque economic asymmetry: media companies externalize all human costs onto vulnerable families while capturing immense commercial value. Under existing law, outdated public-record exceptions leave families entirely powerless to stop the commercial exploitation of their most intimate tragedies.
[P1.4] Legitimate journalism serves the public interest through institutional accountability and established ethics. True-crime entertainment, by contrast, discards that discipline for commercial titillation. Imposing stronger ethical standards and targeted legal constraints—such as mandatory victim notification, enforceable guidelines against sensationalizing living victims, and civil remedies for resulting harassment—does not suppress free expression. It establishes basic guardrails against an extractive industry that treats human agony as a harvestable commodity.
