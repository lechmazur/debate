[P1.2] This safeguard applies strictly when no safe alternative is available. [P1.3] A police knock at two in the morning does not magically house the person inside; it only forces exhausted people onto dangerous sidewalks or back onto the road while sleep-deprived.
[P1.4] Worse, routine fines and towing destroy the fastest bridge back to housing. Consider a warehouse worker sleeping in a sedan between shifts while saving for a security deposit. [P1.5] Once steep impound fees outstrip what an unhoused worker can pay, temporary vehicular homelessness hardens into chronic street homelessness. Even by a neighborhood’s narrowest self-interest, seizing cars simply pushes desperate people onto sidewalks and parks.
[P1.6] Ending routine punishment for vehicular sleeping is not a license for disorder. Authorities can still enforce laws against blocking fire lanes, dumping waste, or threatening public safety, while expanding safe parking options.
