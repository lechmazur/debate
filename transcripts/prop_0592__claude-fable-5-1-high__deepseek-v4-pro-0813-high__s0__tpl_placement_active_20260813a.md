Adversaries do not plan around Europe's slowest vote; they plan around Europe's weakest enforcement.
[C1.3] Coercion without consent is a poor foundation for economic warfare. Sanctions can require a state to sever its main energy supplier, abandon export markets, or expose its firms to retaliation. That fractures the very legitimacy a sanctions regime needs to be renewed and tightened over years.
[C1.4] Unanimity does not create the Cyprus or Hungary disputes; it forces them to be resolved before the Union speaks. QMV would not remove those underlying grievances. [C1.5] Trade defence is not a strong analogy. Those are routine administrative measures under existing rules, not coercive foreign-policy sanctions that can hit a member's strategic supplies. The EU has sustained costly Russia measures because every state owns them. That durability, not an extra few weeks of speed, is what makes sanctions meaningful.
[C1.6] A fast vote with fragmented execution is not faster action; it is faster paper.
