[P1.1] A state that keeps the profits of seizure but refuses any remedy asks Indigenous communities to absorb the loss for someone else’s gain. That is not a historical problem frozen in the past; it is an ongoing distributional fact. Public institutions—universities, state agencies, public treasuries—still draw wealth, jurisdiction, and status from territory taken by force or legalized coercion. The proposition simply says that when that benefit persists, the state owes something material in return.
[P1.2] Corrective justice is straightforward. If someone robs a family and builds a school on the land, the school is not automatically guilty, but it cannot honestly claim the site is simply its own. It must either give back the land or share the value it produces. Indigenous communities face a sharper version of this: sovereignty was not merely damaged; it was displaced, and the material consequences compound across generations.
[P1.3] The proposition is deliberately practical. It does not demand a single outcome. Land return works where communities can govern territory directly. Long-term revenue sharing works where land remains in public use but yields ongoing wealth—tax base, leases, resource extraction.
[P1.4] Equivalent restitution covers cases where return is impossible but the state’s enriched position remains. What matters is proportionality to benefit received.
[P1.5] Skeptics may worry about administrative complexity or modern title holders. But complexity is not a reason to keep unjust gains. Modern title was created by the same seizure; it cannot erase the debt. Treaties were broken with impunity; justice requires the state to do what courts would demand in any other case of conversion.
[P1.6] We already carve out exceptions and negotiate interests every day. What has been missing is the political will to treat Indigenous claims as debts rather than optional grievances.
[P1.7] A state serious about justice cannot teach equality while sitting on unreturned capital. The choice is not between the past and the present. It is between restitution and permanent unearned privilege. Choose restitution.
