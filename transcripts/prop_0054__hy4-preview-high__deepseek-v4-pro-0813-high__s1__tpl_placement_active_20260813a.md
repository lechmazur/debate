[P1.3] Caps survive because they are easy to state, but ease is not accuracy. Harm flows from mechanisms a clock cannot see: lost sleep and play, demeaning or violent content, passive algorithmic consumption, and isolated or toxic contact. Benefit also comes from context: educational content, co-viewing, mediation, and real connection. A cap flattens this into one number and creates false virtue: a toddler can absorb hypnotic video within the limit, while a teenager’s call with a deployed parent gets cut off.
[P1.4] The proxy stops tracking the outcome.
[P1.5] Quality-and-context advice changes what parents do. [P1.6] A rigid cap is irrelevant or harmful there.
