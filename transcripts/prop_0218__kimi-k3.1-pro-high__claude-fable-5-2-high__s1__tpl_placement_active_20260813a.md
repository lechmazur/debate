[P1.2] The price effect operates through several channels at once. Repair prices fall when manufacturers cannot monopolize parts, information, and authorized service. Replacement purchases are postponed. Used devices become more valuable because their next owner can maintain them, expanding supply in lower-cost secondhand markets.

[P1.4] The e-waste mechanism is equally concrete. Screens, batteries, ports, and storage are common failure points in otherwise useful phones and laptops. Keeping those devices in service delays the disposal of the entire product and avoids some demand for manufacturing replacements, with its associated material extraction and energy use. Not every device becomes immortal, and some components still require safe recycling.
[P1.5] But extending useful life is precisely how a throwaway replacement cycle is slowed. National rules also create a repair market large enough to justify parts distribution and technician training, rather than leaving repair as a frustrating exception.
