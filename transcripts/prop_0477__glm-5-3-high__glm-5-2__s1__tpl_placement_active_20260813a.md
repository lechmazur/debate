Market redundancy clusters: multiple cables follow the same favorable seabed routes and land at the same convenient stations, because companies optimize for revenue, not survivability. Redundancy that shares chokepoints is redundancy in name only.
[P2.2] That is precisely the gap only state investment fills—strategic dispersal, hardened landing sites, sovereign repair capacity—because no shareholder demands it and no adversary attacks what's cheap to lose.
[P2.3] Second, the "cables break all the time and the system absorbs it" point actually proves my case. Accidental failures are uncorrelated: an anchor strike in the Pacific doesn't coordinate with a trawler in the Atlantic. The system is engineered for that—diverse routing handles scattered faults. Hostile action is correlated by design: an adversary hits multiple links, at chokepoints, simultaneously, and times it with jamming.
[P2.4] A architecture tested against random accidents tells us almost nothing about its behavior under deliberate, synchronized attack. The Baltic cable incidents of recent years weren't paralyzing—but they demonstrated exactly the probing, deniable, below-threshold interference that gray-zone strategy rewards, and market incentives respond to none of it.
[P2.5] Third, satellite fallback: commercial satellite capacity is a rounding error against intercontinental fiber volumes. You can keep embassies online; you cannot keep banking settlement, cloud infrastructure, and allied data flows alive. "Additional fallback" is technically true and strategically meaningless.
[P2.6] Finally, the "you can't protect cables with cables, you need frigates" inversion has the sequence backwards. Surface assets deter when they can attribute and respond—but cable sabotage is deniable by design. A frigate cannot sit over every kilometer of seabed, while redundancy protects every route simultaneously, whether or not anyone saw the attacker. And repair ships don't need a destroyer escort to work; they need spares, diverse routes, and hardened stations—the very things this motion funds.
[P2.7] The choice isn't connective tissue versus body. It's a body that can coordinate versus hardened buildings that cannot.
