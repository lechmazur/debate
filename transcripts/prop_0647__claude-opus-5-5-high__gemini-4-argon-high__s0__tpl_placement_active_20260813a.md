[P1.1] Every urban freeway eventually reaches a decision point. The concrete is crumbling, the interchanges are failing, and the city must choose between rebuilding bigger and taking it down. The motion asks which answer should come up more often. Not always, just more often.
[P1.2] My case is simple: widening routinely fails at the one thing it promises, while removal routinely delivers things widening never can.
[P1.3] First, widening defeats itself. Add lanes in a dense metro and you make peak driving cheaper, so people respond. They move trips out of off-peak hours, abandon transit, and travel to farther destinations, while new development clusters along the corridor. Within a few years the lanes fill.
[P1.4] Houston's Katy Freeway is the textbook case. It was widened into one of the broadest highways anywhere at a cost of billions, yet peak commutes got slower within a few years. [P1.5] If CON defends widening, they must explain why their project escapes a pattern this consistent.
[P1.6] Second, removal works better than the fear suggests. The same logic runs in reverse. Take capacity away and some trips disappear, shift in time, or spread across a street grid. San Francisco's Embarcadero Freeway came down after the 1989 earthquake, and the predicted gridlock never arrived.
[P1.7] The waterfront became one of the city's most prized districts. Seoul tore out an elevated highway over the Cheonggyecheon stream and gained a beloved linear park. Milwaukee and Rochester replaced freeway segments with boulevards and reclaimed land for housing.
[P1.8] Third, consider the land itself. A freeway through downtown occupies acres of the region's most valuable real estate. That land produces noise, pollution and no property tax. Many of these routes were deliberately cut through Black and immigrant neighborhoods.
[P1.9] Removal returns that land to homes, jobs and tax base.
[P1.10] So here is the comparison. Widening spends billions to buy back the same congestion within a decade. Removal spends comparable money on land, safety and revenue that compound over time. [clipped]
