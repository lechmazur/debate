[P1.1] The core difference between these two policies is the difference between a guarantee and a permission slip. A four-day workweek gives every employee a concrete, scheduled, recurring unit of rest. Unlimited PTO gives them an ambiguous invitation to negotiate time off against their manager's expectations, their workload, and their colleagues' judgment. Structure beats vagueness, and that single fact decides this debate.
[P1.2] Consider how each policy actually behaves in practice. Unlimited PTO is notorious for producing *less* vacation taken, not more. When there's no defined balance, there's no entitlement to spend — only a social calculation: Will I look uncommitted? Is my teammate taking less?
[P1.3] The default becomes working, and the policy quietly rewards the anxious and the overworked. Tellingly, unlimited PTO also erases accrued-vacation liability from company books, which is why it's often adopted as a cost-saving measure dressed up as generosity. Employees sense this, and a perk that feels like a trick does nothing for retention.
[P1.4] A four-day week inverts every one of those failure modes. Rest is the default, not a request. It's universal, so no one is penalized for using it. It's coordinated — the whole team is off together — so nobody returns to an avalanche of unanswered messages, the very thing that makes vacations stressful.
[P1.5] Large multi-company trials in recent years, notably in the UK, reported meaningful drops in burnout and resignations, with most participating firms choosing to keep the schedule. That pattern is exactly what you'd expect from a benefit people can actually use.
[P1.6] Finally, the motion specifies a *pilot* — a bounded, measurable experiment. You can track retention, sick days, and output before and after, and reverse course if needed. Unlimited PTO offers no such clarity: usage is uneven, effects are diffuse, and rollback feels like a betrayal.
[P1.7] If the goal is retention and well-being, choose the policy that delivers rest by design rather than dangling it as a dare.
