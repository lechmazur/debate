[P1.1] When a child opens a modern social or video platform, they enter an asymmetric contest. On one side is an underdeveloped prefrontal cortex, still learning impulse control and emotional regulation. On the other is a predictive engine trained on vast troves of behavioral data to maximize watch time and capture attention at all costs.
[P1.2] Today, app makers offer parents a false choice: either ban an app entirely—isolating a child from peers and digital learning—or hand them a hyper-personalized recommendation feed engineered for compulsive engagement. The rudimentary controls platforms currently provide, such as blunt screen-time caps or coarse content filters, treat only the symptoms. They leave the actual engine intact. Parents cannot simply turn off the algorithmic "For You" stream while keeping the messaging or search functions that their children actually need.
[P1.3] Empowering parents to block or delay algorithmic recommendations changes that dynamic. It restores intentionality. By giving parents the option to disable predictive feeds, an app transforms from a hypnotic slot machine into an active utility: a place where a child deliberately searches for content, communicates with friends, or views chronological posts from accounts they intentionally chose to follow. Parents can then introduce algorithmic features gradually, matching the technology to their specific child's maturity rather than an arbitrary corporate default.
[P1.4] This control belongs with families, not product teams. No software engineer in Silicon Valley can know when a twelve-year-old is uniquely vulnerable to depressive spirals, disordered eating, or compulsive rabbit holes. Parents bear the lifelong legal, moral, and emotional responsibility for their children’s development. Denying them the levers to moderate the very systems curating their children’s reality is indefensible.
[P1.5] The central question of this debate is simple: who should decide how a child’s attention is shaped—the parents invested in their well-being, or the engagement algorithms optimized to profit from their screen time?
