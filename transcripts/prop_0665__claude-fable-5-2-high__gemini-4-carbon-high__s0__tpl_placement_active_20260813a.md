Donor eggs cut maternal transmission risk to zero. Mitochondrial replacement therapy is therefore not "the one intervention that stops the disease" [P1.5]. Compared to donor-egg IVF, MRT prevents zero additional illness; its sole marginal benefit is preserving one parent's genetic tie.
[C1.2] Worse, choosing MRT over donor eggs actively reintroduces danger to the child. Nuclear transfer is no clean battery swap [P1.9]. Trace mutated mitochondria transfer alongside the nucleus and can out-replicate healthy donor mitochondria as cells divide (a documented hazard called reversion), reviving the very fatal disorder MRT aimed to prevent. And unlike embryo screening [P1.6], which selects healthy existing embryos, MRT permanently alters the female germline, passing residual mutations and untested nuclear-mitochondrial pairings to unconsenting future generations.
[C1.3] When other patients lack usable eggs after chemotherapy or ovarian failure, public clinics offer donor eggs, not bespoke germline engineering. Public medicine should never pay more to give a child worse protection against lethal disease.
[C1.4] Moreover, the intensive-care savings claimed in [P1.5] rest on a false baseline. Measured against donor-egg IVF rather than unassisted conception, MRT saves zero hospital spending. Instead, it adds steep costs for specialist micromanipulation, extra donor eggs lost during nuclear transfer, decades of follow-up, and intensive care whenever reversion strikes. Under finite public budgets, those resources come straight from living mitochondrial patients (most carrying nuclear or spontaneous mutations MRT cannot prevent) who urgently need respiratory and palliative care, and from infertile couples already rationed out of routine IVF.
[C1.5] Finally, withholding public health subsidies does not abandon families to rogue clinics [P1.8]. Early infant outcomes [P1.7] cannot prove lifetime or multigenerational safety.
