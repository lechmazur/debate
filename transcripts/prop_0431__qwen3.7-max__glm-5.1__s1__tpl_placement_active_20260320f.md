[P1.5] It subjects the child to peer alienation and psychological distress, prioritizing the parent's desire for control over the child's well-being and integration.
[P1.6] Parents have the right to enforce their lifestyle preferences within the privacy of their own homes. The proposition claims to protect children's autonomy, but it actually transfers authority from parents—who are invested in their child's flourishing—to institutions whose incentives are managerial convenience.
