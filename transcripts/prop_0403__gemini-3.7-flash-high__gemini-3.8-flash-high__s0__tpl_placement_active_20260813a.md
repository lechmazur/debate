[P1.1] Public financing of sports stadiums is not unconditional charity; it is a multi-decade civic investment funded by working taxpayers. When franchise owners accept hundreds of millions of dollars in municipal bonds, land gifts, and infrastructure subsidies, they enter into a reciprocal partnership with their host city. Allowing teams to unilaterally abandon those communities violates basic financial fairness and leaves taxpayers holding the bill.
[P1.2] First, public subsidies create long-term municipal debt and stranded assets. Stadium financing is routinely backed by 30-year municipal bonds, special tax districts, and dedicated infrastructure spending. When a franchise relocates, the host community is frequently left servicing debt on specialized, underutilized concrete for decades while the owner captures massive windfall appreciation in a new market. A binding community approval process guarantees that the public who underwrote the enterprise cannot be structurally disenfranchised while still paying for it.
[P1.3] Second, unconditional relocation rights fuel a predatory cycle of civic extortion. Franchise owners regularly weaponize the threat of departure to extract ever-larger subsidies, pitting cash-strapped cities against one another in a destructive race to the bottom. Subsidies are sold to voters on the explicit promise of enduring local economic activity, civic identity, and shared prosperity. When relocation is unilateral, owners socialize the investment risks and privatize the capital gains.
[P1.4] A binding approval process does not forbid relocation; it simply balances the scales of power. It establishes a necessary principle of commercial reciprocity: if a team demands total, unconstrained private mobility, it is free to privately finance its own facilities. But when a franchise voluntarily accepts major public capital, it surrenders the right to unilaterally walk away. Requiring binding community consent ensures that those who paid for the foundation of a franchise have a decisive voice in its future.
