[P1.1] A democracy can survive bad arguments, even offensive jokes. It cannot survive a world where any recording of a public official can be counterfeit and no one can tell. That is the case for banning hyper-realistic political deepfakes.
[P1.2] The core harm is not that deepfakes are false. False speech is everywhere and often protected. The harm is that they counterfeit the evidentiary raw material of politics. A fake audio clip of a candidate bargaining for a bribe, or a fake video of a mayor ordering police to attack protesters, does not merely persuade; it fabricates the event itself.
[P1.3] It spreads before fact-checkers can work, and its residue lasts: even after debunking, millions remember the image and millions more say “maybe.” Worse, the same technology gives corrupt officials a ready-made defense—“that recording is fake”—so authentic evidence becomes contestable. Call it the liar’s dividend. The result is not more free debate but less: citizens retreat into tribe because no shared facts remain.
[P1.4] The motion is narrow enough. It targets hyper-realistic political deepfakes—material designed or reckless enough to be taken as real, about public political figures and events. It does not ban criticism, exaggeration, or obvious parody. Satire that needs to fool people to work is not satire; it is deception wearing a joke’s costume.
[P1.5] The cost to anonymous speech is likewise modest: anonymous pamphlets, memes, text, caricature, and plainly styled fake performances all survive. Anonymity protects the speaker; it does not entitle anyone to manufacture fake evidence.
[P1.6] Democracies already prohibit impersonation, fraud, and deceptive election practices. A deepfake ban is the digital extension of a familiar rule: you may argue fiercely, but you may not forge the facts. The occasional chilling effect is real, but it is outweighed by preserving the possibility of informed self-government. When the choice is between some satire lost and the public’s ability to believe its own eyes and ears, democracy must choose the latter.
