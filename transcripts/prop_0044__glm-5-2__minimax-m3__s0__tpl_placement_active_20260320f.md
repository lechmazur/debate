[P1.1] Every smart device carries a hidden expiration date. The manufacturer knows it when the product ships; the buyer does not. That information gap is the entire case for this proposition.

Once app updates cease, core functions degrade. Yet today, a consumer comparing two products sees price, specs, and warranty terms—but not the single number that determines how long the device remains viable.
[P1.3] Support lifespan is a material fact, arguably more consequential than megapixels or storage capacity. Manufacturers already plan these timelines internally; the question is whether buyers get to see them before money changes hands.

[P1.6] Physical packaging matters specifically. Terms buried in a website FAQ or a EULA do not reach the buyer at the moment of decision. A label on the box does.
[P1.7] The stakes extend beyond individual purchases. Premature obsolescence drives e-waste, and consumers who cannot compare lifespans cannot push back against it. Disclosure at the point of sale is the simplest mechanism for aligning what manufacturers know with what buyers need.
