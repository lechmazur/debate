[P1.1] Live face recognition on AR glasses does not merely photograph a street. It names the people on it, in real time, without their knowledge and without a realistic way to refuse. That is a different technology from a phone in a pocket or a posted CCTV box, and it should be banned in most public settings.
[P1.2] Public anonymity is how a person attends a protest, leaves an abuser, walks into a clinic, or simply exists among strangers without becoming a searchable file. The glasses look ordinary. They sit on a face. They can run continuously.
[P1.3] The person being identified cannot put them away, cannot step out of frame, and cannot consent after the match has already appeared in the wearer's overlay.
[P1.4] The overlay is the injury. Pair a live match with public records, leaked dumps, or social profiles and a stranger becomes a name, a workplace, an old arrest, sometimes an address. A crowded bar or a school pickup line becomes a lookup table. False matches invite confrontation.
[P1.5] True matches invite stalking and selective targeting of journalists, witnesses, and political opponents. Before any of that happens, people already change behavior: they avoid crowds, cover their faces, or stay home. That chill is itself a loss of public life.
[P1.6] A narrowly defined professional exception can survive this ban because it is bounded by role, place, and accountability: a clinician inside a hospital, a posted security post, an officer under warrant and audit. Consumer use on a sidewalk, in a park, on a subway, at a school gate is none of those things. It is one private party converting everyone else's identity into a personal database.
[P1.7] If we wait until the glasses are everywhere, privacy becomes the exception and identification becomes the air. Ban the public default now. Carve the few professions that can justify the intrusion. Other people's faces are not scenery.
