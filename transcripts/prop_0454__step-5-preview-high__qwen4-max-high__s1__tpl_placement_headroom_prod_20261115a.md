[P1.1] Unemployment is not a moral failing; it is a market event that arrives faster than legislatures can respond. When a shock hits, workers lose income within weeks, while parliaments debate relief for months. Automatic stabilizers that expand cash transfers as unemployment rises remove that lethal delay. They do not create new bureaucracy; they simply enlarge an existing pipeline the moment demand collapses.
[P1.2] The alternative is discretionary austerity or delayed stimulus. History is littered with premature cutoffs and package delays that turn cyclical pain into permanent scarring. A worker who loses housing and healthcare during a six-month legislative lag does not simply pause consumption; they fall into a trap from which recovery takes years. Cash transfers prevent that destruction.
[P1.3] They keep families attached to their communities, maintain consumption floors, and give firms a reason to retain workers rather than fire them into a spiraling labor market.
[P1.4] Think of extended unemployment benefits that automatically lengthen duration when jobless claims cross a threshold, or tax credits that grow as payroll income falls. They are self-financing in the only sense that matters: they preserve the ecosystem that generates future revenue.
[P1.5] Yes, debt rises. But debt incurred during a downturn is not a waste; it is insurance purchased at exactly the moment the house is burning. A deeper recession shrinks the tax base, raises future welfare costs, and destroys productive capacity, generating far worse fiscal arithmetic over time. The counterfactual is not a balanced budget; it is a longer depression.
[P1.6] The proposition asks governments to trade temporary debt for human dignity and economic continuity. That trade is not merely acceptable—it is the mark of a rational state. Automatic stabilizers do not spend money because government loves spending. They spend because the alternative is letting people drown while politicians debate the price of the rope.
