[P1.1] Public funding requires public accountability. Religious schools that accept taxpayer money must meet the same civic, curricular, and non-discrimination standards as public schools, or they should not receive the funds. This is not an attack on religion but a defense of democracy and the common good.
[P1.2] Without strong civic standards, publicly funded religious schools could teach that only adherents of their faith are true citizens, or that secular law is subordinate to religious doctrine. In Poland, state-funded Catholic institutions have explicitly taught that gender equality is a threat to traditional family structures, fostering social divisions that public education should heal, not deepen. Such narratives, when subsidized by all taxpayers, undermine the pluralism sustaining democratic societies.
[P1.3] Without rigorous curricular standards, students may graduate without understanding evolution, with distorted historical narratives, or without basic scientific literacy. In Israel, publicly funded ultra-Orthodox schools have produced generations of students with minimal secular education, leaving them ill-prepared for higher education and the modern workforce while still drawing public resources.
[P1.4] Without non-discrimination standards, schools could use public money to exclude LGBTQ+ students, deny admission to non-believers, or fire teachers based on personal characteristics. In the UK, faith schools receiving public funds have faced repeated accusations of discriminating against LGBTQ+ students and staff.
[P1.5] The solution is clear: conditional funding preserves religious character while protecting public values. Schools can teach theology, but must also teach civic responsibility, scientific inquiry, and respect for all. The alternative—unconditional funding—forces taxpayers to subsidize institutions that may actively undermine the shared values making diverse democracies thrive.

[C1.3] My case rests on three points.

[C1.7] The motion fixates on sameness and ignores results.
[C1.8] Third, the pluralism trap. PRO invokes democracy and the common good, but a democracy proves its confidence by tolerating institutions that teach differently. [C1.9] Force every funded school into one civic mold and you don't heal division; you teach minorities that the state funds only those who think like the majority.
[C1.10] The right principle is a safety floor plus equal, outcome-based treatment. The motion offers a demanding ceiling that funds religious schools in name and abolishes them in substance.
