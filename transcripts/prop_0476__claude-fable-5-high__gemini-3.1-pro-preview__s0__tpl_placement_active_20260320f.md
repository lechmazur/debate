[P1.1] Low Earth orbit is becoming a junkyard, and right now the cost of trashing it is zero. A company can launch a satellite, profit for years, and abandon it as a derelict tumbling at seventeen thousand miles per hour—a permanent hazard everyone else must dodge. That is a textbook externality, and deorbit bonds are the textbook fix: post money up front, get it back when you retire your spacecraft responsibly, forfeit it if you don't.
[P1.2] The mechanism matters because the alternatives have failed. Voluntary guidelines like the twenty-five-year deorbit norm have been honored mostly in the breach; compliance has historically been mediocre, and there is no enforcement when a defunct operator simply shrugs. Fines after the fact don't work either—a bankrupt company can't pay one, and bankruptcy is exactly when satellites get abandoned. A bond solves this elegantly: the money exists before the failure.
[P1.3] Even if the operator dissolves, the forfeited funds can pay for active debris removal of the very object left behind. The polluter pays, in advance, by design.
[P1.4] The stakes are not hypothetical. The 2009 Iridium–Cosmos collision created thousands of trackable fragments from a single impact. With tens of thousands of commercial satellites planned this decade, each derelict raises collision risk for everyone—including the operators who behave responsibly. Kessler-style cascade risk doesn't need to be certain to justify cheap insurance against it; it only needs to be plausible, and it is.
[P1.5] Nor is this burdensome. Bonds are refundable for responsible actors—a well-run operator gets every dollar back, perhaps with interest from escrow. The only companies who pay are those who litter. That's not a tax on space commerce; it's a filter that rewards good engineering, prices risk honestly, and lets insurers and capital markets do what they do best.
[P1.6] Orbits are a shared commons. We require bonds for mines and oil wells on Earth for exactly this reason. The question isn't whether to internalize this cost—it's why we haven't already.
