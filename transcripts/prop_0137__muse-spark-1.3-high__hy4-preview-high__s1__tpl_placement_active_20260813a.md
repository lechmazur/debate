[P1.1] When an employer markets egg freezing as its flagship women's benefit, it tells ambitious young women that the path to success runs through postponing motherhood.
[P1.2] That message only counts as empowerment if the same employer also makes early motherhood viable. Without generous paid leave, affordable childcare help, flexible return paths, and coverage for trying now — like fertility treatment and pregnancy support — freezing is not one option among many. It is the only option the company will pay to make easy.
[P1.3] Consider a 29-year-old analyst who wants a child soon. If her firm offers weeks of patchy leave, rigid hours, and no childcare support, but will fully pay to freeze her eggs, the nudge is obvious: wait. That is steering, not choice. And it conveniently serves the employer, which keeps a full-time worker now and pushes the cost and disruption of caregiving into an indefinite future.
[P1.4] A benefit that aligns corporate convenience with a painful, uncertain medical procedure should not be sold as liberation.
[P1.5] Because freezing is arduous and uncertain. It means weeks of hormonal stimulation, surgical retrieval, annual storage fees, and later rounds of in-vitro fertilization with no guarantee of a baby, especially if eggs are frozen later or few in number. To headline that gamble while neglecting proven supports that help women have healthy pregnancies and keep careers on track is misleading and inequitable. It helps highest-paid professionals who can afford to use stored eggs later, while doing nothing for the scheduler, nurse, or retail manager who needs leave and childcare today.
[P1.6] The proposition does not ban egg freezing. It sets a basic test of good faith: do not celebrate delay as your signature solution for women unless you also fund the conditions for not delaying. Pair the technologies. Then women can genuinely choose timing, rather than having delay chosen for them.

[C1.5] The real test is comparative: does this benefit leave women with more choice than they had without it? For many, yes. For lower-wage workers, the answer is to demand broader supports instead, not to cancel an option for those who have it. Egg freezing is not the enemy of family-friendly work; it is one more tool.
[C1.6] We should welcome every additive benefit and keep arguing for more, not impose a gag rule that makes an imperfect gift the enemy of the good.
