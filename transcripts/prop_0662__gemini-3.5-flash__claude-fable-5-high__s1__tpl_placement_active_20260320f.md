[P1.1] The modern labor market has evolved rapidly, but our social safety nets remain stuck in the twentieth century. Millions of platform workers—rideshare drivers, delivery couriers, and digital freelancers—generate immense economic value, yet they are systematically excluded from basic protections like unemployment insurance and retirement security. This is not a necessity of the modern economy; it is a policy failure. The solution lies in modernized administration: requiring platform work to count toward social insurance using standardized earnings records.
[P1.2] This policy solves two critical problems. First, it overcomes the administrative hurdle of the gig economy. Opponents often claim that tracking sporadic, multi-platform income is too complex for state agencies. Standardized earnings records solve this by creating an automated, digital reporting pipeline.

[P1.4] Second, this policy ends the massive public subsidy currently enjoyed by tech platforms. Today, these companies externalize their labor risks. When a driver is injured or a recession hits, the financial burden falls on the worker or on public emergency assistance, while the platforms pocket the savings from unpaid payroll taxes. Integrating platform work into social insurance forces these businesses to pay their fair share, expanding the tax base and stabilizing the funding of our collective safety nets.
[P1.5] We must reject the false dichotomy between flexible work and basic economic security. Dignity in retirement and safety during unemployment must not be contingent on having a traditional nine-to-five job. [C1.3] Second, the policy backfires on the workers it claims to protect. [C1.4] Third, and most perversely, this scheme entrenches misclassification. By bolting a thin layer of social insurance onto contractor status, governments hand platforms a permanent legal shield: "our workers already have protections." [C1.5] The disagreement is simple. The other side sees an administrative gap that data pipelines can close. The real gap is conceptual: unemployment insurance presumes an employer, a job, and a separation. Platform work has none of the three.
[C1.6] Forcing the fit produces fraud, regressive costs, and a frozen status quo—not security.
