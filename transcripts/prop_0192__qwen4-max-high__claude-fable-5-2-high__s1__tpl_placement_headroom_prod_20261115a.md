[P1.2] If a paper company can sue in its own name, refusing a right against chaining to a grieving elephant is not metaphysics — it is a choice about whom the law protects.
[P1.3] The relevant capacities are not speculative. Great apes recognize themselves in mirrors, plan ahead, and transmit local traditions. [P1.4] Confinement does not merely restrict these creatures; it dismantles the very thing they are. A tank cannot hold a pod. A chain cannot hold a home range. Decades in a laboratory cage cannot hold a mind built for foraging, politics, and family.

[P1.7] Only a right, held by the prisoner rather than the owner, changes who must justify confinement.

Either the right forbids that, which is cruelty, or it permits confinement whenever humans judge it beneficial.
[C1.3] That is welfare law wearing a costume. Guardians [P1.5] do not rescue it: a guardian is a human deciding what the animal wants. Personhood changes who litigates, not who decides.
[C1.4] Second, the foundation is dangerous. [P1.6] calls species membership "bare" and lets capacities do the work. [C1.5] And a capacity line cannot hold: pigs, corvids, and octopuses are already knocking. PRO must either draw an arbitrary circle around three charismatic groups or accept consequences the motion disowns.
[C1.6] Third, "the cages remain" is outdated. Several countries have ended invasive research on great apes, banned wild animals in circuses, and prohibited displaying whales and dolphins, all by ordinary statute and with no metaphysical revolution. Legislatures can write exceptions for sanctuaries, rescue, and conservation breeding; an abstract right handed to courts cannot.
[C1.7] Corporations and rivers [P1.1] prove my point: they are human-built tools, run entirely by humans for human purposes. Animals are protected by duties we impose on ourselves. Write the duties; skip the fiction.
