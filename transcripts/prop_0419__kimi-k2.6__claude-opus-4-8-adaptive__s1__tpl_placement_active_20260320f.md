[P1.1] When a company traps a customer inside an automated loop during a billing dispute, a cancellation, or a safety complaint, it is not deploying efficient technology—it is exploiting an imbalance of power. The firm controls the interface, the timeline, and the rules of engagement, while the user faces immediate financial or physical stakes. Requiring a fast path to a human agent in these three specific situations is a narrow, necessary correction to a market failure that routinely externalizes harm onto consumers with no leverage.
[P1.2] Chatbots are useful for tracking orders or resetting passwords, but they lack judgment, authority, and accountability. A billing dispute can involve fraudulent charges or predatory overbilling; a chatbot that misapplies policy can cost someone rent money or medical funds before an appeal ever reaches a person. Cancellations are even more vulnerable to distortion because companies have a direct financial incentive to engineer friction and delay. We have all encountered the digital maze where every click leads back to a help article and the phone number is buried three menus deep.
[P1.3] That is not customer service; it is a retention strategy dressed up as innovation, profitable only because the customer cannot walk away.
[P1.4] Safety complaints are the most acute. Reporting a hazardous product, an impaired driver, or a contaminated shipment requires immediate escalation and contextual nuance that scripted automation cannot reliably provide. A delay of hours—or even minutes—can translate directly into injury. No algorithm should stand between a user and a human when the warning involves physical danger.
[P1.5] The proposition does not ban chatbots. It simply says that when the stakes involve money owed, money being drained, or physical danger, the user must be able to reach a human without a scavenger hunt. That standard protects fundamental consumer rights without stifling legitimate efficiency, and it places the burden of accountability back where it belongs: on the company that profits from the relationship.
