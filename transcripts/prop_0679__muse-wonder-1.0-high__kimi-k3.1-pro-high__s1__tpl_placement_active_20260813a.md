[P1.1] In criminal court, evidence does not need to be efficient or polished; it needs to be answerable. Someone must be able to stand behind it, explain how it was made, and be challenged on where it might be wrong. AI-generated summaries, translations, and reconstructions fail that test by default, which is why they should be excluded unless a human expert independently verifies them.
[P1.2] These tools share a dangerous flaw: they produce fluent output that can depart from the source without clearly signaling uncertainty. A summary of an interrogation or body-camera transcript can drop a qualifier, compress a timeline, or omit an exculpatory detail. A translation of a wiretap can flatten ambiguous slang, dialect, or sarcasm into a single incriminating meaning. A reconstruction is worse still: enhance a blurred image, clean muffled audio, or rebuild a scene, and the system fills gaps with plausible invention — a sharper license plate, a clearer word, a more complete face.
[P1.3] The jury then sees invention presented as record.
[P1.4] That creates a reliability and confrontation gap no instruction can cure. You cannot cross-examine a model about its prompt, its version, its training limits, or why it chose this word over another. The presenting lawyer can shrug and say the tool produced it. There is no oath, no professional license at stake, no witness whose credibility can be tested.
[P1.5] Yet the output arrives looking authoritative, neatly formatted, and easy to believe — precisely the kind of evidence most likely to be overweighted when liberty turns on proof beyond a reasonable doubt.
[P1.6] Presumptive inadmissibility is not a ban. It simply places the burden on the party offering the evidence to do what criminal proof already demands: bring a human expert who independently checks the output against the original material, explains what was added, altered, or inferred, and testifies subject to cross-examination. If a translation is sound, a qualified translator can verify it. If a summary is faithful, a reviewer can attest to it.
[P1.7] If a reconstruction is reliable, an expert can separate observation from generation.

[P2.1] The alternative is to presume accuracy and test it later if anyone objects. In a criminal trial, that reverses the burden. A translation is 'faithful' only after someone qualified compares it to the recording; before that, faithfulness is precisely what is unknown. Verification is not a ritual beside accuracy—it is how accuracy is established when output rewrites words, selects details, or supplies new ones.
[P2.2] The comparison to a photograph or spreadsheet fails for the same reason. A photograph does not paraphrase a scene, choose a synonym for ambiguous slang, or omit a sentence it deems unimportant. A formula can be inspected and recalculated. A generative summary or reconstruction asks jurors to accept interpretive choices hidden inside fluent output.
[P2.3] Disclosing a prompt does not explain those choices, and running the same tool again proves only that it can repeat itself, not that it was right.
[P2.4] Proportional scrutiny without a presumption leaves that work to jurors and opposing counsel after the polished version has already framed the story. Counsel cannot spot an omitted qualifier in hours of audio by glancing at the original, and a resource-poor defendant is least able to hire the linguist needed to expose a bad wiretap translation. The presumption puts the cost on the party asking the court to rely on the shortcut—usually the prosecution, which must prove guilt beyond a reasonable doubt.
[P2.5] Human experts can err or rubber-stamp, but that argues for independent, testifiable verification, not none. The expert compares output to source, identifies additions and ambiguities, and faces cross-examination, oath, and professional consequence. A flawed but answerable check is not improved by removing it. An expert's endorsement can be challenged; a model's confidence cannot.
[P2.6] Different uses determine how easily the presumption is rebutted, not whether it should exist. A locator summary tied to preserved footage may need straightforward confirmation. A reconstruction inventing detail no original can validate will rarely qualify. Scaled verification is reliability before admission, not after the verdict is in view.

[P4.4] The presumption therefore does the precise sorting criminal proof requires. Faithful, verifiable AI assistance gets in through an answerable witness.
