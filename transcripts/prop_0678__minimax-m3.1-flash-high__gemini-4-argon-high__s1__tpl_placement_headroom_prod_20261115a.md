[P1.1] Monitoring remote workers is not a minor HR convenience; it is a shift in who controls the workplace, and the law cannot keep pace with the tools. The motion offers a workable principle: no AI-driven video or keystroke surveillance of workers without genuine opt-in consent and firm limits. Adopt it and the legitimate uses of remote-work measurement survive intact. Refuse it and every employer gains unilateral access to the most intimate data it can legally obtain.
[P1.2] Start with coercion. A worker who can lose a job for declining a webcam feed has not consented; they have submitted. Consent carrying the threat of retaliation is a form, not a bargain. Genuine opt-in means a real alternative: keep the camera off, stay employed, same pay, same standing.
[P1.3] Without that, "consent" is a signature on a consent decree.
[P1.4] Second, the harms are not speculative side effects but ordinary effects of constant behavioral observation: workers self-censor, suppress breaks and legitimate private moments, and carry surveillance stress into home life. Video in home offices is worse still, because the lens catches family members, roommates, and medical episodes no employer has any claim to. Keystroke logging is less visible but more complete, reconstructing a workday minute by minute and turning fatigue and distraction into performance data.
[P1.5] Third, the legitimate benefits are served precisely by the limits the motion demands. Team-level aggregated metrics reveal bottlenecks and guide staffing without identifying the worker who looked away for a moment. Anonymized incident data catches genuine misconduct. Individual scoring is where the danger concentrates, because automated judgments punish disability, neurodivergence, caregiving, and quiet thinking — differences productivity models read as laziness.
[P1.6] So the package is clear: consent with a real opt-out, purpose limitation, minimization, short retention, no off-duty or bystander capture, and aggregate-only reporting unless a specific documented incident triggers human review. The alternative is a private surveillance apparatus deployed at software speed, with no standard, no limit, and no exit.

Voluntary monitoring remains available for legitimate security, while coercive monitoring does not become a condition of employment. CON identifies risks, but never shows that the most intimate available remedy is necessary or proportionate.
