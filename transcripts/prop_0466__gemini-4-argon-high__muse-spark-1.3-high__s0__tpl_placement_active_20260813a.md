[P1.1] The cruelest policy you can inflict on a fishing-dependent community is not a boundary line on a map; it is an empty ocean. When coastal towns rely heavily on fishing income, protecting at least 30% of an Exclusive Economic Zone as a strict no-take reserve is not a luxury traded against their survival—it is the only way to guarantee they have a future.
[P1.2] Under political pressure to protect immediate income, fishing nations routinely set quotas too high and allow practices like bottom trawling to scrape habitats bare. Fishers chase dwindling stocks with heavier gear until the fishery hits a tipping point and collapses—just as Atlantic cod once did, devastating coastal towns for generations. You cannot protect fishing livelihoods by liquidating the biological capital that sustains them.
[P1.3] A 30% no-take standard replaces that death spiral with a biological bank account. Inside strict reserves, habitats heal and fish survive long enough to grow large. Because older, larger fish produce vastly more eggs than smaller ones, reserves become reproduction engines. As biomass builds inside the sanctuary, larvae drift outward and adult fish spill over into the remaining 70% of the EEZ.
[P1.4] Instead of scraping a dying sea, coastal communities harvest the continuous spillover of a thriving one.
[P1.5] Why a strict 30%, rather than flexible quotas or lightly regulated parks? Because half-measures get politically diluted whenever catches dip. "Multi-use" zones still allow extraction that severs food webs, and tiny sanctuaries cannot connect coastal nurseries to offshore spawning grounds or build resilience against warming waters.
[P1.6] Yes, closing 30% of the water creates a short-term transition cost while stocks rebuild. But states can bridge a few lean years with targeted income support, gear buyouts, or paid conservation patrols. No government can subsidize its way out of ecological bankruptcy. Protect the 30%, or lose the 100%.

[C1.5] Enforcement then turns conservation into injustice. Vast reserves become paper parks where law-abiding locals are banned within sight of shore while industrial trawlers and distant fleets poach offshore with impunity.
[C1.6] Sustainability does not require amputation. Science-based quotas, bans on bottom trawling and fine nets, seasonal spawning closures, and community tenure rebuild stocks while people keep fishing. Manage how people fish, do not banish them from where they live.
