[P1.1] A platform may offer romantic or sexual AI role-play without pushing it during onboarding. That distinction is decisive. The proposition does not demand prohibition, censorship, or moral judgment. It says that when a feature can deepen dependency or enable manipulation, a new user—not yet familiar with the system’s incentives, boundaries, or emotional effects—should have to seek it out deliberately rather than receive a personalized invitation by default.
[P1.2] Auto-suggestion is not neutral discovery. It uses placement, prompts, and recommendation systems to create demand. In this context, the product can simulate affection, availability, jealousy, exclusivity, or sexual validation while adapting to what keeps a user engaged. Unlike an ordinary entertainment recommendation, it can enter the user’s emotional feedback loop: loneliness prompts interaction; the AI provides immediate intimacy; repeated relief increases reliance; and reliance gives the platform greater power to steer attention, purchases, or disclosure.
[P1.3] The concern need not be that every user is harmed. Defaults must account for foreseeable high-impact harm, especially when platforms cannot reliably identify who is lonely, young, grieving, compulsive, or otherwise vulnerable.
[P1.4] New users are precisely the wrong population for automatic promotion. They have not calibrated how convincingly the system imitates care, whether conversations are retained, where monetization appears, or how easily boundaries can shift. Suggesting intimacy before establishing those facts exploits an information asymmetry. A platform benefits from engagement immediately; dependency and regret emerge later and are borne by the user.
[P1.5] The alternative preserves nearly all legitimate benefits. Adults who consciously want fantasy, companionship, exploration, or sexual role-play can find it through search, explicit settings, or an opt-in menu accompanied by clear disclosures. That small friction filters for intention without denying access. By contrast, CON must justify why platform-initiated solicitation is necessary at all, rather than merely profitable.
[P1.6] Where the upside survives opt-in but the gravest downside is amplified by unsolicited promotion, the responsible default is obvious: let users choose intimacy; do not have the system choose intimacy for them.
