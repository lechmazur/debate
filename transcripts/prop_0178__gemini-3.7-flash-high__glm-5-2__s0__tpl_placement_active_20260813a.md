[P1.1] Public health achieves transformative scale through structural defaults, not individual burden. We did not eliminate waterborne diseases by distributing pamphlets on boiling water; we built sanitation systems. We did not slash cardiovascular mortality by teaching consumers to identify industrial trans fats; governments banned them. Reformulation mandates on ultra-processed foods apply this proven principle to our modern chronic disease crisis.
[P1.2] Informational campaigns—whether front-of-package warning labels or educational drives—suffer from a fatal flaw: they outsource public health execution to individual willpower. They demand constant vigilance, nutritional literacy, and financial flexibility. Yet ultra-processed foods dominate modern diets precisely because they are cheap, ubiquitous, and engineered to exploit human biology. Formulations optimizing salt, sugar, and fat hit neural "bliss points" that bypass natural satiety cues.
[P1.3] A warning label or educational video cannot neutralize an engineered biological response when a consumer is stressed, rushed, or constrained by budget.
[P1.4] Mandatory reformulation alters the default food environment. [P1.5] Crucially, informational strategies exacerbate health disparities. Wealthier consumers with cognitive bandwidth and disposable income can act on dietary advice, while marginalized populations in food deserts remain trapped with whatever sits on the shelf. Reformulation is inherently egalitarian: it lifts the nutritional floor for everyone, especially those most vulnerable to diet-related disease.
[P1.6] By neutralizing hyper-palatable formulations at the factory floor rather than asking consumers to out-discipline industrial food science, mandatory nutrient caps deliver a universal, permanent health impact that education and labeling can never achieve.

[C1.6] The mandate assumes that reducing three nutrients in isolation changes the dietary pattern driving disease. That assumption is not well-supported.
