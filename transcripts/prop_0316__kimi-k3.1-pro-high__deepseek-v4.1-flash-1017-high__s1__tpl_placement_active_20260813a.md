[C1.1] The case for this right rests on a seductive but false premise: that knowing “AI is involved” gives users meaningful control. It does not. Dating apps already sort, filter, and predict—by location, preferences, activity, paid boosts, and reported behavior. An AI label singles out one implementation detail while leaving the rest of the machinery hidden.
[C1.2] A user told “an AI ranked your profile” has learned almost nothing they can act on. The notice is either banal—of course software mediates matching—or so vague that it creates a false sense of understanding.
[C1.3] That matters because the harm described in [P1.1], mistaking engineered invisibility for personal rejection, is not cured by boilerplate disclosure. It may be aggravated: users will be invited to game an opaque ranking system, chase myths about “the algorithm,” and blame themselves when their guesses fail. What they need is accurate outcome information and effective anti-discrimination enforcement, not a legally guaranteed peek at a mechanism they cannot interpret.
[C1.4] The right is also unworkable at the level the motion demands. Ranking systems combine many signals and change constantly. A meaningful explanation would require revealing proprietary weights, sensitive inferences, and information about how other users are matched—precisely the material bad actors use to spam, scam, or manipulate visibility. A vague explanation avoids that risk only by being useless.
[C1.5] The motion cannot have it both ways: if the notice is broad, it cannot support the choices and challenges PRO promises; if it is specific enough, it creates the gaming and security harms.
[C1.6] Notice is not accountability. Individual users cannot audit a model from a pop-up. Systemic audits, regulators, and consumer-protection rules can examine bias and deception without turning every profile into a reverse-engineering project. A right to know the fact of AI is a procedural placebo: it burdens honest platforms, misleads users into thinking they have control, and hands an advantage to those who exploit the system.
