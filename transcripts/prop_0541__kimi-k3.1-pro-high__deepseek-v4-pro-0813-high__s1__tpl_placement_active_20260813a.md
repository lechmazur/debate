[P1.1] The United States should stop equating military power with the price and sophistication of individual platforms. Procurement should optimize for combat power that survives losses, adapts quickly, and can be replenished at wartime scale. Attritable systems—cheap enough to risk, lose, and replace—serve exactly that purpose. Shifting a significant share of funding toward them is not disarmament; it is correcting a portfolio dangerously biased toward scarcity.
[P1.2] First, attritable mass restores resilience. A small fleet of exquisite aircraft, ships, or vehicles can deliver extraordinary performance while remaining operationally fragile: every loss removes a large share of national capability, and replacement may take years. Distributed drones, decoys, loitering munitions, and unmanned surface systems let commanders accept losses without accepting defeat. They can saturate defenses, expose sensors, and force an adversary to reveal its positions.
[P1.3] Recent conflicts have repeatedly shown inexpensive uncrewed systems destroying or threatening far costlier equipment; the lesson is not that drones win every mission, but that concentrating capability in a few vulnerable assets creates opportunities for cheaper opponents.
[P1.4] Second, this shift improves the cost-exchange problem. If adversaries can compel us to expend scarce, expensive munitions or platforms against cheap threats, even tactical victories can become strategic exhaustion. Attritable systems let us impose costs rather than merely absorb them. Their value lies in affordable capacity: more sensors, more launchers, more decoys, and more chances to learn and attack.
[P1.5] Third, cheaper systems accelerate adaptation. Exquisite programs often lock requirements into long development cycles, while modular designs using mature components can be produced in larger numbers and updated as threats change. [C1.1] The proposal assumes cheap means replaceable, and replaceable means strategically useful. That chain breaks under real operational conditions. A force can lose many attritable systems and still lose the war if those systems never accomplish the mission.
[C1.2] Cheap platforms are cheap for a reason: limited range, payload, sensors, electronic protection, and autonomy. Against a sophisticated adversary, they will not be killed by expensive missiles one-for-one; they will be blinded by jamming, denied navigation, spoofed, or destroyed by short-range guns, lasers, and cheap interceptors. A cloud of drones that cannot communicate or find its target imposes no favorable cost exchange—it imposes wasted sorties, bandwidth, and logistics. The conflicts PRO cites involve adversaries without layered air defenses or dense electronic warfare; extrapolating that to a peer fight is exactly how procurement goes wrong.
[C1.3] Attritable mass is also not independent capability. It depends on exquisite enablers: penetrating sensors to find targets, survivable communications, tankers, electronic attack, and space-based navigation. Cutting investment in exquisite platforms degrades the very systems that make drones useful. You do not replace a stealth aircraft with a hundred drones; you replace its survivable sensor and weapons load with a more fragile network that can be interrupted at a single link.
[C1.4] Finally, a “significant share” shift creates industrial and strategic risk. High-end design, stealth manufacturing, engine production, and test infrastructure cannot be turned back on quickly once demand collapses. Cheap systems are not automatically replenishable at scale either; they compete for engines, electronics, propellants, and skilled workers. A portfolio that underfunds exquisite platforms may be left with abundant expendables and no way to strike the targets that actually decide a campaign.
[C1.5] The decisive test is not whether a cheap drone can occasionally kill a tank. It is whether the force can find, reach, and destroy the adversary’s most dangerous systems in a denied environment. Mass without mission accomplishment is just expensive clutter. Preserve the exquisite systems that create the conditions for everything else to operate.
