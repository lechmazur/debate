[P1.1] Frontline EU states do not guard their own national borders. They guard the common external border of every country in the Schengen area. That hard fact makes the status quo indefensible, and mandatory burden-sharing quotas the only fair, workable option.
[P1.2] Right now, a migrant boat landing on Lampedusa, Lesvos, or Andalucia becomes 100% the responsibility of that one coastal state for initial processing, housing, and screening. But if that person is granted asylum, they can lawfully travel and settle anywhere in the EU. All the risk, all the upfront cost is concentrated on four countries; all the resulting rights are shared equally across 27. That is a broken free rider arrangement, not a functioning system.
[P1.3] This failure hurts everyone. It traps asylum seekers in overcrowded, dangerous camps where processing can lag for years. It breaks local public services and erodes political support for asylum entirely in frontline states. And worst of all for the rest of the union, when frontline systems collapse, people do not stay put.
[P1.4] They move north undocumented, unvetted, unaccounted for—exactly the chaotic outcome every member state says it wants to avoid.
[P1.5] Quotas solve this asymmetry. They require every state to take a proportional share of verified asylum claimants during surges, matching the burden to the shared benefit of common internal borders. This is not charity. This is the basic condition for running a shared border union.
[P1.6] You cannot have open internal borders inside the club while dumping 100% of the border cost on the countries that happen to face the sea. There is no fair or stable alternative.
