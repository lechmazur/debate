[P1.1] The decisive fact is that military AI is industrial before it is algorithmic. Frontier models are trained and deployed on leading-edge accelerators, packaged with advanced memory, and produced through a toolchain—lithography, deposition, etch, inspection, metrology—that only a small allied ecosystem supplies at the frontier. Cut off that equipment, and you do not merely inconvenience a commercial market; you slow an adversary’s ability to build the compute base for autonomous swarms, real-time targeting, cyber operations, and AI-assisted command-and-control.
[P1.2] Export restrictions need not be perfect. The proposition asks whether governments should restrict, even at major cost—not whether they can hermetically seal the technology. Controls raise cost, delay, and uncertainty. An adversary can use older nodes and smuggled chips, but leading-edge training and inference become slower, more expensive, and more power-hungry.
[P1.3] In military AI, where advantage compounds through faster iteration and deployment, a few years of delay is strategically enormous. It can be the difference between facing a mature defense and facing a surprise capability.
[P1.4] The commercial cost is real: lost sales, weaker economies of scale, and pressure from allied firms. But those losses are private and measurable; the risk is public and potentially catastrophic. A company books revenue today; societies face tomorrow’s AI-enabled coercion. When the same tools serve both commercial AI and military AI, the externality justifies a security fence.
[P1.5] The strongest objection is that controls spur indigenous substitution. Sometimes they do—but substitution is the point. Building an independent toolchain takes enormous capital, years of trial-and-error, and still leaves the adversary behind the frontier. Smuggling and loopholes argue for tighter enforcement with allies, not surrender.
[P1.6] The real choice is not perfect denial versus open trade. It is whether democracies accept major but survivable commercial pain now, or let adversaries buy the industrial base for military AI. Restrict the exports.
