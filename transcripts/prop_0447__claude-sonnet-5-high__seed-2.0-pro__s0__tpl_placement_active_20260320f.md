[P1.1] The core problem this proposal solves is simple: large companies have engineered a gap between who controls the work and who pays for the worker. A delivery platform decides the routes, the prices, the ratings, the termination — but a shell subcontractor two rungs down the chain pays the social contributions, often badly or not at all. A manufacturing giant sets delivery deadlines so tight that a subcontractor can only meet them by misclassifying workers or underpaying into the pension and health systems.
[P1.2] The large company captures the value; the smallest, least capable link in the chain absorbs the compliance risk. That is not a market outcome, it is a designed liability shield.
[P1.3] The EU should require large companies to bear a higher share of social contributions when they use platforms and subcontracting chains for three linked reasons.
[P1.4] First, control implies responsibility. The entity that dictates working conditions and captures the economic surplus of the arrangement is the entity best placed to fund the worker's social protection. Shifting cost toward that entity aligns liability with actual power, correcting a mismatch that current law lets big firms exploit.
[P1.5] Second, this stops a race to the bottom. Right now, subcontracting chains are attractive precisely because they let a lead firm outsource legal and financial risk to entities too small or too transient to be meaningfully sanctioned. If a Spanish construction subcontractor vanishes after a fine, the general contractor walks away clean. Make the top of the chain jointly liable for a bigger contribution share, and the incentive to build these dodges evaporates.
[P1.6] Third, it protects public systems that everyone else pays into. When gig and subcontracted workers are underinsured, they still get sick, retire, and lose jobs — and the shortfall lands on general taxation or on underfunded pension systems, subsidized by workers whose employers paid properly. Large companies are currently free-riding on the solidarity of ordinary taxpayers and honest competitors. This proposal simply ends that subsidy.
