[P1.1] Start with the basic transaction. A streaming platform does not just transmit art; it measures consumption, sets the price, and distributes payouts from numbers only it can see. Requiring standardized viewing and payout data asks platforms to show the meter and the math to those paid by it. Today the meter is hidden.
[P1.2] A songwriter may get a quarterly statement with one line—"streaming royalties"—and a dollar amount, but not the play counts by country, subscription tier, or per-unit rate after pool allocation and deductions. She cannot tell whether ten thousand plays or ten million produced that check. That is a black box where the licensee of the work also serves as unaccountable accountant.
[P1.3] Voluntary transparency does not fix this. Dashboards are inconsistent, partial, and revocable; one service reports rough totals, another hides rate components, and none expose the allocation method. Without a standard schema, an artist cannot compare platforms, an auditor cannot test accuracy, and small rights holders cannot negotiate. Large labels may win audit rights, but independent creators—least able to absorb underpayment—have no practical remedy.
[P1.4] Standardized data fixes that. With common fields—plays, territory, plan type, revenue pool, deductions, applied rate—creators can detect errors, challenge misallocation, benchmark deals, target promotion. A podcaster paid a flat fee tied to "estimated ad revenue" could see whether downloads were undercounted or ads underpriced once impressions, completion rates, CPMs, and revenue share are disclosed. That is accountability without dictating prices.
[P1.5] The objections are manageable. This is not a demand for users' personal data or source code. It is access, for each rights holder, to standardized usage and payout records for their own catalog, with aggregation and confidentiality rules. Machine-readable standards lower costs compared with endless disputes.
[P1.6] If a company pays creators by data only it generates, they deserve the counts and formula. An unverifiable rate is a rumor. Transparency is not hostility; it is the minimum condition for paying creators fairly.
