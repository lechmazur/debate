[P1.1] A country is not a hotel. It is a shared project, and the price of joining a shared project is learning its rules and its language. That is all the motion asks: that newcomers be expected to pick up the common public language and the core civic norms—equal treatment under law, settling disputes through courts rather than clans, free speech, the equal standing of women—rather than being told these are optional extras.
[P1.2] Notice what the motion does not demand. Nobody must abandon their faith, their food, their holidays, or the language spoken at their own kitchen table. "Core civic" and "public" are the operative words. The clash is narrow and real: should a society say "we expect this of you," or "do as you please"?
[P1.3] Expectation wins for three reasons.
[P1.4] First, language is the infrastructure of a free life. A mother who cannot speak to her child's teacher, a worker who cannot read the contract he signs or the safety notice on the wall, a patient who needs a cousin to translate her diagnosis—these people are not liberated by "optional" integration. They are dependent, isolated, and easy to exploit. The expectation to learn the common language is the most pro-immigrant policy there is, because it is the gateway to everything else.
[P1.5] Second, civic norms are the only thing that protects the immigrant's own children. A society that treats the equality of daughters or the right to leave a religion as "cultural preferences" has nothing to say to the girl born here who wants a different life than her community permits. Norms you refuse to expect are norms you cannot defend.
[P1.6] Third, "optional" is not neutral. It produces parallel communities—separate schools, separate justice, separate information worlds—and those breed exactly the resentment that hands power to the hardest anti-immigrant politics. Clear expectations, paired with real help to meet them, are the stable middle.
[P1.7] Expectation is reciprocal: the host owes classes, pathways, and fairness. But a country that will not ask anything of newcomers is not generous. It is indifferent.
