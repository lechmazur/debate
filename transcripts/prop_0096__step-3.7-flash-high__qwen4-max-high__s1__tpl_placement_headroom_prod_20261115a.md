[P1.1] Performers’ voices are their most personal, irreplaceable professional asset, tied directly to their identity, career trajectory, and ability to earn a living. Current standard performer contracts rely on sweeping, vague boilerplate that grants studios indefinite, unlimited rights to replicate and deploy AI versions of actors’ voices for any purpose, across any medium, in perpetuity, with no additional notice, compensation, or input from the performer. This creates catastrophic, unaccountable harm for working performers. A mid-tier actor who signs a contract for a 2024 streaming drama might find their AI voice used to dub a 2025 low-budget horror sequel they explicitly refused to audition for, or even to generate fake audio of them endorsing a product they oppose, with zero legal recourse because the boilerplate claims “all audio rights for all derivative and future media uses.” For performers whose careers depend on the unique recognition of their vocal timbre, this is not a minor technicality—it is the unlicensed theft of their core professional property.
[P1.2] Most performers, especially new and working-class ones, have no ability to negotiate these clauses away, as they are almost universally presented with take-it-or-leave-it contracts. The opt-in consent requirement corrects this power imbalance by mandating that studios explicitly request permission for any AI voice use outside the original scope of a project, and fairly compensate performers for that additional right. This is not a radical or burdensome ask: we already require explicit consent to use a performer’s visual likeness for commercial purposes, and a voice is equally tied to personal identity and professional value.
[P1.3] Relying on boilerplate leaves performers vulnerable to an industry that has repeatedly shown it will prioritize cheap, unregulated AI tools over the rights of the workers who make its content possible.
