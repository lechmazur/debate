[P1.1] Urban gun violence is highly concentrated. In many cities, a small share of individuals and tight social networks account for most shootings. That fact decides this debate: a strategy aimed at those people can buy far more safety per dollar than adding officers across a city.
[P1.2] Focused deterrence names the narrow set of high-risk individuals, brings them in, offers services and credible exit routes, and makes clear that the next shooting will bring focused enforcement. Violence interrupters extend the same logic to the street: they mediate disputes, cool retaliatory cycles, and work in hospitals and neighborhoods where police are often the last to hear useful information. These programs treat the shooter, the potential shooter, and the feud as the unit of intervention.
[P1.3] Expanding police staffing is the opposite. It is broad, slow, and expensive. A city must recruit, train, equip, pay, and pension officers for decades. Much of the new capacity is absorbed by calls for service, traffic, mental-health encounters, and patrol coverage, not targeted gun-violence prevention.
[P1.4] The marginal officer has only a diffuse effect on homicide, and hiring locks in recurring costs while focused deterrence can be stood up with a small team and local credible messengers.
[P1.5] The cost comparison is decisive. A modest focused deterrence or violence-interruption effort can prevent shootings by interrupting retaliatory chains; preventing one retaliatory homicide already avoids enormous criminal-justice, medical, and victim costs. Officer expansion costing the same amount may add only a handful of officers per shift across a city. That is lower precision and lower leverage.
[P1.6] This is not a claim that enforcement is irrelevant. Focused deterrence uses targeted enforcement as one lever. It is a claim that the proposition wins on cost-effectiveness because it addresses the actual concentration of gun violence, while staffing expansion pays for capacity rather than precision.

[C1.8] Finally, PRO's math hides costs: the police, prosecutor, and probation time behind every lever; and a ceiling — focused deterrence reaches only group-related shootings, while staffing answers every call, every hour, for every victim.
