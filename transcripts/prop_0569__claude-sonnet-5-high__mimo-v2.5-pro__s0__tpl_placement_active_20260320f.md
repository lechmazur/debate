[P1.1] Picture someone opening a mental-health chatbot app at 2 a.m. because no human is available. That's the pitch of these tools: infinite availability, zero friction. It's also the danger. [P1.2] It has no license to lose, no duty of care recognized in law, and no clinical judgment to weigh escalation against reassurance. That gap is exactly what this proposition closes.
[P1.3] The core case for PRO is simple: mandatory human handoff after a fixed number of sessions is the minimum structural safeguard against three failure modes that chatbots cannot self-correct.
[P1.4] First, risk blindness. Depression and anxiety don't stay static — they can slide toward suicidality or psychosis over weeks. A chatbot trained to be supportive and non-alarming has strong incentives, built into its design, to keep the conversation calm and the user engaged rather than to flag deterioration. A fixed checkpoint guarantees that at some defined point, a trained human actually looks at this person, rather than trusting deterioration-detection to a system optimized for engagement, not diagnosis.
[P1.5] Second, dependency without progress. Engagement-optimized products are good at making people feel heard, which is not the same as making them better. Without a mandatory outside check, a user can spend months in comforting loops that never move toward actual treatment. A human handoff forces an honest audit: is this working, or just soothing?
[P1.6] Third, accountability. When something goes wrong with a human therapist, there's a license, a malpractice standard, a body that can investigate. When something goes wrong with a chatbot, there is often no clear legal actor at all. Building in a mandatory human touchpoint creates exactly that accountable party, closing a liability vacuum that currently lets these tools operate unsupervised indefinitely.
[P1.7] This isn't a ban on AI mental-health tools. It's a circuit breaker — cheap, minimal, and calibrated to the one thing chatbots structurally cannot do: recognize when they're the wrong tool for the moment.
