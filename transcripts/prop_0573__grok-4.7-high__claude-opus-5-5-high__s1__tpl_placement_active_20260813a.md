[P1.1] Streaming platforms hold the only complete record of how creative work is consumed and paid. Creators and rights holders get summaries they cannot audit, rates they cannot reconstruct, and rankings they cannot explain. That is not a glitch. The platform counts the plays, sets the pool, applies the formula, and sends a number.
[P1.2] The person who made the work cannot verify any step. Requiring standardized viewing and payout data is the minimum fix for a market that will not produce transparency on its own.
[P1.3] Standardization makes the requirement real. A common definition of a stream, a common audience and territory breakdown, and a common statement of the inputs behind each payout let a rights holder reconcile one service against another and against their own books. Without a shared format, each platform invents its own vocabulary and calls the fog “proprietary insight.” With it, a musician can see whether income fell because listeners left or because the split quietly changed. A film rights holder can tell whether a title is recommended or merely hosted.
[P1.4] A songwriter can challenge a missing credit with evidence instead of an unanswered ticket.
[P1.5] Voluntary dashboards have not closed the gap. Platforms publish metrics that aid retention and bury those that would expose under-counting, misallocation, or favoritism toward catalog they own. Majors sometimes buy private data deals; everyone else lives on faith. A mandate levels access.
[P1.6] It does not open recommendation code or hand over user identities. It shows the people whose work generates the revenue how that work was counted and how the money was divided—the disclosure any honest distributor owes a supplier.
[P1.7] The costs are administrative. The gains are accountability, fairer bargaining, fewer royalty fights, and a creative economy that can plan instead of guess. Honest numbers survive a standard file. Dishonest ones are why the requirement exists.

[C1.2] First, standardization assumes all streaming works alike. It doesn't. Spotify splits a revenue pool by share of streams. Netflix usually pays a flat license or production fee up front, so there is no per-view payout to reconcile.
