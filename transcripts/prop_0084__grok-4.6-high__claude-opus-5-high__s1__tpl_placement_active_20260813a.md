[P1.1] Dating apps are not message boards. Weak identity checks are therefore not a small product quirk. They are a design that invites fraud into the most intimate parts of people’s lives.
[P1.2] A profile can still be assembled from stolen photos, a borrowed name, and a rehearsed script. The person on the other end invests time, trust, and sometimes cash. [P1.3] When the first serious filter is a selfie that can be faked or a number that can be bought, the platform is selling access, not people.
[P1.4] Stronger verification—matching a live face to an ID, confirming that a real adult is behind the account—will slow sign-up and reduce anonymity. That is the point. Anonymity is a feature for posting opinions. It is a defect when the product is a date.
[P1.5] You should not remain a ghost while asking someone to meet you after dark.
[P1.6] The friction will lose some users. It will also lose scammers, impersonators, and people who need the app to stay murky. The remaining matches will be more real. Users will waste fewer months on fiction.
[P1.7] Vulnerable people will be harder to isolate and fleece. And the person who shows up will more often be the person in the photos.
[P1.8] If an industry profits from pairing strangers, it can absorb a longer onboarding to make those strangers less imaginary. Convenience is not the highest value when the next step is a human being.
