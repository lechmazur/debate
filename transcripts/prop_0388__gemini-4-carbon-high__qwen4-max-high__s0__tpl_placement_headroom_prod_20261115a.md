[P1.2] Strict youth spending limits and default protections, where spending starts locked and prompts are muted, provide essential circuit breakers against sudden household debt and early conditioning toward problem gambling.

Relying on unrestricted extraction from vulnerable outliers, including impulsive minors, to subsidize everyone else's entertainment is predatory cross-subsidization, not generosity. Imagine a neighborhood arcade offering free admission to local kids, funded entirely by letting a fraction of those teenagers empty their savings into backroom slot machines.
[P1.4] Nobody would celebrate that trade-off as accessible entertainment. Free access stops being a social good the moment it requires exploiting minors' cognitive vulnerabilities to pay the bills.
[P1.5] Crucially, youth safeguards do not abolish free-to-play games. If a game's survival genuinely depends on luring fourteen-year-olds into uncapped, casino-style spending sprees, that business model deserves to adapt or fail.
