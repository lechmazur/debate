[P1.1] The core issue is simple: algorithmic recommendation systems are designed to maximize engagement, and children are especially vulnerable to that optimization. Parents, who bear the consequences, currently get far less control than the platforms that design these features.
[P1.2] Every major platform runs recommendation algorithms that decide what a child sees next. These systems are engineered to keep users scrolling, watching, and returning. The platform's interest is engagement. The parent's interest is the child's wellbeing.
[P1.3] When those conflict, the platform's defaults win — not the parent's.
[P1.4] Current parental controls are inadequate by design. They are buried in settings menus, default to permissive, and require parents to parse technical distinctions between "recommendations," "suggestions," and "discover" features that even adults struggle to understand. A parent who wants to turn off autoplay or disable a recommendation feed faces a maze of toggles, some of which cannot be fully disabled at all. One design decision turns a feature on for millions of children; disabling it requires each parent to navigate controls that shift with every update.
[P1.5] Children's developing decision-making makes this urgent. A recommendation system that learns a child's vulnerabilities — their anxieties, curiosities, insecurities — and feeds them content optimized to exploit those traits is not neutral. Parents have legal and moral authority over their children's upbringing. That authority should include the ability to meaningfully block or delay features that shape what their children see and think.
[P1.6] The structural conflict of interest is undeniable. App makers profit from the engagement these features generate. Parents don't. When a platform decides whether a parent can disable a recommendation feed, the arbiter of a child's welfare is the party selling access to that child's attention.
[P1.7] Parents should have more power than they currently exercise. The defaults should favor parental choice, not platform revenue.
