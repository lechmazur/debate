[P1.1] The labor market already lets people assemble a living from ride-hail, delivery apps, and a chain of short-term contracts. What it refuses them is security attached to that living. Governments should require a portable benefits floor because substantial earnings should not sit outside the social contract just because they arrive in pieces.
[P1.2] The courier clearing serious weekly income across two apps and a warehouse stint can be deactivated on Tuesday and have no paid sick day if she is hurt on Thursday. Portability is the only design that fits that reality. Benefits locked to one employer evaporate when income is split. A floor funded by prorated contributions from each platform and hiring firm can follow her.
[P1.3] It also kills the free-rider problem: no platform will volunteer a cost its rivals can skip.
[P1.4] A floor beats the two bad alternatives. Doing nothing socializes the downside. Emergency rooms, later-life poverty, and public assistance absorb what platforms externalize. Full employee reclassification can smash the flexibility many of these workers actually use.
[P1.5] A required minimum—health access, a retirement contribution, short-term wage-loss protection—keeps contracting intact while refusing to treat real livelihoods as a benefits desert. Complexity is already here; the only question is who holds the risk.
[P1.6] The substantial-income trigger is the guardrail. Weekend gas money is not the target. The worker whose main earnings come from apps and successive contracts is. That aims the mandate at economic dependence, not casual side work.
[P1.7] Competition will not produce this. The first platform to attach benefits raises prices or cuts take-home pay while rivals stay cheaper. Only a government requirement equalizes that cost. These workers already produce value and pay taxes.
[P1.8] Pretending irregular form dissolves ordinary obligations is a choice to subsidize platforms with household and taxpayer risk. A portable floor closes that subsidy without inventing a new employment category.
