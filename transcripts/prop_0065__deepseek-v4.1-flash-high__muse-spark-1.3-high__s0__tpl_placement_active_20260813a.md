[P1.1] Reliability is not the issue. A safe, accurate tool can still be a social poison. Screening embryos for height, appearance, or predicted cognition is not medicine; it is the use of reproduction to buy competitive and aesthetic advantage. Law should restrict it even when the science works, because the harms are social, structural, and unavoidable.
[P1.2] If a few parents select taller sons, they do not just get taller sons. They raise the height premium; other parents now face the message that ordinary height is a disadvantage they failed to prevent. The choice becomes an arms race. The same happens with appearance and cognition.
[P1.3] In a generation, normal human variation is recast as a defect, and parental love is filtered through a marketplace of specifications.
[P1.4] Predicted cognition is especially dangerous. It is the trait most entangled with class, race, and existing privilege. Families with money will screen first, and their advantages will compound. Then the winners will point to biology to justify inequality: the child who scored higher was “built” better, so the hierarchy is deserved.
[P1.5] That is not speculation; it is how every new advantage gets converted into a claim of natural superiority.
[P1.6] Autonomy does not rescue this. Reproductive autonomy protects the right not to be coerced; it does not create a right to design another person. The child cannot consent. Being selected for appearance or height sends a clear message: you were made to meet a spec, not welcomed as you are.
[P1.7] That is a profound dignitary harm, not a private hobby.
[P1.8] And the market makes freedom a lie. When trait selection is legal, it will not be equally available. It will be a luxury good, then a baseline expectation. The poor are not choosing freely when the normal range becomes a stigma.
[P1.9] Reliable technology makes that pressure more effective, not less coercive.
[P1.10] So draw the line at disease. Treat suffering; do not engineer status. Legal restriction is how a decent society refuses to turn children into products and inequality into biology.
