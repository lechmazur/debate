[P1.1] Every year, taxpayers fund thousands of clinical trials and social-science studies, and a large share of the results simply vanish. Studies with null or unwelcome findings sit in file drawers; studies with flashy positive results get published, cited, and turned into policy and prescriptions. The published literature is not a record of what we learned—it is a record of what was convenient to report. That is the disease this motion cures, with two reinforcing remedies: mandatory preregistration, and a journal commitment to publish regardless of outcome.
[P1.2] First, accountability. Public money buys public knowledge. When a funded trial is never reported, the public paid for evidence it never received. Worse, suppressed negative results actively mislead: the antidepressant literature is a notorious case where unpublished negative trials made drugs look substantially more effective than the full evidence supported.
[P1.3] Doctors prescribed, patients suffered side effects, and insurers paid—on a distorted record. Preregistration creates a permanent public ledger: we know what was studied, what was promised, and we can demand the answer.
[P1.4] Second, scientific validity. Preregistration kills the quiet frauds of outcome-switching and p-hacking. When researchers can pick their hypothesis after seeing the data, statistical significance becomes theater. Specifying outcomes in advance restores the meaning of a p-value.
[P1.5] And it costs nothing in flexibility—exploratory analyses remain fully permitted; they are simply labeled as exploratory, which is honesty, not handcuffs.
[P1.6] Third, the publication guarantee fixes the incentive at its root. Registered-report formats, where journals accept studies on the strength of methods before results exist, have shown dramatically higher rates of null findings—evidence that the conventional literature is skewed. Guaranteeing publication means careers stop depending on getting lucky with data.
[P1.7] Note the motion's modesty: it covers publicly funded research only, and clinical-trial registration is already partially mandated by law. We are extending a proven, working principle, not running an experiment. The burden falls on anyone defending the file drawer.
