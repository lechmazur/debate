[P1.1] Lifetime tenure transforms constitutional courts into arbitrary gerontocracies, where the fundamental law of a nation hinges on actuarial tables and politically timed retirements. Fixed, non-renewable terms resolve this institutional crisis by dramatically restoring democratic legitimacy while leaving genuine judicial independence entirely secure.
[P1.2] First, fixed terms repair the broken democratic link between the court and the citizenry. Under life tenure, judicial vacancies occur erratically, turning each vacancy into an all-out partisan war and incentivizing justices to strategically time their departures to favor their preferred party. This transforms high courts into generational land-grabs. Predictable, staggered terms regularize the appointment process.
[P1.3] When every governing cycle receives equal, scheduled representation on the bench, constitutional interpretation evolves steadily alongside the democratic body politic, rather than freezing the ideological preferences of a long-past era.
[P1.4] Second, fixed terms do not erode judicial independence because independence requires insulation, not immortality. The danger to independence arises when judges face political retaliation or must audition for their continued employment. A long, strictly non-renewable term—such as twelve to eighteen years, standard across peer constitutional courts globally—eliminates this risk entirely. Because justices can never be reappointed or demoted by the political branches, they have zero incentive to pander to sitting politicians.
[P1.5] Their salaries remain protected, their tenure guaranteed, and their decision-making unencumbered.
[P1.6] Democratic legitimacy is the ultimate foundation of judicial authority; a court perceived as an unaccountable, partisan aristocracy will eventually suffer an enforcement crisis when the public rejects its mandates. Moving from lifetime tenure to a substantial, non-renewable term incurs no loss of adjudicative backbone, but it permanently extinguishes the destabilizing spectacle of death-watch politics. That is a decisive institutional upgrade.
