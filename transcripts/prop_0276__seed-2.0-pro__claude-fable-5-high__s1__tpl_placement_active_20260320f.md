[C1.1] Begin with the question PRO never answers: who decides, automatically, that a delay was "controllable"? Weather in Chicago strands a crew that was supposed to fly your Denver flight. Is that weather or crew scheduling? An air traffic ground stop cascades into a mechanical inspection delay.
[C1.2] Controllable? Causation in a networked airline system is genuinely contested, often involving three or four interacting factors. "Automatic" compensation does not eliminate disputes — it relocates them into an opaque internal cause-coding system where the airline classifies its own liability with no passenger claim on record at all. The complaint-heavy process PRO decries at least leaves a paper trail.
[C1.3] Automation makes the airline judge, jury, and payer of its own case, and PRO should not be surprised when the share of delays coded "uncontrollable" mysteriously climbs.
[C1.4] Second, the cost argument cannot be waved away. PRO concedes payouts would rise roughly tenfold. That money comes from fares — and it functions as mandatory delay insurance bundled into every ticket. The student flying home for $89 on a budget carrier is forced to buy a product she may not want, while the business traveler with credit-card protections and flexible fares collects twice.
[C1.5] Flat automatic payouts are crude: the same check goes to someone who shrugged off a delay in the lounge and someone who missed a wedding. Markets already price this risk through travel insurance and fare classes; a mandate destroys that choice.
[C1.6] Third, mandates warp operations. Hard compensation thresholds create cliff-edge incentives: cancel rather than delay past the trigger, pad schedules so "on-time" means slower published flights, and quietly drop thin routes to small cities where one mechanical issue wipes out the day's margin.
[C1.7] If the grievance is friction, fix friction: one-click claims, mandatory response deadlines, penalties for wrongful denial. That preserves verification and the paper trail. PRO's motion instead demands automaticity — and automaticity is precisely the part that fails.
