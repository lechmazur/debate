[C1.3] By the time a state-approved textbook or standardized exam on AI detection is rolled out, the technology it describes will be obsolete, leaving students trained in yesterday’s defenses.
[C1.4] Second, siloing this training into its own subject actually weakens it. Media literacy is not a standalone academic discipline; it is the active application of critical thinking, historical context, and scientific skepticism. When we isolate "manipulation resistance" into its own class period, we teach students to treat critical thinking as a technical checklist for specific digital tools, rather than a habit of mind. The best defense against a doctored historical video is a deep understanding of history; the best defense against pseudo-scientific AI claims is solid scientific literacy.
[C1.5] Finally, the opportunity cost is devastating. Schools are currently facing a generational crisis in basic reading and numeracy. Adding a new, highly specialized core subject means reclaiming hours of instructional time from these already struggling foundational areas. We cannot teach advanced algorithmic skepticism to students who lack the baseline reading comprehension required to analyze a basic text.
[C1.6] Instead of creating a rigid, soon-to-be-obsolete core subject, schools must embed these concepts organically within existing disciplines. We must protect our core curriculum to ensure students have the fundamental intellectual bedrock necessary to navigate any future technological landscape.
