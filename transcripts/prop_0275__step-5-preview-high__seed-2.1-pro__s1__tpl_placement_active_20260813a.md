[P1.1] The proposal is simple: before you commit, you know the full price and the actual constraints. Today, vacation-rental platforms do the opposite. They advertise a headline nightly rate, let you fall in love with the photos, start filling in dates and guest counts, and only then surface the cleaning fee, the resort fee, and the house rules that might make the stay impossible. This isn't disclosure; it's ambush.
[P1.2] Consider the family booking a six-night reunion for eight. The nightly rate looks manageable. Only after they click into checkout do they discover the cleaning fee exceeds two nights combined, or that the property bans visitors and enforces a 10 p.m. quiet rule that kills the reunion. Walking away now feels irrational—they have already invested the search, the comparison, the mental ownership of the trip.
[P1.3] The platform monetizes that commitment.
[P1.4] Cleaning and resort fees are legitimate costs, but their timing is not. A fee revealed before commitment lets users compare properties on total price. A fee revealed after commitment trains users to distrust every headline number. The result is a worse market: platforms compete on artificially low base rates while shifting real costs downstream.
[P1.5] House rules deserve the same treatment. They are not fine print; they are fit-determining facts. A guest traveling with a service animal, a large extended family, or a late-arriving red-eye needs to know the rules before investing time, not after. If the property does not fit, the user loses the vacation; the host loses the booking; the platform loses trust.
[P1.6] Disclosure before commitment is the minimum standard for informed choice. We already demand it in lending and retail because the alternative is a market built on psychological traps. Vacation rentals should be no different.

[C1.1] This rigid, one-size-fits-all mandate will not deliver transparent, user-friendly booking—it will clutter search, raise prices for most travelers, and bury genuinely important details under a mountain of irrelevant fine print. [C1.2] Forcing platforms to surface every fee and full rule set before users click to checkout will push hosts to set fees at the worst-case maximum—say, a deep-clean rate for a rowdy 8-guest weekend—so even solo two-night travelers who leave the unit spotless pay top dollar, and eliminate opt-outs for resort amenities so no guest can skip paying for perks they will never use.
[C1.3] Second, cramming every rule and fee onto pre-checkout listing pages makes comparison harder, not easier. When every listing is bloated with 20+ lines of granular rules and fee schedules, users cannot quickly scan for the core priorities that drive their choice: location, size, base rate, availability. The high-stakes disclosures that actually matter for accessibility or trip fit—like service animal policies, late check-in rules, or large-party bans—get lost in a sea of trivial boilerplate, rather than being clearly presented to users once they have narrowed their choices, input their specific trip details, and are ready to evaluate real, personalized fit.
