[C1.2] The categories themselves are not clear enough to mandate. “Medical,” “cosmetic,” and “wellness” overlap in ordinary language and vary across regulators. “Helps calm breakouts,” “supports the skin barrier,” “for stressed skin,” “non-comedogenic,” “dermatologist-inspired”—these phrases sit on contested borders. A celebrity brand’s lawyers may sort them after months of review, but the motion reaches influencers posting reels, lives, and affiliate links. Requiring them to perform real-time legal triage in a fifteen-second video is not a trivial burden, especially when the disclosure must remain clear across captions, cuts, and livestreams.
[C1.3] It is a recipe for chilling speech, pushing creators toward vague euphemisms or away from ingredient education entirely.
[C1.4] Worse, the required distinction would likely become boilerplate.
