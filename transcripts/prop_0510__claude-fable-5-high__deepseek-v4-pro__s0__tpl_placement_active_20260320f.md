[P1.2] Start with the arithmetic everyone forgets. A debt ratio has a denominator. If you choke off stabilizers mid-downturn, demand collapses further, GDP shrinks, and the ratio you were trying to protect rises anyway. The eurozone learned this the hard way after 2010: countries forced into pro-cyclical tightening saw output crater so badly that debt-to-GDP climbed despite the cuts.
[P1.3] Greece tightened relentlessly and ended the decade with a higher debt ratio than when it started. Austerity in a slump is not fiscal virtue; it is sawing off the branch the debt ratio sits on.
[P1.4] Second, downturns leave scars. This hysteresis permanently shrinks the tax base that must eventually service the debt. Stabilizers are precisely targeted at preventing it—money flows automatically to the people most likely to spend it, exactly when private demand is weakest, and withdraws automatically in recovery.
[P1.5] No stimulus bill, no pork, no lag, no exit problem.
[P1.6] Third, the alternative is incoherent. If stabilizers cannot expand when debt is elevated, then in practice they can almost never expand, because debt is elevated nearly everywhere and recessions are exactly when ratios spike. CON must defend deliberately deepening recessions to chase a ratio that their own policy worsens.
[P1.7] The real test of fiscal responsibility is long-run sustainability, not the headline number next quarter. By that test, letting stabilizers breathe is the conservative choice.
