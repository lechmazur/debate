[P1.1] Dense city centers are not open beta-testing grounds for autonomous software; they are finite, complex ecosystems where public safety, transit flow, and street space hang in a delicate balance. The proposition does not call for an outright ban on innovation. Instead, it demands basic municipal governance: keeping robotaxi fleets geofenced and numerically capped until cities establish enforceable rules for curb management, labor equity, and safety protocols.
[P1.2] First, physical curb space is inherently scarce. When uncapped commercial autonomous fleets flood dense downtowns, they create crippling bottlenecks. Without clear curb allocation and stopping rules, robotaxis idle, block active transit corridors, double-park in bike lanes, and deadhead through congested streets while waiting for algorithmic dispatch. Limiting fleet sizes ensures that commercial testing does not paralyze the public transit and commercial logistics systems that cities depend on every day.
[P1.3] Second, public safety demands proactive containment rather than reactive cleanup. Autonomous fleets continue to struggle with high-density edge cases—blocking active fire trucks, freezing inside construction zones, and obstructing emergency personnel. Geofencing keeps vehicles away from vulnerable chokepoints, while numeric caps ensure that local emergency services are not overwhelmed while municipal reporting standards and manual override protocols are being developed.
[P1.4] Third, cities must govern the labor and transit transitions before market saturation creates irreversible harms. An unregulated influx of automated fleets directly undercuts municipal mass transit ridership and abruptly displaces professional drivers without clear workforce transition frameworks or public transit revenue-sharing mechanisms in place.
[P1.5] Deploying technology at scale before establishing basic ground rules is reckless. Once thousands of unmanaged vehicles flood urban centers, reversing the damage to public transit, street accessibility, and municipal finances becomes nearly impossible. Capping and geofencing robotaxis is the only prudent mechanism to ensure that when automation expands, it serves the public interest rather than exploiting public space.
