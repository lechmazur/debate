[P1.1] Cheapening every liter of fuel at the pump is welfare for the affluent disguised as relief for the poor. Wealthier households own private cars, drive larger vehicles, and burn far more fuel than poorer families walking or riding buses. Universal subsidies therefore funnel most public spending to richer drivers while starving clinics, schools, and transit. Worse, global oil spikes automatically swell that uncapped bill, blowing a hole in budgets right when governments most need fiscal room.
[P1.2] Suppressing costs through broad price controls is even more destructive. Capping prices by decree, whether on fuel itself or across downstream transport and food, never erases real supply costs; it dumps losses onto importers, refiners, farmers, and bus operators. Forced to sell below cost, formal suppliers cut back or shut down. The predictable result is dry fuel pumps, rationing queues, smuggling, crumbling infrastructure, and black markets where working families pay exorbitant illegal prices anyway, or backdoor bailouts that recreate the fiscal crisis.
[P1.3] Phasing out universal subsidies for targeted cash transfers solves this by protecting people rather than distorting prices. Phasing means responsible sequencing, not overnight shock therapy: governments register households and deliver cash upfront through bank accounts, mobile money, or utility and social records, then step subsidies down gradually while indexing transfers to living costs. Cash offsets both pump prices and indirect increases in food and bus fares, and targeting can reach broad working majorities simply by screening out affluent earners rather than imposing complex hurdles on the poor.
[P1.4] Above all, cash preserves the price signal. Even when a poorer family receives more cash than it loses on groceries and transit, every marginal liter of fuel still reflects its true economic and environmental cost. Affluent heavy consumers finally pay their own way, curbing congestion, smog, and carbon emissions, while reclaimed revenue funds generous household relief and better transit. Subsidizing fuel rewards waste by the rich; transferring cash secures dignity for everyone else.
