[P1.1] The fundamental question in this debate is who bears the burden: must students plead, document, and wait for basic comprehension tools, or should institutions treat access as standard educational infrastructure?
[P1.2] Presumptive access replaces gatekeeping with Universal Design. Under the status quo, accommodation models impose a heavy administrative and social tax on disabled, neurodivergent, and chronically ill students. Obtaining formal accommodations requires expensive clinical documentation and weeks of bureaucratic delay, during which students fall irrevocably behind. Making captions, recordings, and digital materials the default eliminates the stigma of disclosure while extending vital flexibility to working students, non-native speakers, and those managing sudden illness or caregiving duties.
[P1.3] Beyond equity, multimodal access is simply superior pedagogy. Complex material is rarely mastered in a single live sitting. The ability to pause, review dense technical concepts, or read along with captions deepens retention and understanding. Far from discouraging engagement, recordings liberate students to actively participate during class rather than frantically transcribing every spoken syllable in fear of missing critical details.
[P1.4] Crucially, the motion does not impose a blunt mandate; it establishes a *presumption*. The pedagogical safety valve is built directly into the topic. If an instructor leads a sensitive seminar, a workshop requiring uninhibited peer critique, or an interactive discussion that would be genuinely chilled by recording, they retain the freedom to opt out for those justified reasons. What the motion targets is withholding access out of mere institutional inertia, instructor inconvenience, or nostalgia for 20th-century delivery.
[P1.5] When technology makes inclusion effortless and exceptions protect sensitive teaching methods, the burden should never fall on vulnerable students to justify their right to learn. The default belongs with access.

[C1.6] Finally, universal presumptive access delivers a false promise of inclusion.
