[P1.1] The European social model was built on a simple bargain: firms that use labour help fund the security that makes labour productive and societies stable. Large companies are now breaking that bargain by taking the work while shedding the payer. Through platforms and long subcontracting chains, retailers, manufacturers and digital labour platforms control how work is done and capture its value, yet classify workers as 'independent' or hide behind thin intermediaries that fail to pay pensions, sickness cover, or unemployment insurance.
[P1.2] The costs do not vanish; they shift onto workers and onto strained national welfare systems.
[P1.3] Consider the food-delivery rider formally classed as self-employed while an app sets hours, routes, pace and deactivation. Or the cleaner at the end of a three-layer facilities chain, employed by a shell that underpays contributions or disappears. In both cases the lead firm gets flexible labour below its real social price.
[P1.4] The EU should close that gap by requiring large companies to pay a higher share of social contributions when they use platforms or subcontracting chains. This is not a ban on flexibility; it corrects a distorted price. If outsourcing genuinely saves costs, let it do so on efficiency, not on exempting the profit-making company from pensions, healthcare and unemployment protection. A higher contribution makes evasion expensive, pulls the social wage back into the corporate ledger, and removes the unfair discount that firms using bogus self-employment enjoy over competitors who employ directly.
[P1.5] Because these arrangements are often cross-border and layered, national rules alone are easy to arbitrage. EU action targets the biggest orchestrators of those chains—the companies with power to choose structures and margins to pay. The decisive test is simple: should labour arbitrage be less profitable than decent employment? Under today's rules, evasion is cheap and undercuts fair firms.
[P1.6] Our reform flips that incentive, funds the social protection Europe depends on, and defends fair competition. The EU should act.
