Individual teacher discretion cannot solve a school-wide cultural problem that requires collective action.
[P1.4] A default ban establishes clear, uniform expectations. It signals that the school prioritizes learning over screens, supports teachers rather than burdening them with inconsistent enforcement, and treats every student equally regardless of which classroom they occupy. [C1.4] The equity claim cuts the other way. Many low-income students rely on smartphones as their only reliable calculator, translator, note camera, planner, and internet access. [C1.6] The better model is shared expectations with teacher-level authority to adapt, not categorical prohibition. That respects professional judgment, addresses hallway and cafeteria behavior through targeted policies, and avoids mistaking uniformity for learning.
