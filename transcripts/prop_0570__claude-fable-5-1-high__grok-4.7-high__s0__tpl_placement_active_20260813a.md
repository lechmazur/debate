[P1.1] A fifteen-year-old writes an essay about the night her grandfather died. Feed it to an AI tutor and it will return, in seconds, tidy notes on topic sentences, transitions, and a slightly weak conclusion. Hand it to her English teacher and she gets something different: "Paragraph three is where you stopped performing and started telling the truth. Cut the first two and begin there."
[P1.2] The difference between those responses is the whole debate.
[P1.3] The motion draws a line where the technology actually changes character. Mastery practice—factoring quadratics, balancing equations, French verb endings, comma splices—has verifiable answers. An AI tutor here is close to ideal: infinite patience, instant correction, no embarrassment for the kid who needs the fortieth attempt, and problems calibrated to exactly where she is stuck. Teachers cannot give thirty students that individually, and pretending otherwise wastes the technology.
[P1.4] Writing feedback is a different act. Writing is communication to a reader, and feedback is that reader reporting what landed. Its value comes from being a genuine response from someone who knows the writer, the class, and what the piece was for. The main source of feedback becomes the audience students learn to write for.
[P1.5] Make it a model, and teenagers will learn—fast, because they are excellent at reading incentives—to produce the smooth, hedged, structurally correct prose the model rewards. It cannot tell them a sentence is boring, brave, or dishonest, because it has no stake in the answer.
[P1.6] Notice the motion says main source, not only source. Grammar checks, a draft-stage reaction at eleven at night, practice with sentence variety—fine. What must stay human is the primary judgment about whether the writing works.
[P1.7] Two more costs of getting this wrong. Reading student writing is how teachers actually know their students; outsource it and the relationship goes with it. And when the primary reader is a machine, students have every reason to let a machine write—accountability collapses on both ends.
[P1.8] Use AI where answers are checkable. Keep humans where meaning is.

[C1.1] Reject the split. Writing improves by the same mechanism as quadratics: fast, specific correction while the student can still change the work. The motion withholds that loop in the name of a teacher comment most students never receive.
[C1.2] The grandfather essay is the wrong case. Most secondary writing is argument, analysis, explanation — claim, evidence, reasoning, structure. A department can state those demands. An AI tutor aimed at them can flag the weak warrant and the paragraph that asserts instead of shows, on draft four, at eleven at night.
[C1.3] The teacher who says “begin at paragraph three” is real and rare. The ordinary return is “develop,” weeks late, after the grade is in. Build policy on the feedback students get, not the comment we wish they got.
[C1.4] They will write for the reward in front of them either way: the rubric, the safe thesis, the teacher’s pet transition. A configured tutor makes that reward visible. It can be told to punish filler and demand a stake. A published standard is a clearer audience than a hurried margin note no one can reconstruct.
[C1.5] Opacity is not humanity.
[C1.6] Outsourcing does not follow from feedback. It follows from a single polished product and no chance to be questioned. Visible revision plus a short conference — what changed, what you can defend — makes authorship easier to see. Ban AI as the main source and the timely editor just goes underground.
[C1.7] Knowing students does not require teachers to line-edit every draft. Burial does the damage. If AI carries volume, the teacher can be the human reader of chosen pieces, the one who asks whether paragraph three is true. The motion already allows night reactions and sentence work.
[C1.8] That is most of revision. Hoarding “primary judgment” mistakes scarcity for virtue.
[C1.9] Volume teaches. Humans design the task and keep the stakes. AI should be the main source of writing feedback. The prohibition is what fails them.
