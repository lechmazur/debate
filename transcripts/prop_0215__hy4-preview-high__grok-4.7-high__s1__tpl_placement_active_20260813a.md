[P1.1] When a child in a church-run school was beaten for speaking her own language, the building, the payroll, and the rulebook were the church’s. That is the case in one image. The proposition is not that today’s congregants are personally guilty for ancestral sins. It is that institutions which ran, staffed, and profited from forced assimilation should pay their share into repair funds, because administrative and financial responsibility survives even when personnel change.
[P1.2] In Canada, Ireland, Australia, and the United States, churches and religious orders were not incidental contractors but daily managers. They managed dormitories, taught curricula of erasure, separated siblings, punished native languages, and relied on children’s labor to keep institutions solvent. Daily control means causal responsibility. Survivors did not experience “state policy” abstractly; they experienced a priest, nun, brother, or minister enforcing it in a building owned by a religious body.
[P1.3] Then follow the money. Whether it was a former state church with retained endowments or a religious order with farms, schools, and buildings, these bodies often still hold assets tied to that period: land, investment portfolios, tax exemptions, state subsidies, and the value of unpaid labor. A diocese that merges or rebrands does not become innocent by changing its name; it remains the continuing legal person holding that endowment. Money is the part that changes survivors’ lives, and retained benefits are the natural source for repair.
[P1.4] Because the link is traceable, the claim is practical, not merely symbolic.
[P1.5] Opponents will call this collective punishment or say the state was the senior partner. That is too convenient. The state may also owe, but these bodies were co-principals and enjoyed unique privileges while doing harm. Requiring proportional contributions does not ask today’s faithful to do penance for someone else’s crime; it asks institutions to disgorge benefits they still hold.
[P1.6] If they keep the land, endowment, and goodwill, they cannot once again leave victims and taxpayers alone to finance repair.
