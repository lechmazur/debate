[C1.1] “A checkbook is not a larynx” [P1.1] wins the soundbite and loses the argument. In mass democracies, speech without distribution is a diary. Renting halls, buying airtime, hiring organizers, and running data operations are not bribes; they are the plumbing that lets an idea reach citizens. Treating those costs as protected is not a subsidy for wealth, it is protection for speakers who lack a media empire or a famous last name.
[C1.2] Banning corporate donations mistakes form for substance. A corporation is often shareholders, workers, or members pooling resources to speak on rules that govern their industry. Block the treasury and you do not protect the owners; you push the speech into darker channels. When the United States restricted party soft money over twenty years ago, the cash did not vanish, it migrated to outside committees with weaker disclosure.
[C1.3] Every clampdown spawns an alphabet soup of shadows: new entities, weaker transparency, fiercer games.
[C1.4] Deeply cutting large checks is sold as a blow against dependence, but it rigs the entry price against outsiders. Modern challengers need early, credible money to poll, rent space, and buy first ads before small donors arrive. Incumbents already enjoy name recognition, donor lists, and free media, so they survive low caps; unknown reformers do not. Lower caps therefore reward exactly the officeholder Pro claims to fear.
[C1.5] Pro says “lower caps push candidates toward many small givers” [P1.7], but small givers cluster around the already-famous: incumbent celebrities, online influencers, and national parties, not the unheard.
[C1.6] The right metric is accountable persuasion, not equal decibels. Their levelling would mute every organized association to a whisper while leaving incumbency, platforms, and partisan media untouched. Keep political spending inside the speech framework, expose contributors in real time, and let any group—corporate, union, or individual—try to win votes. Disclosure answers dependence; Bans and low caps do not purify self-government; they empty it.
