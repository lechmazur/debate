[P1.1] Across Europe and much of North America, forests are quietly failing to grow back. Not because of logging, but because deer, elk and boar now exist in densities no predator disciplines. Saplings get browsed to nothing, understorey birds lose their nesting layer, riverbanks are stripped bare and collapse into the water. A wood full of mature trees and no young ones is not a forest; it's a museum with a closing date.
[P1.2] In Scotland, in central Europe, in the eastern United States, that's the baseline we're defending when we defend predator absence.
[P1.3] Only predators fix this continuously, at scale, and for free. Culling is the standard alternative, and it fails on its own terms: it happens seasonally, in accessible places, in daylight, and it does not change how herbivores behave. Wolves and lynx do something no rifle can — they make deer move. Herds stop loitering in the same gully for weeks, browsing pressure spreads out, riverside vegetation returns, and with it beavers, songbirds, insects, fish.
[P1.4] That cascade is the Yellowstone story in outline, and it is why "just pay more stalkers" is a permanent subsidy for a job predators do better and never stop doing.
[P1.5] Then the hard part of the motion, which I take head-on. Rural objection is real and it deserves money, fencing, guardian dogs, rapid compensation, and genuine consultation. What it does not deserve is a veto. We do not let the households nearest a river decide national water quality, or landowners decide whether a species goes extinct.
[P1.6] Biodiversity, flood regulation, and functioning forests are shared inheritances; the people who happen to live beside the last suitable habitat are custodians, not owners. And the costs at stake, while painful individually, are modest and insurable — Italy, Spain, Romania and Germany farm sheep in wolf country right now, with losses that are a rounding error against national livestock numbers.
[P1.7] Objection is a reason to compensate generously and plan carefully. It is not a reason to leave ecosystems permanently broken.
