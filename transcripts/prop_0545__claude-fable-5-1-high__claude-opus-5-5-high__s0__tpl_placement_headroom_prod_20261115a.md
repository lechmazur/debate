[P1.1] Every day in opt-in countries, people die waiting for organs that were buried instead—organs from people who, when asked, said they wanted to give. Opt-in does not measure consent; it measures whether someone filled out a form at the licensing office.
[P1.2] Presumed consent sets the default to match what most people actually want. Anyone who truly objects can register that objection in minutes, as only a small percentage do in opt-out countries. [P1.3] The effect is measurable. [P1.4] When Wales moved to deemed consent, family consent rates climbed and the rest of the UK followed.
[P1.5] Now the ethical objection. Presumed consent as practiced is soft: families are still approached, and a family that objects is not overridden. No organ is taken against a known wish. What changes is the question.
[P1.6] Under opt-in, a grieving family must guess what their loved one wanted, in the worst hour of their lives. [P1.7] The motion says "most patients." The patients who matter are on waiting lists. For them, a default that respects majority preference, preserves individual opt-out, and keeps the family veto is both more effective and more humane. The status quo lets people die for want of a form.
