[C1.1] This proposition’s framing misrepresents what sponsored placements do, and who they benefit. Heavily weighted sponsored slots are not a hidden trick to bury cheap independent restaurants; they are the core revenue stream that lets delivery and reservation platforms operate without charging users upfront access fees, per-reservation surcharges, or punishingly high base delivery rates that would price out the exact low-income diners PRO claims to defend. Grocery stores place paid products at eye level, billboards line commuter routes, and local papers run paid restaurant ads all without being accused of “manipulating” customers, because clearly marked commercial placement is a standard, transparent way to fund a free public-facing service.
[C1.2] The claim that users “struggle to find” cheaper independent options ignores basic app design: every major platform lets users sort by price, distance, rating, or cuisine in a single tap, so default sponsored top slots do not block access to tailored results, they simply curate the unfiltered landing page for users who have not set specific filters. Sponsorship is not reserved for deep-pocketed chains: most platforms charge sponsors only per actual click, order, or completed reservation, meaning small independent spots pay nothing for impressions that do not turn into business, a far lower-risk marketing path than print flyers, local radio ads, or street signage that require upfront payment with no guaranteed return.
[C1.3] Crucially, these paid slots let brand-new independent spots, which have not yet accumulated hundreds of ratings to climb organic rankings, compete for attention instead of being permanently buried under long-established venues. Barring heavy sponsorship weighting would not help budget diners—it would force platforms to hike user fees, cut investment in map accuracy and order reliability, and remove the lowest-risk marketing channel new independent restaurants have to reach first-time customers.
