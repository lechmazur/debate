[C1.2] Start with the economics. Physical first-sale works because a used book is a degraded, singular object — scarcity is preserved by physics. [C1.3] Publishers and studios know this, which is exactly why licensing exists — not as corporate greed, but as the mechanism that lets a twenty-dollar game or fifteen-dollar ebook exist at all instead of a hundred-dollar one priced to offset unlimited leakage.

[C1.5] Third, permanence risk is real but rare and already correcting itself through market competition — DRM-free storefronts, offline modes, backup allowances — without needing to torch the licensing structure that funds creation.
[C1.6] The choice isn't "ownership versus exploitation." It's "flexible access at scale versus a smaller, pricier, piracy-vulnerable market." CON wins because licensing isn't the failure of digital markets — it's the reason they work.
