[P1.1] Open a food app, search a nearby restaurant, and the most expensive way to get that meal is already chosen for you. Priority delivery is toggled on, the fees are stacked, the cheaper pickup tab is buried two taps away. That is not convenience. It is choice architecture designed to extract money from inattention.
[P1.2] Defaults matter enormously because most people stick with them. Behavioral research and everyday experience agree: the pre-checked box wins. When platforms set the priciest fulfillment path as the default, they are betting that busy, hungry users will not uncheck every add-on, compare fee schedules, or hunt for pickup. Often they are right, and a $12 burrito becomes a $22 order once delivery, service, small-order and priority fees pile on.
[P1.3] Flipping the default to pickup or standard, lower-fee delivery does not ban expensive delivery. It simply stops taxing distraction.
[P1.4] That shift protects the people hurt most. Tight-budget families, students, and workers ordering lunch do not benefit from being steered to surge-priced courier delivery when the restaurant is a five-minute walk away or on their commute home. A pickup default makes the true price visible and puts savings where they belong. Restaurants also gain, because inflated delivery totals suppress order volume and push customers to blame the kitchen for platform markups.
[P1.5] The alternative is to bless manipulation as business. Platforms will still offer express delivery, still profit, still employ couriers. They will just have to sell speed honestly rather than smuggle it in as the starting point. If fast delivery is truly worth the premium, customers will choose it when asked clearly.
[P1.6] That is fair competition, not paternalism: choice remains, but the starting line no longer favors the house. Users who need delivery for disability, weather, or late hours can still get it in one tap.
[P1.7] Default to the affordable option and let luxury delivery earn its click.
