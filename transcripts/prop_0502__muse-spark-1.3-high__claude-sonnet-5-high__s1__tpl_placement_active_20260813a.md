[P1.1] The Arctic is melting from a frozen desert into the world's most dangerous prize. Its ice stabilizes climate, its ocean feeds currents, its thaw threatens coastlines everywhere. To leave it to competing territorial grabs is to let a handful of coastal states decide the fate of a system all humanity depends on. That is neither safe nor legitimate.
[P1.2] Current governance cannot handle this. The Arctic Council can study and recommend but cannot bind, cannot regulate militaries, and pauses whenever great-power tension rises. The law of the sea draws lines on water but says almost nothing about an oil blowout under ice, black carbon from new shipping lanes, or collapsing fish stocks moving poleward. Gaps become invitations.
[P1.3] And territorial competition makes every gap worse. When sovereignty is the prize, states must drill to prove use, sail warships to prove control, and file ever-larger continental shelf claims to lock in seabed. Russia plants a flag on the North Pole seabed, reopens Cold War airbases, and claims vast ridges; NATO answers with exercises; non-Arctic powers like China push a Polar Silk Road to force their way in. Fishermen, shippers, and oil firms race ahead of rules because the rules are whoever gets there first.
[P1.4] A binding treaty modeled on Antarctica breaks that logic. The Antarctic system froze territorial claims without erasing them, banned military activity, made science cooperative, and later added strict environmental protection. An Arctic version would do the same adapted core: freeze overlapping ridge and passage disputes, demilitarize, set binding standards for drilling, shipping, fishing, and spills, with guaranteed seats and revenue shares for Indigenous peoples who actually live there.
[P1.5] No coastal state loses its people or coastline under such a treaty. What it loses is the right to gamble with a global commons for short-term advantage. Peace over arms race, precaution over oil rush, shared stewardship over carve-up: that is why a binding Arctic treaty is necessary.

[C1.1] The Antarctic Treaty worked because Antarctica was a blank slate: no permanent population, no sovereign citizens, no cities, no existing economies built on the land. The Arctic is the opposite. Four million people live there. Whole national economies—Norwegian shelf oil, Russian gas, Canadian and Alaskan communities—are already built on settled sovereignty.
[C1.2] You cannot "freeze" claims over inhabited, governed territory the way you freeze claims over an empty ice continent. Asking Russia, Canada, the US, Norway, and Denmark to suspend sovereignty over their own coastlines and citizens isn't a modest reform; it's a nonstarter no legislature will ratify.
[C1.3] And the states who actually hold the claims have already told us this. In 2008 the five Arctic coastal states signed the Ilulissat Declaration, explicitly rejecting a new comprehensive Arctic treaty and affirming that the existing Law of the Sea framework is the right vehicle for resolving shelf and boundary questions. That is not a governance gap waiting to be filled; it is a deliberate, binding legal architecture the claimants chose because it lets rules and sovereignty coexist.
[C1.4] PRO wants Antarctic-style demilitarization now, of all moments—when Russian nuclear submarines patrol under Arctic ice as the backbone of its deterrent, and NATO treats the Arctic as its northern flank during active confrontation with Moscow. The Arctic Council itself has been effectively paralyzed since 2022 because Russia was frozen out over Ukraine. That is the live test case: even soft, non-binding cooperation collapses under real geopolitical stress. A binding treaty requiring unanimous ratification by the same adversaries would be far more fragile, not less—and negotiating it would take decades while the ice keeps melting now.
[C1.5] What actually works are targeted, binding sub-agreements negotiated among Arctic states as needed: the search-and-rescue agreement, the oil spill response agreement. That's binding law without demanding an impossible sovereignty freeze. Incremental and real beats comprehensive and unratifiable.
