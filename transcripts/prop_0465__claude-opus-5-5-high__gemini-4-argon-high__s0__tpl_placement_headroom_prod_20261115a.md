[P1.1] A river can be poisoned, drained, or dammed into a ditch, and in most legal systems nobody has standing to object on its behalf. Downstream fishers must prove personal financial loss. Communities who have relied on that water for centuries must show a property interest. The river itself, the thing actually being destroyed, is legally invisible.
[P1.2] Legal personhood fixes that gap, and the motion asks for it only where the people closest to the river request it.
[P1.3] First, personhood is an enforcement tool, not mysticism. Corporations are legal persons because a structure needed a way to sue, be sued, and hold rights. A river with personhood gets named guardians who can go to court, demand impact assessments, and hold polluters liable for damage to the ecosystem, not just to someone's land title. New Zealand's Whanganui River settlement created exactly this: guardians drawn from both the Māori iwi and the Crown, speaking for the river.
[P1.4] Colombia's courts did something similar for the Atrato River after years of mining pollution.
[P1.5] Second, the request trigger matters. This motion does not let distant activists freeze a country. It responds to Indigenous and local communities, often the people whose treaties were broken and whose voices environmental review routinely sidelines. Granting their request converts informal moral claims into durable legal standing.
[P1.6] That is self-determination with teeth.
[P1.7] Third, "complicates infrastructure" is a feature, not a bug. Personhood does not ban dams or highways; the Whanganui still supports existing uses. What it does is force developers to account for costs they currently dump onto ecosystems and the communities downstream. A project that cannot survive scrutiny from the river's guardians is a project that was relying on silent, uncompensated destruction.
[P1.8] The real question is simple: when a project damages a river, who gets to speak? Today the answer is often nobody. PRO makes the answer somebody.

[C1.7] We should give communities direct statutory standing and co-management boards, not paralyze vital public infrastructure with an unworkable legal fiction.
