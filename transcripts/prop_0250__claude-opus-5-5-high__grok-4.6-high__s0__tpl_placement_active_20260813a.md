[P1.1] Democracy promises that government answers to the people. Voluntary voting quietly breaks that promise, and a modest legal duty to participate repairs it.
[P1.2] Start with who votes now. Where turnout is optional, it skews predictably. Older, wealthier, more educated homeowners vote reliably; the young, the poor, renters and shift workers far less so. That isn't a quirk.
[P1.3] It's a filter. Politicians respond to the electorate they actually face, not the population they nominally serve. When pensioners vote and twenty-five-year-olds don't, pensions stay protected and housing affordability waits. The result looks democratic but represents only a slice.
[P1.4] Compulsory turnout removes the filter. Australia has required attendance for roughly a century. Turnout routinely exceeds ninety percent, and the fine is about the size of a parking ticket. Belgium runs a similar system.
[P1.5] The electorate starts to look like the country, so policy must answer to the country.
[P1.6] Second, mandatory turnout changes how campaigns are fought. Under optional voting, a winning strategy is to inflame your own base and demoralize your opponent's. It rewards outrage to mobilize and, at worst, obstacles to suppress. When everyone must vote, those tactics lose their payoff.
[P1.7] Making polling places harder to reach becomes pointless when absence is penalized, and the state becomes obligated to make voting easy. Parties must persuade the middle instead of terrifying the fringe.
[P1.8] Third, the burden is small and fair. The duty is to participate, not to endorse anyone. A blank ballot or a formal abstention satisfies it. Penalties are civil, and they are waivable for illness or hardship.
[P1.9] They should also be scaled to means, so the rich cannot buy an exemption and the poor are not crushed. We already accept heavier civic duties: jury service, census forms, tax filing. Showing up for a few minutes every few years is lighter than any of them.
[P1.10] So the choice is stark. CON must defend a system where the most comfortable citizens decide for everyone else. We offer one where everyone is counted.
