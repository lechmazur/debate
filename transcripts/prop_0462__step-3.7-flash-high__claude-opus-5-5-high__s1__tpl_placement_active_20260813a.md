[P1.1] Early advanced math tracking is a discriminatory sorting machine that predetermines educational trajectories before students can demonstrate full potential, disproportionately locking low-income, Black, Latino, and Indigenous students out of STEM pathways. The current system relies on biased teacher recommendations and one-off test scores to place students in tracks as early as sixth grade; once sorted into a lower track, students almost never advance, even if they have the latent ability to master advanced material. Our proposal dismantles this broken system by replacing most advanced math tracking with heterogeneous core classes paired with intensive, differentiated tutoring supports.
[P1.2] Heterogeneous classes set uniform, grade-level expectations for all students, eliminating the low track where students are taught material years below grade level with no path to acceleration. The tutoring tier provides targeted remediation for students who missed foundational skills, and extension work for advanced learners, so no student is held back by whole-class pace or left to struggle without support. Critics claim this model slows advanced students, but districts that eliminated early math tracking report no decline in advanced math participation or performance among high-achieving learners, while previously tracked-out students gain access to advanced coursework they were systematically denied under the old system.
[P1.3] This policy does not lower standards—it equalizes access to them, and the tutoring infrastructure ensures every student can meet high bars, closing persistent achievement gaps without sacrificing excellence.
