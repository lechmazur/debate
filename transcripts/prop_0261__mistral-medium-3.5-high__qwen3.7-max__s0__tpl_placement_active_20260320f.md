Digital inheritance law must end this arbitrary distinction.
[P1.2] Consider what’s at stake: a parent’s curated e-book library representing years of reading, a musician’s collection of purchased samples essential to their craft, or a lifetime of digital family photos. Some US states have begun addressing this with laws granting executors access to digital accounts, but these are incomplete solutions.
[P1.3] We need a clear, national guarantee that purchased digital assets transfer to next of kin, just as physical property does. In an age where digital purchases often replace physical ones, law must evolve to reflect reality.
[P1.4] Guaranteeing digital inheritance rights isn’t about forcing companies to maintain services indefinitely; it’s about ensuring that what you’ve purchased can be passed on.
