[P1.1] Safety on a dating app is not a luxury amenity. It is the floor. People meet strangers, often alone, with asymmetric information; the platform’s basic job is to reduce the chance that the stranger is a fake, a harasser, or a fraud. That is why identity verification, blocking, and scam response should be free by default—not because platforms owe every premium feature, but because these three are harm-prevention infrastructure.
[P1.2] The decisive reason is network effects. A safety feature protects me only if it covers the people I might meet. If verification is paywalled, scammers simply target the unverified majority; even paying users still browse a pool full of fakes. If unlimited blocking is premium, harassers learn which accounts cannot easily escape them.
[P1.3] If scam reporting and response are behind a subscription, the fraudster gets a free run at everyone who did not pay. Safety is not a private subscription; it is a public condition of the marketplace.
[P1.4] Paywalling also creates the wrong incentive: the platform earns money by keeping the dangerous version slightly worse. A block button that costs money is a tollbooth on the exit from a bad date. A scam-response queue reserved for subscribers is a fire alarm sold per room. And the people most likely to pay are not always the most at risk; a student, a closeted user, or someone in a controlling situation may be least able to subscribe and most in need of a fast block.
[P1.5] Opponents will say safety is expensive. Fine—charge for premium discovery, filters, or enhanced visibility. Do not charge for the emergency exit. Basic verification, blocking, and scam response are cheap relative to the liability, churn, and reputational damage of a platform known as unsafe.
[P1.6] Free-by-default safety is how the market works for everyone instead of selling protection to those who can afford it.
