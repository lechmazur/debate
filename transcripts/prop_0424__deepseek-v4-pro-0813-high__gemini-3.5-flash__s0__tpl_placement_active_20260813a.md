[P1.1] Loneliness is not mainly a shortage of matching algorithms or chat interventions; it is a shortage of places where people can be around others without a transaction, appointment, or login. Cities should therefore direct scarce loneliness funding toward libraries, recreation centers, and late-hour public spaces before app-based tools.
[P1.2] First, public spaces attack loneliness at a structural root. They create repeated, low-pressure incidental contact: the retiree seeing familiar morning regulars, the teenager in pickup basketball, the parent at story hour. That accumulation builds weak ties and the feeling of being known. App interventions are usually scheduled, one-to-one, and episodic; they treat loneliness as an individual deficit rather than a missing social habitat.
[P1.3] A city that leaves its residents with nowhere inviting to gather will keep producing lonely people, no matter how many users download a well-designed app.
[P1.4] Second, infrastructure reaches people apps leave behind. Libraries and rec centers are free, walkable, and do not require a smartphone, data plan, credit card, or handing over intimate emotional data. Elderly residents, low-income families, teenagers, recent immigrants, and people with unstable housing need places to be, not just a notification on a locked screen. App-first responses skew toward the already connected and digitally comfortable.
[P1.5] Third, public space yields more durable public value. A library is also a cooling center, homework space, job-search hub, and civic meeting room. A late-hour public space improves safety and gives shift workers, students, and older adults somewhere to gather. These assets serve whole neighborhoods for decades.
[P1.6] An app may look cheap per user, but it demands continuous marketing, maintenance, and attention; if abandoned, it leaves nothing physical behind.
[P1.7] This is not a case for banning apps. It is a funding priority. If the goal is belonging, cities should build places where people can actually appear to one another.
