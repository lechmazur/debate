[P1.1] Driving a car is a high-speed, high-stakes activity that demands continuous visual attention. Automakers must retain physical buttons for core functions like climate control, hazard lights, and defrosters because tactile controls fundamentally protect driver safety, whereas touchscreens actively compromise it.
[P1.2] The fatal flaw of the touchscreen is its lack of tactile feedback. You cannot feel a digital sub-menu or a virtual slider. Because drivers cannot rely on muscle memory to operate a flat pane of glass, they are forced to look away from the road to confirm their inputs. At highway speeds, looking down for just two seconds to adjust a touchscreen climate menu means driving completely blind for the length of half a football field.
[P1.3] Physical dials and switches, by contrast, possess distinct shapes and boundaries. Drivers can operate them entirely by touch, keeping their eyes exactly where they belong: on the road.
[P1.4] Furthermore, the core functions in question are urgent safety utilities, not optional entertainment features. If a windshield suddenly fogs over during a storm, a driver needs instant, guaranteed access to the defroster. Forcing them to tap through layered digital menus while exterior visibility drops is a recipe for disaster.
[P1.5] Compounding this danger is the reality of software reliability and environmental conditions. Touchscreens freeze, lag, and occasionally crash. If a car's infotainment system reboots, a driver must never lose the ability to activate their hazard lights. Additionally, capacitive screens are notoriously difficult to operate on bumpy roads or while wearing winter gloves—precisely the conditions under which a driver most needs the heater and defroster.
[P1.6] Automakers are moving toward touchscreen-only interiors primarily because flat digital panels are cheaper to manufacture and wire than complex physical switchgear. But maximizing assembly line profit margins at the expense of intuitive, reliable, and safe vehicle operation is an unacceptable trade-off. To ensure safety in critical moments, core driving functions must remain physical.
