A legal cap on offsets answers the wrong problem. Fraudulent or low-quality credits should be excluded by verification standards, liability, and disclosure rules. High-quality out-of-chain mitigation should still count, because the atmosphere benefits from genuine tonne reductions no matter which corporate ledger records them.
[C1.2] If a credit is fake, no percentage makes it acceptable; if it is real, an arbitrary cap needlessly discards it.
[C1.3] This cap also misreads how global value chains work. A retailer, airline, or food company often controls only a thin slice of its lifecycle emissions. Requiring most cuts “in-value-chain” pushes firms toward reductions that are easy to audit inside their own operations while starving cheaper, larger reductions elsewhere. If a shipping company can achieve more climate benefit by funding methane destruction or forest protection than by marginally improving a vessel that will be replaced in five years, a rigid cap forces the worse climate choice.
[C1.4] The hardest-to-abate sectors expose the rule’s cost. Cement, aviation, agriculture, and some heavy freight will have residual emissions for years. They need credible offsets not as a license to pollute, but as a bridge to pay for removal and avoided emissions while low-carbon technologies scale. Banning offsets beyond a small share risks making net-zero commitments legally untenable, which weakens corporate incentives rather than strengthening them.
[C1.5] Finally, the proposition would starve the very finance that credible offsets provide. Demand from corporate claims supports verified methane destruction, forest protection with real baselines, and early-stage carbon removal. If quality is the concern, regulate quality: registries, standardized baselines, penalties for misrepresentation, and public accounting. A blunt cap does none of that; it substitutes an arbitrary ratio for evidence-based policy.
[C1.6] Vote CON: regulate bad offsets, don’t outlaw the cost-effective mitigation and climate finance that good offsets supply.
