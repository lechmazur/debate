[C1.2] A ban on behavioral ads would not remove the auction; it would make it dumber. Contextual targeting is often cruder: a teen watching fitness content may see more weight-loss ads, not fewer.
[C1.3] Second, the brain-development argument proves too much. Impulse regulation and susceptibility to influence continue into the twenties. If under-sixteen is the line, it is an arbitrary line, not a scientific boundary. [C1.4] The claim that she cannot recognize persuasion ignores that adolescents routinely spot sponsored content, compare prices, and distrust obvious manipulation.
[C1.5] Third, prohibition would create worse privacy problems. Verification may require identity documents or behavioral inference, creating a data trail more sensitive than the ad profile it supposedly prevents. Children would lie upward, or platforms would age-gate content and push teens toward darker corners.
[C1.6] The better path is transparency and control: require platforms to explain targeting in plain language, let parents or teens switch off personalization, and ban targeting by sensitive categories like weight or self-harm. That protects minors without pretending a categorical ban can work.
