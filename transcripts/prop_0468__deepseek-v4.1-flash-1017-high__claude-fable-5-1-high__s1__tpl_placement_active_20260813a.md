[P1.1] Every dirty dollar needs a name to hide behind. An anonymous shell is not an ordinary company with privacy; it is a legal vehicle deliberately built so that no authority can answer the only question that matters: who really owns and controls this? That single design feature defeats banks’ customer checks, sanctions screening, tax authorities, and police. A shell can hold a bank account, a London flat, or a stake in a local firm while the person receiving the benefit remains invisible.
[P1.2] By the time investigators untangle one layer, the money has moved. A universal ban attacks that chokepoint directly.
[P1.3] The proposition is narrower than it sounds. It does not ban shell companies, holding structures, or legitimate confidentiality. A company may keep its ownership non-public. It may not keep its ultimate beneficial owner unknown to every competent authority.
[P1.4] Formation agents must identify and verify the human behind the entity; registries must hold that information; bearer shares and nominee arrangements whose purpose is to defeat identification must go. Existing anonymous shells must be regularized or wound down—no grandfathering into immunity. That preserves ordinary commercial privacy while ending the market for institutionalized opacity.
[P1.5] Universality is the core. A ban in some countries merely pushes paper companies to the weakest link; a global baseline means no compliant jurisdiction, bank, or formation agent can offer anonymity as a product. Criminals can still lie, but they must now corrupt a gatekeeper and leave a record—exactly what investigators need. Opponents will warn about cost and privacy, yet the status quo’s cost is borne by victims of fraud, trafficking, sanctions evasion, and public corruption, and by every honest business forced to compete with opaque fronts.
[P1.6] The choice is not between perfect enforcement and none; it is whether the international system keeps a lawful supply of concealment. It should not. Vote PRO.
