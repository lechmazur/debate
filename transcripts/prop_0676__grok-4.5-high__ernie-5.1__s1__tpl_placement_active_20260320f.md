[P1.1] Automated video and personality assessments promise faster hiring but deliver bias, opacity, and pseudoscience at industrial scale. Governments should ban or heavily restrict them because they systematically degrade fair opportunity, invade privacy, and rest on weak predictive claims that markets refuse to police.
[P1.2] The mechanism is simple and corrosive. Software scores facial micro-expressions, vocal cadence, word choice, or forced-choice personality items against an “ideal” profile usually derived from past employees. Those historical data already embed race, gender, disability, age, and class patterns. The algorithm then multiplies them: darker skin under uneven lighting, non-native accents, reduced eye contact from autism or trauma, speech differences, or even resting facial expressions get scored as risk.
[P1.3] Candidates never see the weightings or the training sample. A human interviewer can be questioned; a black-box model cannot. The result is not efficiency—it is discrimination that scales without leaving fingerprints.
[P1.4] Validity is equally thin. Most off-the-shelf personality inventories explain only modest variance in later job performance, and commercial emotion-recognition claims fare worse still. Firms almost never publish independent, job-specific validation or disparate-impact audits. Rejection therefore arrives as unexplained silence.
[P1.5] Applicants cannot improve, contest, or even confirm that the decision tracked legitimate criteria rather than model error or cultural conformity.
[P1.6] Privacy loss is absolute. Candidates must perform under surveillance and surrender behavioral biometrics that employers can store, re-analyze, or sell. Refusal equals automatic elimination. No meaningful consent exists under that power imbalance.
[P1.7] Private markets will not correct this. Job seekers lack leverage, vendors sell proprietary “AI magic,” and HR departments optimize for short-term filtering cost rather than long-term fairness or accuracy. Only government restrictions—outright bans on high-stakes automated scoring without proven validity and mandatory bias audits—can restore a baseline of equal treatment. Efficiency purchased by silent, unaccountable exclusion is not progress; it is a new gatekeeping technology that locks out talent for the wrong reasons.
