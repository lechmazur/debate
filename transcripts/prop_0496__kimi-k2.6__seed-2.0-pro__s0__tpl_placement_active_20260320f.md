[P1.1] Democracy depends on one stark principle: when a state kills in your name, you must be able to hold someone electorally accountable. Private military contractors shatter that chain. By inserting corporate employees into combat roles, governments replace a public chain of command answerable to citizens with a private one answerable to shareholders and profit margins. When violence becomes a revenue stream, the incentive to escalate or prolong conflict comes from quarterly earnings reports, not national strategy.
[P1.2] That is not outsourcing logistics; it is outsourcing accountability itself.
[P1.3] The structural problem runs deeper than individual scandals. National soldiers operate under legal frameworks, legislative oversight, and transparent rules of engagement. Contractors operate under classified contracts and corporate nondisclosure. When a uniformed soldier commits an atrocity, ministers testify, inquiries convene, and voters respond at the ballot box.
[P1.4] When a contractor does the same, liability vanishes into a fog of subcontracting, jurisdiction-shopping, and executive privilege. The public cannot punish what it cannot see, and legislators cannot oversee what is hidden behind trade-secret claims.
[P1.5] Equally corrosive is casualty opacity. Contractor deaths do not appear on official military tallies or trigger the political shock that national losses produce. Leaders can escalate conflicts, stretch commitments, and absorb bloodshed without the electoral feedback that normally restrains democratic war-making. A government that would face fierce opposition for deploying ten thousand uniformed soldiers can slip thousands of contractors into the same war zone while keeping Parliament or Congress deliberately underinformed.
[P1.6] The ballot box cannot correct a policy the electorate does not know exists.
[P1.7] This is not a debate about efficiency or niche support roles; it is about constitutional evasion. Banning private combat contractors restores the necessary condition for democratic accountability: that every trigger pulled in an international conflict traces back to a decision made by an elected official whom the public can remove. Without that direct line, we do not have limited war; we have hidden war, and hidden war is the enemy of self-government.
