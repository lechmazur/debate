[P1.1] When a consumer clicks "Buy" and pays full retail price for a book, film, album, or game, a basic economic promise is made: money is exchanged for a durable good. Digital marketplaces have broken that promise, charging ownership prices while burying fine print that grants only a fragile, revocable license. Consumers must retain meaningful ownership rights over digital purchases for three reasons.
[P1.2] First, basic transactional fairness demands that consumer rights match the price paid. Digital media is routinely priced identically to physical copies. Yet when you buy a physical disc or book, the publisher cannot break into your home and confiscate it because a back-end licensing deal expired. In the digital realm, this happens constantly.
[P1.3] Platforms have remotely deleted purchased novels from e-readers, erased bought films from user libraries, and bricked paid video games by shutting down authentication servers. If corporations want the total control of a rental model, they must call it a rental and price it accordingly. Charging ownership prices for revocable access is an institutionalized bait-and-switch.
[P1.4] Second, revocable licenses create an abusive power asymmetry. Because access is tethered to a live platform account, an erroneous algorithmic ban, a corporate bankruptcy, or a storefront closure can wipe out thousands of dollars of a consumer’s legally purchased library overnight without refund. Meaningful ownership—specifically the right to download, back up, and use purchased media locally without perpetual corporate permission—insulates consumers from platform coercion and collapse.
[P1.5] Finally, ownership is vital for cultural preservation. When media exists only as revocable licenses, books, music, and films can be retroactively edited or erased from history whenever rights holders change their minds. Granting consumers durable rights to possess, archive, and transfer the media they paid for ensures that human culture survives corporate whims. If you pay to buy it, you should own it.
