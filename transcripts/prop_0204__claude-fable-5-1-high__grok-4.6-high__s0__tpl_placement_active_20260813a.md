[P1.1] The word doing the work in this motion is "presumptively." Not "always." Not "ban." A presumption is a default that the acquirer can rebut.
[P1.2] The question is simply who carries the burden of proof when a firm tied to a rival government wants to buy a lithium deposit, a cobalt refinery, or a rare-earth separation plant. Today, in most jurisdictions, the state must prove harm. The default should run the other way, for three reasons.
[P1.3] First, the errors are asymmetric. Wrongly blocking a deal costs one seller a somewhat lower price; other buyers exist, and the ore stays in the ground for tomorrow. Wrongly approving a deal hands a rival durable control over a chokepoint that takes a decade or more to replicate. Mines, refineries, and permitting timelines cannot be rebuilt at the speed of a crisis.
[P1.4] When one error is cheap and reversible and the other is expensive and permanent, rational review tilts toward the cheap mistake.
[P1.5] Second, the threat is proven, not hypothetical. Rare-earth flows to Japan were squeezed during a 2010 territorial dispute. Export controls on gallium, germanium, and graphite have been imposed in recent years explicitly as leverage. A firm that is state-owned, state-financed, or subject to laws compelling cooperation with its government is not a neutral commercial actor; the pattern of behavior is already on record.
[P1.6] Third, case-by-case review fails precisely where it matters. Reviewers face an information problem: ownership is layered through shell entities, and intent is unobservable until the moment of coercion. The acquirer knows its own ties and plans; the reviewer does not. Shifting the burden simply puts it on the party that holds the information.
[P1.7] A genuinely independent firm can show ring-fencing, offtake guarantees, and clean ownership. A state instrument cannot, and that inability is exactly what the review should surface.
[P1.8] A presumption is how serious institutions handle high-stakes uncertainty. Banks presume default risk; pharmacology presumes toxicity until proven safe. Critical minerals deserve the same discipline.

[C1.8] Pharmacologists presume toxicity because the pill goes in a body. A mine sits under another government’s law. Banks do not refuse every borrower with a foreign parent; they price the risk.
