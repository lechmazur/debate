[C1.1] The proposition rests on a comfortable assumption: that the evidence showing mental-health benefit is strong, that screening is rigorous, and that long-term regret is rare. Each of those assumptions is shakier than the resolution suggests.
[C1.2] On evidence: The studies typically marshaled are short-term, observational, lack proper control groups, and suffer from substantial loss to follow-up. That loss is not random. The patients most likely to drop out are precisely those who detransitioned, experienced complications, or worsened. The clean comparisons in clinic summaries are not the same as rigorous comparative evidence.
[C1.3] Long-term registry data from Scandinavia has produced more mixed results than the clinic-based literature implies, including persistent elevations in psychiatric hospitalization.
[C1.4] On screening: The resolution says "carefully screened," but in practice, screening protocols vary widely across clinics and countries. Many adolescents now presenting have co-occurring autism, trauma, or other psychiatric conditions that complicate differential diagnosis. The recent shift in the patient population, from predominantly early-childhood onset boys to adolescent-onset girls, has not been reconciled with older desistance research suggesting most children with dysphoria eventually desist.
[C1.5] On harm: Puberty blockers are not a neutral pause. They affect bone density accrual, brain development, and fertility potential. Most adolescents started on blockers proceed to cross-sex hormones, which carry documented risks: cardiovascular strain, irreversible infertility, sexual dysfunction, and surgical complications. Detransition is difficult to measure because the cohort is young and follow-up is poor, but the available signals are not reassuring, and the social and medical costs of reversal are severe.
[C1.6] The proposition asks us to weigh a contested short-term mental-health gain against undercounted long-term physical and psychological harm. On the actual evidence, that trade does not clearly favor intervention, and we are not obligated to accept an unproven benefit as a settled truth.
