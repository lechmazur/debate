[P1.1] Mass deportations treat irregular migration as a police problem. It is a labor-market problem. Gulf economies cannot run without large inflows of construction, service, and domestic workers. North African states send workers, host them, and watch others transit toward Europe.
[P1.2] The jobs are already there. Narrow legal channels do not stop movement; they push it through smugglers, deserts, and overstays.
[P1.3] Legal labor corridors give states the control they claim to want. Bilateral agreements, seasonal contracts, and guest-worker programs let governments set quotas, run checks, and bind employers to wages and housing. Workers arrive documented. Remittances move through banks.
[P1.4] Circular schemes make return the plan, not a failed raid. Smugglers lose customers when a cheaper, safer option exists. Origin governments prefer a corridor to a cemetery. Destination states get labor without a permanent undocumented underclass.
[P1.5] That is how irregularity shrinks.
[P1.6] Deportations do the opposite. They cost more, recycle the same people—returnees often try again because the wage gap remains—and leave jobs unfilled, so employers hire off the books and the next convoy forms. Transit states that rely on mass expulsions watch neighbors dump the problem back and partners demand still more. Gulf raids without expanded recruitment only deepen irregularity or stall projects.
[P1.7] This is not open borders. It is a managed tap instead of a broken dam. Legal corridors create constituencies for order: employers who can hire legally, origin states that gain remittances, workers who have something to lose. Enforcement stays for those who refuse the legal path.
[P1.8] The first move must still be opening that path, because people will keep coming to jobs that already exist. Deportation-first policy pretends those jobs are optional. They are not. The alternative is more boats, more graves, and the same shortages.
[P1.9] States that accept this will cut irregular flows faster, cheaper, and with fewer deaths than states that keep loading planes.
