[P1.2] Start with collision risk. Every satellite added multiplies conjunctions with everything already up there, including hundreds of thousands of fragments too small to track but large enough to kill. The 2009 Iridium-Cosmos collision alone produced thousands of lasting debris pieces; a single 2021 anti-satellite test forced repeated space-station dodges. Operators already execute tens of thousands of automated avoidance maneuvers a year — a system that works until one solar storm, one software error, or one dead satellite at the wrong altitude starts a cascade.
[P1.3] Kessler syndrome is a chain reaction no government can stop once lit. A moratorium is the only lever that actually reduces risk while rules are being written.
[P1.4] Second, astronomy. Nobody licensed away the night sky, yet a handful of firms have effectively appropriated it. [P1.5] A pause is the first time the public interest is even priced into a launch decision.
