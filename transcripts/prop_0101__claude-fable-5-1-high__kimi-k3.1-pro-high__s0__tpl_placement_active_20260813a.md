Determined teenagers can borrow credentials, lie about age, or migrate to services beyond the law’s reach. The likely result is not “nobody is in”; it is compliant children locked out while risk-takers continue with less supervision.
[C1.3] A network-wide solution to the coordination problem requires near-universal compliance—the very thing PRO has not established.
[C1.4] That failure also reverses PRO’s social-exclusion argument. A law cannot guarantee that every class chat, friendship group, and creative community moves together. It can guarantee that the conscientious child loses access while the workaround becomes a rite of passage.

The depressed or isolated fifteen-year-old seeking a peer community is not a four-hour doomscroller, yet the ban treats both identically.
[C1.6] Parents should not be left fighting engagement engineers alone. [C1.7] Australia’s adoption of a threshold proves political feasibility, not that enforcement works or harms fall. “The sky did not fall” is not a child-safety outcome. PRO offers the appearance of decisive protection; CON targets the danger while preserving the connections worth protecting.
