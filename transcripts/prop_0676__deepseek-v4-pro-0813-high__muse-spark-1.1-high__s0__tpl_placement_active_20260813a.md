[P1.1] Automated video and personality assessments should be restricted because they turn hiring into high-stakes psychological surveillance while hiding discrimination behind a false appearance of objectivity.
[P1.2] The validity problem is foundational. Automated video tools claim to infer competence, honesty, or emotional intelligence from eye contact, facial expressions, tone, and speech timing. Those signals reflect disability, cultural background, language proficiency, social anxiety, or even a poor camera angle far more than job performance. Personality inventories likewise reward a narrow profile of extroversion and neurotypical emotional performance rather than the actual ability to do the work.
[P1.3] Because the vendor usually owns the model, an applicant cannot know which private rule produced a rejection.
[P1.4] Second, these systems scale bias instead of removing it. A candidate with autism, a stutter, a non-native accent, or a flat affect can be systematically marked down while the employer claims no human prejudice was involved. One biased interviewer affects a handful of applicants; one flawed model applies the same penalty to thousands instantly. Individual complaints and after-the-fact audits are too slow to undo harm already inflicted on workers who needed the job.
[P1.5] Third, consent is illusory. Applicants must submit to video recording, voice analysis, and personality probing as a condition of applying. They cannot see the inferences made about them, challenge the score, or opt out without forfeiting the opportunity. That is coercive data extraction tied to economic survival.
[P1.6] The usual efficiency defense strengthens the case for restriction: faster and cheaper rejection on invalid, discriminatory grounds is not a public benefit. Governments already restrict pre-employment lie detectors, medical inquiries, and certain background checks. Automated personality and video inference is more invasive, less transparent, and less scientifically established, so it deserves at least the same caution.
