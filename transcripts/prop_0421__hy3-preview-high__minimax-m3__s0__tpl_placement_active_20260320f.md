[C1.1] The premise of this motion is backward. The "final checkout screen" is not where fees are hidden — it is where they are disclosed, itemized, and made visible. A delivery app that shows "Subtotal: $20, Service Fee: $3, Delivery Fee: $2, Total: $25" is more transparent than one displaying a flat $25 from the start. Bundling fees into a headline price does not increase transparency; it destroys it.
[C1.2] Consider what mandatory pre-checkout disclosure actually requires. It forces businesses to either guess at fees that depend on real-time variables — delivery distance, demand surge, payment method, pickup versus dispatch — or to bake them into a single bundled number that conceals their composition. Either outcome is worse for the consumer. The first is inaccurate; the second is opaque.
[C1.3] Restaurants face the same problem. A mandatory service charge often depends on choices made during the meal itself: whether wine was ordered, how many guests showed up, whether a special request was added. A restaurant cannot pre-calculate a 15% service charge before the customer has eaten. Ticketing platforms adjust fees by delivery method — e-ticket, will-call, or mail — none knowable until the buyer chooses.
[C1.4] The motion also misreads consumer psychology. People do not resent seeing a $3 service fee at checkout. They resent not understanding what they paid for. Itemized disclosure answers that question.
[C1.5] A bundled pre-checkout price leaves customers guessing which portion is tax, which is fee, which is the underlying product. The screen PRO wants to eliminate is the one doing the most informational work.
[C1.6] Finally, the motion is paternalistic. It assumes adults cannot evaluate a clearly itemized total on the final screen and need the state to dictate the order in which information appears. What matters is that all mandatory charges are visible and unavoidable at the moment of commitment — which they already are. If PRO's concern is informed consent, that consent is delivered by itemization, not by chronological rearrangement.
[C1.7] PRO solves nothing that itemization doesn't already solve, and creates two new harms: premature inaccuracy and forced bundling.
