[P1.1] The default for a human life should not be corporate impersonation.
[P1.2] A mimic chatbot is not statistics. It is a synthetic person that speaks as you, remembers as you, and can be prompted to say anything. When that is built from your emails, texts, voice notes, videos without your permission, it violates the most basic principle of autonomy: control over who you are to others. That control does not evaporate at death.
[P1.3] We enforce wills, we protect bodies, and in many states we enforce post-mortem publicity rights precisely because identity persists as an interest worth protecting.
[P1.4] Second, the harms are real and unfixable. The dead cannot correct the record. A model trained on your data can be made to apologize for things you never did, confess to beliefs you hated, endorse a candidate you opposed, or comfort your child with false intimacy. Families are forced to grieve twice — once for the person, once for the puppet wearing their face.
[P1.5] And unlike a biography that quotes you, a chatbot continues to generate new statements under your name forever, for profit you never shared. That is not innovation. That is identity theft without a victim able to call a lawyer, which is exactly why law must step in.
[P1.6] Opt-in solves this cleanly. Those who want a legacy avatar — a grandmother preserving stories, an author preserving craft — can sign clear consent while alive, with limits. Those who do not want it are protected by default. Opt-out is a fiction when the subject is dead; you cannot ping someone after they die to object.
[P1.7] Companies want a default of free-taking because every inbox is free training data. The law should set the default the other way: your life is yours until you say otherwise. If anyone should approve or profit from your afterlife, it is you, beforehand.

[C1.7] The motion also treats a published interview and a private inbox as the same data, and a family memorial the same as a commercial puppet. Existing rights can tell those cases apart. A pre-death paperwork ban cannot.
[C1.8] Law should police leftover rights and deception, not invent a licensing regime the dead of 2015 could never have entered. Unsigned silence is not a command that later speech forget your voice.
