[P1.1] Solar geoengineering is not a laboratory curiosity. It is a deliberate intervention in a shared global system — the atmosphere — whose effects cross every border on Earth. That is precisely why the sequence matters: first we build the rules, then we experiment in the field. Reversing that order hands a small number of states and private actors the power to alter everyone's weather while everyone else watches.
[P1.2] Field experiments are not neutral acts of science. Stratospheric aerosol injection, even in "small" tests, is designed to demonstrate the feasibility of a technology whose deployment would redistribute rainfall, monsoon patterns, and agricultural yields across regions that never consented. Once a program has generated data, trained scientists, built hardware, and created constituencies with a stake in continuation, governance becomes harder, not easier. The experiment produces the political facts that make binding rules nearly impossible to negotiate afterwards.
[P1.3] This is why a moratorium must be temporary and gate-locked to a treaty: it preserves optionality, it does not close the door.
[P1.4] Voluntary restraint has already failed. Firms have lofted particles from balloons and called it research; national regulators scrambled after the fact. That is the preview of ungoverned experimentation — small actors, unilateral claims, no liability regime, no monitoring standard, no shared decision about who gets to decide. A binding international framework is the only instrument that can allocate liability, mandate transparency, require environmental assessment, and give affected states — especially the most climate-vulnerable — a real voice.
[P1.5] Least-emitting nations stand to lose the most from altered monsoons, and they are precisely the voices absent when a research program announces its flight plan.
[P1.6] The opposing position effectively asks us to accept permanent governance failure as the price of incremental data. But the value of a few additional field data points is small compared with the cost of legitimizing an ungoverned pathway to planetary-scale intervention. Precaution here is not paralysis; it is sequencing. Build the rules first.
[P1.7] Then decide together.
