[P1.1] An influencer with five million followers who praises a skincare serum, a trading app, or a weight-loss drink is not chatting with friends. She is broadcasting an advertisement to an audience larger than many television shows reach, with none of the warnings television would require.
[P1.2] Traditional media personalities work under a simple principle: paid persuasion must be unmistakable before it begins to persuade. Sponsorship is announced upfront, in clear language, not buried after the sales pitch. A presenter cannot pretend a scripted endorsement is a spontaneous personal discovery, and a broadcaster cannot blur an advert into editorial content to make viewers lower their guard.
[P1.3] Influencer marketing needs that strictness even more, because its power comes from blurring exactly that line. A thirty-second television spot announces itself through music, branding, and an ad break. A sponsored haul, morning routine, or 'what I actually use' story looks like intimate, unpaid life. Followers — especially younger followers who trust the creator parasocially — are not evaluating a pitch; they think they are receiving a friend's recommendation.
[P1.4] A vague '#partner', a tiny label, or a disclosure after a swipe-up link exploits that trust.
[P1.5] The products make the stakes concrete. Traditional advertising rules are strictest precisely where undisclosed incentives can cause debt, damaged health, or unsafe choices. Influencers now routinely promote those same categories: supplements, cosmetic procedures, crypto and trading platforms, gambling, and diet products. If a radio host could not hide being paid to promote them, a creator with ten times the reach should not be able to either.
[P1.6] This is not a ban on endorsements and it does not burden hobbyists. The motion targets large audiences, where reach, income, and professional teams make compliance easy: say it is an ad, say it early, say it in the video and the caption, and face real penalties for hiding it. Anything weaker creates a regulatory loophole — move the ad to Instagram, and the honesty rules disappear.
