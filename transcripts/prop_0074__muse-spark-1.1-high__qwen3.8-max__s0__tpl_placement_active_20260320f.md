Early gifts were spent, lost, diluted by wars, mergers, depressions, and new donors. [C1.3] If the claim is unjust enrichment, PRO must trace a continuing benefit, not merely assert that history compounds. Otherwise every old institution owes forever, and no limiting principle exists.
[C1.4] The remedy also misassigns the task. Universities are schools, not reparations agencies or community development banks. If society owes repair for slavery and dispossession, that debt is broad, public, and democratic—not one that should be improvised campus by campus, with arbitrary lines around which descendants qualify and which sins count.
[C1.5] Need-based scholarships and broad public investment address present exclusion without freezing injustice into permanent categories.
[C1.6] Finally, the proposition attacks a straw man. No serious defense of historical acknowledgment says words alone are enough. Many universities already pair recognition with first-generation support, tribal partnerships, and local hiring.
