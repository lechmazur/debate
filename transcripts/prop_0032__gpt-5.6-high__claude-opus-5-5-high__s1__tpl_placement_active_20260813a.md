[P1.1] A high-impact predictive result is not ordinary consumer information. A report suggesting elevated risk of hereditary cancer, a serious cardiac condition, or an untreatable neurological disease can reshape medical decisions, family relationships, and a person’s sense of the future. The company that delivers such a result should therefore ensure that qualified interpretation is available before disclosure.
[P1.2] First, meaningful consent requires comprehension, not merely clicking “I agree” when purchasing a kit. Genetic risk is unusually easy to misunderstand. A result may reflect probability rather than diagnosis; its significance may depend on family history, ancestry, test limitations, and whether a clinical laboratory confirms it. A certified genetic counselor can explain penetrance, uncertainty, false reassurance from negative findings, and appropriate next steps.
[P1.3] Without that context, companies are handing customers conclusions whose practical meaning many cannot reliably determine.
[P1.4] Second, this requirement prevents foreseeable, high-stakes harm. A frightened customer may pursue unnecessary procedures, alter medication, or disclose alarming information to relatives before confirmation. Another may interpret a limited negative result as a clean bill of health and skip appropriate screening. Counseling does not eliminate uncertainty, but it converts raw data into safer decisions and directs customers toward clinical follow-up.
[P1.5] Third, responsibility should follow profit and control. The company selects the variants, designs the report, markets its usefulness, and controls the moment of disclosure. It should not externalize the predictable costs of confusion onto customers, families, and healthcare systems. If a business cannot support responsible delivery of its most consequential product, it should not sell that product as consumer-ready.
[P1.6] This is a narrow, proportionate safeguard: only high-impact predictive health results, only access to certified counseling, and access can be provided remotely. It need not give counselors veto power or force customers into treatment. Rather, it preserves autonomy by making the customer’s choice informed. A few minutes of friction and modest cost are outweighed by avoiding irreversible decisions made from misunderstood risk.
[P1.7] The principle is simple: the more consequential the result, the stronger the duty to deliver it responsibly.
