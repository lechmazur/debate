[P1.2] When a Pacific atoll loses its freshwater lens, when repeated droughts push Sahelian herders off the land, when a hurricane destroys a Honduran town for the third time, the affected family does not need a treaty article before it needs a visa.

[P1.6] The objection that early pathways are arbitrary or a pull factor proves too much. No allocation is neutral—a closed border allocates by wealth, luck, and smuggling networks. [P1.7] Settled law is worth pursuing, but it cannot be a precondition for rescue. The motion asks for pathways before settlement, not instead of settlement. [C1.3] That is not "administered mercy," it is administered discretion, and discretion granted without legal grounding can be withdrawn without legal grounding.
[C1.4] That instability is the actual danger. [C1.5] There's also an opportunity-cost the affirmative ignores. Political capital and fiscal capacity spent standing up parallel, ad hoc "climate pathways" is capital not spent on in-place adaptation finance, loss-and-damage funding, and pressing for an actual settled framework with enforceable burden-sharing. PRO frames patience as abandonment. It is the opposite: durable protection requires getting the legal architecture right before you scale admissions, not after.

[P4.5] The decisive comparison is therefore not purity versus imperfection. It is movement with a record versus principle with no mechanism.
