[C1.1] The proposition asks us to ban a technology, not a fraud. “Hyper-realistic political deepfake” has no workable legal boundary: at what point does synthetic audio become satire, parody, documentation, or political expression? That line would be drawn by state officials, replacing private judgment with speech control.
[C1.2] PRO identifies the danger: fabricated recordings can mislead voters. But the chosen remedy creates two serious problems. First, a ban cannot reliably distinguish counterfeiting from political art. Investigative artists may need to illustrate corruption; satirists often work by placing plausible words in powerful mouths; anonymous activists may need synthetic voices to survive repression.
[C1.3] Political parody can reveal hypocrisy precisely by making deception recognizable as performance. A ban would punish expression for being realistic rather than for deceiving anyone. “Even at some cost” conceals a cost borne by precisely the people political speech is meant to protect.
[C1.4] Second, enforcement would either be ineffective or intrusive. A creator-based ban would be porous: code and models travel across borders, and anonymous publication defeats attribution. A platform-detection mandate would require mass inspection of political speech and likely greater collection of biometric and behavioral data, expanding surveillance in the name of protecting elections.
[C1.5] The better framework targets deceptive conduct rather than synthetic media: provenance tools, disclosure rules for political synthetic content, swift penalties for impersonation and forgery, and fast election warnings. These measures can make deception harder and traceable while preserving satire and protected anonymous expression. They will not make every fake harmless, but neither will an undifferentiated ban.
[C1.6] PRO’s mistake is treating authenticity and truth as identical. A ban may reduce one tactic while weakening the democratic speech needed to expose the larger problem. Synthetic media should not receive special immunity, but a democracy should not purchase protection from deception by surrendering the tools of scrutiny and dissent.
