[P1.1] The distinction at the heart of this motion is simple: there is a difference between learning and working for free. When a placement is genuinely educational—supervised, structured, teaching skills the intern couldn't get by simply doing the job—that's an apprenticeship in spirit, and the motion leaves it untouched. What the motion targets is the other kind: the "internship" that is really an unpaid job. Someone answers phones, files reports, does the coffee runs, produces work the organization depends on—and receives nothing.
[P1.2] That is not education. That is labor, and labor should be paid.
[P1.3] Let me offer three reasons this deserves heavy restriction, not a shrug.
[P1.4] First, exploitation. If an organization extracts real productive value from a person's time, calling it an "internship" doesn't change the economics—it just relabels wage theft as opportunity. We already prohibit paying workers below a minimum wage; allowing the price to fall to zero because someone is young and desperate for a foot in the door is the same harm, worse.
[P1.5] Second, entrenched inequality. Unpaid work is only available to those who can afford to work for nothing—people with parents subsidizing their rent in expensive cities where prestige industries cluster. Media, politics, fashion, law: these sectors funnel their gatekept early roles to the already-wealthy. The talented young person from a poor family literally cannot compete, not on merit, but on who can survive months without income.
[P1.6] Restriction cracks that gate open.
[P1.7] Third, market distortion. Free labor undercuts paid entry-level jobs. Why hire a junior when a rotating supply of unpaid graduates will do the same work? Restriction protects the very jobs young people need.
[P1.8] The elegant thing about this motion is its carve-out. It doesn't ban internships. It doesn't ban education. It says: if you're teaching, carry on; if you're employing, pay.
[P1.9] The burden falls only on those relabeling exploitation as generosity.
[P1.10] The question for CON is stark: what legitimate interest requires the freedom to have real work done for nothing?
