[P1.1] The next public-safety dollar should buy fewer shootings, not merely more patrol presence. [P1.2] Most shootings are not random crimes deterred by an additional officer on a corner. They are arguments, social-media slights, robberies, and neighborhood or gang conflicts that escalate until someone retaliates. Police generally arrive after the shots. A credible neighborhood outreach worker can arrive before them: mediate the specific conflict, separate likely retaliators, and stay involved long enough to break the cycle.
[P1.3] Programs using credible messengers, street outreach, hospital-based intervention, and intensive life coaching intervene directly in the chain that produces shootings. They also connect the highest-risk people to jobs, therapy, housing, and supervision, which changes the incentives that drive retaliation.
[P1.4] The cost comparison is lopsided. A single additional officer carries salary, benefits, training, overtime, supervision, equipment, and liability, often well over $100,000 annually before that officer prevents one marginal shooting. A small outreach team—several stipended workers plus a supervisor—can saturate the small network responsible for most local shootings for less than one officer’s fully loaded cost. Preventing even a handful of retaliatory shootings among that finite population produces a far lower cost per shooting prevented than an additional patrol hire can credibly deliver.
[P1.5] Policing also generates friction in precisely the neighborhoods where cooperation is most needed. Outreach workers build trust, de-escalate conflicts, and improve the flow of information. An additional officer mostly adds reactive capacity; an additional outreach worker adds mediation, prevention, and service access.
[P1.6] The real comparison is not civilian intervention against a perfect police force. It is the next dollar spent on a targeted, credible-messenger intervention versus the next dollar spent on a slow, expensive, reactive police hire. On that standard, scaling civilian community violence intervention wins.

[C1.6] Gambling public funds on fragile mediation networks while diluting core enforcement capacity will not reduce shootings cost-effectively.
