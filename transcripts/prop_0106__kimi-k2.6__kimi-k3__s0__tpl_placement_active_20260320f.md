[P1.1] Gender dysphoria is a serious medical diagnosis, and puberty blockers and hormone therapy are established, evidence-based treatments—not ideological experiments. When parents and qualified clinicians agree that care is appropriate, denying access does not protect children; it forces them through a puberty that medical consensus recognizes as directly harmful to their psychological and physical well-being.
[P1.2] The opposition will frame this as caution, but caution cuts both ways. Withholding treatment is an active decision with active, irreversible consequences. For a transgender adolescent, unwanted puberty permanently alters bone structure, deepens voices, and triggers breast growth that later demands painful and expensive surgical correction. The resulting distress is not abstract; it manifests in clinical depression, social withdrawal, and sharply elevated suicidality.
[P1.3] Puberty blockers are reversible; they act as a pause button that grants young patients and their families critical time to explore identity without the clock of irreversible biological change ticking against them. Cross-sex hormones are introduced only after prolonged, multidisciplinary evaluation. The alternative—watchful waiting through forced biological change—inflicts permanent physical and psychological damage that grows harder to repair with each month of delay.
[P1.4] The resolution already embeds rigorous safeguards: a formal diagnosis, parental consent, and independent clinical judgment about appropriateness. This is the same framework we use for other serious pediatric interventions, from chemotherapy to long-term psychiatric medication. We trust families and specialists to make these calls in every other domain because they—not politicians—hold the relevant expertise and bear the consequences.
[P1.5] Opponents ask the state to override a unanimous medical and familial consensus with political prohibition. That is not protection; it is centralized paternalism dressed up as concern, substituting ideological discomfort for clinical judgment. When qualified clinicians and parents jointly determine that blockers or hormone therapy are medically necessary, government obstruction causes measurable, lasting harm that falls heaviest on the most vulnerable patients. We should treat the diagnosis, respect the process, and trust the professionals at the bedside.

[C1.1] When a treatment's pioneers start walking away, the rest of us should pay attention. Sweden and Finland, which helped originate pediatric gender medicine, have sharply restricted these interventions after systematic reviews found the supporting evidence weak. England's NHS ended routine puberty-blocker prescriptions after an independent national review described the evidence base as remarkably weak. The proposition asks you to treat as settled what the countries with the longest experience increasingly treat as unproven.
[C1.2] PRO's "pause button" framing fails on its own terms. Nearly every adolescent who starts blockers proceeds to cross-sex hormones—so blockers function less as a pause than as the first step on a conveyor. And the pathway's endpoint is permanent: infertility, impaired sexual function, compromised bone density, bodies changed for life. PRO warns that withholding treatment is irreversible; but treatment is irreversible too—irreversible by our own hand, on a healthy body, on the basis of a teenager's self-understanding.
[C1.3] That self-understanding is exactly the problem. Adolescence is a period of profound identity flux. Many young people with gender dysphoria—particularly the large share of current referrals with co-occurring depression, anxiety, or autism—have historically come to peace with their bodies without medical intervention. A fourteen-year-old cannot meaningfully consent to sacrificing fertility and sexual function; that is why we restrict their contracts, tattoos, and marriages.
[C1.4] Nor does "parents and clinicians agree" supply the safeguard PRO claims. Parents consent under duress, told that refusal risks their child's suicide—a framing that is both contested and coercive. And clinicians trained in the same affirmation protocol are not independent checks on one another; agreement inside a single model is not consensus.
[C1.5] The burden here belongs to PRO. Extraordinary, body-altering interventions demand extraordinary evidence. When that evidence is this contested and the pioneers are retreating, restraint is not cruelty—it is medicine's oldest command: first, do no harm.
