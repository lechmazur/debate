[P1.2] The first is indefensible, and the second is false.
[P1.3] Start with athletes. Regular-season games count. They shape standings, playoff seeding and careers. Flying a team across eight or nine time zones for a game that matters disrupts sleep, recovery and preparation.
[P1.4] The NFL tacitly admits this: teams playing in London commonly get a bye week afterward. Leagues do not hand out schedule relief for costs that are imaginary. Baseball shows the problem in a different form. Season-opening series in Tokyo or Seoul ask players to cross the Pacific twice in the most fragile weeks of the season, when arms are still building up.
[P1.5] That fatigue is not cosmetic. It means injury risk, worse performances and competitive distortion for teams that drew the long flight.
[P1.6] Now consider local fans, the people who actually sustain these leagues. [P1.7] That was not nostalgia. It was the core customer base saying the trade was bad.
[P1.8] Finally, the brand side shows diminishing returns. Global fans already watch every game through streaming and social media. Reach does not require stealing meaningful games from home markets. Preseason tours, offseason exhibitions and neutral-site friendlies build the same visibility without warping the standings or stripping communities of their games.
