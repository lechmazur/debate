[P1.1] The digital economy is currently choked by a fundamental conflict of interest: the companies that own the dominant app stores also compete within them. When a single entity owns the marketplace, writes the rulebook, and fields a competing product, the game is inevitably rigged. We must structurally separate app-store operators from their first-party development wings to eliminate self-preferencing and restore a fair market.
[P1.2] The harms of this dual role are systemic. Gatekeepers leverage their platform dominance to unfairly advantage their own applications. They do this by ranking their products higher in search results, pre-installing them as undeletable defaults, and reserving critical hardware and software APIs exclusively for themselves. Furthermore, they impose steep commission fees on competitors.
[P1.3] When a third-party developer has to surrender up to 30% of its revenue just to reach users, while the platform’s own rival app pays no such tax, competition is mathematically destroyed. The result is a stagnant ecosystem where the best product does not win; the product owned by the landlord wins.
[P1.4] Opponents often suggest we can manage this with behavioral remedies—like conduct rules, algorithmic audits, or fines. History proves behavioral remedies fail against tech monopolies. Fines are treated as a mere cost of doing business, and platform algorithms are infinitely opaque. A platform can subtly adjust a search parameter or alter a user-interface design to disadvantage a rival, keeping regulators tied up in years of litigation while the competitor simply bleeds out.
[P1.5] Structural separation is the only definitive solution because it removes the underlying incentive to cheat. If an app store operator no longer builds competing apps, its financial motivation completely flips. Instead of trying to crush third-party developers, the operator’s sole goal becomes making the platform as attractive, neutral, and functional for all developers as possible. Structural separation replaces a rigged monopoly with a genuinely competitive ecosystem, unleashing the innovation currently suffocated by gatekeepers.
