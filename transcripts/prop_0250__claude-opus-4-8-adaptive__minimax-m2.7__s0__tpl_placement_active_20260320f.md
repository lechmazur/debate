[P1.1] A democracy's legitimacy rests on a simple claim: that those who govern speak for the governed. But when only half the country shows up, that claim becomes a fiction. Elections decided by enthusiastic minorities don't measure the public will—they measure who had the time, the resources, and the anger to turn out. Mandatory voting fixes this at the root, and I'll defend it on three grounds: representativeness, equality, and the quality of politics itself.
[P1.2] First, representativeness. Voluntary turnout is not random—it is systematically skewed toward the wealthy, the older, the more educated. The people who skip elections are disproportionately the poor, the young, the precarious. So "voluntary" democracy hands disproportionate power to the already-powerful and writes the most vulnerable out of the equation.
[P1.3] Compelling participation pulls the whole electorate to the ballot box, so that outcomes reflect the actual population, not a self-selected slice of it. That is what the motion means by "representative outcomes."
[P1.4] Second, equality. We already accept enforceable civic duties—jury service, taxes, census participation, even compulsory schooling. Each imposes a modest burden in exchange for a functioning shared system. Voting is the most fundamental of these.
[P1.5] A proportional civil penalty—think a small fine, scaled to ability to pay, with conscientious-objection options like a blank ballot—is among the gentlest obligations the state imposes. Australia has done exactly this for roughly a century, sustaining turnout around ninety percent without tyranny, while remaining a robustly free society.
[P1.6] Third, political quality. When everyone votes, parties cannot win simply by mobilizing their base and suppressing the other side. They must appeal to the median citizen, the disengaged middle. That structurally rewards moderation and broad coalition-building over polarization and turnout warfare.
[P1.7] Compulsory voting drains the incentive for voter suppression entirely—there is nothing to suppress.
[P1.8] The motion specifies stable democracies, proportional penalties, and a representative aim. That is a calibrated, tested reform. The burden on CON is to show why a self-selected electorate better represents a nation than the whole nation does.
