[P1.1] The moment a university started writing checks to its quarterback from ticket and television money, the debate over whether college athletes are "really" workers ended. What remains is a narrower question: who writes the terms of that check? Right now the answer is one side. Schools and conferences settled an antitrust case, set the revenue cap, set roster limits, built a clearinghouse to approve or reject athletes' outside deals, and called the result "compliance."
[P1.2] Athletes were not at the table when any of it was decided. That is not a system; it is a set of rules imposed on people who had no vote.
[P1.3] Collective bargaining fixes the one defect every ad hoc arrangement shares: unilateral authority. A negotiated agreement puts revenue share, injury protection, medical coverage after eligibility ends, scheduling limits, transfer rules, and a grievance process into a single enforceable document that athletes helped write. Compare that to today's reality, where an athlete's rights depend on which state's NIL statute applies, which collective happens to fund his school, and whether an administrator decides a deal looks too much like pay-for-play. A walk-on cut when roster caps arrive mid-summer has no appeal.
[P1.4] A player whose deal gets flagged has no neutral arbiter. That is what "ad hoc" means in practice.
[P1.5] Here is the point that should decide the round: bargaining is also the only legally durable path for the schools themselves. Every salary cap in American professional sports survives antitrust scrutiny for exactly one reason: it was bargained with a union. A cap athletes never agreed to is a lawsuit waiting to happen, which is why the current settlement structure will be relitigated in a few years. The NFL and NBA are not chaos; they are stable precisely because owners and players sign a contract with an expiration date and a table to return to.
[P1.6] Fair pay, real protections, and legal stability all run through the same door. Compliance offices cannot deliver any of them. A negotiating table can.
