[P1.1] The current practice of prioritizing sponsored restaurants in food delivery and reservation apps isn't just a neutral business choice—it actively harms consumers, small local businesses, and the fabric of neighborhood food ecosystems, and reform is urgent.
[P1.2] Take a working parent in a midsized city looking for an affordable weeknight dinner. They open their delivery app, sort by "recommended," and see only chain restaurants and sponsored spots charging $15+ for a basic meal, while a well-loved family-owned Burmese restaurant with $8 dishes, local produce, and rave neighbor reviews is buried 12 pages down. This is no accident: apps like Uber Eats and DoorDash have publicly noted that sponsored placements make up the majority of top search results, and OpenTable’s default reservation sort prioritizes restaurants that pay for promotional slots over those with the most available tables or highest user ratings.
[P1.3] Critics will frame this as a free market choice, but these platforms are not neutral billboards. They are the primary discovery layer for most diners today, with most consumers reporting they use delivery apps to find new local restaurants. When they hide cheaper independent options behind paid walls, they are not just selling ad space: they are manipulating user choice to extract more fees from both restaurants and consumers. Independent spots already pay 20-30% commission on every order just to be listed; forcing them to pay extra for visibility to compete with chains with massive marketing budgets is a rigged game.
[P1.4] The end result is fewer affordable options for consumers, shuttered family-owned restaurants, and homogenized neighborhood food scenes where every block has the same three chain spots. That’s a cost no community should have to pay to line the pockets of app executives and corporate chains. We should require apps to clearly label sponsored spots, reserve top organic slots for independent and lower-cost options, and ban practices that push cheaper local restaurants so far down results that users cannot find them without deliberate effort.
