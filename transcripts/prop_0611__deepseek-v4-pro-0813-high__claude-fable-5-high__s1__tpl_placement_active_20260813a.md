[C1.4] Second, prioritization has a price. [C1.5] Third, the alternative is not a "tripwire versus nothing" choice. Rotational multinational presence, prepositioned stocks, exercised reinforcement plans, and long-range strike deliver deterrence by denial while preserving flexibility and avoiding the permanence that hands Moscow a mobilization pretext and its propagandists a gift. Notably, this posture has already worked: Russia, even at maximum aggression, has never touched NATO territory. The attacks on Georgia and Ukraine prove Russia preys on non-members — not that NATO's posture failed.
[C1.6] The choice is between resilient, flexible deterrence and expensive, brittle symbolism that shortens the fuse. NATO should defend its east — without building the tinderbox on its own border.
