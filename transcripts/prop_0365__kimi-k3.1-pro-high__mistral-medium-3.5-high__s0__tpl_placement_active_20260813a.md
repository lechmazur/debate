[C1.1] Cinemas are commercial enterprises, not public amenities, and their survival depends on maximizing revenue from each screen. Premium large-format showings deliver exactly that, generating the margins that sustain the entire operation. The proposition asks us to handicap this revenue stream whenever it leaves some moviegoers with inconvenient standard options—but inconvenience is not exclusion, and theaters cannot be obligated to run unprofitable showtimes to accommodate every preference.
[C1.2] PRO’s core claim—that standard screenings become "too few"—rests on an undefined and unworkable standard. Markets provide their own correction: if standard demand truly exceeded supply, theaters would add showtimes, because empty seats mean lost money. The persistent premium dominance demonstrates that customers value the enhanced experience enough to pay for it during peak times. Furthermore, blockbusters are spectacle-driven products, and premium formats are the superior delivery mechanism for that spectacle.
[C1.3] Artificially suppressing their scheduling to protect standard options would degrade the theatrical experience and accelerate the shift to home viewing that threatens the industry.
[C1.4] Forcing theaters to prioritize standard screenings over premium demand is not consumer protection; it is economic interference that would ultimately reduce both revenue and quality, harming the very ecosystem PRO claims to defend. The market, not arbitrary scheduling mandates, should determine screen allocation.
