[C1.1] Laws are not written for one idealized patient in a vacuum ([P1.2]); they govern real healthcare systems shaped by unequal care, exhausted families, and budget pressures. Once the state legalizes medical assistance in dying, continuing to live becomes a burden to justify, and no procedural checklist can stop a legal option to die from becoming a quiet pressure to die.
[C1.2] First, "strict safeguards" ([P1.7]) cannot detect the pressures that actually drive requests. Waiting periods, capacity tests, and second opinions only filter out overt incompetence or explicit threats; they are blind to structural and internalized coercion. Picture a fully competent grandmother with progressive illness watching her daughter lose wages and sleep to care for her because home nursing is unaffordable. When she calmly, repeatedly asks a doctor for death so she stops being a burden, she satisfies every safeguard on paper.
[C1.3] The state stamps caregiver guilt and social neglect as "autonomy."
[C1.4] Second, including "degenerative suffering" turns progressive disability into a lethal eligibility category. Unlike the final days of terminal illness, degenerative conditions span years or decades. Offering suicide prevention to able-bodied citizens in crisis while offering lethal prescriptions to people with degenerative conditions writes a deadly double standard into law: disabled, dependent lives are less worth saving. Moreover, "unbearable suffering" is subjective and frequently peaks during the initial shock of losing independence, yet assisted dying makes a transient, navigable despair permanent.
[C1.5] Finally, PRO poses a false choice between lethal prescriptions and forced agony ([P1.1], [P1.5]). Refusing ventilation protects bodily integrity against unwanted invasion; administering lethal drugs crosses a vital line by making death itself a medical treatment. Even when terminal symptoms resist ordinary relief, aggressive palliative medicine, including continuous sedation to unconsciousness, ends experienced agony without turning physicians into agents of death. Once healthcare treats killing as therapy, a cheap, immediate prescription inevitably crowds out the costly work of helping suffering people live.
