[P1.1] Pricing carbon at home while leaving borders open to untaxed, high-emission imports is not climate leadership—it is industrial suicide. As the European Union tightens its domestic emissions cap, European manufacturers pay steep costs to decarbonize. Without a carbon border adjustment, foreign competitors operating without serious carbon pricing can effortlessly undercut clean European producers. That does not reduce global emissions; it merely exports European factories, jobs, and smokestacks overseas.
[P1.2] Crucially, this adjustment must cover most imported industrial goods, not just a few raw commodities. If the EU taxes imported raw steel and aluminum but exempts downstream manufactured goods like machinery, appliances, or auto parts, it creates a perverse loophole. Foreign firms will simply forge the dirty steel at home and export the finished product into Europe untouched, shifting carbon leakage one step down the supply chain. Only a broad industrial scope protects the entire manufacturing value chain.
[P1.3] Beyond self-defense, a broad border adjustment turns the European single market into a catalyst for global decarbonization. Foreign governments hate watching their exporters pay climate levies into the EU treasury. Because a border adjustment credits carbon prices already paid in the country of origin, trading partners gain a powerful financial incentive to establish their own domestic carbon pricing—keeping the revenue locally while raising global climate standards.
[P1.4] Finally, the EU must hold firm even if major trading partners retaliate. Capitulating to tariff threats sets a fatal precedent: that foreign capitals hold a permanent veto over internal European climate law. Moreover, the risks are fundamentally asymmetrical. Trade retaliation is a manageable, targeted friction against specific export sectors—sectors Brussels can directly compensate using the very revenues collected by the border levy itself.
[P1.5] By contrast, abandoning the border adjustment guarantees the permanent hollowing-out of Europe’s industrial base and the collapse of its climate architecture. Europe must price carbon at the border, comprehensively and without apology.
