[P1.1] Governments already pick winners in protein. Right now they pay billions to make climate change worse. Shifting that money is not industrial meddling; it is stopping industrial self-sabotage.
[P1.2] Conventional animal agriculture occupies most agricultural land yet delivers a minority of calories and protein, while driving methane, deforestation, manure-driven water pollution, and biodiversity loss. Precision fermentation makes the same molecules — whey, casein, egg white, heme — using microbes fed on sugars, not a cow that must be kept alive for years. That cuts land use by an order of magnitude, slashes emissions, and removes the constant methane belch and slurry lagoon. When governments subsidize feed crops, grazing payments, and cheap manure disposal, they are financing externalities they then pay again to clean up.
[P1.3] Freeing even a fraction of pasture allows reforestation and rewilding that draws down far more carbon than feed additives ever could.
[P1.4] Subsidy for subsidy, low-emissions protein is a better public investment. A dollar that props up a feedlot locks in antibiotic overuse, zoonotic risk, and drought vulnerability. The same dollar put toward fermentation infrastructure buys resilient, weather-independent production, domestic food security, and patentable biomanufacturing capacity. This is budget-neutral reallocation, not new spending.
[P1.5] We already did this with energy: feed-in tariffs made solar cheap. Protein needs the same push. Companies can already brew real dairy proteins without cows, but they cannot scale against an incumbent that gets land, water, and insurance subsidized.
[P1.6] This shift does not abandon rural communities. Most animal ag payments flow to large integrators and feed-grain giants, not small farmers. Reallocating toward fermentation plants that buy biomass feedstocks, plus payments for freed land to be restored for carbon and habitat, keeps income in rural areas while finally paying for outcomes voters actually want: less warming, cleaner water, secure food.
