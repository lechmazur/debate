[P1.3] Traditional "animal welfare" laws cannot solve this. Only legal personhood—moving these beings from "things" to "persons"—creates an enforceable shield against captivity and exploitation, allowing courts to protect their bodily liberty directly.
[P1.4] This reform is both bounded and practical. Recognizing non-human personhood does not mean giving dolphins the right to vote, just as personhood does not give a human infant the right to drive. Rights are tailored to the capacities of the holder, and transitions for existing captives are safely managed through accredited sanctuaries rather than reckless release into the wild. For autonomous, socially complex beings, justice requires a basic negative right: freedom from imprisonment and commercial exploitation.
[P1.5] When a mind is complex enough to suffer the agony of enslavement, the law must stop treating it as an object.
