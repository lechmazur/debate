[C1.1] The proposition protects a price, not the habit. The real mass-cultural threat is not that a Friday ticket costs more than a Tuesday one; it is that theaters, squeezed by thin margins, raise flat prices, cut showtimes, close locations, or push customers toward streaming. Dynamic pricing for standard seats is the tool that lets cinema stay common by making each seat price work to fill the house.
[C1.2] Cinema is a fixed-capacity, perishable good. A 7 p.m. Friday showing of a tentpole may sell out; a 2 p.m. Wednesday showing may be nearly empty.
[C1.3] If standard seats must carry one blunt price, the theater either overcharges the empty show or undercharges the full one. Overcharging empties the lobby; undercharging creates shortages, sellouts, and scalping, and it forces the average ticket price upward to cover costs. Aggressive variation—higher when demand is intense, much lower when demand is weak—shifts people into the cheap seats, rewards flexibility, and keeps more screens open.
[C1.4] PRO says habits need legibility. True, but legibility is not sameness. Audiences already understand matinee, weekend, discount Tuesday, and IMAX pricing. A dynamic system can be public and rule-bound: demand tiers, posted caps, a cheap standard option at every performance, and adjacent lower-priced showtimes.
[C1.5] That is not mysterious personalization; it is the same logic as happy hours, off-peak transit fares, and early-bird tickets. The habit survives when people can find a price they can say yes to.
[C1.6] The cultural harm PRO fears—opening night becoming elite—already happens whenever a popular film sells out at an artificially low price. Then access goes to whoever refreshes fastest, camps out, or resells. Letting the theater price peak demand directly can fund cheaper access everywhere else: student mornings, senior afternoons, small-town screens, and the concessions stand that makes a night out affordable. If we want cinema as a mass habit, we should price standard seats aggressively enough to keep theaters alive and seats filled.
