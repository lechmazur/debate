[P1.3] Companies would have to place a certified counselor, by phone or video, in the path before the reveal, with the customer free to proceed after a short conversation or to decline. [P1.4] Three reasons this is the right rule.

A counselor translates a probabilistic signal into a plan: confirmatory testing, relatives who should be informed, and insurance gaps that the federal nondiscrimination law does not cover.
[P1.6] Second, this is already the standard everywhere else. The same BRCA result ordered through a physician comes with counseling. Direct-to-consumer sales should not be the loophole where identical medical information arrives stripped of medical care.
[P1.7] Third, it is cheap and feasible. Telehealth counseling exists at scale, companies already tier their products, and the cost lands on the high-impact tier only.
[P1.8] The question is simple: should the most consequential medical information a person ever receives come with less support than a prescription refill?
