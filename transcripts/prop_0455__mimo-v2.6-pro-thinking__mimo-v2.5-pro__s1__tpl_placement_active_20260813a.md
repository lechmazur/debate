[P1.1] Flaring and venting above a strict threshold is not energy production; it is the deliberate waste of a product that is already being extracted. A country that lets methane burn uselessly into the sky while claiming to face a supply crisis is arguing against itself.
[P1.2] Start with the physics. Methane is a far more potent greenhouse gas than carbon dioxide over the near term—on the order of dozens of times stronger on a twenty-year horizon. That matters because near-term warming is exactly what determines whether we cross thresholds we cannot cross back: ice sheet collapse, permafrost thaw that releases yet more methane, and the burst of feedback loops that outlast any political cycle. A ton of CO2 persists for centuries; methane's punch is concentrated in the decades when it hurts most.
[P1.3] Routine flaring is worse than venting alone, because incomplete combustion releases unburned methane alongside the CO2 produced in the flame. This is the cheapest, highest-yield climate abatement available to any government, precisely because it destroys no useful output.
[P1.4] Second, the motion's tradeoff is largely illusory. Routine flaring and leakage means gas that is already flowing to the wellhead and simply escaping. Capture it and it becomes saleable fuel—domestic supply, not lost supply. Equipment to separate, compress, and route associated gas is mature and widely deployed; when operators say it "can't" be captured, what they usually mean is that flaring is slightly cheaper than the pipeline or reinjection that would handle it.
[P1.5] A threshold rule prices that externality correctly and shifts investment toward the capture that adds to supply.
[P1.6] Third, the motion is narrow by construction. "Routine" flaring excludes emergency shutdowns and safety releases. "Strict thresholds" permit small, transient emissions where capture is genuinely uneconomic. What remains is large-scale, avoidable waste practiced because it is free.
[P1.7] A short-term supply dent is a real cost—but compare it to the near-term warming this single policy defers. Governments should not subsidize atmospheric waste with the public's climate.
