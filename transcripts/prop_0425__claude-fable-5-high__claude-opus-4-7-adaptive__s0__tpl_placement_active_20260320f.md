[P1.1] The question is not whether digital tools have any value. It is which deserves the marginal dollar first, and on that question the answer is decisive: fund the rooms, the clubs, and the rituals before the apps.
[P1.2] Start with what the problem actually is. The defining crisis on campuses today is loneliness and disconnection, and loneliness is, by definition, a deficit of real relationships. Students do not lack screens; the average undergraduate already spends hours a day on platforms engineered by the world's best attention engineers. A university-procured wellness app enters that environment as the least compelling icon on the phone.
[P1.3] Anyone who has watched a campus roll out such a platform knows the pattern: a launch email, a brief spike, then a graveyard of unused logins while the license fee renews annually. Buying another digital layer to cure digital-age isolation is treating dehydration with salt water.
[P1.4] In-person infrastructure attacks the cause directly. Clubs create repeated, low-stakes contact, which is precisely how friendships form. Common spaces generate the unplanned collisions no app can schedule. Recurring events build the weekly rhythms that turn strangers into a community.
[P1.5] These ties are what keep students enrolled when courses get hard, what catch warning signs of crisis before any mood-tracking dashboard would, and what alumni remember decades later. Belonging predicts retention, and retention is the university's financial and moral bottom line.
[P1.6] Notice also where the money goes. Funding a club or staffing a lounge keeps dollars circulating among students and campus staff. Platform contracts ship tuition money to outside vendors for products that students rarely asked for and barely use.
[P1.7] So the test for this debate is simple: which investment produces durable human connection per dollar? Digital platforms produce dashboards and engagement metrics. In-person investment produces friendships, mentors, and reasons to stay. Universities should secure the foundation before paying for a digital veneer on top of it.
