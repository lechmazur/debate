The right test is counterfactual: if today’s teens faced the same family conflict, academic pressure, and economic uncertainty, but without algorithmic platforms occupying hours of daily life, would the crisis be as severe? Probably not.
[P1.2] Social media is not one exposure but a delivery system for several harms at once. It turns comparison from an occasional schoolyard experience into a continuous ranking against curated bodies, lifestyles, and popularity metrics. It lets exclusion and humiliation follow teenagers home, makes harassment scalable and permanent, and supplies graphic or self-destructive content precisely when a user is vulnerable. Notifications and endless feeds displace sleep, exercise, face-to-face friendship, and undistracted recovery.
[P1.3] These effects reinforce one another: poor sleep weakens emotional regulation; weakened regulation invites compulsive scrolling; scrolling provides more comparison and less sleep.
[P1.4] The causal pattern is especially persuasive because the mechanism matches adolescent development. Platforms monetize exactly those sensitivities through public counts, streaks, recommendations, and intermittent rewards. This is not neutral communication technology accidentally revealing preexisting sadness.
[P1.5] It is an environment engineered to capture attention by intensifying emotionally charged social feedback.
[P1.6] CON will point out that distressed teens may use social media more.
