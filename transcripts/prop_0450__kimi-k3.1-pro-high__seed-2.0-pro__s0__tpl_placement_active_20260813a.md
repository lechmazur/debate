[P1.1] Japan and South Korea should restart safe reactors and expand nuclear capacity because their central energy problem is not a shortage of climate ambitions. It is dependence on imported fuel, exposed supply chains, and power systems that must decarbonize without failing industry or households.
[P1.2] Both countries are densely populated, resource-poor manufacturing economies. When gas or coal prices surge, they cannot simply switch to a domestic pipeline or vast hinterland. Their import bills rise, electricity prices follow, and strategic vulnerability deepens. Nuclear fuel is different: it is compact, stockpiled, and sourced through diversified markets.
[P1.3] A reactor therefore provides more than electricity; it provides insurance against blockades, shipping disruption, and the next fossil-fuel shock.
[P1.4] Restarts are the fastest place to begin. Existing plants already have sites, grid connections, and operating institutions. Keeping sound reactors offline while buying more liquefied natural gas is not caution neutralized; it is a deliberate choice to accept avoidable emissions and exposure. Expansion through life extensions and well-governed new capacity then supplies firm, low-carbon power as transport, heating, data centers, and industry electrify.
[P1.5] This is not nuclear instead of renewables. Japan and South Korea need much more solar and wind, but limited land, variable weather, and seasonal demand make an electricity system built around firm clean power more resilient. Nuclear generation runs when winter demand rises and renewable output falls. Counting only a wind farm’s construction cost while ignoring storage, transmission, backup, and curtailment compares components with a system.
[P1.6] Fukushima demands humility, not permanent technological surrender. Restarts must require independent regulation, upgraded safeguards, transparent inspections, credible evacuation planning, and secure waste management. But every energy source carries risks; fossil dependence carries certain pollution and recurring geopolitical exposure. The honest comparison is not nuclear versus a risk-free ideal.
[P1.7] The opposition must show that Japan and South Korea can achieve equivalent reliability, security, and deep decarbonization faster without nuclear. Until then, rejecting safe restarts and disciplined expansion sacrifices both objectives this motion names.
