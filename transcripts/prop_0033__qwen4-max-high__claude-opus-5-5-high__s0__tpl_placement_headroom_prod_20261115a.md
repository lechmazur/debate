[P1.1] Democracy runs on reasons that can be answered. When a campaign runs a television ad, opponents and reporters can inspect it and reply. Behavioral micro-targeting built on inferred psychological profiles destroys that possibility: a different emotional pitch to each psychological segment, delivered privately, so the claim made to the frightened voter is never available to the skeptical one.
[P1.2] Picture two neighbors. One is scored as anxious and gets an ad warning that her street is next. The other is scored as cynical and low-trust and gets an ad suggesting both parties are identical and voting is pointless. Same campaign, opposite strategies, zero public record.
[P1.3] No journalist can fact-check a message shown to two hundred people selected for a vulnerability nobody disclosed.
[P1.4] That word — inferred — is the hinge. These profiles aren't stated preferences a voter chose to share. They're guesses extracted from clicks, dwell time, and purchases, frequently wrong, and impossible to contest. You cannot correct a label you don't know you carry.
[P1.5] A campaign is not responding to your expressed concerns; it is betting on which emotional lever moves you without your knowledge. That is not persuasion, it is manipulation with a marketing budget.
[P1.6] The prohibition is narrow, and my opponent must engage it as written. Campaigns keep issue ads, broadcast, geography, language, canvassing, rallies, and targeting on what people actually say they care about. What they lose is the covert inference-and-exploit layer. Every message they can still send is one they could defend in public.
[P1.7] So the clash is simple: does a campaign's efficiency at privately exploiting psychological weakness outweigh voters' ability to see and answer the case being made to them? If these techniques barely work, banning them costs nothing. If they work, that is precisely the danger — an electorate convinced by reasons it was never permitted to hear.

[C1.5] Second, the ban redistributes power toward those who already hold it. Broadcast advertising rewards big budgets and name recognition.
