[P1.1] A system that cancels your health coverage the moment you're too sick to work isn't insurance; it's a bet you lose exactly when you need it most. That defect in employer-based coverage is structural, not fixable by tweaks. Single-payer wins this comparison on security, efficiency, and freedom.
[P1.2] Security first. Tying the medical floor to job status means coverage vanishes at the worst possible moments: layoffs, recessions, or illness itself. When the pandemic hit, millions lost their jobs and their families' coverage in the same month. And cruelest of all: a cancer patient who can no longer work loses the very insurance paying for her treatment.
[P1.3] Single-payer decouples survival from employment. The floor stays under you whatever happens to your job.
[P1.4] Efficiency next. Insurance is math — it spreads risk, and it works best across the largest pool. Employer systems fragment the population into thousands of small pools skewed by industry, age, and firm size, then pile on administrative waste: every hospital billing dozens of insurers, every insurer staffing departments to deny claims. Single-payer consolidates one pool and one rulebook, which is why Canada's system spends far less per person on administration.
[P1.5] A single payer also has real leverage over drug and hospital prices — the main driver of runaway costs — where thousands of small insurers have none.
[P1.6] Finally, freedom. Job lock is real: workers stay in jobs that waste their talents because leaving risks a child's asthma care. Would-be entrepreneurs never start their businesses. Firms competing internationally carry health costs their rivals' governments absorb.
[P1.7] Single-payer makes labor markets more dynamic, not less.
[P1.8] The motion concerns basic coverage — a floor, like fire protection or primary schooling. Floors belong to everyone, not to whoever HR happens to cover this quarter. Employer insurance rents out the floor with a termination clause; single-payer simply guarantees it. Any defense of the status quo must explain why your access to a doctor should depend on your boss.
