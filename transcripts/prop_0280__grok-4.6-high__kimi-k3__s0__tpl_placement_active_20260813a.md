[P1.1] Wedding culture no longer asks couples to mark a commitment. It asks them to stage a production. The white dress, the swollen guest list, the venue that costs more than a used car, the photographer, the favors nobody keeps: this is not inherited meaning so much as a commercial script. Couples should feel little obligation to follow it.
[P1.2] The expense is not a side effect. It is the product. Families and vendors treat a wedding as a once-in-a-lifetime spectacle that must look costly to count. People without inherited wealth then begin a marriage already carrying debt, or they empty savings that could have been a home, an emergency fund, or simply breathing room.
[P1.3] Starting a life together underwater is not romance. It is a transfer of money from the couple to an industry that profits from their fear of looking cheap.
[P1.4] The performance is worse because it pretends to be meaning. Guests are ranked. Speeches are written for applause. The couple spends the day being arranged for photographs rather than being present with each other.
[P1.5] Much of what people defend as “tradition” is recent marketing: the diamond, the destination, the matching aesthetic. When the day is built for the feed and the relatives, the couple becomes the content, not the reason.
[P1.6] Obligation is the word that matters. Nobody is attacking joy, ritual, or a party people actually want. The claim is narrower. Given how costly and staged the default has become, couples should not feel they owe that default to parents, culture, or an imagined audience.
[P1.7] A courthouse, a backyard, twenty people and a potluck are not lesser marriages. They are refusals to confuse love with theater.
[P1.8] If a couple freely wants the spectacle and can pay without harm, that is preference. Preference is not duty. A marriage is judged by how it is lived, not by how well it impersonated a magazine.

[C1.6] Weddings also knit two families and honor parents. Declare the couple the only stakeholder and you abolish what makes a wedding more than a party.

[C1.8] A marriage is judged by how it is lived. That is exactly why its first promise should be made the old way: out loud, in front of everyone, because something is owed.
