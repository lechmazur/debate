[P1.1] The comparison here is not welfare against zero cost. It is a broad, sustained welfare gain against a productivity cost that is largely imagined — and largely recoverable.
[P1.2] Knowledge work rewards judgment, synthesis, and sustained attention. Those capacities degrade under chronic partial availability. The engineer answering Slack at 11pm after a full day of meetings does shallower work than the same engineer fresh at 9am. Research on fatigue, sleep restriction, and cognitive load converges on this point.
[P1.3] So a large share of the supposed productivity loss was never output to begin with: it is notification volume, meeting spillover, and rework mistaken for production.
[P1.4] The welfare gain, by contrast, is concrete and cumulative. An eroded boundary produces sleep loss, exhaustion, and a low-grade anxiety tethered to the phone itself. The burden lands hardest on caregivers, juniors with little bargaining power, and anyone whose manager signals expectations through late-night messages. A right to disconnect turns an unspoken demand into an explicit, enforceable boundary: the evening is the worker's unless the employer will say so, and pay for it.
[P1.5] The claim that firms would fix this themselves ignores collective action. No single company will credibly restrict availability while its competitors don't. Only a legal norm makes the rule stick — and the law does not ban emergencies, it prices them. Where such rules have been adopted, knowledge sectors did not see the predicted collapse; firms adapted with better staffing and clearer escalation paths.
[P1.6] Cross-time-zone teams and genuine emergencies are edge cases, and well-drafted laws handle them with on-call agreements and compensation. Mitigable friction is not an argument against durable welfare.
[P1.7] The motion asks whether worker welfare improves more than productivity is damaged. Welfare is broad, compounding, and real; the productivity cost is narrow, offsetting, and mostly the loss of cheap excess availability. That asymmetry decides the question.
