[P1.1] The case for abolition does not rest on sympathy for terrorists or mass murderers. It rests on what the death penalty does to the state that wields it—and on the risk that no system can eliminate.
[P1.2] Start with irreversibility. Every justice system run by human beings produces wrongful convictions. Eyewitness testimony is unreliable. Confessions can be coerced.
[P1.3] Forensic labs make errors. When the sentence is life imprisonment, a later finding of innocence can at least partially repair the harm. When the sentence is death, it cannot. This is not a theoretical risk.
[P1.4] DNA exonerations have freed people from death row in multiple countries. The more serious the crime, the greater the public pressure for a conviction, and the higher the risk that pressure overrides evidence. Terrorism cases are especially vulnerable: political urgency, public fear, and minority scapegoating create exactly the conditions most likely to produce wrongful conviction.
[P1.5] Then consider what execution grants the state. The power to kill a citizen in custody is the most absolute power any government holds. A state that claims the authority to decide who deserves to die has crossed a line that cannot be safely drawn. Once the death penalty exists for the "worst of the worst," the definition of worst expands.
[P1.6] Categories blur. Political crimes creep in. The history of capital punishment is largely a history of that expansion.
[P1.7] For terrorism specifically, execution is not just pointless but counterproductive. The terrorist wants a spectacle. A state execution is a spectacle—staged, solemn, and guaranteed to generate exactly the martyrdom and propaganda the terrorist sought. Life without parole denies that.
[P1.8] It says: you are not important enough for the state to kill you.
[P1.9] The death penalty does not uniquely deter crimes whose perpetrators often plan to die. It does irreversible harm when the system errs. And it hands the state a power no institution should hold. Abolition is not leniency.
[P1.10] It is a boundary.
