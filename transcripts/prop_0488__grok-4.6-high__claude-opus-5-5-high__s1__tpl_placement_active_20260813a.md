[P1.1] The world's food system now depends on a handful of companies for the seeds in the ground and the chemicals that keep them alive. That is a chokepoint, not a market. Resilience is redundancy: independent germplasm, competing breeding strategies, different chemical modes of action, and firms that can fail without taking the harvest with them. Concentration does the opposite.
[P1.2] Mega-mergers folded seed houses and pesticide makers onto the same boards. Farmers buy a package: a patented variety they cannot legally save, engineered for one company's herbicide, sold through one channel. On-farm genetic diversity collapses toward whatever the patent office will fund. When weed resistance spreads, a factory goes down, or sanctions freeze an active ingredient, the shock is no longer a firm problem.
[P1.3] It is a harvest problem.
[P1.4] Innovation under monopoly is not diversity. Research follows the royalty. Traits that lock in chemical sales beat drought-tolerant landraces, neglected crops, and farmer-led breeding. Public and regional programs starve against bundled intellectual property, data, and shelf space.
[P1.5] Price power then strips farmers of the surplus they would spend on adaptation, insurance, and new varieties.
[P1.6] Break-ups attack the structure, not the press release. Split seed from chemistry. Split the remaining giants so no firm dictates both the genome and the spray tank. Competition multiplies independent research, revives public breeding, and recreates suppliers who do not share one factory, one patent thicket, or one lobby.
[P1.7] Food is more systemic than any bank that was ever too big to fail. Leaving this intact is a wager that the next drought, pest wave, or supply shock will be polite. Resilience is not politeness. It is the ability to lose one company and still eat.
