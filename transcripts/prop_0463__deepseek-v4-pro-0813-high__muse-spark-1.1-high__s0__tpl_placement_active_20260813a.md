[P1.1] The core question is not whether national loyalty has any value, but what happens when it collides with the basic welfare of people outside the border. In that collision, national loyalty is the weaker moral claim because its force depends on membership, while duties to universal human welfare depend on urgent human need.
[P1.2] Moral claims gain weight from the interests they protect. A duty to prevent starvation, epidemic disease, or the destruction of someone’s home protects interests that are basic to any person. National loyalty protects a relationship to a particular political community. Shared identity, history, and citizenship can be meaningful, but none of them is as basic as avoiding severe suffering.
[P1.3] When the two point in different directions, the claim tied to life, health, and subsistence should carry more weight.
[P1.4] Nationality is also morally arbitrary. No one chooses where they are born, and borders are the product of historical accident, conquest, and political bargaining. Those facts may create legal obligations, but they do not create a stronger moral reason to prefer a co-national’s lesser interest over a foreigner’s survival. If two people need the same scarce medicine, the difference between their passports does not justify letting one die so the other can receive a smaller benefit.
[P1.5] This is not an abstract problem. Vaccine nationalism showed the pattern clearly: wealthy states secured doses for their own low-risk populations while high-risk people in poorer states waited. National loyalty said, “our people first.” Universal welfare said, “protect those most likely to die.” The first claim could justify preventable death; the second directly opposed it. Duties to universal human welfare were stronger precisely because they were not hostage to borders.
[P1.6] Some defenders say national solidarity builds trust and makes welfare institutions possible. That gives national loyalty only instrumental value. If loyalty is justified because it serves human welfare, then it cannot override that same welfare when the two conflict. A subordinate claim is, by definition, the weaker moral claim.
