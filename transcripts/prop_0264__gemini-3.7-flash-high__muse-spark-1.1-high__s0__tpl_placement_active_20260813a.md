[P1.1] In virtually every high-stakes profession where errors can ruin lives—from surgery to civil engineering—practitioners carry personal professional liability insurance. Policing should be no exception. Today, when an officer commits severe misconduct, the financial burden does not fall on the perpetrator; it falls on municipal taxpayers. Cities routinely pay millions in settlements out of general revenue funds, draining budgets for schools, infrastructure, and public health to cover police wrongdoing.
[P1.2] Taxpayers have no operational control over officer behavior, yet they serve as an unconditional indemnity fund for bad policing.
[P1.3] Mandating personal liability insurance fixes this broken incentive structure through two vital mechanisms.
[P1.4] First, it establishes basic financial fairness. Misconduct payouts should be absorbed by the risk pool of the profession, not by innocent residents who are often the very communities harmed by over-policing. While municipalities can provide standard allowances to help cover baseline premiums for all officers, the marginal financial liability for high-risk behavior must belong to the individual.
[P1.5] Second, and most importantly, it creates genuine accountability. Internal affairs divisions, union contracts, and departmental politics frequently shield repeat offenders from meaningful discipline or termination. Actuarial risk assessment, however, does not care about the "blue wall of silence." Insurers evaluate claims, excessive force patterns, and disciplinary records objectively.
[P1.6] Safe, conscientious officers will enjoy low, stable rates, while reckless officers will see their premiums climb steeply.
[P1.7] Crucially, an officer who repeatedly abuses authority will eventually become uninsurable. When an insurer refuses to underwrite someone due to extreme risk, that officer can no longer remain on the street. This accomplishes what internal discipline so often fails to do: it systematically prices toxic actors out of the profession before their next catastrophic violation.
[P1.8] Personal liability insurance replaces taxpayer-funded impunity with individual accountability. It protects public coffers, rewards professional officers, and ensures that the true cost of misconduct stays where it belongs.
