[P1.1] Essential insurance is not a consumer good. It is the mechanism we built to stop bad luck from destroying someone's life.
[P1.2] Actuarial fairness sounds neutral on paper: charge the diabetic more, charge the person born with congenital heart disease more, charge the single parent working night shifts more. But these are not reckless choices. These are luck. When you price someone out of insulin coverage because their genes gave them type 1 diabetes, you are not being fair.
[P1.3] You are telling people that if they lost the birth lottery, they do not get to participate in society on equal terms.
[P1.4] Social solidarity is not a vague moral platitude. It is the unwritten deal that makes insurance work at all. If only sick people buy insurance, the entire system collapses. Everyone pays in when they are well, so you get coverage when you are not.
[P1.5] Actuarial risk pricing actively unravels that deal. It lets healthy people opt out of paying for anyone else, and leaves the sick trapped paying impossible, unpayable rates for coverage they cannot survive without.
[P1.6] This is not a radical proposal. We already accept the principle everywhere it matters. We do not charge people who live in wildfire zones ten times more for home insurance, because we decided no one should lose their home just for where they were born or could afford to live. We ban car insurance rating on zip codes that correlate with race, not because accidents are not more common there, but because we will not punish people for systemic disadvantage.
[P1.7] This is not about abolishing risk adjustment entirely. It is about setting a hard legal floor. No one can be priced so far out of essential coverage they cannot access it, no matter what their statistical risk is. If that means healthy people pay a tiny bit more, that is the very small cost of living in a society where bad luck does not equal a death sentence.
