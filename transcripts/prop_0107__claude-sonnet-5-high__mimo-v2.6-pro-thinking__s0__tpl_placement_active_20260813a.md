[P1.1] The proposition is narrow and that narrowness is exactly where PRO wins. We are not debating whether gender-affirming care should be handed out on demand to anyone who asks. We are debating outcomes for adolescents whose dysphoria is persistent, and who have gone through careful clinical screening. That framing already builds in the caution critics want, and it is the population where the evidence most clearly favors intervention.
[P1.2] Start with the mechanism. Gender dysphoria in adolescents with a long, consistent, insistent history is not a passing mood; it is a chronic mismatch between body and identity that produces measurable psychological harm: elevated depression, anxiety, self-harm, and suicidality. Talk therapy alone does not resolve that mismatch, because the mismatch is physical. Puberty blockers and, later, hormones are the only interventions that directly target the proximate cause of the distress rather than merely coping with its symptoms.
[P1.3] That is why clinicians who work with this population describe visible relief once physical development starts aligning with identity: sleep improves, school engagement improves, suicidal ideation drops.
[P1.4] Now weigh the two risks the resolution asks us to compare. On one side: chronic, cumulative psychiatric harm from years of unaddressed dysphoria during the most identity-forming period of a person's life. On the other: regret, which in populations that went through genuine multi-year screening protocols is a small-minority outcome, not the norm. Puberty blockers in particular are the most reversible tool in this toolkit; they function as a pause button, buying years of additional observation before anyone touches an irreversible step.
[P1.5] That is the opposite of a rushed pipeline — it is a staged, revisable process that lets true persistence separate itself from adolescent uncertainty over time.
[P1.6] So the actual comparison is: a well-documented, severe, chronic harm on one side against a smaller-probability, often less severe harm on the other, in a population specifically selected because their dysphoria has already proven durable. That comparison is the case CON has to beat, not a strawman about unscreened, impulsive treatment.
