[P1.1] Social media platforms are no longer digital novelties; they are the essential infrastructure of modern public life. They function as the town square, the primary storefront for small businesses, and the indispensable conduit for political speech, journalism, and civic organizing. Yet today, a handful of dominant platforms exercise unilateral authority to instantly sever users from this sphere without warning, specific explanation, or any meaningful recourse. Leaving this power unchecked allows corporate arbitrariness to dictate who may participate in society.
[P1.2] The current ecosystem is governed by algorithmic error and black-box moderation. Automated filters routinely misinterpret context, sweeping up satire, political dissent, and investigative reporting in broad enforcement purges. Because immense network effects leave users with nowhere else to go, platforms face zero competitive pressure to respect user rights. They operate as unaccountable judges and executioners, relying on opaque enforcement because handling appeals or explaining bans cuts into profit margins.
[P1.3] The result is a profound chilling effect on lawful speech and devastating disruptions to livelihoods.
[P1.4] A legal mandate for due process—requiring advance notice of alleged infractions, explicit written reasons, and a viable appeal mechanism before imposing a suspension or ban—is the only meaningful remedy. Due process does not strip platforms of the right to enforce legitimate rules; it simply introduces basic procedural hygiene. Requiring platforms to state the specific standard violated and hear a defense before exile ensures that innocent users are not silenced by faulty algorithms or ideological whims.
[P1.5] When access to digital networks determines one’s ability to earn a living, report the news, and exercise fundamental speech rights, due process cannot remain an optional corporate luxury. Requiring notice, reasons, and appeal before platform exclusion restores basic justice to the modern public square.
