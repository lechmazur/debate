[P1.1] Privacy-by-design for minors is not a speech code. It is a default architecture: private accounts, no behavioral advertising, no location broadcasting, no open DMs from strangers. The proposition asks government to make large platforms start minors from that safer baseline instead of the current one, which treats a child's data and audience as engagement fuel.
[P1.2] The harm reduction is measurable because defaults are the most powerful regulator of online behavior. Minors rarely hunt through settings, and platforms bury them behind dark patterns; parents cannot supervise every device. When a 13-year-old joins a platform, the status quo decides whether her profile is public to anyone, whether advertisers build a dossier of her insecurities, and whether recommendation engines can amplify whatever keeps her scrolling. Flip the defaults and you reduce the measurable inputs of harm: fewer stranger contacts, less data brokerage, less algorithmic profiling, and clearer safety reporting.
[P1.3] Platforms already instrument these flows; a mandate turns private A/B tests into public accountability.
[P1.4] That protection does not require censorship. It does not delete a single lawful post, block a viewpoint, or silence a teenager. It changes who may collect, target, and see—not what may be said. A minor can still publish, organize, join support communities, and opt into a public account after a real choice.
[P1.5] What disappears is the platform's silent extraction and exposure.
[P1.6] The real objection will be age assurance, not censorship. But establishing that an account belongs to a minor can rely on existing device, app-store, and self-declared signals rather than a speech-chilling ID regime. And even imperfect age signals do not censor; they trigger a stricter data and audience setting, nothing more.
[P1.7] The choice is between a rigged default that harvests children and a neutral default that lets them participate without being preyed upon. That is measurable harm reduction with no significant censorship.
