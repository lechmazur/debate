[P1.1] Women-only ride-hail options exist because a late-night car with a stranger is not a neutral marketplace. For many women it is a concentrated risk of harassment or assault that mixed matching does not solve. Platforms already advertise panic buttons, trip-sharing, and driver screening because the product fails some riders on trust before it fails them on price.
[P1.2] That failure is not symmetric. The fear is of being trapped with a male stranger who controls the vehicle, the route, and whether the car stops. Women-only pairing cuts that pairing directly. It will not erase every crime, but it shrinks the set of rides women themselves treat as unacceptably risky.
[P1.3] Trust here is practical: whether a nurse finishing a shift requests a car or calls a friend; whether a student goes home alone. Those forgone trips are a real cost, paid mostly by women.
[P1.4] Operational complexity is real and overstated. Ride-hail already matches on vehicle type, pooling, destination, ratings, and surge. An opt-in gender filter is another attribute, not a new industry. Matching inefficiency is likewise already the business model.
[P1.5] People wait longer for a larger car or a reserved pickup. They will wait longer for a safer pairing if they judge the trade worthwhile. That is revealed preference, not system collapse.
[P1.6] The proposition does not demand zero wait or perfect utilization. It demands that safety and trust gains justify the friction. They do, because sexual violence and the anticipatory restriction of movement are high-stakes harms, while extra matching time is a low-stakes, opt-in cost borne by people who value the option. A few extra minutes of wait is cheap compared with a night of avoided travel or a single assault.
[P1.7] Background checks and cameras should continue; they do not replace the pairing women actually request when they can get it. If the product can segment for luxury, it can segment for not being afraid.

[C1.6] That is the deeper cost: displacement. Engineering hours, driver incentives, and legal risk spent on segmenting the fleet are hours not spent on measures that cover every ride: PIN-matched pickups, audio recording, live trip monitoring, and fast driver deactivation. Those protect the woman on the ninety percent of trips the filter cannot serve, and they protect drivers too.
[C1.7] The motion demands improved safety, not reduced anxiety. PRO must show cars arrive, identities hold, and the trade beats universal protections. A menu option that mostly times out does not clear that bar.
