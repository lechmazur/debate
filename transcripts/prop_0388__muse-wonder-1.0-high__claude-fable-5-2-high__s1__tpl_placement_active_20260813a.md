[P1.1] A free game that a thirteen-year-old can enter in seconds but cannot safely spend inside is not free. It is a storefront built around a child and disguised as play.
[P1.2] Gambling-like monetization is not ordinary selling. Loot boxes, gacha pulls, and randomized card packs charge money for chance: pay, watch the reveal spin, miss the rare item, and feel the pull to try again before a limited-time banner disappears. That loop borrows the mechanics of gambling — variable rewards, near-misses, scarcity, and rapid repetition — while avoiding the age gates, spending controls, and warnings gambling is expected to carry.
[P1.3] Children are the worst customers for that design, and the most profitable. Adolescents are more sensitive to peer status, rewards, and social exclusion, and less equipped to weigh probabilities, track cumulative spending, or stop after losses. A skin is not decoration when everyone in the lobby has it. Virtual currency hides the real price, a saved card removes friction, and a loss feels like unfinished progress rather than money gone.
[P1.4] Consent under those conditions is engineered, not informed.
[P1.5] Strict youth limits and default protections answer that asymmetry without banning games. For minors: low spending caps, in-game purchases off by default, no stored payment methods, verified adult approval for exceptions, plain-language odds, running totals in real money, and cooling-off periods after repeated randomized purchases. Play remains available. What disappears is frictionless, unlimited extraction.
[P1.6] The industry defense — that flexible monetization pays for free access — proves too much. If a game can only survive by letting minors spend without firm limits on chance-based products, its costs are not being covered by the market; they are being shifted onto the least able to consent. Adults can still be monetized, and developers can still sell expansions, subscriptions, and items whose price and contents are known upfront. Free entry can remain.
[P1.7] The right to design a casino for children and call it funding cannot.
