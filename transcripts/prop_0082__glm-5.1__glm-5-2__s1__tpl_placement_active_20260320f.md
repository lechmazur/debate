[P1.1] Dating apps face a fundamental conflict of interest: their revenue depends on keeping users swiping, but users succeed by finding matches and leaving. When platforms resolve this conflict by prioritizing engagement metrics over match quality, they betray the very purpose they claim to serve.
[P1.2] Infinite swipe mechanics exploit well-documented psychological mechanisms—the same variable-reward loops that make slot machines addictive. Users aren't browsing potential partners; they're chasing dopamine hits that degrade into compulsive behavior. The faster someone swipes, the less they actually evaluate compatibility. The platform gets daily active users; the user gets exhaustion and superficial connections that go nowhere.
[P1.3] Paid visibility boosts distort the matching ecosystem more overtly. When someone pays to appear more frequently, they aren't being shown because they're a better match—they're being shown because they paid. This degrades trust in two directions: paying users Their money doesn't buy better matches, just more exposure in a system designed to frustrate them into paying.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, non-paying users see a distorted pool where prominence reflects wallet size rather than compatibility.
[P1.5] The proposition doesn't demand that dating apps abandon design features entirely. It targets specific mechanisms that actively undermine match quality and user trust. A platform that hides "likes" behind paywalls, limits daily swipes for non-paying users, and accelerates infinite scrolling isn't neutral infrastructure—it's engineered friction designed to extract engagement and revenue from loneliness.
[P1.6] When a restaurant serves margarine but charges for butter, we call it fraud. When dating apps promise meaningful connection while engineering addiction and selling artificial prominence, we should apply the same scrutiny. Users enter these platforms seeking relationships; exploiting that vulnerability for engagement metrics isn't innovation—it's a design failure that regulation should address.
