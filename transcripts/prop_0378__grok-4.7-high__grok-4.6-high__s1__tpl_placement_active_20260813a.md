[P1.1] Adult friendship has become hard to sustain because the ordinary machinery that once produced it has been dismantled. Repeated, low-pressure contact—the same faces at the same hours, week after week—is how adults actually become friends. Stable blocks, spare time at work, clubs, and casual gathering spots supplied that contact without anyone scheduling a friendship strategy. Those supports have eroded: more mobility, longer or fragmented work, fewer shared institutions, and digital contact that rarely replaces showing up in person.
[P1.2] A person who relocates for work, commutes, and comes home to a building with no shared life can go years without a friend who would notice if they disappeared. That is not a character flaw. It is what the schedule and the street now produce.
[P1.3] The costs do not stay private. Chronic loneliness tracks with poorer health, heavier use of care, weaker productivity, and thinner civic trust. Hospitals, employers, and cities already absorb the fallout. Calling friendship a purely personal matter ignores that pattern.
[P1.4] We do not treat traffic deaths as a private driving problem once street design makes crashes routine. We change the design.
[P1.5] Workplaces, cities, and public institutions are already the designers. Whether a job lets colleagues know one another, whether a neighborhood has a library, park, or hall people can use without a reservation and a fee, whether transit and hours make return visits possible—these are institutional decisions. Leaving them unexamined does not respect privacy. It locks in scarcity.
[P1.6] The response should be active and modest. Protect time and norms at work so real ties can form. Fund and program civic spaces so adults become regulars, not one-time event attendees. Treat social connection as a legitimate aim of public institutions, alongside health and education, without assigning friends or policing intimacy.
[P1.7] Friendship stays voluntary. The conditions that make it possible should not stay private.

[C1.1] Adult friendship is often hard. That does not make it a job for HR, city hall, or public agencies. The proposition treats a voluntary, uneven, personal bond as if it were infrastructure. It is not.
[C1.2] Friendship is chosen. It depends on taste, timing, chemistry, and what people willingly give up. Adults already trade evenings for wages, children, partners, and rest. Those trades are not a design defect.
[C1.3] They are how grown lives work. A person who has few close friends after a move is not proof that the street failed them. It is proof that friendship was never a service the public could assign.
[C1.4] The traffic analogy collapses. Crashes are involuntary physical harm on shared pavement. Two coworkers who never become friends are not a crash. Measuring “connection” at work turns professional life into a social project.
[C1.5] Then come mandatory mixers, engagement scores, and quiet pressure on people who want distance: night-shift parents, religious minorities, anyone whose real ties live outside the office. A firm that pays fairly and keeps hours predictable already does enough. Making friendship a workplace aim is not modest. It is the employer claiming jurisdiction over intimacy it cannot judge.
[C1.6] Cities and public institutions already fund parks, libraries, and halls for literacy, play, and recreation. That is justified without recasting those places as friendship factories. Once friendship is the named policy, programmers must show they produced regulars and ties, not merely offered space. Budgets follow metrics.
[C1.7] Metrics follow surveillance of attendance, belonging, and “social capital.” Voluntary groups still form friends because they are not accountable to a loneliness target.
[C1.8] Loneliness has many causes: temperament, family change, grief, screens, hours people accept for money. Healthcare can treat illness. Public space can remain open. Neither becomes competent by declaring adult friendship a social-policy concern.
[C1.9] The private character of friendship is what keeps it unforced. Institutionalize the aim, and you get the appearance of community with none of the consent.
