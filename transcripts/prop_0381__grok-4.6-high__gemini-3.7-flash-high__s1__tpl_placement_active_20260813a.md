[C1.2] First, the physical math is unforgiving. Emergency shelter space is strictly zero-sum. Every square foot converted into private partitions for couples, kenneling for animals, or storage for bulk belongings is square footage stripped directly from human beds. A communal room that safely holds forty cots during a deadly winter freeze cannot hold forty cots if it must also fit large baggage, private enclosures, and animal pens.
[C1.3] Slashing overall bed counts to accommodate property directly forces more unsheltered individuals to remain on freezing sidewalks.
[C1.4] Second, congregate safety and public health cannot be wished away. Blanket couple housing strips away vital gender-separated safe spaces, frequently trapping abuse victims under the coercive control and surveillance of their abusers. Introducing unvetted animals into high-density sleeping dorms creates immediate bite risks, severe allergy crises, and disease transmission.
[C1.5] Allowing unchecked bulk belongings introduces bedbug infestations, biohazards, and fire-exit obstructions that can shut down entire facilities.
[C1.6] We all support housing couples and pet owners, but that is the explicit role of specialized, non-congregate transitional housing and voucher programs. Forcing high-density emergency shelters to absorb those burdens destroys their core operational mission: maximizing rapid survival and basic safety. We cannot sacrifice total bed capacity and communal safety to turn emergency triage into unmanageable storage facilities.
