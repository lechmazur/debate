[P1.1] Fragmented service industries suffer from a structural race to the bottom. In sectors like hospitality and social care, competition relies on cutting labor costs. Firms cannot raise standards voluntarily without losing market share to undercutting rivals. Repeated minimum-wage hikes fail here.
[P1.2] They are a blunt instrument applied across the economy, ignoring productivity differences between a tech startup and a nursing home. A uniform hike risks job losses in low-margin sectors while leaving non-wage exploitation untouched. Legislators forcing a uniform number often trigger inflationary pressures that erode real gains, necessitating another political battle shortly after. This cycle creates uncertainty for workers trying to plan their lives around income that fluctuates with political whims.
[P1.3] Sectoral bargaining solves fragmentation by setting standards across the industry rather than the whole economy. When employers and workers agree on binding minimums for a specific sector, it removes labor costs from the equation of competition. Firms compete on quality, not on who pays the least. This levels the playing field, preventing responsible employers from being punished while bad actors thrive on exploitation.
[P1.4] Furthermore, sectoral bargaining delivers more than a wage floor. It addresses the specific precarity of service work: unpredictable scheduling, unsafe conditions, and lack of training. Minimum-wage legislation rarely touches these issues. A care worker needs guaranteed hours as much as higher pay.
[P1.5] Sectoral agreements capture this complexity because industry insiders understand operational realities better than distant legislators. They negotiate tiered standards reflecting experience, rather than a flat floor that compresses pay structures. By linking pay to skill acquisition, sectoral deals encourage retention and reduce the turnover costs that plague fragmented services.
[P1.6] Finally, stability matters. Minimum-wage hikes are subject to political volatility, changing with every election cycle and creating uncertainty for business planning. Sectoral bargaining institutionalizes wage setting, allowing for predictable adjustments tied to sector productivity. For fragmented industries trapped in low-wage equilibrium, sectoral bargaining offers a tailored, comprehensive, and stable mechanism that blanket hikes simply cannot match.
