[C1.1] Cities do not face a false choice between a perfectly managed plaza and a lawless alley. They face a choice between treating public intoxication as a contained privilege and refusing to normalize it on the street. Open-container zones do not merely relocate existing drinking; they advertise it, expand it, and concentrate its worst effects where density turns ordinary impairment into crowd chaos, noise, assaults, and medical emergencies that overwhelm the very staff and cameras the proposal relies on.
[C1.2] The French Quarter and Power & Light are not proof of concept. They are cautionary exhibits of how “tight management” looks in practice: vomiting tourists, opportunistic crime, and a constant drain on police and cleanup that nearby residents never consented to subsidize. Visibility does not equal control. A camera records a fight; it does not prevent the sixth drink that starts it.
[C1.3] Marked cups and extra lighting do nothing for the person who leaves the zone drunk, drives, or continues the party two blocks outside the boundary where the special rules end and the problems do not.
[C1.4] Prohibition of open containers does not invent drinking in stairwells. It channels social drinking into bars and restaurants that already carry liquor licenses, trained servers, liability insurance, and age verification—settings where someone is actually accountable when a patron is overserved. That is regulation with teeth, not a decorative security presence paid for by taxpayers so that visitors can wander with plastic cups.
[C1.5] Compliance arguments cut the other way. Once a city carves out a zone where public drinking is celebrated, enforcement everywhere else looks arbitrary, boundaries blur, and the exception becomes the expectation. Selective enforcement does not disappear; it simply migrates to the edges and to whoever cannot afford the entertainment district. The safer, fairer rule is the simple one: drink where the seller is licensed and responsible, not where the city has agreed to look the other way inside a painted line.
