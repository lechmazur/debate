[P1.1] Global supply chains are undergoing their most profound structural realignment in half a century. For Latin America, this represents a generational window to escape the middle-income trap and commodity dependence. But nearshoring will not automatically deliver durable development. Without an assertive, state-led industrial policy, foreign investment merely creates shallow assembly enclaves—importing foreign components, exploiting low wages, and exporting profits without transferring technology or building domestic capacity.
[P1.2] Prioritizing nearshoring-focused industrial policy is essential for three reasons.
[P1.3] First, deep industrial integration requires strategic intervention. To ensure nearshoring builds resilient domestic ecosystems rather than temporary assembly plants, states must deploy local content rules, targeted subsidies, and selective trade protections. These measures compel multinational corporations to integrate domestic suppliers and transfer technical know-how. While such policies involve transitional trade friction, they represent the proven mechanism by which developing nations graduate from low-tier assembly to high-value manufacturing.
[P1.4] Second, the associated fiscal burdens are productive investments, not deadweight costs. Upfront state spending on grid modernization, logistics corridors, technical training, and strategic incentives builds the physical and human foundation required to attract high-complexity industries like electronics, medical devices, and clean energy components. This initial fiscal commitment generates a dynamic return: it expands the formal tax base, raises industrial wages, and diversifies export revenues, ultimately strengthening long-term fiscal solvency.
[P1.5] Third, the opportunity cost of passivity is catastrophic. Decades of uncoordinated market openness have entrenched Latin America's vulnerability to volatile commodity cycles and premature deindustrialization. If the region refuses to absorb upfront fiscal costs and defend its emerging industrial clusters, nearshoring capital will either bypass Latin America entirely or lock it into low-margin, easily relocated assembly.
[P1.6] Short-term fiscal expansion and targeted protective measures are not structural liabilities; they are the necessary price of admission to capture permanent, high-value global manufacturing.
