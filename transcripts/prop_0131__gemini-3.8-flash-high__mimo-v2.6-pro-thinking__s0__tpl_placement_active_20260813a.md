[P1.1] Every modern welfare state rests on an unsustainable fiction: that families possess an infinite supply of unpaid labor to absorb the care needs of an aging and disabled population. That assumption is collapsing. Declining birth rates, rising retirement ages, and the necessity of dual-income households mean families can no longer absorb round-the-clock care alone. The choice facing governments is not between compensating family members or getting free care; it is between supporting family caregivers now or watching the broader care system fracture.
[P1.2] First, relying on unpaid care is fiscally self-defeating. When a relative is forced out of the workforce, the state loses tax revenue while the caregiver sacrifices career progression and pension security. When these caregivers inevitably burn out under physical and financial strain, the burden does not vanish; it lands directly on emergency rooms and publicly funded nursing homes. Institutional placements cost public budgets vastly more than direct caregiver stipends.
[P1.3] With formal home-care workforces already suffering severe shortages, paying family caregivers is the most cost-effective preventive strategy available to avert expensive institutional collapse.
[P1.4] Second, care is indispensable economic labor. Informal care falls overwhelmingly on women, driving lifelong wage penalties and elder poverty. It is indefensible to maintain that managing medication, feeding, and providing personal hygiene only possesses value when billed by an outside contractor, but becomes economically invisible when performed by a daughter or spouse. Denying pay does not reduce costs; it merely forces families to absorb a massive, unacknowledged public subsidy at the expense of their own financial futures.
[P1.5] Governments cannot continue balancing long-term care budgets on the exhausted goodwill of families. Generously compensating family caregivers is neither charity nor an optional perk; it is the only viable way to stabilize our healthcare infrastructure, protect household solvency, and value essential labor.
