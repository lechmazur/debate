[C1.3] Māori language nests began as community projects built by families; Hawaiian returned through parents who founded immersion schools. [C1.4] Second, PRO's strongest argument destroys their conclusion. States, they say, broke these cultures—children punished for speaking their home language. Precisely. So why should survivors' memories now sit in state custody, catalogued by the institution that tried to erase them?
[C1.5] Governments do not merely fund archives; they curate them. [C1.6] Third, "standing" is fiction. No legislature binds its successors; endowments are raided in austerity, and electoral cycles are grant cycles with higher stakes. [C1.7] Memory deserves owners, not administrators. Private stewardship fails in small ways, one project at a time. A state fund fails at scale, for everyone, all at once. Keep memory in the hands of the people it belongs to.
