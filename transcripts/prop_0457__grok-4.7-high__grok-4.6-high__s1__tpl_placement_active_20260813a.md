[P1.1] The permitting system is now one of the largest sources of emissions in the energy transition, because it keeps cleaner power—and the wires that carry it—off the grid. Delay is not a neutral pause. Every extra year a wind project, solar farm, or transmission line sits in sequence is another year the coal or gas plant it would displace keeps running.
[P1.2] The motion does not abolish environmental standards. It says streamline significantly, even when that reduces some local review and litigation. Core protections—species, safety, pollution limits, compensation for genuine harm—can remain. What should not remain is a stack of overlapping reviews, sequential sign-offs, and open-ended lawsuits that function as a veto for anyone with time and counsel.
[P1.3] A regional line is a public good. Its climate and reliability benefits spread across millions; its visual and land-use costs fall on a few counties. Unlimited local process systematically overweights the concentrated cost and underweights the diffuse benefit. That is not careful environmentalism.
[P1.4] It is a bias toward the status quo.
[P1.5] That status quo already has an environmental record. Existing fossil plants were often built under weaker modern review, yet they face no equivalent gauntlet merely to keep operating. New clean projects must clear every hoop to replace them. The dirtier option wins by default.
[P1.6] Interconnection queues already hold hundreds of gigawatts of proposed clean generation that cannot reach customers without transmission, and major lines often take the better part of a decade to permit. Financing dies, supply chains move, and communities stay tied to higher prices and dirtier power.
[P1.7] Streamlining matches process to purpose. Firm timelines, a lead agency, a bounded comment period, and a narrower path for late or duplicative claims do not license recklessness. They force decisions. Climate damage compounds; a slower search for a perfect process loses to a faster adequate one.
[P1.8] Governments serious about cutting emissions should stop treating the infrastructure that cuts them as the thing most in need of being stopped.
