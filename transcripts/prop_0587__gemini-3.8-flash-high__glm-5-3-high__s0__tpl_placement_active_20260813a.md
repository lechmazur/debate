[P1.1] Domestic subsidies and regulations hit an inescapable mathematical wall: the United States generates barely over ten percent of global greenhouse gases. Even if domestic measures achieved perfection tomorrow, they leave nearly ninety percent of global emissions untouched. Worse, relying on domestic tools alone creates severe carbon leakage. [P1.2] The planet experiences no net relief; emissions simply migrate abroad.
[P1.3] A U.S. carbon border adjustment breaks this trap by turning the world’s largest consumer market into an international decarbonization engine. Over a five-year horizon, it outperforms domestic-only action across two decisive fronts.
[P1.4] First, speed of leverage. [P1.5] A border tariff forces foreign conglomerates to clean up their production methods today, route lower-carbon goods to market, or lobby their home governments to enact equivalent domestic pricing so that tax revenue remains at home.

If the United States enacts a complementary border adjustment, the two largest import markets unite into an inescapable climate club. [P1.7] Global heavy industry is forced to clean up production wholesale.
[P1.8] Subsidies and regulations alone are an open door through which foreign emissions escape. A carbon border adjustment makes decarbonization the non-negotiable cost of doing business.
