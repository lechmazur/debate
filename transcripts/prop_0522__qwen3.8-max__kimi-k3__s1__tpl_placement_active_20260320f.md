[P1.1] High-income countries should expand climate-linked humanitarian pathways now because the people most exposed to climate displacement cannot wait for a treaty that may never arrive on time. The core issue is not whether the legal label “climate refugee” is settled; it is whether states will offer lawful routes before displacement becomes chaos.
[P1.2] Climate disruption already forces people from homes through sea-level rise, repeated flooding, crop failure, and slow-onset salinization. A farmer in the Sahel whose wells fail, a family in a delta town repeatedly flooded, or a Pacific island community losing freshwater lenses may have no realistic domestic alternative. If no legal route exists, movement does not stop; it becomes irregular, more dangerous, and more expensive for everyone.
[P1.3] Pathways—humanitarian visas, temporary protection, evacuation schemes, labor and education routes, and planned relocation—can be expanded without waiting for a final convention. They are administratively flexible: they can be temporary, regional, quota-based, or tied to disaster declarations. They also preserve agency. A person who can apply from home or from a neighboring country is less likely to be trapped, trafficked, or forced into unsafe return.
[P1.4] The strongest reason to act before the law is settled is that legal uncertainty is not a reason for inaction; it is a reason for pragmatic protection. High-income states can design pilot programs, collect data, and build norms. Those programs can later harden into law. Expansion now also prevents high-income states from later claiming unforeseeability when disasters become displacement crises.
[P1.5] By contrast, waiting for a comprehensive regime risks years of preventable harm while political negotiation stalls.
[P1.6] Opponents will warn about definitional disputes or burdens. But pathways need not resolve every attribution question. They can target clear climate vulnerabilities and displacement risk. The question is whether high-income countries will use existing tools to reduce suffering now.
[P1.7] They should.
