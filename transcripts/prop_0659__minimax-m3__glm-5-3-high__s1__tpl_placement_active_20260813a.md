[P1.1] The case for prohibition rests on three converging facts. First, the underlying science is broken. Researchers in affective science have spent two decades showing that emotion is constructed contextually and does not read cleanly off the face.
[P1.2] When these contested models are fed into software, the software inherits those weaknesses and amplifies them through opaque machine learning. A hiring tool built on shaky premises doesn't belong anywhere near a hiring decision. Vendors sell these tools with confidence the science cannot justify.
[P1.3] Second, even granting the technology some signal, its error rates fall unevenly. Studies of commercial face-analysis systems have repeatedly shown worse performance on darker-skinned faces and on women, and emotion classifiers compound that with culturally specific assumptions about what a smile or a frown means in interviews. A flawed tool that disproportionately rejects protected groups is not a neutral productivity enhancer. It is a civil rights liability wearing a lab coat, and every misread face becomes potential litigation.
[P1.4] The employment-discrimination implications alone should keep every HR department awake at night.
[P1.5] Third, the harms to workers are not collateral, they are structural. Continuous emotional surveillance chills authentic expression, especially for neurodivergent workers, people with disabilities that affect facial movement, and anyone having a bad day. A worker who knows their resting face is being scored has a strong incentive to perform affect rather than do good work. That distortion is the opposite of what hiring and evaluation are supposed to produce.
[P1.6] And the chilling effect would inevitably smother workplace conversations, including union organizing and basic feedback to managers.
[P1.7] Prohibition isn't anti-technology. It is a targeted response to a tool that is unreliable, discriminatory in its effects, and corrosive to workplace dignity, while offering no clear business benefit that couldn't be obtained in interviews and reviews.
