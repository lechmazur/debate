[P1.1] The central mechanism is algorithmic amplification. A chronological feed is mostly neutral about which post reaches you: it shows what the accounts you chose published, newest first. A ranked feed is not neutral. It selects for what the platform predicts will hold your attention, and the most reliable engagement signals are anger, disgust, anxiety, and moral outrage.
[P1.2] That switch from chronological to engagement-ranked feeds supercharged toxicity.
[P1.3] Chronological feeds break the outrage flywheel. A pile-on requires strangers to see the offending post. Ranked feeds deliver it to them because the first angry replies mark it as high engagement. Chronological feeds do not automatically push a niche conflict into millions of unrelated timelines.
[P1.4] Conflict stays closer to its original audience, and without algorithmic distribution, the payoff for posting something purely inflammatory shrinks.
[P1.5] Default settings determine whose behavior scales. Most users never change defaults. When the default is engagement ranking, every creator competes in a market where the most extreme take wins attention; moderates are structurally disadvantaged. Under a chronological default, reach depends on audience and timing, not on how effectively a post provokes.
[P1.6] Creators would still seek attention, but the path to attention would not require turning every disagreement into a crusade.
[P1.7] This is not a utopian claim. Harassment and cruelty would still exist. But the comparison is about amplification. Removing the ranking engine removes the main industrial mechanism that converted anger into reach and reach into time on site.
[P1.8] Chronological defaults would keep conflict more local, reduce the incentive to manufacture outrage for distribution, and expose ordinary users to less ambient hostility. That is precisely why social media would be less toxic today if chronological feeds had remained the default.
