[P1.1] The modern workforce has fundamentally changed, yet our social safety net remains welded to a single employer model that no longer fits reality. Millions of workers now earn substantial incomes through a patchwork of gig platforms and short-term contracts—driving for multiple ride-share services, coding for successive startups, consulting across three clients in a quarter. These workers often earn full-time wages, yet they receive none of the full-time protections: no health insurance, no retirement contributions, no paid sick leave, no unemployment insurance if a platform deactivates them overnight.
[P1.2] This is not a marginal problem affecting casual side-hustlers. It is a structural failure that leaves high-earning independent contractors one hospital visit or algorithm change away from financial catastrophe. When a worker splits labor across Uber, DoorDash, and Upwork, each platform claims it is not the "employer," so none provides benefits. The worker falls through every crack while generating real economic value.
[P1.3] The solution is a portable benefits floor: a mandatory, worker-owned account that travels with the individual across platforms and contracts. As these workers earn substantial income, platforms would make prorated contributions into this portable account—funding health coverage, retirement savings, and paid leave that accumulates regardless of which app they open that morning. The benefit follows the person, not the job classification. This is administratively feasible because contribution rates can scale with earnings, and existing payroll infrastructure already handles multi-employer reporting.
[P1.4] This approach respects the flexibility that draws people to gig work while restoring the basic security that traditional employment once guaranteed. It prevents a race to the bottom where platforms compete by stripping protections, and it ensures that substantial earners contribute to their own stability rather than shifting risk to taxpayers. Without portability, we condemn a generation of productive workers to precarity. Economic security should not be a luxury reserved for those with a single W-2 employer.
