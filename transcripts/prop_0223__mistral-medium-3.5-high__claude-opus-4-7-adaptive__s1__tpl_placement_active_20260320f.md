[P1.1] Automated speed enforcement consistently outperforms increased police traffic stops in reducing traffic fatalities because it directly and continuously addresses the primary cause of severe crashes: excessive speed. Unlike human officers limited by numbers, shifts, and attention spans, cameras operate 24/7 across all lanes, enforcing limits without fatigue, bias, or the need for dangerous roadside interactions. The evidence is compelling: Washington D.C.'s speed camera program reduced speeding-related fatalities by over 70% at camera locations, while London's fixed camera network cut fatal and serious collisions by 42% at monitored sites.
[P1.2] Critically, the deterrent effect extends beyond camera zones as drivers internalize the risk of detection throughout the road network, creating system-wide safety improvements that sporadic police presence cannot achieve.
[P1.3] Traditional traffic stops produce comparatively modest and inconsistent safety gains while imposing significant negative externalities. Each stop creates a dangerous roadside interaction that has injured and killed both officers and civilians, and high-speed pursuits—though statistically rare—result in hundreds of deaths annually in the U.S. alone. More fundamentally, discretionary enforcement perpetuates racial disparities: multiple studies and real-world data show Black and Hispanic drivers are stopped at disproportionately higher rates than white drivers, despite similar violation rates. This pattern of biased enforcement erodes public trust and community cooperation without delivering superior safety outcomes.
[P1.4] Automated systems eliminate these harms by applying the law uniformly and objectively, without dangerous confrontations or biased discretion.
[P1.5] The choice is clear and the comparison decisive. For reducing traffic fatalities, automated enforcement achieves greater, more reliable safety improvements while avoiding the human costs, social tensions, and inconsistent application that inevitably accompany increased police traffic stops. On both the core metric of effectiveness and the critical consideration of negative externalities, automated speed enforcement is the unambiguously superior approach for any jurisdiction serious about saving lives on its roads.
