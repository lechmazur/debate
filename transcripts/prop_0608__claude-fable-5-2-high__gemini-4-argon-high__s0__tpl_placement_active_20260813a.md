Sellers gain from inflating that baseline, and buyers gain from not asking.

Plugging leaking methane wells, destroying industrial refrigerants, or mineralizing carbon into rock are external offsets, yet they are directly metered and permanent.
[C1.2] Conversely, most corporate emissions sit in sprawling Scope 3 supply chains, which are not read off a fuel meter; they are estimated using rough industry averages. If the problem is junk credits, mandate strict quality and permanence standards—do not restrict where real mitigation happens.
[C1.3] More fatally, [P1.7] admits this law only polices a voluntary label. [C1.4] The result is "greenhushing": corporations abandon public targets, stop funding external climate projects, and cut zero additional internal emissions.

Worse, scaling permanent carbon removal for global net-zero tomorrow requires massive corporate purchasing today to drive down costs.
[C1.6] Forcing a service firm to spend fortunes chasing marginal supply-chain cuts, rather than funding far cheaper verified removals or developing-world decarbonization, yields less climate action per dollar. Regulate offset integrity, not corporate boundaries.
