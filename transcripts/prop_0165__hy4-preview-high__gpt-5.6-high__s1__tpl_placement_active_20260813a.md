[P1.1] Public EV charging is not a gadget market; it is the roadside utility that decides whether an electric car is practical. When every network builds its own walled garden—its own app, membership, plug-and-charge credential, or payment rail—the result is not vibrant competition but a balkanized toll system. A driver does not experience real choice; she experiences a low battery, an unfamiliar charger, and a login screen that rejects her. That is the failure we should design out now.
[P1.2] Charging is a network good. Its value rises when any car can use any charger, and collapses when each station is a private island. Proprietary control here is not neutral product differentiation; it is a strategy to capture users and lock them in. The operator that owns the charger also controls discovery, pricing, and data.
[P1.3] Rivals then compete for exclusivity, not reliability. The predictable result is duplicated apps, idle chargers that members cannot access, and thin coverage on routes profitable to no single network.
[P1.4] Open interoperability means any driver, any charger, any payment. A mandate would not make the state the charger designer; it would set the interface—the digital handshake, payment, availability data, roaming—so firms compete on uptime, speed, price, and amenities. That is how railroads eventually agreed on gauge, how ATMs accept other banks' cards, and how cellphones roam. Companies can still build better hardware and nicer lounges; they simply cannot win by fencing off drivers.
[P1.5] Much of this market is being built with public rights-of-way, highway funding, and subsidies. Attaching interoperability to them is not heavy-handed regulation; it is the ordinary condition for using a public asset. Timing matters, too. Once proprietary networks harden, switching costs and stranded infrastructure make later interoperability far more expensive.
[P1.6] The motion is not anti-innovation. It is the precondition for a mass market: proprietary firms can still win on the charger, but they must win on service.
