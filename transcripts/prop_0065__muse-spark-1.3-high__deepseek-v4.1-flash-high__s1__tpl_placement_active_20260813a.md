[P1.1] Perfect prediction would not turn height, eye color, or predicted test scores into health. Screening embryos for such traits treats children as configurable products, not patients to be healed. The law already distinguishes therapy from enhancement for good reason, and that line holds even when the technology works.
[P1.2] These traits are largely positional. A few centimeters of extra height or a few IQ points predicted on paper matter mainly because others lack them. If every affluent family buys the same advantage, nobody gets ahead, but everyone faces new pressure to screen, discard more embryos, and spend more just to stay level. Private choice becomes collective coercion.
[P1.3] And access will never be equal. IVF plus screening costs thousands, requires clinics, time, and discarded embryos. Wealthy parents would convert money directly into perceived biological advantage for their children, compounding wealth with genetics. A society that rightly bans selling organs or buying university seats should not permit buying a taller, whiter, “smarter” starting lot in life.
[P1.4] The deepest cost falls on the child. A child selected for predicted cognition arrives with a forecast taped to her back. Love risks becoming conditional on delivery: you were chosen to be tall, brilliant, beautiful — perform it. [P1.5] History warns what markets normalize. Twentieth-century eugenics began with respectable talk of improving stock. Today’s private eugenics needs no state coercion; consumer choice plus clinic advertising does the work. Law is how a democracy says some paths to a child are incompatible with equality even when technically feasible.
[P1.6] Freedom is not the absence of law, but protection from such arms races. Restricting non-disease screening preserves genuine reproductive liberty for all.
