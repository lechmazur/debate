[P1.1] An actor’s face and voice are not raw studio property; they are a living person’s identity, craft, and livelihood. Traditionally, directors shaped performances by choosing among takes an actor actually gave. Generative AI breaks that compact. Synthetic voice tools and AI de-aging do not merely polish a performance; they fabricate one that never happened.
[P1.2] Before studios materially alter a living actor’s work this way, explicit consent and compensation are essential.
[P1.3] First, explicit consent protects autonomy and reputation. Audiences judge the actor on screen, not the software. Imagine an actor delivers a restrained scene, only for a studio to use voice cloning months later to make them sob, slur, or speak controversial new lines. Or consider an uncanny de-aging pass that makes a dramatic lead look lifeless.
[P1.4] The living actor absorbs that career damage. Standard contracts granting studios ownership of a shoot’s footage were meant so editors could cut scenes, not so algorithms could puppet a human being. Because studios hold immense leverage over working actors, hidden boilerplate waivers cannot suffice; consent must be explicit.
[P1.5] Second, compensation stops studios from using an actor’s own biometrics to extract unpaid value. Historically, changing dialogue or vocal delivery in post-production meant paying the actor to return to the recording booth. Mapping a performer’s youthful likeness onto a body double, or selling a multi-era performance, meant negotiating and paying for that expanded role. Synthetic voice and de-aging let studios capture those exact commercial benefits while cutting the actor out of the paycheck.
[P1.6] Mandatory compensation ensures studios pay for the synthetic work they generate, just as they pay for reshoots today.
[P1.7] Crucially, this rule applies only to material alterations. Routine audio cleanup, pacing cuts, and minor visual fixes proceed untouched. No technology is banned and no consensual art is blocked; studios simply must ask and pay before turning a living performer into an unpaid digital puppet.
