[P1.1] The transition to clean energy is not merely an environmental imperative; it is a fundamental shift in global economic and geopolitical power. To secure this future, countries must use industrial policy to localize critical clean-tech supply chains, treating short-term costs and trade frictions as necessary insurance premiums for long-term stability.
[P1.2] First, hyper-globalized supply chains present an unacceptable security risk. Currently, the processing of critical minerals and the manufacturing of essential components like solar wafers and battery cells are dangerously concentrated in single foreign markets. If a country relies entirely on imported green technology, it merely trades a dependency on foreign petrostates for a dependency on foreign battery monopolies. As the world recently witnessed with the weaponization of natural gas, adversarial regimes can and will choke off energy supplies for geopolitical leverage.
[P1.3] Localizing clean-tech manufacturing through targeted subsidies and procurement protects the broader economy from catastrophic supply shocks and extortion.
[P1.4] Second, domestic industrial policy is the only way to make the green transition politically sustainable. Decarbonizing the economy requires decades of unwavering public support. If voters perceive the climate transition as a mandate to offshore their manufacturing base to cheaper foreign markets, they will reject these policies at the ballot box. By tying climate goals to domestic job creation and industrial revitalization, governments build a durable, working-class political coalition that ensures decarbonization survives election cycles.
[P1.5] Finally, the supposed drawbacks—higher prices and trade tensions—are acceptable, and in some ways, beneficial. A localized supply chain may incur higher upfront prices, but this is a vital resilience premium. Furthermore, the "trade tensions" caused by aggressive domestic subsidies actually trigger a competitive global race to the top. When one major economy heavily subsidizes green tech, trading partners respond in kind to remain competitive.
[P1.6] This dynamic unleashes massive waves of global capital into the sector, rapidly scaling deployment, driving down long-term technological costs, and accelerating the overall net-zero transition. We must secure our green economic future by building it at home.
