[C1.2] A much more aggressive inheritance tax also invites the very planning that preserves dynastic advantage. [C1.3] Meanwhile, less mobile assets—family firms, farms, private companies—face liquidity shocks and forced sales. Those costs fall on workers, communities, and smaller enterprises, not just heirs.
[C1.4] PRO’s contrast with high earners is also too neat. Top annual income is not just consumption; it is the raw material of dynastic wealth. [C1.5] If the concern is rigged starting lines, taxing only the final transfer ignores the annual compounding of advantage.
[C1.6] Finally, equality of opportunity depends more on what society does with revenue and public institutions than on punitive transfer taxes. Broad-based taxes on capital income, closing loopholes, and investment in early education, housing, and entrepreneurship do more to level starts than a narrow inheritance levy. The motion names a real problem but chooses the wrong lever: it taxes an event, not the durable structures of advantage.
