[P1.1] Liberal democracies already accept that some transactions require proof of age. We card people at casinos, liquor stores, and betting shops—not because we suspect every customer is a minor, but because the cost of verifying is trivial next to the harm of not verifying. Online pornography and gambling are the same products, delivered through a channel children can reach from their bedrooms at midnight. The only real question is whether the verification can be done without building a surveillance apparatus, and the motion says it can.
[P1.2] That qualifier—"privacy-preserving"—does most of the moral work, and it's honest. Modern verification systems are built to prove one fact, "this person is an adult," and nothing else. Privacy-preserving designs use tokenization and zero-knowledge techniques so the site learns yes-or-no, never your identity, and the verifier never learns what site you visited. This is not a browser history registry or a national porn database; it's closer to a bouncer glancing at your ID and handing it back.
[P1.3] Compare that to what already exists: payment processors, advertisers, and ISPs already know far more about adult browsing than any age-verification token would.
[P1.4] Against this stands an inconvenience argument: some adults find the check intrusive. So they find seatbelts intrusive. They find airport screening intrusive. Liberal societies routinely impose small, well-designed frictions on adults when the protected interest is serious and the burden is proportionate.
[P1.5] A thirty-second, anonymized age check for gambling—a product engineered for addiction, with documented harms to families and finances—is about as proportionate as friction gets. The intrusion is real but marginal; the protection it buys is substantial.
[P1.6] The alternative to mandatory checks isn't adult liberty in its purest form. It's effectively unrestricted access for children, because voluntary age gates are clicked through by any twelve-year-old who can read. When an industry won't self-police, and the harms fall on those least able to protect themselves, mandatory minimal checks are exactly what justified governance looks like.

[C1.2] 'Privacy-preserving' describes a design aspiration, not what a compulsory, population-scale system becomes. Somewhere an adult presents a passport, face scan, bank record, or phone credential; somewhere a token is issued, timed, and verified. Even if the site sees only 'over 18,' issuers, IP addresses, and device fingerprints can be correlated, retained, breached, subpoenaed, or repurposed. Governments cannot guarantee every vendor stays minimal, every record stays deleted, or a future government resists expanding the checkpoint from age to identity.
[C1.3] The burden is more than thirty seconds of annoyance. Pornography is lawful adult expression, and anonymity around sexuality protects people for whom exposure is danger, not embarrassment: LGBTQ adults in hostile homes, people in controlling relationships, and survivors seeking sexual-health material. Others lack the required ID, smartphone, credit history, or a face automated systems read reliably, and are locked out of legal content and regulated gambling markets. A lawful choice that depends on being documented and biometrically legible is rationed, not respected.
[C1.4] Nor does compulsion reliably work. Determined minors and frustrated adults can use VPNs, foreign or noncompliant sites, borrowed credentials, or social platforms where explicit material circulates unchecked. Burdening compliant sites drives traffic to the least regulated corners, with no moderation, safer-gambling limits, or help tools. It performs protection while weakening it.
[C1.5] Less intrusive means can be strengthened without universal compulsion: device- and account-level parental controls at setup, opt-in filtering, family education, and for gambling tighter enforcement around payments and advertising by regulated firms. Those target risk without universal proof of adulthood.
[C1.6] Justified policy must work, be necessary, and preserve privacy in the real world, not on a vendor diagram. Mandatory checks fail all three.
