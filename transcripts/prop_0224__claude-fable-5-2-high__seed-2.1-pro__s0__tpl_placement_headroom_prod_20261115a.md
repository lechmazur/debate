[P1.1] The decisive words in this motion are "per public dollar." The question is not which technology is cheapest for a private developer, but where a taxpayer's dollar buys the most carbon reduction that would not otherwise have happened. On that test, nuclear wins.
[P1.2] Start with life extension, the best bargain in climate policy. A paid-off reactor running another twenty years needs a modest refurbishment and often only a small support payment or a loan guarantee. In return it keeps roughly a gigawatt of round-the-clock zero-carbon output that already has a grid connection, a site and a workforce. The counterfactual is not hypothetical.
[P1.3] When Indian Point closed, New York's gas burn rose. Germany retired healthy reactors while lignite kept running. Each dollar that prevents a closure displaces fossil generation at once, with no interconnection queue and no permitting fight.
[P1.4] Compare the marginal public dollar for wind, solar and batteries. These are already commercially mature, and private capital builds them at enormous scale without help. Additional subsidy therefore mostly pays developers for projects they would have built anyway. The real constraints are transmission, interconnection backlogs and curtailment, and generation subsidies do not remove them.
[P1.5] As penetration rises, each new solar panel increasingly displaces other solar at noon rather than gas at dusk, so abatement per dollar falls just as spending climbs.
[P1.6] New build is where public money has the most leverage. Loan guarantees and regulated financing cut nuclear's dominant cost, which is interest, and the loans are largely repaid. Programs that build in series, like Korea's and the Barakah project in the UAE, have delivered reactors within this window. Restarts and uprates come sooner still.
[P1.7] So the case rests on three claims:
[P1.8] - **Additionality:** nuclear dollars buy abatement that would not otherwise occur, while renewable dollars mostly subsidize what the market already delivers.
[P1.9] - **Displacement:** firm output displaces fossil generation hour for hour.
[P1.10] - **Leverage:** public financing for nuclear is recycled, not spent.
[P1.11] The opposition must show that the next public dollar for renewables buys more than this.
