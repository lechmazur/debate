[P1.1] Choosing not to have children is not selfish. It is, more often than not, an act of radical honesty. The people who make this choice are not withdrawing from the world: they work, pay taxes, care for ageing parents, mentor teenagers, volunteer after disasters, feed their neighbours when they are sick. They contribute fully to community, they just decline to take on the unique, irreversible lifetime obligation of raising another human being.
[P1.2] That is not selfishness. That is knowing your limits. A person who accepts they cannot give a child consistent safety, attention, or peace is acting far more responsibly than someone who has a child out of unexamined social habit.
[P1.3] Society does not treat any other major life choice this way. Nobody shames someone for not becoming an intensive care nurse, even though societies everywhere are desperate for nurses. Nobody corners a parent of one child at every family dinner to demand when the second will arrive, as if this is a moral failure. Only childlessness is framed as a collective problem to fix.
[P1.4] This insistence causes real, unnecessary harm. The quiet, constant shame that follows childless adults through weddings, work parties, holiday dinners does not convince anyone to have a child they did not want. All it does is make millions of perfectly good, contributing members of society feel like they are incomplete, like they are failing at being an adult, for a choice that harms no one.
[P1.5] This is not an argument that no one should have children. It is the much simpler claim that this choice is neutral, reasonable, and deserves to be treated as such.
