[P1.1] A residential lease is one of the largest financial commitments an individual makes. Yet, the rental market operates on profound information asymmetry. Landlords possess comprehensive historical data about their properties, while prospective tenants make binding, year-long commitments based on a staged, fifteen-minute viewing. [P1.2] We must apply this exact logic to chronic environmental defects: recurring short-term rental parties, severe noise, and chronic package theft.
[P1.3] These are not transient annoyances; they are systemic failures that destroy a tenant’s right to quiet enjoyment and physical security. Only the landlord sees the accumulation of police reports, security footage, and prior complaints that reveal these chronic, structural patterns.
[P1.4] When landlords withhold this information, they trap tenants in unbearable conditions, ultimately forcing them to either endure daily misery or break their leases at severe financial penalties. True consent to a contract requires informed agreement to its material conditions. Mandating disclosure of these specific, severe issues aligns the rental market with basic principles of contract fairness and consumer protection, preventing landlords from profiting off hidden defects.
[P1.5] The opposition may argue tenants can simply research online reviews or ask neighbors. But this improperly shifts the entire burden of discovery onto the weaker party, ignoring that online reviews are easily fragmented, manipulated, or actively suppressed by property management companies. It ensures that when tenants sign a lease, they are consenting to the actual reality of the home they are renting, rather than a carefully sanitized illusion.
