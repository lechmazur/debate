[P1.1] The eurozone's post-2010 crisis response did not preserve European solidarity; it converted the Union into a ledger of winners and losers. The design ensured that stabilization costs would be borne politically by the many, while benefits accrued to mobile capital and creditor states. By forcing debtors to internalize a crisis they did not solely create, while shielding creditor surpluses from painful adjustment, the architecture of bailouts and austerity manufactured distrust across the continent. Solidarity is voluntary and mutual; what the eurozone offered was transactional rescue—liquidity in exchange for sovereignty, with the bill sent to those least able to pay.
[P1.2] In the periphery, “solidarity” arrived as conditionality. Greece, Portugal, and Ireland were subjected to Troika memoranda that slashed pensions, wages, and public investment, deepening depressions and driving youth unemployment to levels that emptied entire generations from the labor market. The rhetoric of European partnership rang hollow against the reality of enforced retrenchment. Spain and Italy, spared formal rescue, were nonetheless shamed into preemptive austerity.
[P1.3] When Greeks voted in 2015 to reject further austerity, the response was not negotiation but capitulation or chaos. That lesson taught millions that the euro was a monetary cage enforced by technocrats, not a partnership of equals.
[P1.4] The core grew resentful in equal measure. German, Dutch, and Finnish publics were told rescue packages were transfers to profligate southerners, a narrative that fed anti-EU parties from the AfD to the Finns Party. Even Ireland, the supposed model pupil, saw basic household charges become flashpoints for anti-establishment anger. Distrust became bilateral: the rescued despised the terms, and the rescuers despised the rescued.
[P1.5] Financial survival is not political legitimacy. The euro may have been preserved, but only by severing the link between European integration and popular consent. A union that endures through coercion rather than conviction accumulates not solidarity, but a quiet, durable fury.
