Calling this an inconsistency confuses different legal purposes.
[C1.2] A system that compels schooling can also hold a child accountable for grave harm he understood.
[C1.3] Culpability below fourteen is real where it matters. Children of ten and twelve plan, conceal and repeat serious violence. Hearings that test whether a particular child grasped the act’s nature and wrongness already protect the immature. A hard minimum of fourteen discards those hearings and exempts even the calculating offender.
[C1.4] Developmental averages describe impulse and peer effects; they do not prove that no child under fourteen can be justly punished, or that punishment means adult prison. Youth procedures match consequence to capacity without pretending capacity is zero.
[C1.5] A blanket raise does not improve outcomes. Diversion for minor crime needs no abolition of prosecution. What entrenches offending is learning that severe harm brings only welfare process and a swift return to the same setting. Criminal orders can mandate treatment, restrict contact and endure.
[C1.6] Welfare secure care often lacks equivalent duration, breach powers and legitimacy. Countries retaining lower ages keep a graduated response the proposed floor would erase. They are not overrun by persecution; they refuse to treat every young offender as incapable.
[C1.7] That floor manufactures usable impunity. Older offenders recruit children beneath the line precisely because conviction is impossible. Horrifying cases are not distractions; they test the policy. A rule that cannot convict the child who tortured a toddler has already failed the next victim.
[C1.8] Fourteen is a preference, not a threshold. States should retain the power to punish the young offender who knew what he did.
