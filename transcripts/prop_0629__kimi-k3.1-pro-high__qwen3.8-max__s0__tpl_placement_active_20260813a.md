[P1.1] The central issue is whether a platform’s control over the doorway to consumers should also give it control over every transaction behind that doorway. It should not. Independent payments create immediate competitive pressure: developers can choose providers on price and reliability, offer cheaper direct subscriptions, and pass savings to users.

[C1.1] The proposition treats the app store as merely a tollbooth. Its real function is a trust layer: before an app can ask for your contacts, camera, or payment card, someone has already screened it, signed it, tied it to a developer identity, and given users a common path for refunds and removal. Mandating independent payments and sideloading for most consumer apps dismantles that layer in the name of price competition.
[C1.2] Independent payments sound liberating until the transaction goes wrong. Store billing gives users one receipt, one subscription page, parental gates, dispute channels, and predictable revocation. Fragment that into hundreds of checkout systems and the predictable results are hidden renewals, fake “official” payment pages, stolen cards, and refunds that vanish into offshore processors. PRO’s savings are speculative; the fraud surface is immediate.
[C1.3] The users hurt first will not be sophisticated developers but ordinary people who cannot tell a legitimate checkout from a convincing spoof.
[C1.4] Sideloading makes the problem worse because it removes the choke point that makes mobile security credible. A phone is not a desktop: it carries banking apps, health records, location, and one-time codes. Malware distributors do not need a perfect exploit if they can persuade users to install a “free premium” game or utility from a website, then request accessibility or SMS permissions. Warnings and later revocation help, but they come after the credential harvest, the ransomware encryption, or the drained account.
[C1.5] The proposition assumes a world of informed adults; the market is children, seniors, hurried commuters, and employees using personal devices for work.
[C1.6] PRO can show targeted commission abuse without proving that a blanket mandate for most apps is safe. The better answer is narrower: police anti-steering and abusive fees, keep the security perimeter. Require the store to open payments and sideloading, and you trade a fixable pricing problem for systemic fraud, privacy theft, and lost consumer trust.
