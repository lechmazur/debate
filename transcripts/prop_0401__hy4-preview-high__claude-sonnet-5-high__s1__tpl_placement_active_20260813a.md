[P1.1] Europe is not short of millionaires; it is short of democratic self-respect. Golden visas and cash-for-passports turn residency and citizenship—the core civic bonds—into luxury commodities. The case is simple: these schemes sell European-wide rights at a national discount, and everyone else pays the price.
[P1.2] Start with what is sold. A permit or passport from one European state is not just a local permission slip. It unlocks travel across the EU, access to the single market, and, for citizenship, rights to live, work, vote, and receive consular protection across the bloc. When Cyprus and Malta marketed passports, they were not merely admitting wealthy individuals to a small island; they were distributing keys to twenty-seven states.
[P1.3] One country’s ‘sovereign choice’ becomes every neighbour’s externality.
[P1.4] Second, the benefits are oversold. Revenue is rarely transformative beside national budgets, and much flows into property rather than productive investment. In Lisbon, Athens, and Barcelona, concentrated demand from visa buyers helped inflate housing for residents who obey the law, work, pay taxes, and wait years to naturalize. An ordinary migrant must prove integration; a wealthy buyer need only prove a bank transfer.
[P1.5] That two-tier system corrodes the principle that membership is earned through contribution, not bought with capital.
[P1.6] Third, the risks are severe. Vetting often relies on intermediaries and thin due diligence, with a built-in incentive to approve. Even a small failure rate is unacceptable when those approved can move across borders and gain legitimacy. After 2022, European states acted against sanctioned oligarchs, some of whom had used investor routes to establish European footholds.
[P1.7] The schemes did not create those individuals, but they made Europe more permeable.
[P1.8] The alternative is not closed borders, but honest ones. Investment can be welcomed through ordinary business routes with real jobs and transparency. Residency should not be a commodity, and citizenship should never be a receipt. Europe should stop selling its membership.
