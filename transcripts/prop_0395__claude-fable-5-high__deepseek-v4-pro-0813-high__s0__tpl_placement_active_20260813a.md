[P1.1] A rule that fires a brilliant 65-year-old engineer while retaining a mediocre 40-year-old is not workforce management; it is discrimination with a payroll rationale. That is the core of this debate: mandatory retirement judges people by their birth certificate rather than their performance, and no serious institution should accept a proxy that crude when better tools sit on the shelf.
[P1.2] Three arguments carry the case. First, principle. We rightly refuse to fire people for their race or sex because group membership tells us nothing reliable about individual capability. Age is the same.
[P1.3] Cognitive and physical aging vary enormously between individuals—some decline at 55, others remain sharp at 80. A blanket cutoff mislabels millions of capable workers as spent, which is precisely why the United States abolished mandatory retirement for most jobs in the 1980s and Canada followed, without economic disruption or a collapse in workplace quality.
[P1.4] Second, practicality. Every employer already possesses the instrument that mandatory retirement pretends to be: performance evaluation. If a 68-year-old surgeon's hands are unsteady, competence testing catches it—and catches the unsteady 45-year-old too, whom an age cutoff misses entirely. Mandatory retirement is not a safety tool; it is a way to avoid honest management conversations, and it fails in both directions.
[P1.5] Third, necessity. Populations across the developed world are aging, pension systems are straining, and labor shortages are chronic in healthcare, skilled trades, and education. Forcing willing, healthy workers out is economically self-destructive: it shrinks the tax base, burdens pensions, and severs people from the purpose and social connection that working provides—both linked to better health in later life.
[P1.6] Note what the motion does not require. "Most occupations" preserves genuine bona fide requirements—airline pilots, perhaps—where individualized testing is truly infeasible. What must end is the routine, lazy default of expiration dates on human beings. Judge people by what they can do.
[P1.7] That principle should decide this debate.
