[C1.1] The claim that these literacies are “as fundamental as reading and math” [P1.5] mistakes urgency for structure. Reading and arithmetic unlock every later subject. AI literacy is a shifting bundle—probabilistic text, recommendations, synthetic media, platform incentives—that depends on reading comprehension, statistics, history, civics, and scientific skepticism. A ninth-grader who cannot evaluate a historical source will not be rescued by a stand-alone period called Manipulation Resistance.
[C1.2] Durable defense is judgment in context, not a subject badge.
[C1.3] “Manipulation-resistant” is too unstable to anchor a core discipline. Who decides which persuasion counts as manipulation? Core status means standards, approved examples, tests, textbooks, certified teachers. In polarized communities, that becomes a fight over which outlets and “biases” officials name; teachers retreat to harmless checklists.
[C1.4] Those checklists age badly: a lesson on uncanny faces may be obsolete while students are still graded on it. Generative systems change faster than curriculum adoption. Freezing them in a required course manufactures false confidence.
[C1.5] Core status also creates a compliance machine: vendor packages, pacing guides, box-ticking assessments. Administrators can say the problem is handled because the course exists. But manipulation is defeated by repeated practice inside real work: checking claims in English, tracing data in statistics, comparing propaganda in history, questioning model output in science. A box on the timetable is weaker than habits rebuilt across subjects.
[C1.6] The equity argument [P1.4] reverses cause and effect. A new core mandate without trained teachers and money is an unfunded paper mandate: wealthy districts hire specialists; poorer districts assign a worksheet or vendor video. The new period must come from somewhere—basic literacy, math, civics, arts, world languages—the knowledge that makes students harder to deceive. Optional, adaptable modules are not neglect in a fast-moving field; they allow revision, age-appropriate teaching, and local fit.
[C1.7] Schools should teach these practices seriously, but the proposition demands a permanent core for an impermanent domain. That is overreach, not preparation.
