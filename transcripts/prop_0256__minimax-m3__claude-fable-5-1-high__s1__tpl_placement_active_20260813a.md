[P1.1] Coastal communities are not just exposed — they are increasingly uninsurable. Insurers are pulling out of high-risk zones. When private capital retreats, the public purse absorbs the loss. Every rebuilt road, seawall, and sewage line in a floodplain is borrowed against the next storm.
[P1.2] Indonesia is already relocating its capital from sinking, flooding Jakarta to Nusantara. China has master-planned entire inland cities. Planned relocation is not theoretical — it is operational reality in major economies.
[P1.3] The case for planned inland climate-refuge cities rests on three claims.
[P1.4] First, repeated rebuilding violates basic public finance. When a house is destroyed twice in a decade and rebuilt twice, the second rebuild is not recovery — it is a bet against physics. Sea level rise, storm intensification, and flood frequency are not cyclical; they are trending worse. Betting on the trend is fiscal malpractice.
[P1.5] Second, planned cities buy compounding returns. Master-planned communities concentrate infrastructure, transit, utilities, and housing in one tract. A grid built once serves a generation. The same dollars scattered across scattered coastal rebuilds buy nothing durable — just another repair cycle.
[P1.6] Third, planned relocation is more humane than retreat by disaster. Letting people stay until the next flood, bailing them out, letting them rebuild, and bailing them out again is cruel and unstable. A planned inland city offers schools, hospitals, jobs, and permanence. Disaster-led relocation offers trailers and trauma.
[P1.7] The opposing view will claim that people have a right to stay and that coastal communities carry cultural and economic value. I grant both. But no one has a right to force taxpayers to underwrite an escalating loss. The choice is not between coastal life and nothing — it is between coastal life subsidized by endless disaster relief, and a deliberate alternative built once and built right.
[P1.8] Planned inland cities are not a retreat from the coast. They are an advance toward fiscal sanity, durable infrastructure, and intergenerational justice.
