AI operates primarily as a complement to human judgment, accountability, and contextual decision-making.
[C1.3] By absorbing routine synthesis, AI lowers the cost of professional services, unlocking vast latent demand among small businesses and individuals who were previously priced out of legal representation, custom software, or strategic consulting.
[C1.4] Furthermore, PRO mischaracterizes job creation as merely a narrow set of niche technical roles like model engineers [P1.2]. In reality, the broadest employment growth stems from the proliferation of new services, new enterprises, and expanded capacity across existing fields. [C1.5] Because white-collar work will evolve through task augmentation and demand expansion rather than outright elimination, policy should not be built on the false premise of net job destruction [P1.4]. AI will not shrink the white-collar workforce; it will make cognitive services more abundant, accessible, and central to the economy.
