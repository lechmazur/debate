[P1.2] Meanwhile the people whose stolen labour produced that capital were freed with nothing, then barred for generations from land, credit, education, and the vote. A debt that compounds on one side and a loss that compounds on the other is not healed by words. It is healed by transfer.
[P1.3] The motion is not asking whether apologies are nice. It asks whether apology should be the main instrument. [P1.4] An apology that leaves the balance sheet untouched is simply a cheaper way of closing the file.
[P1.5] These states have already proven they know how to pay reparations; they simply paid the wrong party. Direct reparations fail three tests: a coherent payer, a coherent claimant, and a remedy that repairs.
[C1.2] First, the payer has dissolved. Imperial Britain or Belgium is not a family vault still holding plantation profits. Modern wealth was made and remade through industrial change, wars, welfare states and work by present citizens — including millions descended from colonised and enslaved people themselves. [C1.3] That is not collecting a debt; it is assigning collective guilt to people who incurred none.
[C1.4] Second, the claimant cannot be drawn without new injustice. After centuries of migration and intermarriage, descent is a gradient, not a register. How much ancestry qualifies? [C1.5] Does someone in Kingston claim from Britain, in New York from America, or both? A state bureau sorting citizens by bloodline would entrench the racial classifications reparations claim to overcome.
[C1.6] Third, the sum is fiction. Pricing slavery requires inventing a counterfactual world without it, then apportioning blame among European empires, American buyers, African intermediaries and successor states. Any figure is a political bid, not a debt — too vast to pay, too arbitrary to settle, and guaranteed to produce resentment, not closure.
[C1.7] The alternative is not empty words. It is apology and truth-telling joined to large, sustained investment allocated by present disadvantage: schools, housing, credit and health in communities scarred by that history, plus serious development partnership abroad. That reaches descendant communities where harm still lives, without pretending bloodline is a bank account.
