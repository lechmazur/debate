[C1.1] Mandatory service charges are often not fixed, uniform costs. They can vary based on party size, time of day, or specific promotions. Baking a single, amalgamated price into the menu would strip restaurants of the agility to adapt these charges without constantly reprinting menus or confusing patrons with a new price list for every conceivable dining scenario. It transforms a dynamic business tool into a rigid, logistical nightmare.
[C1.2] The PRO argument also ignores the very real problem of psychological price anchoring. A menu price that includes a mandatory service charge would be significantly higher, potentially scaring off customers who might have otherwise enjoyed the meal and gladly paid the supplementary charge. By separating the charge, restaurants provide a more accessible entry point, allowing customers to mentally commit to the core value of the meal before encountering the additional, obligatory cost. This is not an "ambush"; it is a standard pricing strategy that reflects the true base cost of the food.
[C1.3] Furthermore, the idea that this practice is an unfair "information asymmetry" is overstated. Mandatory service charges are almost always stated on the menu, often in a discreet note at the bottom or top. The information is available; the customer simply has to read it. We do not argue that the price of a car should include the destination fee in the window sticker in giant bold font, because consumers understand that final prices can involve standardized, ancillary costs.
[C1.4] Demanding absolute, all-inclusive pricing on the menu forces an oversimplification that doesn't match the complexity of modern restaurant operations.
[C1.5] Finally, forcing this practice could lead to higher menu prices across the board. Restaurants, fearing customer backlash if the "include everything" price is too high, might simply increase base prices to absorb the charge, making the menu less competitive and hurting the very diners the PRO side aims to protect. The current system, when transparently noted, offers a clearer breakdown of where the money goes, allowing customers to see exactly what they are paying for beyond the food itself.
