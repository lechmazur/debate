[P1.1] Choosing not to have children is a reasonable, unselfish exercise of adult autonomy, and framing childlessness as a societal problem to solve is a harmful holdover of outdated norms that devalues individual choice and distracts from what parenting actually requires. Parenthood is an 18-plus year, irreversible commitment that demands immense time, money, emotional labor, and stability. Choosing to opt out when you know you cannot provide a child with a safe, loving, secure home is not selfish—it is a responsible, compassionate choice that avoids subjecting a hypothetical child to neglect or hardship.
[P1.2] For low-income workers, disabled people, and queer people who already face systemic barriers to providing that stability, the pressure to have children is not just unfair, it is cruel. The narrative that childlessness is a flaw to be fixed also erases the countless meaningful, impactful ways adults contribute to society that have nothing to do with raising children. People care for aging parents, mentor at-risk youth, volunteer in their communities, build art, advance research, and support their chosen family—none of these contributions are less valuable simply because they do not involve reproduction.
[P1.3] The label of "selfish" routinely applied to childfree people is a glaring double standard: no one calls someone selfish for choosing to have children, even though that choice creates new dependents who will use shared public resources and face the impacts of climate change, but opting out is framed as a moral failure. Worst of all, the panic over declining birth rates distracts from the actual crisis facing would-be parents: a lack of affordable childcare, paid parental leave, housing security, and healthcare support.
[P1.4] Instead of shaming people for not having children they cannot afford to raise, we should be supporting the people who do choose parenthood, so that choice is a viable, joyful option rather than a financial gamble no one can afford to take.

The “problem” is not that people are choosing not to have children—it is that society has made having children an impossible choice for everyone but the wealthy.
