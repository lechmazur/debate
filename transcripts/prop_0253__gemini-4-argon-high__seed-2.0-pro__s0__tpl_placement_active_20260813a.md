[P1.1] Gig platforms insist their workers are independent contractors running their own small businesses. Yet these same platforms confiscate the single most valuable asset any independent business builds: its reputation. When a ride-share driver with thousands of safe trips or a freelance designer with years of five-star client reviews tries to switch to a competing app, they are forced to start from zero. Because platform algorithms bury unrated newcomers and customers distrust blank profiles, leaving an established app means taking an immediate, punishing pay cut.
[P1.2] Mandatory reputation portability ends this artificial lock-in. Right now, incumbent platforms know workers cannot afford to abandon their verified history. That captivity gives platforms the leverage to quietly hike commission fees, cut base pay, and subject workers to opaque algorithmic management. Worse, if an automated system mistakenly deactivates a worker's account, their entire professional track record vanishes overnight.
[P1.3] Standardized data-transfer tools give workers ownership of their own labor history, ensuring they can walk away from exploitative terms—or survive an arbitrary deactivation—with their livelihood intact.
[P1.4] Beyond protecting workers, portability unlocks genuine market competition. Today, a challenger platform offering lower fees and better pay struggles to survive the cold-start problem: customers demand vetted workers, but veteran workers cannot afford to rebuild their credentials from scratch. Just as mobile number portability stopped telecom giants from holding consumers hostage through their phone numbers, reputation portability forces gig platforms to compete on fair pay and service quality rather than the height of their walled gardens. Nor does this require every company to adopt identical rating scales.
[P1.5] Standardized transfer tools simply export the verified underlying records—jobs completed, cancellation rates, and raw feedback scores—allowing the receiving platform to translate and display that track record transparently. Workers earned their reputation; platforms should not be allowed to hold it hostage.

[P2.2] They keep the exact same "safety valve" [C1.3] they have today. What the status quo actually does is force the vast majority of reliable workers who earned spotless records into that same zero-history penalty box whenever they try to switch apps.

Second, platforms do not invest in route mapping or dispute policies just to hoard driver ratings; they invest because customers immediately abandon apps with late deliveries and poor support. Traditional businesses also invest heavily in operational systems and evaluation rubrics, yet no one argues a contractor shouldn't take their verified resume or portfolio to a competitor.
[P2.4] Stopping a driver from proving they completed thousands of five-star trips does not protect innovation; it just protects monopoly lock-in.
[P2.5] Finally, standardized data transfers do not force platforms to adopt identical operational rules [C1.4]. Each receiving platform remains completely free to decide how to contextualize or display that external track record, such as granting newcomers a "Verified Industry Veteran" badge while they build platform-specific reviews. That preserves every platform's unique operational standards while ending the hostage-taking of worker reputations.
