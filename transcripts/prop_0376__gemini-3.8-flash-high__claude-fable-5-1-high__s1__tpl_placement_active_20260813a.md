[P1.1] High-interest revolving credit is marketed as a financial lifeline, but in reality, it functions as quicksand. When an annual percentage rate crosses 25 or 30 percent, the mathematics of compounding interest cease to provide genuine liquidity; they guarantee long-term insolvency.
[P1.2] The central objection to an interest rate cap is that lenders will respond by cutting credit access for higher-risk borrowers. We should accept that premise directly. Cutting off access to predatory credit is not an unfortunate side effect—it is the very mechanism of consumer protection.
[P1.3] Consider the reality of high-cost revolving debt. When a financially vulnerable household charges a $1,000 emergency expense to a card with a 30 percent interest rate, minimum payments barely service the accumulating finance charges. The principal balance remains almost entirely untouched. What began as a temporary liquidity shortfall is converted into an enduring extraction engine, siphoning scarce income away from basic necessities and straight into bank revenues.
[P1.4] In this market tier, profitability relies not on prompt repayment, but on perpetual, trapped indebtedness.
[P1.5] We do not allow the sale of unsafe vehicles or adulterated medicines simply because low-income consumers cannot afford premium options. The principle of consumer safety applies just as forcefully to financial products. Labeling high-rate debt as "access" is a dangerous distortion: access to an instrument mathematically designed to compound distress is not financial inclusion, but institutional exploitation.
[P1.6] A statutory rate cap establishes basic market discipline. If a lender cannot extend credit without demanding usurious rates to offset high default risk, that transaction should not occur. While losing an expensive line of credit creates immediate budgeting friction, it prevents a manageable cash shock from metastasizing into chronic insolvency, wage garnishment, and long-term ruin. Credit that only survives by consuming the borrower does not deserve legal sanction.
[P1.7] A rate cap draws a necessary boundary between legitimate lending and predatory extraction.
