[P1.1] Decarbonization is a race against a clock, and a race is won by whatever can be built fastest, cheapest, and in the most places. On all three counts, renewables have overtaken nuclear so decisively that nuclear can no longer claim to be the necessary centerpiece of most national strategies.

A solar farm needs none of that and delivers power within a year or two.
[P1.3] Now look at what the world is actually building. Solar and wind now add several hundred gigawatts annually, while net nuclear capacity grows by a handful. [P1.5] The last stubborn slice of firm power is a niche problem with many candidate solutions — not a reason to organize an entire strategy around the slowest, costliest option.
[P1.6] So here is the test: my opponent must show not that nuclear helps somewhere, but that most countries cannot decarbonize without making it central. Countries are already proving otherwise.

[C1.3] Treating firm power as a niche assumes away the emissions the strategy is meant to eliminate.

[C1.5] “Most countries” collapses different cases. [C1.6] The test is emissions over time. A plan that adds renewables but preserves coal “just in case,” or depends on gas, may look modern while remaining fossil-dependent.
