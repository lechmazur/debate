[P1.1] Streaming sold itself as the antidote to cable’s worst flaws: hidden fees, rigid year-long contracts, hundreds of unwanted channels bundled in to jack up prices, opaque add-ons customers never meant to keep. Today, too many major platforms are sliding right back into that exploitative model: rigid premium bundles that lock users into discounted rates they lose if they adjust their plan mid-cycle, auto-renewing add-ons buried three menus deep, no option to pause for a month when traveling or between must-watch shows, no straightforward way to split a shared plan across separated household members without paying punitive upcharges that render à la carte choice meaningless.
[P1.2] Most people have kept a $10 or $15 monthly subscription active for half a year or more because canceling required navigating a labyrinth of manipulative retention offers, or because they planned to get back to a show eventually but never did. Simple, monthly, no-penalty pausing, splitting, and reconfiguration does not just fix a trivial user frustration—it realigns the entire industry’s incentives with making good content, not trapping subscribers. If a viewer can pause a service the second they finish the one show they signed up to watch, studios cannot coast on a thin library of old titles and cheap filler content to collect passive, forgotten recurring revenue.
[P1.3] If users can split plans fairly across trusted friends or post-move household splits without jumping through arbitrary verification hoops, prices stay anchored to what people actually value, not what they are too busy to untangle. Lock-in only works for providers who know their product is not good enough to earn a voluntary subscription every single month. The entire point of cutting the cord was to escape that extractive dynamic, not replicate it behind a sleeker, app-based interface that uses the exact same dark patterns cable relied on for decades.

[C1.5] Second, PRO says easy pausing will force studios to make better content [P1.2]. It does the opposite. A prestige series takes years and enormous budgets to produce. [C1.7] Third, "splitting plans across trusted friends" [P1.3] is password sharing under a friendlier name. Shared accounts are priced as one household. When providers lose that revenue, the people paying alone cover the gap.
