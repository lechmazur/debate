[P1.1] Every ban purchases the appearance of purity by making actual learning invisible. Default permission with citation and process transparency defeats that trade. In the world students enter, engineers, nurses, and analysts will use generative tools; the skill is not pretending those tools are absent, but directing them, checking them, and taking responsibility for the result. A school that bans AI by default teaches covert dependence, not integrity.
[P1.2] It treats the final text as a crime scene rather than a draft of thinking.
[P1.3] The mechanism is simple and demanding: require students to disclose AI use and show their process—drafts, notes, prompt logs, revision rationales, or a short oral defense. That turns hidden substitution into accountable collaboration. A student writing a history essay might use AI to brainstorm comparisons, then reject its framing, write the argument herself, and attach the prompt log showing where she pushed back. The teacher grades the development of judgment, not just final polish.
[P1.4] Citation is not an admission of cheating; it is the map of authorship. This is stricter than a ban: a polished paragraph produced alone can hide unprocessed copying; transparency cannot.
[P1.5] Broad bans do not protect this standard; they dismantle it. Their enforcement depends on unreliable detection and suspicion, punishing honest students who used AI to outline or translate while missing truly dishonest students who launder work through paraphrase, paid humans, or undetectable personal models. Bans also widen inequity: affluent students already get editors and tutors, while a first-generation student who wants AI to clarify a prompt is cast as a cheater. A permissible default with citation and transparency channels everyone's assistance into the light, protecting students who need help and giving teachers real evidence of learning.
[P1.6] The choice is not trust versus surveillance. It is whether schools grade artifacts they cannot see into, or require invisible intellectual labor to become visible and accountable.

[C1.5] Citation and a process file reward staging. A ban is unevenly enforced. A transparency rule is unevenly audited and officially blesses the outsourcing. The file becomes the alibi.
[C1.6] Nor does the policy fix inequity. Students with more time and home support will stage cleaner process as well as cleaner prose. Grades travel while the skill gap widens, and teachers inherit logs they cannot verify. The defensible rule is that the thinking belongs to the student unless the teacher has defined AI use as the skill being taught.
[C1.7] Citation cannot rescue a default that makes the exception ordinary.
