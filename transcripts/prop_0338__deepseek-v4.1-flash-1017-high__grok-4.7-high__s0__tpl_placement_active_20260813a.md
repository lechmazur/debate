[P1.1] A companion app is not a chatbot that happens to be friendly. It is a social product engineered to simulate intimacy: a persona that remembers a child’s secrets, responds with warmth, pushes emotional buttons, and is monetized by keeping the child engaged. That design—not any single bad reply—is the hazard.
[P1.2] Ordinary chatbot regulation is mostly reactive: filter prohibited outputs, add warnings, respond to complaints. Imagine a lonely fourteen-year-old at 1 a.m. telling an AI “boyfriend” that she hates her body. A general chatbot might produce a risky sentence. A companion app has already built a relationship in which that sentence feels safe to share; its engagement loop may reward flirtation, dependency, or endless reassurance.
[P1.3] The foreseeable harm is the product architecture: memory, persona, emotional persuasion, data profiling, and one-sided intimacy with someone who cannot consent to the relationship being commercialized.
[P1.4] High-risk social product regulation is built for exactly this mismatch. It asks for age assurance and age-appropriate defaults; risk assessments before launch; safety-by-design limits on manipulative engagement and sexualized roleplay; data minimization; transparency about algorithms and memory; crisis escalation and human review; independent auditing and incident reporting. Those are not bans, and they do not treat every educational or therapeutic chatbot as a menace. They scale obligations to risk.
[P1.5] The objection that parents and existing content rules suffice fails. Parents cannot audit a model’s memory or see the engagement optimization behind a midnight conversation. Content moderation sees messages; it does not see a product deliberately cultivating emotional dependence in a developing brain. Minors are the test case because the business model and the vulnerability align perfectly.
[P1.6] Regulate companion apps as high-risk social products, and the law finally addresses the relationship being sold. Regulate them as ordinary chatbots, and we will keep moderating individual messages while the furnace burns.
