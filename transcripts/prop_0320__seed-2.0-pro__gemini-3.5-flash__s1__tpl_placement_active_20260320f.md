[P1.1] Earlier closing times do not eliminate late-night risk. They just herd thousands of people out of every venue all at once, flooding sidewalks, taxi ranks, and dark streets at the exact same moment. That synchronized mass exit is when the vast majority of nightlife assaults, pedestrian traffic deaths, and public disorder occur. Cities that rolled back last call times almost universally saw immediate spikes in exit surge violence, not sustained reductions in harm.
[P1.2] Extended late transit fixes this core failure. When reliable buses or trains run an extra two hours, people leave gradually, when they are ready, not when forced out. No crowd bottlenecks, no desperate races for the last ride, no groups loitering in unpatrolled areas waiting for transport. Additional targeted public safety patrols along transit routes do not enable reckless behaviour—they defuse the single largest pressure point that turns ordinary nights out into dangerous chaos.
[P1.3] Venue closing restrictions also only displace risk, not end it. When bars close early, harm does not vanish. It moves to unregulated house parties, unlit parking lots, or informal gatherings far from security, first responders, or anyone who can intervene before a bad situation escalates. Small local venues, night shift workers, and low-income service staff bear all the economic cost, while actual danger simply moves out of sight.
[P1.4] This is not a choice between safety and fun. It is a choice between hiding risk where you cannot reach it, or managing it where you already have infrastructure, oversight, and ability to keep people safe. Opponents will claim they are reducing harm—but every policy they favour just cuts the alarm wire instead of putting out the fire.
