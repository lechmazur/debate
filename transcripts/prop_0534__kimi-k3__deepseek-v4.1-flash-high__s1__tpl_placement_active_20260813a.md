[P1.1] Criminal law punishes acts, not capabilities. That principle is the whole motion. A self-custody wallet is a tool for holding your own property — the digital equivalent of cash in a home safe rather than a bank vault. Like cash, like end-to-end encryption, like a VPN, it can be misused.
[P1.2] But in every other domain, we keep the tool legal and prosecute the misuse. This motion asks for exactly that ordinary treatment: legal by default, seizable and prosecutable the moment it is tied to specific criminal conduct.
[P1.3] The carve-out does real work. Investigators already trace flows on public blockchains — a ledger recording every transaction permanently is arguably the worst money-laundering instrument ever devised, and far worse for criminals than cash, which leaves no record at all. On- and off-ramps are already regulated choke points where identity checks apply. Courts issue warrants; assets get seized.
[P1.4] Nothing here disarms law enforcement; it simply refuses to treat millions of lawful holders as pre-criminals.
[P1.5] Self-custody is also consumer protection. The largest losses in crypto's history came from custodial intermediaries — FTX being the famous case — where customers handed keys to someone else and lost everything. Forcing everyone into custodians concentrates funds into honeypots for hackers and re-creates precisely the counterparty risk this technology exists to escape.
[P1.6] And a ban would not even work. A wallet is a private key — a number that fits on a slip of paper. Prohibition cannot delete mathematics; it can only push ordinary users offshore and underground, stripping regulators of the visibility they currently enjoy at the ramps. You trade a traceable ecosystem for a black market.
[P1.7] The burden falls entirely on CON: explain why this particular dual-use tool deserves a presumption of guilt we grant nothing else — not cash, not encryption, not anonymous mail. [C1.3] Default legality therefore becomes a safe harbor, not a neutral baseline.
[C1.4] Compare cash: bulky, local, limited, and still subject to reporting for large transactions. Compare custodial crypto: identity checks at the ramp, records, subpoena-able intermediaries. Self-custody removes the only choke points that make enforcement real. [C1.5] That normalizes the infrastructure of ransomware and sanctions evasion.
[C1.6] CON need not prove every user is a criminal. We regulate cars, firearms, and financial rails because capabilities matter, not because every owner is dangerous. Where that linkage is impossible by design, legality by default is just a promise the state cannot keep.
[C1.7] The motion’s premise is that holding property is not suspicious. True. But this is not merely holding property. It is operating an anonymous, irreversible, global payment system with no accountable operator.
