[P1.1] Exporting electronic waste to lower-income countries is toxic colonialism that lets high-consumption societies offload the dirtiest half of their discarded devices. [P1.2] A ban on such exports, plus a mandate for domestic recycling or certified regional processing under enforceable standards, ends the escape hatch. Originating countries must internalize the true cost of their consumption. Certification blocks the race to the bottom: only sites meeting labor, emissions, and tracking rules handle the stream.
[P1.3] The profit motive flips—from cheapest dump to cleaner recovery and better product design that lasts longer and dismantles safely.

This is not about keeping materials “at home” for protectionist reasons; it is about preventing the deliberate transfer of irreversible harm onto communities least able to absorb it.
[P1.5] Countries that generate the waste retain the duty to manage it without exporting poison.
