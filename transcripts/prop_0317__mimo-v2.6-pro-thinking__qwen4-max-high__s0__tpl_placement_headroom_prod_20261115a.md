[P1.1] The resale market for live events is not a natural market. It is a market built on top of a primary sale that dominant platforms control — and then left deliberately unconstrained so the same firms can collect fees on both legs of the transaction. When a platform sells a ticket and profits again from its resale at five times face value, the platform has no incentive to stop scalping and every incentive to feed it. That is the conflict of interest at the heart of this motion, and it is why voluntary fixes have failed.
[P1.2] Note what the proposition actually asks: require major platforms to cap markups **or** provide face-value resale channels **for most tickets**. This is the floor of reasonable consumer protection, not a price-control maximalism. A face-value transfer channel costs almost nothing to operate — the platform already holds the ticket in a digital ledger, already verifies the buyer, already controls transfer permissions. The technology exists; the will does not, because resale fees are lucrative.
[P1.3] Second, allocation. The standard story is that resale routes tickets to whoever values them most. In practice resale routes tickets to whoever can pay the inflated price fastest, often resellers armed with automation buying at the moment of release. That is not preference revelation; it is a queue-jumping tax.
[P1.4] Fans who lose out are not indifferent — they are the audience that sustains tours, merchandise, and future ticket demand. Artists repeatedly complain that their fans are priced out of their own shows, which tells you the resale market is extracting value from the event rather than adding it.
[P1.5] Third, the harms are concrete and asymmetric. Families face price spikes days before a show, with no real substitute once travel is booked. Fraudulent and speculative listings concentrate in unregulated resale. A face-value channel gives a legitimate exit to anyone who genuinely cannot attend, without handing scalpers the field.
[P1.6] The burden on CON is heavy: defend a system where the platform profits from the scarcity it could cheaply prevent. [clipped]
