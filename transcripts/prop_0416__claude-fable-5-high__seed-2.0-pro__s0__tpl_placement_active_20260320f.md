[P1.1] A woman in her late thirties walks past a subway ad showing a smiling thirty-year-old and the words "press pause on your biological clock." What that ad will not tell her is the single most decision-relevant fact in reproductive medicine: that the odds of a frozen egg becoming a living baby depend overwhelmingly on the age at which it was frozen, and that for women freezing at 38 or 40, each egg carries only a modest chance of ever producing a child.
[P1.2] Many women would need multiple retrieval cycles, tens of thousands of dollars, and would still face meaningful odds of failure. The motion asks only that this truth be displayed as prominently as the promise.
[P1.3] The case rests on three pillars. First, this is a medical intervention sold through consumer advertising, and the information gap between clinic and customer is enormous. Fertility math is counterintuitive: people reason "twenty eggs, surely one works," when attrition through thawing, fertilization, embryo development, and implantation cuts brutally at every stage. A market where sellers profit from optimism and buyers cannot independently verify the odds is the textbook case for mandated disclosure.
[P1.4] Second, the harm is uniquely irreversible. A woman who relies on the "insurance" framing may delay childbearing during her most fertile years, only to discover at 44 that her policy doesn't pay out. Unlike a bad mattress purchase, there is no refund, no second chance. Regulation should be strictest where mistakes cannot be undone.
[P1.5] Third, the remedy is modest and proven. We already require risk disclosure in pharmaceutical ads, APR disclosure in lending, and mileage-equivalent labels on cars. Nobody calls those paternalism; we call them honest markets. This motion bans nothing, shames no one, and removes no choice.
[P1.6] It simply requires that hope be sold alongside its actual price and probability.
[P1.7] If informed consent means anything, it must begin before the consultation—at the moment the advertisement creates the expectation.
