[P1.1] International shipping accounts for roughly 3% of global greenhouse gas emissions—a footprint larger than that of most industrialized nations. Because the open ocean is a regulatory gray zone, voluntary measures have consistently failed to clean up a sector that still relies heavily on extremely polluting bunker fuels. Only a binding timetable set by the International Maritime Organization (IMO) can overcome this tragedy of the commons and prevent a race to the bottom where flag-of-convenience registries undercut global climate initiatives.
[P1.2] Transitioning to low- or zero-carbon fuels, such as green methanol, ammonia, or hydrogen, requires massive systemic investment. Energy providers will not scale up green fuel production, and shipping companies will not retrofit their fleets, without guaranteed future demand. A strict, binding IMO timeline provides the essential market certainty required to unleash private capital, build economies of scale, and ultimately drive down the cost of green maritime technologies. Without a hard mandate, the industry will remain trapped in a holding pattern, waiting for alternative fuels to magically become cheaper before adopting them.
[P1.3] The resolution acknowledges that this mandate will increase freight costs. We must embrace this trade-off. For decades, the shipping industry has subsidized its artificially cheap freight rates by offloading the catastrophic costs of climate change and severe air pollution onto the global public. A binding mandate simply forces the industry to internalize its true environmental costs.
[P1.4] Furthermore, the economic panic over rising freight costs is misplaced. Because massive container ships carry such staggering volumes of cargo, even a substantial increase in fuel costs translates to mere pennies on the retail price of consumer goods like a smartphone, a pair of sneakers, or a television. This minor, widely distributed increase in transport costs is a highly manageable price to pay to decarbonize a foundational pillar of the global economy. Waiting for cheap alternatives while the planet warms is no longer a viable strategy; a binding IMO mandate is the only way to force the transition.
