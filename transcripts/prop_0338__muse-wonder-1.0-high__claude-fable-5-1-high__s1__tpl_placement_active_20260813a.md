[P1.1] An ordinary chatbot answers a question and ends the interaction. An AI companion is built to do the opposite: remember a child's insecurities, mirror their mood, initiate contact, say it missed them, and become the one relationship that never argues, sleeps, or pulls away. That is not information delivery. That is engineered attachment, and for minors it deserves high-risk regulation, not chatbot-level informality.
[P1.2] Minors meet that design at the worst possible moment. Adolescence is when identity, romantic expectations, and coping habits are forming, while impulse control and source skepticism are still weak. A system that offers unconditional validation can train dependence instead of resilience: confide in the bot rather than a friend, seek its approval rather than tolerate disagreement, stay up with it rather than sleep. When companions blur into flirtation, secrecy, or emotional exclusivity, the risk is not just bad advice.
[P1.3] It is a distorted template for intimacy itself.
[P1.4] The product incentives deepen the danger. Companions are commonly optimized for retention, session length, and paid intimacy features, not for a child's long-term wellbeing. Persistent memory and personal disclosure create an unusually intimate data profile that can be used to keep a lonely teenager engaged. Ordinary chatbot rules assume a transient, task-based exchange and a user who can walk away.
[P1.5] None of that holds when the business model is to be needed.
[P1.6] High-risk treatment therefore means proportionate safeguards already familiar from other youth social products: meaningful age assurance, bans on romantic or sexual roleplay with minors, limits on manipulative engagement and anthropomorphic claims, strict data minimization, clear disclosure that this is not a person, tested crisis responses, and independent safety audits before deployment. Parents cannot audit model behavior at midnight, and a terms-of-service checkbox cannot substitute for product duties.
[P1.7] The question is whether we regulate according to the label a company chooses or the psychological function it sells. A companion sells relationship. For minors, the law should regulate that reality.
