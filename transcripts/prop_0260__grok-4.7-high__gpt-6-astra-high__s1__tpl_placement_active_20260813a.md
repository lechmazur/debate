[C1.5] An opt-out does not eliminate that effect: PRO’s own case depends on defaults strongly shaping behavior. We cannot count that power as the policy’s benefit while ignoring its consequences for funding.

[C1.7] There is a more defensible legal boundary: restrict nonconsensual tracking directly, require meaningful privacy controls, and let users choose broader ad blocking. Publishers have no entitlement to anyone’s attention. But neither does opposition to surveillance establish a case for legally prescribing ad suppression.
