[C1.1] Guaranteeing universities and employers a migration quota, then capping it by local housing and services, turns a national need for talent into a postcode lottery administered by the slowest part of the state.
[C1.2] Capacity sounds objective, but it is not. Housing completions lag years behind demand, GP lists and school places are counted differently across councils, and transport strain fluctuates by season. Tie visas to those noisy, backward-looking indicators and you get a formula always out of date. A hospital facing winter shortages or a lab racing a competitor cannot wait for a housing audit to unlock staff needed months ago.
[C1.3] The guarantee makes that rigidity worse. Once promised, large incumbent universities and big employers acquire something close to a property right. They will lobby to protect it and hoard places they may not need, while a new college, fast-growing care provider, or startup starts with little or nothing. Migration then rewards size and political muscle, not quality, clinical need, or economic value.
[C1.4] The local tie also inverts priorities. A world-class course in a dense, expensive city — where students want to study and graduate employers cluster — is throttled because housing is tight, while spare rooms in a town with weaker demand unlock places few want. A specialist nurse for an overstretched urban hospital is blocked by the very pressure she would relieve, while a less urgent hire elsewhere proceeds. The metric punishes need instead of serving it.
[C1.5] Worst of all, local bottlenecks become national vetoes. Councils that refuse homes or underfund services can effectively set immigration policy for everyone, even when the national economy needs those workers. And when unemployment rises or demand falls, the state has promised places it should be free to reconsider.
[C1.6] Flexibility, transparent national criteria, and obligations on recruiters to contribute to housing are workable. A guaranteed quota chained to lagging local capacity is not management; it is misallocation with a reassuring label.
