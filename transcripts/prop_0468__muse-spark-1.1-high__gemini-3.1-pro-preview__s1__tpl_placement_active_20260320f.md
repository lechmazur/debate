[P1.1] An anonymous shell company is a legal fiction that exists to break finance's most basic rule: know who you are dealing with. It has no office, no staff, no product. Its sole function is to sever the link between an asset and its human owner. No legitimate economy needs that severance from regulators.
[P1.2] That severance is the infrastructure for every financial crime we claim to fight. Cartels launder cash through layers of BVI and Delaware LLCs. Kleptocrats loot public budgets then buy London real estate through nameless vehicles. Sanctioned Russian oligarchs kept yachts and bank access after 2022 using the same trick.
[P1.3] The Panama Papers and the 1MDB scandal didn't show abuse of an otherwise useful tool; they showed the tool working as designed. Anonymous shells make crime invisible, prosecution impossible, and recovery of stolen wealth hopeless. The cost lands hardest on poor countries bled by illicit flows they cannot trace.
[P1.4] Piecemeal reform fails because secrecy is mobile. If Britain requires owners, money flies to a haven that doesn't. That is why secrecy has concentrated in a few jurisdictions that sell anonymity as a product. Only a universal rule — no company can be formed, keep a bank account, or own property without a verified ultimate beneficial owner on file with regulators — ends the race to the bottom.
[P1.5] The UK register and EU disclosure directives already prove verification is technically trivial and cheap.
[P1.6] Legitimate privacy does not require anonymity from law enforcement. A founder, a victim hiding from an abuser, a firm in a sensitive merger can have ownership held confidentially by authorities and disclosed only under warrant or audit, not posted publicly. What no legitimate actor needs is a state-issued invisibility cloak that also hides from banks, tax agencies, and investigators. Banning anonymous shells doesn't ban limited liability or holding structures; it bans lying about who controls them.
