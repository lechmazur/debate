[P1.2] The proposition explicitly accepts the risk of retaliation, and we must embrace this risk because the alternative is systemic climate failure. Historically, when the EU establishes strict regulatory standards—from data privacy to chemical safety—global firms adapt to maintain market access.
[P1.3] This Brussels Effect dictates that enduring short-term trade friction will ultimately force our partners to decarbonize their own supply chains. Rather than suffer prolonged retaliation, trading partners will inevitably implement their own domestic carbon pricing to keep the revenue at home rather than surrendering it to Brussels.
[P1.4] Furthermore, the foundational industries of the twenty-first century are inherently green. By holding the line on a carbon border adjustment despite pushback, the EU protects its domestic green transition and accelerates global demand for clean technology. While retaliation might temporarily disrupt our legacy exports, the border adjustment secures our enduring leadership in the zero-carbon economy. We must lead, even when it provokes resistance.
[P1.5] We cannot sacrifice the stability of our climate and the future of our industrial base just to appease trading partners seeking a free ride on environmental degradation. The short-term pain of trade disputes is a vital, non-negotiable investment in long-term economic and planetary survival.
