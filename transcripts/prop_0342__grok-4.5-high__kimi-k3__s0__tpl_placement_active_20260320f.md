[P1.1] In a world where AI can forge a video of a world leader confessing crimes he never committed, or where feeds are engineered to hijack attention with outrage rather than truth, treating AI literacy and manipulation-resistant media literacy as optional is educational malpractice. These are not enrichment electives. They are foundational skills for autonomy, civic judgment, and survival in the information environment students already inhabit—and they must be treated as a core subject, mandatory and protected like reading or mathematics.
[P1.2] Optional modules fail the equity test immediately. The curious and supported take them; the students most vulnerable to deepfake scams, synthetic propaganda, algorithmic radicalization, and identity manipulation simply opt out. Core status is the only structure that guarantees every graduate can interrogate a generated image, detect synthetic voice, map how recommendation systems amplify falsehood, and resist emotional engineering designed at industrial scale.
[P1.3] The threat is not abstract. Generative tools already flood homework, news, social proof, and public debate. A student who cannot distinguish human from machine output, or who lacks frameworks for source provenance and incentive analysis, graduates unprepared for work, voting, or personal decision-making. Media literacy once meant spotting slanted headlines.
[P1.4] Today it must include zero-cost forgery, infinite content supply, and precision targeting of cognitive biases. Anything short of systematic, assessed instruction leaves the skill uneven and fragile.
[P1.5] Core designation forces dedicated time, teacher preparation, and standards instead of weekend workshops that vanish under testing pressure. Optional status telegraphs secondary importance and gets crowded out. We already watched financial and digital add-ons fail for exactly this reason. Elevate the defense: continuous instruction, not a one-off unit, so that critical evaluation becomes habitual rather than ornamental.
[P1.6] The alternative is a generation fluent in tools it cannot govern and news it cannot trust. Core treatment matches the severity of the threat. Optional treatment deliberately leaves children undefended.

[C1.3] Second, core subjects are permanent; this content expires. Standards, textbooks, and teacher pipelines take years to build, then freeze. Optional or integrated modules can pivot each term; a mandated core cannot.
[C1.4] That is institutionalized obsolescence dressed up as rigor.
[C1.5] Third, assessment corrupts the skill. Make it core and it gets tested; tested, it gets gamed. [C1.6] The equity argument cuts the other way too. Core status guarantees enrollment, not competence. Wealthy districts will build real courses; under-resourced districts—the very students PRO invokes—will stretch already-short staffs into compliance worksheets. [C1.7] The better instrument is integration: provenance in history, statistical skepticism in mathematics, rhetoric and evidence in English—durable, adaptable, no new silo required. Treating manipulation resistance as a core subject buys a permanent, test-gamed, fast-obsolescing placeholder. That is not defense; it is expensive reassurance.
