[P1.1] A large, across-the-board minimum wage hike is a blunt instrument swung at a deeply uneven economy. Sectoral bargaining and wage boards solve the central dilemma of wage policy: how to secure substantial pay raises where industries can afford them without destroying jobs in sectors that cannot.
[P1.2] Consider the economic reality of the United States. A major logistics hub, a corporate fast-food chain, and a rural independent retailer operate in completely different worlds. They have vastly different profit margins, labor shares of revenue, and abilities to pass costs onto consumers. A large across-the-board minimum wage increase ignores these differences.
[P1.3] If lawmakers set a single flat rate high enough to capture the wealth of profitable sectors, they inevitably price workers out of low-margin, labor-intensive businesses. Sectoral wage boards prevent these job losses by calibrating wage floors to the actual financial capacity and price elasticity of each specific industry.
[P1.4] Second, sectoral systems protect employment by leveraging insider knowledge and leveling the competitive playing field. When a board brings an industry’s employers and workers together, participants know where the actual breaking points are. Because the resulting standard applies to every direct competitor within that specific market, no firm fears being undercut by a low-road rival. That industry-wide floor allows smooth absorption through reduced turnover and modest, uniform price adjustments rather than layoffs.
[P1.5] Moreover, unlike a flat statutory mandate, sectoral boards can tie higher pay tiers to industry-specific training and skill standards, boosting worker productivity to help fund the raises.
[P1.6] Finally, sectoral boards are dynamically adaptable. Legislative minimum wage campaigns produce rigid, politically timed spikes that cannot react if one part of the economy falters. Wage boards include direct employer representation and monitor their sectors continuously, pacing phase-ins to weather industry-specific shocks and protect jobs. Tailored economic precision beats a blind, one-size-fits-all mandate.
