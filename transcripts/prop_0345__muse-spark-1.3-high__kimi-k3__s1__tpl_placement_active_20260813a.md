[P1.1] A mandate that tells a single parent to add ten hours of commuting a week, pay for childcare, or quit is not a vibe decision. For large employers, return-to-office orders reshape housing, caregiving, disability access, and who can stay in the workforce. Power on that scale demands reasons on that scale — tied to the work actually being done.
[P1.2] Broad appeals to culture cannot provide that. Culture is real, but as a justification it is unfalsifiable. It explains everything and therefore nothing: the same word is used to require three days, then five, to cover downtown teams and remote coders alike. When a reason cannot be tested, it cannot be challenged, improved, or distinguished from pretext — like filling expensive leases or reasserting managerial control.
[P1.3] Workers are left to obey a slogan.
[P1.4] Requiring role-specific business reasons fixes the mismatch between blanket rule and varied work. A lab technician who handles physical samples, a retail floor lead, and a claims analyst answering tickets do not share the same need for presence. An employer that must say which tasks require co-location, how often, and why will discover what good managers already know: some roles thrive on in-person coaching and client contact, others lose focus and time to forced commutes. That discipline produces smarter hybrid schedules instead of empty offices full of people on video calls.
[P1.5] This is a modest check, not a ban on offices. Large firms have HR departments, workforce data, and scheduling tools; asking them to write down why a role needs to be present is far less burdensome than asking thousands of employees to reorganize their lives on faith. If presence truly improves mentoring, sales, safety, or service, that case can be made — and workers, courts, and the public can respect it.

The value of any one person showing up depends on others showing up. A rule demanding each role be justified in isolation systematically excludes the true answer — like asking each musician to explain why the orchestra needs them, specifically, in one room, together.
[C1.3] The mandate isn't arbitrary; the atomization is.

[C1.6] The motion duplicates it with a paperwork regime that punishes candor and rewards carefully lawyered job descriptions, while doing nothing for the single parent except giving her employer's lawyers a new form to file.
