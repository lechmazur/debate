[P1.1] Pretrial liberty is real, but it is not absolute. Courts already restrain liberty to stop credible harm: stay-away orders, quarantine, mental-health holds. The novelty here is not the goal—public protection before trial—but the evidence: validated instruments that flag serious violent reoffending, not merely any rearrest. When a tested tool says a person already charged with violence is likely to kill or maim again, the state's first duty is to prevent that irreversible harm.
[P1.2] Validated tools need not be perfect to be transformative; they replace a single judge's erratic intuition with structured evidence that can be audited, challenged, and corrected.
[P1.3] This approach accepts a hard price: detention rates will rise. That price is justified because the correct metric is not how many people are held, but how many funerals are prevented. Pretrial detention is regulation pending trial, not punishment; it carries counsel, a hearing, and a clock toward trial, so its hardship is bounded even when keenly felt. The errors are asymmetrical.
[P1.4] A false positive costs liberty, which is grave but temporary, reviewable, and bounded by speedy-trial rights; a false negative can cost a life, and no later ruling restores a victim. Monitoring does not physically stop a determined attacker; it only documents the tragedy.
[P1.5] Picture a defendant arrested for aggravated assault with a prior strangulation case and a gun charge, scored high-risk. Under today's caution, he is released with electronic monitoring because detention feels too aggressive. Weeks later the victim is dead; the monitoring merely recorded the route to the crime. Broader authority plus validated tools would have interrupted that path.
[P1.6] Algorithmic bias is real, but refusing tools does not protect defendants; it hides discretion behind a robe, where bias is harder to see and fix. A validated tool can be tested for disparate impact and recalibrated; unchecked intuition cannot. Audit the tool, confine it to serious violence, and detain the dangerous few.
