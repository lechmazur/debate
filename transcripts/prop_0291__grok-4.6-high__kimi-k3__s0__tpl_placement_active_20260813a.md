That is an identity layer welded onto general-purpose computing. My opponent insists this “is not a surveillance state” [P1.4].
[C1.2] But the mechanism that proves to an app that this user is fourteen is the same mechanism that can prove to a government who is holding the phone. You cannot order a bypass-proof cage for minors without the bars existing for everyone.
[C1.3] The technical premise is also backwards. Recommendation happens on company servers, not on the device. An OS toggle can only transmit an age claim and hope apps honor it—the suggestion PRO mocks—or compel verified age checks on everyone, meaning every teenager, and soon every adult, must prove their age to scroll. And “no for-you feed” is not the “neutral library” PRO imagines [P1.6]; ranking is also what buries the worst content.
[C1.4] Switch it off and you get the firehose, not the shelf.
[C1.5] “Teenagers” spans thirteen to seventeen. The motion hands one parent a unilateral, appeal-proof kill switch over an entire information diet—no proportionality, no recourse. For the teen in a controlling or abusive home, the device is often the lifeline: the support community, the quiet search for help. This motion gives the person they may most need protection from a tamper-proof lock.
[C1.6] Worst, it fails on its own terms. One browser app contains the entire web; a friend’s phone is not locked. The teen routes around, learns concealment instead of judgment, and at eighteen meets the full-strength machine with zero practiced resistance. You do not teach swimming by banning water until adulthood.
