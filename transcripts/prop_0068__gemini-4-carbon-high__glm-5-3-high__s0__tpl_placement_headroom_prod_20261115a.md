[P1.1] Labeling AI-generated content without labeling AI-optimized targeting regulates the puppet while hiding the puppeteer. Suppose a political operation or predatory lender hires human writers to draft fifty emotional appeals, then uses machine-learning optimization to analyze behavioral trails and serve the exact fear-inducing variant most likely to sway a specific user. Under generation-only rules, that campaign carries no disclosure, even though AI performed the decisive manipulative work: inferring vulnerability and matching the pitch to the target. Modern digital persuasion depends far less on whether a machine drafted the sentence than on algorithmic systems engineered to exploit who bends to which message.
[P1.2] Requiring major platforms to label AI-optimized persuasion campaigns remedies two serious harms. First, it restores individual agency against asymmetric manipulation. When optimization models infer a person's financial anxiety, health fears, or partisan resentments and continuously tune outreach until resistance drops, the user faces a covert behavioral trap. A concise label informs people that a message was algorithmically tailored to persuade them or their micro-group, engaging conscious skepticism without censoring a single word.
[P1.3] Second, labeling protects democratic and market accountability. Open persuasion happens in daylight, where misleading or contradictory claims invite counter-speech. Micro-targeted AI campaigns fracture the public square, letting actors whisper incompatible promises to isolated micro-groups in the dark. Visible labels expose that segmentation to citizens, watchdogs, and competitors.
[P1.4] This requirement is also workable because it governs major platforms and organized persuasion campaigns, not ordinary search results or baseline feed ranking. Major platforms control the primary pipeline of micro-persuasion, directly operating predictive audience clustering, automated creative testing, and conversion-optimized delivery while monitoring coordinated influence drives. Because platforms already log when campaigns deploy algorithmic micro-targeting, attaching a clear label demands transparency, not technical reinvention. Labeling synthetic content while hiding synthetic persuasion leaves the deepest manipulation untouched.
