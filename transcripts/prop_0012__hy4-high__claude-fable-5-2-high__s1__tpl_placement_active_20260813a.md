[P1.1] Human remains are not specimens that happen to be culturally sensitive; they are persons whose bodies were often made into specimens through conquest. A skeleton in a museum drawer is somebody's ancestor, removed from a burial ground, battlefield, hospital, or prison and kept by an institution with power to say no. Return is not a reward for proving kinship with perfect paperwork; it is repair for a taking that should never have been treated as property.
[P1.2] Return requires no contempt for science, only the right ordering of claims. Research value is a possible public benefit; custody of a relative is a claim of dignity, kinship, and self-determination. A living community should not keep litigating its grief while a university adds another measurement. If a DNA or isotope study might matter, ask the community and work by consent, with limited protocols and preserved data; do not make continued possession the price of an ancestor's release.
[P1.3] Research performed because the holder can refuse return is coercion by delay.
[P1.4] The research exception would swallow the rule. Researchers can almost always name future value: better scans, ancient pathogens, migration histories, methods not yet invented. If "valuable continued scientific study" can pause return, every collection becomes temporarily permanent, and Indigenous and colonized communities bear the cost of an indefinitely expanding agenda. The institution decides what counts as value; it gains publications, prestige, and control while invoking universal knowledge.
[P1.5] That is a conflict of interest, not neutral stewardship. Prompt return moves museums from owners to borrowers. They can catalog, publish data, and propose collaborative studies with deadlines; they cannot keep human beings as leverage for possible knowledge. Scientific questions can be postponed, shared, or answered another way; a community's authority over its dead should not be held hostage to a lab schedule.
[P1.6] A university that needs someone else's ancestors to conduct ethical research has already described the problem. Return first, then study only by permission.
