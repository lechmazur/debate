[P1.1] When a popular concert or playoff game goes on sale today, thousands of fans wait in digital queues only to watch tickets vanish in seconds—and immediately reappear on the exact same platform for triple the price. This is not a healthy market discovering true value. It is parasitic arbitrage driven by a perverse incentive: major ticketing platforms collect fees on the initial sale, and then collect even larger, percentage-based fees when that same ticket is resold at an inflated price.
[P1.2] Because platforms profit from the markup, they have every reason to indulge commercial scalpers and no reason to protect fans.
[P1.3] Requiring major platforms to cap resale markups or provide face-value resale channels for most tickets fixes this broken market at its root. First, it breaks the business model of industrial scalping while preserving genuine flexibility. Speculators and ticket-buying bots add zero value to live events; they do not play an instrument, build a stadium, or take a financial risk on an unproven tour. They simply manufacture scarcity to extract rent.
[P1.4] When resale is capped or routed through a face-value exchange, the financial incentive to hoard inventory evaporates. Real fans get tickets at the primary box office, and fans whose plans change still have a safe, liquid way to recoup their costs.
[P1.5] Second, this requirement restores pricing power to the people who actually create the event. Artists, leagues, and promoters frequently price tickets below what the wealthiest buyers would pay in order to cultivate loud, socioeconomically diverse, lifelong fanbases. In an uncapped system, an artist’s effort to keep shows affordable does not help fans; it merely hands a larger windfall to brokers.
[P1.6] Finally, regulating major platforms is uniquely enforceable because modern ticketing is no longer paper-based. Tickets today rely on rotating digital barcodes locked inside dominant platform apps. Requiring those platforms to enforce capped markups or face-value exchanges within their own ecosystems stops predatory middlemen and returns live events to the fans.
