[P1.1] Literacy has never been optional when a new communication technology reshapes who can speak, what can be forged, and how fast a lie spreads. That is the present condition. [P1.2] Treating AI literacy and manipulation-resistant media literacy as an optional module is like offering reading as an after-school club: the students most exposed to harm are least likely to choose the defense, and the damage happens before they know they were unarmed.
[P1.3] Optional status also widens inequality. Digitally privileged students get the skill at home or in electives, while students with less support remain targets for scams, synthetic-image exploitation, polarizing feeds, and manipulated peer drama. [P1.4] This capacity is not a vocational extra; it is a condition for every other subject. A history class can be derailed by a forged primary source, a science class by AI-generated notes, and a school community by a fabricated recording of a teacher or student. [P1.5] That requires practice, not exposure.
[P1.6] The crowded-curriculum objection cuts the other way. If time is scarce, schools should prioritize capacities that make all other learning possible. This is the educational equivalent of vaccination.
[P1.7] A vaccine left optional fails at the point of epidemic, and it fails along existing fault lines. The guaranteed defense is the curriculum.

[C1.5] Universal protection does not require a new core subject. Schools can require modules inside existing classes, assemblies and tutor time for scams and image abuse, and targeted help for vulnerable pupils, all updated yearly without displacing foundations.
