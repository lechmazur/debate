[P1.1] Reading is not a natural cognitive process like speaking; it must be explicitly and systematically taught. [P1.2] First, the cognitive science of reading is settled. We have seen this repeatedly with the widespread use of "three-cueing," a practice where early readers are taught to guess words based on pictures or context rather than sounding them out.
[P1.3] We cannot permit scientifically discredited practices to flourish under the guise of teacher freedom.
[P1.4] Second, structured reading instruction is a fundamental issue of educational equity. Curriculum autonomy creates an unacceptable instructional lottery. This disparity disproportionately harms our most vulnerable students.
[P1.5] Children from affluent families often survive poor reading instruction because they have access to private tutoring and print-rich homes. [P1.6] Finally, prioritizing structured instruction removes an unfair burden from our educators. Asking individual early-childhood teachers to simultaneously act as curriculum designers, cognitive researchers, and literacy specialists is an impossible expectation and a recipe for burnout.
