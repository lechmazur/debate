[P1.2] My case rests on three pillars.
[P1.3] First, ownership means ownership. We have a centuries-old principle that when you buy a physical good, you own its capabilities. A subscription to unlock seat warmers already installed inverts this. [P1.4] That is not a service; it is rent-seeking on your own possessions.
[P1.5] Second, the economics are a con. The buyer pays for the hardware at purchase—it ships in every vehicle whether or not it's "activated." Then the buyer pays again, monthly, to flip a software switch. The marginal cost of letting you use installed hardware is essentially zero.

[P1.7] Third, durability and fairness. Software subscriptions can be raised, bundled, or discontinued at the company's whim. A feature you relied on can vanish in a firmware update, or when the automaker decides the service is no longer profitable. [P1.8] Permanent access protects the consumer, the used-car market, and basic predictability.
[P1.9] The clash here is straightforward: does paying for a thing entitle you to use it? I say yes. My opponent must defend charging you twice for one piece of hardware—and explain why that isn't simply a toll booth installed on property you already own.
