[C1.2] Sea-level rise and extreme heat almost never displace people in isolation. Migration is mixed: saltwater intrusion, debt, crop prices, local governance, family networks, and storms interact. A legal right would force adjudicators to decide which claimant’s hardship is sufficiently climate-caused. Two families in the same delta may face similar risk, yet one has documents proving lost land and the other cannot separate environmental decline from poverty.
[C1.3] The promised limitation becomes arbitrary line-drawing, and every hard case pushes the threshold outward.
[C1.4] Heat claims are worse. Unlivability depends on housing, water, health care, labor rules, and adaptive capacity, not temperature alone. Courts or officials would have to declare whole regions uninhabitable, second-guess local adaptation, and rank suffering across countries. That converts asylum from protection against persecution into a broad remedy for environmental inequality.
[C1.5] It also diverts attention from cheaper adaptation—cooling centers, work-hour limits, water management—that can make heat survivable. It risks eroding public support for the refugee system precisely when conventional refugees still need it.
[C1.6] PRO frames the choice as sealed borders or a legal right. That is false. States can already build practical protection: temporary disaster visas, humanitarian parole, planned relocation agreements, regional labor mobility, and resettlement tied to objective indicators. These measures can be capped, reviewed, and shared among neighboring states.
[C1.7] They address displacement without creating a durable legal status that states may fear as uncontrollable. A named asylum right gives opponents a simple target and can harden borders.
[C1.8] The better route is targeted, revocable, cooperative protection—not a new right that invites litigation, inconsistent standards, and political backlash. A right also encourages claimants to arrive first and assert status later, undermining orderly planning. Oppose the motion because it makes protection less workable, not more humane.
