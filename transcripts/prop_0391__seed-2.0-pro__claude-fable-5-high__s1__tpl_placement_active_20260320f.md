[C1.4] First, aggressive enforcement at scale means machines, not judgment. No company hand-reviews fifty million daily chat messages. [C1.5] Innocent players eating bans is not a hypothetical edge case; it's the predictable mechanics of automation under pressure to punish more.
[C1.6] Second, aggressive enforcement hands harassers a weapon. When penalties trigger fast and reviews are shallow, mass false-reporting becomes the meta. Griefers already report-brigade players they dislike — streamers, women on voice chat, anyone winning. Stricter enforcement makes that brigade more lethal, converting the moderation system itself into a harassment tool.
[C1.7] Third, the real solution is player power, not platform aggression. Mute, block, opt-in voice, configurable filters, avoid-as-teammate lists — these let each player set their own line instantly, with zero false positives. A woman who mutes a lobby is protected the moment she clicks; a woman waiting on a moderation queue is not.
[C1.8] The chilling effect is real too: when the cost of an ambiguous joke is your account, the rational move is silence — and in team games, communication is gameplay. We're offered safety; we'd receive quieter games, weaponized reporting, and the same determined abusers on fresh accounts.
