[P1.1] For a very young child, reading a story is not primarily about hearing words. It is about learning that words come from someone who is with her. She points at the bear, mispronounces it, asks why he is sad, drifts off topic, and the adult pauses, follows her gaze, answers the actual child in front of him, and returns to the page. That messy, contingent back-and-forth is how vocabulary, attention, narrative, and trust are built together.
[P1.2] An AI companion or smart speaker can produce a smooth story in a pleasant voice. What it cannot do is share attention in the same way. It does not truly notice the hesitation before a question, the body tensing at a scary page, or the word the child is trying out for the first time. It responds to prompts, not to a relationship.
[P1.3] When that response routinely replaces a parent's, the child gets more output and less interaction — fluency without attunement.
[P1.4] Bedtime makes the cost clearest. The routine works because it is boring, familiar, and embodied: the same caregiver, lowered voice, physical closeness, gradual winding down, reassurance after a bad dream. A device is designed to be available, responsive, and engaging on demand. Even without a screen, a novel voice, endless story generator, or conversational loop can stretch arousal rather than settle it, and it teaches the child to seek a machine, not a person, at the moment of vulnerability.
[P1.5] This is about routine outsourcing, not an occasional story while a parent cooks. Occasional help preserves the parent as the anchor. Routine substitution changes the default. Parents get less practice reading their child's cues, children get fewer chances to learn real conversation — interruption, misunderstanding, repair — and intimate minutes that build attachment are transferred to a product built to retain attention.
[P1.6] A young child does not need perfect pronunciation or infinite stories. She needs a known voice that stops, listens, and answers her. That cannot be delegated without losing the thing the routine was for.
