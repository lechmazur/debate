[P1.1] A supreme court possesses neither purse nor sword; its authority rests entirely on public trust. Life tenure corrodes that trust by turning constitutional governance into an actuarial lottery. One president may appoint three justices in four years while another appoints none in eight. Justices strategically time retirements to favor friendly administrations, and appointers select ever-younger ideological combatants to lock in partisan control for decades.
[P1.2] Long, staggered, non-renewable fixed terms—typically twelve to eighteen years—restore democratic legitimacy. Regular appointments ensure the court reflects the electorate's sustained democratic will rather than accidents of mortality. Every presidential term yields an equal, predictable number of seats. Because no single vacancy locks in the law for half a century, confirmation battles cease to be existential partisan warfare, rehabilitating the court as a neutral arbiter rather than a captured political prize.
[P1.3] This immense legitimacy gain comes at virtually no cost to judicial independence. Critics conflate fixed terms with short, renewable terms. When a term is long and strictly non-renewable—the proven model in respected constitutional courts like Germany’s—a sitting justice has zero incentive to please the government. Politicians cannot reward or punish them with reappointment.
[P1.4] Nor does the fear of justices "auditioning" for post-court jobs hold up. Without the perverse incentive to pick forty-year-olds to maximize time on the bench, appointers can select seasoned, late-career jurists for whom a fifteen-year term is a capstone, not a stepping stone. Moreover, simple guardrails—guaranteed lifetime salaries, continued senior service on lower courts, and strict post-term employment bars—easily neutralize any financial motive to cater to future private employers.
[P1.5] Ultimately, democratic legitimacy is the prerequisite for judicial independence. When an unaccountable, life-tenured court drifts decades behind the public, it invites court-packing, jurisdiction-stripping, and open defiance. Fixed terms secure the democratic consent that shields the judiciary from political retaliation, delivering a massive gain in legitimacy while preserving independence where it matters most.
