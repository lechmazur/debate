[P1.1] A fabricated video of a candidate announcing they will rig the election, confessing to a crime, or ordering violence is not commentary. It is forged evidence, injected into the exact moment when citizens must decide who wields coercive power. Democracies already punish fraud, forgery, and false statements in narrowly defined electoral contexts — ballot tampering, fraudulent signatures, libel. Banning hyper-realistic political deepfakes extends that logic to the medium where deception now scales fastest.

The "marketplace of ideas" presumes a shared evidentiary baseline: that when we see a recording, it records something.

PRO’s claim that this “adds nothing to argument” [P1.3] erases that tradition.

[C1.6] The ban worsens the collapse PRO fears [P1.5]. A damaging real recording can be denounced as synthetic, removed pending review, and buried in an authenticity fight while the news cycle dies.
