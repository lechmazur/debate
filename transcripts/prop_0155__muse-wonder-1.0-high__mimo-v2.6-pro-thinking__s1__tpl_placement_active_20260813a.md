[P1.1] Real-time facial recognition turns every public camera into a permanent, suspicionless lineup. Unlike an officer glancing at a crowd, the system scans, identifies, and can log everyone who passes — commuters, worshippers, protesters, children — without any reason to suspect them of anything. That is not investigation. It is population monitoring, and it destroys the practical anonymity that lets people move, assemble, and speak in public without first clearing themselves with the state.
[P1.2] Prohibit that, but allow post-incident use with a warrant, and policing keeps a powerful tool inside constitutional guardrails. After a robbery, shooting, or kidnapping, investigators can bring specific footage from a specific crime to a judge, show probable cause, and search for the unknown suspect captured in that footage. The match is a lead to be corroborated, not a verdict, and the warrant creates a record of who was searched, why, and what was retained.
[P1.3] The risks are categorically different in real time. A mistaken match reviewed at a desk can be discarded after checking other evidence. A mistaken match on the street triggers an immediate stop, a detention, possibly drawn weapons. That danger is not evenly distributed: facial recognition has repeatedly performed worse on women and people of color, so live deployment turns known inaccuracy into split-second confrontations with the innocent.
[P1.4] Real-time use also cannot be warrant-contained. You cannot get individualized authorization to identify thousands of uninvolved people in hopes one matches. Once the network is built for violent felons, mission creep is almost inevitable — to outstanding warrants, shoplifting, immigration enforcement, or tracking protest organizers. The infrastructure itself invites expansion because the marginal cost of adding a watchlist is near zero.
[P1.5] This line is both principled and practical: targeted identification after a crime, with judicial approval, helps solve serious cases; continuous identification of everyone, with no suspicion at all, makes public space conditional. Ban the second, warrant the first.
