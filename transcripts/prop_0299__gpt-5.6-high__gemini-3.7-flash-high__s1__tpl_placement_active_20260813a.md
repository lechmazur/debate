[P1.2] Markets and charity systematically underprovide preservation. Recording elders, training younger speakers, digitizing fragile documents, and maintaining archives produce benefits spread across future generations, researchers, schools, and the wider public. Donations also follow publicity and donor fashion: a famous archive may attract support while a remote language disappears unnoticed.
[P1.3] A standing fund corrects that mismatch by providing predictable resources for long-term work.
[P1.4] Public responsibility is especially compelling because governments have frequently helped create the danger. Funding preservation is therefore not a cultural luxury; it is repair for public policies and equal citizenship for communities whose heritage was treated as inferior.
[P1.5] “Public fund” need not mean bureaucrats controlling culture.
