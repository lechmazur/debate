[C1.1] PRO creates a false dilemma: either mandatory charges are bundled into the sticker price of every burger and cocktail, or customers are ambushed at checkout. But there is an honest, practical standard that serves diners and workers far better: clear, prominent upfront disclosure of separate service charges directly on the menu.
[C1.2] Forcibly bundling mandatory service fees into baseline item prices causes three severe problems.
[C1.3] First, it destroys labor transparency. Restaurants increasingly adopt mandatory service charges not as stealth profit, but as dedicated, trackable funds for back-of-house equity, livable wages, and staff healthcare. When that charge is itemized and clearly stated on the menu, diners see exactly what supports labor versus gross food margins. Folding that fee into a single sticker price makes meals look arbitrarily inflated, obscures whether workers are genuinely receiving dedicated support, and creates paralyzing confusion over whether diners are still expected to tip.
[C1.4] Second, the proposition breaks down under conditional mandatory charges. In the real world, many mandatory service fees are context-specific—most notably, mandatory automatic gratuities for parties of six or more. Forcing every mandatory fee into the displayed item price is mathematically unworkable: it forces restaurants to either overcharge solo diners to absorb group costs or publish messy, multi-column menus that confuse everyone.
[C1.5] Third, line-item disclosure preserves operational clarity. Separating service charges from food costs allows restaurants to adapt transparently to shifting wage mandates and healthcare costs without disguising structural labor investments behind food inflation.
[C1.6] Diners absolutely deserve full cost clarity before they order. But prominent, upfront menu disclosure—clearly stating the service fee on the menu before a single plate is ordered—eliminates the surprise bill without destroying wage transparency, penalizing small tables, or imposing rigid, opaque pricing models.
