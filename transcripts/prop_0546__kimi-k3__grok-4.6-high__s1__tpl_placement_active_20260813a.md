[P1.1] Judge this motion against the real counterfactual, not an imaginary pristine press. The status quo is thousands of counties with no courthouse reporter, no one at the school board meeting, and a Facebook feed where the local paper used to be. That vacuum is the engine of both harms in this motion.
[P1.2] Polarization first. When local coverage dies, voters don't stop reading news — they switch to national outlets and social feeds that sort every issue into red versus blue. Research consistently finds that after local papers close, split-ticket voting drops and voters grow more party-line. A mayor's bad zoning deal becomes "Democrat bad" or "Republican bad" instead of a concrete local failure.
[P1.3] Local journalism depolarizes precisely because potholes, water mains, and school budgets aren't ideological; they give neighbors a shared set of facts to argue from.
[P1.4] Corruption second, and this is where the evidence is hardest to wave away. When the last watchdog paper in a county closes, municipal borrowing costs rise, public wage bills creep up, and procurement scandals surface later — because investors and prosecutors know no one is watching. Corruption is a crime of opportunity, and a reporter's notebook is cheap deterrence.
[P1.5] Now the alleged risk: capture. CON must show that firewalled funding — arms-length boards, statutory charters, multi-year appropriations insulated from annual politics — produces propaganda. The actual track record points the other way: countries with well-funded public media under charter governance sit near the top of every press-freedom and low-corruption index.
[P1.6] Meanwhile, "private" news is hardly capture-proof; it's captured by owners, advertisers, and outrage economics. The choice isn't independence versus state control. It's accountable, firewalled public funding versus billionaire patrons and algorithmic sludge.
[P1.7] Weigh the risks asymmetrically. News deserts' damage to polarization and corruption is documented, current, and compounding. Capture risk is conditional, designable-around, and policed by the plural private and nonprofit press that public funding supplements rather than replaces. PRO funds a watchdog and risks a flaw; CON keeps the vacuum and guarantees the rot.
