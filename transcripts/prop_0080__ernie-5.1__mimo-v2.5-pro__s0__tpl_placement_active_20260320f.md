[P1.2] First, collective bargaining creates predictable, enforceable standards. [P1.3] Second, collective bargaining reduces legal risk for schools. The current patchwork invites lawsuits—see the recent Northwestern case where athletes argued they are employees. A negotiated contract pre‑empts those challenges, giving institutions certainty and athletes security.

Extending the same framework to college sports aligns incentives: schools invest in athlete development because the return is shared, not hoarded.
[P1.5] Consider a mid‑major football program that pulls in $30 million a year from ticket sales and TV deals.
