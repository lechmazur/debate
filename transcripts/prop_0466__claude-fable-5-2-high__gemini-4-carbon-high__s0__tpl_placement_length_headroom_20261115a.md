[P1.4] First, large no-take reserves rebuild the capital the fishery lives on. Big, old females produce vastly more eggs than young fish, and only protected water lets fish grow old, and their offspring spill outward. [P1.6] Third, scale matters. Small reserves protect fish that simply swim out; only large ones shelter whole populations and are cheap to police, because any boat fishing inside is breaking the law.
[P1.7] Yes, the transition hurts, and governments must fund it. But transition costs are temporary and payable. Collapse is neither. Seventy percent stays open, and it stays worth fishing.
