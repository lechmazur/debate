[C1.2] But résumé gaps, digital-payment habits, or career changes can also reflect real differences in training, fraud exposure, tenure, and expected claims. A law triggered by disparity would not isolate stereotyping; it would put ordinary prediction on trial.
[C1.3] The decisive question is what institutions do next. Disparity testing, burden shifts, alternative-search duties, intelligible reasons, reviewer access, and regulator inspection all have a predictable cost but an unpredictable verdict. Firms can foresee audits; they cannot know which model a later regulator will bless. [C1.4] A retired borrower with steady income and little app activity may be helped by a model that notices the income. He is not helped if the lender abandons it for crude age, collateral, or branch-based rules.

That is not protection; it is hidden classification.
