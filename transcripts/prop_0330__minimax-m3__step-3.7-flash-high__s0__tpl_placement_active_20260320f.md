[C1.1] The proposition rests on a false premise: that live sports betting advertising is uniquely harmful compared to other gambling ads, justifying differential restriction. In reality, this policy would create arbitrary economic harm while failing to address actual problem gambling risks, and distracts from more effective, equitable regulatory solutions.
[C1.2] First, the claim that live sports broadcasts have a uniquely large minor audience is factually shaky. Nearly all regulated gambling jurisdictions already ban all gambling advertising—including lottery, casino, and poker ads—during any broadcast where minors make up more than 15 to 25 percent of the audience. Live professional sports broadcasts do not meet that threshold, with median viewer ages in the high 30s to mid 40s for major leagues. Singling out sports betting ads for extra restrictions does not protect minors more effectively; it simply redirects revenue away from sports leagues that fund youth athletics, with no corresponding public health benefit.
[C1.3] Second, the argument that live sports’ emotional atmosphere makes viewers uniquely vulnerable to impulsive betting ignores that all gambling advertising is engineered to trigger impulsive responses. A lottery ad run during a high-stakes reality show finale, or a casino ad during a tense drama, targets the same heightened emotional state PRO claims is exclusive to live sports. If the concern is emotional arousal, the rational response is to restrict all gambling ads during high-arousal content across all platforms, not to pick out sports betting as a scapegoat.
[C1.4] Third, the claim that sports betting ads corrupt the integrity of sport is backwards. Regulated sports betting partnerships come with mandatory integrity safeguards that ban betting on matches by players, coaches, and officials. Unregulated grey-market gambling—which already floods illegal streams of live sports with unvetted, aggressive ads—has no such safeguards. Harsher restrictions on legal sports betting ads will not eliminate gambling advertising from live sports broadcasts; it will just push more viewers to illegal platforms with no consumer protections or age verification.
[C1.5] This policy would harm legal stakeholders, fail to reduce problem gambling, and leave audiences worse off.
