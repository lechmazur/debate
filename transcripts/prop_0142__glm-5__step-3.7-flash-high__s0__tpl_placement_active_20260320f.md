[C1.1] The proposition sounds reasonable until you examine what it actually demands. A blanket prohibition on "heavy beauty retouching" and "AI-generated body enhancement" would require government censors to judge the artistic merit of every photo and video aimed at young audiences. Where does "heavy" retouching end and standard color correction begin? Is smoothing skin in a beauty ad illegal, while the same technique in a superhero film is protected as cinematic art?
[C1.2] These lines cannot be drawn without arbitrariness, chilling legitimate creative expression across fashion, beauty, and entertainment. Young viewers already encounter airbrushed perfection in PG-rated movies, video games, and sports photography. Choosing only influencer content and advertising as the target exposes the proposal as symbolic gesture politics rather than serious harm reduction.
[C1.3] The disclosure mandate fares no better. Requiring influencers to label "digital alteration" ignores how modern media production actually works. Every image involves layers of editing—lighting tweaks, color grading, cosmetic post-processing. A blunt requirement would either be meaningless or force creators to list every technical step, making content creation impossibly burdensome.
[C1.4] What would a disclosure look like on a twenty-second TikTok? The compliance costs fall disproportionately on independent creators while major studios breeze past similar rules.
[C1.5] The supposed "measurable psychological harm" is real but complex. Body dissatisfaction stems from myriad sources—peer dynamics, family environment, pre-existing mental health conditions. Isolating retouched advertising as the primary cause oversimplifies a nuanced public health problem. Rather than banning content, we should strengthen media literacy education, which has proven more effective at building resilience than censorship.
[C1.6] Most importantly, this is a content-based restriction on speech. Even regulating commercial speech aimed at minors requires the least restrictive means. A vague prohibition backed by an unworkable disclosure scheme fails that test. The solution to persuasive imagery is not silencing it—it's teaching young people to see through it.
