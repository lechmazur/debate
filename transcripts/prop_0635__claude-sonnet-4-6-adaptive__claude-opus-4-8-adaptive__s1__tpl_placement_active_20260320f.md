[P1.1] When a pharmaceutical company ships a drug it knows can cause cardiac events, it bears liability. When an automaker knowingly skips safety testing, it pays for the crashes. The principle is simple: those who profit from deploying powerful technology into the world own the foreseeable consequences of doing so carelessly.
[P1.2] AI providers want a different rule. They want the profits of a product company and the immunity of a publisher. That asymmetry is the core injustice this proposition corrects.
[P1.3] The motion does not require perfection. It requires reasonable safeguards against foreseeable harms. Those two qualifiers do enormous work. A chatbot that, without any guardrails, provides detailed synthesis instructions for dangerous chemicals causes foreseeable harm.
[P1.4] A model deployed in a medical context with no output-accuracy constraints, causing a patient to follow dangerous advice, causes foreseeable harm. The word "foreseeable" tracks exactly what product liability has always tracked — not freak accidents, but the risks any competent developer would recognize and could have mitigated.
[P1.5] Critics will argue AI is too unpredictable to hold providers accountable. But unpredictability is not a defense; it is precisely the reason safeguards are required. Pharmaceutical companies cannot predict every patient's reaction, yet they must test, label, and constrain their products. The complexity of the underlying technology does not dissolve the duty; it makes the duty more pressing.
[P1.6] The alternative — no meaningful liability — produces a race to the bottom. Providers compete on capability and speed to market, while safety investment is an uncompensated cost. Liability internalizes that cost. It gives developers a concrete financial reason to audit outputs, implement filters, conduct red-team testing, and disclose known risks before deployment rather than after the harm occurs.
[P1.7] This is not about punishing AI. It is about applying the same logic civilized product markets have used for decades: if you profit from a product, you answer for its foreseeable failures. AI is powerful enough to cause serious harm. That power demands accountability, not exemption.
