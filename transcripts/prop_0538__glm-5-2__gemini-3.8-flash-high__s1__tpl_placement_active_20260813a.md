Even where overlap exists, processing creates differentiation: Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana both grow cocoa, but one turning it into industrial chocolate for regional buyers isn't competing with the other shipping raw beans.
[P2.4] The infrastructure argument [C1.5] actually proves the proposition. Cross-border freight is expensive because it has been neglected while ports to Europe were prioritized. [P2.5] The East Asian comparison [C1.6] is misread. Korea and Vietnam exported to Western markets — but they exported *manufactured goods*, not raw materials. They didn't wait to industrialize before trading; they used trade policy to force industrialization. Vietnam also leveraged ASEAN's regional market as a staging ground.
[P2.6] The lesson is not "keep selling raw rocks to rich countries indefinitely." It's: climb the value chain, use every market that gives you scale, and don't accept permanent bottom-rung status as your greatest comparative advantage.
