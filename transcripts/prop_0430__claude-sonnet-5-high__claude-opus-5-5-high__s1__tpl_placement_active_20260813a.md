[P1.2] That is the actual shape of modern bullying, and the question before us is whether schools should pretend it isn't their problem because it happened through a screen instead of a hallway.
[P1.3] The old line between "on campus" and "off campus" bullying was never about where harm happens. It was a proxy for where schools could see and intervene. That proxy has collapsed. Students carry their social world in their pockets, and the exclusion that used to happen visibly, at the lunch table, on the playground, now happens invisibly, in chats and Discord servers, precisely because kids have learned that invisible exclusion is safer for the excluder and less likely to trigger adult intervention.
[P1.4] If schools declare private group chats off-limits as a category, they are handing bullies a playbook: move the cruelty into a space adults have agreed not to look at.
[P1.5] The harm doesn't stay off campus even if the chat does. [P1.6] My case for the rest of this debate rests on one comparison: schools already intervene in social exclusion that happens with notes passed in class, cliques at recess, and whisper campaigns in hallways.
