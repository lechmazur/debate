[P1.1] Large-scale personal-data harvesting for targeted advertising should start from a simple legal premise: tracking people across apps, sites, locations, purchases, searches, and inferred vulnerabilities is not ordinary commerce. It is surveillance conducted for persuasion. If a company wants to build a behavioral dossier on someone to sell access to their attention, the burden should be on that company to obtain explicit opt-in consent—not on the individual to discover, understand, and escape a system designed to be invisible.
[P1.2] The core harm is not just “annoying ads.” It is loss of practical control over identity and choice. Targeted advertising markets reward firms for collecting more data, retaining it longer, sharing it wider, and inferring more sensitive traits: health worries, financial stress, sexuality, political leanings, addiction risk, relationship status. Even when a platform never displays a raw data point, the system monetizes predictions about the person. That creates risks of manipulation, discrimination, chilling effects, and data breaches that individuals cannot realistically trace or prove after the fact.
[P1.3] That is why a presumption of unlawfulness is the right legal tool. Case-by-case after-the-harm enforcement fails because the user rarely knows what was collected, who received it, or how it shaped what they saw. “Notice and choice” fails when consent is buried in long policies, bundled with access, or extracted through dark patterns. Explicit opt-in changes the default: no large-scale harvesting unless the person affirmatively agrees, with real knowledge and a real alternative.
[P1.4] The likely defense is that targeted ads fund free services and help businesses reach customers. But convenience is not a license to construct surveillance infrastructure by default. Advertising can still exist: contextual ads, search ads based on current intent, subscriptions, loyalty programs, and opt-in personalization all remain available. Small businesses do not have a right to buy predictions generated from people who never knowingly agreed to be profiled.
