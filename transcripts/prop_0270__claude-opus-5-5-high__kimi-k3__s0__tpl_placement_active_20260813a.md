[P1.1] Every parent who has tried to win the device war app by app knows how it ends. You block TikTok, and YouTube Shorts fills the gap. You set a timer on one game, and three more are downloaded by Friday. The trouble is not weak parental willpower.
[P1.2] Parents are running a defensive campaign against products engineered by thousands of well-paid designers to be hard to put down. Schools and pediatricians who hand parents that strategy are handing them a losing position.
[P1.3] The proposition offers a better target. Stop asking "which app is the enemy?" and ask "what is my child losing?" The answer is consistent: sleep, the unstructured boredom that forces a child to invent something, and free play with other kids. Those are the goods screens most reliably displace, and they are where the evidence is sturdiest.
[P1.4] Researchers still argue about whether particular platforms harm mental health. There is far less argument that a teenager scrolling at midnight is losing sleep, and that lost sleep damages mood, attention and learning.
[P1.5] Framing wellbeing around those goals does three things app policing cannot.
[P1.6] First, it survives the next app. "Phones charge in the kitchen overnight" works whether the threat is Snapchat, a new game, or a technology that doesn't exist yet. A blocklist is obsolete the moment it's written.
[P1.7] Second, it turns conflict into cooperation. A child hears "I'm banning your favorite app" as punishment. "You need nine hours and an afternoon outside" is a goal the family can share. A pediatrician can check it and a school can reinforce it with recess and homework-free evenings.
[P1.8] Third, it generates the right limits without the endless fight. This is not permissiveness. Protecting sleep means firm device-free bedtimes. Protecting play means real time off screens.
[P1.9] The rules follow from the goal instead of being negotiated one app at a time.
[P1.10] The real choice is between an unwinnable war of attrition and a clear account of what childhood needs. Doctors and teachers should give parents the second.
