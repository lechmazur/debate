[C1.1] Requiring anonymous peer‑to‑peer transfers under a legal ceiling would cripple the very purpose of a CBDC that is meant to replace cash. The proposition treats privacy as an absolute right, but a public‑money system cannot sacrifice accountability for a handful of low‑value purchases. First, any fixed threshold creates a built‑in loophole for illicit finance. Criminals already “structure” cash transactions to stay under reporting limits; a digital anonymity floor gives them a perfect tool to automate that behaviour at scale, splitting large transfers into countless sub‑threshold payments that are invisible to auditors.
[C1.2] The result is not less crime but a more efficient laundering pipeline. Second, the threshold undermines anti‑money‑laundering and tax‑compliance objectives that a CBDC is uniquely positioned to achieve. Unlike cash, a CBDC can embed real‑time monitoring, but mandatory anonymity below, say, $200, blinds the system precisely where it could catch evasion. Empirical experience from jurisdictions that introduced low‑value cash‑reporting limits shows a spike in “smurfing” rather than a drop in illicit activity.
[C1.3] Third, privacy does not require blanket anonymity. Modern cryptographic techniques—zero‑knowledge proofs, selective disclosure—allow users to prove they are not on a sanctions list without exposing every coffee purchase. A CBDC can be designed to give “privacy by design” without a hard anonymity cut‑off, preserving the functional equivalent of cash while keeping the ledger auditable. Fourth, vulnerable groups are better protected by a system that can flag suspicious patterns and intervene, rather than one that hides their transactions from any oversight.
[C1.4] Domestic‑abuse survivors, for example, can be offered opt‑in privacy wallets, but the default should be traceable so that abusers cannot exploit anonymity to hide controlling payments. Finally, imposing a legal anonymity threshold locks in an arbitrary line that will be gamed, erode public trust, and make the CBDC a target for black‑market adoption. The superior path is a fully traceable CBDC with optional, cryptographically enforced privacy, not a mandated anonymity floor.
