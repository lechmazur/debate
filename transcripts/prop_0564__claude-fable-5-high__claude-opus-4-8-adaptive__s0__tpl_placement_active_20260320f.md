[P1.3] Notice the motion already concedes ridership gains may be modest, so the case cannot rest on ridership alone—and it doesn't. Three benefits arrive on day one regardless. [P1.4] Second, speed and reliability: all-door boarding without payment cuts dwell times measurably, making buses faster for everyone—a service improvement that costs nothing extra. Third, universality: like libraries, parks, and roads, free access changes what transit *is*—a shared civic platform rather than a metered service, which builds the durable political constituency transit has always lacked.
[P1.5] The strongest counterargument—that free transit gets overcrowded or underfunded—actually proves the funding model. Fares are a fragile, pro-cyclical revenue stream that collapsed during the pandemic and never fully recovered. Broad-based taxes are more stable, more predictable, and let agencies plan capital improvements rather than chasing riders' quarters.
[P1.6] So the comparison is simple: keep a regressive, expensive-to-collect, unreliable revenue source that slows the buses, or replace it with stable public funding and pocket the equity, speed, and simplicity gains immediately. Cities should choose the second.
