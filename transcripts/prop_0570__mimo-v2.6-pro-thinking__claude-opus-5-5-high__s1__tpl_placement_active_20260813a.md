[P1.1] Writing feedback is a judgment about choices; mastery practice is correction against a standard. That difference decides this motion.
[P1.2] AI tutoring excels where the target is convergent. A student drills quadratic factorizations, gets instant correction, tries again, and masters the procedure through repetition with unlimited patience and zero embarrassment. The same holds for comma rules, subject-verb agreement, conjugations, vocabulary review. The feedback has a right answer, the practice is safe to repeat, and immediate correction shortens the loop.
[P1.3] Handing this work to AI is a clear win: more reps, faster signal, freed teacher time. Nothing in my case disputes that.
[P1.4] But writing feedback answers questions with no answer key. Does this paragraph earn its claim? Is this voice right for this audience? Should this essay trust the reader or explain more?
[P1.5] A teacher who knows the student can see the sentence that dodges the hard thought, the thesis that flatters the assignment's surface, the argument that mistakes fluency for substance. The judgment is relational and developmental — it depends on who the writer is becoming.
[P1.6] AI cannot do that job as the main source, for three reasons. First, its feedback tends toward the generic and the encouraging: praise for a "strong thesis" and a suggestion to "add more detail," advice that teaches the student nothing about their actual decision. Second, writing feedback is supposed to teach judgment, and students who let the model adjudicate their drafts stop asking what they themselves think is good. The writer's ear atrophies.
[P1.7] Third, feedback only shapes a writer when it comes from a source whose judgment the writer accepts as meaningful. "The model liked it" is not a reason to revise; a reader the student respects being unconvinced is.
[P1.8] Consider the concrete case. Mia writes an essay claiming social media harms teens, with three agreeable paragraphs. An AI tutor praises structure and asks for a counterargument. Her teacher writes one line: "You already knew this conclusion before you researched.
[P1.9] What did you actually find out?" Only one of those turns her into a writer. [clipped]
