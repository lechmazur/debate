Keeping chronology as the default would not require giving up spam filters, moderation or an optional discovery feed.
[P1.6] It would mean that objecting to cruelty did not routinely double as a vote to give it a better spot in everyone else’s feed. Over years, fewer such rewards would mean fewer pile-ons for their targets to endure and fewer examples for aspiring crowd-pleasers to imitate.

[C1.1] Chronology would change where toxicity appears, not necessarily how much of it exists. The opponent’s opening treats recommendation as the cause of cruelty: remove ranking, supposedly, and hostile behavior loses its reward. But the incentive to produce harassment comes from the response it attracts, not solely from the way that response is distributed. A platform can rank every post and still reward cruelty; it can display posts chronologically and still let an angry audience sustain the feud.
[C1.2] Consider the prolific account that posts several hostile messages every day. In a follower-based chronological feed, that account can reach the same audience repeatedly; ranking may restrict some posts, while chronology rewards persistence. If this actor adapts, the result is not a smaller stream of abuse but a steadier dose. Conversely, supporters usually post less often.
[C1.3] Under chronology, a measured reply is easily buried beneath the next angry update, whereas recommendation can also surface supportive material. The opponent cannot claim “less toxic” merely by making some toxic pile-ons less viral: the attack may become persistent, fragmented across many accounts, and harder for one target to escape.
[C1.4] Harassment will also migrate beyond the main feed. Replies, quote-posts, group chats, direct messages, and off-platform communities are all places where recommendation is unnecessary. Even if the home feed became chronological by default, an optional discovery feed or a rival application would preserve the profitable route from controversy to audience. Removing one default does not remove the market for attention that makes cruelty attractive.
[C1.5] Most importantly, proposition confuses amplification with toxicity. Chronology might make one viral insult less spectacular while making abuse more frequent or harder to escape. To win, it must show that fewer harmful interactions occur overall, not merely fewer algorithmic pile-ons. Changing where abuse travels does not prove less abuse.
