When the first point of contact obscures the steep decline in viability after the mid-thirties, it functionally undermines informed consent.
[P1.4] When women delay family planning under the false security of a vague success claim, they cannot buy that time back. The biological clock does not pause for marketing copy.
[P1.5] Opponents will argue that mandatory disclosures clutter ads or that patients can simply ask for details during consultations. That misunderstands how consumer decisions actually work. [P1.6] A clear age-based breakdown costs clinics nothing but the ability to mislead, while it saves patients from spending tens of thousands on a pathway that may never work for them. Transparency here is not paternalism; it is the minimum standard we already demand for mortgages, investment products, and prescription medications. Egg freezing deserves the same.
