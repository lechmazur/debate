[P1.1] Every student is drafted into the financial system the moment they take a first paycheck, sign a lease, or accept student-loan paperwork. Requiring a serious financial-literacy course is not adding a luxury elective; it is handing every graduate the operator's manual for debt, taxes, and budgeting before those forces start compounding against them; nobody gets to sit out taxes, interest, or rent.
[P1.2] The case is equity. Leaving this to family background turns adult stability into an inheritance. A teenager whose parents understand withholding, interest capitalization, and credit utilization starts miles ahead of a first-generation student who must reverse-engineer a pay stub or loan disclosure alone. Elective exposure widens that gap: electives attract students who already have the confidence and coaching to choose them, while the students most targeted by payday lenders and high-APR offers are the least likely to enroll.
[P1.3] A graduation requirement converts privilege into a floor.
[P1.4] Ignorance is expensive and unevenly distributed. Compound interest is patient. A modest balance on a high-APR card can take years to clear on minimum payments, with interest swallowing the purchase. A borrower who misunderstands grace periods, income-driven repayment, or deferment can turn a temporary hardship into default, fees, and damaged credit that follows them into housing and jobs.
[P1.5] Budgeting is not 'common sense'; it is applied arithmetic with penalties and asymmetric information. We already require math, civics, and health because those tools govern adult life; financial illiteracy is the one gap that can bankrupt a person who did everything else right. Financial literacy is where they cash out.
[P1.6] CON must defend a status quo in which the most financially fragile students get the least preparation. A serious course answers that: trained instruction with real documents—pay stubs, leases, tax forms, loan disclosures—plus simulations and a real assessment students must pass to graduate. The choice is not school versus family. It is guaranteed competence versus luck.
