[P1.1] Publicly funded research is a public trust, yet the scientific enterprise suffers from a severe epistemic distortion: an incentive structure that rewards statistically significant, sensational results over cold truth. Under current norms, researchers face immense pressure to p-hack, alter hypotheses after results are known, and bury inconvenient null results in file drawers. When taxpayers finance a clinical trial or social policy experiment, they are paying to discover reality, not to subsidize a curated highlight reel of false positives. Mandatory preregistration solves this by drawing an immutable, auditable line between rigorous hypothesis testing and exploratory data mining.
[P1.2] Yet preregistration alone addresses only half the equation if academic journals remain gatekeepers of prestige that reject unexciting outcomes. Coupling funding mandates with outcome-agnostic publication—evaluating studies on methodological design, statistical power, and theoretical rationale before data collection—dismantles this publication bias at its root. Learning that an experimental drug fails to outperform a placebo, or that a costly educational intervention yields zero measurable benefit, is just as scientifically vital as discovering a breakthrough. Suppressing such outcomes wastes public billions, encourages dangerous clinical misdirection, and forces future researchers to duplicate failed paths.
[P1.3] This policy does not strangle scientific creativity. Researchers remain entirely free to chase unexpected anomalies and conduct post-hoc analyses; they simply must label them honestly as exploratory rather than confirmatory. Far from constraining science, guaranteed publication liberates it. It frees researchers from the career-ending threat of the null result, empowering them to tackle ambitious, high-risk questions with total methodological honesty.
[P1.4] Science cannot be self-correcting when the evidence base is systematically incomplete. By requiring preregistration for public money and binding publication to methodological rigor rather than positive findings, we align scientific incentives with empirical truth and restore integrity to the research that shapes human lives.

[P2.4] Presenting post-hoc excavations as pre-planned confirmations directly fuels the reproducibility crisis. Preregistration does not silence curiosity; it preserves the statistical validity of confirmatory testing while keeping exploratory science completely honest.
