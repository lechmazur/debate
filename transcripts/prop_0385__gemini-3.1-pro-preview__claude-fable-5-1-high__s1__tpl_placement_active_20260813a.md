[P1.1] Blasphemy laws do not protect the divine; they protect the powerful. Even when majorities claim these laws preserve public order or sacred respect, in practice, they are inherently weaponized tools used to persecute religious minorities, silence political dissidents, and settle petty personal disputes. When a state criminalizes subjective offense to a religion, it hands a loaded weapon to the dominant group.
[P1.2] The primary defense of these laws is that they prevent societal unrest by appeasing religious outrage. This logic is fatally flawed. Blasphemy and religious-insult laws do not quell mob violence; they legitimize and incentivize it. By legally validating the premise that religious insult warrants severe punishment, the state emboldens vigilantes.
[P1.3] When a mob knows the government shares their underlying belief that an insult is a crime, they are encouraged to riot to demand extreme enforcement, or worse, to take executions into their own hands. True public order relies on the rule of law, equal protection, and the de-escalation of violence—not on legally codifying the most volatile sensitivities of the majority.
[P1.4] Furthermore, no society or belief system can progress without the freedom to question entrenched orthodoxies. What a religious majority condemns as blasphemy today is often what history recognizes tomorrow as vital social reform, scientific inquiry, or human rights advocacy. By shielding doctrines from scrutiny, these laws paralyze intellectual and moral development. They trap societies in a state of compulsory reverence, where peaceful dissent is treated as a criminal act.
[P1.5] A just state’s duty is to protect individuals from physical harm and discrimination, not to protect abstract ideas from criticism. The price of catering to majority religious sensitivities is the systemic oppression of the vulnerable and the outright denial of fundamental free expression. Abolishing blasphemy and religious-insult laws is not an attack on faith; it is a necessary prerequisite for genuine human rights, equal citizenship, and sustainable public peace.
