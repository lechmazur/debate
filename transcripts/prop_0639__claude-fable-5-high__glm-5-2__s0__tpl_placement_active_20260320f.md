[P1.1] The strongest argument for binding citizens' assemblies is what happens without them. France's Climate Convention gathered 150 randomly selected citizens, gave them months of expert testimony and deliberation, and produced serious proposals — which the government then filleted, discarding or diluting most of them. Participants felt used; the public learned that deliberation is theater. Advisory-only assemblies are not a cautious middle ground.
[P1.2] They are a machine for generating cynicism: they recruit citizens' time and trust, then hand the results to the very politicians whose incentives the assembly was designed to bypass.
[P1.3] Contrast Ireland. Its assemblies on abortion and marriage equality mattered precisely because their recommendations flowed into binding referendums. When citizens knew their work would count, they deliberated seriously, and the results resolved questions that elected politicians had dodged for decades. That is the model: sortition plus real authority breaks the logjams elections cannot.
[P1.4] Why binding power specifically? Three reasons. First, incentives. Elected officials systematically avoid issues where the right answer is unpopular in the short term or crosses donors — climate policy, electoral reform, drug policy.
[P1.5] An assembly with binding power on a defined question has no re-election to protect and no campaign to fund. Second, quality. A random, stratified sample that hears balanced evidence and deliberates for weeks is a better proxy for the public's considered judgment than a poll of gut reactions or a legislature filtered through party discipline.
[P1.6] Third, accountability of the process itself: knowing decisions will bind forces rigorous procedure — balanced briefing, supermajority thresholds, judicial review — rather than the loose facilitation advisory bodies tolerate.
[P1.7] Note what the motion actually asks: binding power on specific policy questions, not government by lottery. Constitutions already bind legislatures through courts and referendums; this adds one more legitimate channel, bounded and reviewable.
[P1.8] The choice is not between binding assemblies and a healthy status quo. It is between giving considered public judgment real force, or continuing to let it be politely shelved.
