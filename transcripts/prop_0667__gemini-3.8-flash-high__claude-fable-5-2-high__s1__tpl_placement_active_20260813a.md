[P1.1] The engine of unauthorized immigration is the labor market. Mandatory nationwide E-Verify replaces this broken honor system with a direct, electronic cross-check against Social Security and Department of Homeland Security databases. By closing the loopholes that allow unscrupulous employers to undercut law-abiding businesses, a universal mandate dismantles the informal jobs magnet that sustains unauthorized employment.
[P1.2] Critically, standardized digital verification reduces discrimination rather than expanding it. Under current practices, an employer anxious about immigration liability often resorts to crude profiling—subjecting applicants with foreign-sounding names, accents, or Hispanic heritage to heightened scrutiny. E-Verify eliminates this subjective guesswork. Because the mandate applies uniformly to every new hire only *after* a formal job offer is extended, pre-employment screening based on appearance is prohibited.
[P1.3] Furthermore, established procedural protections—most notably the Tentative Non-Confirmation process—give work-authorized citizens and immigrants a formal, legally protected runway to resolve administrative discrepancies before any adverse action can be taken.
[P1.4] Finally, fears of crippling labor shortages misunderstand basic economic adaptation. Applying E-Verify strictly to new hires ensures a phased transition rather than an abrupt shock to existing operations. When firms can no longer rely on an artificially suppressed underground labor pool, markets adjust through healthy mechanisms: employers raise entry-level wages to draw domestic workers off the sidelines, invest in labor-saving technologies, and tap legal guest-worker channels. Experiences in states with mandatory verification demonstrate that while transitional friction exists, economies rebalance without systemic collapse.
[P1.5] Mandatory nationwide E-Verify establishes a fair, verifiable standard that protects lawful wages, curtails illegal hiring, and treats every job applicant under the exact same rule of law.
