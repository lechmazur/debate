[P1.1] A child’s access to effective reading instruction should not depend on which classroom door they enter. It is a demand that foundational learning be planned rather than left to chance.
[P1.3] The first reason is how beginners become independent readers. [P1.4] Teachers can choose engaging examples and adjust pacing, but omitting that progression is not merely a different style: it risks leaving children without the tools the next lesson assumes.
[P1.5] The second reason is equity. Some children arrive with extensive literacy support and receive additional help whenever school instruction falls short. Others depend almost entirely on school. Prioritizing autonomy makes those children bear the risk of uneven curricular choices.
