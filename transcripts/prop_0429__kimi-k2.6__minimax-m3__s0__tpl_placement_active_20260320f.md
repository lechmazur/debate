[P1.1] Every ping on a parent’s phone reframes the classroom as a surveillance aquarium. [P1.2] The primary harm is developmental. Children build self-regulation by navigating moments of friction, boredom, or social conflict without an adult swooping in at each beat. [P1.3] That is not partnership; it is remote-control parenting. These platforms convert educators into customer-service representatives for dozens of anxious adults, each demanding immediate explanation for every micro-data point. The teacher’s gaze shifts from the student to the screen, and from the class to the inbox. [P1.5] Instead of resolving a minor disruption with a quiet word, teachers must now document and defend it in real time.
[P1.6] Schools possess both the institutional authority and the educational obligation to resist this.
