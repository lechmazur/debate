[C1.2] In digital markets, early-stage acquisitions happen long before a product is fully monetized. The value lies in future integration—a vision that cannot be conclusively proven in a regulatory hearing today. If the future is unknowable, the acquirer cannot definitively prove it either. By treating uncertainty as guilt, PRO’s "rebuttable" presumption functions as a *de facto* ban on tech acquisitions.
[C1.3] This breaks the engine of technological innovation. PRO worries about the lost competitive future of acquired startups. Had the burden been on the acquirers to legally prove those unprecedented future benefits at the time of purchase—before the products had even fully materialized—both deals would likely have been blocked.
[C1.7] The current system rightly requires regulators to prove harm, protecting the pipeline that funds new technology. Flipping the presumption doesn't safeguard the future; it starves it of capital.
