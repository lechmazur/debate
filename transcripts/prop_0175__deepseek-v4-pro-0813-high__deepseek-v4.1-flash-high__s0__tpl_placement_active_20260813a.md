[P1.1] Licensing requirements in interior design, hair braiding, and low-risk trades are primarily cartel rules wearing a safety costume. The design gives it away: they impose broad, expensive, time-consuming training unrelated to the actual risk, they are often shaped by boards filled with existing practitioners, and they routinely grandfather current operators while tightening entry for newcomers.
[P1.2] Hair braiding illustrates the problem clearly. In many states, braiders must first obtain a full cosmetology license—training dominated by chemical straightening, coloring, and cutting—even though braiding involves none of that. The public-safety case is vanishingly weak: customers can inspect photos, consult clients, and sue for real harm. The main effect is to force aspiring braiders, often low-income women and women of color, to spend thousands of dollars and hundreds of hours before earning a living.
[P1.3] That is not consumer protection; it is tuition extraction and competition suppression.
[P1.4] Interior design follows the same pattern. Selecting furniture, finishes, and layouts does not create structural or life-safety hazards. Where structural, electrical, or plumbing work is involved, architects and engineers already bear code responsibility. Licensing interior designers mostly protects established designers from price-cutting newcomers.
[P1.5] It does not stop unsafe buildings, because the unsafe parts are regulated separately.
[P1.6] Low-risk trades are equally over-licensed. A bad paint job, uneven tile, or imperfect drywall can be seen, reviewed, and remedied through contracts, insurance, or courts. The market already punishes sloppy work. Licensing adds a prior restraint that raises prices and reduces entry without adding meaningful safety.
[P1.7] The clearest tell is who writes the rules. Licensing boards are often dominated by current practitioners rather than independent safety experts. They favor broad mandates, unrelated coursework, and high fees while grandfathering themselves. That is the structure of a guild defending incumbents, not a regulator targeting harm.
[P1.8] For these low-hazard fields, the entry barrier is the point, not a side effect.
