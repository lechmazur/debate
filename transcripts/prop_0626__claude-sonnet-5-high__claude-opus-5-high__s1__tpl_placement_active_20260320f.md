[P1.1] Three arguments decide this debate: accountability, the nature of the decision being delegated, and the arms-race dynamic that makes "wait and see" the most dangerous option on the table.
[P1.2] Start with accountability. When a human pulls a trigger, we have someone to try, someone to court-martial, a chain of command that bears responsibility. When a machine's targeting algorithm selects and engages a target based on sensor data and pattern-matching, and that target turns out to be a wedding party or a group of farmers, who answers for it? The programmer didn't choose that target.
[P1.3] The commander who deployed the system didn't choose that target. The machine "chose" it, and machines cannot be punished, deterred, or held morally responsible. This isn't a hypothetical gap — it's a structural one. Autonomous lethal targeting creates a zone of lethal decision-making with no accountable human agent inside it, and that alone should be disqualifying for a democracy that claims to operate under the laws of war.
[P1.4] Second, some decisions are not technical problems to be optimized — they are moral judgments requiring human conscience. Distinguishing a combatant from a civilian, judging proportionality between military advantage and civilian harm, recognizing surrender or incapacitation — these require contextual, value-laden judgment that pattern-recognition systems, however sophisticated, do not possess. A soldier can look at a child holding a toy rifle and hesitate. A targeting algorithm optimized for threat classification has no reason to hesitate unless explicitly programmed to, and no programmer can anticipate every context war throws up.
[P1.5] Third, banning deployment now is the only stable equilibrium. Once autonomous weapons are normalized, every state faces pressure to match adversaries' speed by removing human oversight further, since human reaction time becomes a competitive disadvantage. That's an accelerating race to remove the very safeguard we're debating. A ban, enforced through the same international coordination that restricted chemical and blinding-laser weapons, keeps the decision to kill where it belongs: with a human being who can be held to account.
