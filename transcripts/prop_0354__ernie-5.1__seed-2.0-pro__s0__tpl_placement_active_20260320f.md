The problem is not that drivers lack information; it is that they cannot reliably act on it. [P1.2] Making intelligent speed assistance the default in every advanced driver‑assistance system removes the weakest link in the chain: human inattention.
[P1.3] Intelligent speed assistance uses GPS‑linked map data and camera‑based sign recognition to keep a vehicle at or below the posted limit, intervening only when the driver exceeds it. [P1.4] By contrast, jurisdictions that have mandated ISA as a default—such as the EU’s recent regulation—have already recorded measurable drops in average travel speed and a corresponding reduction in speed‑related crashes, without a rise in driver complaints. [P1.5] The core trade‑off the opposition raises is driver autonomy. [P1.6] Drivers can still override ISA with a firm press of the accelerator, preserving ultimate control while ensuring that the default state protects them from the most common cause of fatal crashes.
[P1.7] Therefore, the policy that maximizes lives saved while preserving the ability to opt out is clear: make intelligent speed assistance the default, not an optional add‑on.
