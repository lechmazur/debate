[P1.1] Buy-now-pay-later is credit in everything but presentation. A provider pays the merchant now, the consumer receives goods now, and repayment is deferred. Calling that an ordinary checkout feature because approval is fast or interest is sometimes zero confuses interface with economic substance. Regulation should follow the risk created, not the button’s design.
[P1.2] The central risk is cumulative, opaque indebtedness. A shopper can open several installment plans across different providers within minutes. Each payment looks small; together they can overwhelm the same paycheck. Automatic debits may then trigger late fees, overdrafts, or pressure to borrow elsewhere.
[P1.3] Unlike a single credit-card balance, fragmented obligations are harder for both consumers and lenders to see. Frictionless approval is convenient precisely because it suppresses the moment when affordability would otherwise be considered.
[P1.4] Credit-card-style protections address these harms directly. BNPL providers should clearly disclose the total repayment schedule, penalties, and consequences of missed payments before purchase. They should conduct proportionate affordability checks, provide fair billing-error and merchant-dispute procedures, restrict abusive fees, and offer consistent hardship and collection standards. Where repayment behavior is reported, it should be accurate and governed by meaningful correction rights. “Much more like” does not mean mechanically copying every rule for revolving, interest-bearing cards; it means applying comparable protections wherever the same credit function and default risk exist.
[P1.5] This also improves competition. Without comparable rules, firms gain an advantage by relabeling lending as checkout technology and shifting risk onto consumers. Responsible providers that assess affordability and resolve disputes properly are then undercut by those offering the fastest approval and weakest safeguards. Sensible regulation rewards better products instead of better evasion.
[P1.6] The choice is not between innovation and regulation. BNPL can remain fast, digital, and often interest-free while meeting baseline duties attached to lending. Convenience is valuable, but it is not a reason to create a regulatory blind spot. If a product creates debt at the point of sale, consumers deserve protections designed for credit, not the minimal oversight appropriate to a payment button.
