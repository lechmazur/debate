[P1.1] A driver with five years and thousands of five-star rides is, on any competing platform, a stranger. She starts at zero: no priority in the queue, no premium jobs, no trust premium in her pricing. That asymmetry is the quiet engine of gig-economy wage suppression, and it is not a natural fact. It is a design choice that platforms have every incentive to keep.
[P1.2] Start with whose record it is. The rating was earned by the worker's labor and written by customers about her. The platform merely recorded it. Letting a firm treat that record as its private asset means a worker's entire professional reputation — the only capital most gig workers have — evaporates the moment she considers a competitor.
[P1.3] No other labor market works this way. A nurse takes her references with her. A chef takes her résumé. Only here does five years of verified performance become non-transferable property of the counterparty.
[P1.4] Second, the economics. Competition disciplines take rates, deactivation practices, and pay only if workers can credibly leave. Portability converts a captive workforce into a mobile one, and mobility is what forces platforms to compete on terms rather than on hostage-holding. It also lowers the barrier for new entrants, who currently face an impossible cold-start problem: nobody trusts an unrated marketplace, and nobody can get rated without one.
[P1.5] Portability is pro-entrant, pro-worker, and anti-monopoly at once.
[P1.6] Third, why the mandate and the word "standardized" both matter. Voluntary interoperability will never arrive, because lock-in is precisely the profit. And bilateral, bespoke deals would only entrench the giants who can afford them. A common schema — task type, volume, completion and dispute rates, cryptographically attested by the issuing platform and exported only at the worker's request — makes records verifiable rather than self-reported, so the receiving platform gets better information than a résumé, not worse.
[P1.7] Carriers once insisted phone-number portability was infeasible and fraud-prone. It shipped, prices fell, and the sky held. The same objections deserve the same answer.
