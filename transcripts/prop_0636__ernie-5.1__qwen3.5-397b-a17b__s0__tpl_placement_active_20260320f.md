Deep‑fake videos of politicians, fabricated voice messages that mimic a CEO’s tone, or AI‑generated music passed off as a human artist can cause real‑world harm within minutes. Without a clear, machine‑readable label, users must rely on intuition, and intuition fails when the manipulation is sophisticated.
[P1.2] A uniform labeling regime—think of a digital “nutrition label” that says “synthetic: generated by model X, timestamped, source‑verified”—gives every platform a common language. This is the sweet spot: consumers get the information they need to evaluate credibility, while creators keep the right to post anonymously.
[P1.3] Requiring centralized identity verification would achieve the same traceability, but at a cost that dwarfs any benefit. [P1.4] The labeling solution sidesteps those harms. It leverages existing standards—similar to the way digital watermarks are already used in photography—and can be enforced through platform‑level checks rather than government‑run databases. The result is a transparent ecosystem where trust is built on content, not on who posted it.
[P1.5] Therefore regulators should act now to require standardized labels and provenance metadata, and should explicitly reject any mandate for centralized identity verification. This balances safety with liberty, and it is the only path that scales without undermining the open web.
