[P1.1] A vote cannot be bought, sold, or scaled. That is the whole point of a vote. The moment we treat political money as ordinary speech, we import a market logic into the one institution deliberately built to resist it: an arena where the billionaire and the bus driver count the same.
[P1.2] Start with the category error. Ordinary speech is non-rival. My arguing does not shrink your audience. Political money is different, because what it buys is finite: a candidate's hours, the last available ad slot before an election, a staffer's attention when a bill is being drafted.
[P1.3] When one donor bids up that scarcity, everyone else's voice is worth less. Money in politics behaves like a bidding war over a fixed supply of political attention, and bidding wars have losers by design. Calling that "speech" flatters it.
[P1.4] Second, corporations are not participants in self-government. A firm cannot vote, cannot serve on a jury, cannot be conscripted. It is a legal device for pooling capital, and its managers owe a fiduciary duty to pursue returns, not the public good. A corporate donation is therefore not a citizen expressing conscience; it is an investment expected to yield regulatory or contractual returns.
[P1.5] That is precisely why it should be banned rather than capped: the transaction has no legitimate expressive core to protect.
[P1.6] Third, notice what a ban does not do. Executives, shareholders, and employees remain fully free to donate as individuals, up to a low cap. The firm can still publish, testify, brief regulators, and argue in public. Nothing is censored.
[P1.7] What ends is the ability to convert balance-sheet size into political leverage.
[P1.8] And this is not utopian. Canada, France and Brazil have all restricted or banned corporate money with tight individual limits, and none has slid into authoritarian silence. Meanwhile the jurisdiction that most fully embraced money-as-speech has produced a donor class of a few thousand people with disproportionate influence.
[P1.9] The choice is not speech versus silence. It is one person, one vote, versus one dollar, one vote.
