[C1.3] A ban in one country simply shifts the transaction to another. We saw this with encryption export controls in the 1990s - they drove development offshore without reducing proliferation. Cutting off their access to legal, accountable surveillance tools doesn't stop them from spying; it pushes them toward unregulated alternatives with no oversight whatsoever.

A government that is authoritarian but cooperating on nuclear non-proliferation is not the same as one actively sponsoring terror. [C1.6] The real answer is not prohibition but rigorous, enforceable licensing with real consequences for misuse. A ban doesn't protect dissidents - it blinds us to what happens next.
