[P1.1] A corporate "net-zero" claim promises physical balance, not an accounting trick. Yet without legal limits on offsets, companies can burn fossil fuels indefinitely while buying cheap external credits and telling consumers and investors their climate impact is zero. Legally capping offsets to a small residual share, while requiring most reductions inside the value chain, is the only way to make those claims honest.
[P1.2] Start with basic physics: fossil emissions and carbon offsets are not interchangeable. Burning coal or oil moves permanently locked underground carbon into the active atmosphere forever. Most offsets rely instead on fragile biological storage or unverifiable counterfactuals, such as planting trees vulnerable to wildfire or paying to protect forests never actually threatened. Worse, voluntary offset markets have a built-in conflict of interest: both sellers and corporate buyers profit when credits are cheap and plentiful, driving a race to the bottom.
[P1.3] Trading certain, permanent pollution for uncertain, temporary credits guarantees continued warming behind clean-looking ledgers.
[P1.4] Unlimited offsets also starve real decarbonization of capital. Cleaning up a value chain, from electrifying fleets to retooling factories and suppliers, costs far more upfront than buying cheap credits. Yet those hard investments are precisely what scale clean technologies and drive down costs across whole industries. When firms can buy the same "net-zero" badge with bargain offsets instead of transforming operations, basic economics pushes executives to delay upgrades and lock in fossil infrastructure.
[P1.5] Only a legal cap fixes this market failure without choking off climate finance. Under voluntary rules, honest pioneers spending heavily to cut supply-chain emissions get undercut by rivals using cheap offsets to make identical green claims. Crucially, this law never stops firms from funding conservation or carbon removal; they can buy unlimited credits, just not to disguise unabated pollution as "net-zero."
[P1.6] Meanwhile, preserving a small offset share sensibly accommodates truly unavoidable residual emissions. Net-zero must mean changing how business operates, not buying permission to stay the same.
