[P1.1] Unconditional cash transfers rely on a flawed assumption: that giving people money automatically translates into meeting their basic needs. This only works in highly competitive markets with elastic supply. But the most critical needs—healthcare, childcare, and transit—are characterized by inelastic supply, local monopolies, and severe information asymmetry. If we hand out cash for childcare in a region with a shortage of providers, the result isn't more care; it is simply higher prices.
[P1.2] Universal basic services bypass this inflationary trap by directly guaranteeing the outcome, ensuring that public funds actually produce public goods rather than just inflating private profit margins.
[P1.3] Second, services like mass transit and broadband are natural monopolies with massive positive externalities. Fragmenting these into individual cash purchases is wildly inefficient. A cash transfer might help one person buy a used car, but expanding public transit moves thousands of people efficiently, reduces carbon emissions, and alleviates urban congestion. Similarly, treating internet as a public utility ensures universal connectivity and standardized infrastructure.
[P1.4] Giving people cash to pay their broadband bill just pads telecom profits without forcing them to build out rural or low-income networks. Public provision leverages economies of scale that individual consumers simply cannot access.
[P1.5] Finally, universal services provide collective risk pooling and quality assurance that cash fundamentally cannot. In healthcare, a flat cash stipend does nothing to protect a family from a catastrophic medical event that vastly exceeds their annual transfer. A universal system pools risk across the entire population, ensuring the sickest are not bankrupted. Furthermore, the government can enforce rigorous safety and quality standards in public childcare and transit.
[P1.6] Cash transfers, by contrast, leave vulnerable populations to navigate complex, often predatory private markets entirely alone. To truly uplift citizens, we must guarantee the service itself, not just the currency.
