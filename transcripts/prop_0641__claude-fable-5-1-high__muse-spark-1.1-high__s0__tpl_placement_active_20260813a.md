The "wait for conclusive data" standard is being applied to exactly one side of the ledger.
[P1.2] That asymmetry is the debate.
[P1.3] Three reasons parity now is the responsible choice, not the reckless one.
[P1.4] First, the data cannot arrive without adoption. Long-term yield evidence requires long-term fields, at scale, across soils and climates. Cover crops, diverse rotations, reduced tillage, and integrated livestock change soil biology over five to ten years, not one growing season. Demanding proof before funding the trials is a catch-22 that guarantees the status quo forever.
[P1.5] Second, the current subsidy structure actively penalizes switching. Picture two neighboring farmers. One keeps buying inputs and collects support tied to that system. The other plants cover crops, accepts a transition-year yield dip, and receives comparatively little.
[P1.6] Thin margins make that gamble irrational, however promising the practices. Parity does not force anyone to change; it simply stops the government from placing a thumb on the scale.
[P1.7] Third, this is insurance, not speculation. When natural gas prices spiked in 2022, fertilizer costs roughly doubled or tripled for many growers, and yields depended on a supply chain running through Russia and Belarus. Regenerative systems reduce exposure to that shock because they build fertility from the soil rather than from a tanker. A government that spends heavily to lock farmers into imported inputs, while spending little to build the alternative, is not being prudent.
[P1.8] It is being fragile.
[P1.9] The status quo is not neutral. Matching the scale is how you make the choice honest.
