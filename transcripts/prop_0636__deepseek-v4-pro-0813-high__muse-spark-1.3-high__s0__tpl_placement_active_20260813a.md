[P1.1] The most dangerous synthetic media problem is not that fakes exist; it is that they travel with the credibility of real evidence. A cloned voice can convince a parent their child is in danger, and a manufactured video of a candidate can circulate before any fact-checker can respond. Standardized labeling and provenance signals target that specific harm: they tell viewers and platforms when audio or video has been synthetically generated or altered, reducing the false authenticity that makes deepfakes useful for fraud, non-consensual pornography, and election manipulation.
[P1.2] Voluntary disclosure cannot produce that result. Labels remain scattered, inconsistent, and easy to omit because platforms face competitive pressure not to make their content look suspect. A regulator-set standard solves the coordination problem with one interoperable format for audio and video. It is disclosure regulation, akin to ingredient labels or sender authentication, not a ban on synthetic media or the tools used to create it.
[P1.3] The proposition’s second half is just as important. It rejects centralized identity verification before posting. Such verification would create a single chokepoint for online speech, expose vulnerable speakers, and transform a content-integrity measure into a surveillance or permission system. Provenance, by contrast, follows the content, not the person.
[P1.4] A whistleblower, dissident, or victim can post pseudonymously while the audience still learns whether the clip is synthetic.
[P1.5] Critics may say malicious actors will strip labels. But removal is not consequence-free: missing or broken provenance becomes a warning signal in itself, and platforms can downrank or flag content that lacks required metadata. Mandated labeling does not need to catch every liar; it must make unmarked synthetic media the exception rather than the default and give ordinary people a fast, consistent cue for skepticism.
[P1.6] Regulators should therefore require standardized labels and provenance for synthetic audio and video, while preserving anonymous and pseudonymous speech. That is the precise intervention that reduces real-world harm without converting every speaker into a registered identity.
