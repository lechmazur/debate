[P1.1] Migration policy faces a false choice between compassion and utility. The proposition recognizes that when long-term integration outcomes are comparable, the tie should break toward family reunification, because family bonds provide the social infrastructure that economic selection alone cannot replicate.
[P1.2] Economic selection optimizes for immediate labor market fit: age, education, language scores. But integration is not merely employment; it is the gradual construction of a stable life in a new society. Family reunification supplies the relational safety net that sustains this process. When migrants arrive with or are soon joined by family, they gain built-in language practice, job referrals through kin networks, and emotional support during the disorienting early years.
[P1.3] These are concrete mechanisms that reduce isolation, improve mental health, and accelerate the informal learning that economic migrants often lack.
[P1.4] Points-based systems admit individuals selected for human capital, yet such migrants often show higher loneliness and weaker local ties in the first decade, even when employment is strong. Family-stream migrants may enter the labor market more slowly, but they anchor in communities through schools, extended kin, and neighborhood networks. Over time, this embeddedness yields more durable integration: better educational outcomes for children, higher civic participation, and lower long-term welfare dependency because families absorb shocks that would otherwise fall to the state.
[P1.5] The proposition does not claim family reunification always outperforms economic selection; it claims that when outcomes are comparable, prioritizing family is rational because it delivers the same results while adding stability, dignity, and fiscal efficiency. A policy that treats migrants as interchangeable economic units ignores that sustainable integration is built on relationships. When we have two paths to comparable outcomes, we should choose the one that strengthens the social fabric rather than fragmenting it.
