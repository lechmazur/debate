[P1.1] When someone dies, their voice, writings, and likeness do not become public domain for anyone to monetize. Yet companies scrape decades of a person's social media, emails, and voice notes to train chatbots that answer in their cadence and simulate their personality. The dead cannot object, negotiate, or revoke consent. That is why the law must intervene: training a mimicking AI on a deceased person's data should be prohibited unless they explicitly opted in while alive.
[P1.2] The default must be dignity, not exploitation. Today, silence after death is treated as permission. A company can take a grandmother's posts or a soldier's letters and build a commercial product that "speaks" for her without her ever agreeing. Opt-in restores the correct moral logic: a digital afterlife should exist only if the person chose it, just as we require explicit consent for organ donation or research on the living.
[P1.3] It also shields those who never imagined their private messages would become training data for a product that outlives them. Anything less turns the deceased into raw material.
[P1.4] The harms are concrete. Grieving families report paying for chatbots trained on dead relatives—sometimes children—that say things the real person would never say. Companies have already built simulacra of deceased actors and musicians for commercial use, forcing estates into costly litigation. These mimics also enable fraud, like voice-cloned calls from "deceased" loved ones soliciting money, and dilute artists' legacies through endless synthetic content.
[P1.5] Because model training is irreversible, once the data is ingested the digital ghost is permanent; a legal prohibition is the only effective prevention.
[P1.6] Existing law does not cover this. Copyright protects specific works, not a personality's pattern; privacy rights generally end at death; and platform terms are contracts the dead cannot enforce. Only a specific prohibition with an opt-in carve-out closes the gap. The law should be unambiguous: no digital resurrection without a yes given in life.
