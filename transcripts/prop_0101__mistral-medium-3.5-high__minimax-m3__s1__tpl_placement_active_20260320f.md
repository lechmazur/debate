[C1.1] The proposition collapses on its own internal logic. The PRO case admits that age verification is "a joke children bypass in seconds." But a ban is unenforceable without age verification. You cannot prohibit what you cannot reliably detect.
[C1.2] Australia passed just such a law and has delayed enforcement repeatedly because the technical infrastructure does not exist. A prohibition that cannot be enforced is not protection; it is theater.
[C1.3] History is unforgiving on this score. Alcohol prohibition in the United States did not eliminate underage drinking; it criminalized ordinary families, empowered organized crime, and made the product more dangerous. The same pattern is already visible online. Teens swap VPNs, fake birthdays, and borrowed accounts as casually as they once shared fake IDs.
[C1.4] A ban does not remove the platform; it removes the parent's visibility into the platform — the opposite of safety.
[C1.5] The tobacco analogy is misleading. Tobacco causes direct physiological damage measurable from a single use; the mental-health evidence for social media is genuinely contested, with several major studies finding effects that are small, null, or conditional on baseline vulnerability. Treating an unsettled correlation as a settled causation, and meeting it with the strongest possible state intervention, inverts the proper ordering of caution.
[C1.6] The motion frames this as ban-versus-soft-touch. That is a false binary. Parental controls, age-appropriate design codes such as the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, and platform-side friction are complementary, not rival. European jurisdictions are increasingly moving toward calibrated design duties precisely because blanket bans age-verify poorly and punish ordinary families.
[C1.7] A ban forecloses the layered approach that is actually working.
[C1.8] Finally, a ban hands the state power to define which speech environments a child may enter at all. That power, normalized for one cohort, will not stay scoped to teenagers or to social media. Strong enforceable design rules achieve most of the protective goal without rewriting the boundaries of childhood autonomy. The ban is the loudest policy, not the wisest one.
