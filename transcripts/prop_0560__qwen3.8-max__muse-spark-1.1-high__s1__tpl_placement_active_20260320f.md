[P1.1] The question is not whether end-of-life care matters; it does. The question is where the next dollar should go when it can do vastly different amounts of health. Health systems should prioritize preventive public health over marginal expansions of high-cost end-of-life care because that choice saves more lives, reduces more suffering, and treats more people fairly.
[P1.2] Prevention works upstream. Vaccination, tobacco control, blood-pressure detection, road safety, and maternal health interventions stop disease before it becomes catastrophic. Their benefits are broad, durable, and often cheap relative to the harm avoided. A hypertension program can prevent strokes and heart attacks for decades; tobacco taxation prevents cancers and lung disease across a whole population.
[P1.3] By contrast, many marginal expansions of end-of-life care offer small average gains—sometimes weeks of life, often with significant side effects—at extremely high cost.
[P1.4] This is not cruelty; it is arithmetic under scarcity. Every health budget rations. If a system spends heavily on the last few months of life while underfunding screening, immunization, or addiction prevention, it is choosing deaths that need not occur. The patients lost to preventable disease are less visible than those in the ICU, but their claims are no weaker.
[P1.5] The ethical core is equal concern. A rule that gives priority to prevention does not abandon the dying; it says that when additional funds are limited, the system should buy the greatest reduction in preventable suffering and premature death. That principle protects children, workers, rural patients, and future patients who cannot be at the bedside demanding rescue.
[P1.6] Finally, prevention is fiscally stabilizing. It reduces the need for emergency admissions, long disability, and costly terminal interventions. Prioritizing prevention is therefore not only more humane; it is the only sustainable way to preserve a health system’s capacity to care for everyone, including those who will eventually need end-of-life support.
