[P1.1] Violent crime is not a mist that settles evenly over a city. It concentrates in a small number of blocks, corners, and late-night corridors. That single fact decides this motion. Expanded hot spot policing with tight accountability aims at the places and moments where violence actually happens.
[P1.2] Longer sentences for repeat offenders aim at people after the harm is done. Prevention at the hot spot beats punishment after the fact.
[P1.3] Hot spot policing works through certainty, visibility, and disruption. When officers are consistently present in a violent micro-place, they interrupt the disputes, drug-market turf fights, and retaliation cycles that produce shootings. They also raise the perceived risk of getting caught—not years later, but right then. Tight accountability is not a concession; it is the mechanism that keeps the strategy legitimate.
[P1.4] Body cameras, clear stop standards, supervision, and community oversight reduce abusive stops and indiscriminate dragnets that destroy the cooperation police need to solve violence.
[P1.5] Longer sentences operate on a different and weaker lever: severity. Most violent crime is not committed by a calculator weighing an extra five years against a Tuesday night argument. And the people already most likely to age out of violence are locked up past their highest-risk years, at enormous cost. Severity also depends on arrest, prosecution, and conviction—steps that hot spot policing can make more likely, but prison terms cannot.
[P1.6] Yes, a small number of repeat offenders drive much violence—but hot spots are where those offenders and their disputes concentrate. Place-based policing reaches them before the next victim, while a longer sentence can only arrive after the next conviction. Longer sentences cannot saturate a corner, separate two rivals at 2 a.m., or interrupt a retaliation cycle before it starts.
[P1.7] With accountability, hot spots can be saturated without becoming occupations. That is the strategy most likely to reduce violent crime more. Vote PRO.
