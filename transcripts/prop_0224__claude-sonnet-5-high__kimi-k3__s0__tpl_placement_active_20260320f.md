[P1.1] The proposition asks which dollar buys faster decarbonization between now and 2036. The clearest answer is hiding in plain sight: keep the reactors we already have running, and layer new nuclear on top where possible. That combination beats an equivalent-scale wind-solar-battery build because it wins on the metric that actually matters — dollars per ton of CO2 avoided, in this decade, accounting for the whole system, not just the generator.
[P1.2] Start with life-extension, which is the real engine of this case. A reactor that's paid off its capital cost and passes a safety review can run another 20 years for a fraction of what it costs to build new capacity of any kind. The plant, the grid connection, the workforce, the cooling system — all sunk, all already integrated. Every dollar spent on license renewal and refurbishment buys firm, 90%-plus-capacity-factor clean power immediately, with no need to build new transmission lines or backup capacity.
[P1.3] Compare that to a wind or solar dollar, which buys generation that runs 25-45% of the time and requires additional dollars — often public dollars — for transmission upgrades, interconnection queues, and storage to make that intermittent output usable at 3am or during a windless week in January.
[P1.4] That's the crux: an "equivalent scale-up" of renewables isn't equivalent in system terms. To match the reliable output of an existing nuclear fleet, you need renewables plus firming — batteries, gas peakers, or overbuilt capacity — and that firming cost is real, is often publicly subsidized, and eats into the emissions-per-dollar case badly.
[P1.5] There's also a brutal counterfactual on the table: when nuclear plants retire early, the empirical pattern across multiple grids has been replacement by gas, not clean energy, because permitting and build-out for renewables can't keep pace with retirement schedules. So life-extension dollars don't just add clean power — they prevent an emissions increase that would otherwise occur. New-build nuclear, slower as it is, adds a second layer of firm capacity precisely where the fastest wins already run out.
