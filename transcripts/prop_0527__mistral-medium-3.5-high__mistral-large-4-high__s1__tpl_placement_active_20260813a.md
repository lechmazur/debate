[P1.1] Antibiotic resistance threatens to undo a century of medical progress, yet antibiotic R&D has collapsed as major firms exit the field. Advance market commitments solve this by guaranteeing developers a return—countries commit to purchasing set quantities at agreed prices if predefined efficacy targets are met.
[P1.2] High unit prices are not a flaw but the necessary mechanism that makes AMCs work. They compensate for low sales volumes by ensuring each dose reflects its value as a last-resort treatment against resistant infections. [P1.3] Countries can mitigate high unit costs through bulk procurement, insurance pooling, or by structuring AMCs to encourage post-exclusivity competition. Meanwhile, the cost of inaction is catastrophic: without new antibiotics, routine surgeries and chemotherapy become high-risk, and resistance could push tens of millions into poverty by 2050. Permitting AMCs, even with high prices, is the only proven way to restart the pipeline. The alternative isn’t cheaper drugs—it’s no drugs at all.
