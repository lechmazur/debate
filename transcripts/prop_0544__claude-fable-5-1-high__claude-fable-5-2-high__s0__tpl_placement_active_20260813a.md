If it is a firearm, roughly nine in ten attempts end in death. If it is pills, a blade, or anything else commonly at hand, the large majority survive, and most of those who survive never die by suicide at all. That asymmetry is the whole debate.
[P1.2] The resolution asks a comparison: does age-21 cut homicide and suicide more than it drives people toward illegal guns or other weapons? Notice that substitution to other weapons is not a cost that offsets the benefit. It is the benefit. Someone who substitutes a less lethal method is exactly the person the law saved.
[P1.3] For suicide, substitution would have to be nearly complete and nearly as lethal to cancel the gain, and no other common method comes close.
[P1.4] For homicide, 18-to-20-year-olds commit gun violence at rates wildly out of proportion to their share of the population, and their violence is disproportionately impulsive and retaliatory. The Uvalde and Buffalo shooters each legally bought rifles at eighteen, days before killing. A law that converts "walk in and buy today" into "find a straw purchaser, risk a felony, wait" is not a formality.
[P1.5] Delay is lethal for impulsivity in the good direction: it lets the moment pass.
[P1.6] Will some get guns anyway? Of course. But illegal acquisition is already how most criminal guns move; the policy does not create that channel, it adds friction at the legal margin where friction works. Fake IDs did not undo the drinking age; teen traffic deaths fell anyway.
[P1.7] CON must show not that evasion exists, but that it is large enough to outweigh the deadliest three years of young adulthood losing instant legal access to the deadliest means.
