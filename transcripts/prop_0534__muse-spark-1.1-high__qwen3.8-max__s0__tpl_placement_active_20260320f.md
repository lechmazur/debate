[C1.1] The proposition assumes the only legitimate response to crypto crime is waiting until a wallet is directly tied to criminal conduct. That is an after-the-fact standard for a before-the-fact risk. Self-custody wallets are not merely private safes; they are bearer payment instruments that can move value across borders instantly, pseudonymously, and irreversibly. When ransomware gangs, sanctions evaders, and fraud networks rely on unhosted wallets, law enforcement cannot be expected to wait for a direct criminal link before restricting the instruments that make the harm possible.
[C1.2] PRO’s burden rule sounds principled, but it collapses in practice. A direct tie often appears only after funds have been mixed, bridged, or converted through offshore OTC desks. By then, victims cannot be made whole and national-security sanctions may already be undermined. The off-ramp strategy is weaker than PRO suggests because value need not immediately touch regulated exchanges: peer-to-peer payments, darknet markets, illicit exchangers, and cross-chain swaps can keep proceeds in self-custody ecosystems.
[C1.3] If the default legality of these wallets is absolute until specific crime is proved, regulators lose the preventive tools used throughout finance: transaction limits, identity checks, record-keeping, and restrictions on high-risk transfer channels.
[C1.4] The comparison to cash also fails. Cash remains legal, but governments still regulate large cash movements, reporting, smuggling, and structuring precisely because physical currency can be abused. We do not treat every object capable of carrying value as immune from regulation until a particular crime is proven. Self-custody wallets deserve the same risk-based treatment.
[C1.5] Political protest does not require a globally untraceable payment rail.
[C1.6] Finally, PRO’s safety argument ignores that self-custody is also unforgiving: lost keys, phishing, coercion, and inheritance failures leave ordinary users without recourse. It would tie regulators to an impossible evidentiary trigger while harms scale globally. The right policy is not criminalization without proof, but legality conditioned on sensible safeguards. The motion bars that balance, and that is why it should fail.
