[P1.1] Coal is the worst large-scale power source we still run on purpose. It emits more carbon per unit of electricity than almost any rival on the grid, and it keeps poisoning the air while governments treat the calendar as a public-relations device. A global phaseout of unabated coal by 2040 is not a stunt. It is the last date that still treats a livable climate as a constraint rather than a slogan.
[P1.2] Unabated is the right word. A plant that actually captures and stores its carbon can stay in the argument. A plant that burns and vents cannot. That line targets the harm, not the fuel in the abstract, and it gives every government an engineering off-ramp instead of a culture war.
[P1.3] Voluntary pledges have already failed. Countries announce peak years, then finance new mines and new boilers. Investors read the absence of penalty as permission, so the plant gets built and locks in another generation of emissions. A 2040 backstop with trade or finance consequences changes the spreadsheet now.
[P1.4] Keep adding unabated coal past the line, and you should face carbon-border treatment, closed export-credit windows, and tighter multilateral lending. Those tools already exist. Using them against the dirtiest power source is not imperialism. It is how you stop one chimney from pricing another country’s coastline.
[P1.5] Fairness is not served by late coal. Poor households need electricity, not a fifty-year stranded asset. Solar, wind, storage, and grids can meet that demand faster when capital is pushed away from coal instead of toward it. Transition money should go to workers and wires, not to a license to keep building the problem.
[P1.6] Without an enforceable date, we get more lock-in and a later, harsher crash. With one, capital moves, and the phaseout becomes a schedule instead of a wish.

[C1.4] Second, the penalties are self-defeating. Trade sanctions and closed lending windows don’t surgically strike coal plants; they raise the cost of capital across an entire economy. [C1.5] You starve the alternative along with the incumbent.
[C1.6] Third, the enforcement mechanism destroys the diplomatic architecture that actually works. Paris succeeded where Kyoto-style mandates failed precisely because it is nationally determined. [C1.7] Note, too, PRO’s convenient escape hatch: “unabated.” Wealthy countries can afford carbon capture theater; poor ones cannot. The line PRO calls neutral engineering is, in practice, a wealth test.
[C1.8] The real choice is not deadline versus drift. It is coercion versus financed transition—technology transfer, concessional capital, deals like South Africa’s just-transition partnership. Carrots are scaling coal retirement now. Sticks will freeze it.
