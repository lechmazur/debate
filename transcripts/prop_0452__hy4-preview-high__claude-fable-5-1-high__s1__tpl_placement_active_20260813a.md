[P1.1] The choice before India is not coal forever versus renewables today. It is whether to keep already-built coal plants running a little longer while India industrializes, or to retire them before reliable substitute power is ready. Prioritizing rapid manufacturing growth—and accepting that this delays the retirement of existing coal assets—is the only strategy that delivers jobs, energy security, and the industrial base required to decarbonize later.
[P1.2] Manufacturing cannot run on intermittent power alone. A textile mill in Tiruppur, a steel re-roller in Gujarat, or a solar-panel factory in Rajasthan needs firm, around-the-clock electricity at predictable cost. Existing coal plants are already built, fueled by domestic reserves, and dispatchable; their emissions are largely sunk. Renewables are falling in price, but without vast new storage, transmission, and grid flexibility they cannot yet carry heavy industrial load every hour.
[P1.3] Forcing coal retirement too early means more outages, expensive imported gas or diesel backup, idle factories, and lost orders—hardly a climate win.
[P1.4] Manufacturing growth is itself climate strategy. India has a vast young workforce, low per-capita income, and minimal historical emissions. Factory jobs create the wages, tax revenues, and engineering capacity that pay for grid upgrades, battery gigafactories, and eventually carbon capture or clean firm power. A wealthier India can afford a fast coal phase-out; a deindustrializing India cannot.
[P1.5] Critically, this is a delay in retiring existing assets, not a mandate to build new coal. Using sunk capital for a decade more is a bridge, not an anchor. Premature retirement would raise power prices, cost jobs, and trigger backlash that destroys the political consensus for climate action. The concrete, near-term gains—millions of jobs and reliable industrial power—far outweigh the marginal emissions from plants that already stand.
[P1.6] India should grow now, keep the lights on with today’s coal fleet, and spend the dividends of growth on a genuinely clean grid. That is how it industrializes first and decarbonizes next.
