[P1.1] The immigration court backlog isn't a neutral inconvenience—it is itself the largest source of both unfairness and noncompliance in the system, and clearing it through more judges and remote hearings attacks the problem at its root.
[P1.2] Start with what delay actually does. Under the current backlog, cases routinely wait years for resolution. For someone with a genuine asylum claim, that means years in legal limbo—unable to work legally in some periods, unable to plan, often separated from family. For someone with no viable claim, it means years during which removal never happens, which teaches everyone that deadlines are decorative.
[P1.3] Compliance is not a personality trait; it's a response to whether the system's instructions seem real. When hearings are scheduled years out and notices arrive for dates that get moved repeatedly, people stop treating them as binding. A functioning calendar, where a hearing notice means a hearing, is the single cheapest compliance tool the government has.
[P1.4] On fairness, the mechanism runs the same direction. Judges drowning in thousands of cases render decisions in minutes, often on incomplete records. Adding judges means more time per case, not less. That directly reduces wrongful removals—because error in these courts is overwhelmingly a product of crushing caseloads, missing documents, and unrepresented respondents, not of scheduling efficiency.
[P1.5] Remote hearings compound the gain. They let a detained respondent appear with counsel who is hundreds of miles away, let interpreters be scheduled reliably, and let evidence be filed and viewed without paper shuffling between facilities. Every one of those fixes makes a wrong decision less likely.
[P1.6] The opposition must argue that speed itself manufactures error. But wrongful removals come from thin records and unrepresented litigants—problems that staffing and remote access alleviate. The status quo isn't careful slowness; it's chaotic slowness. Delay doesn't protect the vulnerable—it erodes their evidence, their witnesses, and their memory while they wait.
[P1.7] Faster courts, staffed properly, protect them.
