[P1.2] Seasonal switching is indefensible. Losing an hour in spring disrupts sleep for days, followed plausibly by more crashes, workplace errors, and cardiovascular strain as millions wake before their biology is ready. The question is not whether to stop switching. It is which permanent time we lock in.
[P1.3] Permanent daylight saving time locks in the worse one. Standard time roughly puts midday near solar noon. Shifting forward permanently moves sunrise and sunset an hour later. That sounds pleasant in June, but in December at northern latitudes and on western edges of time zones, sunrise can approach 9 a.m.
[P1.4] Workers commute in darkness, children wait for buses in darkness, and people lose the morning light that anchors circadian rhythm.
[P1.5] That light is not a preference; it is physiology. Bright light after waking suppresses melatonin, sets the body clock, and makes it easier to sleep at night. Evening light does the opposite, pushing sleep later while school and work starts stay fixed. Permanent summer time therefore creates chronic social jet lag: later natural sleep, earlier required waking, shorter sleep — hardest on teenagers, early-shift workers, and western communities.
[P1.6] Over time, poor sleep is bound up with obesity, diabetes, depression, and worse learning and productivity.
[P1.7] Evening leisure and retail gains do not outweigh that. Modern lighting and air conditioning have eroded old energy-saving claims, and summer evenings are already long under standard time. What permanent standard time protects is the scarce resource: winter morning light.
[P1.8] History warns the same way: year-round daylight time in the 1970s United States and more recently in Russia was abandoned after backlash over dark winter mornings. Abolish the switch — but choose health, safety, and solar alignment permanently.
