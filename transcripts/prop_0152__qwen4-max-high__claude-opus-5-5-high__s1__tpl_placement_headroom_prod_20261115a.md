[P1.1] Start with the phrase that decides this: some room. Not sovereignty, not exemption from law — a narrow permission that evaporates the moment exit becomes fictional. Any horror story about a coerced employee or a trapped child describes a case this proposition already excludes.
[P1.2] Now consider what the flat ban buys. A Sikh academy where boys wear turbans, a Jewish school where kippot are expected, a Muslim school where hijab is unremarkable — visible shared practice is how a minority community carries itself across generations. Order those schools to be religiously neutral in dress and you have not produced neutrality; you have made the majority's default clothing the only permitted public appearance. France's restrictions on conspicuous religious symbols in state schools landed hardest on Muslim girls, which is what "neutral" rules usually do.
[P1.3] The conditions are not decoration; they are the enforcement mechanism. Genuinely voluntary means informed at entry, reversible without penalty, and no retaliation for leaving. Realistic alternatives means a comparable school or job actually reachable — not a theoretical option three towns away that costs a family its livelihood. A religious hospital that is the only employer in a valley holds no permission here.
[P1.4] Neither does the sole school in a district. Where consent is a fiction, the permission lapses by its own terms.
[P1.5] So the opposing case must show that conditional permission is worse than prohibition — that a family freely choosing a faith-shaped schooling should be denied it so nobody anywhere can be pressured. That trades real, exercised liberty for administrative tidiness. And the burden of proving exit is real belongs on the institution, not the person leaving. The remedy for counterfeit consent is to police the conditions hard, not to abolish the choice.
