[P1.1] A paper ticket was never revocable. You bought it, and if your plans changed, you handed it to a friend. Digital ticketing quietly abolished that right—not through any law, but through rotating barcodes and locked accounts. The proposition restores that baseline of ownership, with one sensible exception built in.
[P1.2] Start with the principle: a ticket is property. Once sold, the organizer's legitimate interest—being paid—has been satisfied. Everything after that is the buyer's business. No bookstore may forbid you from giving away a novel you bought; no restaurant resells your chair if you cancel.
[P1.3] Non-transferable tickets are pure post-sale control, and anyone defending them must explain why a concert should be the one purchase you never truly own.
[P1.4] Then look at who transfer bans actually hit. Not professional scalpers—they operate at industrial scale through bots and broker networks, and a transfer toggle barely inconveniences them. The people burned are ordinary fans: the nurse whose shift changes, the parent whose child gets sick, the friend who can't make it. Under a locked-ticket regime they eat the cost or the seat sits empty.
[P1.5] Multiply that across a season and you get worse attendance, worse atmosphere, and fans subsidizing rigidity.
[P1.6] Follow the money and the picture sharpens. Blanket transfer bans don't eliminate resale; they reroute it. When the primary seller also runs the only permitted resale platform, "anti-scalping" becomes a license to sell the same seat twice and collect fees twice. Fans should not mistake market capture for consumer protection.
[P1.7] Finally, notice what the motion already concedes. Organizers may cap resale prices, require face-value fan exchanges, limit tickets per buyer, delay barcode delivery—tools aimed squarely at scalping. Several jurisdictions already require a transferable option alongside exactly such limits, and the sky has not fallen. So the real clash is narrow: can a total ban on fan-to-fan transfer be justified when the motion itself hands organizers precise instruments?
[P1.8] Blunt prohibitions that punish fans while enriching gatekeepers cannot survive that comparison.
