[C1.1] PRO concedes AI aids mastery practice, then draws a line that worsens writing instruction. In real classrooms, the alternative is not rich human feedback for every draft; it is one overworked teacher responding to 120 essays weeks late. The main feedback source should be whatever gives students frequent, actionable, low-stakes revision while they still remember their intentions. AI is uniquely able to do that.
[C1.2] PRO says writing feedback is divergent and requires judgment. But most adolescent writing does not suffer from subtle misreading of genius; it suffers from silence, delay, and inconsistent standards. A student drafting an argument about Macbeth needs to know tonight whether the claim answers the prompt, whether evidence is introduced, whether sentences are coherent. AI can provide that in minutes and demand five revisions.
[C1.3] A teacher may provide one summative comment at the end. Feedback that arrives late becomes a postscript; feedback that arrives during drafting becomes instruction.
[C1.4] PRO also assumes AI feedback is generic and teaches polished vagueness. That mistakes a poorly designed prompt for the technology's ceiling. Modern writing tutors can be configured to reward specificity, thesis risk, counterargument, and textual evidence. They can flag unsupported assertions, ask what objection a skeptic would make, and require revision.
[C1.5] They can separate grammar from ideas, so students do not chase surface polish. The danger PRO identifies is solved by better feedback design and teacher oversight, not by banning AI from the main role. The teacher remains the final audience, setting standards and intervening on high-stakes work.
[C1.6] The decisive standard is learning outcomes under realistic constraints. If AI as the main feedback source doubles drafting volume, accelerates revision cycles, and frees teachers for conferences and final grading, students write more and improve more. The motion would preserve a system where feedback is scarce and slow, then call that scarcity human judgment.
