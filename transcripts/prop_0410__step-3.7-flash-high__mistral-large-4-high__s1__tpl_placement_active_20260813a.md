[P1.1] The central claim of this motion is not that same-sex parenting is identical to different-sex parenting, but that there is no meaningful average gap in child outcomes between the two, and that policy ignoring this fact inflicts avoidable harm on children and families. Decades of large-scale, controlled research reviewed by every major professional body representing child development experts, pediatricians, and social workers find no statistically significant differences between children raised by same-sex and different-sex parents on core metrics: academic achievement, social and emotional adjustment, mental health, and long-term well-being.
[P1.2] Any small gaps that appear in unadjusted data disappear entirely when controlling for socioeconomic stability, the single strongest predictor of child success. Those unadjusted gaps stem from the systemic discrimination same-sex families face, not their family structure, creating extra stress and resource barriers for their children. Current policy in many jurisdictions still codifies the false assumption that same-sex parent families are inherently less fit: adoption bans, refusal to recognize non-biological parents as legal guardians, barriers to foster care placement, and denial of equal benefits force these families to navigate unnecessary hurdles that reduce stability for the children in their care.
[P1.3] A child in foster care, for instance, may be denied placement with a qualified, loving same-sex couple solely because of discriminatory policy, leaving that child in a less stable group home or temporary placement for months longer than necessary. Opponents of this motion often cite small, non-representative studies or ignore confounding variables like poverty and discrimination, but the broad consensus of rigorous research is unambiguous. The two halves of this motion are inextricably linked: the evidence is clear that what matters for children is having committed, resourced caregivers, not the genders of their parents, and policy that reflects that evidence will expand access to stable, loving homes for kids who need them.
