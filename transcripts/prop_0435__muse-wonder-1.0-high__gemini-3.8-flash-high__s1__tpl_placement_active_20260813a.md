[P1.1] Later closing is not just a longer party. It is a city moving thousands of intoxicated people through quiet streets at 2, 3, and 4 a.m. The profit from those extra hours goes to the venue. The vomit on the sidewalk, the bass through an apartment wall, and the drunk driver searching for a way home do not.
[P1.2] Those costs land on residents, cleaners, police, and everyone sharing the road. Extended hours should therefore be earned with a plan, not granted as a free public subsidy.
[P1.3] First, transit is safety. When venues close after trains and buses stop, patrons do not disappear. They drive when they should not, crowd unlit corners waiting for expensive rides, or wander long distances home. A later license without late transport is a predictable recipe for crashes and assaults.
[P1.4] Requiring a stronger transit plan — extended night buses, coordinated last-train timing, staffed pickup zones, or venue contributions to shared service — turns dispersal into a managed flow instead of a nightly scramble.
[P1.5] Second, sanitation and noise mitigation protect the neighbors who never consented to become part of the venue. Extra hours mean extra bottles, waste, street urination, and amplified sound when people are trying to sleep before work or school. Requiring cleaning beyond the front door, sound limits and insulation, door staff who move crowds on quietly, and a real complaint response does not kill nightlife. It stops one business from exporting its operating costs onto the block.
[P1.6] Finally, this conditional approach is what makes later hours durable and fair. Cities can approve some venues — those in suitable districts that meet the standard, alone or through a shared district plan — while denying a free pass to those that cannot operate responsibly. Without that bargain, backlash builds, residents organize, and councils roll hours back for everyone. With it, nightlife gains legitimacy, residents gain protection, and the city gains a night economy that can actually last.

Conditional licenses create staggering: approved venues in suitable districts close later, spreading the peak, but with transport, lighting, pickup zones, and quiet-dispersal staff attached.
[P2.5] The choice is not between perfect public funding and private tolls. It is between the venue internalizing the night it profits from, or residents and early-morning workers continuing to subsidize it with sleep, safety, and cleanup.
