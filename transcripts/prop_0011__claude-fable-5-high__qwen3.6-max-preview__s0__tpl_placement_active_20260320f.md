[P1.1] When evidence on academic gains is mixed, the decisive question becomes: who gets to choose? Wealthy parents already have school choice — they buy it through housing markets and private tuition. The only families bound to a single assigned school, regardless of whether it works for their child, are those without money. Vouchers extend to a janitor's daughter the exit option a lawyer's son has always enjoyed.
[P1.2] That is a matter of basic fairness, and it doesn't wait on a unanimous verdict from the research literature.
[P1.3] Notice what "mixed evidence" actually means. It means vouchers produce roughly comparable academic results — sometimes better, sometimes flat — typically at lower per-pupil cost, while delivering things test scores never capture: parental satisfaction, student safety, schools matched to a child's needs. Studies of choice programs have repeatedly found gains in graduation rates and parent-reported wellbeing even where test scores move little. If two systems deliver similar academics but one costs less and parents prefer it, "mixed evidence" is an argument *for* expansion, not against it.
[P1.4] The burden should fall on those who would compel attendance, not those who would permit choice.
[P1.5] Now the resource objection. Money following a child to a new school is not a school "losing resources" any more than a grocery store loses resources when a customer shops elsewhere. The student leaves, and so do the costs of educating that student — and since vouchers are usually worth less than full per-pupil spending, districts often keep money for children they no longer teach. Public schools exist to serve children; children do not exist to fund institutions.
[P1.6] Finally, exit pressure works. Districts facing competition have real incentives to improve, respond to parents, and stop taking enrollment for granted — a discipline monopolies never face.
[P1.7] The case is simple: equal access to choice, comparable outcomes at lower cost, and accountability through exit. Mixed evidence is no excuse for trapping children in schools that are failing them right now.
