[P1.1] When a rider pays forty dollars for a surge-priced trip across town, they naturally assume their driver is reaping the reward of high demand. Meanwhile, in the front seat, the driver sees a fourteen-dollar payout, wondering why fares are so low. Both parties are being played by the same black box. Modern ride-hail platforms have decoupled what riders pay from what drivers earn, using opaque algorithms to charge riders the maximum they will bear while offering drivers the bare minimum they will accept.
[P1.2] Requiring platforms to display the full fare split by default ends this manufactured information asymmetry and restores basic market honesty.
[P1.3] First, transparency is a prerequisite for a functioning market. Ride-hail companies insist they are merely digital marketplaces connecting independent businesses—the drivers—with consumers. Yet in no other legitimate brokerage market does the intermediary hide its commission on every transaction. When the platform’s take rate fluctuates wildly and invisibly from trip to trip, neither riders nor drivers can evaluate the true cost of the intermediary service.
[P1.4] Showing the split by default forces platforms to compete on operational efficiency and fair commissions rather than on who can build the most extractive pricing algorithm.
[P1.5] Second, opacity distorts decisions inside the car, directly harming drivers. When fares spike during bad weather or rush hour, riders frequently reduce or skip tips because they assume the extra money goes to the person behind the wheel. By hiding the split, platforms quietly capture the surge while leaving drivers to absorb rider frustration and lost gratuities.
[P1.6] Finally, disclosure must be the default, not buried three menus deep in a post-hoc web portal. Information only disciplines a market when it is frictionless and visible on the ride screen itself. If platforms want the legal benefits of classifying drivers as independent contractors, they must provide the basic transactional transparency that any independent actor requires.
