[P1.1] Mandatory nationwide E-Verify for new hires would close the largest remaining open door to unauthorized employment. Paperwork alone is easily forged; an electronic match against Social Security and immigration records is far harder to beat. When every new hire must be checked, unauthorized workers lose reliable access to formal jobs and employers who skip the system face clear liability. The result is not theater or one-off raids, but a structural reduction: jobs that once sustained unauthorized work become scarce or risky enough that many simply exit the formal market or never enter it.
[P1.2] The same mechanism limits discrimination rather than enlarging it. E-Verify does not invite employers to size up accents, names, or appearance; it takes the documents offered and returns a confirmation or a resolvable mismatch. That standardization reduces the incentive for informal profiling that thrives when status checks are discretionary. Existing anti-discrimination rules still apply, and the electronic trail itself creates an audit point that paper hiring never did.
[P1.3] Claims of sweeping new bias ignore how the system works: it is color-blind by design once the worker is past the door.
[P1.4] Labor shortages will not explode either. Unauthorized labor fills niches, yet markets adjust. Wages rise, legal workers—citizens, green-card holders, visa holders—are drawn in, tasks are reorganized or automated, and recruitment widens. States that already imposed E-Verify on larger employers or public contractors did not face permanent mass vacancies; temporary frictions appear, then recede.
[P1.5] Agriculture and construction feel the shift first, but they do not define the entire economy, and those sectors have repeatedly shown capacity to adapt when rules tighten.
[P1.6] The proposition therefore stands on a straightforward comparison. Deterrence and detection scale nationwide and hit unauthorized employment where it lives—at the point of hire—while the predicted side effects of large-scale discrimination or lasting labor shortfalls are overstated relative to the system’s structure and ordinary market responses. That is the path that delivers meaningful reduction without the claimed trade-offs.
