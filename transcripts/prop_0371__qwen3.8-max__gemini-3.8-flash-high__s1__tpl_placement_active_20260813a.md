[P1.1] The strongest reason to adopt this rule is that carry-on baggage is no longer functioning as an optional add-on; it has become a basic component of air travel that airlines use to make headline fares look artificially low. A reasonable baseline—one small overhead bag plus a personal item—should be included in the ticket price, while airlines remain free to charge for larger, heavier, or additional bags.
[P1.2] The core problem is not that airlines charge for extras. It is that fragmentation destroys price comparison and turns boarding into a series of traps. A traveler comparing two fares cannot easily tell whether the cheaper ticket includes overhead space, especially when rules vary by fare class, route, loyalty tier, and aircraft. The result is a market where advertised prices understate the true cost of a normal trip.
[P1.3] That harms consumers and punishes honest competitors, because carriers that include a carry-on look more expensive than rivals selling stripped tickets and adding fees later.
[P1.4] This is not a trivial convenience issue. Many passengers need a carry-on for work trips, family travel, medication, fragile items, or tight connections where checked bags may not arrive. For them, the fee is effectively unavoidable. When an unavoidable cost is hidden outside the base fare, it operates less like choice and more like bait-and-switch pricing.
[P1.5] A baseline rule would also improve operations. Clear, inclusive carry-on rights reduce gate confrontations, last-minute bag checks, and boarding delays caused by passengers trying to avoid fees by cramming items under seats or disputing size limits. Airlines still control safety and space limits; they simply must sell the basic ticket with a known allowance.
[P1.6] The central clash is simple: the opposing side treats baggage fees as optional pricing flexibility. But if the fee is predictable, widespread, and necessary for ordinary travel, the market needs one transparent standard. Requiring a reasonable carry-on baseline restores honest pricing without banning legitimate upcharges for genuinely extra baggage.
