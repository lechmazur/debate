[P1.1] The need for long-term care is a catastrophic, unpredictable financial risk. While everyone ages, no one knows if they will suddenly pass away or require a decade of intensive dementia care. Treating this ruinous cost as an individual or family responsibility is a profound policy failure that demands a universal social insurance solution.
[P1.2] First, the private market for long-term care is structurally broken. Because only those who suspect they will need care buy into the market, insurers face severe adverse selection. To survive, they charge exorbitant premiums, cap benefits, or simply exit the market entirely. Consequently, the vast majority of people cannot afford private policies.
[P1.3] When private savings inevitably run out, the current means-tested fallback forces seniors to literally impoverish themselves—liquidating a lifetime of savings and assets—just to qualify for basic safety-net care. A universal system pools the risk across the entire population, solving the adverse selection problem and providing care without requiring seniors to become destitute first.
[P1.4] Second, relying on family care inflicts massive collateral damage on the broader economy. When formal care is unaffordable, the burden falls overwhelmingly on unpaid family members, predominantly women. This forces prime-age workers to reduce their hours or abandon their careers entirely to manage medications and daily needs. The macroeconomic cost is immense: lost wages, diminished tax revenues, and lower retirement savings for the caregivers, which perpetuates generational poverty.
[P1.5] Universal long-term care systems keep working-age adults in the labor force, boosting economic growth and gender equity while ensuring the elderly receive professional, safe care.
[P1.6] Finally, demographic realities make the status quo mathematically impossible to sustain. As populations age and birth rates decline, the ratio of potential family caregivers to seniors is collapsing. Families simply cannot absorb this escalating demand. A universal system is not merely a moral imperative for elder dignity; it is an economic necessity to prevent a predictably aging demographic from dragging down the entire workforce and plunging millions of families into financial ruin.
