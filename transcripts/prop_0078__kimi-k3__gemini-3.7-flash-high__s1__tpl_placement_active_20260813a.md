[C1.1] The foundation of a free, liberal democracy is not ideological conformity or linguistic uniformity; it is the rule of law. A legitimate state demands that every resident, citizen and newcomer alike, obey the law, pay taxes, and respect the legal rights of others. But the proposition demands something far more intrusive: transforming a legal compact into a cultural loyalty test by decreeing that adopting civic norms and a common language must be expected rather than treated as an organic, voluntary choice.
[C1.2] First, this motion conflates legal compliance with ideological policing. Society functions through shared legal boundaries, not enforced internal orthodoxy. If an immigrant obeys the law, pays taxes, and respects others, the state has no business policing their cultural philosophies or private values. Free societies protect the right to dissent, to live traditionally, and to remain culturally distinct.
[C1.3] Requiring ideological conformity under the vague banner of "civic norms" undermines the very pluralism it claims to protect.
[C1.4] Second, treating integration as an enforced expectation weaponizes assimilation against the most vulnerable. It frames integration as a one-way burden, ignoring real structural barriers. Elderly relatives, shift workers, and traumatized refugees frequently depend on native-language networks and diaspora communities for mutual aid, childcare, and basic survival. Framing these enclaves as failures of civic duty stigmatizes necessary support systems and fuels nativist hostility rather than resolving it.
[C1.5] Finally, linguistic and cultural autonomy is not a civic hazard; it is a profound strength. Dynamic, multilingual democracies thrive when newcomers can navigate public life on their own terms, contributing diverse perspectives and global connections. Integration is not a prerequisite to be demanded by the state; it is an organic, multi-generational process nurtured by equal opportunity, accessible public services, and mutual respect. For these reasons, cultural integration must remain an open choice, not an enforced mandate.
