[P1.1] The richest people in modern economies have quietly opted out of the tax system. A surgeon earning $400,000 pays income tax on every dollar. A billionaire whose portfolio gains $400 million pays nothing, because unrealized gains aren't "income." He borrows against his shares to fund his lifestyle—loans aren't income either—and when he dies, the stepped-up basis erases the gain forever.
[P1.2] This is the "buy, borrow, die" strategy, and it means our income tax is largely voluntary at the very top. Consumption taxes are worse still: the wealthy consume a tiny fraction of their wealth, so these taxes land hardest on ordinary families.
[P1.3] That's the core of this debate. An income-and-consumption system measures the wrong thing for the wealthiest households. A modest annual tax on large net worth—say, one or two percent above a high threshold like $50 million—measures the right thing: actual economic power, wherever it sits.
[P1.4] Three reasons this wins. First, fairness with teeth. Effective tax rates currently fall as you climb into the top fraction of a percent—working professionals often pay higher rates than billionaires. A wealth tax restores the basic principle that taxation tracks ability to pay.
[P1.5] Second, efficiency. A wealth tax is effectively a tax on capital sitting still. The investor earning strong returns barely notices two percent; the heir parking dynastic wealth in low-yield safety feels it. That nudges capital toward productive use rather than passive accumulation.
[P1.6] Third, democratic health. Extreme concentrated wealth converts directly into political power—lobbying, media ownership, captured regulators. Taxing it annually slows the compounding feedback loop where wealth buys rules that protect wealth.
[P1.7] Anticipate the objections: valuation and avoidance are administrative problems, and we already solve them—property taxes value homes annually, estates are appraised at death, and modern information-sharing has gutted offshore secrecy. Hard is not impossible.
[P1.8] When the current system literally cannot see the largest fortunes, supplementing it isn't radical. It's repair.
