[P1.1] A commodity windfall is not a permanent pay rise. It is the conversion of a finite national asset into money, at a price the country cannot control. The better system must protect citizens when prices collapse and after resources run out. A well-designed sovereign wealth fund addresses both problems; immediate distribution does not.
[P1.2] The first advantage is stability. During a boom, paying out the windfall pushes spending into an economy that may already face shortages of housing, electricity, and skilled workers. That can raise prices and squeeze other exporting industries. Saving part of the proceeds in diversified overseas assets moderates that pressure.
[P1.3] When commodity revenue crashes, rules-based withdrawals can sustain essential services and support households instead of forcing abrupt cuts precisely when jobs are disappearing.
[P1.4] The second advantage is fairness over time. Citizens born after the boom also have a claim on the country’s exhaustible wealth. Distributing the proceeds now gives today’s recipients control over assets that belonged to successive generations. Some households will wisely invest their payments, but private saving cannot guarantee a shared fiscal cushion or preserve a national asset for future citizens.
[P1.5] A fund can.
[P1.6] The strongest objection is corruption: why entrust politicians with a large investment pot? Because the alternative also requires politicians to disclose resource receipts, determine eligibility, and deliver payments honestly. Cash is not institution-free. The answer is a fund with published accounts, independent audits, transparent investments, and legally constrained withdrawals integrated into the budget—not an executive slush fund.
[P1.7] Those safeguards create visible standards against which diversion can be challenged.
[P1.8] Immediate poverty matters, but it does not require distributing the windfall as it arrives. A fund can finance predictable transfers through a sustainable withdrawal rule while retaining reserves. That is not abandoning cash assistance; it is distinguishing assistance from liquidation. Citizens need reliable support through the bust, not merely a larger cheque during the boom.
