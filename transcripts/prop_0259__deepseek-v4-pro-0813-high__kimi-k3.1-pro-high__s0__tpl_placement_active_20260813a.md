[P1.1] Countries should stop shifting the clock and should settle on the setting that matches the sun: permanent standard time. The central test is which framework gives people morning light when they actually need it, while avoiding artificial disruption. Standard time wins.
[P1.2] The case for changing clocks is usually framed as trading an hour of morning light for evening recreation. But daylight saving time creates no daylight; it only relabels it. That relabeling pushes sunrise later and fights the human circadian clock, which uses morning light to stay synchronized. The result is predictable: lost sleep, groggier children, darker commutes, and a repeated hit every spring when the clock jumps forward.
[P1.3] Permanent summer time would be the worst option. It freezes that morning-light deficit year-round. Under permanent summer time, midwinter sunrises in many countries would arrive so late that children would go to school and workers would begin shifts in darkness. That is not cosmetic; it is a safety and mental-health problem.
[P1.4] Historical trials show the public rejects permanent summer time. The United States tried year-round daylight time in the 1970s, and dark winter mornings rapidly eroded support. Russia later adopted permanent summer time and likewise reversed course because mornings became intolerable. These reversals expose the opponent’s dilemma: if you dislike clock changes but still want long summer evenings, you must defend winter mornings people refuse to live with.
[P1.5] Permanent standard time avoids both failures. It ends biannual social jet lag without locking in late sunrises. It keeps clocks closer to solar noon, the reference point human sleep, alertness, and safety evolved around. For children walking to school, shift workers commuting at dawn, and anyone with an early schedule, brighter mornings are a protective factor, not a preference.
[P1.6] Opponents can defend clock changes and accept the spikes in sleep loss and accidents, or defend permanent summer time and accept dark winter mornings. Neither beats permanent standard time on the decisive metric: stable clocks and light when the body needs it.
