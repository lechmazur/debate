[P1.1] Cash bail operates on a fundamentally flawed premise: that a defendant’s bank account correlates with their danger to the community. In reality, money bail does not assess risk; it prices it. A dangerous defendant with financial resources can purchase immediate freedom, while an indigent person accused of a minor, nonviolent infraction languishes in a jail cell. Replacing this archaic system with validated risk assessments and supervised release directly solves this distortion, lowering pretrial jail populations without compromising public safety.
[P1.2] First, consider jail populations. Low-risk individuals are released back to their jobs and families under proportional supervision.
[P1.3] By ending the unnecessary detention of individuals who pose no threat, pretrial jail populations immediately decline.
[P1.4] Second, this shift does not increase violent crime. Supervised release is not an unconditional walk free; it is an individualized, structured management system. [P1.5] Judges gain the legal authority to detain high-risk individuals on public safety grounds alone, regardless of their finances.
[P1.6] Jurisdictions that have pioneered this transition, such as New Jersey and Washington, D.C., prove the model works.
