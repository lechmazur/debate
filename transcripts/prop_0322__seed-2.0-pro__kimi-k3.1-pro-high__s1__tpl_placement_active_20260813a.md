[P1.1] Host-read ads and endorsements only work because of trust. Nobody tunes into a creator for the copywriting of the ad script—they listen because they believe they are hearing the actual voice, the actual judgment of the person they chose to follow. When that voice is generated by AI, when the host never opened their mouth to record those lines, that is not a minor production shortcut. That erases the entire basis of the audience's trust.
[P1.2] This rule does not ban AI voice use. It does not fine creators for saving time on post production. It only requires one single, trivial extra line: telling the audience that this endorsement was voiced by AI. Nobody is harmed by this disclosure, except people who are actively trying to trick their viewers.
[P1.3] This is not some hypothetical future problem. Already right now, creators are running AI-voiced ads for health supplements, financial services, and skincare products where the host never even read the ad copy, let alone tried the product. Audience members have bought dangerous unregulated supplements on the back of that faked trust, with no way to know the endorsement was never real.
[P1.4] We already require creators to disclose that a segment is a paid ad at all. This is just the logical next step of that same principle. The audience deserves to know not just that they are being advertised to, but that the person they trust actually said the words they are hearing. There is no good reason to hide this fact.
