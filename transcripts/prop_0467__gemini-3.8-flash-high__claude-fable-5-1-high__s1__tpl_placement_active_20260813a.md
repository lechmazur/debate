[P1.1] Modern warfare no longer requires gunpowder to inflict catastrophic violence. Severing an electrical grid in sub-zero winter, poisoning a metropolitan water supply, or paralyzing a nation’s emergency hospital networks inflicts mass casualties and societal collapse just as effectively as a salvo of cruise missiles. International law cannot fetishize the instrument over the consequence. When a cyber assault dismantles the lifelines sustaining human life, it is an armed attack in every sense that matters.
[P1.2] International jurisprudence already acknowledges the "scale and effects" test: what defines an armed attack under Article 51 of the UN Charter is the magnitude of devastation, not whether it arrived via missile or malware. Yet hostile regimes deliberately exploit a legal gray zone, treating civilian networks as consequence-free battlegrounds. They gamble that targeted states will paralyze themselves with semantic debate while cities go dark. Formally categorizing infrastructure cyberattacks as armed attacks closes this impunity gap and establishes credible deterrence.
[P1.3] Recognizing equivalence does not mandate reckless escalation; it unlocks lawful, proportional options within the framework of sovereign self-defense. When facing catastrophic destruction, a nation cannot be trapped in an ineffective digital-only exchange, especially against asymmetric adversaries with few networks to counter-strike. Retaining proportional military response options—governed strictly by necessity, distinction, and the laws of armed conflict—denies aggressors an asymmetric sanctuary. It ensures hostile actors cannot dismantle a society from behind a keyboard while claiming immunity from real-world consequences.
[P1.4] A missile that breaches a dam and a keystroke that opens its floodgates produce the exact same horror: drowned communities and civilian slaughter. International law exists to safeguard human survival. It must recognize that when the foundations of civilian life are destroyed, the medium is irrelevant—an act of war has occurred.
