[P1.1] A streaming service should be able to stop making a series. It should not be able to make an already released series legally unwatchable simply because disappearance improves its balance sheet. The distinction is between freedom over future investment and responsibility for works already offered to the public.
[P1.2] Require original films and series to remain publicly available for a defined minimum—say, five years after release. Apply that rule prospectively, so services can budget and negotiate rights accordingly. Availability need not mean permanent inclusion in the original subscription: a service could maintain access itself or arrange a reasonably accessible licensed outlet. This is a distribution obligation, not an order to finance another season or preserve every platform forever.
[P1.3] The central harm is exclusive control becoming exclusive erasure. When a physical release leaves stores, purchased copies can still circulate. When a streaming-only original disappears, there may be no lawful alternative at all. Viewers lose access; creators lose an accessible record of their work; and a film that needs time to find its audience loses that opportunity.
[P1.4] An internal accounting decision can therefore impose costs on people who have no say in it.
[P1.5] Competition does not reliably solve this. Another streaming service cannot simply offer a vanished exclusive without permission. Nor can viewers protect themselves by subscribing elsewhere when the work is unavailable everywhere. The rightsholder controls the very alternative that supposedly disciplines its conduct.
[P1.6] The strongest objection is cost: continued availability can involve royalties, licensing, and administration. But those are reasons for a predictable, limited obligation—not unrestricted withdrawal. Prospective rules let those costs enter commissioning decisions and rights contracts, while alternative distribution avoids requiring every title to retain expensive promotion or prominent placement.
[P1.7] The choice is not between unlimited public access and creative freedom. It is between a modest, planned availability window and allowing exclusive cultural works to vanish whenever removal becomes financially convenient. Public release should carry that minimum responsibility.
