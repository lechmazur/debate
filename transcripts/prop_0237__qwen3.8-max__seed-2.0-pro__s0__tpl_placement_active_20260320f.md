[P1.1] The strongest reason to adopt this proposal is that the current system confuses access to territory with access to protection. Asylum law effectively requires people to reach U.S. soil before their claim is heard. That turns protection into a race won by those with money, smugglers, and endurance—not always those at greatest risk. A safe-third-country framework, paired with expanded legal pathways, replaces that lottery with an orderly system: seek protection in the first genuinely safe country or through U.S.-managed channels, and do not gain extra eligibility by breaking through the southern border.
[P1.2] The exchange is the point: limitation without pathways is rejection; limitation with pathways is triage.
[P1.3] This is the only realistic way to reduce the incentives that enrich cartels and kill migrants. When asylum depends on arrival, the rational message is: pay a smuggler, cross the desert or river, and present yourself at the border. The result is overcrowded facilities, years-long backlogs, and politics that produce harsh improvisations—walls, expulsions, family separation. Sharply limiting border asylum claims removes the smuggling premium.
[P1.4] Expanded humanitarian visas, regional processing, refugee referrals, and labor or family parole then give frightened people a real alternative: a legal application instead of a lethal gamble. A regional appointment is safer than a river crossing.
[P1.5] The opposition will say this abandons refugees. But the status quo abandons them more cruelly. It leaves the most vulnerable—those without funds or connections—unable to reach a port of entry, while it overwhelms officers who must triage weak and strong claims together. CON cannot answer the incentive problem: if anyone may trigger asylum by reaching the border, the border becomes the intake system.
[P1.6] The proposition does not end protection; it relocates it to safer, earlier, and more manageable points. If the goal is saving lives and preserving public support for asylum, the United States should trade chaotic border eligibility for generous legal pathways.
