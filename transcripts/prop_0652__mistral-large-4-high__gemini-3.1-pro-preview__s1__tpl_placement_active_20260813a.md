Like the printing press, the photocopier, and the camera before it, AI is a general-purpose tool. While it can be misused, its primary function is unprecedented creative expression and productivity.
[C1.2] Imposing shared liability on tech platforms for user-prompted infringement would destroy the foundation of digital innovation. We must reject the proposition for three reasons.
[C1.3] First, the proposition violates the established principle that toolmakers are not liable for user misconduct if their technology has substantial non-infringing uses. When Sony introduced the VCR, Hollywood sued, claiming it was a piracy machine. The courts rightly ruled that because the VCR had legitimate uses, Sony was not liable for users who pirated movies. Generative AI is widely used to write code, synthesize research, and design wholly original art.
[C1.4] Holding the platform liable because a fraction of users deliberately prompt it to generate copyrighted material punishes the toolmaker for the sins of the user.
[C1.5] Second, PRO ignores user agency. An AI does not spontaneously generate a Taylor Swift voice clone; a user must explicitly instruct it to do so. The platform provides a digital brush and canvas, but the user chooses what to paint. If someone uses a word processor to plagiarize a novel, we do not sue Microsoft.
[C1.6] Liability must rest with the individual who possesses the intent and directs the specific action.
[C1.7] Third, shared liability would force platforms into extreme, preemptive over-censorship. To avoid ruinous litigation, platforms would have to implement aggressive filters that blindly block any output remotely resembling existing works. This would severely restrict fair use, parody, and transformative art. Furthermore, only the largest tech monopolies could afford the massive compliance and legal costs, immediately crippling open-source AI and small startups.
[C1.8] The current notice-and-takedown system already allows creators to remove infringing content without strangling innovation. Forcing platforms to share liability for what their users choose to create is a destructive overreach.
