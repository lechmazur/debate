[P1.1] A credit report has one job: to tell a lender how someone is likely to handle a debt they choose to take on. Judge every argument by that test. Medical debt fails it, because it records what happened to a person's body, not how that person handles obligations.

[P1.4] Second, medical debt is unusually unreliable data. The amount owed often depends on insurers fighting over coverage, coding errors, surprise out-of-network charges, and bills sent to collections while claims are still pending. When the input is this noisy, keeping it on the report doesn't make lending more accurate.
