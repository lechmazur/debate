[P1.1] Every previous automation wave hit the body: muscles, then routine handwork. AI is the first general-purpose technology aimed squarely at cognition itself, and that is why this time is different in kind, not just degree. White-collar work—drafting, analyzing, coding, summarizing, processing claims, generating reports—is precisely the territory where large language models already perform at or near junior-professional level. When the disruption targets the majority of an economy's wage bill, the arithmetic of replacement versus creation stops being reassuring.
[P1.2] Consider the asymmetry. A single AI system that drafts competent contracts can eliminate task-hours across every law firm simultaneously. But the jobs it creates—prompt engineers, model evaluators, compliance staff—are created once, centrally, and do not scale with the number of workers displaced. Automation destroys jobs proportionally to output; it creates jobs in fixed quantities.
[P1.3] That's why "software ate bookkeeping but we got spreadsheet analysts" is a weak analogy: spreadsheets still needed a human per spreadsheet. AI systems need one human per thousand tasks.
[P1.4] Second, the historical equilibrium argument assumes new jobs match the skills and locations of the displaced. A 55-year-old paralegal in Cleveland will not become an ML researcher in the Bay Area. Even if aggregate employment eventually rebalances over decades, the transition window—ten, twenty years—features concentrated unemployment, wage depression in all routine-cognitive occupations, and a collapse of the mid-skill ladder that entry-level workers used to climb. Graduate hiring freezes across consulting, tech, and media are the early tremor, not the anomaly.
[P1.5] The proposition's second clause follows from the first. If disruption of this scale is even reasonably likely, the cost of preparing early—retraining pipelines, portable benefits, taxation frameworks for automated output—is trivially small next to the cost of being wrong. Insurance against catastrophic scenarios is basic governance.
[P1.6] So the case rests on three claims I will defend throughout: the substitution-creation asymmetry, the transition-cost reality regardless of long-run equilibrium, and the asymmetry of policy risk. Meeting one of them isn't enough; CON must beat all three.
