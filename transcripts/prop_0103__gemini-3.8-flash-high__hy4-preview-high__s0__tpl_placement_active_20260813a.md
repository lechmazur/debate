[P1.1] Point-of-sale background checks are a momentary snapshot, not a safety system. A buyer can pass an instantaneous database query and walk out ten minutes later with lethal machinery, regardless of whether they know how to safely clear a malfunction, secure the weapon away from children, or intend to transfer it to a prohibited buyer in the parking lot. Relying exclusively on point-of-sale screening leaves public safety blind to basic competence and toothless against illegal diversion.
[P1.2] A federal licensing standard that requires prior background vetting and practical safety training for every firearm purchase establishes an indispensable baseline of accountability. This policy addresses the structural failures of current law in three decisive ways.
[P1.3] First, it dismantles the straw-purchasing and trafficking pipeline. When every acquisition requires an active, verifiable federal license, the informal gray market collapses. Straw purchasers face personal, auditable exposure for the firearms tied to their credentials, deterring the bulk diversion that currently floods city streets across state borders where state-level laws differ.
[P1.4] Second, it enforces basic competence. We do not permit individuals to operate motor vehicles on public roads without demonstrating minimum operational proficiency. Requiring certified safety training ensures owners understand safe handling and secure home storage, directly curbing unintentional shootings and preventing household firearms from falling into the hands of unauthorized minors.
[P1.5] Third, the licensing process introduces vital, structural friction. The administrative steps required to secure a license—vetted verification and safety instruction—create a natural cooling-off period. This procedural pause interrupts acute crises, significantly reducing impulsive acts of interpersonal violence and firearm suicide, which accounts for the majority of American gun fatalities.
[P1.6] Federal licensing does not deny firearms to law-abiding citizens; it ensures that those who exercise that right possess the vetting and baseline competence that lethal tools inherently demand. Point-of-sale screening checks a box at the register. A federal license guarantees responsibility before the trigger is pulled.
