[C1.1] The tell PRO wants you to notice cuts the other way. Firms don't advertise personalized discounts not because concealment is predatory, but because the moment a discount is known, the firm's incentive is to eliminate the discount, not the markup. That is the single fact this whole debate turns on: disclosure and bans do not push prices down toward some fair single number. They push prices up toward the ceiling, because firms would rather charge everyone the higher price than admit some customers get less.
[C1.2] Think about why price discrimination exists at all. A firm facing one uniform price sets it high enough to capture profit from willing payers, which prices out the price-sensitive buyer entirely. Personalized pricing lets that same firm serve the price-sensitive buyer too, at a lower price, because the algorithm can tell them apart from the customer who won't blink at full fare. Ban that sorting or force it into the open, and the rational response isn't generosity to the low-price customer — it's withdrawing the low price, because offering it publicly invites the high-price customer to simply claim it too.
[C1.3] The result is fewer transactions, less access, and a higher floor price for everyone at the bottom.
[C1.4] Now look at "effectively the same product." That phrase sounds modest until you try to enforce it. Insurance premiums differ by risk. Airline seats differ by how much inventory risk the airline bears at the moment of sale.
[C1.5] Ride-hail prices differ by real-time supply of drivers. None of these are the identical product PRO imagines — they're the same label attached to a different risk, different timing, different scarcity. A disclosure-or-ban rule needs a bright line between "personalization based on legitimate cost differences" and "personalization based on desperation," and no regulator has ever drawn that line without either gutting ordinary dynamic pricing or leaving loopholes wide enough to drive a truck through.
[C1.6] PRO is offering you sunlight. What you'd actually get is a ratchet that locks in the highest price as the only price.
