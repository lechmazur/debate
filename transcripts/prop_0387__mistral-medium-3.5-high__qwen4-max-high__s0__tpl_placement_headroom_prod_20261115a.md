[P1.1] Esports athletes face systemic exploitation across three critical areas that demand immediate labor intervention. First, abusive contracts are rampant: players frequently sign multi-year agreements containing non-compete clauses that can span entire continents, revenue splits that leave them with as little as 10% of their own earnings, and perpetual rights to their likenesses and personal brands. Some contracts grant organizations indefinite control over players’ social media accounts, while others feature non-compete restrictions lasting up to two years after a player leaves.
[P1.2] These agreements are routinely presented as non-negotiable ultimatums to young players who lack both legal representation and a basic understanding of the terms they’re signing. Second, underage exploitation is widespread—minors as young as 13 are actively scouted and signed to professional contracts in major titles like Fortnite and League of Legends, then immediately subjected to the same grueling schedules as adult professionals, often without parental oversight or proper consideration for their developmental needs.
[P1.3] Third, the physical and mental toll is extreme and unchecked: players endure 14-hour daily practice sessions, consecutive all-night tournaments across global time zones, and constant performance pressure that leads to chronic repetitive stress injuries, severe vision problems, and debilitating burnout. Unlike traditional sports leagues, esports lacks collective bargaining agreements, healthcare guarantees, or injury protections for its athletes. When a teenager’s career ends abruptly—whether from a repetitive strain injury, being arbitrarily dropped by their team, or industry-wide burnout that leaves them mentally and physically exhausted—they are frequently left with no safety net: no severance pay, no healthcare coverage, crippling non-compete restrictions that prevent them from working in their field for years afterward.
[P1.4] The rapid commercialization of esports has created a predatory system where players are the only stakeholders without power or protections. Stronger labor safeguards are not merely ethical—they are essential to prevent the industry from continuing its current practice of burning through young talent while organizations reap unchecked profits.
