[P1.1] A city should raise revenue from the value of scarce locations, not penalize homes being built or changing hands. Land-value taxation does that: it taxes the site’s underlying value, excluding buildings and improvements. Its central advantage is aligning public revenue with productive land use. Transaction taxes obstruct exchange; rent caps constrain rental income.
[P1.2] Neither offers the same combination of housing incentives and a durable revenue base.
[P1.3] Consider two equally valuable sites near a transit station: one holds apartments, the other a surface parking lot. Under a land-value tax, their site-tax bills are comparable. Building more homes does not itself increase the tax, while withholding a valuable site still carries an annual charge. That encourages owners to make scarce, accessible land available for productive use.
[P1.4] It also lets the city fund services without taxing every additional floor or renovation.
[P1.5] Transaction taxes instead impose a charge precisely when property changes hands—including transfers to someone better able to redevelop it. They discourage moves and can obstruct land assembly. Rent caps may protect incumbent tenants, but binding limits on rental returns can discourage new rental investment, maintenance, or continued rental use. Exemptions can soften those effects; they do not turn rent caps into a source of infrastructure funding.
[P1.6] For local finance, the distinction is equally important. Land cannot relocate to escape taxation. A recurring levy does not require a stream of property sales, unlike transaction-tax revenue, which weakens when turnover falls. And when public investment makes locations more valuable, taxing that land value helps return part of the publicly created gain to the budget supporting those services.
[P1.7] Assessment and transition require care: regular valuations, transparent appeals, and payment deferrals secured against property can address practical burdens. Zoning reform remains necessary where construction is prohibited. But those are implementation tasks, not reasons to prefer taxes that impede transactions or controls that restrain returns. Land-value taxation provides the stronger foundation: finance the city without penalizing its growth.
