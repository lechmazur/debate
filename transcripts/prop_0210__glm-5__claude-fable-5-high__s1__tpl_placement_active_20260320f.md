[P1.1] Hidden negative trial results don't merely distort science—they kill. The systematic suppression of unfavorable clinical trial data has caused documented patient harm across decades of medical practice. When published evidence systematically overstates drug efficacy and understates risks, doctors prescribe treatments based on false premises and patients bear the consequences.

These aren't hypothetical harms; they are the predictable outcomes of an evidence system corrupted by selective disclosure.

[P1.5] CON faces an uncomfortable position: defending the "right" of companies to benefit from market access while withholding information that could prevent patient harm. They must argue that commercial convenience outweighs the lives damaged by systematically biased evidence.

[C1.5] Third, mandatory public disclosure—as opposed to regulator disclosure—carries real costs. And because disclosure reveals competitive strategy, companies will rationally respond by running fewer exploratory trials in risky areas like rare diseases, or by simply skipping smaller markets whose access isn't worth global disclosure obligations—shrinking drug availability precisely where it's thinnest.
