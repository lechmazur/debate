[P1.1] When police treat the DNA of a teenager who uploaded her ancestry results as a crime‑solving tool, they turn a private act into a surveillance net that drags in millions of innocent people. A strict prohibition is the only safeguard that matches the scale of the threat.
[P1.2] Commercial genealogy databases now hold genetic profiles of tens of millions of users who never consented to law‑enforcement use. Those profiles were submitted for personal curiosity, health insights, or to reconnect with distant relatives. Using them for cold‑case investigations means the state can, without a warrant, harvest the genetic identity of anyone who ever clicked “I agree” to a service’s terms of service. The legal baseline is consent to a commercial platform, not consent to police probing.
[P1.3] The result is a de‑facto DNA dragnet that the Fourth Amendment was designed to forbid.
[P1.4] The benefit side is seductive but thin. The handful of high‑profile cases solved through genealogical matches—most notably the 2018 Golden State Killer arrest—represent a fraction of a percent of the millions of profiles that would be exposed to routine queries. For every case closed, thousands of families face the risk of false‑positive leads, misidentified relatives, and the psychological trauma of being swept into a criminal investigation. Minority communities, already over‑represented in some databases because of higher adoption of direct‑to‑consumer tests, bear a disproportionate share of that risk.
[P1.5] Moreover, the practice creates a chilling effect on scientific participation. If people fear their DNA will be weaponised, they will stop contributing to research that advances medicine and ancestry science. The loss is collective; the gain is episodic.
[P1.6] A strict prohibition, not a vague policy, draws a clear line: no access, no loopholes, no “case‑by‑case” discretion that can be stretched into routine use. Only an absolute ban protects the fundamental right to genetic privacy while still leaving investigators with every traditional forensic tool at their disposal.
