[C1.2] First, competence. Loneliness is rarely a venue problem. It flows from grief, divorce, relocation, illness, the slow drift of lives — a shortage of depth, not of rooms. A city can build a community center; it cannot put a confidant inside it.
[C1.3] The proposition's chain — subsidize a storefront, out pops friendship — is speculation dressed as policy. The tools that genuinely reach lonely people already exist: mental-health care, bereavement support, home visits. [C1.4] Second, and deeper: friendship is constituted by being freely given. It is the one relationship that cannot be owed, assigned, or optimized without ceasing to be itself. The "retreat formats that encourage vulnerability" promised in [P1.4] do not create friendship; they create compliance with a social KPI, supervised by the person who signs your paycheck. [C1.5] Company towns were a warning, not a model.
[C1.6] Third, the redundancy test. Everything sensible in this agenda is already justified on independent grounds. [C1.7] Adults deserve time, money, and cities that make connection possible. Equip people, yes. But the moment friendship becomes a deliverable, it stops being friendship and becomes programming. Keep it private — not because it doesn't matter, but because it does.
