[C1.1] GDP growth must remain the primary headline target because it alone provides the clarity, comparability, and direct policy responsiveness that governments need to function. The PRO’s critique—that GDP counts cleanup costs or ignores unpaid work—describes known distortions already addressed through satellite accounts, not reasons to abandon the metric. More significantly, GDP responds directly to fiscal and monetary policy, making it an actionable guide for decision-makers. The historical evidence is overwhelming: as GDP per capita has risen, life expectancy has more than doubled globally, child mortality has fallen by over 90% in many countries, and literacy has become nearly universal.
[C1.2] These are not mere correlations but causal relationships where economic growth provides the resources to fund healthcare, education, and infrastructure—the very foundations of well-being.
[C1.3] Well-being cannot serve as a headline target because it is an inherently subjective, multi-dimensional concept that cannot be reduced to a single actionable number. Bhutan’s Gross National Happiness index remains a philosophical construct with no consistent methodology, no international comparability, and no clear policy transmission mechanism. Without an objective yardstick, economic management becomes a contest of values: should governments prioritize mental health over physical safety, or urban amenities over rural development? GDP cuts through this by providing a clear signal of economic health.
[C1.4] Most importantly, well-being is predominantly a lagging indicator that governments cannot directly influence. You cannot stimulate national happiness through policy levers, but you can create its preconditions through sustained economic expansion. Every major advance in human welfare—from the eradication of smallpox to universal clean water access—has been underpinned by the resources that growth provides. Abandoning GDP as the headline would sever the link between policy actions and tangible outcomes, risking a dangerous prioritization of measurable feelings over the fundamental economic conditions that actually improve lives.
[C1.5] The current scoreboard is imperfect, but it is the one that has consistently delivered the resources necessary for genuine, sustained human progress.
