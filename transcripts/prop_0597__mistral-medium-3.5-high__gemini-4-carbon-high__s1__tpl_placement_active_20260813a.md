[P1.1] International rules should permit heritable human genome editing for serious monogenic diseases once safety thresholds are met. The reason is simple: this technology can permanently eliminate preventable suffering across generations. Diseases like Huntington’s, which causes inevitable neurological decline, sickle cell disease, which brings chronic pain and reduces life expectancy by decades, and spinal muscular atrophy, which is often fatal in infancy, are all caused by single-gene mutations. Germline editing can correct these mutations at the embryonic stage, preventing transmission and sparing future children from these conditions entirely.
[P1.2] This is fundamentally different from somatic editing, which only treats symptoms in existing patients. The proposition’s safety condition is non-negotiable: we advocate permission only after exhaustive, multi-generational validation in appropriate animal models and long-term human follow-up. The ethical imperative is overwhelming and urgent. To withhold this intervention when it can prevent such profound, predictable suffering would constitute a moral failure of historic proportions.
[P1.3] International permission is necessary to ensure equitable global access, establish uniform safety standards, and facilitate coordinated oversight. Without it, we risk a fragmented landscape where some nations deny access while others proceed without adequate safeguards. Critics cite off-target effects and unintended consequences, but these are precisely the risks that rigorous safety thresholds are designed to mitigate. Advances in editing precision demonstrate that off-target risks can be reduced to clinically acceptable levels.
[P1.4] The slippery slope to enhancement is a distraction: the proposition strictly confines use to serious monogenic diseases, and the same validation requirements that prevent harm would also block non-therapeutic applications. Current alternatives like preimplantation genetic diagnosis merely allow selection among existing embryos; they cannot repair the underlying mutation. When safety is assured, the just course is clear: permit heritable editing to eradicate these diseases from human lineage.
