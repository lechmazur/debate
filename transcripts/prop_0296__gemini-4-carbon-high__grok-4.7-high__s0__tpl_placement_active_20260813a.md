[P1.1] Public holidays, schools, and streets are not historical archives; they are instruments of state honor. Textbooks, museums, and classrooms exist to study the past in full context, and renaming a street deletes nothing from them. Civic naming does something fundamentally different: it announces whom a community reveres. When a government compels Black students to attend a school named for a slaveholder, asks Indigenous citizens to celebrate a holiday honoring a colonizer, or leaves a dictator’s name on a family’s street, the state is not neutrally preserving the past.
[P1.2] It is forcing equal citizens to pay daily civic tribute to legacies built on their subjugation.
[P1.3] Colonizers, slaveholders, and dictators belong together because conquest, human bondage, and tyranny are the direct negations of freedom and equal citizenship. Targeting names that primarily honor those roles sets a disciplined boundary, not an endless purity test. It leaves intact holidays celebrating broader civic ideals or democratic offices, and it does not strip honors from flawed scientists, artists, or reformers who never conquered peoples, owned human beings, or ruled by dictatorship. It targets only the public veneration of domination.
[P1.4] "Historical continuity" cannot justify keeping such honors. First, continuity on a signpost does not preserve historical understanding; it sanitizes it. Commuters learn nothing about colonial atrocities from driving down a boulevard named for King Leopold II, just as students learn nothing serious about slavery from wearing a Confederate general’s name on a school jersey. If anything, the public deliberation surrounding renaming teaches communities far more real history than wallpaper familiarity ever did.
[P1.5] Second, continuity is no virtue when the tradition itself was exclusionary. Many such names, from Francoist streets in Spain to segregation-era Confederate schools, were chosen precisely to assert dominance; even older ones merely reflect who once monopolized civic power.
[P1.6] Inertia is not neutrality. Governments should teach the whole truth in classrooms, and reserve public honors for legacies every citizen can inhabit with dignity.
