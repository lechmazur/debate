[P1.1] Decarbonization means replacing fuels we burn continuously with hardware we manufacture upfront: batteries, solar cells, wind turbines, and refined minerals. If importing nations simply chase the lowest sticker price today, they merely trade yesterday’s vulnerability to volatile petrostates for tomorrow’s vulnerability to a single manufacturing monopoly. When a geopolitical crisis, export ban, or regional shock can halt a nation’s grid expansion and automotive sector overnight, cheap imports are a mirage.
[P1.2] Using subsidies and public procurement to localize supply chains is an essential insurance premium against weaponized interdependence.
[P1.3] Second, the clean energy transition will fail if it cannot survive domestic politics. [P1.4] A slightly pricier transition that maintains democratic support will actually happen; a cheap, import-dependent transition that triggers populist backlash will be repealed.
[P1.5] Finally, the costs specified in the motion—higher prices and trade tensions—are unavoidable realities of correcting a distorted market.
