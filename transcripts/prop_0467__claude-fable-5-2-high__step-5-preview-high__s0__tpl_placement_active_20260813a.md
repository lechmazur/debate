[C1.1] The proposition mistakes a grading scale for a threshold. “Armed attack” is not a label for severe harm; it is the legal trigger that authorises war between states. Equivalence by effect would collapse the distinction that makes escalation controllable, and it would do so precisely where verification is weakest.
[C1.2] First, effects-based law ignores the only property that has ever made armed-attack attribution workable: a verifiable source. A missile leaves a launch site, a supply chain, a state’s signature. Destructive malware leaves borrowed keyboards, spoofed flags, criminal botnets and proxy servers. PRO calls attribution an evidentiary burden on the responder, as if the problem were paperwork.
[C1.3] It is not. It is structural. When a regional blackout demands a response within hours, the evidence available within hours is exactly the evidence most easily forged. A strike on the wrong state is not a legal irregularity to be corrected later; it is a war.
[C1.4] Second, PRO answers attribution and escalation but never meets necessity, the requirement that sits alongside proportionality. Self-defence must be necessary to repel an ongoing attack. Malware is copied, mirrored and re-injected; it survives any bombing. A kinetic strike cannot pull code out of a turbine.
[C1.5] So the promised “proportional military response” either fails necessity and is unlawful, or succeeds only as punishment dressed as defence — precisely the reprisal the Charter forbids.
[C1.6] Third, an effects test sweeps in what the motion never intended. Ransomware crews already darken hospitals. Propagating code crosses into neutral states. If damage alone is the metric, ordinary criminals acquire armed-attack status and civilian networks in third countries become lawful targets.
[C1.7] The status quo is not immunity. Existing law already permits self-defence where operations reach armed-attack scale; what PRO really wants is more political will. You cannot legislate that, and trying manufactures first-strike justifications out of pre-positioning that today merely warrants monitoring.
