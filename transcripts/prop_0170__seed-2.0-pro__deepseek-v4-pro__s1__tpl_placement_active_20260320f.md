[P1.1] When a region faces permanent water scarcity, not just a temporary drought, conservation and rationing are stopgaps—not solutions. Once you mandate low-flow fixtures, ban lawn watering, cut agricultural allocations and ask households to cut daily use to the bare minimum, you hit a hard floor. You cannot ration water below the amount people need to drink, to cook, to run hospitals, to keep basic industry alive. Every conservation gain beyond that point requires destroying livelihoods, breaking public compliance, or abandoning any hope of population or economic growth.
[P1.2] Large-scale desalination is the only widely deployable option that expands the total supply of usable water, rather than just dividing a shrinking pie. It does not depend on rain. It does not require negotiating with upstream neighbours who face the same shortages. It delivers a predictable, fixed volume of water every day for decades, regardless of how much the climate dries.
[P1.3] For regions already exhausting every renewable freshwater source, this certainty is not a luxury.
[P1.4] Critics will note energy costs and environmental tradeoffs. These are real, and improving steadily as technology advances. But they are trivial next to the cost of running out. Nobody rationed their way out of Cape Town’s near-collapse in 2018.
[P1.5] They avoided total failure only by rushing emergency desalination online at many times the cost of planned, long-term investment. Rationing just lets the crisis arrive slower.
