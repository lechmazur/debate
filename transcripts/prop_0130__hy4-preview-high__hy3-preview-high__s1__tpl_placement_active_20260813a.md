[P1.1] The proposition forces one question: when an object was taken by colonial force, does a foreign museum’s superior humidifier keep it? It does not. The central clash is not metal or marble; it is ownership, justice, and remedy. Looted artifacts were not gifts, purchases, or impartial finds; they were extracted amid coercion, punitive expeditions, and imperial domination.
[P1.2] Curators may treat the surface, but possession continues the taking.
[P1.3] Start from first principles. If someone steals a family’s ceremonial bronze and later stores it in a better climate than the family’s home, the thief cannot defend keeping it by praising his own vault. Return is not optional because the possessor has become a good custodian; it is required because he is a wrongdoer. Colonial looting is not a garage-sale misunderstanding; it is a rights violation whose remedy is restitution.
[P1.4] Preservation cannot carry CON’s burden. Even if some Western institutions now have better labs, preservation is a management problem, not a title problem. Their wealth and expertise were often built on the empire that took the objects. Justice uses that expertise to aid return, not to justify exile: send conservators, fund climate-controlled facilities, train local staff, share loans.
[P1.5] A ‘universal museum’ is just an imperial collection with better lighting. The Edo museum plan for the Benin Bronzes and the Acropolis Museum built for the Parthenon Marbles show origin institutions can conserve well when will and resources follow the object home.
[P1.6] Retention also inflicts ongoing harm. An object in London or Berlin is severed from its makers, and descendants are told their heritage is safer in an old imperial capital than in their own care. It tells a people that only empire can be trusted with their own past. That is colonialism’s injury itself.
[P1.7] The path is clear: we need not deny preservation matters, only that it transfers ownership. Return the artifacts, cooperate on conservation, and stop rewarding better custody with permanent possession.
