That is the first fatal flaw in the proposition. Credential recognition is overwhelmingly controlled by professional regulators, licensing boards, and sub-national governments. Labour-market testing is one of the few levers immigration policy actually holds.
[C1.2] Telling immigration policy to prioritize recognition speed is telling it to prioritize something it cannot deliver, while abandoning the thing it can.
[C1.3] Second, note what "faster" means. Not fairer, not better — faster. The honest bottleneck in recognition is genuine equivalence: distinguishing a rigorous foreign programme from a diploma mill, verifying documents from institutions with no reliable registry, confirming clinical currency after years out of practice. Speed is the wrong dial.
[C1.4] Push it and the first failures are patients and building occupants, and the second failure is political: one scandal involving a fast-tracked licence poisons public support for skilled migration for a decade.
[C1.5] Third, and most importantly, my opponent assumes shortage is a fact about the world. It is usually a claim made by employers. "Shortage" often means "shortage at the wage I want to pay." Labour-market testing is the only instrument that forces that claim to be tested against actual recruitment and actual wage offers.
[C1.6] And notice how cheap the test is when the shortage is real — an emergency department that has advertised for a year passes it instantly. Testing only bites where the shortage is manufactured. That is not friction; that is calibration.
[C1.7] Which means testing protects migrants too. The worst outcomes in skilled migration are not unemployed engineers; they are tied, sponsor-dependent workers in care and IT undercutting the market and unable to leave. Recognition without testing hands employers a larger pool at a lower price. Testing sets the floor those workers stand on.
[C1.8] Recognize credentials properly, by all means. But the priority — the thing immigration policy owns and the public consents to — is verifying the demand is real.
