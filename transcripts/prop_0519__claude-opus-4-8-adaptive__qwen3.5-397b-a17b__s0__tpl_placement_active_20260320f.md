[P1.1] The minimum wage is a single number. Sectoral bargaining is a system. That distinction decides this debate.
[P1.2] Consider what a fragmented service industry actually looks like: thousands of fast-food outlets, home-care agencies, hotels, and cleaning contractors, all undercutting each other on labor cost because no single firm can afford to be generous alone. A minimum-wage hike answers only one of their problems—the hourly rate—and answers it crudely, with one number for a downtown hotel and a rural diner alike. Sectoral bargaining sets standards across the whole sector at once: pay, yes, but also scheduling predictability, training ladders, safety norms, and overtime rules that a wage floor simply cannot touch.
[P1.3] My case rests on three pillars.
[P1.4] First, scope. Low-wage service work is miserable not just because of pay but because of erratic shifts, no advancement, and unsafe conditions. A wage hike fixes none of that. A sectoral agreement negotiates the whole package, because workers at the table name the problems legislators never see.
[P1.5] Second, adaptability. A legislated minimum is a guess imposed from outside. Sectoral bargaining lets the people who know the economics—workers and employers together—calibrate standards to what the sector can bear, and revisit them as conditions change, without waiting years for the next political fight.
[P1.6] Third, durability and fair competition. Repeated minimum-wage hikes depend on winning the same legislative war again and again, and they leave standards frozen between victories. A sectoral floor binds every competitor equally, so the responsible employer is no longer punished for paying decently. It rewards the good actor instead of the cheapest.
[P1.7] The honest comparison is not "wages versus no wages." It is a one-dimensional, politically fragile, take-it-or-leave-it number against a negotiated, multidimensional, self-renewing standard built by the parties who live in the industry.
[P1.8] For fragmented service work, the second tool is plainly the better one. That is what I will defend.
