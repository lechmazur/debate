[P1.1] The question is not whether digital identity carries risk. It is which risk Africa should choose: the certain, daily harm of exclusion, or the contingent, governable risk of surveillance.
[P1.2] Today, roughly half a billion Africans lack recognized proof of identity. That absence is a locked door: without it you cannot open a bank account, register a phone, receive a subsidy, enroll a child in school, or claim land. Service delivery runs on identity, and the people who most need public services are the ones the paper state cannot see. Only a continent-wide framework operates at the scale of the problem.
[P1.3] The delivery gains are concrete. When India moved welfare onto biometric ID, millions of ghost and duplicate beneficiaries were purged, and payments that once leaked through layers of intermediaries reached households directly. Rwanda's Irembo already delivers hundreds of government services online; Kenya's mobile-money economy, built on registered identities, brought financial inclusion to tens of millions. Fragmented national projects have proven the concept; an African Union framework multiplies it through interoperability and shared standards that small states cannot build alone.
[P1.4] The surveillance objection assumes the counterfactual is privacy. It is not. The counterfactual is the status quo: dozens of fragmented, insecure national databases, foreign vendors writing the rules, governments surveilling informally without audit trails. Authoritarian abuse never required digital ID; paper files and phone records served for decades.
[P1.5] What a Union-wide framework uniquely provides is a single point of leverage to impose data minimization, purpose limitation, encryption standards, and redress. The Union already has a continental data-protection convention; the framework is how its promises become enforceable rather than aspirational.
[P1.6] So choose which harm to fear: the one we can engineer against, or the one hundreds of millions live with every day. The certain harm outweighs the contingent one, and that decides the motion.

At continental scale, that surveillance risk is not contingent or governable. It is structural, irreversible, and it corrupts the very delivery it promises.
[C1.2] PRO offers a false binary: exclusion or privacy. The real choice is between decentralized, purpose-limited identity tools and one interoperable biometric architecture spanning dozens of states with weak judiciaries and strong incentives to track dissidents, refugees, and minorities. Link national registries and you build a turnkey panopticon, not merely added risk. A refugee fleeing repression in one country can be identified and returned from another because her biometric reads continent-wide; a dissident who crosses a border remains visible.
[C1.3] That is not abstraction; administrative data becomes a cross-border tracking grid.
[C1.4] PRO calls the framework a "single point of leverage" for data minimization [P1.5]. But the AU lacks EU-style supranational enforcement, and member states routinely ignore its conventions. A single point of leverage is also a single point of capture: one weak link, one compelled government, one breach, and hundreds of millions are exposed. The insecurity of national databases PRO cites is not solved by connecting them; it is multiplied.
[C1.5] Data minimization standards are only as strong as the weakest or most repressive participant.
[C1.6] Nor are delivery gains certain. Biometric ID can exclude as well as include. India’s Aadhaar produced authentication failures that denied food and pensions to eligible poor and elderly whose fingerprints had worn off; it did not just purge ghosts. Service delivery can improve through targeted, opt-in, federated registries and offline verification, without building irreversible surveillance capacity.
[C1.7] Exclusion can be attacked directly and incrementally; incremental fixes do not require continental biometric enrollment. Surveillance infrastructure, once built and biometrically enrolled, cannot be un-built. We must not trade certain daily exclusion for permanent continental tracking. The motion fails.
