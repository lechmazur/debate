[P1.1] When you buy a car, you are buying steel, chips, and circuits—not a time-share. Yet automakers now sell vehicles with hardware fully installed: heated seats, adaptive cruise, advanced driver assistance, even engine performance upgrades. Then, once the sale is complete, they flip a software switch and demand monthly rent to unlock what you already own. This is not innovation; it is a bait-and-switch that converts ownership into an indefinite rental agreement.
[P1.2] The principle is simple. If I purchase a physical object, I acquire the right to use its full capabilities permanently. A toaster does not require a monthly fee to toast bread. A house does not stop heating because the builder wants recurring revenue.
[P1.3] Cars are no different. When manufacturers deliberately cripple installed hardware to extract recurring payments, they are effectively seizing back property they already sold. This violates the basic expectations of contract and consumer protection that underpin every functioning market.
[P1.4] Consider the safety implications. Modern vehicles rely on software for braking, steering, and collision avoidance. If a subscription lapses, a server goes down, or a billing dispute arises, should your car refuse to start? Should emergency braking vanish?
[P1.5] Treating a two-ton machine that families depend on for work, school, and medical emergencies like a streaming service creates unacceptable risks. Hardware you paid for should function safely for the entire life of the vehicle, regardless of whether you continue paying rent to the manufacturer.
[P1.6] This is not about opposing legitimate new services. Navigation updates, roadside assistance, or concierge support can fairly cost money. But the hardware itself—the heated seats, the sensors, the computing power already installed—must remain permanently accessible. Otherwise, we do not own our cars; we rent them indefinitely while the manufacturer holds the keys.
[P1.7] Permanent access to purchased hardware is the minimum standard of fair dealing in a market economy.
