[P1.1] Governments already subsidize agriculture without conclusive long-term data on its consequences. We subsidize synthetic nitrogen without settled accounting for nitrate in drinking water, subsidize pesticides without settled data on pollinator collapse, subsidize tillage-heavy commodity systems while topsoil disappears ten times faster than it forms. The question is not whether to act before perfect data — we already do. It's which gamble we can afford.
[P1.2] Conventional input subsidies pay farmers to externalize costs. Every ton of fertilizer runoff creates a public bill: algal blooms that close fisheries, treatment plants to clean what shouldn't be polluted, nitrous oxide that warms far more than CO2. Regenerative practices — cover cropping, reduced tillage, diversified rotations, managed grazing, compost — internalize benefits. They build organic matter that holds water, sequesters carbon, and feeds soil biology.
[P1.3] We are not paying for ideology; we are paying for flood control, drought insurance, and carbon storage the market does not reward.
[P1.4] The yield objection measures the wrong thing. Governments don't subsidize farming to maximize this year's bushels per acre; they subsidize to ensure food for decades. A system extracting 3% more yield today while losing an inch of topsoil per decade is not productive, it's liquidation. Resilience is yield over time, and parity corrects that myopia.
[P1.5] Waiting is not neutral. Definitive yield data takes 20 to 30 years to mature. By then degraded ground does not bounce back, aquifers do not refill, and farmers who knew how to steward land have retired. If we are wrong about regenerative yields, we paid for healthier watersheds and more stable communities.
[P1.6] If we are wrong about conventional holding, we face irreversible loss of productive capacity.
[P1.7] Subsidy parity is how we generate the data opponents demand — by letting thousands of farmers afford the transition without bankruptcy. That is fiscal responsibility, not gambling.

[C1.3] PRO proposes to build a second such structure while pointing at the wreckage of the first.

[C1.5] Third, yield is not "the wrong thing" [P1.4]; it is the environmental variable. [C1.6] PRO's "resilience is yield over time" quietly assumes the answer to the question the motion admits is open.
[C1.7] The responsible path exists: fund outcome-based pilots, pay for measured soil carbon and nitrate reduction, scale with evidence, and redirect conventional subsidies as data arrives. Prudence is not paralysis. Parity before evidence is simply the old mistake with better branding.
