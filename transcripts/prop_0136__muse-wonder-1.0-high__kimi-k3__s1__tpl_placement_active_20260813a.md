[P1.1] For a young couple in Seoul, Osaka, or Singapore, the question before a first child is not whether the government will send a cheque. It is whether they can secure a home larger than a studio, keep both jobs, and find childcare they can afford next year and every year after. Fertility falls when those answers stay no, and one-off bonuses do not change them.
[P1.2] A baby bonus is small, brief, and badly timed against the real price of a child. Even a generous payment covers only a fraction of what parents face in rent, lost earnings, childcare fees, and education over nearly two decades. Rational couples therefore bank it, spend it on debts, or bring forward a birth they planned. That produces a short-lived bump in timing, not a larger generation.
[P1.3] East Asia has run this experiment: cash incentives proliferated while fertility kept sliding.
[P1.4] Housing affordability attacks the first bottleneck: family formation. Across urban East Asia, marriage is delayed because couples cannot afford to leave parental homes or move from one-room rentals unsuitable for a baby. High deposits, mortgage thresholds, and unstable renting push the first birth later, compressing the window for a second. Cheaper family-sized rentals, housing priority for newlyweds and parents, and support for larger homes do not bribe a birth; they make the sequence of marriage, home, and child feasible earlier.
[P1.5] Childcare attacks the second bottleneck: remaining parents without abandoning work. Where places are scarce, expensive, or mismatched with long working hours, mothers face a stark choice between income and a second child, and many stop at one or none. Affordable, available childcare lowers a recurring monthly cost, protects careers, and makes time constraints manageable. That long-horizon security is what couples need to commit to another child.
[P1.6] Bonuses subsidize the moment of birth. Housing and childcare change the economics of the eighteen years that follow, which is why they will raise birth rates more.

[C1.5] There is also deliverability. [C1.6] Which lever raises births more? The one that demonstrably has.
