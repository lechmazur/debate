[P1.1] Safe-third-country agreements allow states to turn away asylum seekers at their borders on the promise that protection exists elsewhere. But protection cannot be defined merely as the absence of immediate bombs or bullets. When a state redirects an asylum seeker to a third country, it claims that the person’s fundamental rights will be honored there. That claim is an empty legal fiction unless that destination guarantees three indispensable, enforceable rights: the right to work, the right to appeal, and family unity.
[P1.2] First, an enforceable right to appeal is the bedrock of procedural justice. Without independent judicial review, designation of a country as "safe" is a political fiat rather than a factual reality. An appeal is the sole mechanism that prevents arbitrary denial, rectifies misidentifications, and halts illegal chain-refoulement back to the persecutor.
[P1.3] Second, the right to work transforms asylum from mere containment into genuine survival. Warehousing refugees in indefinite aid dependency, legal limbo, or forced reliance on informal, exploitative labor markets is not sanctuary; it is institutional destitution. Meaningful refuge requires the lawful ability to feed oneself and live in dignity. Without work rights, refugees are driven into dangerous secondary migration, defeating the very stability safe-third-country rules purport to offer.
[P1.4] Third, family unity is non-negotiable. Asylum seekers are people embedded in families, not detached bureaucratic files. Subdividing families across borders or sending parents away from children under the pretext of territorial jurisdiction violates fundamental human dignity.
[P1.5] Safe-third-country rules inherently shift burdens from states with resources to transit countries that often lack them. Lowering the bar below these three pillars reduces international protection to geographical buck-passing, trapping vulnerable people in legal purgatory. If a third country cannot enforce the rights to sustain oneself, challenge one's removal, and remain with one's family, it is not safe. Safe-third-country rules are justified on these terms, and only these terms.
