[P1.1] Lock a human being in a concrete box the size of a parking space for 23 hours a day. No conversation, no touch, no work, no education. Light on. Toilet in the floor.
[P1.2] Food through a slot. After a few days you stop sleeping. After ten you hear voices. After fifteen you are someone else.
[P1.3] That is psychological torture, and torture is defined not by broken bones but by severe mental suffering intentionally inflicted by the state for punishment, intimidation, or control. Prolonged solitary meets every element: the suffering is severe and well documented, the isolation is deliberate, and its purpose is penal control.
[P1.4] The fifteen-day threshold is not activism. It is the point where temporary distress becomes lasting injury. Neuroscience and prison psychiatry converge: the brain needs social and sensory input to regulate mood, time perception, and reality testing. Deprive it for two weeks and you get a predictable syndrome — hypervigilance, emotional flattening, intrusive thoughts, hallucinations, paranoia, and uncontrollable self-harm impulses.
[P1.5] Suicide rates in solitary units run multiples of general population rates. That harm persists long after release, making reintegration harder, not easier.
[P1.6] Security cannot save it. Prisons that reduced prolonged solitary — through small-group units, staff-intensive de-escalation, and separation that preserves human contact — saw assaults fall, not rise. Solitary manufactures the very instability it claims to contain, then recycles traumatized people back to the yard or the street.
[P1.7] The Mandela Rules, drafted by corrections experts worldwide, prohibit solitary beyond fifteen consecutive days for precisely this reason. When a practice causes profound psychological disintegration, lacks unique penological necessity, and is condemned as torture under international standards, abolition is the only honest response. We do not tolerate other forms of state torture because they are useful. We abolish them because they are wrong.

[C1.1] The proposition collapses a vital distinction: cruel, unreviewed isolation is wrong, but not every separation lasting longer than fifteen days is psychological torture, and abolishing it outright would make prisons less safe and more violent. Torture is not any unpleasant confinement. It is the intentional infliction of severe suffering to break a person. Solitary can be used that way, and must be prohibited when it is.
[C1.2] But it can also be the least bad way to stop a prisoner who is ordering murders, sexually assaulting cellmates, or leading riots from a general-population wing. The moral question is not whether isolation is painful; it is whether the state has any humane means left to incapacitate an immediate threat.
[C1.3] PRO’s fifteen-day line does not solve harm; it shifts it. Duration alone cannot tell us whether a prisoner is being tortured; conditions, purpose, review, and alternatives do. If administrators cannot separate a dangerous person after two weeks, the alternatives are predictable: chemical sedation, prolonged physical restraint, blanket lockdowns, transfers to distant facilities, or leaving vulnerable prisoners exposed to extortion and assault. A rule that forces a prison to return a predatory gang leader to the yard because the calendar says fifteen days has passed is not compassion.
[C1.4] It is abdication. The people most likely to suffer are not officials, but weaker inmates and officers.
[C1.5] The better standard is regulation, not abolition. Require meaningful out-of-cell time, mental-health monitoring, education, contact with family and counsel, independent review, and a clear path back to general population. Where separation is protective or medical—someone targeted for debts, identity, testimony, or acute crisis—duration may legitimately exceed fifteen days if conditions remain humane. Banning the category because of its worst abuses throws away the safeguard.
[C1.6] PRO describes real injuries, but their remedy mistakes the disease for the tool. We should abolish torture, not the state’s capacity to separate people safely. Vote CON.
