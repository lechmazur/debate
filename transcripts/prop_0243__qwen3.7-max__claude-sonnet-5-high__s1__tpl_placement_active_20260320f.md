[P1.1] Ride-hailing platforms have fundamentally altered urban traffic dynamics by introducing massive amounts of deadheading—drivers cruising empty while waiting for a fare or en route to a pickup. Before these platforms, a typical personal car trip meant one vehicle moving from point A to point B, then parking. Today, a single ride-hail trip generates significantly more Vehicle Miles Traveled because the driver is already circulating in the dense urban core before the passenger even enters the vehicle. This structural inefficiency directly clogs city streets.
[P1.2] Furthermore, ride-hailing exacerbates congestion through induced demand and transit cannibalization. Rather than simply replacing personal car trips one-for-one, these platforms routinely pull riders away from high-capacity public transit, walking, and biking. When a single subway car carrying fifty people is replaced by ten separate ride-hail vehicles navigating the exact same surface streets, the spatial efficiency of the city collapses. The sheer convenience of app-based hailing lowers the friction of car travel, generating new trips that simply would not have occurred otherwise and adding net-new vehicles to already saturated corridors.
[P1.3] The opposition will likely argue that ride-hailing reduces personal car ownership and eliminates the need to circle the block looking for parking. But this ignores where and how these commercial vehicles actually operate. The modern congestion crisis is concentrated in dense downtowns and commercial corridors, precisely where ride-hail vehicles concentrate their deadheading and pick-ups. Even if an urban resident sells their personal car, the ride-hail vehicle serving them spends vastly more time active on the road.
[P1.4] A parked personal car takes up zero moving road capacity; a cruising ride-hail vehicle actively consumes moving lane space. Ultimately, the net addition of circulating, empty, and low-occupancy vehicles in our most dense urban centers makes the overall congestion picture undeniably worse. By prioritizing the convenience of individual car trips over the spatial reality of city streets, ride-hailing has definitively worsened the gridlock we all share.
