[P1.2] The case rests on three pillars.

[P1.4] Second, feasibility. Note the proposition's standard: wait times and convenience *close to* standard service — not identical, but not second-class. [P1.5] The curb-cut history is instructive: industries insisted accessibility was impossibly expensive, until mandates made it routine — and then everyone with a stroller, suitcase, or delivery cart benefited too.
[P1.6] Third, the alternative is worse. Without a mandate, companies rationally skim profitable riders and externalize the cost onto paratransit systems that require booking a day ahead for a trip across town — a humiliation no nondisabled rider would accept.

[C1.7] The deepest problem is that unworkable mandates provide political cover, not better service. Cities can claim to have acted while delivering nothing.
