[P1.2] Real-time surveillance is not a targeted search. It scans crowds without individualized suspicion, flags people in seconds, and pushes officers to act before anyone has verified the match. [P1.3] Live systems also operate under exactly the conditions that make errors dangerous—variable cameras, poor angles, time pressure—and a flashing alert invites overtrust. The structural risk does not depend on one bad algorithm. It is built into suspicionless, instant identification.

[P1.5] That preserves the genuine investigative value: matching a suspect from a robbery video, identifying a victim, developing leads—while keeping the search anchored to evidence rather than sweeping everyone. [P1.7] The choice is between using face recognition as a targeted evidence tool and using it as a perpetual suspicion machine. PRO chooses the tool with a warrant, not the machine without one.
