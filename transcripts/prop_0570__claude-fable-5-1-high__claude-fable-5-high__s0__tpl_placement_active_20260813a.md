[P1.1] Here is the dividing line the whole motion turns on: some learning has answers you can check, and some learning is an act of judgment about a human reader. AI tutors belong on the first side of that line, and secondary schools should put them there deliberately.
[P1.2] Mastery practice is where AI is genuinely good. Factoring quadratics, conjugating irregular verbs, balancing equations, spotting a comma splice in a sentence someone else wrote: these have right answers, the feedback loop can be instant, and the tutor never gets tired or impatient on the fortieth attempt. A fifteen-year-old who needs thirty more problems on solving for x should get them at 9pm with immediate correction. No teacher can provide that at scale, and no student is harmed when a machine says "wrong, try again" about a fact that is actually wrong.
[P1.3] Writing is a different kind of thing. When a student writes an essay, the question is not "is this correct" but "does this persuade, move, or clarify for a reader." Feedback on writing is a reader saying: I got lost here, I didn't believe you here, this sentence surprised me. A language model has no stake in being persuaded.
[P1.4] It produces plausible-sounding feedback tuned toward smooth, generic, five-paragraph prose, and it praises fluency that says nothing. Students quickly learn to write for the machine, and the machine rewards sameness.
[P1.5] There is also a diagnostic cost. Reading student writing is how a teacher learns what a student actually thinks, where their reasoning breaks, and what they care about. Outsource that reading and the teacher loses the single richest window into the student's mind.
[P1.6] The strongest objection is workload: teachers cannot give deep feedback on 150 essays. The motion answers it. Let AI absorb the drill, the retrieval practice, the mechanics, precisely so that the teacher's finite attention is spent where only a human reader counts.
[P1.7] Supplementary use is fine. But the main voice responding to a teenager's writing should be one that can actually be moved by it.
