[C1.1] The proposition asks us to accept a permanent narrowing of speech protections for a remedy that fails its targets while chilling the very discourse it claims to protect. That trade is bad on both ends.
[C1.2] First, the speech cost is not "narrow" in any operational sense. Statutes must define "sexually explicit," "identifiable," and "deepfake" broadly enough to catch new techniques, and those definitions inevitably sweep in political satire, artistic expression, and journalism. A private right of action multiplies the chill: unlike criminal prosecution, which requires prosecutorial discretion, a civil claim can be filed by anyone with a grievance. The result is self-censorship—creators avoid controversial material not because it is illegal, but because defending a lawsuit is ruinous even when they win.
[C1.3] Second, the remedy misses the actual wrongdoers while crushing legitimate speakers. The harmed teenager typically faces an anonymous classmate or a foreign operator who is judgment-proof and beyond the reach of domestic courts. A private right of action yields a judgment she cannot collect, while the local journalist or activist using synthetic media in a story faces crippling defense costs against plaintiffs with deeper pockets. We trade broad, durable speech rights for a litigation lottery that punishes the speakers we can reach and ignores the predators we cannot.
[C1.4] Third, the proposition presents a false choice. We can address nonconsensual explicit imagery through targeted criminal penalties for malicious creation, mandatory platform takedown duties with enforcement, and technical watermarking standards—without carving new exceptions into speech protections. Once a legal system accepts that disfavored content categories lose their constitutional shield whenever a private plaintiff claims harm, the precedent does not stay confined to pornography; it expands to defamation, emotional distress, and beyond.
[C1.5] The status quo has gaps, but the proposed fix sacrifices the wrong thing to close them. Protecting victims requires real teeth against anonymous abusers, not a chilling instrument that silences the public square. Vote CON.
