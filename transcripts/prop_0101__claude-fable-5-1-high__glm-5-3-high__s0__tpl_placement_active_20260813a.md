[P1.1] Everyone agrees the status quo is failing children. The only real question is whether the fix should be a clear age line or another decade of dashboards, toggles, and "wellbeing features." Choose the age line, for three reasons.
[P1.2] First, parental controls fail because the harm is collective, not individual. A thirteen-year-old whose parents lock her out of Instagram is not protected; she is exiled. The group chat, the party invitations, the social currency of her whole grade now happen somewhere she cannot follow. So conscientious parents face a brutal choice: expose your child to the harm, or expose her to isolation.
[P1.3] Most eventually cave. That is not a failure of parenting; it is a coordination problem, and coordination problems are exactly what law exists to solve. A universal ban does for social media what a school phone ban does for phones: it makes the healthy choice the default for everyone, so no individual child pays a social price for it.
[P1.4] Second, "softer design changes" ask the arsonist to install smoke detectors. The engagement mechanics driving the harm, infinite scroll, algorithmic feeds tuned to outrage and comparison, streaks, and notifications engineered for compulsion, are the business model. Companies will trim around the edges, publish glossy safety reports, and quietly keep the engine running. A ban removes the incentive to optimize children's attention at all, because children stop being the inventory.
[P1.5] Third, bright lines are enforceable and legible in a way that nuance is not. We do not ask parents to individually negotiate whether their fourteen-year-old may buy cigarettes or drive; we set an age and hold sellers responsible. Australia has already moved toward exactly this model, placing the compliance burden on platforms rather than on exhausted parents. Age verification is imperfect, but so is every age limit we already accept, and imperfect enforcement of a clear rule beats perfect enforcement of no rule.
[P1.6] Under sixteen, the cost of being online is borne by children; the profit is captured by platforms. A ban simply refuses that trade.
