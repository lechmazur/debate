[P1.2] The alleged efficiency cost is a mirage. Opacity does not produce efficient labor markets; it produces efficient exploitation. [P1.3] Consider the mechanism: the "ask gap." When blind to budgets, women typically propose lower starting figures than men for identical roles, not because they value their work less, but because they lack the insider market data that privileged networks provide. [P1.4] The trade-off itself is asymmetrical. Any short-term friction in hiring—slightly longer postings, adjusted HR workflows—is transient and administratively trivial. Companies in Colorado and jurisdictions across Europe adapted within months. By contrast, the equity gains compound over decades.
[P1.5] A five-percent initial gap, left unchecked, metastasizes into thirty-percent lifetime earnings disparities through raises anchored to low bases.
