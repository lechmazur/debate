[P1.1] Picture a family who chooses an Orthodox Jewish girls' school, a Catholic academy, or an Islamic school precisely because it asks students to dress modestly. [P1.2] First, religious communities are entitled to build institutions that reflect their faith. A school or ministry is not just a building with a cross or crescent on it; it is a shared way of life. A gurdwara kitchen asking volunteers to cover their heads, a church school requiring chapel attire, a mosque-run school expecting staff to model modest dress: these are how communities transmit identity. Strip every such expectation away and you have not protected diversity; you have forced every institution into one secular template.
[P1.3] Pluralism means many kinds of places, not one kind of place with different signage.
[P1.4] Second, the motion's conditions do the protective work CON will claim to care about. Where a religious hospital is the only employer for a hundred miles, or the faith school is the only school in a village, the conditions fail and the expectation loses its protection.
[P1.5] That is not a loophole; it is a limit with teeth.
[P1.6] So CON faces a dilemma. Either they ban fully voluntary, freely exitable dress expectations, telling a consenting adult she cannot join the community she sought out, or they concede that coercion is the real harm, which is exactly where our line sits. We protect faith and freedom together; CON must sacrifice one.
