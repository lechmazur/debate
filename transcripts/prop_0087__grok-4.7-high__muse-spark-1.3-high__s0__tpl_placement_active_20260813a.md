[P1.1] Ultra-fast casino games and in-play betting do not merely accelerate an old pastime; they change the decision environment in which harm occurs. When a spin or a micro-bet arrives every few seconds, the gambler has almost no interval in which to notice losses, reassess stakes, or exit. That compression of time is the distinct harm profile, and it justifies tighter rules than those applied to slower products.
[P1.2] Slower gambling still carries risk, but its structure builds in friction. A lottery draw happens on a schedule. A traditional race or table game has pauses, other players, and a visible end to the round. Those features create moments of reflection that ultra-fast products are engineered to erase.
[P1.3] Continuous play, auto-spin, cash-out buttons, and bets on the next incident in a live match keep arousal high and judgment low. Variable-ratio reinforcement, delivered at a tempo slower markets cannot match, drives persistent play far more efficiently.
[P1.4] Picture a phone during a match: a lost bet on the next throw-in replaced within seconds by a bet on the next foul, while a slot tab spins beside it. No cashier, no queue, no one across the table saying stop. The result is a different pattern of loss of control—rapid chasing, dissociative zone play, and impulsive top-ups—because the next opportunity is immediate rather than tomorrow. People prone to impulsivity are especially exposed.
[P1.5] Rules that treat a weekly draw and a game that can empty a balance in minutes as equivalent miss the design difference that generates the harm.
[P1.6] Governments should therefore regulate these fast products more aggressively: minimum event intervals, bans on auto-play and near-continuous in-play markets, stake limits that tighten with frequency, forced breaks, and alerts tied to session speed. These are product standards aimed at the features that create the distinct harm, leaving slower forms under a lighter regime. Equal rules for unequal products would under-protect those most exposed to designs built for continuous loss.
