[P1.1] Copyright exists to reward human authorship, and the proposition simply asks the person claiming a monopoly to show that a human actually authored something. That is not a new burden. It is the bargain copyright has always struck.
[P1.2] Start with the Constitution: Congress may secure rights to "Authors" for their "Writings." Every court to consider the question, including the recent Thaler litigation over a machine-generated image, has held that authorship means human authorship. The Copyright Office's handling of the comic "Zarya of the Dawn" drew the same line: the human-written text and arrangement got protection, the Midjourney images did not, because typing a prompt and picking a favorite is not fixing expression yourself. The proposition codifies a standard already emerging, and adds the sensible step of documentation.
[P1.3] Why documentation? Because without it the human-authorship rule is unenforceable. A registration form is a sworn claim. If anyone can check a box saying "I contributed creatively," the rule collapses into an honor system, and the people most eager to abuse it are not working artists but volume operators.
[P1.4] Picture someone typing "cat in a spacesuit, oil painting" and generating five hundred variants before lunch. Should they walk away with five hundred copyrights, each lasting a lifetime plus seventy years? That is not incentivizing creativity; it is enclosing the commons at the cost of a few cents of compute.
[P1.5] Compare the photographer. Since Burrow-Giles, photographs are protected because the human chose the pose, lighting, framing. The same test works here. If you generate an image, then composite it, repaint sections, direct dozens of iterations toward a specific vision, and keep your layers and revision history, you have creative control and you can prove it.
[P1.6] You get your copyright.
[P1.7] Human artists are already competing against machines that can flood every market. The least the law can do is refuse to hand those machines' outputs the same monopoly it grants real authors.
