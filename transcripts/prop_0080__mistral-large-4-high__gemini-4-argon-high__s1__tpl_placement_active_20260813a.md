[P1.4] Consider the alternative: without collective representation, revenue sharing becomes a tool for schools to set terms unilaterally. [P1.5] It transforms revenue sharing from a unilateral gift into a negotiated contract, ensuring that the athletes generating billions in media rights and ticket sales have a seat at the table where those dollars are divided.
[P1.6] The motion does not require dismantling NIL entirely; it requires recognizing that direct revenue sharing demands the stability and fairness of labor law. Collective bargaining provides institutional memory, standardized terms, and enforcement mechanisms that ad hoc systems cannot. Schools get labor peace and predictable costs.
