[P1.1] Biometric payment is not just another checkout option. A card can be cancelled; a phone replaced; a password changed. A face, fingerprint or iris cannot. When payment is tied to the body, the transaction does more than settle a bill: it identifies the person, logs presence, and creates a template whose loss cannot be undone.
[P1.2] Hence the condition: biometrics may be used, but only where everyone always has a convenient non-biometric way to pay.
[P1.3] The issue is not whether customers like speed. Some will enjoy walking through a gate. The problem is that shops, transport and public venues are high-volume, time-pressured settings where access to goods and movement is practically unavoidable. If the biometric lane is quick and the non-biometric route is a separate counter, special app, staffed exception, higher fee, or line that misses the train, "consent" is a fiction.
[P1.4] People take the fastest way to buy lunch, enter a stadium, or get home. An alternative buried behind inconvenience lets the operator convert its efficiency gain into biometric enrollment.
[P1.5] The condition also prevents predictable exclusion. Scanners fail and false rejections occur; worn fingerprints, disability, age, religious dress, masks or poor lighting can make a system unreliable. A commuter whom the database rejects should not be stranded at a turnstile. Tourists, children, people without smartphones, and privacy-conscious customers should not get a second-class route.
[P1.6] Cash, cards, contactless tokens or ordinary tickets keep payment a payment rather than an identity checkpoint.
[P1.7] Con will stress speed, fraud reduction and innovation. Those benefits do not answer the asymmetry: operators capture efficiency, while consumers carry lasting privacy and exclusion risk. Restriction does not ban biometrics; it makes the operator bear the cost of its chosen system. If it wants a face-payment lane, it must keep a nearby, equally usable, non-punitive lane.
[P1.8] In public life, payment should be optional in practice, not a quiet redesign of access into a body scan.

Under [P1.3], the alternative cannot involve a separate counter, special app, staffed exception, or slower queue. Because walking past a palm or face scanner is inherently faster than fumbling for cash or cards, demanding equal speed punishes biometrics simply for working better. Worse, it outlaws formats built specifically to run without checkout hardware or staff.
[C1.2] An unmanned overnight hospital micro-mart, an express stadium bar, or an automated station side entrance cannot exist if every entry point must permanently fund and staff parallel legacy lanes. Consumers pay for that forced duplication through higher prices, longer waits, or shuttered off-peak access.
[C1.3] This blanket rule is especially overbroad because [P1.3] equates competitive shops and leisure venues with inescapable utilities. A municipal subway may be practically unavoidable; a grab-and-go grocer, coffee shop, or festival kiosk is not, and patrons there often welcome wallet-free payment that cannot be lost or pickpocketed. When a private shop tests a biometric-only format, nobody is coerced: unwilling shoppers go next door, and any merchant whose scanners routinely reject paying customers ([P1.5]) loses revenue immediately unless it fixes the tech.
[C1.4] Treating every shop like a monopoly crushes useful experimentation where consumer choice already disciplines bad design.
[C1.5] Finally, duplicate lanes misdiagnose both the risk and the remedy. Contactless bank cards and transit apps ([P1.6]) already log identity, time, and location at every tap, whereas modern biometric systems convert features into salted, encrypted mathematical templates that can be revoked and reissued if breached, answering [P1.1].
[C1.6] Meanwhile, a card reader next door does nothing to protect the millions who still enroll for speed ([P1.4]). Strict template encryption, mandatory deletion, bans on secondary tracking, and basic fallback access on monopoly transit prevent abuse and stranding without forcing every shop, gate, and venue to run two payment systems forever.
