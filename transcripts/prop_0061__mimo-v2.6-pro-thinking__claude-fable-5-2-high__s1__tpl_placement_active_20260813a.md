[P1.1] Japan's population is falling by roughly half a million people a year, and the median age keeps climbing. The question is not whether decline happens, but how Japan absorbs it. Automation and "shrinking expectations" are not a strategy — they are a way of rationing decline. Immigration is the only lever that simultaneously adds workers, consumers, and fiscal contributors, and it does so now rather than in some speculative technological future.
[P1.2] Start with the arithmetic. Automation raises output per worker, but it does not pay pension contributions, buy houses, or start families. Robots do not consume. A shrinking domestic market discourages exactly the business investment Japan needs, and smaller cohorts mean fewer young people to care for a swelling elderly population.
[P1.3] Care work, construction, logistics, nursing — these are labor-intensive, relational sectors where automation is a complement at best, not a substitute. Japan already faces acute shortages in these fields, and the shortfall grows every year the working-age population contracts.
[P1.4] "Shrinking expectations" deserves a plainer name: managed decline, with lower wages, closed rural schools and hospitals, and a heavier tax burden on the young. That is not cultural continuity; it is slow institutional hollowing. Proponents of decline imagine a smaller but richer Japan, yet per capita prosperity depends on productivity growth fast enough to outrun a shrinking denominator — historically a losing bet for societies that tried it.
[P1.5] Immigration is not a wild experiment. Japan has already moved in this direction with expanded technical intern and specified skilled worker programs, and the country has benefited visibly from foreign labor in elder care, agriculture, and convenience retail. The real debate is scale and design: faster pathways to permanent residency, language support, credential recognition, and genuine integration. These are policy choices within Japan's control; robot breakthroughs are not.
[P1.6] The honest comparison is between a tool that solves three problems at once and one that solves none of them fully. More immigration, done deliberately, is Japan's most reliable answer to decline.
