[P1.1] The choice is not between coal and climate. It is between sequencing that works and purity that fails. India's electricity demand is rising with every new factory, cold chain, data center, and urban home. The large majority of its power still comes from coal.
[P1.2] If India retires those existing plants before reliable alternatives can replace them, it does not get clean growth; it gets blackouts, expensive power, and abandoned industrial plans.
[P1.3] Rapid manufacturing is India's best climate policy. Solar panels, batteries, electric vehicles, grid equipment, and efficient appliances are manufactured goods. To build them at scale, India needs factories, skilled workers, ports, and dependable power. A garment or electronics plant cannot run on intermittent electricity and promises.
[P1.4] Existing coal assets are already sunk capital; running them longer is not the same as building new coal. It is a bridge while storage, transmission, and nuclear or hydro where available are expanded. Delaying their retirement is the cheapest way to keep the lights on and the transition politically alive.
[P1.5] Consider the counterfactual. If India closes coal early, manufacturing migrates to places with reliable power, jobs disappear, and the tax base for renewable deployment shrinks. Voters facing unemployment and blackouts turn against climate policy. That is how transitions fail.
[P1.6] By contrast, manufacturing growth creates the fiscal capacity to finance grids, batteries, and retraining. It also reduces energy poverty, which matters morally: India's per capita emissions remain far below developed countries', and its first duty is to its people.
[P1.7] The real test is not whether a coal plant closes this year. It is whether India can industrialize without expanding coal and then retire faster from a position of strength. Existing coal delayed, new coal avoided, clean manufacturing accelerated: that is the winning sequence. Premature closure is not virtue; it is a recipe for backlash and continued coal dependence.
