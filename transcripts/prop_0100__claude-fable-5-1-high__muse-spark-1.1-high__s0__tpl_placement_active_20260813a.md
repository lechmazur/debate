[P1.1] The motion turns on two words: "central" and "most." Nobody here needs to argue that reactors should be blown up tomorrow, or that France was wrong in 1975. The question is whether a typical country planning its grid for the 2030s must put nuclear at the center of its plan. It no longer must, because the alternative already exists at scale and at a price nuclear cannot touch.
[P1.2] Consider what changed. A decade ago, solar and wind were subsidized curiosities. Today they are the cheapest new electricity almost everywhere sunlight and wind exist, and the world is adding them at a pace measured in hundreds of gigawatts a year. Battery costs have fallen so far that grid storage went from pilot project to routine procurement.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, the flagship Western reactors of this generation—Flamanville, Olkiluoto, Vogtle, Hinkley—each arrived roughly a decade late and at multiples of their original budgets. A decarbonization strategy whose centerpiece arrives in 2040 is not a strategy for cutting emissions this decade. It is a promise to your grandchildren.
[P1.4] Now consider "most countries." Only around thirty nations operate reactors at all. The other hundred-plus lack the regulators, the nuclear-trained workforce, the financing, and often the grid size to absorb a gigawatt-scale unit. Tell Kenya, Chile, Vietnam, or Morocco that decarbonization runs through nuclear and you have told them to wait twenty years.
[P1.5] Tell them it runs through solar, wind, storage, and transmission and they can start pouring concrete this year—and many already have.
[P1.6] The honest objection is firming: what happens on a still winter night? The answer is a portfolio, not a reactor. Storage handles hours; interconnection moves power across weather systems; flexible demand shifts load; existing hydro and lightly used gas cover the rare gap while cleaner fuels mature. Grids in South Australia, parts of Europe, and Texas already run high renewable shares while reliability improves.
[P1.7] Nuclear, rigid and slow to ramp, is poorly suited to that gap-filling role anyway.
[P1.8] Keep the reactors you have. But the center of the strategy has moved.
