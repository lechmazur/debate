[P1.1] Public spaces are the last place we expect to be searched. Biometric mass surveillance changes that: face recognition and gait/voice matching can identify everyone in a crowd, in real time, without consent, suspicion, or even a camera operator deciding each person matters. That is not a camera; it is a searchable population index. The law should treat it as presumptively unlawful unless a narrowly defined emergency authorizes it.
[P1.2] Three reasons. First, scale converts incidental visibility into state power. Walking down a street may be public, but a persistent biometric dragnet lets government reconstruct attendance at protests, clinics, mosques, union halls, or meetings—not by watching one suspect, but by processing everyone. That is a general warrant in digital form.
[P1.3] The Fourth Amendment and analogous privacy principles do not allow suspicionless searches just because a person could have been observed.
[P1.4] Second, mission creep is predictable. Systems built for terrorism or missing children drift to immigration, minor warrants, protest policing, and commercial data-sharing. Once infrastructure exists, the path of least resistance is more scanning, longer retention, and looser thresholds. False matches impose real costs: detention, investigation, humiliation, especially where error rates are highest.
[P1.5] Third, chilling effects are the point. People who know every face is being matched alter lawful behavior: they avoid dissent, sensitive care, and association. A free society cannot require citizens to prove they belong in public.
[P1.6] The emergency exception is what makes this proposal moderate. Genuine imminent threats may justify temporary, targeted, independently authorized use under defined limits. Narrow means imminent, time-limited, geographically targeted, and subject to prompt independent review—not a standing license to scan daily life. But the baseline must be no, because the alternative is permanent, opaque, suspicionless biometric infrastructure that is nearly impossible to unwind.
[P1.7] The CON side must defend that permanent default. I do not.
