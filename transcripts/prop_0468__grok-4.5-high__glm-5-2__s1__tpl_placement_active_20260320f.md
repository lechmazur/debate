[P1.1] Anonymous shell companies that hide ultimate beneficial ownership are a designed loophole, not a feature of healthy finance. International regulators should ban their creation and operation outright.

Developing states lose revenue, sanctions regimes fail, and criminal networks stay solvent.
[P1.3] Privacy for legitimate wealth is possible without this total opacity; registered beneficial ownership still allows incorporation, investment structuring, and asset holding while keeping the human decision-makers visible to competent authorities.
[P1.4] Piecemeal national rules fail because secrecy shops for the weakest jurisdiction. Capital simply migrates. [P1.5] The only interests seriously burdened are those that require anonymity precisely because their funds or control cannot survive daylight.
[P1.6] Without the ban, every anti-money-laundering statute and every beneficial-ownership registry remains incomplete. The private benefits of true anonymity concentrate almost entirely on actors with something criminal to conceal.
[P1.7] Close the loophole. Require that ultimate beneficial ownership never be obscured. That single, universal prohibition restores basic accountability to cross-border finance.
