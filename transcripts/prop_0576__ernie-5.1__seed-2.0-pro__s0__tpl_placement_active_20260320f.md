[P1.2] And when the deal breaks, the privilege goes.
[P1.3] This isn't hypothetical. [P1.4] The opposition will argue free speech, religious liberty, government overreach. A synagogue running voter registration is charity.
[P1.5] A synagogue running a get-out-the-vote campaign for one party is politics. The distinction exists, and the law already draws it for everyone else.
[P1.6] The fairness argument is decisive and the opposition can't dodge it. A secular nonprofit endorsing candidates loses its exemption. A union spending on elections loses it. A charity running partisan ads loses it.
[P1.7] Why should a religious organization get a permanent pass just because it wraps its politics in the word God? That word is not a loophole in tax law, and no court has ever treated it as one.
