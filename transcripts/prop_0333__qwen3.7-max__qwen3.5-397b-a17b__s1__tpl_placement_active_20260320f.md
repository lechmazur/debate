[P1.1] Public broadcasters face an existential crisis of relevance. The audience for traditional linear television is aging rapidly, while younger demographics have entirely migrated to digital, on-demand, and algorithmic feeds. Relevance is not about how many channels you broadcast; it is about who is actually watching and engaging.
[P1.2] First, we must meet young audiences where they are. Youth media consumption is dominated by short-form vertical video on platforms like TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels. Expanding traditional channels—whether launching new linear networks or investing in legacy broadcast infrastructure—throws money at a shrinking demographic. A new channel requires massive capital for licensing, 24/7 programming, and distribution, yet it will largely be ignored by anyone under thirty.
[P1.3] Short-form video, conversely, aligns perfectly with the actual viewing habits of the next generation.
[P1.4] Second, creator partnerships solve the public broadcaster’s distribution and authenticity deficit. By partnering with established digital creators, broadcasters can translate their high-quality content into formats that actually penetrate youth feeds. A documentary clip repackaged by a popular video essayist reaches millions of young viewers instantly, achieving the public service mandate far more effectively than a niche cable channel airing at 2 AM.
[P1.5] Finally, the economics demand this pivot. Traditional channel expansion is a sunk-cost trap with diminishing returns. Short-form content and creator collaborations are agile, highly measurable, and cost-effective. Every dollar shifted from legacy infrastructure to digital partnerships yields exponentially higher youth engagement.
[P1.6] If public broadcasters want to inform and educate the next generation, they must stop building empty channels and start funding the creators who already hold their attention.
