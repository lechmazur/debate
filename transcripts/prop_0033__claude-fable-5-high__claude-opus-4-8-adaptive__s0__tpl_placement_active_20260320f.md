[P1.1] Democracy assumes one thing above all: that political persuasion happens in the open, where claims can be heard, contested, and corrected. Behavioral micro-targeting built on inferred psychological profiles destroys that assumption. It lets campaigns whisper a different message into each voter's ear—calibrated not to inform them, but to exploit the specific anxieties, insecurities, and emotional triggers an algorithm has guessed about them. That practice deserves prohibition, and here is the case.
[P1.2] First, it is manipulation, not persuasion. Persuasion appeals to reasons a voter can evaluate. Psychological targeting works precisely by bypassing evaluation—showing fear-laden content to people profiled as neurotic, or grievance content to people profiled as resentful, at moments they are most susceptible. The voter never knows why they saw the ad or that it was engineered for their inferred weaknesses.
[P1.3] A practice whose effectiveness depends on the target's ignorance of how they're being moved cannot be defended as free political speech serving deliberation.
[P1.4] Second, it shatters accountability. When a campaign makes one promise on television, journalists and opponents can challenge it. When it makes a thousand micro-variations visible only to fragmented psychological segments, contradictions go undetected and lies die unrebutted. The Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed the ambition: harvest intimate data, model personalities, and target the manipulable.
[P1.5] Whether or not that effort fully worked then, the machinery has only improved since.
[P1.6] Third, the profiles themselves are non-consensual. No voter agreed to have their emotional stability or fears inferred from clicks and likes and then weaponized against them. This is intimate surveillance for political exploitation.
[P1.7] Note what the prohibition does not touch. Campaigns may still target by geography, age, party registration, or stated issue interest. They may still persuade, mobilize, and advertise broadly. We ban one narrow technique: targeting based on inferred psychological and emotional profiles—the technique designed to find your soft spot and press it.
[P1.8] A democracy of manipulated voters is a democracy in name only. Prohibit the practice.
