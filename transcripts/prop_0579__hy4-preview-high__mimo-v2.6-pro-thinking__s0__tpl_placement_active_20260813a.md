[P1.1] The motion turns on a comparison, not on a fantasy of perfect security. Today, proving who you are is both fraud-prone and exclusionary: benefit systems lose money to duplicate and synthetic identities, while people without a fixed address or bank are turned away. A national digital ID offered as an option attacks both failures with one mechanism: a verifiable credential that lets a holder prove a specific fact—an account is real, an entitlement is valid, a person is over eighteen—without handing over a paper dossier every time.
[P1.2] That shift matters because most fraud today exploits weak, easily copied proofs. A stolen utility bill or scanned passport allows someone to open accounts, claim subsidies, or take over a phone number. A signed digital credential is checked cryptographically and can be revoked, so the old economies of forged documents shrink. India’s experience linking biometric-backed IDs to benefit transfers reduced duplicate and ghost beneficiaries; Estonia shows how a national e-ID can make taxes, prescriptions, and company formation routine rather than queued.
[P1.3] The privacy case is comparative too. A digital ID need not be a surveillance dragnet; properly built, it can reduce exposure. Selective disclosure replaces the bar-tender copying your full license or a website storing your birthdate forever. The credential can reveal only “over 18,” “active benefit claim,” or “account owner,” limiting what any counterparty learns and holds.
[P1.4] Optionality is the backstop: no one is compelled to enroll, so continued adoption depends on the system staying trustworthy. Data already lives across banks, telecoms, and tax records; the marginal surveillance power of an opt-in phone-based proof is small if statute forbids bulk access and audits every query.
[P1.5] Breach risk is real, but the current system already warehouses scanned IDs in hundreds of weaker places. One purpose-limited system with logging beats many leaky ones. The net effect: fraud falls, access widens, and privacy per transaction rises. That is why the option prevails.
