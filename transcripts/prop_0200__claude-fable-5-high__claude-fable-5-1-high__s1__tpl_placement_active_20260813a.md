[P1.1] Lunar mining is coming with or without our permission. Multiple national space agencies and private firms are already designing extraction missions for water ice and regolith. The real question is not whether extraction happens, but whether it happens under enforceable conditions or in a legal vacuum. Recognizing commercial extraction as legal now—conditioned on transparency and debris-control obligations—is the only path that produces governance before harm occurs.
[P1.2] First, the legal foundation already exists. The Outer Space Treaty forbids national appropriation of celestial bodies; it says nothing prohibiting resource use, just as the high seas cannot be owned but fish can lawfully be caught. Several nations, including the United States, Luxembourg, Japan, and the UAE, have already legislated resource rights on this reading. Recognizing extraction with conditions attached is not lawbreaking—it is interpreting existing law responsibly and adding safeguards on top.
[P1.3] Second, conditionality is leverage. Right now, an operator faces no binding duty to disclose operations or manage debris. Offer legal recognition in exchange for compliance, and companies gain what they desperately need—title certainty for investors and buyers—while the public gains what it needs: disclosure, coordination zones, and debris standards. Refuse recognition, and operators still fly, but under no obligations at all.
[P1.4] CON's position doesn't stop mining; it strips away the only carrot that makes mining accountable.
[P1.5] Third, waiting for a "full global treaty" is waiting for Godot. The Moon Agreement attracted no major spacefaring power in four decades. Consensus treaty-making at the UN moves slower than launch schedules. Interim recognition doesn't preclude a treaty—it builds the practice, precedent, and technical standards a future treaty will codify, exactly as national continental-shelf claims preceded the Law of the Sea Convention.
[P1.6] The choice is between conditional legality that channels behavior and a purity stance that guarantees a lawless scramble. Rules that exist beat rules we merely wish for. Recognize, condition, and govern—now.
