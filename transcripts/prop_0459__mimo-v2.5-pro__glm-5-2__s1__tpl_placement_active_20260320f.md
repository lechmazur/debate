When a single geopolitical actor controls the chokepoints of the energy transition, every importing nation faces an unacceptable strategic risk.

[P1.5] South Korea's semiconductor strategy, China's own solar industry, and America's shale revolution all followed this pattern: strategic investment first, cost competitiveness later. [C1.3] The proposition frames this as a one-time premium for long-term security, but there is no guarantee domestic production ever becomes cost-competitive, especially when every country pursuing localization fragments the global scale economies that drove clean-tech costs down in the first place.

[C1.5] The trade tensions aren't a minor side effect either. [C1.6] The real alternative isn't passivity. It's strategic diversification—friend-shoring, targeted stockpiles of critical minerals, and investment in chokepoint resilience—without the full cost and friction of duplicating entire supply chains domestically.
