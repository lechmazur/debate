[P1.1] Prohibition does not suppress demand for cannabis; it transfers supply to whoever can enforce it with violence. In much of Latin America, that means armed gangs, local mafias, and trafficking networks. Cannabis is bulky, widely consumed, and traded constantly, so it provides criminal groups with routine cash flow, street-level employment, and leverage over neighborhoods. A national legal framework takes that market out of their hands by replacing armed dealers with licensed producers, tracked distribution, age limits, and taxable retail.
[P1.2] The strategic value is not that legalization solves all crime. It is that it cuts a high-volume, steady revenue stream and changes how the state spends its coercive power. Today, police and soldiers spend enormous effort chasing a plant that millions of people already consume. That generates bribes, informants, and prisons full of low-level offenders while leaving investigators little capacity to pursue extortion, homicide, money laundering, and cocaine trafficking.
[P1.3] If cannabis is regulated, the state can redirect enforcement toward the crimes that most directly terrorize citizens, and it can collect revenue for treatment, prevention, and community policing instead of surrendering it to criminal budgets.
[P1.4] National regulation is crucial because patchy local rules simply move smuggling routes. A uniform system with licensing, seed-to-sale monitoring, and reasonable taxes can shrink the illicit market rather than merely rebrand it. Uruguay’s experiment shows a small country can regulate cannabis nationally; North American markets show that legal supply can increasingly displace illegal dealers when rules are practical. Latin American governments need not copy every detail, but they should use the same core tool: remove the profit from the criminal class.
[P1.5] Opponents will say cartels can switch to other drugs. Of course they can. But weakening organized crime is about reducing resources, corruption, and territorial control. Legalizing cannabis does all three.
