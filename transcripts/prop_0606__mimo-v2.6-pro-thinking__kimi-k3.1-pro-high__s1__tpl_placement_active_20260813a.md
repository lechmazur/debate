[P1.1] A patent is not a natural right. It is a bargain a state strikes with an inventor: a time-limited monopoly in exchange for disclosure and eventual public access. When the monopoly's price puts an essential medicine beyond ordinary reach, the bargain has failed on its own terms. The state is entitled to reclaim what it granted — through compulsory licensing, on payment of reasonable royalties to the patent holder.
[P1.2] This is not expropriation; it is the patent system working as designed, and as international trade rules expressly permit.
[P1.3] The proposition's real strength is the word "threshold." Critics imagine compulsory licensing as arbitrary, political confiscation. A defined affordability criterion — say, a medicine priced beyond a set share of ordinary income, or beyond a multiple of generic production cost — converts emergency discretion into a predictable rule. Countries that set the threshold publicly give manufacturers ex ante notice, courts an administrable standard, and negotiators a starting point.
[P1.4] Predictability protects firms more than the status quo, where pressure builds until it explodes into one-off seizures.
[P1.5] The evidence that this works is well known. When generic competition and compulsory licensing broke patent control over first-generation HIV antiretrovirals, prices fell from levels that made treatment impossible in most of the world to a fraction of that, and treatment scaled to millions. Similar moves on cancer drugs in middle-income countries and the bitter disputes over COVID-19 vaccine access show the same pattern: patent-backed pricing is the binding constraint on access, and licensing is the lever that moves it.
[P1.6] The innovation objection proves too much. Essential-medicine R&D is driven by markets and often publicly funded; a licensing regime confined to essential products at above-threshold prices, with royalties paid, leaves the profitable mass of pharmaceutical markets untouched. What it denies is only the right to hold a life-saving treatment hostage.
[P1.7] CON must argue that a lawful tool exists, lives are at stake, and we should not use it. That is a heavy burden.
