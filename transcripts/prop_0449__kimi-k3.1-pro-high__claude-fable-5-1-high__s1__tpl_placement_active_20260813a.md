[C1.1] Strip the motion to its mechanism and ask one question: who actually gets penalised? Not Britain, France, or Germany, which burned coal for a century and can retire their last plants on schedule. The penalties land on India, Indonesia, Vietnam, South Africa, Bangladesh—countries whose coal fleets are often under fifteen years old, whose grids were built around them, and whose citizens use a fraction of the electricity an American does. A rule whose costs fall almost entirely on the poorest participants is not a level playing field.
[C1.2] It is the old carbon-colonial bargain with a tariff schedule attached.
[C1.3] The penalties are also self-defeating on their own terms. Finance penalties cut off capital from the exact countries that need it to build the storage, transmission, and firm clean power PRO rightly says must replace coal. You cannot starve a country of finance and simultaneously demand it finance a transition. Trade penalties are worse: they invite retaliation, fragment markets, and hand every coal-dependent government a nationalist grievance that makes retirement politically impossible rather than easier.
[C1.4] Then there is the credibility gap. The largest coal fleet on earth is China's. Does anyone believe Europe or the United States will impose enforceable trade sanctions on China for running coal plants in 2041? Either the penalties exempt the powerful in practice—so they only bite the weak—or they are enforced and trigger a trade war that dwarfs any climate benefit.
[C1.5] Both outcomes discredit the regime.
[C1.6] History already ran this experiment. Kyoto's binding targets with consequences produced withdrawals and empty seats; Paris's pledge-and-review produced near-universal participation and the first real bending of emissions curves. Coercion shrinks the coalition; the climate problem needs the coalition whole.
[C1.7] The alternative is not "trusting promises." It is differentiated timelines, finance that arrives before plants close, and transition partnerships that make coal uncompetitive rather than illegal. PRO's uniform deadline plus punishment offers moral clarity for the rich and penalties for the poor. That is why it fails.
