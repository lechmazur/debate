[P1.2] Consider what minimums do in practice. A developer building apartments in a sprawling metro area must often construct one or two parking spaces per unit. That concrete costs tens of thousands of dollars per space, costs passed directly to renters and buyers. [P1.3] Cities like Buffalo and Minneapolis that eliminated parking minimums have seen housing production accelerate without collapsing into parking chaos.
[P1.4] The car-dependency objection is the heart of the opposition, and it deserves a direct answer. A maximum parking requirement does not ban parking. [P1.5] What it eliminates is the zombie parking: the empty spaces mandated by code that sit half-full, wasting capital and space.
