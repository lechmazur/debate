[P1.1] Populist movements in functioning democracies are not healthy correctives to elite failure, but almost always advance warnings that the guardrails of representative democracy are already eroding, and their rise accelerates rather than reverses institutional decay and democratic backsliding.
[P1.2] Populism’s defining premise—that a homogeneous, "pure" people is locked in a moral struggle against a corrupt, illegitimate elite—by its nature delegitimizes all counter-majoritarian institutions: independent judiciaries, non-partisan civil services, free press, and minority rights protections. These are not elite perks to be dismantled; they are the guardrails that prevent majority tyranny and ensure peaceful transfers of power. When populist movements gain traction, their core project is not to hold specific elites accountable, but to concentrate power in the hands of a populist leader who claims to speak for "the people" alone, with no mediating checks on their authority.
[P1.3] Consider Hungary’s Viktor Orbán, who rose to power in 2010 on legitimate public frustration with post-2008 austerity and elite corruption. Rather than addressing those specific grievances, he spent the next decade packing the constitutional court, shutting down independent media, rewriting the constitution to entrench single-party rule, and passing laws targeting LGBTQ+ and Roma minorities. The initial populist wave was not a fix for elite failure—it was the opening act of a systematic demolition of democratic institutions.
[P1.4] Proponents of populism as a corrective claim it forces elites to respond to public anger, but this ignores that the tools populists deploy—eroding electoral integrity, silencing dissent, undermining independent oversight—are the exact mechanisms of democratic backsliding. When popular discontent channels into populist movements, it does not reset corrupt systems; it replaces them with unaccountable authoritarian-leaning rule. The warning sign is not the movement itself, but the fact that enough public trust has eroded for such a movement to take hold, and its success only hastens that decay.
