[P1.1] A community should not pay twice to host a private facility: once through foregone taxes, and again through higher utility bills, infrastructure spending, or depleted water reserves. If new substations, transmission, generation capacity, pipelines, or treatment facilities are built for them but recovered across ordinary customers, the advertised “investment” is partly a transfer from households and existing businesses to a highly capitalized corporation.
[P1.2] The proposition offers a disciplined remedy, not a ban. Tax incentives may continue, but they should be capped unless the data center bears the incremental local costs it creates: dedicated grid upgrades, capacity and reliability burdens, water infrastructure, and scarcity-related costs. Once those costs are covered, governments retain room to compete for genuinely beneficial projects.
[P1.3] This safeguard is especially necessary because the bargaining process is structurally distorted. Without a cap, officials can claim credit for headline investment while shifting long-term costs onto future ratepayers and administrations.
[P1.4] Full-cost pricing also improves where data centers are built. [P1.5] The decisive principle is simple: public incentives should purchase public benefit, not conceal private operating costs.
