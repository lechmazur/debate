[C1.4] Next, the stolen-property analogy PRO relies on is legally unfounded. Modern property law protects bona fide purchasers precisely because chains of title cannot be unwound indefinitely without destroying the stability that property systems require. More importantly, the modern state is not the same legal entity as a colonial government acting three centuries ago. Current taxpayers—including recent immigrants, descendants of indentured laborers, and members of other disadvantaged groups—would fund these obligations despite having no role in, and often no ancestral connection to, the original dispossession.
[C1.5] That is not remedy; it is the state transferring burdens onto people who did not create them.
[C1.6] Finally, where genuine, traceable grievances exist, legal mechanisms already function. [C1.7] Justice for Indigenous communities is a serious obligation. This proposition, as written, does not deliver it—it creates an unbounded and politically exploitable commitment that undermines both rule of law and the communities it purports to serve.
