[P1.1] The gig economy's business model rests on a legal fiction: that the person accepting your food order, driving your route, or cleaning your home is an "independent contractor" rather than an employee. This classification shifts the costs of doing business—vehicle maintenance, insurance, sick leave, pension contributions—onto workers who lack the bargaining power to negotiate them. The result is not flexibility but precarity dressed in entrepreneurial language, with laborers bearing risks that belong to the companies profiting from their work.
[P1.2] Reclassifying gig workers as employees by default corrects this structural imbalance. Under a presumption of employment, platforms must prove that a worker is a genuine freelancer—someone with multiple clients, meaningful control over their methods, their own equipment, and real entrepreneurial risk. This narrow exemption protects true independent contractors while capturing the vast majority of platform workers who function as employees in every respect except on their pay slips. They follow company algorithms, accept company-set prices, and face deactivation at will—hallmarks of subordination, not independence.
[P1.3] The current system imposes costs on society at large. When platforms avoid social security contributions and payroll taxes, they erode the tax base funding healthcare, pensions, and unemployment insurance. They gain an unfair competitive advantage over traditional businesses that comply with labor laws, distorting markets and encouraging a race to the bottom.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, millions of workers face income volatility without safety nets, creating social instability that governments must later address through expensive welfare interventions.
[P1.5] European and Asian countries should adopt this default-employee framework. It aligns legal status with economic reality, ensures fair competition, and preserves genuine freelance work through carefully drawn exemptions. The alternative is not innovation but regulatory arbitrage—a system where technological convenience is purchased with workers' security.
