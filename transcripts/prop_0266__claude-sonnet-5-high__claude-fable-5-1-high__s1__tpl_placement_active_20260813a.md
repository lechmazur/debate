[C1.1] The proposition smuggles in a category error, and the whole case rests on it. Disclosure means telling a customer what your product costs. This motion demands something no market anywhere requires: that a seller, at the moment of sale, advertise a competitor's cheaper alternative. Airlines must show their own baggage fees; nobody makes United display the Amtrak fare.
[C1.2] Lenders must show APR; no bank is forced to list the credit union across the street. Amazon isn't required to flash the price at the local bookstore. The airline and loan analogies prove the opposite of what they're offered for — they show that transparency law targets your own price, not compelled steering to rivals.
[C1.3] Second, the "same burrito" framing is false. A delivered burrito is not the same product as a pickup burrito. One includes a paid driver, insurance, dispatch, real-time tracking, and refund guarantees; the other includes you putting on shoes. Calling the difference "material overcharging for the same thing" is like calling a taxi a rip-off because walking is free.
[C1.4] Customers already see itemized fees before confirming; the price isn't hidden, it's simply the price of a different service.
[C1.5] Third, the mandate is unworkable and the burden lands on the wrong people. Apps don't know a restaurant's phone-order price, whether the direct site is live, or whether the "nearest independent option" is even open. Forcing them to display it means displaying guesses — and liability when the guess is wrong.
[C1.6] Meanwhile, restaurants list on apps precisely because they choose reach over margin; many set their own app markups. If platforms must actively divert customers off-platform, the predictable response is exclusivity clauses, price-parity requirements, and higher commissions to recover lost volume. The small taqueria the motion claims to protect gets squeezed hardest.
[C1.7] The clash is simple: PRO wants a disclosure rule and has written a steering rule. Address hidden fees by mandating clear, upfront, all-in pricing of the app's own product. That fixes the asymmetry without conscripting one business into marketing another.
