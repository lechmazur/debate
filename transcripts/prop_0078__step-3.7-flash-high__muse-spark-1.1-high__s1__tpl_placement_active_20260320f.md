[P1.1] The proposition does not demand immigrants abandon their cultural heritage; it only requires them to meet the baseline prerequisites for equal, functional membership in the societies they choose to join. Treating integration as optional, as CON will argue, creates a two-tiered system of citizenship, where marginalized immigrant enclaves are permanently locked out of public life, and social trust between communities collapses.
[P1.2] First, a common public language is a non-negotiable practical requirement, not a cultural demand. Without it, immigrants cannot access emergency healthcare, navigate legal or tenancy systems, advocate for their rights, or communicate with neighbors in crisis. For example, a monolingual immigrant in Germany who cannot speak German cannot understand a doctor’s diagnosis, read a rental contract, or report a crime to police, leaving them vulnerable to exploitation and imposing avoidable costs on public services all taxpayers fund.
[P1.3] Second, core civic norms are the shared guardrails that allow diverse groups to coexist peacefully. These include baseline commitments to gender equality, rule of law, non-violent conflict resolution, and respect for the dignity of all people regardless of identity. Treating these as optional invites direct harm: unintegrated enclaves that reject norms banning domestic violence or child marriage expose vulnerable women and children to abuse the state is obligated to prevent.
[P1.4] Third, voluntary integration has already failed wherever it has been tried. In parts of France and the UK where decades of lax integration policy allowed parallel, linguistically isolated communities to form, social trust between immigrant and native-born groups is near historic lows, radicalization rates are higher, and public services cannot reach residents who cannot communicate with them. This harms everyone: immigrant families trapped in marginalized enclaves, and native-born citizens who see no shared basis for mutual obligation. CON will frame this requirement as forced assimilation, but that is a deliberate mischaracterization.
[P1.5] No one is demanding immigrants stop celebrating cultural holidays or speaking their native languages at home. They are only being asked to meet the same baseline expectations all citizens are held to, for the good of the whole community.
