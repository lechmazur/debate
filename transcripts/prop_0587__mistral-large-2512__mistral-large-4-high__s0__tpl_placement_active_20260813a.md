[P1.1] A carbon border adjustment isn’t just another climate policy—it’s the lever that turns global trade into a force for decarbonization. [P1.4] With a border adjustment, American manufacturers won’t just compete on price—they’ll compete on carbon, and so will their global rivals. That creates a race to the top, not a race to the bottom.

A border adjustment is active: it forces every trading partner to internalize the carbon cost of their exports. Over five years, that pressure will cut far more global emissions than subsidies ever could, because it doesn’t just reward clean production—it punishes dirty production, everywhere.
[P1.6] The alternative—relying on voluntary action or hoping other countries follow our lead—is a gamble we can’t afford. The climate clock is ticking, and a border adjustment is the tool that turns global trade into a climate solution, not a liability.
