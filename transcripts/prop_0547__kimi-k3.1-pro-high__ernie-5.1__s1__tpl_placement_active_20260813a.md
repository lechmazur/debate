[C1.1] The proposition asks you to isolate one variable—feed architecture—and blame it for rising adolescent anxiety and depression. That isolation is precisely where the case collapses.
[C1.2] First, selection effects destroy the causal story. Adolescents already coping with anxiety or depression actively seek out content that resonates with their emotional state, whether the feed is chronological or optimized. A teenager feeling isolated will find isolation content regardless of sorting mechanism. Attributing the harm to the algorithm rather than to the underlying vulnerability inverts the direction of causation.
[C1.3] The feed did not create the insecurity; it merely reflected what was already there.
[C1.4] Second, chronological feeds are not the benign baseline the proposition implies. Without any filtering or ordering, a teen scrolls into whatever was posted most recently—which can include a random mixture of self-harm, violence, body shaming, or distressingly graphic news that happened to be uploaded that minute. Engagement optimization, whatever its flaws, can actually steer users away from one-off shocking material toward content clusters where they find community, humor, or coping strategies. The algorithm's "feedback loop" is not unidirectional; it can reinforce positive engagement just as easily as negative.
[C1.5] Third, adolescents are not passive receptacles. Teens curate their own experience—they follow creators, use search, report content, and shift between platforms. A feed type that delivers polished lifestyle videos repeatedly might prompt a teen to laugh, parody, or simply skip. Treating teenagers as prisoners of whatever autoplay delivers next underestimates their agency and overstates the algorithm's power.
[C1.6] The real question is whether swapping one delivery system for another measurably shifts population-level mental health outcomes. Existing research on social media and youth wellbeing shows small, inconsistent effects that get swamped by family context, sleep habits, and pre-existing conditions. Changing the sort order on a video app is a thin intervention to bear the weight of this proposition.
