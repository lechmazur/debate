[P1.1] The choice is not between staying and retreating. Climate-vulnerable cities will retreat—from certain blocks, floodplains, shorelines, and subsiding ground—because water and heat will decide that for them. The real choice is whether retreat is planned while options remain, or chaotic after repeated disasters destroy the tax base, housing stock, and institutions. This motion chooses planning.
[P1.2] Mandatory long-term relocation planning is not midnight bulldozers. It means cities must map which areas cannot be defended, set triggers and timelines, identify receiving neighborhoods, finance moving infrastructure and households, and protect residents' rights before land values collapse. Done early, relocation can keep neighbors and congregations together, move schools and clinics in sequence, and let people sell with compensation rather than flee with nothing.
[P1.3] Waiting sounds compassionate, but it is a recipe for injustice. After a major flood or storm, the people with credit and cars leave; the elderly, renters, and poor often remain in damaged homes. Rebuilding money flows unevenly. Insurance quietly withdraws.
[P1.4] Local government, already overwhelmed, negotiates each buyout in crisis mode. New Orleans after Katrina is the warning: many residents never returned, and redevelopment was uneven—not because relocation was planned, but because it wasn't.
[P1.5] Voluntary planning fails because no mayor wants to announce which neighborhood has no long-term future. Politicians delay, owners keep building, and the bill grows. A mandate changes the default: cities must prepare, not merely react. It also saves money, since moving a water main before it is destroyed is cheaper than rebuilding a ruined city block by block.
[P1.6] Opponents will call this defeatism. It is the opposite. Planning is how a city keeps agency, dignity, and order when nature forces the question. The cities that begin now will choose where and how to move.
[P1.7] The cities that wait will have retreat chosen for them—late, unequal, and cruel.
