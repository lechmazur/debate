[P1.1] The case for banning heritable germline editing rests on one irreducible fact: it is the only medical intervention where the patient is every descendant who will ever exist, and none of them consented.
[P1.2] Somatic gene therapy treats a living person who can weigh risks and benefits. Germline editing of embryos makes a one-time, irrevocable decision for every generation that follows. There is no recalling the edit. There is no monitoring for long-term harm and withdrawing treatment.
[P1.3] The change is permanent, cumulative, and inherited by people who cannot refuse it.
[P1.4] This matters because we do not understand the genome well enough to predict what a germline edit does across generations. What looks like a clean fix for one condition may introduce subtle harms that only surface in descendants, by which point the edit is distributed throughout the lineage.
[P1.5] The standard medical ethics framework—risk, benefit, consent, reversibility—collapses because three of those four pillars are absent.
[P1.6] Then there is the line between therapy and enhancement. Once you permit germline editing for serious disease, pressure builds to expand the category: why not edit for lower disease risk? [P1.7] The only reliable firewall is a ban on heritable editing for reproduction, while leaving somatic therapy untouched.
[P1.8] This is not an anti-medicine position. It is a line drawn where medicine stops treating individuals and begins designing lineages. Somatic therapy heals the person in front of you. Germline editing for reproduction manufactures the people who come after.
[P1.9] That power should not exist.
