[P1.1] Parents hold the legal and moral responsibility to guide teenagers safely toward adulthood, yet modern digital architecture renders household boundaries practically unenforceable. [P1.2] Allowing parents to impose operating-system-level age and recommendation limits fixes this structural asymmetry at the root.

[P1.4] Meanwhile, limiting algorithmic recommendations leaves teenagers free to text friends, join group chats, and intentionally search for health or educational resources. Because parents are allowed, not forced, to apply these guardrails, families can relax boundaries gradually as a thirteen-year-old matures into a seventeen-year-old, preserving private communication without resorting to spyware or isolation.
