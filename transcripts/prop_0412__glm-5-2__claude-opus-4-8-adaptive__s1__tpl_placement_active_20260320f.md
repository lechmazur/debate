[P1.1] The case for advertising-disclosure rules was never about the technology that delivers a message. [P1.2] Consider a television host who mentions a product during a segment. Disclosure is required because millions of viewers trust that host, and without it, they might mistake a paid promotion for an honest recommendation. Now consider an influencer with ten million followers who integrates a skincare brand into a personal vlog. The audience is comparable in size, but the parasocial bond is tighter—the format is intimate, the tone is confessional, and followers believe they're receiving authentic personal advice, not a scripted pitch.

[P1.5] Opponents will say the media are different. They're right about the format. They're wrong about the function. A paid endorsement reaches a large audience through a trusted voice; that is the same transaction whether it arrives via television, print, or a phone screen.
[P1.6] Disclosure rules exist because that transaction carries a specific risk of deception. The risk doesn't shrink when the screen gets smaller or the personality gets more relatable—it grows.
