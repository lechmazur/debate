[C1.1] Books and films are static, self-contained artifacts: hand an archive a paperback or video file, and the work survives. Software is fundamentally different, making legal deposit "just as" governments mandate for books and films a category error.
[C1.2] Consider the very fragility [P1.2] highlights: servers shut down, APIs vanish, and license checks expire. A multiplayer online game, a social app, or a networked digital artwork is not a standalone file; it is a live ecosystem of cloud servers, dynamic databases, and user interaction. That traps any mandate in a fatal dilemma. If the state merely demands the consumer installer, the archive gets a dead digital husk that will not even boot once servers close.
[C1.3] Yet if the state compels a functional deposit, creators cannot just mail a copy; they must build standalone server emulators, strip DRM, and hand over back-end infrastructure. That is not incremental deposit; it is coerced software engineering.
[C1.4] Worse, what [P1.5] waves away as "design questions" are unresolvable legal and security hazards. Modern apps weave together licensed third-party engines, codecs, and middleware that publishers do not own and cannot legally deposit. Moreover, studios reuse core netcode and proprietary engines across commercial products for years. Forcing firms to surrender DRM-stripped builds and server code into centralized state archives creates a massive cybersecurity honeypot: a single breach leaks live trade secrets and exposes zero-day vulnerabilities in code still powering active apps.
[C1.5] Finally, restricting the mandate to "culturally significant" software creates a bureaucratic catch-22. Archives cannot ingest millions of routine app updates, yet cultural significance in software, from viral indie games to experimental net art, mostly emerges retrospectively, after servers vanish. Real-time state selectors will miss grassroots works while imposing punishing re-engineering costs on small creators lacking legal or engineering teams. Preservation requires copyright exemptions for archivists and voluntary funding, not a coercive mandate that either hoards dead binaries or compromises live code.
