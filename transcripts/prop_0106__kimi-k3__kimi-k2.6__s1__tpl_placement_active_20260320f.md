[P1.1] In every other corner of pediatric medicine, the rule is the same: when a child has a diagnosed condition, parents and qualified clinicians weigh the evidence and decide together. This motion asks nothing more for gender dysphoria — not treatment on demand, but access when diagnosis, parental consent, and clinical judgment all align. To oppose it is to say the state should override all three and impose a blanket ban. That is the extraordinary claim here, and it bears the burden.
[P1.2] First, refusing treatment is not neutrality — it is a decision with irreversible consequences. Puberty does not wait for certainty. A transgender girl forced through testosterone puberty acquires a deepened voice and a masculinized skeleton no later treatment fully reverses; a transgender boy develops breasts that may eventually require surgery. Blockers exist precisely to press pause.
[P1.3] They have been used for decades in children with precocious puberty, their effects are substantially reversible, and they buy time to see whether dysphoria persists before any longer-term step is taken.
[P1.4] Second, the stakes are measured in lives. Adolescent gender dysphoria is consistently linked with severe depression, self-harm, and elevated suicide risk, and major medical bodies in pediatrics, endocrinology, and psychiatry recognize these treatments as legitimate for carefully selected patients. Clinicians who treat these adolescents report marked improvements in functioning and wellbeing. When the child, the parents, and the treating team — the people who actually know the patient — converge on the same judgment, a legislator's veto is not caution; it is hubris.
[P1.5] Third, the safeguards are written into the motion itself: formal diagnosis, parental agreement, qualified clinicians, staged care with hormones only after blockers and further assessment. Rare regret cases argue for careful practice, not prohibition — we refine medical standards; we do not ban treatments because outcomes are imperfect.
[P1.6] The question is simple: who decides — the family and doctors in the room, or a one-size-fits-all law that refuses to look at the child in front of it?

[C1.1] The motion treats puberty as a pathology to be arrested, asking us to trust that an adolescent diagnosis can reliably predict who needs a lifetime of medicalization. That trust is misplaced.
[C1.2] Puberty blockers are sold as a harmless pause, but they do not freeze a child in neutral. They halt bone accretion, alter brain development during critical windows, and isolate the child in pre-puberty while peers mature. The result is not deliberation but momentum: once on blockers, the overwhelming majority of minors proceed to cross-sex hormones, permanent infertility, and an irreversibly altered endocrine system. Calling this "reversible" misrepresents the clinical reality; the psychological and physiological cascade is exceptionally difficult to stop.
[C1.3] The diagnostic category is no firmer. Gender dysphoria in minors is frequently unstable. Many of these children would otherwise see their distress resolve through natural puberty, growing up to be gay or lesbian adults comfortable in their natal sex. Medicalizing them does not rescue a fixed identity from a rejecting body; it pathologizes ordinary developmental uncertainty and locks a changing mind into a permanent pharmaceutical and surgical trajectory.
[C1.4] The motion says safeguards exist because parents and qualified clinicians agree. But consensus is not a substitute for competence. No adolescent brain can fully reckon with the permanent loss of fertility and sexual function, or with a lifetime of pharmaceutical dependence. Parents cannot authorize a harm their child is not yet mature enough to comprehend, and clinicians operating under pressure to affirm cannot manufacture consent where true comprehension is developmentally impossible.
[C1.5] Refusing these interventions is not state cruelty or legislative indifference; it is the only position that preserves every future option. Natural puberty is not a disease to be blocked. It is the developmental baseline that allows a child to become the adult they are actually going to be.
