[C1.1] Everyone in this debate agrees on the ideal: judge people by ability. The disagreement is about what actually happens when you abolish the clean, predictable rule and replace it with case-by-case assessment of every aging employee. The answer is ugly, and it is worse for older workers themselves.
[C1.2] First, individualized assessment is not a benign substitute; it is a machine for humiliation and litigation. Abolish the retirement age and the seventy-year-old surgeon does not simply "keep working" — she gets cognitive testing, peer surveillance, documented complaints, and eventually a termination hearing where her decline is entered into the record. Mandatory retirement lets people leave with a party and a pension. The alternative is being fired, individually, for failing.
[C1.3] A universal rule that applies to everyone equally is more dignified than a personalized verdict of incompetence.
[C1.4] Second, decline is real but stealthy. It arrives gradually, unevenly, and — crucially — invisibly to the person experiencing it. Testing catches gross impairment, not the slow erosion of judgment, stamina, and adaptability. In medicine, aviation-adjacent work, driving, policing, and the judiciary, we typically discover the deficit after the harm.
[C1.5] An age line is a blunt instrument, but blunt instruments prevent errors that fine-grained tools miss.
[C1.6] Third, mandatory retirement is one half of a bargain. Seniority pay, strong tenure protections, and generous pensions exist because employers know the commitment has an end date. Remove the end date and employers will rationally strip out seniority raises and job security for everyone over fifty. Older workers trade a fixed exit for decades of protection; abolition unwinds the protection, not just the exit.
[C1.7] Finally, renewal matters. Predictable turnover opens promotions, refreshes ideas, and prevents institutions from calcifying into gerontocracies where the young wait indefinitely for the old to fail publicly.
[C1.8] The choice is not "birthday versus ability." It is a humane, predictable rule versus surveillance, litigation, and blocked institutions. Keep the rule.
