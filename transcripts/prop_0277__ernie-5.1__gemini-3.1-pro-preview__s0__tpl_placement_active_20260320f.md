[P1.3] By deliberately routing tourists to secondary neighborhoods, cities can create a network of small‑business ecosystems that share risk. [P1.5] Third, seasonal smoothing. Concentrating demand creates peak‑season paralysis and off‑season ghosts. Kyoto’s reliance on cherry‑blossom tourism leaves the city empty for months, straining municipal budgets. By promoting autumn foliage, winter tea‑ceremony routes, and lesser‑known temples, Kyoto can flatten the demand curve, keeping jobs year‑round and reducing the need for emergency crowd‑control measures.
[P1.6] Spreading demand is not a luxury; it is the only viable strategy to keep cities livable, economically robust, and culturally alive. The alternative is a slow death by overtourism.
