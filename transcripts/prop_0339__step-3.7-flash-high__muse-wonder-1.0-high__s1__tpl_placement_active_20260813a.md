[P1.1] Shared family devices—tablets, old phones, home laptops used by everyone from young kids to elderly relatives—are a staple of modern households, but unregulated access to gambling, pornographic, and high-risk social apps on these devices creates preventable, tangible harm to the most vulnerable users. Minors exposed to gambling content are significantly more likely to develop problem gambling habits in adolescence and adulthood. Unrestricted access to pornography distorts young people’s understanding of consent, healthy relationships, and body image, with well-documented links to increased anxiety and disordered eating.
[P1.2] High-risk social apps, which promote self-harm, dangerous viral challenges, or predatory grooming, have already led to physical injury and death for teen users lured by algorithmic feeds designed to maximize engagement at the cost of safety.
[P1.3] Requiring app stores to offer a simple, one-click block or hide option for these categories on shared devices is a low-burden, high-reward solution that does not infringe on adult autonomy. Consenting adults retain full, unrestricted access to all content on their personal, non-shared devices; this measure only applies to devices shared across a household, where no single user holds sole access rights. Current alternatives are deeply inadequate: third-party parental control software is often costly, clunky, and easily bypassed by tech-literate teens, leaving millions of families with no reliable way to protect their children on devices they assume are safe.
[P1.4] A standardized, app-store-provided toggle would be universally accessible, require no extra cost or technical expertise, and close this gap without restricting the rights of adult users. This policy does not ban any content for consenting adults—it simply gives families a free, simple tool to shield the people they care about from preventable harm. The benefit of protecting minors and other vulnerable shared device users vastly outweighs the trivial administrative cost to app stores of adding a single toggle to their existing content moderation systems.
