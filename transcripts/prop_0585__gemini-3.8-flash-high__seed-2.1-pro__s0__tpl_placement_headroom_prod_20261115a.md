[P1.1] Decarbonization is fundamentally a spatial challenge. Clean electricity is produced where natural resources are richest: wind across the Great Plains, solar across the desert Southwest, and hydro in the north, whereas consumer demand clusters in distant metropolitan corridors. High-voltage transmission expansion and regional market integration resolve this structural mismatch directly. By linking distinct weather systems and unifying regional economic dispatch, transmission converts fragmented, isolated balancing authorities into an integrated, resilient, continent-scale machine.
[P1.2] The physical and economic efficiency of grid integration far surpasses long-duration energy storage. Moving electricity across modern high-voltage lines yields efficiency rates exceeding ninety percent. In contrast, speculative long-duration storage technologies, such as green hydrogen synthesis, flow batteries, or thermal media, suffer punitive round-trip energy losses of thirty to fifty percent. Subsidizing storage as our primary strategy requires massive, wasteful overbuilding of renewable generation merely to feed that thermodynamic dissipation.
[P1.3] Transmission delivers clean power instantaneously, capitalizing on temporal and geographic diversity: when overcast skies blanket coastal cities, inland wind fills the void; as evening arrives in the east, western solar bridges the peak.
[P1.4] Crucially, transmission maximizes capital utilization. Interregional lines and unified power pools deliver value every single hour of the year by leveling price spikes, balancing frequency, and optimizing generation across state borders. In contrast, dedicated long-duration storage assets are engineered specifically for infrequent, multi-day weather anomalies. They sit capital-trapped and idle for months at a time, driving up system costs with abysmal capacity factors and enormous capital recovery burdens.
[P1.5] Expanding transmission footprints aggregates diverse customer loads, slashes redundant reserve margins, and prevents localized grid collapse far more reliably than isolated energy stockpiling. We cannot solve a continental supply problem by hoarding power in silos; we must move it.
