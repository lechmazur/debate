[C1.1] The PRO case treats debt relief as a procurement contract: we cancel payments if you spend on resilience. That turns an emergency lifeline into a conditional grant with creditor-defined terms. The central clash is whether automatic conditionality makes relief faster, fairer, and more effective. It does none of these.
[C1.2] First, debt distress after a climate shock is a liquidity emergency. Governments need immediate breathing room to import fuel, pay health workers, restore power, and rebuild homes. Automatic climate-investment conditions do not create that breathing room; they defer it until projects are designed, certified, and audited. After Pakistan’s floods, the urgent need was unconditional fiscal space and debt suspension, not a compliance regime deciding which drainage schemes qualify.
[C1.3] If relief arrives only after conditionality is satisfied, the doom loop PRO fears has already tightened.
[C1.4] Second, the instrument is mismatched to the problem. Resilience investments pay off over years, sometimes decades; sovereign debt service is due now. Tying relief to long-horizon capital spending can force vulnerable states to privilege creditor-approved infrastructure over social protection, local adaptation, or disaster compensation. It also rewards countries with bureaucratic capacity to draft bankable projects and punishes fragile states with the least capacity—precisely those most exposed.
[C1.5] Third, “automatic” does not depoliticize conditionality; it hard-codes it. Someone must define resilience, verify spending, and decide compliance. Creditors and donors will control those definitions, even if plans are nominally national. They can also claim climate virtue while shifting the adjustment burden onto countries least responsible for emissions.
[C1.6] The Seychelles and Belize swaps are cited, but they were small, bespoke, voluntary transactions with favorable circumstances—not evidence that automatic, large-scale contingency works under distress.
[C1.7] The better route is unconditional debt relief plus separate, grant-based climate finance, catastrophe clauses, and insurance. That supports resilience without holding solvency hostage. The proposition makes relief slower, more conditional, and less equitable. Oppose.
