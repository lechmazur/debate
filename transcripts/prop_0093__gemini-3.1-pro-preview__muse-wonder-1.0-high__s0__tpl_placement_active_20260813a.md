[P1.1] Curb space is a city’s most valuable, finite physical asset. For decades, we have squandered it by allowing private individuals to store empty, two-ton metal boxes on public land, often for free. This is a massive spatial subsidy that chokes urban mobility. Cities must urgently reallocate this space toward buses, deliveries, and bike lanes to maximize efficiency, safety, and equity.
[P1.2] First, consider basic geometric efficiency. A single parked car occupies about 150 square feet of prime real estate while sitting completely idle for the vast majority of the day. In that same space, a dedicated bus lane can move thousands of commuters per hour. A protected bike lane can safely process a continuous stream of active travelers.
[P1.3] When we prioritize stationary private cars over moving people, we mandate congestion.
[P1.4] Furthermore, failing to reserve space for logistics paralyses the entire street network. Without dedicated loading zones, delivery drivers are forced to double-park. This creates a dangerous domino effect: double-parked trucks block a live lane of traffic, causing immediate gridlock, delaying public transit, and forcing cyclists to swerve blindly into the flow of heavy vehicles. Dedicating curb space to active deliveries keeps the rest of the street functional and significantly reduces fatal traffic conflicts.
[P1.5] Second, this is a matter of profound equity. On-street parking disproportionately benefits a subset of residents who own private vehicles, while actively penalizing lower-income residents who are more likely to rely on public transit. When a bus full of fifty people is stuck in traffic because the curb lane is dedicated to parked cars, the city is prioritizing the convenience of a few absent car owners over the time, wages, and mobility of dozens of transit riders.
[P1.6] We cannot magically widen our city streets; we must use the space we have smarter. Protecting on-street parking artificially limits a city's capacity to function. Reallocating the curb to transit, logistics, and active mobility creates safer streets, faster commutes, and a more dynamic urban economy. It is time to treat the curb as a vital artery, not a parking lot.
