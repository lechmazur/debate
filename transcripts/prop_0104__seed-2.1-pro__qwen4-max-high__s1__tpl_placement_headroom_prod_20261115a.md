[P1.1] Roughly three out of every four guns used by youth to die by suicide, carry out school shootings, or cause unintentional fatal shootings and disabling injuries come from a home where the weapon was left unlocked, loaded, and accessible to a minor. [P1.2] The impact of those rules is not speculative: when secure storage is the default, curious 8-year-olds cannot pull a loaded handgun from a nightstand to show a friend, impulsive teens navigating a first breakup or acute mental health crisis cannot grab a family gun in the 10-minute window where suicidal urge feels overwhelming, and kids planning attacks on classmates cannot take a parent’s rifle from an unlocked closet without raising alarms. The supposed burdens on lawful ownership are trivial by comparison.
[P1.3] These rules do not ban any firearm, do not require surrender of weapons, and do not prevent trained adults from accessing secured guns quickly for self-defense, even as they eliminate the far more common risk of a child reaching that same gun first. [P1.4] There is no legitimate ownership interest so weighty that it justifies leaving weapons accessible to children when the cost of securing them is so low.
