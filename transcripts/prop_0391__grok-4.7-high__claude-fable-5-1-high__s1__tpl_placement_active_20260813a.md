[C1.2] At that scale, "aggressive" cannot mean a careful human weighing context. It means keyword filters, behavioral scoring, and report thresholds that trigger penalties automatically. Those systems are good at catching detectable words and terrible at catching what PRO actually named: the coordinated pile-on, the griefer who follows one player across matches without ever typing a flagged term. So the dial moves and the outcomes invert.
[C1.3] A squad of friends trading insults they have used for years eats a chat ban; the patient stalker is untouched because he never tripped a filter.
[C1.4] Worse, aggressive enforcement hands harassers a new weapon. Once a handful of reports reliably produce a suspension, mass-reporting becomes the harassment. A visible woman or a streamer with a hostile audience is precisely the person who gets brigaded off the ladder by the system PRO says protects her. Low-cost, predictable harm, in PRO's own words, now delivered by the moderation team.
[C1.5] Meanwhile the cheap, proven tools sit underused: default mute for strangers, per-player blocks that persist across queues, opt-in voice, avoid-this-player lists that shape matchmaking, private lobbies and community servers with their own norms. These give the target control instantly, without a queue, and without asking a machine to judge whether "bad idea, idiot" was banter or abuse.
[C1.6] That is why the culture complaint deserves more than a sneer. The players saying enforcement kills spontaneity are describing what gets caught: regional slang, in-group joking, competitive heat. When the collateral damage is ordinary speech and the worst actors reroll on alt accounts, "more aggressive" is not more protection. It is more false positives with a virtue label.
[C1.7] Design for victims. Do not just raise the hammer.
