[C1.1] PRO’s core error is treating safety as a single gate, after which all foodstuffs become interchangeable. Food law is not a safety checkbox; it is an architecture matched to how a product is made. Conventional meat rules encode an agricultural supply chain—animal identification, veterinary oversight, slaughter inspection, carcass-by-carcass tracking. Cultivated meat is an industrial bioprocess: cell banks, bioreactors, growth media, scaffolds, downstream purification.
[C1.2] Its failure modes are pharmaceutical and chemical, not agricultural. Same basic terms therefore is not equality; it is a category error that leaves regulators inspecting the wrong things.
[C1.3] The margarine tale proves this, not the opposite. What was wrong with margarine regulation was punitive taxation and color bans. What was right was treating it as what it was: a distinct industrial substitute for a natural agricultural product, requiring its own standards and clear labels. PRO wants to skip the lesson and insist that once an industrial replica is deemed non-toxic, it must inherit the entire legal identity of the original.
[C1.4] That is not principled; it is lazy.
[C1.5] Then there is deception. “Meat” is not a neutral descriptor; it is a legal and biological category tied to animal husbandry. Same basic terms would let cultivated meat default into that category, blurring the line between animal and vat. PRO gestures at truthful labeling but cannot deliver it under the motion: if the terms are the same, the default name is the same. A consumer who wants to know what they are buying is entitled to a regime that distinguishes novel manufacturing from traditional farming, not one that pretends the distinction does not exist.
[C1.6] Finally, novelty carries residual uncertainty. Safety standards are written for known risks. Long-term allergenicity of novel proteins, instability of immortalized cell lines, and pharmaceutical-grade inputs are not yet fully mapped. Equal terms remove the precautionary margin that genuinely new food platforms warrant.
[C1.7] Governments should regulate cultivated meat—stringently, under its own framework—not pretend it is the same thing as conventional meat.
