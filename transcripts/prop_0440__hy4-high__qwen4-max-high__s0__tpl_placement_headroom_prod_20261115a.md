[P1.1] The question is not whether police matter; US cities need investigation, emergency response, and lawful capacity after a shooting. The question is what a marginal dollar buys. Expanding staffing buys an expensive, permanent input. Focused deterrence and violence interruption buy a targeted break in the mechanism that produces shootings.
[P1.2] Cost-effectiveness means shootings avoided per dollar, including collateral costs.
[P1.3] Gun violence is concentrated in a small number of blocks, disputes, groups, and retaliation cycles. A staffing increase is spread across shifts, districts, calls, reports, and overtime; even when it helps, much of the purchase is work only loosely tied to the next shooting. Focused deterrence starts from the opposite premise: identify the few people driving risk, state that a specific violent act will bring a swift response, offer immediate practical help, and reserve enforcement for the narrow set that continues.
[P1.4] Violence interruption works earlier, using credible messengers to cool a dispute or stop retaliation before a gun is carried. Small teams, flexible hours, and modest service dollars are cheaper than a new payroll tier.
[P1.5] That arithmetic is brutal for a staffing-only answer. An added officer means salary, benefits, training, supervision, equipment, pension obligations, and recurring overtime, year after year. That money competes with mediation, victim services, relocation, hospital-based outreach, and credible follow-up. Broad hiring also has diminishing returns: more patrol can increase stops and arrests without improving witness cooperation, and aggressive enforcement in the same neighborhoods can make the next investigation harder.
[P1.6] CON can cite response times, clearance, and general deterrence; those are real, but diffuse and expensive when bought through across-the-board staffing. Keep the police department, but stop making headcount the default: dollars aimed at the small network doing the shooting, and at the moment before retaliation, prevent more gun violence per dollar than adding officers whose time is dispersed.
