[P1.1] Once a university cuts athletes a check from sports revenue, the amateur fiction is over. The question becomes whether that economic relationship gets governed by a real framework or by a patchwork of state laws, conference bylaws, and compliance memos that nobody fully controls.
[P1.2] Ad hoc NIL and compliance systems fail on three counts. First, they're inconsistent: an athlete's compensation and protections vary by state, conference, and individual school policy. Second, they're unaccountable: compliance offices answer to the institution, not to athletes. Third, they're unstable: rules shift with each lawsuit, each NCAA panic, each legislative session.
[P1.3] Athletes have no seat at the table when the ground moves beneath them.
[P1.4] Formal collective bargaining solves what patchwork cannot. A union contract is enforceable, predictable, and bilateral. Athletes gain a defined voice in the terms of their labor—compensation, health coverage, transfer rules, practice hours. Schools gain labor peace: a single agreement replaces dozens of conflicting state regimes and insulates the program from constant regulatory whiplash.
[P1.5] Both sides get a grievance process instead of athletes' only recourse being litigation or public pressure.
[P1.6] This isn't speculation. Every major American league where revenue is shared between institutions and labor moved toward collective bargaining because ad hoc arrangements collapsed under their own weight. The NFL, NBA, and MLB all tried voluntarism first. All abandoned it.
[P1.7] College sports is now running the same experiment, slower and messier, with teenagers negotiating against billion-dollar athletic departments through Instagram agents and booster collectives.
[P1.8] The proposition is narrow: at schools that directly share revenue, athletes should have the same representation standard the rest of the economy uses. The alternative isn't amateurism preserved. It's professionalism without the protections—and the imbalance that follows is exactly what collective bargaining exists to correct.
