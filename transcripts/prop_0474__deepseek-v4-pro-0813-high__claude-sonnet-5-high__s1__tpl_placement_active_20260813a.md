[P1.1] A botnet is not spontaneous. It depends on durable infrastructure: command-and-control servers, bulletproof hosting, payment relays, and transit providers that ignore abuse notices. A state that knowingly tolerates that infrastructure year after year is not simply failing to police crime. It is providing the launching pad for a weapon that can be pointed at any country.
[P1.2] That is why such states should face sanctions comparable to those used against serious cyber-aggressors.
[P1.3] First, the harm is equivalent. Ransomware and DDoS operations built on tolerated botnets have disrupted hospitals, pipelines, schools, banks, and public services. To the victim, it does not matter whether the attack came from a state agency or a criminal proxy operating from a state-protected haven. The harm is measured in frozen systems, extorted money, and endangered patients, not in the attacker’s formal employer.
[P1.4] Second, persistence proves state policy. Occasional failure may be incapacity, but repeated public evidence, continued abuse from the same infrastructure, no meaningful prosecution, and refusal to cooperate on takedowns show tolerance as a choice. This is often cheaper and more deniable than launching state malware directly. A state can let criminals run the botnet, enjoy the disruption and illicit revenue, and claim deniability.
[P1.5] That is outsourced aggression, and it should not receive a lighter penalty.
[P1.6] Third, sanctions reach the enabler. Arrests of operators are rare and slow; diplomatic notes are easily ignored. Targeted financial restrictions, asset freezes, export controls, and travel bans raise the cost of hosting impunity. If we sanction direct cyber attackers for destructive operations, it is inconsistent to spare the state that rents them the weapon’s brain.
[P1.7] The alternative is a world where the same serious cyber harm escapes consequences simply because a government used a criminal middleman. That would leave the norm against botnets unenforceable.

[C1.1] Proportionality is not a technicality — it is the whole point of a sanctions regime, and PRO's opening asks the world to abandon it.
[C1.2] First, "the harm is equivalent" does the real work in this case, and it's a category error. Harm-to-victim tells us how bad an event is, not who authored it. A hospital ransomware outage is terrible whether caused by a state intelligence unit or a criminal renting server space in a country with a weak cybercrime unit — but sanctions regimes exist to change behavior of the responsible actor, and those two actors respond to entirely different levers. Treat them identically and you lose the ability to target the actual decision-maker.
[C1.3] Second, "knowingly tolerate" is doing enormous, unverifiable work. Distinguishing deliberate state complicity from mere incapacity requires exactly the kind of granular intelligence that is hardest to get and easiest to fake or spin. Bulletproof hosting thrives precisely in places with underfunded prosecutors, corrupt local police, and overwhelmed courts — not necessarily in places where the presidential palace is pulling strings. PRO's own "persistence proves policy" standard would sanction Moldova-style institutional failure exactly as hard as deliberate Kremlin-style outsourcing.
[C1.4] That's not deterrence, that's collective punishment of the poor and dysfunctional.
[C1.5] Third, collapsing the distinction wrecks the deterrence gradient that makes sanctions work at all. If a country that merely fails to police a criminal ring receives the same penalty as one that launches NotPetya, there is no marginal reason for a state on the fence to avoid direct action — the ceiling punishment is already reached. Graduated tools — targeted financial notices, capacity-building conditioned cooperation, incremental designations — preserve headroom to escalate against real aggressors. PRO's flattened penalty ladder removes that headroom.
[C1.6] Finally, harsh, comparable sanctions destroy the one thing that actually shuts down botnets: cooperation on takedowns. A government facing sanctions regardless of what it does next has no incentive to assist an investigation tomorrow. PRO's remedy guarantees the disease it claims to cure.
