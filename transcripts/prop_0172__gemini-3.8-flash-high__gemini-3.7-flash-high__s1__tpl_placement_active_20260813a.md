[P1.2] Animal research oversight—anchored by institutional care committees and the principles of reduction, refinement, and scientific justification—does not require proof of existential self-awareness. [P1.4] Science must respect the precautionary principle. Triggering that framework the moment an organoid demonstrates learning ensures that innovation proceeds with rigor, accountability, and essential moral safeguards.

[C1.2] First, learning is not sentience. Forcing in vitro tissue cultures into animal protocols—which mandate veterinary oversight, caging audits, and humane endpoints designed for whole organisms—creates an absurd circularity where the replacement is regulated as the animal itself.
[C1.4] Third, rejecting the animal framework does not create an ethical void. Established biomedical frameworks, including stem-cell governance, biosafety protocols, and human tissue oversight, already regulate cellular research, genetic modification, and developmental limits without misclassifying cell clusters as sentient animals.
[C1.5] Subjective suffering, not mere electrophysiological responsiveness, is the legitimate threshold for animal protections. Until an entity possesses the integrated biological substrate for felt experience, shoehorning proof-of-concept organoids into animal welfare frameworks stifles life-saving neuroscience while protecting a capacity for suffering that simply does not exist.
