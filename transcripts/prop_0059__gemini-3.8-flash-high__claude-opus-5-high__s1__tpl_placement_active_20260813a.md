[P1.1] The dominant dating-app model is fundamentally misaligned with human connection. It operates not as a matchmaker, but as an attention-extraction engine. Platforms monetize continuous searching, not successful pairing. Every time two users fall in love and delete the app, the platform loses active users and potential paying subscribers.
[P1.2] Consequently, modern dating apps are engineered around retention and churn, actively undermining relationship formation for the vast majority of people.
[P1.3] First, the business model relies on a predatory conflict of interest. Platforms utilize variable-ratio reinforcement—the same psychological mechanism that powers slot machines. Algorithms artificially throttle visibility, dangle phantom notifications, and monetize basic functionality. Authentic connection requires vulnerability, patience, and closure; the dating-app model demands repetitive, compulsive consumption.
[P1.4] Second, the swipe interface commodifies human beings and induces choice paralysis. Reducing complex people to superficial, disposable cards cheapens commitment. When users are fed the illusion of an infinite catalog of potential partners, real-world compromise vanishes. Everyday human flaws become intolerable when an idealized alternative is supposedly just one swipe away.
[P1.5] This dynamic fosters chronic indecision, ghosting, and a hyper-critical consumer mindset that prevents relationships from ever taking root.
[P1.6] Third, the market dynamics yield systemic burnout. The dominant model produces severe asymmetry: most men face an algorithmic desert that breeds isolation and rejection fatigue, while most women are inundated with overwhelming volumes of low-effort, low-intent interactions. Rather than facilitating partnership, the experience exhausts both sides, eroding social trust and dating morale.
[P1.7] Occasional success stories do not validate a broken paradigm. We must judge the system by the median user experience, not the lucky outliers. For most users, the dominant dating-app model substitutes organic courtship with a gamified treadmill that makes forming a durable relationship significantly harder, more cynical, and unequivocally worse.
