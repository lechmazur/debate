[P1.1] A three-dollar dress crosses the border in a small parcel, clears no meaningful inspection, carries no traceable manufacturer, and lands on a child's doorstep. That is not a glitch in the ultra-cheap marketplace model—it is the model. These platforms built their advantage on two gaps: de minimis shipping rules that let millions of small parcels bypass the scrutiny container cargo faces, and supply chains so fragmented that no one can say who made the product or under what conditions. This motion simply closes those gaps.
[P1.2] Start with safety. Domestic retailers must meet standards on flammability, lead content, choking hazards, and chemical dyes. A marketplace shipping direct from an anonymous factory meets almost none of that, because regulators cannot inspect parcels at this volume and platforms claim they are mere intermediaries. European safety alerts have repeatedly flagged dangerous children's clothing, jewelry with heavy metals, and electronics sold through exactly these channels.
[P1.3] The risk is structural: when a seller can dissolve and re-register overnight, accountability is impossible by design. Stronger obligations—verifiable seller identity, compliance documentation before listing, platform liability—are the only tools that reach this model.
[P1.4] Second, labor. A blouse cannot cost four dollars unless someone downstream absorbed the difference. Subcontracting layers and opaque sourcing are precisely how forced-labor cotton and sweatshop wages get laundered into "great deals." Transparency obligations—disclosing factories, mapping supply chains—cost honest firms little.
[P1.5] They only punish those whose margins depend on concealment.
[P1.6] Now the conceded cost: yes, this may slow ultra-cheap shopping and raise prices modestly. But cheapness achieved by billing the true cost to poisoned workers and unsafe children is not cheap—it is just invoiced elsewhere. We never accepted "but it's affordable" as a defense of lead in toys from domestic stores. Poverty should not mean the poor get the toxic shirt.
[P1.7] Equal safety rules for equal products, regardless of shipping lane, is the baseline this motion defends.
