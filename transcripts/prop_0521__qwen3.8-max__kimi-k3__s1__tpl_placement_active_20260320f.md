[P1.1] Portable training accounts turn employer spending from a handcuff into sector infrastructure. Under the status quo, training dollars and benefits often vanish when a worker leaves, even if the skills remain useful across the same industry. That creates lock-in, not loyalty. A nurse may avoid moving to a hospital that better fits her specialty because continuing-education funds reset to zero.
[P1.2] A construction electrician may decline a safer employer because his funded certification record is tied to the old firm. The worker loses bargaining power; the sector loses better matches.
[P1.3] The core principle is simple: employer-funded training is deferred compensation earned by labor, not a gift conditioned on captivity. Workers pay for it indirectly through lower starting wages, effort, and time spent training. If the account travels with them within the sector, it functions like a pension or health benefit that respects modern careers. Restricting portability to the same sector preserves the legitimate employer interest: the funds remain tied to industry-relevant credentials and cannot become a general subsidy for quitting into an unrelated field.
[P1.4] This also fixes the training market’s biggest failure. Employers underinvest in transferable skills because they fear competitors will capture the return. Portable sector accounts make that investment safer and more productive: the worker can use the credential, the sector gets a deeper labor pool, and firms compete on productivity rather than on trapping benefits. If a company wants retention, it should offer better wages and conditions, not confiscate the skills a worker has already earned.
[P1.5] Portability does not force firms to subsidize exit from the sector; it keeps skills available to the sector’s employers, including the original firm if it stays competitive.
[P1.6] The choice is between a system that treats training as firm property and one that treats it as earned worker capital. Because mobility, fairness, and sector-wide skill formation all point the same way, the proposition should pass.
