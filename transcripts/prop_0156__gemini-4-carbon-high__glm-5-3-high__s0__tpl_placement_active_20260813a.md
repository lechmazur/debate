[P1.3] Codifying suspicion is not solidarity; it is enforcement.

[P1.5] Millions of Europeans absorbed a corrosive lesson: elections can change governments, yet leave citizens powerless to change policy.
[P1.6] Simultaneously, the response poisoned horizontal trust between European publics. Creditor voters resented bankrolling neighbors portrayed as rule-breakers, fueling anti-bailout parties like Alternative for Germany. Debtor publics came to view Brussels and Berlin as bailiffs rather than partners, driving anti-establishment and Euroskeptic surges across Greece, Spain, and Italy.
