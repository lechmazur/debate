[P1.1] The question is not whether religion may appear in public schools. It is what the school owes the child who says no. That is where this motion wins.
[P1.2] "Optional" on paper is not voluntary in fact. If the alternative to religious instruction is sitting in a corridor, an unsupervised study hall, or a room where you are the only kid who left, dissent carries a social and academic price. Children pay that price quietly and parents never see it. That is conformity under color of choice.
[P1.3] Requiring a comparable secular alternative — same time block, same facilities, comparable seriousness, whether ethics, philosophy, world literature, or supported study with real help available — removes the penalty for saying no. Only then is the choice symmetrical. Only then does "optional" mean anything.
[P1.4] The secular alternative is not a poison pill aimed at religion. It is what protects the minority family: the Muslim child in a majority-Catholic town, the child of non-believers, the kid whose parents dissent from their neighbors' congregation. Without a dignified alternative, those children are marked as the ones who left. With one, they keep equal standing in the school day.
[P1.5] That is the difference between a pluralist school and a sectarian one wearing a volunteer sign.
[P1.6] "Clearly voluntary" is the enforcement teeth, and it is deliberately strict: informed parental permission, no grades, credit, or teacher favor tied to participation, no authority figure recruiting, annual re-affirmation, transparent oversight. Schools hold power over children, so ambiguity resolves against the program. The burden sits with the provider to prove freedom.
[P1.7] Now compare the alternatives CON must defend. A flat ban tells religious families their deepest commitments are unfit for the building — that is not neutrality, it is hostility dressed as fairness, and many democracies run in-school religious education with opt-outs and parallel options without the sky falling. Unconditional permission is worse: it invites proselytism, sectarian capture, and stigma for refusers. PRO is the only design that honors both liberty and conscience.
