[P1.1] The real question is whether one rightsholder should hold a veto over an entire technology. The motion gives a clear answer: no free ride, but no blockade either. A compulsory license turns millions of impossible one-to-one negotiations into a predictable payment stream. That is not exotic; copyright already uses collective and compulsory mechanisms where valuable uses are aggregate and transaction costs are prohibitive—mechanical licensing for songs, broadcast royalties, copying levies.
[P1.2] Training generative models on copyrighted works is that problem at industrial scale.
[P1.3] Voluntary clearance sounds fair until you picture how it works. A model needs broad, diverse material. Any single publisher, label, or studio can demand an outsized price, refuse entirely, or simply be impossible to locate. The result is not a thriving market; it is concentration.
[P1.4] Only the largest firms can afford catalog-wide deals, while smaller developers and independent creators are shut out. Or everyone scrapes in the dark and creators get nothing. The compulsory scheme replaces that with mandatory payment, rate-setting, audit, and distribution to rightsholders.

[P1.6] The motion regulates the input; it does not erase authorship or authorize copying the work into the market.
[P1.7] The objection will be that property includes the right to say no. But copyright is already a statutory bundle balanced against public interests, and refusal rights are least defensible when they cannot produce bargaining—only holdout and abandonment. If the choice is between a system that pays creators broadly and one that rewards only the biggest dealmakers, the compulsory license is the more pro-creator, pro-market rule. Vote PRO.

Neither ingests a catalogue to learn its style, voice, structure, and appeal, then produces endless substitutes in the same market. Training is not distribution of a work.
[C1.3] It is extraction of its productive value. Some authors will accept that for a high price, some only with limits on outputs, attribution, or genres, and some not at all for artistic or ethical reasons. A flat compulsory payment treats all those choices as the same purchasable input.
[C1.4] The pricing problem is fatal. There is no sensible rate for “training.” Is a breakthrough novel worth the same per page as a stock photograph, a local news article, or a hit chorus? Value does not come from copying a file once; it comes from what the model learns across millions of works and later monetizes in ways no rate board can predict. Set the fee low and the scheme is a subsidy from creators to the richest technology and media firms.
[C1.5] Set it high and only those giants can pay, while the revenue is spread so thinly through collecting societies, publishers, labels, and studios that individual creators see symbolic checks.
[C1.6] Voluntary licensing is not fantasy; catalog deals, collective licenses, and opt-out tools are already emerging precisely because permission has value. A compulsory ceiling would strangle that market before it matures, converting every negotiation into “take the statutory rate.” Transaction costs can be eased with voluntary collectives and standardized terms without abolishing consent.
[C1.7] Paying into a fund is not fairness if the price, the terms, and the right to compete against you were never yours to set.

[C2.5] The older compulsory licenses do not bridge that gap. A cover recording or broadcast retransmission delivers an identifiable work under narrow, auditable conditions. [C2.6] Here refusal is not symbolic rent-seeking; it is how creators differentiate terms—higher pay for higher-risk uses, permission for some purposes, refusal where substitution is direct.
[C2.7] Category rates do not cure underpayment. Dividing books from music from images still treats a defining catalogue and disposable filler as near-equivalents. Allocation by volume rewards the largest publishers, labels, and studios administering catalogues, not the creators whose work drives value. Audit can count files ingested; it cannot value what was learned or the revenue that learning generated.
[C2.8] The choice is not payment versus nothing. It is an administered taking versus voluntary collective licensing that lowers transaction costs while preserving differentiated consent. Only the latter lets price and limits be discovered rather than imposed.
