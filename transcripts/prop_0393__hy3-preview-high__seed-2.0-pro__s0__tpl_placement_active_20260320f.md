Retrofitting a standard building after construction is wildly more expensive than installing reinforced walls, zero-step entrances, and adaptable bathrooms during the initial build. [P1.3] Second, we must move past the myth that accessibility only serves a small minority. Universal design benefits parents with strollers, delivery workers, older adults aging in place, and anyone recovering from injury. A slightly wider doorway or a no-step entry is not a "special" feature; it is a basic component of a modern, dignified society.
[P1.4] Finally, the cost increase is "modest" precisely because it is a drop in the bucket compared to the lifetime social and economic costs of exclusion. When people cannot access housing, they face isolation, institutionalization, or expensive medical complications. [P1.5] Choosing to save pennies today at the expense of decades of exclusion is a false economy that we cannot afford.
