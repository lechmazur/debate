[P1.1] The core question is whether a handful of platform owners should remain permanent gatekeepers over how billions of people acquire software and pay for it. They should not. For most consumer apps, app stores should be required to permit independent payment systems and sideloading.
[P1.2] Today, dominant stores control not only distribution but also the rules of payment. They can tax a wide range of digital commerce, block developers from telling users about cheaper options, and delay or reject apps for reasons that have little to do with security. A game studio, a newspaper, a tutoring app, or a dating service may have to surrender a large slice of revenue and accept unilateral terms just to reach users. That is not a neutral safety measure; it is market power.
[P1.3] Independent payments restore ordinary competition. Developers can use lower-cost processors, local payment methods, family billing, or direct subscription systems. Users can see real prices, and stores must earn their fees instead of collecting them by default. If a store offers better discovery, updates, or refund protection, it can still win business—but it cannot hold the checkout counter hostage.
[P1.4] Sideloading is the same principle for distribution. It lets users obtain apps from a developer’s website, an alternative marketplace, or an enterprise channel when the main store excludes them, overcharges them, or simply does not serve their needs. Desktop computing has long allowed this, and the world did not collapse. Mobile can do the same with targeted safeguards.
[P1.5] Security is important, but it does not require a monopoly. Stores can still scan apps, verify developers, require clear permissions, warn users about risky software, and revoke malicious certificates. The right model is layered protection, not a locked door with a tollbooth.
[P1.6] For most consumer apps, the issue is not whether phones can be safe; it is whether users and developers get a meaningful choice. Competition, not gatekeeper control, is the better foundation.
