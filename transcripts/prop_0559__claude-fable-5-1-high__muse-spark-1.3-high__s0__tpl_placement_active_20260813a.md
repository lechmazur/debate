[P1.1] Your friendships are not Facebook's property. Yet if you leave tomorrow, you lose them—not because the friendships end, but because the platform holds the only copy of the map. That is the core of this debate: whether a company may hold your relationships hostage as its business model, or whether the law should let you take them with you.
[P1.2] Start with the market failure. Social platforms do not win because they are best; they win because everyone is already there. Economists call this a network effect, but from the user's chair it is simply a switching cost so high that competition stops functioning. A better, safer, less manipulative service cannot win you over one by one, because you cannot go until everyone goes.
[P1.3] That is why platforms can degrade feeds, harvest data, and change terms with impunity. Exit is the discipline that markets depend on, and here exit has been engineered out.
[P1.4] We have fixed exactly this problem before. When regulators mandated telephone number portability, carriers insisted it was technically impossible and ruinously expensive. It worked, prices fell, and no one today imagines returning to a world where changing carriers means telling everyone a new number. Email runs on open protocols; you can switch providers and keep every contact.
[P1.5] Open banking rules forced banks to expose account data through standard interfaces, and a wave of new financial services followed. Interoperability is not utopian; it is the ordinary condition of most infrastructure we rely on.
[P1.6] The "legally required" part is essential, not optional. The social graph is the moat; no incumbent will drain its own moat voluntarily. GDPR-style portability already exists in principle, and what it yields is a zip file no other service can read. Only a mandate for interoperable, standardized transfer makes the right real.
[P1.7] Expect privacy objections. They cut the other way. Today, users desperate to migrate hand passwords to scrapers—the very pattern behind past data scandals. A regulated, consent-based export with standard interfaces is safer than the black-market alternative the status quo produces.
