[P1.1] Air travel is not the transport of an empty body; it is the transport of a person. Requiring airlines to honor a reasonable carry-on baseline in the base ticket price is essential to restore honest market competition and eliminate predatory drip pricing.
[P1.2] The fragmentation of baggage fees is not an expansion of consumer freedom; it is deliberate price obfuscation. Booking platforms rely on transparent comparison to drive efficient markets. When carriers strip away the carry-on, they bait travelers with artificially deflated base fares, hiding the real cost of transit until deep in the checkout flow or at the departure gate. Once a passenger arrives at the terminal, their leverage vanishes.
[P1.3] Airlines exploit this trapped position by imposing punitive gate fees that bear no relationship to any marginal handling cost.
[P1.4] This unbundled regime also creates systemic operational friction. Fragmented fee enforcement turns gate agents into revenue police, sparking contentious disputes, slowing down boarding, and causing cascading flight delays. Far from optimizing cabin space, nickel-and-diming incentivizes passengers to gamble on personal-item loopholes, creating boarding bottlenecks that degrade the entire travel system.
[P1.5] A mandatory carry-on baseline does not stifle airline business models. Carriers remain free to price and monetize genuine extras: checked luggage, seat selection, extra legroom, or secondary bags. What it ends is the deceptive practice of quoting a phantom price for a product that cannot realistically be used as advertised. Basic human transit requires carrying basic human necessities.
