[P1.1] Curb space is the most valuable real estate in any city, and we are giving it away for free to the vehicles that use it most aggressively. That is the heart of this debate. A delivery van double-parks because the curb is full and idling costs nothing. An Uber stops in a travel lane because pulling over carries no price and no penalty.
[P1.2] The result is the daily congestion, blocked bike lanes, and crawling buses that every urban resident recognizes. Free curb space is not generous; it is a subsidy for chaos.
[P1.3] My case rests on one principle: when a scarce resource is priced at zero, it gets overused and misallocated. We accept this everywhere else. We meter parking. We charge for electricity by the kilowatt-hour.
[P1.4] We don't hand out free water during a drought. The curb is no different. During the morning rush, that loading zone is worth a fortune; at 11pm it's nearly worthless. A flat price of zero ignores that completely.
[P1.5] Dynamic pricing simply makes the price track the value, so the space goes to whoever needs it most urgently.
[P1.6] Notice who benefits. A pharmacy delivery of medication will gladly pay for guaranteed curb access and a quick turnaround. [P1.7] And the gains compound. When delivery vans aren't double-parked, buses move and the thousands of people aboard them get home faster. When ride-hail vehicles use designated, priced pickup points, cyclists stop getting forced into traffic. The revenue can fund the very curb management and transit that everyone uses.
[P1.8] The question is not whether the curb should be managed. It already is, badly, by a first-come queue that rewards the most disruptive behavior. The only real choice is between smart prices and dumb gridlock. I'll defend smart prices.
