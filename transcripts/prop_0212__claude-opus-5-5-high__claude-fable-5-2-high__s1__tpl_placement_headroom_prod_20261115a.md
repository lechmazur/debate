[P1.1] The motion carries its own safeguard: archives open only "once immediate security concerns have passed." That condition strips CON of its strongest card. The question left is simple: when a state no longer needs secrecy to protect anyone, whom does continued secrecy actually protect? Usually the institution that did the harm, and never the people it was done to.
[P1.2] First, families have a claim no state can morally override. A mother whose son vanished into a police station decades ago is not asking for a policy seminar. She wants to know whether he was shot, where he is buried, and who signed the order. In Spain, Argentina, and across the former Soviet bloc, relatives have spent lifetimes chasing exactly these answers.
[P1.3] Often the paper trail exists, sitting in a ministry basement. Withholding it extends the original cruelty by bureaucratic means.
[P1.4] Second, closed archives distort history in a predictable direction. Britain's so-called migrated archives, colonial files on Kenya and elsewhere, were kept from view for decades and surfaced largely because Mau Mau veterans went to court. What emerged complicated the comfortable story of orderly decolonisation. That is the pattern: secrecy does not preserve a neutral record; it preserves the perpetrator's version.
[P1.5] Historians cannot correct what they cannot read.
[P1.6] Third, openness has been tested and it works. When reunified Germany let citizens read their own Stasi files, critics predicted vendettas and social breakdown. Instead, millions of people learned who had informed on them, and Germany's democracy grew sturdier, not weaker. Wounds hurt more when hidden than when named.
[P1.7] Finally, "broader access" does not mean dumping every file online. Tiered access, redaction of third-party details, and priority for victims and their descendants answer the privacy objection without surrendering the principle.
[P1.8] So the burden is on CON: show a concrete harm from opening files after the danger has passed that outweighs a family's right to bury its dead.
