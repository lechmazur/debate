[P1.1] Coal is the single largest source of energy-related carbon emissions on the planet, and unlike aviation fuel or steelmaking, we already know how to replace it. Solar and wind are now the cheapest new electricity in most of the world; storage costs keep falling. The problem is not technology or economics. It is a collective action failure — and this motion is the only serious answer to it.
[P1.2] Here is the core argument: voluntary climate pledges without enforcement have been tried for three decades, and they fail predictably. Every country has an incentive to let others decarbonize first while it burns cheap coal a little longer. Paris-style promises get made, then quietly missed, because missing them costs nothing. Enforceable trade and finance penalties change the payoff structure.
[P1.3] When noncompliance means carbon border tariffs on your exports or restricted access to development finance, coal stops being the cheap option even in narrow national accounting. That is how you convert a hopeful pledge into a credible commitment — the same logic the WTO uses for trade rules and the FATF uses for money laundering. Enforcement is not radical; it is how every functioning international regime works.
[P1.4] Notice how carefully the motion is drawn. "Unabated" means countries retaining coal with genuine carbon capture comply. 2040 gives fifteen-plus years — roughly the natural retirement age of much of the existing fleet — so this largely means not building new coal and orderly closures, not sudden blackouts. Penalties can and should be paired with transition finance for coal-dependent developing economies; indeed, the credible threat of penalties is precisely what unlocks that finance, because lenders and Just Energy Transition partners know the shift will actually happen.
[P1.5] So the clash is this: either you believe polite non-binding targets will suddenly start working after thirty years of missing them, or you accept that the world's most replaceable, most lethal fuel needs a deadline with teeth. Everything else is delay dressed up as pragmatism.

[C1.4] The second makes the regime non-global on day one.
[C1.5] Enforcement cannot be made fair. Border tariffs and withheld finance hit export-dependent, capital-scarce economies hardest. China can absorb or retaliate. India can wait out a club it never joined.
