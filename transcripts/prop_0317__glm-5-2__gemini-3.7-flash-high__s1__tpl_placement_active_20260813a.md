[P1.1] The central question is whether the resale market should override the intentional pricing decisions of artists and event organizers. When an artist prices a ticket at $60, that reflects a deliberate choice—to make the show accessible to actual fans, to build a long-term audience, to honor the community that built their career. Uncapped resale doesn't correct a market failure; it creates one, by transferring the value of that pricing decision from fans and artists to intermediaries who contribute nothing to the event.
[P1.2] Major platforms don't just tolerate this transfer—they profit from it. The largest primary ticketing platform also operates one of the largest resale marketplaces, taking a percentage cut on both sides of every transaction. Every dollar added to a resale price is a dollar from which the platform extracts its fee. The incentive structure is broken: the party controlling access to tickets also profits when those tickets become unaffordable at face value.
[P1.3] Requiring a markup cap or a face-value resale channel directly corrects this conflict.
[P1.4] The harm is concrete and visible. Dedicated fans who cannot pay $400 for a $60 ticket are replaced by whoever has the most disposable income. Professional scalpers and bot operators, who never intend to attend, buy inventory precisely because the uncapped upside rewards hoarding. The audience that shows up isn't the audience the artist chose—it's the audience that arbitrage selected.
[P1.5] The motion's "or" matters. Platforms may cap markups or provide face-value resale channels. That flexibility respects different business models while preserving a core principle: tickets should serve attendees, not speculators. Face-value resale tools, verified fan systems, and paperless ticketing already exist and work.
[P1.6] The proposal scales proven mechanisms rather than inventing untested mandates.
[P1.7] No one is barring fans from selling tickets they genuinely cannot use. The question is whether resale infrastructure should be optimized for extraction or access. Right now it serves extraction. A cap or face-value channel changes that.
