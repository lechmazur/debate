[P1.1] Anti-encampment laws should be judged by the practical alternative they make possible, not by whether a city can point to a shelter somewhere on paper. The operative distinction is enforcement without options versus enforcement that moves people into a functioning system. If police can dismantle an encampment while offering no usable place to sleep, store belongings, obtain sanitation or health services, and remain without immediate return to the same site, the law is not solving homelessness; it is making it less visible.
[P1.2] Genuinely available shelter must be reachable in ordinary circumstances, open when people need it, safe, and sufficient for those directed there. Storage matters because bulky possessions are often the only capital a person owns; confiscating them can erase work, medication, and documents needed to secure housing. Services must include not merely referrals but actual pathways to mental-health care, addiction treatment, sanitation, and employment. A phone number or voucher that cannot be redeemed does not constitute an alternative.
[P1.3] This standard also applies to storage and to long-term stabilization: temporary containers should not become the destination, and cities should provide lawful, secure storage, coordinated case management, and a credible route into permanent housing. Otherwise enforcement merely rotates the same population among sidewalks, shelters, and parking lots. The question is not whether homelessness must remain unregulated; it is whether regulation is justified when the city has first created a realistic, usable alternative.
[P1.4] That standard also prevents enforcement from exporting the problem. Without alternatives, a campaign can worsen sanitation and safety while moving the same population elsewhere. With them, targeted rules can address obstruction, dangerous waste, or disorder without treating every unhoused person as a criminal. Cities must demonstrate actual capacity, accessibility, and sustained operation.
[P1.5] If options are full, delayed, conditional on surrendering belongings, or hours away, enforcement is displacement.
