[P1.2] Start with what has already happened. The barrier that killed this field in the 1990s was hyperacute rejection, driven by sugar antigens on pig cells that human antibodies attack within minutes. Gene editing removed those antigens and inserted human regulatory genes. [P1.3] Formal FDA-cleared clinical trials of pig kidneys have begun. Ten years ago this was science fiction; three years ago it was compassionate use; today it is a trial protocol. Extrapolate that curve another fifteen years and "routine" is the conservative forecast.

[P1.5] So why accelerate regulation now? Because the comparator matters. A dialysis patient with no living donor and a decade-long wait is not choosing between a pig kidney and a perfect human one. They are choosing between a pig kidney and progressive death.
[P1.6] We accepted exactly this logic for HIV drugs and cancer therapies: faster approval, tighter post-market monitoring, patients who are actually dying in the risk calculation. Acceleration is not deregulation—it is matching scrutiny to the stakes.
[P1.7] Every year of delay is roughly six thousand American funerals. That is the cost CON must defend.
