[P1.1] When you open a food‑delivery app, the first three slots you see are almost always paid placements. [P1.2] First, consumer welfare collapses when price‑sensitive options are buried. Users must scroll past sponsored banners, spend extra minutes searching, and often settle for higher‑priced meals because the cheaper alternatives are invisible. [P1.3] Second, the competitive landscape suffocates independent restaurants. Small operators typically cannot match the marketing budgets of chain restaurants that can purchase premium slots. [P1.4] Third, platforms have a duty of transparency. [P1.5] The opposition will claim that sponsored listings fund platform development and help restaurants gain exposure. [P1.6] In short, privileging sponsors to the point where cheaper nearby independents become hard to find harms consumers, starves communities, and undermines the very function of a food‑delivery app. The platform should be a bridge, not a gatekeeper that sells the bridge.
