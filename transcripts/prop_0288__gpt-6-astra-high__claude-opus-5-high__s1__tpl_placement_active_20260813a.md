[P1.1] An AI flag is a reason to investigate, not a license to deprive. Public agencies should never suspend benefits, visas, or fraud claims solely on that flag without meaningful human review. The crucial distinction is between using software to direct attention and allowing software’s suspicion, by itself, to interrupt someone’s rights, income, or legal status.
[P1.2] A risk model identifies patterns associated with suspected wrongdoing. That is not the same as establishing wrongdoing in this person’s case. An unusual address, inconsistent records, or an unfamiliar employment pattern might indicate fraud—or a recent move, an agency’s clerical error, or perfectly lawful work. Even a useful model will produce false positives.
[P1.3] Its overall accuracy cannot tell us whether the individual facing suspension is one of them.
[P1.4] The consequences are not merely administrative. A benefits interruption can mean missed rent or medication. A visa suspension can disrupt work and family life. Restoring someone’s position later does not necessarily repair those losses.
[P1.5] Calling a suspension “temporary” does not make its effects temporary. And requiring innocent people to untangle an automated mistake after losing essential support puts the burden precisely where resources are thinnest.
[P1.6] Meaningful review need not be a lengthy hearing. It means an accountable person examines the relevant information, considers plausible errors or context, and has real authority to reject the recommendation. A rubber stamp is not review. Humans also make mistakes, but an independent check can catch errors that an automated pipeline would otherwise reproduce unchecked.
[P1.7] The safeguard is scrutiny, not faith in human infallibility.
[P1.8] The efficiency objection therefore presents a false choice. Agencies can automate screening, prioritize urgent cases, request verification, and review high-risk cases rapidly. They can suspend when reviewed evidence warrants it. What they cannot legitimately do is substitute a score for that judgment.
[P1.9] If government chooses to deploy suspicion at scale, it must fund accountability at scale—not finance its convenience through innocent people’s deprivation.
