[P1.1] Today, major social media platforms function as the essential infrastructure of modern public life. They are where independent journalists break news, small businesses reach customers, and citizens participate in democratic discourse. When a platform abruptly bans an account, it is not merely enforcing a private dress code; it is imposing a form of digital exile that can destroy livelihoods and silence vital speech overnight. Leaving this immense gatekeeping power to opaque, automated algorithms and unaccountable corporate fiat is untenable.
[P1.2] Legally mandating basic due-process protections—notice, specific reasons, and an opportunity for appeal before suspension or banning—is a necessary correction. Currently, millions of users are subjected to arbitrary moderation errors. Algorithmic sweeps routinely misinterpret satire, censor reporting on human rights abuses, or flag benign political speech, instantly terminating accounts with zero explanation and no human recourse. A requirement to provide advance notice and cite the specific community standard violated forces platforms to act deliberatively rather than recklessly.
[P1.3] Procedural fairness protects users from both ideological discrimination and commercial whims. When platforms must show their work and hear an appeal, they cannot quietly purge disfavored viewpoints or hide systemic algorithmic failures behind generic "terms of service" boilerplate.
[P1.4] Opponents will claim this burden paralyzes platform security and content moderation. But due process does not mean immunity for bad actors; it establishes basic institutional governance. Legal frameworks routinely pair standard procedural guarantees with narrow, exigent exceptions for imminent physical harm or severe criminal activity. What due process eliminates is not safety, but unaccountable, black-box negligence.
[P1.5] If we accept that these platforms wield unprecedented power over public discourse and economic survival, we cannot permit them to operate as arbitrary autocracies. Requiring notice, reasons, and a fair appeal before severing a user's digital existence is the bare minimum standard for accountability in the modern public square.
