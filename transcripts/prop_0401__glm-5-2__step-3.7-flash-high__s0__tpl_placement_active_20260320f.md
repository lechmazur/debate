[P1.1] Golden-visa and citizenship-by-investment programs convert a public good — membership in a political community — into a commodity priced for the global wealthy. The core problem is not administrative. It is that these programs corrode the meaning of citizenship while generating concrete harms that outweigh their fiscal benefits.
[P1.2] Start with housing. In Portugal, golden-visa-driven property investment helped push Lisbon and Porto real estate beyond the reach of working residents. In Cyprus and Malta, passport sales funneled capital into luxury developments that sit largely empty while local populations are priced out. The "investment" governments celebrate is often a wealth transfer from ordinary renters and buyers to property developers and program intermediaries.
[P1.3] Then there is the security dimension. EU institutions have repeatedly flagged golden passport schemes as vectors for money laundering and illicit finance. When residency is sold rather than earned through genuine connection — family, work, study, or asylum — due diligence is structurally compromised. The buyer is someone who can pay hundreds of thousands of euros for fast-track access.
[P1.4] That is precisely the profile that should trigger more scrutiny, not less. Customer logic replaces sovereign logic: the applicant is paying a premium, and the state behaves like a service provider.
[P1.5] But the deepest issue is democratic. Citizenship carries voting rights, social protections, and a stake in collective self-governance. When a state sells it to the highest bidder, it tells its own citizens that their membership is transactional — that the passport they hold as a birthright or earned through years of integration has a price tag. It also creates a two-tier system: one path for the rich, another — longer, harder, more uncertain — for everyone else, including people who actually build their lives in these countries.
[P1.6] The revenue from these programs is marginal against national budgets and replaceable through less corrosive means. What is not easily replaced is the integrity of citizenship once it has been marketized. Membership in a political community should be granted on principled grounds, not auctioned.
