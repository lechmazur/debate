[P1.1] The motion asks a comparative question: would social media be less toxic — not perfect, less — if feeds simply showed posts in the order people you chose to follow posted them? The answer is yes, for a reason rooted in basic incentive design.
[P1.2] Engagement-ranked feeds are optimization machines, and the thing they optimize for is emotional arousal. Outrage, contempt, and fear reliably generate more clicks, comments, and shares than nuance or good news, so an algorithm trained to maximize engagement systematically surfaces the angriest version of every conversation. This isn't speculation; it's the predictable output of the objective function. Facebook's own leaked internal research reportedly found that reweighting toward "engaging" interactions amplified anger and divisiveness, and that publishers noticed and adjusted their content accordingly.
[P1.3] That last part is the crucial mechanism: algorithmic ranking doesn't just filter content — it trains creators. When rage is what gets distributed, rage is what gets produced.
[P1.4] A chronological feed breaks this loop in three ways. First, reach is bounded by your chosen network: a stranger's inflammatory post can't be injected into your feed because it's "performing well." Second, virality slows down; outrage cascades depend on algorithmic acceleration to reach millions in hours.
[P1.5] Third, and most importantly, the incentive to manufacture outrage collapses. If posting inflammatory content earns you the same distribution as posting normally, the professional outrage economy — the engagement farmers, the quote-tweet dunking industry — loses its business model.
[P1.6] Expect the reply that early chronological platforms had trolls too, that Usenet had flame wars. True and irrelevant. Humans have always been capable of cruelty online; the question is whether the product amplifies it or leaves it inert. Chronological feeds let toxicity exist.
[P1.7] Engagement ranking selects for it, rewards it, and scales it. A world that kept the default which merely tolerates our worst impulses, rather than one that industrializes them, is a less toxic world. That is exactly what the motion claims.
