[P1.1] The question before any water-scarce region is simple: do you want to manage scarcity forever, or end it? Conservation and rationing manage scarcity. Desalination ends it. That is the whole debate.
[P1.2] Start with the arithmetic. Conservation has a hard floor. Once households have low-flow fixtures, once farms have drip irrigation, once leaks are fixed, there is nothing left to squeeze. A growing city facing a shrinking river cannot ration its way to a positive balance — it can only decide who goes without.
[P1.3] Rationing does not create a single litre; it allocates a deficit. Desalination is the one option on the table that actually adds supply, and it adds supply that does not depend on rainfall that climate change is making less reliable every decade.
[P1.4] Look at what happens when a region commits. Israel in the early 2000s was a textbook rationing state: campaigns, quotas, drought emergencies. It then built a handful of large coastal plants, and within roughly fifteen years it went from chronic shortage to selling water to its neighbours and refilling the Sea of Galilee. Costs at the biggest plants fell to roughly the price of a cup of coffee per cubic metre.
[P1.5] Compare Cape Town's "Day Zero" in 2018 — a city that conserved heroically and still faced turning off the taps, because conservation alone was always one bad rainy season from collapse.
[P1.6] Rationing also has costs that never show up on a balance sheet. It is regressive: the wealthy drill boreholes and buy tankers while the poor queue. It guts agriculture and the jobs tied to it. It makes water a permanent political fight.
[P1.7] A region that says "mainly conservation" is choosing recurring crisis as policy.
[P1.8] The standard objections — energy, brine — are engineering problems with known answers: renewable-powered plants, diffuser outfalls, co-location with solar. The objection to rationing is not an engineering problem. It is that it leaves people dry.
[P1.9] Conserve, yes. But build the plants first, because only one of these strategies has ever made a water-scarce region stop being water-scarce.
