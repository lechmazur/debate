[P1.1] The hidden engine of wrongful removal in immigration court is not haste; it is the multi-year backlog that leaves cases to rot. When a hearing waits years, memories fade, witnesses scatter, country conditions change, children age out of eligibility, and counsel drift away. Delay manufactures error. Expanded staffing and remote hearings attack that backlog directly, so faster cases are heard with more resources, while evidence is fresh and parties are present.
[P1.2] Compliance rises for the same reason. Remote appearance removes the logistics that cause nonappearance: an eight-hour drive, lost wages, no childcare, or a detained person's hazardous transport. A single mother can log in instead of missing court; a detained respondent can appear without a risky transfer. Fewer people vanish from the docket, and in absentia removals—often the most wrongful orders of all—fall.
[P1.3] People also comply more with outcomes when the process reaches them promptly and treats them as present participants rather than faceless files.
[P1.4] The fairness case is equally concrete. Critics equate remote hearings with second-best justice, but the real comparison is remote versus no timely hearing at all. A video docket with a qualified interpreter and secure document access lets a judge assess credibility and breaks the geographic bottleneck that freezes calendars. Consider an asylum seeker whose only witness is a neighbor now abroad: years later that witness is gone; a staffed remote hearing within months captures the testimony while it exists.
[P1.5] Wrongful removals grow when courts are rushed and understaffed, not when they are enlarged. More judges, clerks, and interpreters buy time per case, not less: deeper research, accurate translation, and room for continuances where complexity appears. The proposal pays for speed with capacity, so any marginal risk from moving faster is smaller than the errors delay already creates. That trade improves compliance and fairness far more than it raises wrongful removals—if it raises them at all.

[C1.7] Showing up on a frozen image is not meaningful participation, and it does not make people more likely to accept an unjust result.
[C1.8] Wrongful removal is a different class of injury. It is exile into danger and a judgment almost impossible to unwind once the plane leaves.
