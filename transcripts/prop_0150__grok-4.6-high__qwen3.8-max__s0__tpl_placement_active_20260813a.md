[P1.3] Users should be able to refuse that machinery without leaving the app. Hiding top-spender rankings takes down the scoreboard that turns generosity into a public tournament. [P1.5] This is the same logic as hiding like counts, muting ads, or killing autoplay. The interface is not a public square required to display every conversion funnel. It is a product. When that product exploits loneliness and FOMO, the least drastic fix is a switch the user controls.
[P1.6] Without those switches, choice is theater. Someone can still send money, but they cannot stop the product from manufacturing the next reason to send it. If a platform can A/B test the perfect gift prompt, it can ship an off switch for the same prompt. Platforms already personalize every recommendation.
[P1.7] They can personalize restraint just as easily.
[P1.8] The clash is ownership. Either users set the terms of their attention and spending, or platforms do. Stronger tools do not abolish creator income. They abolish the claim that extraction is just part of the experience.
