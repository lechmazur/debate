[P1.1] When your paycheck is calculated using a number only your employer can see, that is not compensation — it is charity with math attached. That is the current reality for filmmakers and performers whose streaming residuals depend on audience reach figures that platforms report selectively, define inconsistently, and sometimes decline to share at all.
[P1.2] The core principle here is simple: when a variable determines what someone is owed, that person has a fundamental right to verify it. We accept this without argument in every other compensation context. Commissioned salespeople get access to sales records. Recording artists can audit streaming play counts.
[P1.3] Royalty contracts routinely include audit clauses precisely because the party calculating the payment shouldn't be the only party who sees the calculation. Streaming platforms have no principled basis for claiming a special exemption.
[P1.4] The practical problem is severe. Platforms currently define "a view" differently — some count a few seconds of playback, others require a higher completion threshold. A performer whose residual contract says they earn more above a certain viewership figure cannot verify whether that threshold was crossed because the platform controls both the definition and the data. Without standardization, even a platform willing to share numbers can share figures that are technically accurate but deliberately uninformative.
[P1.5] The 2023 writers' and actors' strikes in Hollywood put this grievance at center stage. One of the documented frustrations was that residual structures built around streaming performance were effectively unverifiable. Creators were being asked to accept streaming's math on faith while platforms simultaneously used viewership data as marketing when numbers were flattering.
[P1.6] The proposition doesn't require platforms to publish all data publicly. It requires them to share standardized, relevant figures with the specific parties whose compensation depends on those figures. That is a targeted, proportionate obligation that solves a genuine structural problem.
[P1.7] The question before this debate is whether a contractual obligation to pay based on reach includes the right to verify that reach. It does. Require the disclosure.
