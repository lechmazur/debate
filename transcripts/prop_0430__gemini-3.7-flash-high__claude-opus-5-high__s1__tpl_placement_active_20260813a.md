[P1.1] The modern schoolyard has no fence. For today’s students, social hierarchy and peer culture are negotiated almost entirely in digital spaces. When an entire cohort creates an invitation-only group chat specifically to isolate, mock, or coordinate the ostracism of a specific peer, that harm does not stop at the schoolhouse door. It walks directly into the classroom, dictating who speaks in class, who sits alone at lunch, and who becomes too terrified to attend school at all.
[P1.2] Treating digital exclusion as a "purely off-campus" matter relies on an obsolete boundary between physical and online life. Relational aggression—the deliberate manipulation of social standing and belonging—is one of the most psychologically damaging forms of bullying. In the digital age, invitation-only chats are the primary mechanism used to orchestrate this harm at scale. When a student is systematically blacklisted from the digital spaces where peer support and school interactions are negotiated, their educational environment is undeniably compromised.
[P1.3] This is not about asking administrators to police private friendships or mandate who must be invited to a sleepover. It is about recognizing deliberate, coordinated social destruction as an actionable behavioral issue. Schools already maintain a clear duty of care to address off-campus conduct—such as online harassment or targeted threats—when it produces substantial disruption inside the school. Excluding digital ostracism from that anti-bullying framework simply creates a loophole that allows bullies to torment peers with impunity by hiding behind private platforms.
[P1.4] When educators dismiss relational cyberbullying as beyond their purview, they abandon vulnerable students to an inescapable cycle of alienation. Treating private digital exclusion as a serious anti-bullying issue gives schools the authority to intervene through restorative mediation, counseling, and clear community standards, ensuring that every child has a safe environment in which to learn.
