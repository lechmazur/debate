[C1.1] The proposition sells a seductive story: one hostile father, one closed mailbox, one missed therapist. But policy cannot be written around the worst-case parent while pretending the typical one doesn't exist. Most young adults on a family plan are still financially entangled with their families—tuition, rent. In that real world, an insurance bill is not a billing artifact.
[C1.2] It is the truth that someone else is paying. If a parent's plan funds the treatment, that parent has a legitimate interest in knowing the claim exists, to manage benefits and receive notice. The proposition doesn't protect privacy; it mandates secrecy in a financial relationship built on disclosure.
[C1.3] Second, blanket concealment is dangerous. Mental illness often impairs insight; the person most reluctant to disclose may be least able to judge risk. Parents are often the first to notice withdrawal, sleeplessness, dropping grades. PRO says duty-to-warn handles emergencies.
[C1.4] But that duty is a last resort after a clinician has enough information. It does not help the parent watching a child deteriorate who has no idea treatment started. A billing notice can turn a bystander into support.
[C1.5] Third, the proposition confuses privacy with isolation. A nineteen-year-old can vote and sign a lease, yes—but voting is a one-time act and a lease has a landlord. Therapy is ongoing, intimate, and expensive. The question is not whether young adults deserve confidentiality within the clinical relationship.
[C1.6] It is whether the law should force the person paying to be kept in the dark by default. That default doesn't create independence. It creates a secret that collapses under a serious crisis.
[C1.7] PRO's example proves too much. If the father is abusive, the answer is targeted confidentiality protections, not a universal rule that every insurer and parent must be blind. We can protect the vulnerable without designing the system around them. The proposition takes the hardest case and makes it the rule, while stripping away the family safety net for everyone else.
