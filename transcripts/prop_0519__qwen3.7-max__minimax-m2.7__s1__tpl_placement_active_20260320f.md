[P1.1] Fragmented service industries—like fast food, retail, and commercial cleaning—are defined by high turnover, thin margins, and fierce price competition. In this unforgiving environment, repeated minimum-wage hikes are a blunt, increasingly ineffective instrument. Sectoral bargaining is vastly superior because it replaces a one-size-fits-all political mandate with tailored, comprehensive industry standards that actually fit the unique contours of the service sector.
[P1.2] First, statutory minimum-wage hikes only address the hourly rate, ignoring the broader precarity of service work. When legislators force a higher wage floor, fragmented employers often absorb the cost by cutting hours, eliminating benefits, or implementing just-in-time scheduling that devastates service workers’ lives. Sectoral bargaining negotiates the entire employment relationship. It secures predictable scheduling, portable benefits, and training funds alongside base pay, preventing employers from gaming the system to offset wage increases.
[P1.3] Second, sectoral bargaining stops the race to the bottom. In highly fragmented sectors, companies compete primarily by slashing labor costs. A universal minimum wage merely raises the floor of this destructive race. Sectoral bargaining takes basic labor costs out of competition entirely.
[P1.4] When every fast-food franchise or commercial cleaner in a region must adhere to the same negotiated standards, firms are forced to compete on service quality and efficiency rather than exploiting the cheapest possible labor.
[P1.5] Finally, sectoral bargaining is economically sustainable where repeated statutory hikes are politically fragile and economically disruptive. Continuous minimum-wage increases eventually hit a wall, triggering rapid automation or small-business closures because they blindly ignore sector-specific profit margins. Sectoral bargaining brings employers and workers to the table to set standards reflecting the actual economic realities of that specific industry. It creates a stable, predictable regulatory environment rather than the boom-and-bust cycle of arbitrary political wage mandates.
[P1.6] We must adopt sectoral bargaining to build a genuinely stable, equitable service economy.
