[P1.1] The proposition asks which gate should be simpler: proving a professional qualification earned online, or proving physical residency when seeking study or work mobility. It should be the former. The core purpose of cross-border mobility for study and work is to match people with opportunities based on capacity to learn and perform. Online credentials speak directly to that capacity.
[P1.2] They can be standardized, independently verified, and tested against transparent competency frameworks. A digital certificate, proctored exam result, accredited degree, or portfolio can be checked with the issuer, compared to a national standard, and, where needed, supplemented by a practical assessment. Physical residency, by contrast, is a proxy for rootedness and administrative convenience. It tells us where someone has slept, paid bills, or been registered; it does not tell us whether they can code, teach, analyze data, or manage a project.
[P1.3] Making credential recognition easier is also the fairer and more efficient rule. Today, talented people in places with fewer elite institutions or unstable documentation systems are forced to prove belonging through addresses, leases, and local presence—records that are often hardest for mobile, poor, or displaced people to produce. A competent accountant, nurse, or software developer should not lose years because a paper trail of residence is missing, while their professional record sits in a verifiable digital ledger. For employers and universities, skill-based recognition reduces shortages and admissions backlogs.
[P1.4] For states, it shifts the gate from opaque residence tests to auditable competence tests.
[P1.5] This does not mean no safeguards. Professions with safety implications can require local law modules, supervised practice, language testing, or clinical verification. But those safeguards should attach to the credential and the job, not to the applicant’s physical address. If the aim is mobility for study and work, the easier path should be proving what someone can do, not proving where they have been.
