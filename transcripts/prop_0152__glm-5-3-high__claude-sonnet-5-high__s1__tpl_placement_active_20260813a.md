[P1.1] The motion asks whether religious institutions should have *some* room for faith-based dress expectations, gated by voluntariness and realistic alternatives. That qualification is not a concession—it's the proposition's strength. It captures what liberal societies already do well.
[P1.2] Consider three mechanisms by which this works. First, matching: a Jewish day school hiring a Hebrew teacher, a mosque hiring an imam, a Catholic hospital hiring a chaplain—these roles exist to transmit a way of life. Dress expectations here are internal to the mission, not arbitrary power over strangers.
[P1.3] Second, notice and choice: someone who applies to work at an Orthodox school knowing its norms has consented in the most meaningful sense available—through exit and entry decisions, the same way we respect choice about working weekends, wearing uniforms, or relocating. Third, the guardrails themselves: where participation is genuinely voluntary and alternatives are real, the classic harms of dress codes—coercion, control of women, punishment of dissent—largely disappear, because the person subjected to the rule can decline or leave.
[P1.4] Contrast what the alternative demands. If no institution may attach any dress expectation to participation, then the Muslim school cannot ask for modest dress, the Sikh institution cannot expect a turban-friendly grooming standard, the monastery cannot ask for habits. That isn't neutrality; it's the secular state regulating religious communities out of their own self-definition. Voluntary associations are where pluralism actually lives.
[P1.5] A democracy that permits religious schools but strips them of every religious expectation has permitted the shell and confiscated the substance.
[P1.6] The honest debate is about the boundaries—what counts as "genuinely voluntary," when alternatives are "realistic." On those questions, the proposition already sides with the vulnerable: children in a region with no other school, employees with no comparable jobs, captive audiences—none of these are covered by the permission. The motion is a floor against coercion and a ceiling against assimilationist overreach. Both halves matter, and the proposition keeps both.
