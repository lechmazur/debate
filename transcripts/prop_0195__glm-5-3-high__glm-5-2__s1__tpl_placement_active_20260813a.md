[P1.1] Precaution, taken literally, is universal. Every action—including inaction—carries risk. What distinguishes the precautionary principle as it is actually applied in regulation is a single asymmetry: it demands proof of safety before an innovation may proceed, while requiring no comparable proof of safety for the status quo it preserves. That asymmetry is where the harm hides.
[P1.2] The principle's defenders always picture the innovation side of the ledger: thalidomide, asbestos, CFCs. But the ledger has two sides, and the principle systematically ignores one of them.
