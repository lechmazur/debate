[P1.1] Deterrence is an expectation about costs, capabilities, and resolve. Sustained Western military aid changes all three. Ammunition, air defense, training, and stockpiles let Ukraine deny Russia a decisive victory; predictable support tells Moscow that time and attrition will not fracture the West; and every month in which Russian forces are pinned down lets NATO harden its flank. Aid makes wider aggression less feasible and less attractive.
[P1.2] Pushing an immediate settlement works in the opposite direction. It would be negotiated while Russia still holds land seized by force and can use occupied ground as leverage. However humane the wish to stop the killing, a settlement pressed before aggression is made unprofitable teaches a simple lesson: invade, absorb costs, wait for Western fatigue, convert battlefield pressure into diplomatic gain. Crimea and the Donbas war in 2014 did not produce a satisfied Russia; they were followed by the full-scale invasion of 2022.
[P1.3] That does not prove causation, but it puts the burden on any settlement-first argument: why would a pause under duress alter the ambition that aid is now constraining?
[P1.4] Immediate settlement also weakens the guarantees a settlement-first case needs. If the West signals speed over credibility, future promises to support Ukraine and Moldova, and to defend NATO’s eastern edge, become less credible. A ceasefire with weak enforcement leaves Russia free to rearm, reorganize, probe elsewhere, and use energy leverage, refugee flows, cyberattacks, or political pressure as coercion. A frozen line is not peace when one side has learned that force moved the line.
[P1.5] Diplomacy is not rejected; sequencing is decisive. Aid creates leverage, preserves a sovereign Ukraine, and raises the price of another move, making a later negotiation enforceable rather than extortionate. Immediate settlement buys a short pause while leaving the incentive intact. To deter wider aggression, Moscow must learn not that the West can tire, but that it can sustain denial—and that the next gamble will be worse than the last.
