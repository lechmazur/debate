[P1.1] The transition to electric vehicles is the most critical infrastructure shift of our time, and its success hinges on a seamless charging experience. When governments allow proprietary charging systems to dominate, they permit the creation of walled gardens that fragment the market, frustrate consumers, and ultimately slow down decarbonization. Mandating open interoperability standards is essential to accelerate consumer adoption, foster genuine market competition, and protect public infrastructure investments.
[P1.2] First, proprietary networks manufacture artificial range anxiety. If a driver buys an EV that only accesses a specific proprietary network, they are effectively stranded when that network is unavailable, even if a competitor’s charger sits empty nearby. Open standards, much like the universal standards we require for household electrical outlets or cellular roaming, guarantee that any vehicle can use any public charger. This physical and digital seamlessness is the single most effective way to eliminate the hesitation of prospective EV buyers.
[P1.3] Second, open standards shift the basis of competition from mere access to actual quality. When networks rely on proprietary plugs or software locks, they compete by hoarding captive users rather than by improving service. Interoperability forces providers to compete on what actually matters to drivers: charging speed, uptime reliability, transparent pricing, and site amenities. We saw this exact dynamic in telecommunications; mandatory roaming and open standards didn't stifle innovation, they forced companies to build better networks and lower prices to win customers.
[P1.4] Finally, public funds heavily subsidize EV infrastructure. Taxpayers should not finance duplicative, fragmented silos that artificially restrict access. If a charging station receives public backing, it must serve the entire public. By requiring open interoperability, we prevent a monopolistic landscape and ensure a unified foundation.
