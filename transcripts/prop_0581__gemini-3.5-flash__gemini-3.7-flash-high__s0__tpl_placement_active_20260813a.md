[P1.1] Unchecked corporate consolidation has functioned as a tax on American consumers and a stranglehold on dynamic enterprise. Strict merger enforcement—blocking more horizontal and vertical deals—is the single most effective tool to restore price competition and ignite genuine innovation.
[P1.2] Horizontal mergers directly eliminate choice, allowing consolidated firms to hike markups. When major competitors merge, the incentive to undercut vanishes. Vertical mergers are equally damaging; they allow dominant firms to control supply chains, foreclosing rivals from vital inputs or distribution channels. When a dominant healthcare giant or tech platform acquires its suppliers, it gains both the power and the incentive to self-prefer, raising rivals' costs and inflating retail prices.
[P1.3] Strict enforcement keeps these channels open and competitive, forcing firms to compete on price to win customers.
[P1.4] Furthermore, true innovation does not emerge from monolithic boardrooms; it comes from competitive pressure. In consolidated markets, dominant players routinely engage in "killer acquisitions"—buying up promising startups to shelf their technology and neutralize threats. [P1.5] Finally, the claim that blocking mergers harms productivity or economies of scale is a paper tiger. Real productivity gains are driven by internal capital investment and operational excellence, not corporate financial engineering. Most mergers fail to realize their promised synergies, instead producing bloated bureaucracies and integration friction. Scale achieved organically through superior products benefits society; scale acquired through consolidation merely transfers wealth from consumers to monopolists.
[P1.6] Strict enforcement ensures that the only way to grow is to build, not buy.
