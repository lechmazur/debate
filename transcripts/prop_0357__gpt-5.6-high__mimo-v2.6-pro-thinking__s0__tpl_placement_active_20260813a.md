[P1.1] A vehicle subscription may legitimately pay for ongoing extras such as live traffic, concierge service, or cellular data. It should not become a tollbooth between an owner and the ability to diagnose, repair, calibrate, or safely operate the machine they already bought. “Core repair functionality” should include access to fault information, service resets, and the lawful pairing or calibration of replacement parts, through an offline or otherwise non-subscription path.
[P1.2] The central principle is simple: a subscription can end a service, but it should not diminish the underlying product. If cancelling connectivity prevents an independent mechanic from reading necessary codes, blocks a replacement component from functioning, or leaves safety-related systems impossible to recalibrate, the automaker has converted ownership into a hidden lease. The buyer paid for the vehicle; software architecture should not let the seller reacquire control after the sale.
[P1.3] This requirement also protects safety and competition. When ordinary repairs carry an artificial subscription toll, owners defer maintenance, usable vehicles are prematurely discarded, and rural or lower-income drivers suffer most because they have fewer authorized service options. Software locks also insulate dealership repair networks from competition. Independent mechanics cannot discipline prices if the manufacturer alone controls the digital permission needed to complete physical work.
[P1.4] Automakers are best positioned to prevent these harms. They choose the architecture, know the components, and can build a secure local diagnostic mode, owner authorization process, or durable service credential from the outset. An individual buyer cannot redesign the car after a server is retired or a subscription becomes unaffordable. Placing the duty on manufacturers therefore minimizes costs rather than shifting catastrophic lockout risk onto millions of owners.
[P1.5] This is not a demand for free cellular service, proprietary source code, or insecure universal access. Security can be preserved through authentication, logging, and limited repair permissions. The rule concerns continuity of essential function, not perpetual provision of every convenience. When software becomes part of the wrench, ending a data plan must not confiscate the wrench.
[P1.6] Basic ownership means the practical right to keep one’s own vehicle working.
