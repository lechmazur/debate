[C1.1] The proposition pretends that eliminating the worst cages and crates is a cheap, clean fix. In practice, a phase‑out of battery‑cage eggs or gestation crates forces producers to rebuild entire houses, purchase new equipment, and lose the economies of scale that keep basic protein affordable. Historical bans in the European Union showed egg prices climb well beyond a “modest” bump; the jump was felt hardest in low‑income neighborhoods where a few cents per dozen translates into a noticeable share of grocery budgets.
[C1.2] When the cost is passed to shoppers, the most vulnerable bear it, not the corporations that reap the profit.
[C1.3] Even if the headline price increase were limited, the ripple effects magnify the burden. Small‑scale farmers, who lack capital for rapid retrofitting, face bankruptcy or forced sale to larger conglomerates that can absorb the expense. Rural employment contracts shrink, and communities that depend on livestock production lose a tax base and a food‑security anchor. In a global market, domestic producers ceding capacity invite imports from jurisdictions with far lower welfare standards, effectively exporting the very cruelty the motion seeks to end.
[C1.4] The motion also misidentifies the lever for improvement. Welfare gains can be achieved through enrichment, better stocking densities, and transparent labeling without dismantling the production model that supplies the bulk of affordable protein. Targeted standards that allow incremental upgrades deliver measurable welfare at a fraction of the cost of a wholesale ban.
[C1.5] Finally, the claim that governments can simply cushion households through existing assistance presumes a political will and fiscal capacity that rarely aligns with the timing of price spikes. In reality, subsidies lag, eligibility gaps persist, and the net effect is a regressive price shock.
[C1.6] For these reasons, the real‑world cost is not modest, the welfare payoff is uncertain, and a blunt phase‑out is a blunt instrument that harms the people it purports to protect.
