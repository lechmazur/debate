[P1.1] Broad reimbursement of preventive obesity drugs without strings is a fiscal gamble that no health system can afford. The latest GLP‑1 agonists can cost several hundred dollars a month, and while they show impressive short‑term weight loss, long‑term data on reduced cardiovascular events, diabetes onset, and overall mortality remain incomplete. Paying for every prescription on a fee‑for‑service basis would flood budgets, crowd out essential services, and lock payers into a cycle of ever‑rising drug spend.
[P1.2] Outcome‑based pricing flips the risk. Manufacturers are asked to prove that their product delivers a pre‑agreed clinical result—say a 10 % sustained weight loss at 12 months or a measurable drop in incident type‑2 diabetes—before full payment is released. This aligns incentives: companies invest in real‑world evidence, post‑marketing surveillance, and patient support programs that improve adherence. Spending caps complement this by setting a hard ceiling on total outlays, protecting systems from the tail‑risk of a blockbuster that underperforms.
[P1.3] History shows this works. Hepatitis‑C cures were initially priced at tens of thousands per course, but outcome‑linked agreements in several countries tied payment to cure rates, rapidly bringing costs down while preserving access. The same logic applies to obesity therapeutics: a cap forces manufacturers to price responsibly, while outcome clauses guarantee that public money is spent only when health gains materialize.
[P1.4] Critics will claim such conditions stifle innovation or delay access. The opposite is true. When firms know reimbursement hinges on demonstrable benefit, they channel R&D toward truly effective molecules and away from marginal improvements. Caps do not ban a drug; they simply require that its price reflect the value it actually delivers.
[P1.5] Thus, conditional reimbursement—outcome‑based pricing or spending caps—protects fiscal sustainability, ensures value, and still opens the door to broad access when the evidence justifies it.
