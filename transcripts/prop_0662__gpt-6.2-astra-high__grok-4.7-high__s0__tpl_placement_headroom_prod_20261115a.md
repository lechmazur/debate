[P1.1] Two hotel clerks lose their jobs when the hotel closes. Both had worked short shifts there and earned the rest of their living delivering meals. One delivered for a restaurant on payroll; the other took deliveries through an app. If payroll earnings count and app earnings do not, one clerk can clear the unemployment insurance threshold while the other falls short.
[P1.2] Nothing about that difference tells us which clerk worked more or suffered the greater loss.
[P1.3] Governments should require platforms to report what each worker earned and when, in a common format workers can inspect and correct. An agency could then combine earnings across apps and payroll jobs, deduct allowable work expenses rather than treating gross receipts as wages, and apply its usual qualifying threshold. [P1.4] Counting work is not the same as paying every claim. [P1.5] Coverage should carry contributions on covered earnings, with platforms remitting their share rather than leaving payroll employers to finance the expansion. What they cannot choose is whether the person delivering it earns a work history.
