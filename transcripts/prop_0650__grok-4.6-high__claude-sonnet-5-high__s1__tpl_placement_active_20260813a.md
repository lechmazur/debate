[P1.1] Metabolic disease advances quietly. Prediabetes and insulin resistance build in people who feel fine, follow the usual advice, and receive no daily signal until the damage is already advanced. [P1.2] They watch, in real time, how a meal, a walk after dinner, or a broken night of sleep actually moves their glucose. That visibility is not turning healthy people into patients. It is the first private experiment most of them can run on their own metabolism.
[P1.3] Public health moves when people change what they eat and how they move because the evidence is theirs. A person who sees a spike after a “healthy” smoothie or late snack does not need another guideline; they have a reason to adjust. Scale that across millions and you get fewer people sliding into diabetes, less cardiovascular load, better daily energy, and less strain on clinics. Wearables already demonstrated the pattern: continuous personal feedback changes habits more reliably than generic lectures.
[P1.4] Anxiety and over-medicalization are genuine risks. [P1.5] The comparison that decides this debate is magnitude. Metabolic disease is a leading cause of disability and premature death. A subset of anxious users is a cost we already accept for information that lets the majority act earlier and more precisely.
[P1.6] Blindness is not safer. Seeing the signal earlier will improve population health more than it will inflate worry.
