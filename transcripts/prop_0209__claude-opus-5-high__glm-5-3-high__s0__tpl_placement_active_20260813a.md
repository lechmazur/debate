[P1.1] A Syrian family stepping off a boat on Lesbos does not become Greece's sole responsibility because of anything Greece chose. It becomes Greece's responsibility because of a rule the rest of Europe wrote — first country of entry — and because of a coastline drawn by geology. That is the whole case in miniature: the current arrangement allocates one of the Union's heaviest obligations by accident of latitude, and then calls the result fairness.
[P1.2] Judge this on one question: which arrangement actually produces a functioning, rights-respecting asylum system across Europe? Concentration fails that test by design. When a few thousand arrivals a week land on islands with capacity for a few hundred, you do not get careful adjudication — you get Moria. You get tents in mud, children waiting years for a first interview, and a state whose rational response is to under-register, wave people north, or push boats back out to sea.
[P1.3] Overload doesn't just hurt asylum seekers; it corrodes the border every other member state depends on.
[P1.4] Spread the same numbers across twenty-seven states with functioning administrations and they become ordinary casework. This is the core asymmetry: the load is crushing where it lands and trivial once divided. Quotas are simply the mechanism that performs the division.
[P1.5] Voluntary solidarity has already been tried and it collapsed. After 2015, states pledged relocation and then delivered a fraction of it; since then Europe has negotiated rescue ships one at a time, dozens of people per boat, while a diplomatic crisis is manufactured over each. Ad hoc goodwill is not a system. It is a hostage negotiation in which the hostages are asylum seekers.
[P1.6] And Schengen makes free-riding indefensible. Malta and Italy guard an external frontier whose benefit — passport-free travel — is consumed by Austria and the Netherlands. If you take the benefit of a shared border, you take the caseload it generates. Mandatory sharing is not charity toward the south; it is the price of the internal market's open doors.

[C1.3] The failure has a mechanism, not just a mood. Quotas assign people to countries they don't want to reach. Secondary movement follows immediately — people vanish from their assigned state and reappear in Germany or Sweden within weeks, as thousands did during relocation. [C1.4] You cannot allocate human beings like bridge repairs. Asylum seekers have agency, preferences, family networks, and smugglers on speed dial.

[C1.6] Frontline overload is a capacity problem, and capacity problems are solved with capacity, not with relocation math.
[C1.7] Finally, the politics: forced quotas radicalized the migration debate across central Europe and helped mainstream parties lose ground to the far right everywhere from Italy to Sweden.
