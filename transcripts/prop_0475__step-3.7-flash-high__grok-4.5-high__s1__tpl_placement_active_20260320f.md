[P1.1] Current agricultural subsidies are propping up the single largest source of food system emissions, while underfunding the low-emission alternatives that can slash that footprint drastically. Conventional animal agriculture accounts for roughly 14.5% of global anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions, driven by methane from livestock, deforestation for feed and pasture, and fertilizer runoff, yet receives the vast majority of global farm support. In the United States alone, nearly 70% of direct agricultural subsidies flow to feed crop and livestock producers, locking in a high-emission system that is increasingly unviable in a warming world.
[P1.2] Precision fermentation, the technology used to produce animal-free heme, dairy proteins, and other functional food ingredients, cuts life cycle emissions by 80 to 90% compared to their animal-derived equivalents, using a fraction of the land and water, with no deforestation or enteric methane required. Reallocating even a fraction of existing subsidies to these technologies would accelerate scaling, drive down consumer costs, and make low-emission protein accessible to mass markets far faster than market forces alone would allow. Look to Singapore, which has invested heavily in alternative protein R&D, bringing the cost of precision-fermented protein to near parity with conventional meat in just a few years—without the kind of large-scale subsidy reallocation this motion proposes.
[P1.3] Opponents will claim such a shift harms farmers, but subsidy reallocation can be designed for a just transition: row crop farmers can shift to growing the high-protein legumes and feedstocks needed for alternative protein production, while precision fermentation facilities create stable, high-wage rural jobs that do not depend on volatile commodity prices or climate-driven crop failures. Unlike conventional animal agriculture, which is vulnerable to drought, disease, and supply chain shocks, these low-emission technologies boost domestic food security and reduce reliance on imported feed and meat products.
