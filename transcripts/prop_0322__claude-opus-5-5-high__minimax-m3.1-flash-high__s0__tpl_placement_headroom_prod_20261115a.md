[C1.2] Consider a creator who knowingly licenses her voice to an advertiser and publicly explains that the spot uses a synthetic version. The proposed law would still compel her to announce the production method, though the audience already knows. Now consider a cloned host endorsing a product she never used. [C1.3] A label can reveal deception; it cannot convert a false claim into real experience.
[C1.4] PRO’s account also assumes disclosure is always a one-sentence fix. “Scripted likeness replication” is nowhere defined. [C1.5] Liability across that supply chain would require extensive definitions and monitoring, not a simple disclosure line.
[C1.6] The better rule is that no creator’s identity may be used without authorization and no endorsement may misrepresent experience. A label cannot cure a cloned host’s false claim that she used the product; law should punish the lie, not stigmatize every authorized synthetic voice.
