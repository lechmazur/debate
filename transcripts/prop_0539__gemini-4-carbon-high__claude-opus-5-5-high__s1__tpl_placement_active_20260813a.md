[P1.1] Unchecked cross-border commercial surrogacy runs on regulatory arbitrage, turning reproduction into a race to the bottom. When profit-driven brokers connect wealthier intended parents with economically vulnerable women abroad outside binding rules, three severe harms routinely follow. Intermediaries capture most of the fees while coercive contracts strip surrogates of autonomy; clinics transfer multiple embryos or schedule unnecessary cesareans to cut costs and match travel plans; and conflicting national laws leave newborns stranded across borders without clear citizenship or legal parents. Cross-border commercial surrogacy should remain legal only when agencies and clinics meet enforceable standards that lock out these abuses before pregnancy begins.
[P1.2] All three standards are indispensable because omitting any one leaves devastating loopholes. Enforceable anti-exploitation rules require independent counsel in the surrogate’s language, transparent fee caps, and escrowed pay delivered directly to her without forfeiting bodily autonomy. Enforceable health protections mandate medical screening, single-embryo transfer defaults, guaranteed prenatal and postpartum care regardless of outcome, and surrogate authority over invasive procedures. Enforceable parentage rules require pre-conception legal clearance ensuring both birth and home states bind intended parents irrevocably, preventing statelessness or abandonment even if parents separate or a child has disabilities.
[P1.3] Regulating agencies and clinics makes enforcement practical: clinics depend on medical licenses, embryology labs, banking, and consular paperwork, letting states verify compliance upstream rather than improvising at borders after birth.
[P1.4] Both alternatives fail those most at risk. Permitting cross-border commercial surrogacy without strict standards lets abusive operators undercut ethical clinics on price and speed. Yet banning it outright neither ends the desire for children nor expands women’s economic options; prohibition pushes arrangements into covert networks where medical care goes unmonitored and exploited surrogates lack legal recourse. A strictly conditional legal pathway channels families toward audited clinics, starves rogue brokers of clients, and secures consent, health, and legal parentage from day one.
