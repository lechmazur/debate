[P1.1] Moving to another country is not like checking into a hotel. It is joining a going concern - a political community with institutions, rights, and duties that already exist. Expecting newcomers to adopt its core civic norms and its common public language is not nativism. It is the basic price of membership, the same price asked of anyone born there.
[P1.2] Core civic norms are not folk dances or cuisine. They are the non-negotiable operating system of a liberal democracy: that disputes are settled by law not violence, that men and women have equal standing, that you tolerate speech you despise, that elections decide power. These are not "one culture among many." They are the conditions that make pluralism possible in the first place.
[P1.3] If integration is treated as optional, we do not get more diversity, we get less - enclaves where those basic protections stop at the boundary.
[P1.4] The common public language is the same. It is infrastructure. When a patient cannot describe symptoms to a paramedic, when a parent cannot read a school notice, when a worker cannot access a better job because interviews happen in another language, the cost is paid daily - and mostly by immigrants themselves. No one needs to abandon a mother tongue at home.
[P1.5] But public life - work, courts, voting, school - requires a usable common tongue. Demanding ever-expanding official translation for every interaction is not sustainable, and it quietly institutionalizes second-class status.
[P1.6] That expectation is liberating, not oppressive. It tells every newcomer: we assume you will be a full citizen, not a permanent guest. You will have the same voice and the same obligations. Without that, we abandon both solidarity and the very liberal values we claim to celebrate.

[C2.4] PRO’s citation of multilingual democracies like Montreal or Zurich ignores that those policies arose from historical suppression of minority languages, and they still allow residents to access public services in their native language while learning the public tongue at their own pace—precisely the optional approach PRO rejects.
