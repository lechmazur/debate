[P1.1] When a private citizen deletes a post, locks an account, or keeps a digital interaction out of public view, they are setting a clear boundary. Data brokers subvert that boundary for profit. [P1.2] First, digital autonomy requires a meaningful right to retreat. When someone hits "delete," or keeps information non-public—like private group activity, backend app location trails, or locked profiles—they explicitly deny or withdraw consent for public circulation. [P1.3] Second, the real-world harms of monetizing these traces are severe and irreversible. Think about who buys deleted or non-public data about ordinary people. A teenager deletes a foolish post or a confession about mental health; a broker packages that ghost trace into a consumer risk score for insurers, landlords, or employers.
[P1.4] Because citizens have no direct relationship with data brokers, they cannot even see or correct the invisible dossiers being used against them.
[P1.5] Finally, this prohibition is precisely targeted. It applies strictly to commercial data brokers and strictly to private citizens' deleted or non-public traces.
