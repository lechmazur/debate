[P1.1] Historic cities are not just open-air museums for tourists: they are living, working communities with a right to basic quality of life. The motion sets a prudent, evidence-aligned threshold for intervention: only cap cruise-ship and large tour-bus arrivals when resident crowding, pollution, and housing pressure outweigh tourist revenue gains. This is not an attack on tourism—it is a defense of the long-term sustainable tourism that depends on preserving the very character and infrastructure that makes these cities worth visiting. Unregulated mass arrivals create cascading, irreversible harms that far outstrip their short-term fiscal benefits.
[P1.2] In Venice, peak cruise days can double the number of people in the 1.5-square-mile historic center in a single morning, overwhelming medieval sewage systems, accelerating erosion of 1,000-year-old building foundations from ship wake, and driving short-term rental prices up sharply since 2000, pushing out tens of thousands of long-term residents. Local grocers, schools, and essential services have shuttered as housing stock is converted to tourist rentals, eroding the lived culture that draws visitors in the first place. Day-trippers from cruise ships and tour buses spend a fraction of what overnight, low-impact tourists contribute, meaning uncapped mass arrivals deliver minimal net revenue while imposing massive costs on residents and the city’s heritage.
[P1.3] The motion’s built-in safeguard ensures caps are only imposed when harms clearly outweigh gains, ruling out arbitrary restrictions. When Dubrovnik capped cruise arrivals at 4,000 per day in 2018 to avoid UNESCO endangered status, overnight tourist spending rose in the following year, as the city attracted higher-value visitors who stayed longer and supported local businesses, rather than crowding public spaces for a few hours of photo ops. The right of residents to affordable housing, clean streets, and functional public services is not a secondary concern to short-term tourist revenue—it is the foundation of a city’s long-term economic and cultural viability.
