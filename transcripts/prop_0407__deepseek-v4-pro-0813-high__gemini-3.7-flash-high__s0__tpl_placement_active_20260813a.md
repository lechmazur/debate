[P1.1] The default rule turns a job into a license to police private life. A worker sells labor, skill, and time, not off-duty identity, beliefs, associations, or lawful choices. If conduct is lawful and does not materially affect job performance, firing over it is not a business judgment; it is economic coercion.
[P1.2] Consider what happens now. A nurse can be fired for a lawful political rally, an accountant for a private social media post, a factory worker for a legal gun hobby, or a teacher for speaking at a school board meeting. In each case the employer may dislike the conduct, but there is no showing that patients, clients, production, or teaching suffered. The worker loses income, insurance, and stability for being a citizen after hours.
[P1.3] The harm extends beyond the fired employee. At-will terminations teach everyone else that keeping a job may require staying silent, avoiding public participation, hiding lawful relationships or religious practice, and conforming to a boss’s personal politics. That is a private speech code enforced through economic fear.
[P1.4] The usual defense of firings is that employers must protect reputation and workplace culture. But protection applies only when off-duty conduct does not materially affect job performance. It would not shield harassment, theft, insubordination, safety violations, confidentiality breaches, or genuine reputational harm tied to the employer’s operations. The employer may still act when there is a real workplace interest—it just cannot fire on speculation, prejudice, or moral disapproval.
[P1.5] The legal rule should shift the burden: once a worker shows termination for lawful off-duty conduct, the employer must demonstrate a material connection to job performance or a legitimate business necessity. That is not a radical restriction on business; it is a requirement to treat firing as a serious act needing a work-related reason.
[P1.6] Without stronger protection, the law gives one side a veto over the whole life of the other. No one should have to surrender their lawful private life as a condition of earning a living.
