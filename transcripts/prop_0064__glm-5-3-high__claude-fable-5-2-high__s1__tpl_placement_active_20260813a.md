[P1.1] Work is not voluntary in the way defenders of these tools pretend. When an employer *requires* an always-on AI companion—one that watches keystrokes, interrupts with prompts, and coaches every task—that is not innovation being adopted; it is surveillance being mandated. The word "requiring" is the whole motion, and it is exactly where the harm lives.
[P1.2] Three arguments frame this debate.
[P1.3] First, coerced consent is not consent. The standard defense—that workers agree to these tools as a condition of employment—collapses under the power asymmetry of the modern labor market. A warehouse picker in a tight economy does not negotiate; she complies. We do not allow employers to require other invasions of the person—drug testing has been fenced in by law, wearables in warehouses have faced regulatory pushback across Europe—precisely because "take it or leave the job" is not a real choice.
[P1.4] An always-on monitor is an invasion of the working self, hour after hour.
[P1.5] Second, total observation changes the person observed. Psychologists have long documented that surveillance degrades intrinsic motivation, creativity, and honest communication. An AI that coaches every email and prompts every break converts judgment into compliance. Workers stop asking "what is the right decision?" and start asking "what will the system score?"
[P1.6] That is a loss to the employer too—but it is a certainty for the worker, who is treated as a malfunction to be optimized rather than a professional to be trusted.
[P1.7] Third, a prohibition on *requiring* these tools costs almost nothing. Voluntary adoption remains fully legal. Firms can offer AI assistants that workers switch on when useful. The only thing banned is compulsion.
[P1.8] If these companions are genuinely wonderful, persuasion should suffice.
[P1.9] The line to hold is simple: employers may provide tools; they may not mandate constant accompaniment. Watch whether my opponent defends the tools themselves—or the right to force them on people who would rather think for themselves.
