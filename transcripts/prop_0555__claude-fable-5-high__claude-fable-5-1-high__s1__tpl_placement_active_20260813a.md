[P1.4] Second, the motion's conditions do real work. Background checks screen out serious criminals. [P1.5] Third, legalization is a fiscal and rule-of-law upgrade. In reality it is a standing offer, and its terms are legible from anywhere on earth: enter unlawfully, stay quiet long enough, hold a job, and the state will regularize you.
[C1.2] Every honest evaluation must price in what that offer does to future behavior, not just what it does for today's residents.
[C1.3] We have run this experiment. The large American legalization of the mid-1980s was sold as a one-time fix paired with enforcement. The enforcement never materialized, and the undocumented population roughly tripled over the following two decades. That is not a coincidence; it is a ratchet.
[C1.4] Each pathway creates the constituency for the next, and each delay in the next one is framed as cruelty to people who reasonably relied on the precedent.
[C1.5] Second, fairness. Right now there are people who filed paperwork, paid fees, and have waited years in queues for the same status. A pathway tells them the surest route was to skip the line. [C1.6] Third, PRO's dichotomy is false. The alternative is neither mass roundups nor "permanent limbo." It is consistent employer verification, targeted removals, and case-by-case humanitarian discretion that governments already exercise without codifying an entitlement. Discretion preserves the norm; a statutory pathway repeals it.
[C1.7] Finally, the "filter" is weaker than advertised. Proving a decade of off-the-books work invites exactly the document fraud that plagued the earlier legalization's farmworker program.
[C1.8] So weigh it: PRO offers relief to one cohort. We defend the integrity of the system every future cohort will face. Ratifying evasion buys a few years of tidiness at the cost of making evasion the durable strategy.
