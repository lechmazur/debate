[P1.1] The question before us is not whether trophy hunting feels distasteful. It does, to many people, including probably some of you. The question is whether that discomfort should override a policy that, under the specific conditions stated in the resolution, demonstrably protects wildlife and funds the communities who share land with it. On that narrower question, PRO wins.
[P1.2] Start with the core mechanism: land without economic value gets converted. A rancher or village surrounded by elephants, lions, or rhinos that destroy crops and kill livestock has every incentive to eliminate them, quietly and permanently, unless those animals are worth more alive-as-wildlife-asset than dead-as-pest. Trophy hunting, when revenue is transparently funneled into conservation budgets and local livelihoods, converts a liability into an asset. Namibia's community conservancy system is the standard example here: by granting local communities direct rights to hunting revenue, areas that once saw wildlife as a threat to be exterminated began protecting it, and populations of species like elephant and black rhino in those conservancies grew rather than collapsed.
[P1.3] That is not a hypothetical mechanism; it is the lived experience of a country that moved from colonial-era wildlife collapse to measurable recovery.
[P1.4] Compare this to the alternative the opponent must defend: a blanket ban regardless of local economic structure. Bans don't eliminate demand for land conversion or poaching; they just eliminate the legal revenue stream that was funding anti-poaching patrols, ranger salaries, and community buy-in. When Kenya banned trophy hunting decades ago, wildlife populations outside strictly protected parks continued declining because there was no longer an economic reason for landowners to tolerate dangerous, destructive animals on their property.
[P1.5] So the resolution isn't asking you to find trophy hunting pleasant. It's asking whether, under strict transparency conditions, legal and regulated hunting outperforms the realistic alternatives at the actual goal everyone claims to share: keeping animals alive and communities supported. PRO's case is that it does, and CON's case requires pretending the realistic alternative is utopian protection rather than quiet extinction by neglect.
