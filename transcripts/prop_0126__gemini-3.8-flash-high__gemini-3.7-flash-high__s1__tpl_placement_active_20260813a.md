[P1.1] When a person enters prison, the state assumes total control over their custody and rehabilitation. Yet for decades, correctional systems have treated the single most powerful rehabilitative tool—family connection—as a predatory revenue stream. Outsourcing communication to private vendors that charge exorbitant rates for basic phone calls and video visits does not just exploit vulnerable families; it actively sabotages public safety. Making these communications free is both an urgent moral imperative and sound correctional policy.
[P1.2] First, continuous family contact is the bedrock of successful re-entry. Incarcerated individuals who maintain regular contact with their families are significantly less likely to reoffend upon release and far less prone to institutional violence while inside. Contact preserves parental bonds, reinforces personal accountability, and ensures a viable support network exists on the outside. When states allow vendors to price family contact out of reach, they deliberately dismantle the exact social safety net that prevents recidivism.
[P1.3] Second, high communication fees function as a cruel, regressive tax on families who have committed no crime. Incarcerated people have virtually no income; the financial burden of five-, ten-, or twenty-dollar phone calls falls squarely on low-income mothers, grandmothers, and children on the outside. Forcing an impoverished family to choose between purchasing groceries and letting a child speak to an incarcerated parent is indefensible. It punishes innocent communities and deepens generational poverty.
[P1.4] Third, the current fee structure is an artificial monopoly enabled by perverse incentives. In the modern era, the marginal cost of routing a digital voice or video stream is negligible. Exorbitant prices persist only because private telecom vendors operate uncompetitive monopolies, frequently enticing prison administrators with lucrative kickbacks known as site commissions.
[P1.5] Communication is not a luxury perk to be monetized; it is essential rehabilitative infrastructure. Replacing predatory vendor contracts with free communication stabilizes facilities, protects innocent families, and pays for itself through reduced crime.

Providing this complex infrastructure entirely free shifts massive operational expenses onto already strained public correctional budgets. [C1.3] Second, zero-cost access creates severe operational chaos and security risks inside facilities. Simultaneously, unconstrained call volumes overwhelm intelligence staff's ability to screen communications effectively, compromising victim safety and facility order.
[C1.4] The responsible path is not forcing public budgets to fully subsidize unlimited calls, but enforcing strict regulatory rate caps that eliminate vendor profiteering while maintaining nominal, cost-recovery fees. Nominal fees manage bandwidth, prevent illicit system capture, fund vital security oversight, and preserve correctional dollars for the core rehabilitative services that truly lower recidivism.
