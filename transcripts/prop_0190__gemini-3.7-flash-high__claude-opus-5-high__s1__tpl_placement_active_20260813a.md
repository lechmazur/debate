[P1.1] Insurance exists to protect people from catastrophe, but pure actuarial fairness perverts that mission. When insurers price essential coverage—such as healthcare, basic disability, and essential shelter protection—strictly according to individualized statistical risk, they penalize human beings for vulnerabilities they did not choose and cannot escape. Social solidarity demands that the fortunate and healthy share the burden of life’s structural fragilities with the sick, elderly, and unlucky. Unchecked actuarial fairness shatters that social compact.
[P1.2] First, actuarial fairness converts unchosen misfortune into systematic exclusion. A person born with a chronic illness, a family history of disease, or residing in an environmentally vulnerable area did not volunteer for that exposure. Under pure risk-based pricing, insurers charge these individuals crippling premiums or deny coverage entirely. This generates an inverted safety net: those with the greatest need for security are priced out precisely because of their need.
[P1.3] Essential coverage is not a consumer luxury like sports cars or speculative investments; it is a foundational prerequisite for health, economic participation, and human dignity. Allowing statistical models to turn the biological and geographical lottery into economic exile is morally indefensible.
[P1.4] Second, social solidarity is the only ethical and stable mechanism for managing essential risk. True security requires broad risk-pooling, where the low-risk cross-subsidize the high-risk through legal standards like community rating. When actuarial differentiation is unrestrained—now supercharged by predictive big data—it splinters society into hyper-individualized silos. Healthy individuals retreat into cheap micro-pools, leaving the vulnerable stranded in underfunded death spirals.
[P1.5] Limiting actuarial fairness by law does not destroy the insurance mechanism; it reclaims it for the common good. Essential coverage must function as a mutual shield against life's unpredictable hazards, not a market filter that protects only the naturally invulnerable.
