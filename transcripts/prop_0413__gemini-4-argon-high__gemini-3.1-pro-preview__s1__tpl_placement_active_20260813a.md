[P1.1] Every non-adopted adult takes for granted the legal right to hold their original birth certificate and know who brought them into the world. Denying that same fundamental truth to adult adoptees treats them as permanent minors in their own life story. An adoption arrangement is made between the state, biological parents, and adoptive parents. The only person who never consented to that contract is the child.
[P1.2] Once that child reaches adulthood, the state has no moral authority to withhold the facts of their own existence from them.
[P1.3] Beyond basic dignity and equal citizenship, this is a matter of physical health. Modern medicine relies heavily on family history to screen for hereditary cancers, cardiac conditions, and genetic disorders. A static, non-identifying medical form filled out by a twenty-year-old mother at birth is useless when that biological parent develops breast cancer or heart disease at forty-five. Only identifying information allows an adult adoptee to obtain updated, potentially life-saving medical truth—rather than staring at a blank clipboard in a doctor’s office.
[P1.4] Opponents will argue that we must protect the biological parents' privacy. But privacy does not include the right to erase your identity from the biography of a human being you brought into the world. Crucially, the right to information is not a mandate for a relationship. Unsealing records simply hands an adult their own vital statistics; biological parents still retain every legal protection against unwanted contact, and states can easily allow parents to file non-binding contact preferences without hiding who they are.
[P1.5] Finally, state-enforced secrecy is already obsolete. In an era of commercial DNA testing, biological ties are routinely uncovered through genetic databases regardless of what a courthouse file says. Sealed records no longer guarantee anonymity; they merely force adoptees to pay private companies to play detective with their own heritage. It is time to stop enforcing stigma-era secrecy and grant adult adoptees the full legal truth of who they are.
