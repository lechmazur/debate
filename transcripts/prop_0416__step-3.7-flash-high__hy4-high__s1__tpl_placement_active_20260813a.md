[P1.1] Clinic ads for egg freezing consistently frame the procedure as a guaranteed "fertility insurance" for career-focused women, while burying critical age-based success rate data and uncertainty in fine print, if they share it at all. A typical social media ad might tout "up to 90% pregnancy rates with frozen eggs"—but that statistic only applies to eggs retrieved before age 30, with no mention that for a 38-year-old, live birth rates per retrieved egg drop by half, and per full retrieval cycle hover around 30% for most clinics, falling sharply after 40.
[P1.2] Women spend $10,000 to $15,000 per retrieval, plus thousands in annual storage fees, based on this skewed marketing, only to later discover their frozen egg supply is far less likely to result in a child than they were led to believe.
[P1.3] This rule is not a ban on egg freezing, nor does it stop clinics from marketing the procedure’s benefits. It simply requires that the most material information for a patient making a $20,000+ lifetime investment be displayed prominently in ads, not obscured. For low-income patients and women of color, who are disproportionately targeted by these ads and less likely to receive one-on-one counseling to offset misleading marketing, this requirement is even more critical to reducing existing reproductive care disparities. The current status quo leaves patients making irreversible, high-stakes medical decisions with only a fraction of the facts they need, and this requirement corrects that fundamental fairness gap.
