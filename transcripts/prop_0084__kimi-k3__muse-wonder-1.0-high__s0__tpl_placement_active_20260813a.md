This happens constantly: consumer protection data in the US alone puts romance-scam losses at over a billion dollars a year, and that's only what gets reported — shame keeps most victims silent. Behind nearly every case sits the same design flaw: a platform that lets anyone invent an identity in ninety seconds.
[P1.2] That is what this motion fixes.
[P1.3] First, verification strikes at the mechanism, not the symptom. Fraud on dating apps depends on one thing — being someone else, cheaply and repeatedly. Scammers cycle through disposable profiles; ban one and they re-register in minutes. Binding an account to a verified identity — an ID check, a liveness selfie, a confirmed phone — destroys that business model, and it deters the stalkers and predators who rely on untraceable accounts too.
[P1.4] We already accept this logic everywhere else: banks verify you, bars check ID, rideshare drivers get screened. Dating apps are the glaring exception, and they are precisely where strangers are paired for intimacy.

Lost anonymity means the platform knows who you are — not your matches; verification can confirm identity privately while you stay pseudonymous to other users. [P1.6] CON is asking us to protect a privacy that barely exists, at the price of fraud that ruins lives.
[P1.7] Third, the word "require" matters. Apps won't do this voluntarily, because fake accounts inflate user counts and engagement. A mandatory standard removes the competitive penalty for doing the right thing.
[P1.8] Weigh this debate on who bears the cost. PRO asks users for a few minutes and a margin of anonymity. CON asks a vulnerable minority to keep absorbing billions in losses, stalking, and assault. There is no comparison.

[C1.3] The cost is not a few minutes. It is permanently linking legal identity to a sensitive record of whom you desire, reject, message, and meet. [C1.4] Mandates also shut out legitimate users: people without passports or licences, undocumented migrants, older users without suitable phones, and anyone a face-matching system misreads. [C1.5] Tools exist that target fraud without this blanket surrender: behavioural detection, warnings and friction around money requests, rapid action on reported patterns, and optional verification for those who want it. PRO instead imposes a permanent privacy and exclusion cost on millions, in hope of raising the workload of criminals already expert at faking identity. Minutes at sign-up can be refunded; an identity database, once built, exposes users for as long as it exists.
