[C1.1] The motion mistakes a slogan for a mechanism. "Share manufacturing technology" is not emailing a patent. It means transferring cell lines, process know-how, validation data, quality systems, suppliers, and trained people. A law can compel a license; it cannot conjure a sterile plant, a reliable input chain, or a regulator ready to approve.
[C1.2] The binding constraint in COVID was often not permission to use a formula but fill-finish capacity, inputs, staff, and regulatory bandwidth. A fixed legal deadline attacks the symbol of scarcity while leaving the machinery untouched.
[C1.3] Worse, the deadline bites at the worst moment: after emergency authorization, when a manufacturer is trying to scale and file approvals. That is when governments would force negotiations, litigation, and technology audits. The predictable result is not speed; it is distraction at peak urgency.
[C1.4] The mandate also reverses the incentive story. The choice is not "share or hoard"; it is "predictable rules or political expropriation." If success triggers compulsory transfer, firms will bargain defensively before the next crisis—delaying partnerships or demanding premiums. The public-funding argument proves too much: governments can attach licensing and access conditions to grants and advance purchase contracts.
[C1.5] That is a condition of the deal, not a retroactive seizure of process knowledge. Ex post compulsion makes future public-private bargains harder.
[C1.6] Voluntary licensing was imperfect, but it produced real partnerships—AstraZeneca with Serum, for example—and many failures were about funding, donations, and weak regional capacity, not just patents. The better remedy is funded technology partnerships, pre-negotiated licenses, and capacity investment.
[C1.7] The central comparison is simple. The proposition offers the appearance of action: a deadline and a duty. It delivers disputes, evasion, and a false sense that the know-how problem is solved. We can transfer technology faster by paying for it, contracting for it, and building the capacity to use it—not by mandating the recipe's surrender at the moment the recipe finally works.
