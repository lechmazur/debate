[P1.2] Community violence intervention programs put credible messengers — people from those very networks — into the places police cannot reach: the hospital waiting room after a shooting, the street corner where a feud is heating up, the funeral where payback is planned. Interrupters mediate conflicts before the trigger is pulled; outreach workers pull high-risk young men into services; hospital-based programs start the conversation at the bedside. Programs like Cure Violence sites in Chicago and Richmond's Advance Peace have been associated in evaluations with substantial reductions in shootings — often a third or more — at budgets that are a rounding error next to a police payroll.
[P1.3] Now price the alternative. [P1.4] Patrol deterrence is diffuse; officers arrive after shots are fired; clearance rates for nonfatal shootings are low; and in low-trust neighborhoods, a heavier police presence pushes witnesses into silence.
[P1.5] The motion asks about cost-effectiveness — shootings prevented per dollar.
