[P1.1] We do not fund fire departments because fires are likely tonight. We fund them because when the alarm rings, "we're researching combustion" is not an answer. That is precisely the posture planetary defense occupies today.
[P1.2] The proposition turns on a single asymmetry. Detection is fast; response is slow. A telescope can find a hazardous object in a night. Building, testing, integrating and launching a deflection mission takes years, and the deflection itself needs years more of travel and lead time, because nudging an asteroid works by applying a tiny velocity change far enough in advance that the miss distance grows to a planet's width.
[P1.3] So the useful question is not "can we deflect an asteroid" — DART already showed that a kinetic impactor measurably changes an asteroid's orbit. The question is whether, on the day we need it, the hardware, launch slot, trained team and legal authority already exist. Under a research-niche model, they do not. We would spend the warning window holding funding hearings.
[P1.4] Second, capability is perishable in a way knowledge is not. A niche produces papers, retirements, and cancelled follow-ons; the engineers who flew one demonstration disperse, the tooling is scrapped, the supply chain forgets. Standing infrastructure — a maintained interceptor design, a survey system that runs continuously, an agreed international decision protocol with pre-authorized triggers — is the only structure that survives a twenty-year quiet stretch and still works in year twenty-one.
[P1.5] Third, the cost comparison is not close. Sustained deflection readiness costs on the order of a modest defense program, while the downside it insures against is a city, a coastline, or a harvest year for the whole species. Nothing else in the disaster portfolio is fully preventable. We cannot stop an earthquake.
[P1.6] We can, uniquely, move an asteroid.
[P1.7] Chelyabinsk arrived unannounced and broke windows across a city. It was small, and we had zero warning. The relevant lesson is not that we got lucky. It is that luck is currently our architecture.
