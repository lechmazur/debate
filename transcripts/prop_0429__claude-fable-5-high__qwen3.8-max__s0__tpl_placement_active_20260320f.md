[P1.1] Picture a seventh grader who forgets his homework at 9:15 a.m. By 9:16, his mother's phone buzzes. By lunch, he's received three texts. He never got the chance to feel the small sting of the mistake, solve it himself, and walk out slightly more capable than he walked in.

[P1.3] Note what we defend: limits, not abolition. Parents should absolutely know about safety issues, serious discipline, and academic trends. What schools should refuse to build is a live ticker of childhood — apps pinging parents each time a child talks out of turn, loses a "point," or gets marked off on a color chart. That design choice converts parenting into surveillance and school into a performance monitored in real time.
[P1.4] Three harms follow. First, developmental: childhood is where kids practice handling small failures autonomously. Real-time dashboards strip out the delay that lets a child own a problem before an adult intervenes. Constant external monitoring teaches children to behave for the notification, not from internal values — and psychologists have long warned that heavy surveillance undermines intrinsic motivation and inflames anxiety in already-anxious kids.
[P1.5] Second, relational: teachers become data-entry clerks logging behavior instead of teaching, and every classroom judgment becomes instantly contestable by a parent mid-lesson. Trust between home and school erodes into second-guessing.
[P1.6] Third, equity and accuracy: minute-by-minute behavior logging amplifies snap judgments. A momentary teacher impression becomes a permanent, parent-visible data point, and children who are fidgety, neurodivergent, or already over-disciplined bear the heaviest scrutiny at home too — sometimes with harsh consequences schools never see.
[P1.7] The burden here matters. We need only show that unlimited, real-time behavior streaming does more harm than thoughtful limits — weekly summaries, threshold-based alerts, human conversations for serious issues. That keeps parents informed while giving children the one thing dashboards destroy: room to grow.
