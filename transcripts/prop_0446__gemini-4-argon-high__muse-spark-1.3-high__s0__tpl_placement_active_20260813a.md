[P1.1] Voluntary climate diplomacy has hit a wall on coal. Unabated coal remains the single largest source of power-sector emissions, yet major economies keep burning it because of a classic collective action trap. No industrializing nation wants to unilaterally retire reliable baseload power if doing so hands a manufacturing cost advantage to rivals. Consequently, vague, non-binding pledges to merely "phase down" coal let nations delay endlessly, hedging against one another while locking in catastrophic warming and deadly local air pollution.
[P1.2] A binding international agreement on a common timeline breaks this stalemate. When the largest economies lock into a synchronized exit, the fear of unilateral competitive disadvantage disappears. Furthermore, a binding timeline sends an unmistakable, irreversible signal to global capital markets. Instead of guessing how long coal fleets might linger, investors, insurers, and utilities get total regulatory certainty.
[P1.3] That immediately redirects massive private capital away from coal life-extension and into grid-scale storage, transmission lines, and renewables—driving down clean energy costs through guaranteed global scale.
[P1.4] Crucially, a common timeline is politically viable because this proposal pairs it with differentiated support. Major economies do not start from the same baseline. While wealthy nations mostly operate aging coal plants near the end of their lifespans, emerging giants like India or Indonesia rely on young fleets with decades of unrecovered capital costs and surging electricity demand. Asking them to strand those assets alone is neither fair nor realistic.
[P1.5] Differentiated support bridges this divide: wealthier economies provide concessional finance, technology transfers, and transition funds to buy out coal contracts early and retrain workers, in exchange for enforceable retirement dates.
[P1.6] This grand bargain fixes the failures of current piecemeal deals, which routinely stall over murky obligations and ad hoc funding. By trading guaranteed financial backing for a shared, binding deadline, major economies can finally retire the dirtiest fuel on earth without sacrificing grid reliability or economic growth.
