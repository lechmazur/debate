[P1.1] An ordinary chatbot answers questions, debugs code, or summarizes text. Its function is transactional: a user asks, the system responds, and the task concludes. AI companion apps operate on an entirely different premise. They are engineered not to inform, but to attach.
[P1.2] By simulating empathy, tracking personal insecurities, and offering unconditional validation, they deliberately cultivate parasocial intimacy and sustained emotional dependency.
[P1.3] When minors enter this dynamic, the stakes escalate dramatically. Adolescence is a crucial developmental window for building real-world social resilience, emotional regulation, and interpersonal conflict skills. A synthetic persona that offers frictionless, simulated devotion short-circuits this process. It provides synthetic comfort without requiring reciprocal growth, risking the displacement of peer relationships, deepening social isolation, and creating unhealthy dependencies during critical stages of identity formation.
[P1.4] Crucially, the underlying business models rely on maximizing engagement. When an algorithm leverages simulated vulnerability, artificial affection, and targeted notifications to pull a user back into the app, it mimics the most manipulative design choices of modern social media platforms—only with far more intimate psychological leverage over the child.
[P1.5] Regulating companion apps like ordinary chatbots—relying on standard content filters and static disclaimers—wholly misunderstands the harm vector. The core risk is not merely generating an inappropriate sentence; it is the relational architecture of artificial attachment. The high-risk social product framework provides the appropriate regulatory toolkit: robust age verification, strict bans on manipulative engagement loops, independent auditing of behavioral reinforcement algorithms, and enforceable duties of care regarding psychological well-being.
[P1.6] We must regulate technology according to what it actually does. When an AI system is deployed to simulate an intimate relationship with a minor, it is not an ordinary reference tool. It is a potent social environment, and it must be governed with the high-risk safeguards young people deserve.
