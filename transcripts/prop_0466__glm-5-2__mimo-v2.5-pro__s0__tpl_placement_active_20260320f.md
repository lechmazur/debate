Large no-take zones protect spawning biomass and age structure inside their boundaries; larvae and adult fish migrate beyond them. This spillover isn't speculation—it's the core mechanism by which reserves pay for themselves.
[P1.3] Fishers near reserve edges routinely catch more per unit effort than those in continuously exploited areas. The reserve becomes an engine of productivity, not a hole in the fishery.

[P1.6] The 30% threshold matters because smaller reserves don't capture enough of key species' ranges to generate meaningful spillover. Token reserves are political compromises, not biological solutions. Thirty percent is where protection begins to shift ecosystem dynamics, not just protect individual patches.
[P1.7] This proposition doesn't dismiss coastal hardship. It demands that governments pair reserve creation with transition support—alternative livelihoods, compensation, phased implementation. The PRO side waves this away with talk of "transition support," but such compensation programs are historically underfunded, inconsistently delivered, and frequently absent altogether in the developing nations where fishing dependence is greatest.
