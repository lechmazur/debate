[P1.1] A headline target is not a statistic. It is a steering wheel. It decides which memo reaches the finance minister, which department gets the win, and which failures are legible enough to cost a government votes. So the question is not "is GDP a useful number" — of course it is, and it stays in the dashboard.
[P1.2] The question is what a government should be *held to*. And on that, GDP is a poor master.
[P1.3] GDP counts the volume of transactions, not whether life got better. A country can post strong growth while working hours lengthen, commutes stretch, loneliness deepens, and life expectancy stalls — the United States managed exactly that through years of the opioid epidemic, with rising output alongside rising deaths of despair. GDP rewards the traffic jam and the oil-spill cleanup, and ignores the parent caring for a disabled child. Even Kuznets, who helped build the accounts, warned they should not be mistaken for national welfare.
[P1.4] Treating a production ledger as the aim of statecraft is a category error we have simply grown used to.
[P1.5] The practical consequence is misallocation. Under a GDP headline, preventive mental health care, air quality enforcement, or reducing chronic pain look like costs.
