[P1.1] Frontier general-purpose AI is the first technology where the same artifact can write code, design proteins, manipulate elections, and be fine-tuned into a weapon—all after the developer’s risk assessment is filed. Risk-based regulation cannot govern that. It asks firms to predict harms, document mitigations, and disclose; it acts after the model is already in the EU market. For frontier models, the relevant failures are irreversible: leaked weights cannot be recalled, and a jailbroken model cannot be un-deployed.
[P1.2] The EU should therefore require a license before deployment.
[P1.3] Licensing is not a ban and not bureaucracy for every chatbot. It is a pre-market gate for models above clear capability or compute thresholds, with conditions set by an independent regulator: red-team results, dangerous-capability evaluations, weight-security plans, incident reporting, and a legal duty to suspend or withdraw if conditions fail. That turns unverifiable promises into enforceable permissions. If a developer cannot show its model is safe enough to deploy, the default should be no deployment—not a fine after the damage.
[P1.4] The risk-based approach suffers a structural flaw: it delegates risk definition to the regulated firm. The firm knows more about the model than the regulator and has incentives to frame risk narrowly. Licensing reverses that asymmetry by making access to the EU market conditional on independent scrutiny. It also creates a single, predictable checkpoint for global developers, rather than 27 inconsistent national interpretations.
[P1.5] Opponents will warn about innovation and open source. But licensing can be tiered, time-limited, and appealable; it targets deployment, not research. Aviation and medicines show that pre-market approval and innovation can coexist. The question is not whether Europe regulates AI, but whether it regulates before deployment or after catastrophe.
[P1.6] Frontier models are too general, too dual-use, and too irreversible for self-certified risk management. License them first.
