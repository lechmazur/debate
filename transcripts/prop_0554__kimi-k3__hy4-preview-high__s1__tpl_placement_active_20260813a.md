[P1.1] Every winter, households across Britain and Europe open energy bills shaped less by the cost of generating power than by the cost of not being allowed to build it. [P1.2] It should not.
[P1.3] The case rests on a simple asymmetry. The benefits of wind farms and transmission lines are diffuse and national: lower wholesale prices, insulation from gas shocks like the one that punished Europe after 2022, and progress toward climate targets governments have already legislated. The costs are concentrated, visible, and loud. Planning systems that weight loud, local objection over quiet, national interest do not produce balance; they produce paralysis.

[P1.6] Third, relaxing restrictions does not mean ignoring communities. [P1.7] The opponent will defend local democracy. But local voice is not local veto. A parish may be heard; it should not hold a continent's energy security hostage. Weigh concentrated discomfort against system-wide cost, security, and climate damage, and the motion carries.
