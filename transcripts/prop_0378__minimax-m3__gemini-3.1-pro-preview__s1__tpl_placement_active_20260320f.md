[P1.1] Loneliness has crossed from personal trouble into public crisis. The U.S. Surgeon General's 2023 advisory warned that social isolation carries mortality risks comparable to smoking roughly a dozen cigarettes a day, and that close to half of American adults report measurable loneliness. That is not a private misfortune suffered by a few; it is a population-level condition with downstream costs in healthcare, mental health, and workforce productivity.
[P1.2] The proposition's premise is therefore already conceded by the data: adult friendship is hard enough to sustain that something must respond. The real question is whether that response will be deliberate or accidental. Right now, it is overwhelmingly accidental, and badly so.
[P1.3] Consider the institutions already named in the motion. Workplaces did not stay neutral on friendship; they restructured around remote software, "productivity" surveillance, and gig scheduling, and the casual coworker bond that sustained millions of adults for decades thinned with them. Cities did not stay neutral either; mid-century zoning starved out the corner store, the diner, and the public square, the very "third places" sociologists identified as friendship infrastructure. Public institutions funded highways, suburban mortgages, and digital networks that pulled adults into longer commutes and longer solitary screen hours.
[P1.4] So the choice is not between policy and privacy. Institutions are already in the friendship business. The motion asks whether they should do it well. That means workplaces designing real gathering space and humane schedules instead of optimizing connection away; cities funding libraries, parks, plazas, and mixed-use neighborhoods that manufacture accidental encounters; and public institutions treating social connection the way they already treat clean air, safe roads, and public health, because the costs of neglect already show up on their balance sheets.
[P1.5] Refusing to act is not the neutral position. It is the decision to keep letting institutions hollow out the conditions for adult friendship while pretending the damage is private.
