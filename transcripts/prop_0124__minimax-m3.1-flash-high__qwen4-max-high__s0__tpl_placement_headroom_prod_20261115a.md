[P1.1] Medical aid in dying should be legal for competent adults whose terminal or degenerative suffering is unbearable. The stake is bodily autonomy: when illness makes life intolerable, a person should not be forced to endure agony because society fears abuse. This does not mean anyone may choose death on impulse. Strict safeguards distinguish a deliberate decision from coercion, incapacity, or treatable depression.
[P1.2] The law must therefore require more than a signature. Eligibility should depend on clear terminal disease or advanced degeneration; decision-making capacity; a voluntary, repeated, informed request; independent confirmation of diagnosis, prognosis, and the absence of realistic relief; and discussion of palliative care, pain control, and hospice. Independent review, a waiting period, documentation, and case oversight would make errors visible and correctable. Reporting should expose irregularities.
[P1.3] This framework addresses fears better than a blanket ban. A ban does not prevent exploitation; it can drive patients toward more dangerous methods and leave families facing deaths without lawful relief. Safeguards, by contrast, target dangers. A regulated system is not automatically safer than a prohibition that forces suffering into the shadows.
[P1.4] Consider a conscious patient with irreversible degeneration, whose pain and loss of autonomy cannot be adequately controlled, and who repeatedly asks to die. If competent adults are forbidden to help, neither personal faith nor the state may override their considered wish; only an injunction against serious pain can. A bounded option also strengthens trust in medicine: patients know relief is available within transparent rules.
[P1.5] The question is not whether death can ever be a terrible choice. Clearly it can. It is whether the law should leave suffering people with no lawful, supervised way to exercise their judgment about their own bodies. A narrow right, protected by demanding safeguards and public oversight, respects autonomy without opening a door to indiscriminate killing.
