[P1.1] A rideshare driver’s five-star record is not a gift from the app. It is proof of on-time trips, careful driving, and satisfied customers. Today that proof dies the moment the driver tries to leave. Rivals see a blank profile, not a professional.
[P1.2] The result is not a free market. It is a captive one.
[P1.3] That captivity is why gig platforms should be required to let workers port ratings and verified work history through standardized data-transfer tools. The duty is narrow. It does not set wages, dictate algorithms, or seize a brand. It says the record of work a person performed must travel with the person who performed it.
[P1.4] Without portability, ratings are a switching cost. A worker who spent years building a score cannot afford to test a rival that pays better or handles deactivation with basic process. Incumbents can worsen terms and still keep the labor, because starting over means fewer jobs, lower earnings, and algorithmic suspicion of “new” accounts. That is lock-in dressed up as trust.
[P1.5] Customers lose as well. A new platform cannot match skilled workers if every experienced courier arrives looking unproven. Competition then fails exactly where it would discipline fees and conditions. Standardized transfer repairs that information failure without asking anyone to take a worker’s word for it.
[P1.6] Volume, job category, and platform of origin can travel with the score, so context survives and fabrication gets harder, not easier.
[P1.7] We already accept this logic. Phone numbers became portable because carriers had turned a customer’s own identity into a barrier to exit. In ordinary jobs, a résumé, references, and licenses do not belong to the last employer. Gig ratings are the résumé.
[P1.8] Platforms may host them. They should not confiscate them.
[P1.9] The alternative is permanent probation for anyone who switches. That is not quality control. It is a moat.

[C1.3] It manufactures a misleading credential.
[C1.4] That misdirection has victims. Passengers, diners, and families hiring through an app rely on ratings as safety signals, not LinkedIn flair. Forcing platforms to accept imported scores pressures them to trust strangers vetted by someone else's standards — standards they cannot audit and may be looser, gamed, or inflated. [C1.5] The mandate would also punish the very investment it exploits. Building deactivation review, anti-brush systems, identity verification costs money. Competition in trust dies so competition in labor can pretend to live.
[C1.6] And there is no captivity to cure. Gig workers already multi-home with two phones on the dash; signup takes days, not years. New platforms win with bonuses, guaranteed earnings, and trials — not by inheriting stars.
[C1.7] Standardization would freeze today's metrics in place, tax startups with compliance costs only incumbents can absorb, and chain workers to one bad stretch forever, with past bias and one angry customer following them everywhere.
[C1.8] Portability does not free workers. It falsifies trust, subsidizes free riders, and sells safety for the illusion of mobility.
