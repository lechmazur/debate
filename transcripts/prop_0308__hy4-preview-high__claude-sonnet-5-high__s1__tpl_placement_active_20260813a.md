[P1.1] Populism does not begin as a policy agenda; it begins as a claim. It says that society is divided between a virtuous, homogeneous "people" and a corrupt, alien elite, and that anyone who mediates between them—parties, courts, the press, election officials—is part of the problem. That framing is the first symptom of democratic decay, because liberal democracy depends precisely on those mediators to make majority rule compatible with minority rights and peaceful transfers of power.
[P1.2] The real elite failures populists name are often genuine. Corruption, inequality, unresponsive parties, and technocratic drift are real. But a healthy corrective strengthens the democratic immune system: independent prosecutors, free media, civil society. Populism’s usual response is to treat the immune system as the disease.
[P1.3] When populists win, they rarely stop at defeating the old elite; they convert a temporary majority into durable dominance.
[P1.4] Hungary is the clearest case. Fidesz came to power amid legitimate anger at corruption and mismanagement. It then used a supermajority to rewrite the constitution, curtail the constitutional court, and bring public media under political control. Poland’s Law and Justice similarly turned public broadcasting into a government instrument and reshaped judicial appointments.
[P1.5] Even in a stronger democracy, the Trump movement’s assault on election administration and the attack on the Capitol showed how quickly populist majorities can be taught to distrust the referees rather than their side.
[P1.6] The decisive question is not whether elites failed. They often do. It is what happens to the institutions that let citizens correct failure without violence. Populism’s moral claim—that "the people" can bypass checks in their own name—makes backsliding more likely, not less.
[P1.7] Once leaders label courts, journalists, and electoral commissions as enemies of the people, every subsequent power grab is justified as the popular will. That is why populist movements are usually a warning sign of decay and backsliding, not a healthy corrective to elite failure.
