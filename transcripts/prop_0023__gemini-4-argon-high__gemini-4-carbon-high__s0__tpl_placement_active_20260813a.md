[P1.1] Financial regulation must govern economic reality, not the database technology used to record transactions. On most cryptocurrency platforms, the vast majority of traded assets are not true decentralized currencies; they are investment contracts. Retail buyers purchase tokens promoted by identifiable teams, banking on those developers' managerial and technical efforts to drive up the price. This creates the exact information asymmetry that securities laws were built to solve.
[P1.2] Calling a stake in a software venture a "token" does not eliminate the investor's need for standardized disclosures, audited financials, and protections against insider manipulation.
[P1.3] Even more dangerous is how these platforms operate. Traditional securities law strictly separates the exchange matching orders, the broker routing client trades, the market maker providing liquidity, and the custodian holding the assets. Most crypto platforms collapse all four functions under one roof. By evading exchange and broker-dealer registration, these platforms routinely commingle customer deposits, list unvetted assets, and trade against their own customers using internal or affiliated market-making desks.
[P1.4] The most devastating collapses in the crypto industry were not novel failures of cryptography; they were classic financial frauds of self-dealing, hidden leverage, and misappropriated client funds. Broker-dealer net capital rules, strict asset segregation, and exchange conflict-of-interest bans are designed specifically to prevent this predation.
[P1.5] Carving out a distinct, lightly supervised category does not modernize finance; it institutionalizes regulatory arbitrage. If intermediaries can bypass strict custody rules, surveillance requirements, and anti-manipulation laws simply by settling trades on a blockchain, the financial sector will race to the bottom. Issuers and brokers would have every incentive to repackage standard securities as digital tokens to escape rigorous oversight, degrading capital markets as a whole. The governing principle must remain simple: same economic risk, same regulatory standard.
[P1.6] Because most crypto platforms function as conflicted, vertically integrated exchanges and brokerages trading speculative investments, bringing them inside the battle-tested securities framework is the only way to protect retail investors and ensure honest markets.
