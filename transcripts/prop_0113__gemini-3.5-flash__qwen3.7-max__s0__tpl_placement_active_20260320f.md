[P1.1] The current global packaging crisis is a textbook example of market failure. For decades, major beverage and packaging producers have operated under a highly profitable but deeply flawed model: they privatize the revenue from their products while socializing the massive costs of cleaning up the resulting waste. By leaving municipal taxpayers to foot the bill for collecting, sorting, and processing discarded packaging, the current system subsidizes corporate pollution. We must transition to a system of Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), where producers bear the true cost of their packaging’s lifecycle.
[P1.2] First, shifting these costs fixes a fundamental economic misalignment. Currently, manufacturers have zero financial incentive to design sustainable packaging. Because taxpayers cover disposal costs regardless of how difficult a package is to recycle, corporations continue to flood the market with cheap, unrecyclable multi-layer plastics. When producers are forced to pay for the end-of-life management of their packaging, waste becomes a corporate liability.
[P1.3] This financial pressure directly incentivizes them to design lighter, highly recyclable, or reusable packaging to lower their own costs.
[P1.4] Second, the status quo is an unfair and regressive tax. Local governments are facing skyrocketing waste management costs, forcing them to either raise taxes or cut funding for essential public services like schools and infrastructure. Taxpayers have absolutely no control over the packaging materials used by global beverage giants, yet they are forced to subsidize their cleanup. It is both economically logical and morally fair that the entities that profit from creating packaging should be responsible for its disposal.
[P1.5] By aligning financial responsibility with environmental impact, we relieve strained public budgets and force a rapid evolution in packaging design. We must hold producers accountable.

[P2.5] Finally, the opposition argues that the true bottleneck is "end-market demand" [C1.6]. But who creates that demand? Currently, municipal taxpayers have no power to buy recycled plastics. It is the packaging producers who must buy these materials to make new bottles.
