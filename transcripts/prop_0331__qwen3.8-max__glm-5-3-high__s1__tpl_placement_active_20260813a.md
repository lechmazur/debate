[P1.1] Loot boxes are not just digital surprise toys; they are a gambling interface aimed at children. A player spends real money, receives a randomized outcome, and hopes for a prize that is deliberately presented as valuable. That is the core architecture of gambling: consideration, chance, and reward. The reward may be a skin, a character, or a power-up, but to the player it has real value—social status, competitive advantage, or tradable scarcity.
[P1.2] When that loop is sold to minors, the state should regulate it as gambling. Calling it a “surprise mechanic” does not change what the child experiences: staking money on an uncertain outcome.
[P1.3] The decisive issue is vulnerability. Minors are still developing impulse control and risk assessment. Randomized rewards use variable-ratio reinforcement—the same mechanism behind slot machines—where unpredictable wins encourage repeated spending. Games add sensory cues, near-misses, limited-time offers, and social pressure.
[P1.4] A child who buys one loot crate is not making a calm consumer choice; they are being shaped into a spender by design. If we would not let a casino sell slot-machine pulls to twelve-year-olds, we should not let a game company sell the same psychological loop under the label “in-game content.”
[P1.5] Regulation also fixes information asymmetry. Gambling rules demand odds disclosure, spending controls, age verification, and restrictions on predatory advertising. Those tools are exactly what minors need here: published probabilities, hard spending caps, no paid manipulation of children’s attention, and meaningful enforcement. Parents cannot police every microtransaction, especially when games disguise chance as gameplay.
[P1.6] CON may say virtual items are not money. That misses the point. The proposition concerns minors and randomized paid rewards. Even if cash-out is absent, the habit formation, financial harm, and deceptive design are real.
[P1.7] For children, the safest and clearest rule is: paid chance-based prizes are gambling, and they deserve gambling protections.
