[P1.1] A street name is not a textbook. When a government carves a name onto a school, a boulevard, or a national holiday, it is not recording history — it is conferring honor, today, with public authority. That distinction drives this entire debate.
[P1.2] CON will defend "historical continuity," but continuity conflates two different acts. Preserving history means archives, museums, curricula, plaques that explain context. Venerating a person means putting his name above the door. Germany teaches the Third Reich rigorously and keeps concentration camps as memorials; it has no Hitler avenues.
[P1.3] Russia renamed Leningrad and Stalingrad; not one page of history was lost. From Constantinople to Mumbai to Zimbabwe, societies constantly update whom they celebrate. Renaming is not amnesia — it is the normal process by which nations decide what their present stands for.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, the cost of keeping these names falls on real people. A Black child in the American South walking into a school named for a Confederate general who fought to keep her ancestors enslaved absorbs a daily message about whose comfort her community values. Descendants of colonized peoples paying taxes to maintain a statue of the man who dispossessed their grandparents are funding their own humiliation. "Continuity" demands they carry that cost forever so others avoid the mild inconvenience of learning a new address.
[P1.5] Notice the motion is carefully limited: names that *primarily* honor colonizers, slaveholders, or dictators. This is not a purge of every flawed figure — that slippery slope is the fantasy CON needs you to fear. Washington owned slaves but is honored chiefly for founding a republic; a Confederate general is honored chiefly for defending slavery. Communities can draw that line case by case, democratically, with museums and markers absorbing the history as they go.
[P1.6] The question tonight is not whether we remember the past. It is whether our present honors reflect our present values. Inertia is not an argument. Vote PRO.
