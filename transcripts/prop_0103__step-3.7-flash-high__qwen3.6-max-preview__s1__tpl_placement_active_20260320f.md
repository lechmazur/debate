[P1.1] The current U.S. system of point-of-sale firearm screening is a patchwork of gaps that lets prohibited buyers easily acquire guns, while doing nothing to reduce accidental harm from untrained owners. [P1.2] This “private sale loophole” is not a minor edge case: in 2015, Dylann Roof, who was barred from firearm possession due to a prior drug arrest, purchased a handgun from a private South Carolina seller with no background check, then used it to murder nine Black churchgoers in the Charleston Emanuel AME shooting. Roof slipped through the cracks of point-of-sale screening not because of a failure of individual dealers, but because the system itself does not cover non-commercial transfers. A federal licensing system fixes this structural flaw by requiring every firearm buyer to present a valid, verified license for any transfer, public or private.
[P1.3] The license application process includes a standardized, comprehensive background check that closes gaps in the current patchwork of state reporting to the National Instant Criminal Background Check System, ensuring prohibited persons cannot acquire licenses. Mandatory safety training for license holders reduces the hundreds of accidental firearm deaths that occur in the U.S. each year, most involving children, by ensuring owners know how to safely store, handle, and use their weapons. This system does not restrict gun ownership for law-abiding citizens; it simply sets a baseline standard to keep guns out of dangerous hands and reduce preventable harm.
