[P1.1] The decisive question in 2022 was never whether the West would help Ukraine. It was when. Aid that arrived in summer and autumn saved Ukraine from collapse, but aid that arrived in March and April could have changed the war’s geometry.
[P1.2] Russia’s invasion had a vulnerable window. Its columns were strung out, its logistics fragile, its units understrength and its occupation thin. Every month of delay let Moscow dig in, mobilize, rebuild and learn.
[P1.3] The pattern is visible. When Western long-range systems finally arrived in summer 2022, Ukraine immediately began destroying Russian ammunition depots and command posts, forcing dispersal and enabling the Kharkiv and Kherson advances. Faster air defense would have blunted missile terror; faster armor and engineering equipment could have helped Ukraine breach Russian lines before dense minefields and layered defenses became the norm.
[P1.4] The objection that aid could not have moved faster because of logistics and training is partly true, but it confuses difficulty with impossibility. [P1.5] Speed signals resolve; delay invites testing.
[P1.6] Ukraine today is weaker because it spent 2022 paying in lives for weapons that arrived after the enemy had adapted.
