[P1.1] Antibiotics are the only medicine that gets weaker the more we use it. Every dose is a withdrawal from a shared account that no one can refill on demand. That makes the central question of this debate not "what does meat cost?" but "which use of a depleting resource do we protect when we cannot protect them all?" And prophylactic dosing of animals that are not sick is, by any honest ranking, the lowest-value use we have — a substitute for cleaner barns, lower stocking density, and later weaning.
[P1.2] Notice what prophylaxis actually is. It is not treating a sick pig. It is putting a low, sustained dose into the feed or water of thousands of healthy animals at once — precisely the conditions that select for resistant bacteria most efficiently, because you kill the susceptible strains and leave the survivors to breed in a warm, crowded, continuously restocked host population. Those bacteria and their resistance genes then travel out on meat, in manure spread on fields, into water, and into hospitals.
[P1.3] A grandmother with a urinary tract infection that no longer responds to first-line drugs never bought that bacon, and never consented to the trade.
[P1.4] The asymmetry is what decides this. Higher meat prices are painful, reversible, and can be offset by policy — food assistance, transition subsidies, tariff-side adjustment. Lost drug efficacy is none of those things. When a class of antibiotics stops working, no subsidy brings it back, and routine surgery, chemotherapy, and neonatal care all get more dangerous.
[P1.5] This is also a textbook case where markets and voluntary pledges cannot work. The individual producer captures the whole yield benefit and bears roughly none of the resistance cost. Only a ban internalizes it.
[P1.6] And we know the sky does not fall: European countries that curbed routine growth and preventive dosing kept producing and exporting pork at scale, absorbing modest costs through better husbandry rather than collapse.
[P1.7] We can afford more expensive meat. We cannot afford a post-antibiotic hospital.
