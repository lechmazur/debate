[P1.1] The motion sets a modest bar: meaningful reduction, not elimination; no large increase in discrimination or shortages, not zero. Hold both sides to that bar.
[P1.2] Start with mechanism. Unauthorized employment runs on a simple weakness: employers currently accept documents that look valid, and a convincing fake costs a few hundred dollars. E-Verify checks the name, date of birth, and Social Security number against federal records, so a fabricated number fails and a borrowed one triggers mismatches and duplicate-use flags. That doesn't make the system perfect, but it collapses the easiest path.
[P1.3] The current patchwork, where only some states and federal contractors must use it, lets work simply migrate to non-participating employers. A nationwide mandate closes that exit.
[P1.4] The best real-world test is Arizona, which mandated E-Verify for all employers in 2008. Research on that period found the state's unauthorized population fell by a substantial share, roughly a fifth or more, and that unauthorized workers were pushed out of formal wage employment, with wages for competing low-skilled legal workers edging up. That is what "meaningful" looks like in practice.
[P1.5] On discrimination: the system returns a tentative nonconfirmation to the employer, and workers get a window to resolve it. Erroneous nonconfirmation rates for authorized workers are low, well under one percent. Arizona, Georgia, Alabama, and Mississippi did not see explosions of civil rights litigation after their mandates. Discrimination exists today precisely because employers eyeball documents and make guesses; a uniform database check replaces that subjective judgment with a rule that applies to everyone.
[P1.6] On shortages: these states did not lose their construction or hospitality sectors. Employers adjusted by raising wages and recruiting legal workers, which is the point. Where genuine gaps appeared, like agriculture, the answer is expanding legal visa channels, not keeping document fraud as unofficial labor policy.
[P1.7] The question is whether we want immigration law enforced at the worksite rationally or not at all.
