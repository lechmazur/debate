[P1.3] Three mechanisms make this worse than the non-personalized alternative.
[P1.4] First, personalization removes escape. A chronological feed serves the same content to everyone; what you see is bounded by what exists in your network. An optimized feed learns that a fourteen-year-old lingers on diet videos, or self-harm content, or clips mocking people like her, and it serves more of exactly that—recursively, indefinitely. The harm is not just exposure; it's the amplification of the individual's specific vulnerability.
[P1.5] Second, engagement metrics reward negative affect. Outrage, envy, and anxiety produce longer sessions than calm content. A system trained on watch time is, functionally, trained on whatever keeps teenagers emotionally activated—and for adolescents, that state is distress.
[P1.6] Third, the variable-reward loop—each swipe potentially delivering something thrilling—exploits the still-developing prefrontal control of teenagers, displacing sleep and face-to-face contact, both established protective factors against depression.
[P1.7] Notice what the defense must claim: that serving infinite, individually targeted, negativity-amplifying content to developing minds is no worse than a bounded list of posts. That claim is not plausible, and the burden is on anyone making it.
