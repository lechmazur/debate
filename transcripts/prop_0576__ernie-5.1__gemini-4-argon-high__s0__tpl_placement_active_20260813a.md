[P1.1] Tax‑exempt status is not a right of belief; it is a public subsidy granted because the organization serves a charitable or spiritual purpose that benefits society at large. When a church, mosque, or faith‑based charity steps out of that role and becomes an overt partisan actor—endorsing candidates, funding campaign ads, organizing rally‑style voter drives for a single party—it converts a public benefit into private political advantage. That conversion must trigger loss of the tax break, for three reasons that together form the heart of the case.
[P1.2] First, fairness demands it. Secular 501(c)(3) charities that cross the line into partisan campaigning automatically forfeit their exemption, and they are subject to the same IRS scrutiny as any political committee. Allowing religious bodies to keep the exemption while engaging in identical activity creates a two‑tier nonprofit system: faith groups subsidize elections while secular groups do not. The principle of equal treatment is a constitutional bedrock; privileging one class of speaker because of its religious label is arbitrary.
[P1.3] Second, democratic integrity is at stake. Tax‑exempt dollars are effectively government money. When they fund candidate‑specific outreach—think of megachurches that have hosted “prayer rallies” that doubled as campaign events, or of religious coalitions that spend millions on partisan voter‑targeting—public resources are being funneled into the electoral process without voter accountability. Removing the exemption draws a clear line: you may speak, you may pray, you may lobby on policy, but you may not use tax‑exempt coffers to tilt an election.
[P1.4] Third, the premise does not chill speech; it merely withdraws a financial benefit. Religious groups retain every constitutional right to express political views, to lobby, to organize. They simply lose the special fiscal privilege that is meant to reward charitable work, not electioneering. The motion therefore protects the original intent of tax‑exempt status while preventing its exploitation as a partisan war chest.
