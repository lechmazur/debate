[P1.1] Every lecture is a door to knowledge. The only real question is who must justify keeping it locked. Today, a student who cannot hear, see, process, or attend must disclose a diagnosis, wait for an accommodation letter, and hope each instructor complies; access is a favor granted late, if at all. The motion reverses that.
[P1.2] It makes captions, recordings, and accessible materials the baseline and makes the institution justify withholding them by a strong pedagogical reason. That allocation is right: access is the precondition of learning, not a bonus.
[P1.3] The reactive model fails on its own terms. Many students never disclose a disability because of stigma or cost, and even when they do, materials arrive late, inconsistently, or not at all. The accommodation frame also hides the larger benefit. Captions and recordings help far more than a diagnosed minority: they help second-language learners, commuters, students with migraines, ADHD, or anxiety, and anyone who wants to rewind a dense proof or search a transcript.
[P1.4] A recording is office hours that never close; captions turn mumbled speech into reviewable text. That is universal design.
[P1.5] Faculty objections are already answered by the motion’s exception. Auto-captioning and lecture capture are routine and low-cost; the remaining workload is modest. Where recording would chill sensitive discussion, breach confidentiality, or make live spontaneity essential, the “strong pedagogical reason” clause permits refusal. Opponents cannot defeat a presumption by citing edge cases the presumption already excludes; they must show exclusion should be the default.
[P1.6] A university that locks lectures behind paperwork makes knowledge conditional on disclosure and luck. Presumptive access ends that lottery. It matches existing legal duties in many systems and wastes far less talent. Judge the motion not by hypothetical abuses the exception blocks, but by the ordinary student it currently fails: the one falling behind because a caption arrived late or never came.
