[P1.1] Social contributions should follow economic control, not the thinnest contract. Large firms source workers through labor platforms, agencies, and subcontracting chains where intermediaries carry slender liability while the lead company fixes prices, schedules, metrics, and access to future work. Workers remain real: they get sick, age, have accidents, and need public support when earnings are unstable. Today the strongest actor keeps flexibility and pushes the insurance bill onto small intermediaries, workers, and taxpayers.
[P1.2] Requiring a higher share of social contributions from large companies using those arrangements corrects the mismatch. It is not a ban on outsourcing or a wage code for every contractor. It says a powerful firm that designs work around discontinuity and layered responsibility must pay more into unemployment, health, pension, and injury protection than if it employed the workforce directly. The levy prices externalized risk and makes boards ask whether a courier, picker, cleaner, or care worker should be employed, trained, and covered.
[P1.3] That incentive beats another transparency form. Contribution rates alter the cheapest-bid calculation: fewer artificial layers, longer contracts, better scheduling, real checks before work is handed down the chain. Picture two large retailers in one EU market: one staffs logistics with regular employees; the other routes identical tasks through apps and thin-margin subcontractors. The second looks efficient only because part of its labor cost is displaced.
[P1.4] A higher share makes competition rest on productivity and service, not on who sheds obligations most cleverly.
[P1.5] Flexibility is predictable. But flexibility bought by making someone else uninsured is arbitrage, not innovation. The rule targets large companies because they set the chain, capture the gains, and can absorb the charge. Firms using platforms to complement decent employment can pay; firms using them to hollow out employment should stop receiving a discount.
[P1.6] The EU should make responsibility travel with power.

[C1.7] PRO wants responsibility to travel with power. Fine — make the lead firm liable for the contributions that exist. Don't invent a new one nobody receives.
