[P1.1] The replication crisis is not a theoretical concern—it is a documented reality that has forced major journals to retract landmark papers and forced policymakers to reconsider interventions built on foundations that crumbled under scrutiny. When only around half of widely cited psychology findings replicate, when numerous high-profile medical claims collapse under confirmatory testing, we are not dealing with isolated bad actors. We are confronting a systemic failure: the research enterprise has been optimized to discover, but not to verify.
[P1.2] This is a classic market failure. Individual researchers chase novelty because novelty is rewarded—publish or perish rewards the striking result, not the careful confirmation. Scientists rationally pursue their own interests, but the collective outcome is a literature riddled with false positives, overestimated effects, and findings that cannot survive contact with independent scrutiny. Private incentives do not produce collective verification.
[P1.3] That is precisely why public intervention is justified.
[P1.4] Public research funders occupy a unique position. They alone can restructure the incentive architecture across an entire field. By reserving a meaningful fixed share of grant budgets for replication studies, funders accomplish three things simultaneously. First, they directly produce the verification the current system lacks.
[P1.5] Second, they signal to the entire research community that confirmatory work carries genuine prestige and funding security—changing behavior not through moral appeal but through structural reward. Third, they protect the public purse: every euro spent confirming a finding before scaling a clinical trial or rolling out a policy is a euro that cannot be wasted on interventions that do not work.
[P1.6] The objection is predictable: will this not starve novel science? But the motion does not mandate that replication consume the majority of any budget. A meaningful fixed share—perhaps five to fifteen percent—is a modest constraint that preserves the vast bulk of discovery funding while correcting a critical asymmetry. Unchecked novelty without verification is not science; it is a lottery.
[P1.7] Replication studies are the quality control that makes the entire enterprise trustworthy. Public funders have both the responsibility and the leverage to impose that control.
