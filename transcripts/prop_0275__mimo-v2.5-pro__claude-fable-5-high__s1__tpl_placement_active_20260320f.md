[C1.1] The proposition sounds like it's banning hidden fees. It isn't. Hidden fees mean charges revealed after you commit money. What this rule actually demands is something far stranger: that every cleaning fee, resort fee, and full set of house rules appear before a user even clicks into a checkout page — a click that costs nothing, commits nothing, and exists precisely so people can review total costs before booking.
[C1.2] That distinction is the whole debate. Informed consent requires complete information before commitment, and the commitment is the booking, not the click. A checkout page that itemizes every fee before any payment satisfies every consumer-protection principle PRO invokes. The motion instead regulates the browsing stage, and at that stage the mandate breaks down on its own mechanics.
[C1.3] First, totals are often unknowable pre-checkout. Cleaning fees scale with party size; some fees vary by season or length of stay; rules differ for pets versus no pets. Forcing platforms to display "the" fee before the user has entered the inputs that determine it forces either worst-case inflated estimates or false precision — both more misleading than a clearly itemized checkout.
[C1.4] Second, house rules expose the absurdity. A typical listing carries dozens of rules: quiet hours, parking limits, septic instructions, pool waivers. Cramming full rule sets into search results doesn't inform anyone; it buries the one rule that matters under thirty that don't. Disclosure research consistently finds that flooding people with text pre-decision produces less comprehension, not more.
[C1.5] A guest is far more likely to actually read rules on a focused confirmation screen than in a wall of fine print stapled to every search tile.
[C1.6] Third, markets are already solving the real problem. Major platforms now offer total-price display in search because travelers demand it. Freezing one specific interface design into law locks in today's screens, punishes innovation, and saddles small platforms with compliance costs — all to mandate information at the moment users are least equipped to absorb it.
