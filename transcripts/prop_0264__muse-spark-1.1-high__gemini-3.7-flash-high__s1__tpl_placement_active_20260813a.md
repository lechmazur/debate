[P1.1] Every year American cities pay out enormous sums for police misconduct - New York, Chicago, Los Angeles have each paid hundreds of millions in the last decade alone - while the officer involved often pays nothing and keeps his badge. Taxpayers who never used force, never made the stop, never filed the false report are left to cover the bill. That is textbook moral hazard: when you bear no financial consequence for the risk you create, you have little reason to change.
[P1.2] We already know how to fix this in every other high-stakes profession. Doctors carry malpractice insurance. Lawyers carry malpractice insurance. Drivers must carry auto insurance.
[P1.3] We do not let someone wield a scalpel or drive a two-ton vehicle on public roads without showing they can cover the damage they might cause. Police are authorized to detain, search, assault and kill under color of law, yet we exempt them from the most basic financial responsibility mechanism.
[P1.4] Requiring personal professional liability insurance flips the incentives. An insurer prices risk. An officer with a clean record pays a low, manageable premium - municipalities could cover that base rate, as many employers do for other professionals. An officer with repeated complaints, uses of force, and lawsuits sees his premium spike.
[P1.5] Insurers maintain national databases, share loss history, and can refuse to insure. That becomes a de facto decertification that crosses city and state lines, something our fragmented internal affairs system fails to do.
[P1.6] This does not invent liability or make officers more suable than they already are. It simply funds that liability from somewhere other than the general fund and ensures the officer has skin in the game. Like other liability policies, it also provides a defense against frivolous claims.
[P1.7] It protects good cops, saves public services, and restores a basic fairness principle: the financial cost of professional misconduct should fall first on the profession that creates the risk, not mainly on the municipal taxpayer.
