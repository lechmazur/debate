[P1.1] Social media supercharged political polarization in ways cable news never could. While cable news segmented audiences by channel, social media segmented them by individual, feeding each user a diet of content calibrated to outrage and confirmation. Algorithms don’t just reflect division—they manufacture it, prioritizing posts that provoke strong emotional reactions because those drive engagement, time on platform, and ad revenue. The result is a feedback loop where extreme content spreads faster, moderate voices get drowned out, and users retreat into ever-more-insular echo chambers.
[P1.2] The scale is incomparable. Cable news reached millions; social media reaches billions daily, with the power to micro-target messages to specific demographics, interests, and even psychological profiles. Facebook’s Cambridge Analytica scandal revealed how voter data could be weaponized to suppress or stoke political passions at an individual level—something impossible for cable networks. Twitter’s trend algorithms can turn a fringe conspiracy into a global talking point overnight, while YouTube’s recommendation engine famously radicalizes users by suggesting increasingly extreme videos.
[P1.3] Moreover, social media turned political identity into social currency. Likes, shares, and retweets reward performative partisanship, incentivizing users to adopt more extreme positions to gain approval within their online tribes. Cable news pundits could shout at each other, but viewers couldn’t instantly amplify and distort those messages to thousands of their own followers. The interactivity of social platforms means polarization isn’t just consumed—it’s co-created by users themselves, accelerating the cycle of division far beyond what passive viewing ever could.
[P1.4] Cable news polarized by offering different realities on different channels. Social media polarized by offering a different reality to every single user—and making those realities more extreme with every click.
