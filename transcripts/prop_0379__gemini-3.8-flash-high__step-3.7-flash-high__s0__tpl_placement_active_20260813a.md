[P1.1] Democracy rests on a foundational promise: political equality. Every citizen carries one vote, regardless of pedigree or wealth. To protect the integrity of self-governance, countries must ban corporate political donations and strictly cap individual contributions.
[P1.2] First, money is not speech; it is unequal amplification. When spending is treated as constitutionally protected speech, the law guarantees that those with vast fortunes wield thousands of times more civic influence than ordinary citizens. A billionaire cannot legally cast ten thousand ballots on election day, but massive political donations achieve that exact effect by buying round-the-clock advertising, securing exclusive legislative access, and setting policy agendas behind closed doors. This does not foster a robust exchange of ideas; it drowns out ordinary voices.
[P1.3] Capping individual donations silences no one. It leaves every citizen free to speak, write, organize, and vote, while preventing extreme private wealth from monopolizing public debate.
[P1.4] Second, corporate donations distort the democratic mandate. A corporation is a state-created legal fiction designed to maximize shareholder returns, not a flesh-and-blood citizen bearing civic responsibilities. Corporate donations are not genuine political expressions; they are strategic investments made with the expectation of a legislative return. When corporate treasuries enter campaigns, the inevitable result is regulatory capture: carved-out tax codes, anti-competitive subsidies, and compromised consumer protections enacted at the public’s expense.
[P1.5] Democracy requires an egalitarian baseline. When politicians must cultivate broad coalitions of modest supporters rather than court a handful of corporate boardrooms and mega-donors, public policy realigns with the common good. Banning corporate money and lowering individual caps does not restrict speech—it protects democracy from degenerating into plutocracy.
