[P1.1] A 14-year-old’s phone is not a neutral tool. It is a portal to algorithmically amplified content, adult material, gambling-like purchase prompts, and contact with strangers. Parents are legally and morally responsible for that child, yet the main tools they are offered are app-level toggles that a motivated teenager can clear, reinstall, or route around in minutes. The proposition fixes that mismatch: it lets parents set limits at the operating-system layer, where they actually bind.
[P1.2] When a limit lives inside an app, the developer has little incentive to enforce it; the business model depends on engagement. When the operating system enforces age and recommendation limits, the app cannot simply ignore the setting, bury it in a menu, or keep serving content after a reinstall. The phone itself withholds the content or caps the feed. That is the difference between a real boundary and a suggestion.
[P1.3] Teenagers are not adults. Their impulse control and risk assessment are still developing, which is why we already restrict driving, drinking, voting, and contracts. Digital environments are engineered to exploit exactly those developmental gaps. A parent who sets a bedtime or blocks a dangerous street is not accused of tyranny; an OS-level content limit is the same judgment applied to a screen.
[P1.4] Recommendation engines are especially important here. They are not neutral mirrors; they optimize for watch time and emotional arousal. A teen searching for fitness content can be pushed toward extreme dieting or self-harm material within hours. OS-level limits can cap or filter those recommendations across apps, rather than forcing parents to discover and configure each app separately.
[P1.5] This is not a surveillance mandate. Parents can choose whether to use these controls and can relax them as the teenager shows responsibility. The question is whether the option should exist and be technically robust. Removing it leaves parents with the illusion of control while platforms retain the real control.
[P1.6] Parents already have the duty; they should be allowed the tool that actually works.
