[P1.1] Tenants are signing contracts while flying blind. A landlord knows whether the Airbnb unit downstairs hosts weekend raves, whether packages vanish from the lobby every Tuesday, and whether the walls are paper-thin at 2 a.m. The prospective tenant knows none of this until the first sleepless night or the first stolen delivery. This is not a minor inconvenience; it is a fundamental asymmetry of information that corrupts the entire lease transaction.
[P1.2] A lease is a bargain for habitability and security, not just square footage and a roof. When a building suffers from chronic short-term-rental parties, severe noise, or package theft, the tenant is not renting the apartment described in the listing—they are renting a lottery ticket where the odds are stacked against sleep, safety, and peace of mind. They cannot meaningfully consent to what they cannot see. Disclosure is the minimum correction for this market failure.
[P1.3] We already accept this principle in housing law. Landlords must disclose lead paint, bed bug infestations, and the presence of registered sex offenders nearby. These disclosures exist because the information is material to health, safety, and quiet enjoyment. Recurring party complaints and chronic theft are equally material to a tenant's daily security.
[P1.4] A tenant who cannot sleep or receive packages safely is not receiving the full benefit of their bargain, and the landlord is profiting from that ignorance.
[P1.5] Disclosure also serves landlords' bottom lines. Informed tenants are retained tenants. Surprise move-outs, early lease-breaking, and vitriolic online reviews cost owners far more than a single paragraph in the lease packet. Transparency filters for tenants who actually fit the building's reality, reducing conflict, vacancy churn, and management headaches.
[P1.6] The proposition asks for nothing radical—only that material facts be shared before money changes hands. Silence is not neutrality; it is a subsidy extracted from uninformed renters who bear the cost of the landlord's withheld knowledge. The lease should be a meeting of minds, not a trap set by the better-informed party.
