[P1.1] Personal medical choices are, at their core, questions of who owns the flesh in which a conscious mind resides. Bodily autonomy answers this unequivocally: the individual does. Appeals to dignity, while emotionally evocative, are notoriously slippery. When dignity aligns with a patient's choices, it is redundant because autonomy already protects their agency.
[P1.2] But when appeals to dignity are used to restrict choice, dignity invariably functions as a tool of moral paternalism—an external standard imposed by doctors, institutions, or the state to override the patient’s explicit will.
[P1.3] Genuine dignity cannot be enforced from the outside; it is the natural product of self-determination. Stripping a competent person of control over their physical person in the name of "human dignity" commits a fatal moral contradiction: it destroys the agent to preserve an abstraction. Consider a terminal patient seeking medical aid in dying to avoid protracted agony. When opponents deny this choice by appealing to the "inherent dignity" of enduring biological life to the end, they reduce the patient to a passive vessel for someone else’s moral philosophy.
[P1.4] That does not honor dignity; it inflicts profound degradation.
[P1.5] Moreover, autonomy carries decisive weight because of the asymmetry of consequence. The individual alone inhabits their body, endures the physical pain, and bears the irreversible outcomes of medical interventions. If appeals to dignity can override bodily autonomy, medical ethics collapses into subjective authoritarianism. Jehovah’s Witnesses could be forcibly transfused, cesarean sections could be mandated by court order, and unwanted life support could be indefinitely imposed—all under the guise of maintaining an external standard of "dignified" human life.
[P1.6] Bodily autonomy provides an unbreachable wall against using a person's body against their will. Dignity without autonomy is merely paternalism dressed in noble vocabulary. For medical choices to remain personal, bodily autonomy must hold supreme moral weight.
