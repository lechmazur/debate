[P1.1] An interview should measure whether someone can do the job, not whether an algorithm likes the shape of their mouth. Emotion-recognition systems claim to read engagement, honesty, or frustration from facial movements, voice pitch, and eye behavior. The central problem is not that the technology is imperfect; it is that it mistakes unreliable physical proxies for inner emotional states. Lighting, camera angle, accent, disability, fatigue, and cultural norms all change the very signals being scored.
[P1.2] A candidate with a hearing impairment, a stutter, a stroke-related facial difference, or simply a less expressive face can be marked as low-energy or deceptive. That turns ordinary human variation into machine-generated job risk.
[P1.3] The bias is not a fixable glitch. Emotion expression is culturally learned. In some cultures, less smiling signals respect, not disengagement. Autistic workers and candidates may not display the expected facial or vocal patterns.
[P1.4] Women and members of marginalized groups already face pressure to perform the “right” emotional style. Emotion AI takes those existing stereotypes and launders them into a numerical score that looks objective while automating discrimination.
[P1.5] Worker evaluation makes the harm continuous. Employees under emotion monitoring must perform a fake emotional state rather than focus on their actual work. A call center worker frustrated by a broken system may be flagged for negative sentiment. Fatigue becomes disengagement.
[P1.6] Anxiety about the evaluation itself becomes poor emotional control. The system punishes workers for having feelings it cannot accurately identify.
[P1.7] Prohibition is the right response because the stakes are high and meaningful consent does not exist. Applicants cannot realistically refuse a hiring algorithm. Employees cannot opt out when their income, visa, or healthcare depends on the job. Employers may evaluate job-relevant behavior, tested skills, and actual performance.
[P1.8] They should not be allowed to punish people for what software claims to read inside their minds.
