[P1.1] Picture a class of twenty-eight students. Twenty-seven are in a group chat. That chat is where homework hints get shared, where weekend plans are made, where the lunch table is decided before anyone walks into the cafeteria, and where screenshots of the twenty-eighth student get passed around with laughing emojis. The next morning, that student sits alone.
[P1.2] Nothing happened "off campus." The chat simply moved the planning out of the teacher's sight. The cruelty landed in the classroom.
[P1.3] That is the case for treating this as a serious anti-bullying issue. Schools already accept that bullying isn't only punches and slurs. Deliberate, coordinated social isolation, the old "freeze-out," has long been understood as relational aggression, and it can damage a child as deeply as physical intimidation: anxiety, avoidance, falling grades, refusal to attend. The digital version is worse, not milder.
[P1.4] It is persistent, it follows the child home at midnight, it can be organized by a handful of students and enforced by dozens, and it leaves the target able to see exactly what they're shut out of.
[P1.5] The "purely off-campus" label fails on its own terms. Schools act on conduct that substantially disrupts learning or a student's ability to participate safely in school life. Exclusion engineered on a phone and executed in hallways, group projects, and team rosters meets that test easily. If a school would intervene when students passed a note saying "nobody sit with her," it cannot shrug when the same instruction arrives by app.
[P1.6] Taking it seriously does not mean policing friendships. Children are entitled to choose their friends, and a birthday chat of six close kids is not bullying. The line is targeting: a pattern, an identifiable victim, coordination, and spillover into school. Within that line, schools should investigate reports, support the excluded student, involve parents, use restorative conversations, and teach digital citizenship.
[P1.7] No mass phone surveillance, no punishing kids for having inner circles.
[P1.8] The alternative is a loophole. Declare the group chat out of bounds, and every bully learns exactly where to do the work.
