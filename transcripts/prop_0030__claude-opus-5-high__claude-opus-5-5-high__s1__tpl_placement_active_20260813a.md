[P1.1] Start with the simplest fact about how Britain earns its living: it sells services and manufactured goods into a market of 450 million people twenty-odd miles away. Brexit put a customs border, rules-of-origin paperwork, veterinary checks, and professional-qualification barriers across that relationship. Nobody has ever argued that adding friction to your largest export market makes you richer. The only serious question is whether some offsetting gain arrived.
[P1.2] It didn't.
[P1.3] Three mechanisms carry this case.
[P1.4] First, trade. A small Welsh cheesemaker who used to ship pallets to Rotterdam on a single invoice now needs export health certificates, a customs agent, and a broker fee that swallows the margin on a small order. Large firms absorb that. Small ones stop exporting.
[P1.5] That is why UK goods trade intensity has underperformed comparable advanced economies since 2021, and why the promised replacement deals — Australia, New Zealand, the CPTPP accession — were estimated by the government's own impact assessments to add a fraction of a percent to GDP, against a much larger loss from EU friction.
[P1.6] Second, investment. Business investment is a bet on future access. From 2016 onward, firms faced years of not knowing what the trading regime would even be, and the answer, when it arrived, was "worse than before." Japanese carmakers, pharmaceutical plants, and bank trading desks made relocation decisions that are effectively irreversible.
[P1.7] Capital that moved to Amsterdam, Dublin, and Frankfurt is not coming back because the paperwork later got tidier.
[P1.8] Third, labour. Freedom of movement supplied care workers, HGV drivers, fruit pickers, vets, and hospitality staff who arrived when demand called and left when it didn't. Replacing an elastic labour supply with a visa system produced acute shortages — the 2021 fuel-delivery crisis being the vivid one — and contributed to a tighter, more inflationary labour market.
[P1.9] CON must show a concrete, realised gain big enough to outweigh all three. Not sovereignty as a value, not regulatory potential, not deals signed. Actual pounds in actual output. That evidence does not exist, because the deregulatory bonfire was never lit.
