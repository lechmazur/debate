[P1.1] Every government that subsidizes fuel is running a welfare program with the worst possible design: it pays out in proportion to how much fuel you burn. The family with two cars and a generator collects far more than the family that takes a minibus. Across the developing world, the richest fifth of households typically capture several times the subsidy benefit of the poorest fifth. We are spending public money to make the SUV cheaper and calling it help for the poor.
[P1.2] The fiscal cost is staggering and uncontrollable. Nigeria at points spent more on petrol subsidies than on health and education combined. Because the bill tracks world oil prices, a war in Europe or a decision in Riyadh can blow a hole in a finance minister's budget overnight. No parliament ever votes on that spending; it simply happens.
[P1.3] Price controls make it worse. Hold the pump price below the border price and you manufacture shortages, queues, and a smuggling industry. Subsidized Nigerian petrol has flowed across borders for decades because the price gap is a business model. Venezuela sold gasoline for practically nothing and ended up with empty pumps.
[P1.4] Controls do not defeat the market; they push it into the shadows where the poor pay black-market prices anyway.
[P1.5] The alternative is simple: take the money and hand it to people. Iran did this in 2010, raising fuel prices while depositing cash into nearly every household account, and the poorest came out ahead. Indonesia in 2015 redirected subsidy savings toward infrastructure and social programs. Mobile money and digital ID now make this feasible in places where it once was not.
[P1.6] A cash transfer of the same fiscal size leaves a poor household strictly better off, because they receive it whether or not they own a vehicle, and they choose how to spend it.
[P1.7] The motion says phase out and replace, not slash overnight. Transfers land first, prices adjust gradually. The failures CON will cite are failures of sequencing, not of the idea. Price controls help the price.
[P1.8] Cash helps people.
