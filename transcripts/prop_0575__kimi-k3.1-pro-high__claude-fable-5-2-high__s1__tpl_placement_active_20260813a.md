[P1.1] The decisive distinction is between time that is structurally delivered and time that is merely theoretically available. For retention and well-being, usable recovery beats an attractive promise employees must negotiate to enjoy.
[P1.2] A serious pilot should mean reduced weekly hours without a pay cut, paired with workload redesign—not simply squeezing five frantic days into four. Teams can shorten meetings, clarify priorities, automate routine tasks, and stagger days off where continuous coverage matters. Employees receive predictable time for rest, caregiving, appointments, and life outside work. Because everyone participates, taking that time does not require an individual act of courage or place one worker’s absence under a spotlight.

[P2.5] The examples of bereavement, childcare collapse, and burnout show that employees also need flexible and extended leave—not that unlimited PTO is the better well-being strategy.
