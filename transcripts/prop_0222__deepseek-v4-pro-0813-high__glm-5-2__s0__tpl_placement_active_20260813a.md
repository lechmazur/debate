[P1.1] Punitive enforcement turns a drug emergency into a criminal spiral. When possession of small amounts triggers arrest, the immediate incentive is to hide use, not to survive it. A person witnessing an overdose may hesitate to call for help because police are the first responders. A user with a criminal record learns that seeking treatment can mark them for prosecution.
[P1.2] Decriminalization reverses that calculation: the first contact becomes a health referral rather than a jail cell. That is precisely where overdose deaths are reduced—not by endorsing drugs, but by making it possible for users to stay alive long enough to receive help.
[P1.3] The downstream crime effects point the same way. Arresting low-level users floods police, courts, and jails with cases that do nothing to disrupt violent trafficking. Those arrests produce criminal records that block housing and employment, two of the strongest predictors of whether someone cycles into property crime or unstable survival.
[P1.4] Meanwhile, enforcement attention is drawn away from the armed supply networks that actually drive drug-market violence. Decriminalizing personal possession frees those resources for serious crime and lets a user remain a patient or worker instead of becoming a repeat offender.
[P1.5] The practical example is Portugal. Its shift from criminal punishment to administrative health diversion did not produce the feared explosion in use, but it did coincide with substantially fewer overdose deaths and a clear drop in drug-related offenses and incarceration. The mechanism is direct: when possession is handled outside the criminal system, overdose calls go up, treatment referrals go up, and the criminal downstream of a possession arrest shrinks.
[P1.6] The opponent may argue that decriminalization sends a bad signal or risks normalization. But the motion is not about symbolic approval; it is about deaths and crimes. On those two metrics, the punitive status quo is the riskier experiment. It suppresses help-seeking, deepens black-market danger, and manufactures offenders out of people who need care.
[P1.7] Decriminalization is the only policy aligned with reducing the very harms the proposition names.
