[P1.1] A Portuguese nurse waits months for a residence permit and proves her worth through work. A foreign investor buys an apartment he will never live in and receives EU residency as a gift with purchase. That contrast is the entire case: golden-visa programs take the one thing a state should never sell — membership itself — and put it on the market for whoever is rich enough to ask.
[P1.2] Start with what is actually being sold. Citizenship and residency are not commodities; they are the legal bond of a political community, and they carry the votes, courts, and protections of every member state. When Malta sells a passport, it is selling access to the entire Schengen zone — meaning one country's revenue becomes twenty-six countries' security risk. The buyers these programs attract are not random rich people.
[P1.3] The due-diligence industry that screens them is hired and paid by the seller, and the record shows what that produces: Cyprus scrapped its scheme after undercover footage showed officials cheerfully arranging a passport for a fictional convicted fugitive; Russian money flowed through these channels for years before 2022 made the danger undeniable. Dirty money does not buy property; it buys impunity with an EU address.
[P1.4] Second, the economic case is hollow. These programs generate trivial sums relative to national budgets — a rounding error dressed up as fiscal strategy — while delivering real, concentrated harm. In Lisbon and Athens, golden-visa property purchases helped inflate housing beyond what local wages could bear, which is precisely why Portugal abolished its real-estate route. The investment that arrives is passive and speculative: it parks capital in empty flats rather than building firms or jobs.
[P1.5] Third, the principle. Europe runs rigorous, often brutal immigration systems for students, workers, and refugees — then maintains a VIP lane for wealth alone. A union that claims to stand for equal dignity cannot keep auctioning belonging. The alternative is simple and already proven: countries that ended these programs lost nothing worth keeping.
[P1.6] End the sale, everywhere, permanently.
