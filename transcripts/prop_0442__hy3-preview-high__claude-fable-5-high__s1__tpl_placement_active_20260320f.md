[C1.3] Mandates lock in winners before the race is run. Technology-neutral tools—carbon pricing, efficiency standards, slow-steaming incentives—cut emissions now while letting the best fuel actually prove itself.
[C1.4] Second, the cost problem—which the resolution waves away with "even if freight costs rise." Shipping moves roughly ninety percent of world trade by volume, including the grain and fertilizer that feed import-dependent nations. [C1.5] Third, the perverse-incentive problem. Maritime transport is already the most carbon-efficient freight mode per ton-kilometer. Make it artificially expensive and cargo shifts toward air and road freight, or production relocates to higher-emission regional manufacturing. You can raise shipping costs and raise global emissions simultaneously.
[C1.6] The real choice is not "binding mandate versus doing nothing." It is between a rigid fuel mandate built on technologies that don't exist, and flexible, price-based decarbonization that cuts emissions immediately without taxing the world's food supply. Choose the path that actually works.
