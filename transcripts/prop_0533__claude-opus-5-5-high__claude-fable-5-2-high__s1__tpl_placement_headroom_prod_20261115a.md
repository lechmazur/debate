[P1.1] The real question here is not whether Egypt's water matters. It does. The question is whether Egypt should hold a veto over how a sovereign country uses a river that rises almost entirely within its borders. It should not.
[P1.2] Start with geography. The great majority of the Nile water reaching Egypt originates in the Ethiopian highlands, mostly through the Blue Nile. Yet the treaties Egypt invokes, from 1929 and 1959, were negotiated without Ethiopia. They allocated virtually the entire river between Egypt and Sudan and assigned Ethiopia nothing.
[P1.3] Demanding that Ethiopia's timeline bend to Egyptian objections means demanding that a colonial-era settlement Ethiopia never signed keep governing its development forever.
[P1.4] Second, consider what the dam actually does. GERD is a hydropower project, not an irrigation scheme. Water spins turbines and flows downstream. The only contested period is filling, and filling is temporary.
[P1.5] After the reservoir is full, the river keeps flowing toward Sudan and Egypt, and regulated flow can actually reduce flood damage and silt buildup downstream. Egypt also has enormous storage behind the Aswan High Dam, a buffer designed precisely to absorb variable years.
[P1.6] Third, the evidence is already in. Ethiopia has filled the reservoir in stages over several rainy seasons since 2020, over loud Egyptian objection. The predicted catastrophe for Egyptian farmers did not materialize. The threat was real enough to justify talks, never real enough to justify a veto.
[P1.7] Fourth, weigh the stakes. Tens of millions of Ethiopians live without reliable electricity, cooking with wood and studying by kerosene. GERD can roughly double the nation's generating capacity. Every season of delay demanded by Cairo is a season of darkness imposed on people who had no say in the original carve-up.
[P1.8] Ethiopia should consult, share data, and fill adaptively in drought years. But consultation is not permission. A downstream objection is a reason to talk, not a right to decide.
