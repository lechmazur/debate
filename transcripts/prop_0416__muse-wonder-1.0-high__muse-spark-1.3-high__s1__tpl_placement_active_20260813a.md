[P1.1] Egg freezing is marketed as insurance: pause the clock, keep your options open, take the pressure off dating and career. The women in the ads are rarely told, in the same size type and the same breath, what that insurance is actually likely to pay out — and how sharply the odds fall with the age at which they freeze.
[P1.2] The missing number is not fertilization or a pregnancy test. It is the chance of taking home a baby from the eggs frozen in one cycle, and that chance depends overwhelmingly on two things ads minimize: age at freezing and how many mature eggs are stored. Freezing in the early thirties with a good number of eggs can offer a reasonable chance. Freezing at thirty-nine or forty, often with fewer eggs per cycle, can mean a low chance even after spending many thousands of dollars and repeating stimulation.
[P1.3] Those are not footnotes. They are the product.
[P1.4] Clinics, however, can choose flattering denominators — eggs surviving thaw, embryos created, pregnancies per transfer among the minority who return and have an embryo to transfer — each technically true and collectively misleading. They can pair that with a single success story while omitting that most frozen eggs do not become babies, that many patients never use them, and that estimates for older ages rest on relatively small numbers and vary by clinic. A consultation months later cannot fully undo a belief and a timeline already formed when the ad created false reassurance.
[P1.5] Requiring prominent, age-based disclosure fixes the mismatch without banning anything. Next to any promise of preserving fertility, a clinic would state, plainly and comparably: estimated live-birth range by age bracket, eggs typically needed, and that outcomes are uncertain and not guaranteed. That respects autonomy rather than restricting it. A choice sold as empowerment should begin with the facts that determine whether empowerment is real or merely expensive hope.

[C1.1] No woman undergoes weeks of injections and surgery because of an Instagram slogan. Ads open a door; medicine happens inside the room. Egg freezing requires bloodwork, ultrasound, individualized counseling, and informed consent before a dollar is spent on stimulation. To regulate the slogan as if it were the consent form mistakes where choice is actually made, and risks wrecking the information that matters.
[C1.2] Second, the supposedly realistic age-based success rate is the least realistic number to print large. A 34-year-old who produces five eggs and a 34-year-old who produces twenty do not face the same odds, and neither is captured by “women 30-34: X percent.” Thaw survival improves with newer vitrification, live births depend on future partner, sperm, uterus, years of storage, and whether eggs are ever used — most are not. Stamping one prominent range on every ad creates false precision, not honesty, and will be outdated the moment techniques shift.
[C1.3] Third, that false precision harms autonomy. Blast a low, scary bracket at thirty-nine and you do not empower her; you shame her out of a consultation where a doctor could assess her actual reserve and options, including multiple cycles or donor discussion. Blast a reassuring bracket at thirty and you lull younger women who may need urgency for medical reasons. Population averages cannot counsel individuals.
[C1.4] Current deception law already bans false success claims. No clinic may advertise a baby guarantee it cannot deliver. What is demanded here is not a ban on lies but a government-mandated layout — bigger type, prescribed brackets — vague, costly to enforce, and chilling to small clinics that cannot fund legal review for every post. The result will be fewer women learning in time that freezing young works best.
[C1.5] If empowerment means anything, it means encouraging a conversation, not printing a warning label large enough to end it.

[P2.1] An ad urging a 38-year-old to ‘pause the clock’ does not merely open a door. It shapes when she walks through it, how long she believes she can safely wait, and what she expects inside. By the consultation she is already anchored on reassurance, has planned around it, and is hearing caveats from the clinic selling her the cycle. A conflicted, late-stage disclaimer does not undo a first impression — it negotiates against it.
[P2.2] The false-precision objection turns the proposal upside down. No one is asking for a single guaranteed percentage stamped on every post. The requirement is an age-bracketed live-birth range, the number of mature eggs that estimate assumes, and an explicit statement that outcomes vary and are uncertain. That is how you expose false precision.
[P2.3] What misleads today is the isolated ‘80% thaw survival’ or pregnancy-per-transfer figure, technically accurate while hiding that most eggs never become a baby and most patients never reach transfer.
[P2.4] Population averages are not a substitute for counseling; they are what makes counseling intelligible. Doctors themselves begin with age and egg count, then adjust for reserve, sperm, health, and plans. A 39-year-old is not shamed by learning her bracket is low — she is protected from buying false hope and can ask sooner about multiple cycles, embryo freezing, or donor eggs. A 30-year-old seeing how sharply odds later fall is not lulled; she learns why waiting until 39 changes the product.
[P2.5] Existing deception law misses exactly this harm because selective truths are not outright lies. No regulator must chase every flattering denominator if every claim of preserved fertility carries the same standard disclosure beside it. A common format is also cheaper and safer for small clinics than bespoke legal vetting of creative copy. Truth placed after the sales pitch is buried information.
[P2.6] Truth placed beside the promise is autonomy.

A small clinic with 40 thaw cases must either advertise shaky in-house numbers or a national average that is not its results. Deception law already forbids inventing success; what remains is not fraud but complexity.
[C2.6] Complexity is resolved in the exam room, not by making the slogan look like the consent form.

[P4.1] A1: The denominator should be the one the promise implies: the chance of at least one live birth from eggs frozen in one cycle — shown by age at freezing, with the number of mature eggs that estimate assumes. Use pooled national outcome data set by the regulator or professional registry and refreshed on a fixed cycle, with clinic-specific figures only as an additional, clearly labelled supplement. That answers updating and the small-sample problem, and gives every ad the same yardstick instead of each clinic choosing thaw survival because it flatters.
[P4.2] A2: Autonomy is protected because a bleak average does not forbid treatment; it tells her why time, egg number, and alternatives matter before money and hope narrow her options. Beside the range sits the uncertainty statement: individual reserve can be better or worse, and assessment can change the plan. If, informed, she chooses not to pursue a low-odds cycle, that is not lost autonomy — it is autonomy exercised. CON's alternative protects bookings, not women, by keeping the discouraging fact small until the seller can frame it.
[P4.3] The consultation argument now collapses. If doctors properly explain attrition, a prominent range will confirm — not contradict — that explanation, and make it harder for marketing to outrun medicine. CON fears that women will misread a standardized national figure, yet defends a status quo where each clinic picks its own denominator, timeframe, and success story. That is not avoiding confusion; it is privatizing it.
[P4.4] What remains is CON's preference for later, private, seller-delivered truth over earlier, comparable, public truth. But egg freezing is sold precisely on timing: freeze earlier, regret less, keep options open. Age is therefore not an intrusive warning — it is material information about the product at the moment the product is pitched. Require the range, the egg-number assumption, and the uncertainty together, prominently, and ads can still invite consultation.
[P4.5] They just cannot invite false reassurance first.
