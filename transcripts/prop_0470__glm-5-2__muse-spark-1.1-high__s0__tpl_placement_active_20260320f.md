[P1.1] Democracies rest on a simple premise: voters must be able to tell what their leaders actually said and did. Hyper-realistic political deepfakes destroy that capacity. When a voter watches lifelike video of a candidate appearing to confess to corruption, utter slurs, or call for violence—and cannot reliably distinguish it from authentic footage—the epistemic foundation of democratic choice collapses. This is not speculative.
[P1.2] The technology to fabricate convincing audio and video of real public figures is already widely accessible, improving rapidly, and has been deployed in elections across multiple countries.
[P1.3] The proposition acknowledges a tradeoff, and that honesty matters. A ban on hyper-realistic political deepfakes would narrow the toolkit available to satirists and would complicate some forms of anonymous political expression. But consider what is actually being traded. Satire works through exaggeration, distortion, and absurdity—through signals that the audience is in on the joke.
[P1.4] Content engineered to be indistinguishable from genuine footage is not satire; it is deception wearing satire's clothing as a legal shield. Anonymous political speech historically functioned through pamphlets, essays, and pseudonymous argument—ideas detached from identity. It never required the right to put photorealistic fabricated words into a real person's mouth.
[P1.5] The deeper point is asymmetry. In an unregulated environment, the liar has the advantage. A fabricated video can reach millions in hours; corrections reach a fraction of that audience and arrive too late. Elections happen on fixed schedules.
[P1.6] A deepfake released forty-eight hours before a vote can swing an outcome before anyone has time to debunk it. Democracy cannot function as a marketplace of ideas when that market rewards forgery faster than it rewards truth. A targeted ban on hyper-realistic political deepfakes—content designed to be mistaken for real footage of real political figures—is a proportionate response to an existential threat to informed self-governance.
