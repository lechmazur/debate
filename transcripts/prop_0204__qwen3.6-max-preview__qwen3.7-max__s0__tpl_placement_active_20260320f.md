[P1.1] Critical minerals are not ordinary commodities. They are the physical substrate of modern defense systems, grid-scale storage, and advanced manufacturing. When a firm closely tied to a rival state acquires a mine, refinery, or processing facility, it does not merely purchase an asset—it purchases leverage. Adversarial governments routinely treat economic interdependence as a coercive tool, using corporate dependencies to threaten export curbs, manipulate pricing, or quietly degrade supply quality during diplomatic or military crises.
[P1.2] Allowing rival-linked entities to control these nodes hands a strategic competitor an off-switch for our industrial and defense base.
[P1.3] A presumptive block is the only standard that matches the asymmetry of this risk. Case-by-case approvals assume regulators can reliably map hidden state influence, forecast geopolitical realignments, and monitor long-term corporate compliance. They cannot. Intelligence on foreign state-corporate ties is inherently fragmented, and once ownership transfers, unwinding it is legally paralyzing and politically costly.
[P1.4] A presumption flips the burden: the acquirer must demonstrate the transaction poses no security threat, rather than forcing the state to prove danger after the fact. This default insulates reviews from lobbying pressure, closes regulatory blind spots, and recognizes that the cost of a false positive is merely delayed capital, while the cost of a false negative is entrenched strategic vulnerability.
[P1.5] The opposition will claim that blocking these deals raises costs, chills investment, or fractures global markets. But capital is fungible; strategic leverage is not. Domestic developers, allied investors, and targeted public financing can fill the gap without embedding adversarial control into supply chains that power missiles, hospitals, and the electrical grid. Security does not require autarky—it requires refusing to outsource foundational inputs to governments that explicitly weaponize trade.
[P1.6] When the asset is critical and the buyer answers to a rival, the safe default is denial. Anything else gambles with infrastructure we cannot quickly replace.
