[P1.2] That is the heart of the harm. Subscription billing is legitimate only because consent is presumed to be ongoing. Dark patterns break that presumption deliberately. The gym that demands a notarized letter, the newspaper that hides cancellation behind a retention agent trained to say no three times, the streaming or shopping service that routes you through a maze of "are you sure" screens and discount offers—none of these are retaining customers.
[P1.3] They are harvesting inertia. Every dollar collected that way is a transfer from a consumer who wanted out to a firm that made leaving expensive.
[P1.4] The scale is not trivial. The average household now juggles many recurring charges, and surveys consistently find people underestimate what they pay and keep services they have stopped using. [P1.5] This is also a market failure, not a consumer-responsibility problem. A firm that makes cancellation honest loses revenue to competitors who make it hard. Friction becomes the competitive weapon, and honest firms are punished. Markets cannot fix a race whose prize is trapping people.
[P1.6] The remedy is modest and symmetric. Whatever channel you used to sign up must work to cancel. This bans nothing: no limit on auto-renewal, pricing, or one clearly labeled retention offer. It requires only that businesses stop taking money as easily as they started.
[P1.7] Any company that built a one-click sign-up flow already owns the technology to build the exit. If that costs them customers, those were never customers—they were hostages.
