[P1.1] Standardized IQ-style tests measure a remarkably thin slice of human intellect: rapid, decontextualized puzzle-solving inside closed, artificial systems. They reward the ability to spot patterns in geometric shapes, manipulate symbols, and recall vocabulary under arbitrary time constraints. Yet across educational sorting and employment screening, these tests are treated as master keys to human potential—a role their narrow scope cannot justify.
[P1.2] Real-world cognitive capability is broad, messy, and context-dependent. High-level performance in academic research and modern enterprise demands critical judgment under uncertainty, the synthesis of conflicting information, long-term cognitive persistence, and communicative reasoning. An IQ test measures none of these. A candidate who scores in the 99th percentile on timed matrix reasoning may be utterly deficient in intellectual humility, contextual adaptability, or creative problem-framing.
[P1.3] Conversely, thinkers uniquely equipped to untangle complex, open-ended challenges are routinely filtered out because they do not optimize for speed on synthetic logic puzzles.
[P1.4] The fatal flaw is the leap from a narrow psychometric artifact to high-stakes gatekeeping. When schools use IQ-style cutoffs to funnel seven-year-olds into gifted tracks, they are not identifying universal academic talent; they are selecting for early facility with closed-form tasks. When corporate recruiters use cognitive aptitude batteries to screen entry-level applicants, they discard domain curiosity, diligence, and practical problem-solving before a candidate can even demonstrate them.
[P1.5] The problem is not that IQ measures nothing—it captures a real, specialized facet of cognitive processing. The problem is that institutions treat this single facet as a universal proxy for competence. Elevating a narrow puzzle-solving metric into a primary filter misallocates human capital, entrenches blind spots, and excludes vital intellectual strengths. A test that captures only a sliver of how minds actually work has no business serving as the gatekeeper of opportunity.
