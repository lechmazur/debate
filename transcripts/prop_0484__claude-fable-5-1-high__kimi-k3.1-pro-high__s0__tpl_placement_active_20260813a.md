Climate decisions allocate energy costs, development opportunities, and livelihoods across every country. Existential danger establishes an overwhelming claim to protection; it does not establish title over everyone else’s choices.
[C1.2] A person whose house is most vulnerable to fire deserves urgent protection, not automatic control of the entire city budget. “Voice should track stake” slides from a right to be heard into a right to rule.
[C1.3] “Proportional to existential risk” also has no workable meaning. Is the metric territorial inundation, loss of statehood, cultural extinction, displacement, or economic collapse? Over what timeframe, under which emissions pathway, and after what adaptation? Tuvalu’s territorial survival cannot be cleanly converted into a number and compared with risks of famine, lethal heat, or state failure elsewhere.
[C1.4] The formula would invite governments to negotiate the risk assessment itself, turning scientific uncertainty into a contest for votes. A rule intended to empower the vulnerable would instead reward the most effective portrayal of catastrophe.
[C1.5] The proposal then faces a decisive institutional dilemma. If consensus remains, weighted voting changes little: reluctant major emitters retain their practical veto. If weighted votes replace consensus with binding outcomes, those emitters can refuse participation, funding, or domestic implementation. Climate agreements are not self-executing court judgments; they depend on governments controlling power grids, industries, and public revenue.
[C1.6] Giving islands more votes on paper while driving indispensable countries away from implementation would worsen the substance PRO rightly says matters more than speeches.
[C1.7] The metric also privileges the survival of a state over the survival of people. A small island facing national erasure would outrank vastly larger numbers of highly exposed people in populous countries whose states will territorially endure. That is not a categorical moral victory; it is a choice to discount millions of lives because their government remains on the map.
[C1.8] Island vulnerability demands enforceable mitigation, adaptation finance, and guaranteed procedural access. This motion instead offers an incalculable formula that weakens legitimacy without securing delivery.
