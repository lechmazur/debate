[P1.1] Airlines and booking sites must disclose the full practical cost of a family trip—including baggage, seat selection and any other mandatory fees—before users can compare options. Families are uniquely vulnerable to hidden‑fee pricing because a single trip multiplies every ancillary charge. A base fare that looks cheap on the screen can explode once four adults and two children each need two checked bags and guaranteed seats; the “real” price often ends up 25‑30 % higher than advertised. When travelers cannot see that total up front, they are forced to guess, to over‑budget, or to abandon a trip altogether—outcomes that run directly counter to consumer protection and market efficiency.
[P1.2] Full‑cost transparency eliminates the deceptive “low‑fare trap.” If every airline is required to present a line‑item total that aggregates fare, baggage allowance, seat fees, and any other compulsory surcharges, shoppers compare like with like. Competition then shifts from who can hide fees behind a glossy landing page to who can offer genuinely lower total prices or better service. That pressure rewards airlines that bundle value responsibly and punishes those that rely on opaque pricing structures to lure families.
[P1.3] The policy also reduces post‑purchase friction. Currently, families often receive surprise invoices at check‑in or at the gate, leading to disputes, negative reviews and even charge‑backs. By front‑loading the full cost, airlines lower the incidence of these complaints, improve brand trust and cut administrative overhead. Moreover, transparent pricing supports better financial planning for households, especially those on tight budgets who cannot absorb unexpected expenses.
[P1.4] Implementation is straightforward: a booking engine can calculate the total cost by multiplying the per‑passenger baggage fee, the per‑seat selection fee, and any other mandatory charges by the number of travelers, then displaying that sum alongside the base fare. The EU already mandates inclusion of all mandatory fees in advertised prices, demonstrating feasibility and consumer benefit. Requiring airlines and booking sites to show the complete family cost before comparison therefore safeguards shoppers, fosters fair competition, and strengthens the overall travel market.
