[P1.1] When an elementary student struggles to decode text or grasp fractions, moving them from a classroom of twenty-four to a classroom of eighteen changes almost nothing about their daily instructional reality. The teacher still teaches to the middle, manages group dynamics, and divides a forty-five-minute lesson across nearly twenty distinct needs. High-dosage tutoring, by contrast, fundamentally changes the learning encounter by placing that student in a one-on-one or small-group setting three or more times a week, where every misunderstanding is caught instantly and every minute is active practice.
[P1.2] The core of this debate is cost-effectiveness: learning gains per dollar.
[P1.3] Reducing class sizes across grades K–8 is perhaps the single most expensive intervention in public education. A twenty-five percent reduction in class size demands a proportional surge in fully credentialed teachers, physical classrooms, administrative overhead, and lifelong pension obligations. Most of that money never touches instruction; it pays for square footage, HVAC, and facilities. Furthermore, broad empirical evidence shows that reducing class size yields only modest, incremental improvements, largely because teachers rarely alter their core pedagogy just because six fewer desks are in the room.
[P1.4] High-dosage tutoring directs every dollar straight to the point of academic breakdown. It does not require brick-and-mortar construction or district-wide hiring of scarce certified staff; it leverages trained paraprofessionals, fellows, and specialized instructors operating under teacher supervision. More importantly, the learning returns are massive. Intense, frequent tutoring regularly generates effect sizes that outpace almost any other educational intervention, especially in foundational K–8 math and reading where targeted feedback is decisive.
[P1.5] When you spend a dollar on class size reduction, you buy a few extra seconds of a teacher’s divided attention. When you spend that dollar on high-dosage tutoring, you buy relentless, personalized instruction that actually closes skill gaps. Tutoring wins decisively on both the numerator of gains and the denominator of cost.
