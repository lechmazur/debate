[P1.1] Standardized IQ-style tests only measure a very specific slice of cognitive performance: abstract pattern matching, speed of simple mental arithmetic, verbal analogies under strict time pressure. That is not nothing. But it systematically excludes almost every form of cognition that actually predicts success in real education and work.
[P1.2] Nobody hires a software engineer to complete shape sequences under 90 seconds. They hire them to debug a messy undocumented system over three days, collaborate through miscommunication, notice when the original problem itself was framed wrong. Nobody admits a nursing student because they can define obscure vocabulary words. They admit them to stay calm during a crisis, track six overlapping patient needs, adjust care when a textbook rule does not fit the person in front of them.
[P1.3] Worse, these tests actively penalize valuable cognitive traits. People who pause to double check, who consider edge cases, who think slowly and deeply will reliably score lower on timed IQ sections even when their actual work output is consistently far better. Reflexive fast problem solving is one useful skill. It is not the master skill.
[P1.4] We have let this narrow proxy become a gatekeeper that sorts millions of people out of opportunity long before they can demonstrate the abilities that actually matter. That is not fair, it is not effective, and it cannot be justified while the tests ignore almost everything we actually value in competent people.

[C1.1] A test does not have to measure everything to be worth using. It has to beat the alternatives. That is the comparison PRO never makes, and it is the one that decides this debate.
[C1.2] Start with the empirical embarrassment for the "narrow band" claim. If these tests really tapped only shape rotation and vocabulary trivia, they would predict shape rotation and vocabulary trivia. Instead, general cognitive ability is among the most robust predictors we have of training success and job performance, across occupations that share almost nothing on the surface — and its predictive power *rises* as jobs get more complex and less scripted. That is precisely PRO's messy undocumented codebase.
[C1.3] Debugging an unfamiliar system with no manual is not a departure from what these tests measure; it is the purest real-world form of it: extracting structure from novel information under uncertainty. The narrow-looking items are a thermometer. A thermometer is a small glass tube and it still tells you about the whole body.
[C1.4] Second, "outsized role" collapses on inspection. In real hiring the dominant gatekeepers are the unstructured interview, the referral, the résumé, and above all the four-year degree — tools with weaker predictive records and far heavier class loading. When American employers were pushed away from direct aptitude testing, they did not switch to holistic wisdom-assessment. They switched to demanding bachelor's degrees, converting a two-hour test into a four-year tuition bill.
[C1.5] Removing the cheap measure does not remove sorting. It hands sorting to whoever can afford the proxy.
[C1.6] Third, PRO's "penalizes the careful thinker" point is testable and fails. Give the same people untimed power tests and the rank ordering barely moves. Speed is not the construct; it is a shortcut to measuring it.
[C1.7] Nobody argues a score should be the sole gate. The claim on the table is that it cannot be *justified* in its role. It can — because judged against the interview, the essay, and the credential, the test is the least arbitrary and least inheritable instrument we have.
