[P1.1] Ask what actually shapes a child's life. Decades of developmental research point to the same factors again and again: warmth and responsiveness, consistent discipline, economic security, low conflict at home, good schools, and stability of caregivers. Nothing in that list requires one mother and one father. It requires present, capable, committed adults.
[P1.2] That is why broad reviews of the literature have repeatedly reached the same conclusion: on average, children raised by same-sex couples show similar outcomes in emotional health, behavior, school progress, and social relationships as children raised by different-sex couples. The pattern holds across many samples and countries, and leading pediatric and psychological professional organizations have recognized that family processes matter far more than family form.
[P1.3] Claims of harm almost always smuggle in a different comparison. They contrast children raised from birth in stable married households with children who experienced parental separation, repartnering, foster care disruption, or stigma — then label the difference as caused by having gay parents. Control for stability, income, and parental education, compare stable household to stable household, and the supposed deficit vanishes. What hurts children is disruption and disadvantage, not the sex of the parents who comfort them after a bad day.
[P1.4] Policy should stop punishing children for adult prejudice. Every year thousands of children wait for adoption or foster placement while qualified same-sex couples are turned away or deprioritized. Marriage recognition, joint adoption, and equal parental rights secure health coverage, inheritance, custody continuity, and decision-making if a parent dies or a couple splits. Denying those protections does not help a single child with straight parents; it only makes children with gay parents less secure.
[P1.5] Equal treatment is not favoritism. It is aligning law with evidence about what children actually need.

[C1.3] Convenience samples of educated same-sex parents, compared against mixed different-sex families including breakups and stepfamilies, almost guarantee a finding of "no difference." [C1.8] Children are not indifferent. They do best with their mother and their father. The law should not pretend otherwise.
