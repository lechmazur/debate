[P1.1] The proposition asks studios to do what every good investor does: stop mistaking predictability for profitability. Franchise sequels are indeed more predictable bets. But a predictable bet can still be a worse allocation of capital — and the studio business is showing the strain of pretending otherwise.
[P1.2] Start with the math studios already run. A mid-budget original that fails costs a fraction of what a bloated sequel that underperforms costs. When a $200 million tentpole misses, it can erase the profits of several other releases; when a $40–60 million original misses, it's a manageable write-down, and when it hits, the returns are spectacular. This is basic portfolio construction: a slate needs low-variance bets and high-upside bets, because the sequels' "safety" is offset by their ever-rising budgets and marketing commitments.
[P1.3] Predictability of gross does not mean predictability of profit. Studios are currently concentrating capital in the asset class where each marginal dollar buys the least upside.
