[P1.1] In 2012 Facebook bought Instagram, a company of about a dozen employees with no revenue, for roughly a billion dollars. Regulators cleared it in weeks. Years later, internal messages surfaced describing the logic as buying a threat rather than competing with it. Under the existing standard, the regulator had to prove harm in a market that barely existed, against a buyer holding every relevant document.
[P1.2] That is not a test; it is a guarantee of failure.
[P1.3] Three reasons the burden belongs on the platform.
[P1.4] First, the platform holds the evidence. It knows why it is buying: the roadmap, the internal threat memos, the projected synergies. The regulator sees a press release. Across law, burdens sit with the party controlling the facts, and merger law already uses structural presumptions for concentrated horizontal deals.
[P1.5] The motion simply applies that familiar logic where information asymmetry is most extreme.
[P1.6] Second, the errors are not symmetric. Digital markets tip. Network effects, data feedback loops, and default placement mean that once a nascent rival is absorbed, the window for the next one narrows or closes. A wrongly cleared deal is permanent; nobody un-merges Instagram a decade later.
[P1.7] A wrongly blocked deal is cheap: the target still exists, can raise capital, go public, or sell to a non-dominant buyer. When one error is irreversible and the other is a detour, a rational system leans against the irreversible one.
[P1.8] Third, the presumption is rebuttable and asks for exactly what firms already claim. Every acquisition announcement promises better products and faster innovation. The motion requires those promises be substantiated to an authority rather than a newsroom. Genuinely beneficial deals pass; deals whose benefits cannot survive scrutiny are precisely the ones to doubt.
[P1.9] The scope is narrow: dominant platforms, not startups buying startups. Over two decades the largest platforms completed hundreds of acquisitions with almost none blocked. Either nearly every one served consumers, or the test is broken. CON must defend the former.
