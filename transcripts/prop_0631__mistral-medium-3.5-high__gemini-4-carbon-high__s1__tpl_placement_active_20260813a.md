[P1.1] The U.S. copyright system is built on human authorship. The Constitution grants Congress power to secure rights for "authors," and the Copyright Act protects "original works of authorship." These terms, as interpreted by courts and the Copyright Office, require human creativity. The "monkey selfie" ruling confirmed that non-human creators cannot hold copyright, and the Office’s recent guidance explicitly states that AI-generated outputs without human input are not registrable.
[P1.2] This is not arbitrary: copyright’s purpose is to incentivize human expression. AI, which does not respond to incentives, should not be granted exclusive rights that would remove works from the public domain without promoting progress.
[P1.3] The proposition’s "substantial creative control" standard is a practical and fair threshold. For example, if a designer uses AI to generate a hundred logo options and then iteratively refines one through multiple rounds of human-directed edits, the final logo should qualify. But if someone types "a sunset over mountains" and takes the first output, that should not. Without this requirement, a single prompt could generate thousands of images or texts, all locked behind copyright, erecting barriers to competition and stifling the very creativity the system aims to foster.
[P1.4] Moreover, the alternative—automatic protection for all AI outputs—creates perverse outcomes. It would let platforms claim rights over vast datasets of machine-generated content, chilling free expression and innovation. Without a human control requirement, corporations would rush to monopolize AI outputs, erecting paywalls around what should be a public resource. It would also make it harder for humans to build on or remix AI outputs, as they would constantly risk infringement.
[P1.5] The public domain is the default for non-human creations, and that default should hold for AI unless a human has truly shaped the result.
[P1.6] In short, copyright must remain tethered to human creativity. The proposition upholds that principle without foreclosing the benefits of AI as a tool.
