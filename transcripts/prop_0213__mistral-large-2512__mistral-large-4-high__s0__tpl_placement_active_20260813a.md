[C1.1] The proposition mistakes a moral preference for a political strategy. In post-authoritarian transitions, the first task is not to heal wounds but to consolidate a fragile democracy; when those two goals conflict, stability must usually win. Truth commissions with public testimony presume that exposure prevents recurrence, but history shows the opposite: forced disclosure in a still-divided society often reignites the very conflicts it claims to resolve, giving defeated elites a rallying cry and frightened populations a reason to retreat into sectarian camps.
[C1.2] Consider the mechanics of transition. When a military junta or armed faction still holds weapons and veto power, public testimony naming their crimes is not catharsis—it is provocation that can collapse the transition. Broad amnesties, by contrast, function as a credible commitment device that neutralizes spoilers. In Mozambique, the 1992 peace agreement used a sweeping amnesty to end a sixteen-year civil war that had killed nearly a million people.
[C1.3] The alternative was not a South African-style commission; it was continued slaughter. The amnesty bought the stability necessary for elections and reconstruction, however imperfect the justice.
[C1.4] Public testimony also inflicts its own harm. Transforming victims into witnesses before the nation turns private trauma into a political spectacle. When perpetrators walk free regardless—whether through amnesty or sheer impunity—the hearing offers recognition without retribution, leaving victims feeling doubly betrayed: first by their tormentors, then by a state that demands their pain for the cameras while protecting their tormentors. Spain's "Pact of Forgetting" allowed a generation to build democratic institutions without the daily reopening of civil war grievances; its costs were real, but its alternative was potentially another confrontation between armies.
[C1.5] The proposition's word "usually" is its fatal weakness. There is no universal sequence where truth precedes peace. Sometimes silence is the price of survival.
