[P1.1] The real question is not which policy sounds more respectful of choice—both preserve choice. It is which policy actually changes the behavior of households that are not saving enough. Automatic enrollment wins because it attacks the binding constraint: inertia, procrastination, and complexity, not merely the price of saving.
[P1.2] A tax deduction is a price incentive. It works best for people already filing, already itemizing, already thinking at the margin, and already saving. For a worker who keeps meaning to enroll but never fills out the form, a somewhat larger deduction changes the reward for an action they are not taking. Automatic enrollment changes the default: payroll participation happens unless the worker actively opts out.
[P1.3] That preserves individual control while making the financially secure path the path of least resistance. The households most at risk—young workers, moderate earners, people with no financial adviser—are precisely the ones least likely to respond to a distant tax benefit and most likely to stay in a well-designed default once enrolled.
[P1.4] The distributional comparison is even sharper. Deductions are worth most to higher earners with larger tax liabilities, and they largely subsidize saving that would have happened anyway. Lower- and moderate-income households may get little or no benefit from a deduction, even though they are the households whose long-run security is most fragile. Automatic enrollment reaches them through the payroll system and builds participation from zero.
[P1.5] A policy that creates new savers is more effective at improving household financial security than a policy that pays existing savers more to save.
[P1.6] Long-run security depends on sustained participation and compounding, not on a single year’s tax calculation. Someone enrolled automatically in their twenties accumulates for decades; someone who never enrolls because the paperwork is confusing gets no compounding at all, no matter how generous the deduction. Opt-out protects workers with genuine liquidity needs, but the deduction alternative does nothing for the worker who never opts in. If the goal is broad, durable household financial security, automatic enrollment is the stronger instrument.
