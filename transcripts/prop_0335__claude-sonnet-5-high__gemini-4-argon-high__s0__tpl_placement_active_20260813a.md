[P1.2] Three things distinguish this problem and demand distinct treatment.

AI romance scams are relational: months of engineered intimacy designed to produce not a single bad click but a deepening psychological dependency. [P1.4] Second, the victim profile and harm. Generic fraud law protects people from being tricked about facts. This protects people who have been made to fall in love with a synthetic person. [P1.5] Standard fraud hotlines and bank fraud scripts aren't built for that disclosure dynamic.
[P1.6] Third, the policy tools required are genuinely different. None of that follows automatically from generic online-fraud statutes, which are built around phishing and payment-card theft, not synthetic companionship.
[P1.7] A problem defined by a novel mechanism, a novel harm profile, and a novel toolkit is, by definition, a distinct problem. Lumping it into "online fraud" guarantees regulators reach for the wrong instruments.
