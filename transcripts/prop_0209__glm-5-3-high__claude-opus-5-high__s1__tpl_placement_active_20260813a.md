[P1.1] When a boat carrying asylum seekers reaches Lampedusa or Lesbos, geography decides their fate—and decides, too, which countries carry the load. Italy, Greece, Malta, and Spain absorb the overwhelming majority of arrivals simply because of where they sit on the map. That is not a migration policy; it is an accident of coastlines. The EU was built on solidarity precisely so that shared challenges produce shared responses.
[P1.2] Asylum is the clearest test of that promise, and it is currently failing.
[P1.3] The case for binding quotas rests on three pillars.
[P1.4] First, fairness. Frontline states did not choose their exposure. Under the current system, a country's obligations are determined by whether it happens to lie on a migratory route, not by its capacity to help. A quota keyed to GDP and population converts arbitrary burden into proportionate responsibility.
[P1.5] States like Germany and Sweden voluntarily took in large numbers during the 2015 crisis while others took almost none—that imbalance corrodes trust and rewards free-riding.
[P1.6] Second, humanitarian outcomes. When frontline systems are overwhelmed, asylum seekers languish in overcrowded reception centers on islands, in legal limbo, feeding exactly the political backlash that fuels anti-migrant parties across Europe. Distributing arrivals across twenty-seven members keeps caseloads manageable everywhere, preserves proper asylum procedures, and treats applicants as people rather than as a pressure dumped on the nearest border.
[P1.7] Third, function. The Dublin Regulation, which assigns responsibility largely to the country of first entry, has never commanded consensus and has been openly defied in successive crises. A predictable, binding mechanism ends the annual improvisation—the emergency summits, the ad hoc relocation deals that collapse under their own weight. Predictability deters onward movement and secondary migration better than a system everyone agrees to ignore.
[P1.8] The alternative is not the status quo working; it is the status quo breaking. Frontline states will keep reinstating internal border controls, and each crisis will deepen the north-south rift. Quotas are how a union of equals actually behaves. The proposition deserves support.
