[P1.1] A rental’s price and conditions should be visible while people are choosing, not after they have chosen. Cleaning fees and resort fees are part of what a stay costs; house rules determine whether that stay is usable. Requiring disclosure before checkout or a booking request makes the advertised offer an offer people can actually evaluate.
[P1.2] Consider someone comparing two apartments for a weekend. One looks cheaper until a cleaning fee reverses the comparison. Or a family finds a suitable property, only to discover at checkout that its arrival time conflicts with a mandatory check-in window. These are not incidental details.
[P1.3] They can change which property the traveler should select.
[P1.4] The harm is not limited to being charged without consent. Late disclosure wastes comparison time and creates pressure to accept worse terms: the traveler has already coordinated plans, examined photographs, and stopped considering alternatives. “You can still leave” is a poor defense of a system that makes people repeat their search to discover the real deal. Disclosure belongs before that avoidable funnel, not merely before payment.
[P1.5] The requirement is also practical. Platforms can display cleaning and resort fees alongside the price and put house rules in a clearly labeled section of the listing, before any checkout or request-to-book action. Where charges depend on dates or party size, show the basis and update the amount when those details are entered. Nothing requires every rule to crowd a search-result thumbnail.
[P1.6] Finally, a common obligation improves competition. When one platform presents the real cost and another postpones mandatory charges, the less transparent offer can appear cheaper without being cheaper. Voluntary disclosure leaves honest presentation at a disadvantage.
[P1.7] The choice is not between transparency and convenient booking. It is between convenience built on usable information and convenience measured by how quickly users enter checkout. Require the terms before that gateway, so properties compete on what travelers will actually pay and receive.
