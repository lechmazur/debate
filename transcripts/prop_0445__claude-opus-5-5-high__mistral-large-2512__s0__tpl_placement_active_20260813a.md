[P1.2] It is between a system that protects jobs for a few and one that protects people for all.
[P1.3] First, rigid contracts suppress the very jobs workers need. When a firm needs state permission to shed workers past a size threshold, and dismissal disputes can drag on for years, rational employers respond predictably. [P1.6] India already has the plumbing for this: digital identity, direct benefit transfers, and worker registries like e-Shram. What is missing is the political trade.
[P1.7] Third, this model works. Denmark's "flexicurity" lets firms adjust easily while generous income support and retraining keep workers secure, and it sustains high employment and high trust. Security comes from the state's guarantee, not from chaining a worker to one employer.
