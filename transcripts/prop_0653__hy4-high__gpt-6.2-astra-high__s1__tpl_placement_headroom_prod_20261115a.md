[P1.1] Companies should not be allowed to let AI turn a named employee into a betting slip. Predicting an individual worker's future productivity, unionization likelihood, or chance of quitting is not neutral forecasting; it creates a hidden risk label before the worker has acted. That label will shape shifts, training, promotion, surveillance, and discipline. Consent is fiction: refusing the score may cost the job.
[P1.2] The problem is not mere inaccuracy. These predictions corrupt the choices they claim to inform. "Likely to unionize" means an algorithmic early-warning system against protected collective action. Flag a worker as organizing; managers can isolate them, stall promotion, or replace them while denying retaliation.
[P1.3] Scores for productivity and quitting are similarly self-fulfilling. If past evaluations rewarded long hours or a manager's favorites, the model learns that as merit. Give the "low potential" caregiver fewer good assignments, and the forecast looks accurate. Deny the "flight risk" a raise, and the eventual resignation is cited as proof.
[P1.4] Accuracy and audits do not solve this. Productivity depends on tools, supervision, training, health, and morale, not a fixed worker trait. Models infer from ambiguous proxies: badge swipes, keystrokes, schedule changes, absences, social ties. The employee cannot cross-examine a correlation; the firm may not understand the score.
[P1.5] After the harm, the algorithmic suggestion becomes an alibi.
[P1.6] The efficiency answer misses the scale change. Managers have always guessed, but AI makes suspicion continuous, cheap, invisible, and actionable across everyone. Aggregate staffing forecasts, real performance records, exit interviews, and direct conversations meet legitimate planning needs without condemning a named person by probability. Human judgment can be challenged, corrected, and tied to evidence.
[P1.7] A predictive score is a verdict before evidence. Ban this use because fair employment means judging workers by what they do, not by a machine's wager on disloyalty, decline, or departure.
