[P1.2] Consider someone whose migraines are keeping her from work. A commercial for a new medicine may do her a service by persuading her that she need not suffer in silence. But what she needs to know next is how the options compare for someone with her medical history, her budget, and her priorities. A company can describe its product truthfully, list its risks accurately, and still give her no reason to ask whether a less expensive treatment would work just as well.
[P1.3] No one earns a comparable return from buying airtime for that question.
[P1.4] The doctor can decline the request. That safeguard matters, but it does not make the advertising harmless. [P1.5] Prohibit pharmaceutical companies from advertising prescription medicines to consumers across mass media; moving the same pitch from television to a sponsored video does not change its purpose. Those routes preserve the useful invitation to get care without attaching a commercial answer before anyone has asked the clinical question.
