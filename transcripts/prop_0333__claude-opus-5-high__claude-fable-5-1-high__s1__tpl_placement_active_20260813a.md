[P1.1] A public broadcaster's licence to exist rests on one thing: that everybody, including people who will pay for it in 2045, actually encounters it. Judge this motion on that metric — not gross hours produced, but whether the next generation of taxpayers has any lived relationship with the institution.
[P1.2] On that metric, traditional channel expansion is spending money in a room the young have already left. A new linear channel, or a new bundle of on-demand catch-up shelves, competes for the attention of people who are already watching — mostly over-50s. It deepens loyalty among the loyal. It is the media equivalent of building another wing onto a library nobody under 25 walks past.
[P1.3] And it is not cheap: channels demand carriage, scheduling, continuity, marketing, and a permanent commissioning pipeline to fill the hours.
[P1.4] Contrast the alternative. Short-form video and creator partnerships put public-service material inside the feeds where under-30s genuinely live — TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram. Germany's public broadcasters recognised this when they built their youth offering as a digital-native, platform-distributed service rather than another terrestrial channel. The BBC moved BBC Three online for the same reason.
[P1.5] These were not fashion decisions; they were arithmetic about where the missing audience was.
[P1.6] Creator partnerships matter for a further reason CON will struggle with: trust. Young people do not inherit deference to institutions — they extend trust to individuals. A public broadcaster that lends its verification, archives and journalistic standards to a creator with a real audience is doing exactly the mission it was chartered for: raising the quality of information in the places where misinformation actually spreads. Fact-checking in short form, on a platform, from a face someone already follows, does more public good than the same money buried in slot 47 of an unwatched schedule.
[P1.7] Note the motion says prioritize, not abolish. Flagship drama, news and radio continue. The question is where the marginal pound goes. Spent on new channels, it buys redundancy.
