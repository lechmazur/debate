[P1.1] Grid reliability and decarbonization both hinge on one physical fact: electricity is only as clean and as reliable as your ability to move it from where it's generated to where it's needed. That is the case for transmission and market integration over storage subsidies.
[P1.2] Start with the math of averaging. Wind is blowing somewhere. Sun is shining somewhere. Storms hit one region while another basks in surplus solar.
[P1.3] A wire connecting Texas to the Southeast, or the Midwest to the Northeast, lets you cancel out local variability across a huge footprint instantly and for free, with no round-trip losses. Long-duration storage tries to solve the same problem by hoarding energy in one place and hoping the timing works out. Transmission solves it by spreading the problem across geography, which is strictly cheaper because weather is spatially decorrelated. You don't need to store what you can simply import.
[P1.4] Second, cost and speed. Storage subsidies pour money into a technology that is still expensive per unit of energy stored over long durations, and every dollar spent subsidizing it is a dollar not spent unlocking gigawatts of already-available cheap renewables that are stranded behind congested interconnection queues. Transmission and market integration multiply the value of existing generation instead of building expensive new hardware to hold energy hostage until needed.
[P1.5] Third, market integration is a force multiplier that storage cannot replicate. When regions can trade power in real time, price signals do the work of a thousand batteries: cheap surplus flows to where it's scarce, reducing curfew-style curtailment and reducing the need for any single region to be self-sufficient. Europe's interconnected grid and the U.S. experience with regional transmission organizations both show that integration reduces reserve margins needed and reduces reliance on any one technology.
[P1.6] Storage has a role, but as a supplement, not the primary lever. Building the wires first is the more effective, lower-cost, faster path to a reliable, decarbonized grid.
