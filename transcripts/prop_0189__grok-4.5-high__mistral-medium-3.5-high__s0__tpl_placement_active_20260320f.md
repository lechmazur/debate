[C1.3] Separation also introduces serious new problems. The proposition demands a fiscally reckless tear-up of urban cores when cheaper, adaptive fixes already work and improve over time.
[C1.4] Many cities are on track to meet clean water goals through incremental plans prioritizing the worst overflows. Full separation ignores that old sanitary sewers still need costly replacement regardless. The smart path is surgical investment in the most problematic areas, not a bankrupting gamble on an inflexible, city-wide solution that may not solve all problems and could create new ones.
