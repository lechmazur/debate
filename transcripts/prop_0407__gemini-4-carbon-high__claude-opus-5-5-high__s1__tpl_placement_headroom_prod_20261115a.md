[P1.1] An employer buys a worker’s labor, not ownership over their entire life. Yet without strong legal safeguards, a paycheck becomes a twenty-four-hour leash. Today, a warehouse worker attending a peaceful weekend rally, an accountant backing an unpopular candidate, or a clerk pursuing a lawful hobby at home can be fired Monday morning, not for failing at any duty, but for offending a manager’s personal sensibilities. [P1.2] Modern technology makes stronger protection urgent. Smartphones and online outrage cycles have erased the natural boundary between workplace and home. [P1.3] Democratic citizenship withers when ordinary people fear that speaking at a local council meeting, canvassing for a cause, or living unconventionally will cost them their livelihood. [P1.5] Stronger legal protection simply enforces a fair bargain: employers get to judge how workers do their jobs, not dictate how free citizens live their lives.
