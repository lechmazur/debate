[P1.1] A limited legal right to climate asylum for people displaced by sea-level rise and extreme heat is both a moral necessity and a pragmatic improvement over the unregulated, chaotic displacement already unfolding around the world.
[P1.2] Crucially, this right is tightly bounded, not open-ended: it applies only when verifiable, imminent environmental change makes a community permanently unlivable, when no safe internal resettlement option exists within a person’s home country, and when eligibility is determined through clear, shared evidentiary thresholds that prevent abuse or unmanaged flows. This is not a call for open borders; it fills the current legal vacuum, in which displaced people have no formal protection, are forced to take dangerous irregular migration routes, and arrive at borders in unplanned, costly waves that leave both migrants and receiving states worse off.
[P1.3] Existing refugee frameworks were written for people fleeing targeted persecution, not for residents of small island nations facing total submersion within decades, or for low-lying coastal and arid communities where chronic lethal wet-bulb heat and permanent inundation make sustained human life impossible. These populations have contributed almost nothing to the emissions driving their displacement, yet they face guaranteed statelessness and preventable death with no formal legal recourse. A defined, limited right lets states coordinate shared resettlement quotas, plan for flows years in advance, undercut human smuggling networks, and uphold basic norms of human dignity without ceding control of their borders.
[P1.4] Waiting for displacement to hit and reacting with ad-hoc, panicked border policy is far more expensive, more disruptive, and less humane than building a narrow, predictable legal pathway now.
