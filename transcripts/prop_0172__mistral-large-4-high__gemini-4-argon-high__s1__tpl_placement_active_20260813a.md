[P1.1] The ethical review of animal experimentation rests on a single criterion: the capacity to suffer. We do not review experiments on rocks because rocks have no interests; we review experiments on mice, dogs, and primates because they can feel pain, fear, and distress. When a brain organoid demonstrates rudimentary learning—when it can form associations, adapt behavior based on environmental feedback, and respond differentially to novel stimuli—it has crossed the threshold from biological tissue to a being with interests. Learning is not merely mechanical information processing; it is the behavioral signature of subjective experience, the mark of a creature that experiences outcomes as good or bad, and therefore has something at stake.
[P1.2] If we accept that moral status flows from sentience rather than species membership, then the biological substrate becomes ethically irrelevant. A neural network capable of rudimentary learning possesses the functional architecture of a mind, however primitive. To deny it ethical review while granting rigorous review to a mouse—whose cognitive capacities may be comparable to or even exceeded by the organoid—is speciesism in its purest form, privileging carbon-based biology over the complexity of neural organization that actually matters.
[P1.3] The frameworks applied to animal research—the 3Rs of replacement, reduction, and refinement, Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees, and systematic harm-benefit analyses—exist specifically to protect beings capable of welfare interests. An organoid that learns has welfare interests: it can be harmed, frustrated, or deprived of expected stimuli. The precautionary principle demands we apply these safeguards now, before the technology scales beyond oversight, creating a fait accompli of unregulated consciousness that we can only mourn, not prevent.
[P1.4] The question is not whether an organoid is a "brain," but whether it can suffer. Rudimentary learning is proof-of-concept that it can. Consistency and precaution require the same ethical review frameworks applied to animal experimentation.
