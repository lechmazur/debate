[C1.3] Second, the proposal misunderstands how science actually corrects itself. Progress does not happen through static, identical repetition. [C1.4] Direct replication, by contrast, is a conservative force. By earmarking funds strictly for repeating yesterday's work, we freeze scientific capital in place.
[C1.5] Third, creating dedicated, ring-fenced career paths for verification [P1.5] introduces toxic incentives. [C1.6] Science is indeed a risk-tolerant enterprise, and some portion of novel projects will inevitably fail. But the cure for this is open-science mandates, pre-registration, and rigorous peer review—not a mandatory tax that starves new ideas. We cannot build the future if we insist on paying scientists to continuously grade yesterday's homework.
