[C1.3] On a busy highway, a sudden, unprovoked deceleration because the car misread a roadside advertisement or an obscured sign is a primary recipe for high-speed rear-end collisions. PRO’s "safe default" actually makes the vehicle unpredictable to the human drivers behind it.
[C1.4] Second, defaulting ISA on creates dangerous friction during critical driving maneuvers. Safe driving is highly contextual. Sometimes, the safest action is to temporarily exceed the posted limit to quickly pass a swerving truck on a two-lane road, or to match the flow of traffic when merging. If the limiter is active by default, the driver must fight the system—pushing through a stiff accelerator pedal or digging through menus—in the exact split-second they need to focus on the road.
[C1.5] Finally, PRO champions the "opt-out" as a safeguard, but ignores automation complacency. When a system defaults to on, drivers stop actively monitoring speed and road context, trusting the machine. When the machine inevitably fails, the driver’s reaction time is severely delayed by automation surprise.
[C1.6] Leaving ISA optional ensures that drivers who understand its limitations can choose to use it, preventing the systemic rollout of a flawed technology that compromises vehicle predictability and overrides human judgment in critical moments. We must not mandate a default that trades active driving for erratic automation.
