[P1.1] Safe third country rules shift asylum responsibility to states asylum seekers previously transited, but this transfer is only legitimate if the receiving state guarantees three core, enforceable rights: work, appeal, and family unity. The entire justification for deeming a country "safe" rests on actual protection, not paper treaty commitments or surface-level metrics like the absence of active conflict. If a transited country denies enforceable work rights, asylum seekers are trapped in exploitative informal labor with no path to stability. Without independent appeal mechanisms, wrongful status denials go unaddressed, leaving people at unchecked risk of refoulement.
[P1.2] If family unity is not enforced, split families face indefinite separation, violating the foundational purpose of refugee protection. These three rights are non-negotiable minimums: work allows self-sufficiency, appeal prevents arbitrary denial of protection, family unity preserves core human dignity. No country that fails to enforce these rights can meaningfully be called safe for asylum seekers.
[P1.3] Any alternative justification for safe third country rules—border control efficiency, regional burden sharing, diplomatic convenience—fails the basic test of rights protection. A country may be labeled safe because it has low domestic conflict, but common practice shows far too many such designations ignore multi-year work bans, no meaningful independent appeal for status denials, and arbitrary family separation policies. For example, some designated safe third countries legally bar asylum seekers from working for years, leaving them dependent on meager aid or exploitative under-the-table jobs, with no avenue to challenge wrongful status rejections.
[P1.4] Sending an asylum seeker to such a jurisdiction abandons them to needless precarity. The proposition draws the only line that aligns safe third country designations with their stated purpose: actual, enforceable protection of affected people, not abstract administrative interests. States that adopt safe third country rules without requiring these enforceable rights are simply offloading vulnerable people to jurisdictions that will not protect them, which is never justified.
