[P1.1] The core of long-run household financial security is not the size of a tax subsidy, but whether a household saves at all. Automatic enrollment succeeds because it directly addresses the psychological and administrative barriers to saving, whereas expanding tax deductions only rewards those who have already overcome them.
[P1.2] First, auto-enrollment leverages the powerful force of human inertia. Under voluntary systems, individuals must actively research funds, fill out paperwork, and make complex asset-allocation decisions. This friction leads to chronic procrastination. By making enrollment the default, the path of least resistance becomes the path to wealth accumulation.
[P1.3] When workers must actively opt out rather than opt in, participation rates skyrocket, particularly among younger, lower-income, and minority workers who are historically left behind by traditional opt-in plans.
[P1.4] Second, tax deductions are fundamentally regressive and inefficient at generating net new savings. A tax deduction’s value scales with a taxpayer’s marginal tax rate. Consequently, increasing these deductions disproportionately benefits high-income earners who already possess the disposable income and financial literacy to save. For the vast majority of working-class households in lower tax brackets, a larger deduction offers negligible incentive and yields almost no extra capital.
[P1.5] Instead of creating new savers, expanded deductions simply subsidize affluent individuals who frequently shift existing assets into tax-advantaged accounts to minimize their tax liabilities without actually increasing their overall savings rate.
[P1.6] Finally, auto-enrollment builds systemic resilience. By establishing a habit of saving early in a worker’s career, it ensures compound interest works in their favor over decades. It transforms retirement saving from an elite financial planning privilege into a universal baseline.
[P1.7] If we want to secure the financial future of households nationwide, we must design systems for real human behavior. Auto-enrollment secures the vulnerable, expands the pool of savers, and builds genuine wealth, while tax deductions merely shelter the wealth of those who are already secure.
