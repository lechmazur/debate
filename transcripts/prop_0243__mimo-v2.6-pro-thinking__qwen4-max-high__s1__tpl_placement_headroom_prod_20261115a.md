[P1.1] The core mechanism is simple arithmetic. Every ride-hail trip has three phases, not one: the driver travels empty to the pickup, carries the passenger, then often cruises empty again toward the next request. Only the middle phase serves a traveler, but all three occupy scarce downtown lane-miles. Add that deadheading to the trips themselves and ride-hail platforms inject more vehicle traffic into the most congested corridors than the trips they replace.
[P1.2] That substitution is the second half of the story. Ride-hail does not mainly pull drivers off the road — the taxi fleets it displaced were smaller and tightly capped. Its real market is trips that would otherwise have happened on transit, on foot, by bike, or not at all. When a two-mile subway ride becomes a cheap app tap, one private vehicle replaces a mode that moves dozens of people per lane.
[P1.3] Research in major U.S. cities consistently points the same direction: platform travel adds net vehicle miles traveled, especially in dense cores, and some of its growth is induced demand — trips generated by the convenience of the service itself.
[P1.4] The congestion is also qualitatively worse than ordinary traffic. Pickup and drop-off happens exactly where space is scarcest: outside restaurants, stations, arenas, and airports. Drivers stop in travel lanes, double-park, circle the block while the passenger walks down, or idle in "TNC" holding lots that then pour into the airport approach road in waves. A single curb occupied for three minutes blocks a bus carrying forty people.
[P1.5] This is not a rounding error on congestion; it is concentrated friction at the bottlenecks that decide whether a network moves at all.
[P1.6] And the platforms amplify their own congestion. Pricing draws more drivers precisely when and where demand spikes, so surge periods flood downtowns with additional empty vehicles competing for the same curb and the same lanes.
[P1.7] The question is net effect on major cities overall. More vehicle-miles, low-occupancy cars replacing high-capacity modes, and bottleneck-blocking pickups all point one way: ride-hail made congestion worse.
