[P1.2] The harm is the mimic. Using public writing to improve a generic model is not the same as shipping a product that pretends to be the dead. That product trades on intimacy: grief, fandom, authority. It can put words in a dead parent's mouth, recast a dead artist's politics, or make a dead colleague appear to endorse what they never would.
[P1.3] Distortion is how these systems work. They fill gaps with plausible sentences the person never said. The dead cannot correct them.
[P1.4] Consent after death is a fiction. Families fight. Estates sell. Companies treat every old email, photo, and post as raw material because almost nobody opted out of a technology that did not yet exist.
[P1.5] Silence was never informed permission to become interactive merchandise. Only an explicit opt-in before death treats the person as the owner of their likeness instead of a leftover dataset.
[P1.6] The law already refuses to treat names, faces, and performances as free for the taking. Publicity rights and control of unpublished work exist because identity survives as a protectable interest. A talking replica is more invasive than a poster. It can be licensed, scaled, and updated forever.
[P1.7] Without a prohibition, the incentive is simple. Digital remains are cheap and profitable. The default becomes harvest first. The living inherit a market in the dead they never authorized, and the dead have no veto.
[P1.8] Opt-in is not hostility to technology. It is the minimum condition for turning a human being into a product.
