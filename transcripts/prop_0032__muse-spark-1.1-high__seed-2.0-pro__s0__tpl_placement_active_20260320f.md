[P1.1] Direct-to-consumer testing collapses two worlds into one checkout cart. That's harmless when you're checking Neanderthal percentages. It's dangerous when you're handed a prediction that you carry a mutation that raises breast cancer risk fivefold, or that you are likely to develop untreatable dementia. High-impact predictive results are medicine, and medicine without interpretation is just data with the potential to wound.
[P1.2] These results are consistently misunderstood without help. Risk is not destiny; 60% lifetime risk is not 100%. A positive on a SNP chip is not a clinical diagnosis - it needs confirmatory sequencing. And a negative on a narrow Ashkenazi-founder panel is not a clean bill of health.
[P1.3] Lay customers cannot be expected to know that. Primary care doctors aren't trained for it either, which is why clinical genetics has always paired testing with counseling. A certified genetic counselor explains penetrance, familial implications, next steps, and what not to do yet.
[P1.4] Without that bridge, predictable harms follow. Severe anxiety, fatalism after APOE disclosure, prophylactic surgeries pursued after unconfirmed positives, false reassurance after incomplete negatives, parents terminating pregnancies on misunderstood carrier data. The distressed customer doesn't call the testing company for help - they book an urgent appointment already terrified, or they make a life-altering decision alone. The company keeps the subscription fee while the public health system absorbs the fallout.
[P1.5] Requiring access before release doesn't ban information; it makes choice informed. Real autonomy isn't getting a red exclamation point at midnight with no one to ask what it means. For billion-dollar firms, providing telehealth access to a certified counselor before unlocking BRCA, Lynch, long QT or APOE results is a minimal, narrowly targeted cost.
[P1.6] We regulate how you dispense drugs. Regulate how you dispense destiny.
