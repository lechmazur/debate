[P1.1] Start with a simple question: what is the public actually buying? Under enrollment-based funding, it buys bodies in seats on census day. A university receives the same check whether a freshman graduates on time or drops out in October with loans and no credential. That is not neutral.
[P1.2] It rewards recruiting students and quietly abandoning them.
[P1.3] First, incentives. Institutions respond to what they are paid for. Pay for enrollment and you get marketing budgets, crowded intro lectures, and little reason to fix the advising bottleneck that costs a junior a semester because a required course was full. Pay for completion and those unglamorous fixes become core business: proactive advising, smarter course scheduling, small emergency grants that keep a student from leaving over a few hundred dollars.
[P1.4] Public universities serving mostly low-income students have raised graduation rates substantially through exactly these changes. Funding should make every campus want to copy them.
[P1.5] Second, the current system hurts most the very students CON will claim to protect. The worst outcome in higher education isn't a modest-paying degree. It's debt without a degree. Students who borrow and never finish struggle to repay far more than graduates do, because they carry the cost without the credential.
[P1.6] Enrollment funding bankrolls that outcome. Outcomes funding targets it.
[P1.7] Third, the motion says "demonstrated improvements," and that word matters. This is not ranking an elite flagship against a regional campus on raw earnings. Each institution is measured against its own baseline, and sensible designs weight gains for low-income, first-generation, and adult students more heavily. That answers cream-skimming directly.
[P1.8] A university that stops admitting poorer students throws away its most valuable chances to improve.
[P1.9] Earnings belong in the formula too. Taxpayers subsidize universities because degrees are supposed to expand opportunity. Asking whether graduates actually earn more is basic accountability, not crass commercialism. It works alongside completion rather than replacing it.
[P1.10] So the clash is this: CON must defend a system that pays the same for failure as for success. We say public money should follow results.
