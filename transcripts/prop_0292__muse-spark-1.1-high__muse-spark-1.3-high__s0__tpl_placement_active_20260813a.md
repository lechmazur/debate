[P1.1] Making a fake porn video of a classmate, coworker or ex used to require skill. Now it takes an app and 30 seconds. That shift is not more discourse. It is a scalable weapon for sexual abuse, overwhelmingly aimed at women and girls, that is cheap to make and nearly impossible to contain once posted.
[P1.2] The injuries are not abstract offense. Victims lose jobs, leave schools, are stalked by strangers who think the images are real, and live with searchable sexual forgery tied to their name for years. The violation is sexual privacy itself — your likeness being used for others' gratification without consent, even if your body was never there. No counter-speech fixes that.
[P1.3] Criminal law alone fails because prosecutors are overloaded and victims want control and removal, not only punishment. Defamation fails because even when labeled fake, explicit forgery still harms. Copyright fails because you don't own your face. Without an explicit claim, victims are forced into ill-fitting doctrines while platforms shrug and creators hide behind novelty.
[P1.4] An explicit private right of action solves that. It gives the depicted person power to sue creators and knowing distributors for damages, injunctions, and swift takedown orders. It puts agency with the victim, creates financial deterrence criminal law cannot, and gives judges a clear, tailored standard instead of stretching old law.
[P1.5] Does that narrow some speech? Yes, and justifiably. Distributing non-consensual sexually explicit forgeries is not political critique, parody of power, or truth-seeking debate. It is low-value false impersonation for sexual use.
[P1.6] Every system already accepts that privacy, sexual harassment, and consent can limit speech. This is that principle updated for AI.
[P1.7] When we must choose between someone's ability to produce fake nudes of a non-consenting person and that person's ability to live, work, and define their own sexuality, the choice is clear. Name the harm and give victims the tools to stop it.
