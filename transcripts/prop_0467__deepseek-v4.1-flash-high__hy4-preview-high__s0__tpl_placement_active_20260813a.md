[P1.1] The motion does not ask whether every hack is war. It asks whether a cyberattack on civilian critical infrastructure that produces armed-attack effects should be treated as one. The answer is yes, because international law governs consequences, not hardware.
[P1.2] When a state operation shuts down a power grid in winter, poisons water treatment, or disables hospital systems across a region, it can kill more people than a missile strike. If law says that identical death and destruction is only a crime or a nuisance when delivered through code, it creates an enormous safe harbor for aggression. Article 51 self-defense must turn on scale and effects: death, injury, physical destruction, or collapse of essential services. That is an effects-based test, not a new license.
[P1.3] Equivalence also does not mean automatic war. It means the victim state may consider proportional military response under the same strict conditions as kinetic armed attack: attribution, necessity, proportionality, distinction, and UN Charter limits. Attribution is hard, but hard evidence questions are not solved by pretending the attack is not attack. States already make attribution judgments; they can meet a clear and convincing standard before using force.
[P1.4] Deterrence is the payoff. If a hostile state believes it can paralyze civilian infrastructure below the armed-attack threshold, cyber becomes the cheapest way to wage war while denying it. Declaring equivalence restores the cost. It tells adversaries: if you cause armed-attack effects, you face armed-attack consequences.
[P1.5] And it gives responsible states a legal framework to coordinate collective response, rather than each improvising.
[P1.6] CON will warn of escalation and of treating civilian infrastructure as a military target. But the rule is narrow: civilian infrastructure remains protected; an attack that causes armed-attack-level harm is precisely what triggers defense. Proportional response can be cyber, diplomatic, economic, or military. The law needs a category for the most dangerous cyber operations.
[P1.7] Treat effects as effects, and war by keyboard cannot hide behind a legal loophole.
