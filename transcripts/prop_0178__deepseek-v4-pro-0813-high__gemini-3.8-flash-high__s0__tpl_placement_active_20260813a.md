[P1.1] Diet-related illness is not primarily an information problem; it is an exposure problem. Ultra-processed foods dominate shelves and diets because they are engineered for cheapness, convenience, and repeat purchase, often with salt, sugar, and fat at levels that override satiety and shape taste preferences. Labeling and education ask individuals to swim against that current at every meal. Reformulation mandates change the current.
[P1.2] The decisive advantage is reach. A cap on salt, sugar, or fat alters the default product before it reaches the shopper. The hurried parent, the child, the shift worker, the person with limited literacy or nutrition knowledge, and the consumer who simply ignores labels all receive the health benefit without needing to notice, understand, or act. Education, by contrast, works best among those with time, resources, and high health motivation, which means it often widens rather than narrows health inequalities.
[P1.3] Magnitude also favors mandates. Small reductions across thousands of ultra-processed products produce continuous, population-wide declines in exposure. Salt caps lower blood pressure risk across a whole population; sugar reduction cuts calorie density and dental and metabolic risk; fat reformulation removes harmful nutrient combinations at scale. Labeling campaigns can shift some purchases, but they rarely change the majority of eating occasions and are easily overwhelmed by price, marketing, and convenience.
[P1.4] Reformulation also resets norms. Once industry must meet limits, product reformulation becomes the baseline, recipes change, and children’s palates develop with lower thresholds for salt and sweetness. Consumers can still choose, but they choose from a safer supply. Labeling remains a useful supplement, but it cannot match the reach, equity, immediacy, or population dose of mandatory caps.
[P1.5] The test is whether an intervention improves health without requiring the whole population to consistently change behavior; only reformulation mandates pass that test at scale.
