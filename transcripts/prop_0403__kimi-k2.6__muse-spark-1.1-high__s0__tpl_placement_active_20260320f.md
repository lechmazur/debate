[P1.1] When a professional sports team accepts a major public subsidy, it does not simply receive a check. It enters a social contract with the taxpayers who funded it. The proposition asks only that this contract be enforced: if public money built your stadium, financed your infrastructure, or filled your tax abatements, you cannot unilaterally dissolve the partnership and flee. A binding community approval process is the minimum democratic safeguard required to prevent the systematic exploitation of cities and their residents by mobile franchise owners.
[P1.2] The mechanism is well understood and repeats across the country. Team owners leverage monopoly control over league membership to extract hundreds of millions in direct subsidies, land, and tax breaks from municipalities already trapped in a race to the bottom. Once the public commits to bonds and decades of debt service, the owner holds all the leverage. Relocation threats resurface whenever a more lucrative market beckons, leaving taxpayers with empty debt obligations, maintenance liabilities, and vacant arenas.
[P1.3] This is not market competition; it is structured extraction. The public is not a venture capitalist accepting risk for upside; it is a captive partner held hostage by artificial scarcity.
[P1.4] Opponents will frame this as an infringement on business flexibility. But that flexibility was already purchased with public funds. You cannot accept a nine-figure taxpayer handout—money forcibly collected from working residents—and then claim an absolute right to abandon the very community that paid for your facility. Toothless town halls and advisory council votes are easily ignored.
[P1.5] Only a binding approval requirement creates the reciprocity necessary to make the subsidy relationship legitimate rather than predatory.
[P1.6] The principle extends beyond any single sport or city. Public wealth should never be expropriated by private franchise owners without durable democratic control. Communities deserve more than promises printed on glossy press releases. They deserve a binding veto.
