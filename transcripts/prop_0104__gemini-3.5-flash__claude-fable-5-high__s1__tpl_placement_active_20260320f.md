[P1.1] Youth firearm deaths are preventable design failures. Safe-storage laws address this crisis by requiring firearms to be locked or secured when not under the owner's immediate control. This mandate targets the primary source of weapons used in youth suicides, accidental shootings, and school violence: the home.
[P1.2] The protective mechanism is simple and powerful. Impulsive youth suicide attempts are highly time-sensitive; inserting even a brief delay to bypass a lockbox can be the difference between life and death. Similarly, curious children cannot accidentally discharge a weapon they cannot access. By establishing a legal standard for responsible storage, these laws shift safety norms and incentivize secure habits, preventing unauthorized access before a tragedy occurs.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, the burden on lawful gun owners is negligible. Opponents argue that safe storage compromises home defense. However, modern technology has rendered this argument obsolete. Biometric safes, rapid-access lockboxes, and quick-release trigger locks allow authorized users to retrieve a firearm in seconds, even in darkness.
[P1.4] Safe-storage laws do not restrict gun ownership, limit ammunition, or prevent carrying for self-defense. They simply require that when a lethal weapon is left unattended, it is secured.
[P1.5] When weighing these interests, the ledger is clear. On one side of the scale is the life of a child—an irreversible, devastating loss. On the other is the minor habit of scanning a fingerprint or entering a quick code.
