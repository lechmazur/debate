[P1.1] Live sports and sports betting are not two products sharing a break. [P1.2] When a casino ad runs during a sitcom, the viewer is not sitting inside the product. When a sports-betting ad runs during a live match, the viewer is. The game supplies the information, the emotion, and the clock. In-play markets turn every timeout into a sales moment.
[P1.3] The ad does not merely remind you that gambling exists; it invites you to bet on the thing you are already watching, right now, with an app in your pocket. That is a different causal path than a delayed invitation to visit a casino next weekend.
[P1.4] The audience is different too. Live sports remain a mass, family, and youth-facing spectacle. Children absorb the pairing of fandom and betting as if it belonged to the sport. Other gambling ads are easier to schedule away from that audience.
[P1.5] The live-broadcast slot is uniquely leaky: you cannot watch the game without taking the pitch.
[P1.6] Harm scales with proximity. Impulse, alcohol, group emotion, and instant markets collide during coverage by design. Treating an in-play promo the same as a Friday-night lottery commercial pretends those environments are interchangeable. They are not.
[P1.7] This is not a general ban on gambling speech. It is a context rule: the closer the ad sits to the live event, the stricter the limit—fewer spots, no in-play prompts, no odds crawls—because that is where advertising stops informing and starts refereeing the match.
