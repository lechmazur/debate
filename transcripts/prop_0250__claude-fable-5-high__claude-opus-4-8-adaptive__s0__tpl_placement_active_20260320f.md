[P1.1] Democracy's central promise is that government answers to all of us — not to the loudest, the richest, or the most enraged. Voluntary voting quietly breaks that promise. In low-turnout democracies, the electorate that actually shows up is systematically older, wealthier, and more educated than the population. Politicians, being rational, build policy for the people who vote and ignore the people who don't.
[P1.2] The result is a feedback loop: the marginalized are neglected, so they disengage, so they're neglected further. Mandatory turnout severs that loop in one stroke. When everyone votes, every citizen's interests carry electoral weight, and no party can win by quietly hoping the inconvenient half of the country stays home.
[P1.3] This is not speculative. Australia has required turnout for roughly a century, with small fines comparable to a parking ticket. Turnout reliably exceeds ninety percent, the system enjoys broad cross-party public support, and Australian elections are notably less consumed by the base-mobilization arms race that defines politics elsewhere. When every citizen votes, campaigns must persuade the middle rather than inflame the fringe — compulsory voting is a structural moderating force precisely when polarization is democracy's gravest disease.
[P1.4] Note what the proposition actually demands: showing up, not voting a particular way. Citizens remain free to spoil their ballot or select "none of the above" where offered. The duty is minimal — minutes, every few years — and the penalty proportional and civil, like the obligations we already accept for jury duty, taxation, and the census. Nobody calls jury duty tyranny; we recognize that self-government requires a floor of shared participation.
[P1.5] So the question for this debate is simple: is a parking-ticket-sized nudge too high a price for a government that genuinely represents its whole people? Any honest weighing says no. The alternative — elections decided by a skewed, shrinking, self-selected slice of the citizenry — is the real affront to democratic freedom.
