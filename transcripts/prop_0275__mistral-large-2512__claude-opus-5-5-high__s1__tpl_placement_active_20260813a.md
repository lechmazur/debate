[P1.1] Transparency is the bedrock of fair commerce, and vacation-rental platforms that hide mandatory fees until the final checkout screen are engaging in a bait-and-switch that harms every traveler. These are not optional add-ons; they are unavoidable costs that every guest must pay. Requiring disclosure before users click into checkout isn’t just reasonable—it’s the only way to prevent platforms from exploiting the sunk-cost fallacy, where travelers who have already invested time comparing options and entering personal details feel pressured to accept hidden fees rather than start over.
[P1.2] This rule doesn’t stifle business—it forces platforms to compete honestly. Hotels already display resort fees upfront, and airlines show baggage costs before checkout. Vacation rentals shouldn’t get a loophole to deceive customers. When platforms bury fees, they distort the entire market: properties with lower base prices but high hidden fees appear artificially cheaper, while honest hosts who include all costs upfront are penalized in search rankings.
[P1.3] Upfront disclosure levels the playing field, rewarding transparency and letting travelers make apples-to-apples comparisons.
[P1.4] House rules are no different. A family booking a cabin shouldn’t discover at checkout that the property bans children under 12, or that quiet hours start at 8 p.m.—rules that could make the stay unusable. These aren’t minor details; they’re dealbreakers that determine whether a rental fits a guest’s needs. Requiring disclosure before checkout respects the user’s time and prevents platforms from trapping travelers in a booking process only to spring last-minute restrictions that force them to cancel or accept an unsuitable stay.
[P1.5] If a platform can’t show its true costs and rules until the final step, it’s not facilitating commerce—it’s running a shell game.
