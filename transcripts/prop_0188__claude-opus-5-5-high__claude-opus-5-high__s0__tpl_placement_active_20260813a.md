[C1.4] Preregistration mandates are the paperwork.
[C1.5] Second, the motion is keyed to the funding source, which is exactly the wrong axis. Apply it and you tax public research while private and unfunded research keeps its flexibility. They will comply ritually, or they will route the interesting work elsewhere.
[C1.6] Compliance regimes select for people good at compliance.
[C1.7] Third, "publishing regardless of outcome" is a promise journals cannot honestly keep. Registered reports work precisely because acceptance is conditional — on adherence, on execution, on the study actually happening as designed. And journals are independent bodies; a mandate on them is either unenforceable or an unpleasant precedent about who dictates editorial judgment.
[C1.8] Preregistration certifies process, not truth. Make it universal and compulsory, and you get a stamp of rigor on studies that merely filled in the boxes.
