A local family relying on a traditional mortgage, needing an inspection, and constrained by standard closing timelines simply cannot compete against a private equity firm.
[P1.3] When a market is already tight, this asymmetric advantage doesn't just shift who gets the house—it systematically crowds out the middle class.
[P1.4] Furthermore, these investors do not solve the housing shortage; they exploit it. They rarely build new single-family homes. Instead, they absorb existing inventory. By monopolizing the starter-home segment, they sever the bottom rungs of the property ladder.
[P1.5] Local buyers are forced into renting, often from the very same institutional landlords who outbid them, resulting in massive wealth extraction from local communities to distant shareholders.
[P1.6] The proposition specifically targets tight housing markets where this crowding out is most acute. It simply removes the most predatory, non-productive demand from the bidding war. It ensures that when a limited supply of existing homes is available, the people who will actually live in them, support local schools, and build long-term equity in the community get a fair shot at buying them.
