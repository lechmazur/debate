[P1.2] First, voluntary commitments are structurally inadequate. Under current frameworks, governments routinely backtrack when domestic fossil fuel lobbies exert pressure. [P1.3] If only a few nations phase out coal, heavy industry simply relocates to jurisdictions with cheaper, dirtier power. A synchronized phase-out creates a unified global market signal, permanently depressing the cost of clean energy alternatives. [P1.4] Second, differentiated support is the engine that makes this common timeline viable. The opposition will likely argue that a uniform deadline ignores economic realities. [P1.5] This is not charity; it is the pragmatic price of global compliance. Without targeted financial support, growing economies will inevitably build new coal plants to meet surging baseline energy demand. [P1.6] Finally, the stakes demand binding coordination. Coal remains the single largest source of global carbon emissions and a primary driver of lethal particulate air pollution. Tinkering at the margins with voluntary targets guarantees we miss critical climate thresholds and lock in decades of warming.
[P1.7] A binding, differentiated phase-out transforms a fragmented, failing voluntary system into a coordinated, enforceable global strategy. We must prioritize actionable, binding commitments over comfortable, ineffective promises that appease fossil fuel lobbies.

[C1.5] Under PRO's treaty, governments facing an energy shock must choose between blackouts and breach—and every breach erodes the international legal order PRO claims to strengthen.
