Pursuing thousands of anonymous, distributed harassers through individual criminal prosecutions is hopelessly slow; by the time police trace a single account, the victim has already been driven from their home or job.
[P1.2] A legal duty to mitigate foreseeable offline harms places responsibility on the only actor capable of stopping the machinery in real time. Applying a standard duty of care does not require platforms to predict every lone actor. [P1.3] Critics often invoke free expression, pretending that disrupting a harassment campaign is indistinguishable from censoring political debate. That confuses viewpoint with coordinated conduct. Mitigating foreseeable offline harm targets network behavior like synchronized brigading, doxxing, and stalking logistics, not dissenting ideas. [P1.4] In every other industry, entities that profit from bringing people together must take reasonable steps to prevent foreseeable physical danger. Digital platforms should not remain above the law when their architecture turns coordinated malice into offline ruin.
