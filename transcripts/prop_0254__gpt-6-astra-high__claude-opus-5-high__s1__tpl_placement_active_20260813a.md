[P1.2] National well-being means measurable outcomes: physical and mental health, material security, access to housing, environmental quality, and people’s assessment of their lives. [P1.3] The first reason is that GDP measures production, not its ultimate value. Pollution cleanup and treatment for preventable illness add economic activity; avoiding that harm may deliver greater welfare with less spending. [P1.5] Second, the headline changes incentives. [P1.6] Finally, this is not an argument against growth. Where higher incomes fund nutrition, housing, and public services, growth is a powerful route to well-being.
