[P1.1] The urgency is clear: public schools must prioritize tangible, embodied enrichment over screen-based expansion. After-school sports build teamwork and resilience while directly combating the growing childhood obesity epidemic and often lead to lifelong healthy habits. Arts programs foster creativity and self-expression, addressing the well-documented and significant decline in fine motor skills from excessive device use, and provide tactile, sensory experiences that digital interfaces cannot match. Unstructured play allows children to develop social navigation, problem-solving, and emotional regulation in ways that structured digital modules cannot replicate, as it encourages spontaneous collaboration and imaginative thinking.
[P1.2] Digital programs cannot substitute for the physical and social development that comes from team sports or collaborative art projects, which inherently require in-person interaction and cooperation. Many students lack reliable internet or devices at home, making digital enrichment inaccessible, whereas a soccer field or art studio is available to all. Excessive screen time is linked to attention issues and reduced physical activity—problems that more digital offerings would exacerbate. Furthermore, digital programs often require ongoing licensing fees, device maintenance, and technical support, creating long-term costs that physical activities do not.
[P1.3] Schools should first ensure every student has access to the proven benefits of movement, creativity, and peer interaction. Only after these foundational experiences are universally available should schools invest in additional digital enrichment. The human development benefits of sports, arts, and play are immediate and irreplaceable; digital programs are supplementary at best. Prioritizing physical and creative activities also addresses equity gaps, as low-income students are less likely to have access to structured sports or arts outside of school, and these programs often require minimal additional infrastructure beyond existing facilities, making them sustainable long-term investments in student well-being.
