[P1.1] A library burns and we call it a catastrophe; a language dies and we call it a charity case. That asymmetry is exactly what this motion corrects.
[P1.2] Cultural memory is public infrastructure. Like flood defenses, it is a public good: no private actor captures the full value of preserving a language or an oral archive, so markets systematically underproduce it. Charity does not fix this — it worsens it. Donations collapse in recessions, when communities are most stressed, and flow to photogenic causes with wealthy patrons, not to a valley of two thousand speakers.
[P1.3] Treating preservation as charitable makes a people's memory hostage to the generosity of strangers.
[P1.4] Second, irreversibility. When the last fluent speaker dies, no later generosity can recover the ecological knowledge, oral law, and poetry encoded in that grammar. Extinction is permanent; charity is episodic. You cannot run a twenty-year immersion pipeline on annual grant cycles and goodwill.
[P1.5] Only a standing fund matches the time horizon of the problem.
[P1.6] Third, responsibility. Many minority languages are endangered not by accident but by policy — residential schools, forced assimilation, laws punishing children for speaking their mother tongue. A state that helped break an inheritance cannot declare its repair a private matter. Public funding here is not generosity; it is restitution.
[P1.7] Finally, this works and it is cheap. New Zealand sustains Māori revitalization through permanent public institutions; Hawai'i's immersion schools rebuilt a speaker base that had nearly vanished; Wales stabilized Welsh through sustained public commitment. Public funding also delivers what charity cannot: continuity across election cycles, teacher-training pipelines, and archival standards at national scale — all for a fraction of routine infrastructure budgets.
[P1.8] The clash tonight is not whether these cultures have value — my opponent will concede that. It is whether something irreplaceable should depend on the most fragile funding model available. It should not. Standing funds are how a society says its memory matters.
