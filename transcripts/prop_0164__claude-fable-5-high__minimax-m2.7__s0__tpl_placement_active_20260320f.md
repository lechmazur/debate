[P1.1] Science’s entire claim to authority rests on one promise: that its findings can be checked. Right now, public funders pay almost exclusively for the first claim and almost never for the check. The result is predictable and documented. Large-scale replication efforts in psychology found that roughly half of landmark findings failed to reproduce.
[P1.2] Cancer biology replication projects struggled to confirm many high-profile preclinical results. Drug companies have reported abandoning programs after failing to reproduce academic findings they had bet millions on. This is not a quirk; it is the structural consequence of a funding system that rewards novelty exclusively.
[P1.3] The core argument is simple: unverified findings are not knowledge, they are inventory. Every dollar spent on a novel project that builds on a false result is a dollar burned. When dozens of labs spend years chasing a finding that was noise, the cost of not replicating dwarfs the cost of replicating. Replication is not a tax on discovery; it is quality control that makes discovery compound rather than collapse.
[P1.4] Why a fixed, reserved share rather than goodwill? Because this is a textbook collective action failure. No individual scientist gains career credit from replication; no program officer gains prestige from funding it. For decades everyone has agreed replication matters and almost no one has paid for it.
[P1.5] Only a structural earmark changes the equilibrium. A fixed share also creates a career path—journals, tenure committees, and trainees respond when funders signal that verification is real science.
[P1.6] And the trade-off is modest by design: a meaningful share, not a majority. Reserve perhaps a single-digit slice of budgets and target it where stakes are highest—findings shaping clinical guidelines, policy, or large follow-on investment. The marginal novel grant we forgo is, statistically, the weakest one funded; the replication we gain protects the value of everything else.
[P1.7] A funder’s job is not to maximize the number of new claims. It is to maximize true ones. That requires paying for verification.
