[C1.1] Schools cannot keep adding core subjects without something breaking. Math, science, and literacy already strain teachers, timetables, and budgets to their limits. Making AI and manipulation-resistant media literacy another assessed spine forces a zero-sum choice: less time for algebra, less time for lab work, or fewer hours learning to write a coherent paragraph. The child who cannot read fluently will struggle with any media literacy framework, core or not—so we should prioritize the foundations that make such literacy possible rather than layering another requirement on shaky basics.
[C1.2] The claim that optional modules fail the vulnerable ignores how schools already deliver targeted support. Counselors, mentors, and existing systems can identify at-risk students and direct them to mandatory interventions far more precisely than any blanket core requirement. A universal class risks disengaging resistant students through forced inclusion, while the truly needy still find ways to avoid engagement. The solution is targeted requirements for those who need them, not universal compulsion.
[C1.3] Moreover, this domain evolves faster than any curriculum committee can move. Today's cutting-edge deepfake becomes tomorrow's laughably obvious fake as detection tools advance. Core subjects require years of stability for textbooks, standardized tests, and teacher training. By the time a new AI literacy core subject is implemented, its examples may be outdated, teaching students to recognize threats that have evolved beyond recognition.
[C1.4] The integration PRO describes is achievable without a new subject. Source verification belongs in history, bias analysis in English, statistical literacy in math, and experimental design in science. This embedded approach reaches every student through existing structures, teaches skills in context, and avoids the opportunity cost of another siloed class. The answer is not another mandatory subject; it's smarter use of the curriculum we already have and that can adapt as the landscape changes.
