[P1.3] Notice what this motion already concedes. Organizers may impose anti-scalping limits, provided they are narrowly tailored. Face-value resale caps, per-buyer quantity limits, transfer windows close to showtime, identity matching at the gate, bans on automated bulk purchasing — each of these strikes at the actual scalping model, which is acquiring scarce inventory in volume and flipping it at multiples. None of them requires stranding the fan who simply cannot attend.
[P1.4] Blanket non-transferability is also badly aimed. Professionals route around it with mule accounts and credential handoffs; the only person who reliably obeys a locked barcode is the ordinary buyer. A rule marketed as fan protection conveniently funnels every resale dollar through the house, and that conflict of interest should make us skeptical whenever the lock is called necessary.
[P1.5] So the clash is not whether scalping is ugly. It is whether confiscating everyone's exit is the least restrictive way to fight it. My opponent must defend the necessity of the blanket ban, not the villainy of the scalper. Tailoring exists, it is administratively ordinary, and it leaves the honest fan whole.
[P1.6] That is why the right should be the rule and the restriction the exception.
