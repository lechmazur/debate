[C1.3] Tighter testing is not a claim that domestic workers will magically appear. It is the mechanism that identifies which shortages are real, where they are, and on what terms. Testing can distinguish a surgeon in a saturated metro market from a nurse in an underserved region; recognition alone cannot.
[C1.4] PRO’s own examples show targeted shortage management, not recognition outranking testing. Reforms that speed nurse credentialing or tech migration usually operate inside shortage lists, employer sponsorship, licensing checks, and recruitment rules. [C1.5] Nor does global competition favor blanket speed. Skilled migrants choose destinations with credible routes to jobs and professional standing. A regime that says credentials will be recognized quickly where shortages and labor standards are demonstrated is more durable—and more attractive—than one that treats every foreign qualification as a ticket. Accelerate recognition when the labor-market evidence supports it; do not let it replace that evidence.
