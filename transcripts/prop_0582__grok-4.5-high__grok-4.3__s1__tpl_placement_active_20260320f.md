[P1.1] Allowing Medicare to cover GLP-1 anti-obesity drugs broadly will cut total US healthcare spending over the next five years once drug costs are netted out. Obesity powers Medicare’s most expensive trajectories: repeated heart-failure admissions, diabetes complications that end in dialysis or amputation, knee and hip replacements, sleep-apnea devices, and eventual long-term care. Those events and their sequelae dominate claims far more than any monthly injection.
[P1.2] GLP-1 agents produce clinically meaningful weight loss of roughly 15 percent or more and simultaneously improve glycemic control, blood pressure, inflammation, and cardiac risk markers within months. In older adults who already carry multimorbidity, that cascade reduces acute events—myocardial infarctions, strokes, decompensated heart failure—and cancels or postpones costly elective procedures. Each avoided hospitalization, stent, or joint replacement frees tens of thousands of dollars that more than cover a year of therapy for multiple patients. Because the highest utilizers sit inside Medicare, even modest uptake generates system-wide savings early enough to appear inside a five-year window.
[P1.3] The sticker-price objection treats drug acquisition as an isolated line item. In reality prices are negotiated, competition among multiple agents is intensifying, and the alternative is continued escalation of polypharmacy, emergency visits, and institutional care. Coverage simply moves the benefit to the population whose baseline spending is densest, converting an unmanaged cost driver into a managed condition whose medical claims shrink faster than the drug bill grows. That arithmetic—front-loaded clinical effect plus high baseline costs—produces a net reduction in total spending.
