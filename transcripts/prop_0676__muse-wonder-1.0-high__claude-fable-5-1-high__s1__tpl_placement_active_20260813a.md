[P1.1] A qualified applicant sits alone at midnight, talking to a laptop. She smiles too flatly, pauses before answering, avoids eye contact with the camera. No human rejects her. Software does — scoring her facial movements, voice pitch, and word choice as low in enthusiasm and cultural fit.
[P1.2] That is not efficiency. It is automated judgment without a defensible basis, and governments should ban or heavily restrict it in hiring.
[P1.3] First, these tools claim more than they can prove. Personality is not readable from a brief video, and traits like conscientiousness do not show up reliably in tone or expression across cultures and contexts. Many systems infer employability from proxies — speech speed, vocabulary, affect — that have weak, unstable links to doing the job. A test that cannot demonstrate it predicts performance for the role is not innovation; it is pseudoscience with a dashboard.
[P1.4] Second, that weakness becomes discrimination at scale. Train a model on past “successful” employees and it learns to favor how those people look, sound, and express themselves. It can penalize a stutter, an accent, a facial difference, autistic communication, flat affect linked to depression, or norms from another culture. The employer may never intend bias, but the algorithm launders it into a numeric score, applied to thousands before any human looks.
[P1.5] Third, candidates have no meaningful consent or recourse. Refuse the assessment and you refuse the job. You rarely see your score, know what triggered it, or get to explain a disability, a bad connection, or an interruption off-camera. Human bias is at least confrontable.
[P1.6] This arrives as unexplained rejection.
[P1.7] Restriction solves this without banning ordinary technology. Scheduling tools and skills tests with proven job relevance can stay. What should go is opaque personality inference — especially from face and voice — unless employers prove validity, submit to independent bias audits, disclose how scoring works, and provide a real human appeal. Livelihood is too important to outsource to unaccountable guesswork.

[C1.7] The motion mistakes the visible flaw for the bigger one. Govern what hiring tools do; don't ban the one kind that keeps receipts.
