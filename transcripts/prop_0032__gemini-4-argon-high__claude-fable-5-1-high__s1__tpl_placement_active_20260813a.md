[P1.1] Finding out you carry a mutation for aggressive breast cancer or Alzheimer’s should not happen the same way you check a package delivery—via an unmediated smartphone notification over breakfast. Direct-to-consumer genetic testing companies market empowerment, but when they deliver high-impact predictive health results without professional guidance, they are selling clinical diagnostics without clinical safeguards. Requiring these companies to provide access to certified genetic counseling before releasing life-altering data is an essential consumer protection.
[P1.2] High-impact genetic risk is uniquely easy to misinterpret. Markers for hereditary cancers, cardiac conditions, or neurodegenerative diseases deal in complex probabilities, incomplete penetrance, and technical limitations—not simple yes-or-no diagnoses. Without pre-release counseling access, two predictable harms occur. First, false terror: a customer sees a positive risk variant, assumes a guaranteed disease, and suffers severe psychological trauma or demands unnecessary clinical interventions before understanding what the result actually means.
[P1.3] Second, and more insidious, is false reassurance. Consumer genotyping chips often check only a tiny fraction of the thousands of known pathogenic variants in a gene. A woman with a family history of breast cancer sees a "negative" consumer result, skips routine screenings believing she is genetically cleared, and misses a lethal tumor because no counselor explained what the test cannot see.
[P1.4] This mandate is narrowly tailored. It leaves ancestry and benign traits completely untouched, targeting only high-impact predictive health results. It simply requires companies to provide access to a certified counselor before the digital envelope is opened, ensuring customers understand the test’s limits and their own readiness for the answer. Currently, testing companies pocket the profits of selling medical prophecies while externalizing the emotional and diagnostic fallout onto unprepared consumers and overburdened primary care doctors.
[P1.5] If a corporation wants to monetize life-altering medical data, providing access to pre-result genetic counseling is the minimum duty of care.
