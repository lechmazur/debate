[P1.1] Every two years, most Americans vote in House races decided before they leave home. That is not bad luck with candidates. It is the predictable output of winner-take-all rules. When a single plurality winner takes the whole district, a vote for anyone outside the two big parties is wasted, and a vote for your own side in a safe district is redundant.
[P1.2] The system manufactures two parties, then hands each of them hundreds of seats where the general election is a formality.
[P1.3] That is the engine of polarization. In a safe seat, the only real threat to an incumbent is a primary, decided by a small, intensely ideological slice of voters. So legislators fear their flank, not the median voter. Compromise becomes betrayal.
[P1.4] And with only two viable options, politics hardens into a binary identity war where beating the other side matters more than governing.
[P1.5] Ranked-choice and proportional voting break that engine at the source. Ranked-choice ends the spoiler problem: voters can back their true favorite without electing their least favorite, and candidates must earn second preferences, which rewards breadth over rage. Maine and Alaska already use it, and in Alaska, candidates with cross-party appeal survived where a closed partisan primary would likely have punished them. Proportional representation goes further: a party winning roughly forty percent of votes wins roughly forty percent of seats.
[P1.6] Rural Democrats and urban Republicans, effectively voiceless today, finally get representation. Most established democracies elect legislatures this way, and New Zealand switched from winner-take-all to proportional rules within living memory without collapse.
[P1.7] This is achievable. The Constitution does not require single-member districts. Congress imposed them by statute, and Congress can change them. States control their own legislatures.
[P1.8] So the question is simple: should your vote count only if it lands on the winning side of a line someone else drew? CON must defend a system that wastes most votes, empowers primary extremes, and locks out every alternative.
