[P1.1] Toxicity isn't that humans got meaner around 2016. It's that meanness started getting free distribution. The shift from chronological to ranked feeds is the difference between a bulletin board and an outrage slot machine.
[P1.2] A ranked feed optimizes for time-on-site, and the cheapest way to keep you scrolling is anger. It learns to show you not what your friends posted, but what will make you reply angrily to a stranger. A chronological feed can't do that. It shows what's new from people you chose, in order.
[P1.3] No learning, no provocation curation. That removes the engine that systematically puts the most divisive post at the top of your day.
[P1.4] That also rewrites creator incentives. When reach is determined by engagement score, the rational move is to be more extreme than everyone else on the timeline. You have to outrage to be seen. When reach is determined by recency, you get seen because you posted and people followed you.
[P1.5] You don't win by being inflammatory, you win by being consistent. So fewer people even try the toxic bait in the first place.
[P1.6] And it contains the blast radius. The worst toxic experiences — dogpiles, quote-dunk factories, harassment mobs — depend on algorithmic injection, where your post is pushed to millions who didn't follow you precisely because they'll hate it. Chronological limits you mostly to an opt-in audience. You can still be criticized, but you aren't fed by default to an adversarial crowd selected for animosity.
[P1.7] Slowing virality matters too. Rumors, conspiracies, harassment campaigns still exist chronologically, but they spread at human network speed, not at recommendation-engine speed. There's no For You funnel dragging a casual viewer into extremism in an afternoon.
[P1.8] Chronological wouldn't make people perfect. It would leave toxicity human-sized, not machine-amplified.
