[P1.1] The gig economy has outsourced systemic risk to the public while privatizing profit. We must modernize the social contract by requiring platform work to count toward unemployment and social insurance, facilitated by standardized earnings records.
[P1.2] First, we must close the vulnerability gap. Platform workers face the exact same economic shocks as traditional employees—sudden deactivation, algorithmic wage cuts, and illness. Yet, as misclassified contractors, they are excluded from social insurance. When a delivery worker is arbitrarily deactivated, they have no fallback, pushing them onto emergency general welfare and shifting the burden to taxpayers.
[P1.3] Without this protection, the financial ruin of a single gig worker becomes a public crisis. Integrating them into the insurance system ensures those who generate platform wealth are protected when that access is severed.
[P1.4] Second, the specific mechanism—standardized earnings records—makes this administratively viable. The traditional barrier to covering gig workers is the friction of tracking fragmented income. But platforms already track every transaction, route, and hour with granular precision. By mandating standardized API reporting formats, governments can seamlessly integrate this existing data into social insurance registries.
[P1.5] This eliminates the administrative burden on the worker, prevents corporate underreporting, and ensures the automatic, continuous accrual of benefits even when workers juggle multiple apps.
[P1.6] Finally, this policy restores market fairness and macroeconomic stability. Traditional businesses pay into social insurance funds. Platform companies bypass these obligations, securing an artificial cost advantage. Requiring platforms to contribute based on standardized records levels the playing field, ensuring profitability is driven by genuine innovation rather than evading the safety net.
[P1.7] Furthermore, when gig workers have unemployment insurance, they maintain their purchasing power during economic downturns, stabilizing the broader economy.
[P1.8] We are not destroying flexible work; we are ensuring it does not destroy the workers who rely on it.
