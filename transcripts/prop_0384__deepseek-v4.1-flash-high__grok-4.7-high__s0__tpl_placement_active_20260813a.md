[P1.1] Public education serves families with radically different beliefs. The proposition doesn't pick one; it sets conditions for optional religious instruction: comparable secular alternatives and clear voluntariness. That is not an establishment of religion. It is a neutral access rule with conscience protections built in.
[P1.2] First, voluntariness must be real. "Optional" means opt-in, no penalties, no default enrollment, no school endorsement. With those safeguards, no child is conscripted into someone else's faith. Parents choose.
[P1.3] Schools already host outside groups for enrichment during non-instructional time; religious groups should not be uniquely excluded when the same safeguards apply.
[P1.4] Second, the comparable secular alternative is the equality guarantee. It prevents the "take religion or sit in study hall" trap. It ensures objectors are not pressured or stigmatized, and it denies religious instruction a captive audience. If a school cannot offer a comparable alternative, the condition fails and the program should not run.
[P1.5] The rule is self-limiting.
[P1.6] Third, on-campus access is an equity issue. Many families cannot drive children to midweek religious classes. An off-campus-only rule privileges affluent, mobile families. Optional on-campus instruction, run by outside providers during non-curricular time, gives working-class and rural families the same chance to exercise their beliefs without forcing anyone else.
[P1.7] The objection is separation of church and state. But the school is not teaching doctrine, funding religion, or compelling attendance. It is acting as a fair host when exit is genuine and alternatives are real. That is neutrality, not endorsement.
[P1.8] Banning all on-campus religious instruction while allowing secular worldview clubs can be its own viewpoint discrimination.
[P1.9] The decisive comparison: under PRO, every student has a comparable option and no one is coerced. Under CON, religious families are either pushed off campus and unequally burdened, or students are left with no structured alternative. The safeguards are what make permission safe. Vote PRO.
