[C1.1] The proposition mistakes a feeling about dignity for a workable legal right. What actually protects people from bad state decisions is accuracy, reasons, and a real route to reversal — none of which requires a human to make the initial call, and some of which humans actively defeat.
[C1.2] Start with what "a right to a human decision-maker" delivers in practice: a person. Not a good decision. The overwhelming pattern in high-stouth administrative adjudication is that humans under volume pressure rubber-stamp. The official with four hundred files and ten minutes does not weigh a child's medical records against a recertification deadline; they sign whatever the case worker pre-filled.
[C1.3] A legal right to that official is a right to a ritual. It satisfies the form of contestability while gutting its substance, and worse, it makes the ritual legally sufficient — once a human "decided," the machine's fingerprints are washed off the record and review gets harder, not easier.
[C1.4] Second, human discretion is not neutral. It is the documented source of disparity in asylum screening, benefits adjudication, and sentencing: outcomes swing with the mood of the hearing, the applicant's accent, the time of day, the decision-maker's priors. A consistent algorithm wrongly denying a class of claimants creates a pattern that can be found, litigated, and fixed in one stroke. A thousand humans each denying a slightly different deserving claimant creates no defendant and no fix.
[C1.5] If the concern is that "error becomes a statistical remainder," the human system is where remainders go to disappear.
[C1.6] Third, the right imposes real costs on the very people it claims to protect. A human gatekeeper is a queue. Delayed benefits mean evictions and untreated illness; a mandatory human layer in immigration backlogs means years in limbo. We are being asked to entrench the scarce, slow, expensive resource as a legal prerequisite.
[C1.7] The right framing is a right to explanation, appeal, and de novo review by whoever or whatever is most accurate. PRO is selling process theater and calling it due process.
