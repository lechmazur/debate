[C1.1] The PRO’s framing of this as a simple question of consumer ownership ignores the tangible, life-threatening and economic harms a blunt national mandate would impose on ordinary Indians, all while undermining the very goals it claims to advance. First, safety. Consumer electronics rely on precision components, most critically lithium-ion batteries, that pose severe fire and explosion risks when replaced with unregulated, counterfeit parts. Multiple incidents across India’s small towns and cities have seen cheap replacement phone batteries cause severe burns and house fires, harms that a mandatory parts access law would amplify by flooding the market with unvetted components stripped of quality controls.
[C1.2] Forcing manufacturers to supply parts to any unqualified repairer, with no guarantee of proper installation or safety testing, is to hand consumers a false promise of affordability that comes with physical risk. Second, this law would gut India’s domestic electronics manufacturing push. The Make in India initiative relies on attracting billions in R&D and production investment from global and domestic OEMs, who protect proprietary component designs and diagnostic tools to recoup their investment. Forcing these firms to share proprietary schematics, parts and tools with unvetted third parties removes any incentive to localize production or launch new products in the Indian market, ultimately raising device prices for consumers—exactly the opposite of the affordability goal PRO claims to champion.
[C1.3] Third, the waste and livelihoods arguments PRO relies on are both misplaced. Unregulated repairs using unvetted parts lead to shorter, not longer, device lifespans: a faulty replacement or botched install often bricks a device entirely, adding to India’s already overwhelming e-waste burden rather than reducing it. Far from uplifting the informal repair workforce PRO celebrates, a mandatory national supply chain would flood the market with cheap, unregulated parts, letting unskilled actors undercut qualified technicians who invest in proper training and quality components, driving down wages and eroding standards across the sector.
