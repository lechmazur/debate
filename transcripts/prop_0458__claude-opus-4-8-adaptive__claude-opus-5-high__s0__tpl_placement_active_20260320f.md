[P1.1] The case for restricting LNG exports rests on a simple truth we keep dodging: every molecule of gas we ship abroad is a molecule we've decided to burn, leak, and lock in for decades. Let me lay out three reasons this restriction is the right call.
[P1.2] First, methane. LNG is not "clean" natural gas—it is gas that has been fracked, piped, compressed, super-chilled, shipped across oceans, and regasified. At every one of those stages, methane leaks. Methane is a greenhouse gas dozens of times more potent than CO2 over the timeframe that matters for avoiding tipping points.
[P1.3] When we expand the export chain, we are not swapping coal for a clean fuel; we are building an enormous new leakage surface. Restricting exports directly shrinks that surface. This is the most concrete lever the US actually controls.
[P1.4] Second, domestic prices and decarbonization. Exporting LNG links American gas to global prices, pulling domestic costs upward. That does two bad things: it raises energy bills for American households, and it makes the transition to wind, solar, storage, and electrification comparatively slower where cheap gas would otherwise have accelerated retirement of dirtier assets. Keeping more supply home stabilizes prices and lets clean electricity outcompete on the margin instead of subsidizing an export windfall.
[P1.5] Third, infrastructure lock-in. Export terminals are multi-decade, multi-billion-dollar bets. Once built, they create a powerful political constituency demanding gas production for thirty to forty years—precisely the decades in which climate science says emissions must fall to near zero. Restriction now avoids stranding capital later and avoids handing the fossil lobby a permanent reason to fight decarbonization.
[P1.6] The core clash will be this: CON will say our gas displaces foreign coal. But that argument assumes best-case leakage, best-case displacement, and ignores that we are financing fossil dependency abroad rather than exporting the clean technology that actually ends the coal era. A gas bridge that never ends is not a bridge—it's a detour.
[P1.7] Restrict exports, cut leakage, protect the transition. That is the responsible path.
