A student who must catch a bus at 7:00 may sleep only about six hours, and that chronic shortfall can impair concentration, emotional control, classroom participation, and safe driving.
[P1.2] These costs fall on students whose families have the least power to renegotiate work shifts.
[P1.3] Transportation and parent schedules are real, but they are design problems. If one policy creates friction, the answer is to distribute it and support it, not make sleep loss the price of attendance.

[P1.5] An 8:30 floor is not a promise that every family’s morning becomes easy. It is a minimum standard: no school should use adolescent development as an excuse for inadequate sleep.
