[P1.1] The question is not whether to price carbon—it's what to do with the money. I defend the proposition: raise carbon prices, return every dollar as equal per-capita dividends, and end the practice of channeling that revenue into green industrial subsidies.
[P1.2] Start with what a carbon price does. It is the only policy that reaches every emission in the economy without regulators guessing which technology, which firm, or which consumer deserves to change behavior. A meaningful price makes dirty electricity, steel, cement, and fuel systematically more expensive relative to clean alternatives, and lets millions of decentralized decision-makers find the cheapest path to decarbonization—rather than a ministry picking winners.
[P1.3] Dividends transform the politics and the economics. Returning revenue equally per person means most households—typically the majority, since the wealthy consume far more carbon-intensive goods than the poor—come out ahead. Climate policy stops being a tax and becomes a transfer that happens to clean the air. This is the design feature that makes a rising carbon price politically survivable.
[P1.4] Canada's federal backstop and Switzerland's levy both used dividend-style rebates precisely because voters tolerate prices they can see refunded.
[P1.5] Contrast that with green subsidies. They fail on three fronts. First, fiscal capture: every subsidized industry lobbies to define itself as green, so subsidy regimes ratchet outward—look at the sprawling carve-outs and domestic-content fights in the US Inflation Reduction Act. Second, they mask the true signal: if the price of carbon stays low because revenue is spent elsewhere, consumers face no incentive to conserve, and subsidies merely bribe people to do what prices would have compelled anyway—at several times the cost.
[P1.6] Third, they are regressive in effect: subsidy dollars flow disproportionately to those who can afford EVs, heat pumps, and new homes.
[P1.7] A price with dividends is transparent, market-wide, distributionally progressive, and immune to being gamed by whoever has the best lobbyists. That is the case I will defend.
