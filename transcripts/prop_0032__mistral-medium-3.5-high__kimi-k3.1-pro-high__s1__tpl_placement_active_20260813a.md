[P1.1] Direct-to-consumer genetic tests now deliver life-altering predictions—Huntington’s, BRCA mutations, cardiac risks—straight to smartphones with no safety net. These are not trivial data points: a positive result can trigger panic, unnecessary surgeries, or fatalism, while a misunderstood negative can breed false reassurance. Genetic risk is probabilistic, penetrance varies, and many variants have unclear significance, yet consumers routinely misread "elevated risk" as certainty. Studies consistently show that without expert interpretation, most users either over- or under-estimate their actual danger.
[P1.2] The harm is concrete and documented. Women have undergone irreversible mastectomies based on DTC reports that clinical testing later proved to be benign variants. Others have spiraled into severe depression after receiving uncontextualized Huntington’s predictions. [P1.3] This isn’t about restricting access to information—it’s about ensuring that access doesn’t become a vector for preventable medical and emotional harm.
[P1.4] Clinical genetics already mandates pre- and post-test counseling for high-impact findings. DTC companies exploit the same genetic science but systematically evade the same safeguards, prioritizing scale and profit over patient safety. The marginal cost of providing counseling access is negligible compared to the human cost of misinformed life-altering decisions. Requiring this support isn’t paternalistic; it’s the minimum ethical standard for delivering information that can redefine a person’s future.
[P1.5] Without it, we’re not empowering consumers—we’re abandoning them to the most terrifying interpretations of their own DNA.
