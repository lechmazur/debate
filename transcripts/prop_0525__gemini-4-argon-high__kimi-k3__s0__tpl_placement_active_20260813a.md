[P1.1] Safe-third-country rules allow a state to turn away an asylum seeker on a single premise: that the person will find genuine protection elsewhere. Such a massive transfer of human responsibility is justified only when it achieves true responsibility-sharing rather than responsibility-dumping. A country is not "safe" merely because the state is not actively shooting at you. If a destination traps people in legal limbo, destitution, or permanent separation from their children, transferring them there does not provide protection—it just moves their suffering out of sight.
[P1.2] Each condition in this motion is a bare minimum against abuse. First, an enforceable right to appeal is the only shield against chain refoulement. Initial asylum screenings are notoriously prone to translation failures, haste, and political pressure. If a third country can reject a claimant without independent review, the transferring state is simply laundering a deportation to persecution through a middleman.
[P1.3] Second, an enforceable right to work separates protection from indefinite warehousing. Without legal employment, refugees are forced into black-market exploitation or constructive refoulement—where sheer starvation drives them back to the danger they fled.
[P1.4] Third, an enforceable right to family unity is vital for both human dignity and border order. Tell a parent in a third country that they can never legally bring their spouse or child to join them, and they will not passively accept permanent fracture; they will pay smugglers to reunite illegally. Denying family unity fuels the exact irregular migration safe-third-country rules claim to stop.
[P1.5] Finally, these rights must be legally enforceable, not diplomatic window-dressing. Unenforceable bilateral promises do not stop an unlawful deportation or allow a refugee to feed their family; only accessible courts and binding laws do. If a state wants to shift its asylum duties onto another country, guaranteeing these three enforceable rights is the minimum price of legitimacy.

[C1.1] "Safe" means safe from persecution. That is the entire concept behind safe-third-country rules, and it is where this motion fails. It takes a narrow safety question — will this person face persecution, torture, or onward deportation in the destination? — and replaces it with an audit of the destination's labor law, courts, and family policy. The word "only" then declares every transfer unjustified unless one country guarantees three specific enforceable rights, however safe that country actually is.
[C1.2] That test is unmeetable, and its practical function is abolition. Appeals in much of Europe run for years; the United States, Canada's only safe-third-country partner, has detained asylum seekers and separated families, so under this motion that entire arrangement was never justified, period. Jordan and Kenya host enormous refugee populations without full formal work rights — ruled out too. The motion does not raise the floor of protection anywhere.
[C1.3] It declares nearly every real arrangement illegitimate and leaves the field empty.
[C1.4] An empty field protects no one. When transfers are off the table, asylum seekers do not gain better rights; they pile into overwhelmed frontline states, overcrowded island camps, and indefinite detention, or they pay smugglers to move onward. Compare the real choices: a stable country offering physical safety and funded assistance but no formal work permit, versus a camp cot with neither safety nor work. The motion forbids the first and consigns people to the second.
[C1.5] Absolutism here guards the checklist, not the person.
[C1.6] The right test tracks actual risk to the individual: an individualized assessment, access to some status procedure, and a hard guarantee against refoulement, enforceable against the sending state that holds the transferring power. Work, appeal, and family rights matter as evidence of genuine safety and as goals for funded partnerships — not as three tripwires that void everything. A rule that demands perfection from poorer hosts while frontline camps overflow is not protection. It is abandonment with clean hands.
