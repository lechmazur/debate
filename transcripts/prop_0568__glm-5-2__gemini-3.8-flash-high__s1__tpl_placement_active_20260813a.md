[P1.7] Privacy compounds the case. Digital payments create permanent transaction records. People buying medicine or personal care items deserve the option of anonymity. Cash preserves it.

[C1.3] Paradoxically, this imposes a regressive tax on food and medicine, punishing the very low-income households PRO aims to protect.
[C1.4] Second, cash invites violent crime. Physical currency makes storefronts and retail workers targets for armed robbery. [C1.5] Third, the mandate misdiagnoses the solution to financial exclusion. If individuals lack digital access, the state should expand financial inclusion directly through zero-fee public accounts, postal banking, or accessible prepaid cards. [C1.6] Finally, commercial incentives already preserve cash where viable. Retailers leave no money on the table; they maintain cash payments whenever consumer demand justifies it. A rigid legal mandate forbids business models like frictionless, automated checkout that lower overhead and expand store footprints into underserved areas.
[C1.7] We must modernize financial access, not force inflationary and dangerous mandates on retail essentials.
