[P1.1] Prevention only works before the harm. A criminal conviction requires a crime already committed, a victim already made, a prosecution already finished. The proposition asks a simple question: when a judge finds credible evidence of imminent danger, may the state temporarily separate that person from the most lethal tool available, or must it stand aside and hope?
[P1.2] Notice what red-flag laws actually do. They do not jail anyone. They do not create a criminal record. They do not permanently strip a right.
[P1.3] They order temporary removal of firearms, typically for days pending a full hearing and at most for a defined period afterward, after a judge, not a police officer or an angry relative, reviews evidence and finds a specific risk. Firearms are returned when the order expires or the person shows the danger has passed. The person keeps every other liberty.

[C1.4] The analogies fail because they are not this right. A license to drive is a privilege the state created. A no-contact order usually follows identified violence against a named victim. A short psychiatric hold is a medical process with clinical criteria, not a gun-confiscation docket.
