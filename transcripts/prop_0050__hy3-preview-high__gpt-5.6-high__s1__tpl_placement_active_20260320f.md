The levy only kicks in, and rises sharply, for each additional flight. This means the tax burden falls entirely on the small fraction of the population doing nearly all frequent flying, almost all of which is non-essential: unnecessary short-haul business trips, luxury leisure travel, and flights where affordable low-carbon rail alternatives exist.

Revenue from the progressive levy can be ring-fenced to expand low-carbon travel access for remote communities that currently have no choice but to fly occasionally, and to fund climate adaptation for frontline communities most harmed by aviation-linked carbon emissions.
