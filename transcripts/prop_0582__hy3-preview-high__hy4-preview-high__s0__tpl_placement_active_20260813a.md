[P1.1] Broad Medicare coverage of GLP-1 anti-obesity drugs will cut total US health spending within five years because these agents attack the metabolic root of the most expensive conditions in the elderly. Obesity is not a cosmetic issue; it drives type 2 diabetes, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, stroke, and dialysis. GLP-1 drugs produce sustained 10–15% body-weight loss and rapid improvements in blood pressure, lipids, and inflammation. Those changes translate into fewer admissions and procedures inside the budget window.
[P1.2] Consider a typical Medicare beneficiary with obesity and early cardiovascular disease. A single avoided heart-failure hospitalization or stroke admission costs tens of thousands of dollars. [P1.3] The opposition will fixate on pharmacy line-item cost. But total spending is what the proposition measures. Broad coverage means the highest-risk seniors actually receive treatment instead of remaining unmanaged until they need ICU care. Reduced ER visits, fewer insulin escalations, less sleep-apnea CPAP dependency, and deferred knee replacement consultations all subtract from the total bill within five years.
[P1.4] Even partial adherence yields population-level savings because the avoided events are so costly.
[P1.5] Five years is ample. Cardiometabolic benefits appear early, and Medicare’s obese population is large; the pooled savings from averted acute episodes accumulate year over year. By year five, the cumulative reduction in inpatient and post-acute care spending exceeds the drug outlay.
