[P1.1] Mandatory nationwide E‑Verify will cut unauthorized hiring by a measurable margin while leaving discrimination and labor supply essentially untouched. The logic is simple: if employers must confirm work eligibility before a new hire steps on the payroll, the cheapest way to fill a job with an undocumented worker disappears. The system already works—more than 700,000 employers use it today, and the error rate is under 1 %. When states such as Arizona, Georgia, and Mississippi required E‑Verify, the share of undocumented workers in the labor force fell by roughly a quarter within two years, while overall employment levels stayed flat.
[P1.2] Implementation costs are negligible; the system plugs into existing payroll software, and compliance takes seconds per hire. Uniform application also curtails the discretionary bias that currently fuels discriminatory hiring.
[P1.3] Critics warn of a surge in discrimination, but the evidence does not support that fear. E‑Verify checks only immigration status, not race or ethnicity, and the law includes a “safe‑harbor” provision that shields employers who follow the process in good faith. In states with mandatory use, complaints of discriminatory hiring have not risen; in fact, the uniform verification reduces the informal bias that currently lets some managers turn a blind eye to a worker’s documents while targeting others.
[P1.4] Labor‑shortage alarm is equally overstated. Unauthorized workers make up less than 5 % of the total U.S. labor force, and they are concentrated in a few low‑skill sectors—agriculture, construction, hospitality. Those industries already rely on seasonal guest‑worker programs and can absorb the modest reduction through modest wage adjustments or modest productivity gains. The experience of Georgia, which adopted a statewide mandate in 2011, shows no measurable drop in output in those sectors.
[P1.5] Therefore, a nationwide mandate delivers the core benefit—meaningful reduction of illegal employment—while the feared side‑effects remain marginal. The policy is a low‑cost, high‑certainty‑reduction tool that the status quo simply cannot match.
