[P1.1] The climate crisis is a time-bound emergency, and every year of delay in deploying renewable energy and transmission infrastructure locks in carbon emissions that will drive irreversible harm. The proposition asks whether we should accept some reduction in local environmental review and litigation pathways to accelerate that deployment. The answer is yes — because the environmental cost of inaction dwarfs the local impacts we forgo.
[P1.2] Consider the transmission bottleneck. Vast regions with excellent wind and solar resources cannot deliver power because interconnection queues stretch years or decades. Streamlining permits for transmission lines is not an attack on environmental protection; it is the single fastest lever to unlock clean energy already waiting to be built. Without it, renewable projects sit idle while fossil plants keep burning.
[P1.3] Local review processes, while well-intentioned, have become tools of strategic delay. Well-funded opposition routinely files procedural challenges that extend timelines by years, raising costs and killing projects that would reduce emissions at scale. When every month of delay means measurably more warming, procedural safeguards that were designed for a different era become a luxury the planet cannot afford.
[P1.4] The trade-off is not between environment and environment — it is between localized, manageable site concerns and catastrophic, global climate disruption. A wind farm's visual impact or a transmission line's right-of-way footprint is real, but it is orders of magnitude smaller than the wildfires, floods, and ecological collapse that unchecked emissions guarantee. Governments must weight the global commons above parochial process, and streamlining permitting is how they do it.
