[P1.1] For decades, Africa’s trade model has been a colonial relic: exporting unprocessed raw materials to former colonial powers and distant wealthy markets, capturing only a tiny fraction of the final value of those goods. Ivory Coast produces 40% of the world’s cocoa, yet earns less than 10% of the global chocolate market’s annual revenue because it exports raw beans rather than processed finished products. This system traps African countries in cycles of poverty, volatility, and external dependence, while fueling the resource curse that turns concentrated extractive industries into sources of corruption and conflict rather than broad-based prosperity.
[P1.2] Prioritizing intra-African trade and manufacturing is the only sustainable path to break that cycle. The African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) already creates a $3.4 trillion integrated regional market, eliminating tariffs between member states to let countries sell value-added manufactured goods to neighbors instead of raw commodities to the West, bypassing unfair external tariff barriers that penalize processed African exports. This cuts out exploitative middlemen, keeps more revenue local, and creates millions of formal jobs for African workers.
[P1.3] This model also builds critical economic resilience. During the 2014-2016 global commodity price crash, oil-dependent Nigeria and mineral-exporting Zambia saw GDP plummet 30% and 25% respectively, with currency collapses and mass job losses. Had those countries diversified into manufacturing for regional markets, those external shocks would have had a fraction of that impact.
[P1.4] Prioritizing intra-African trade and manufacturing is not isolationism. It is a deliberate rebalancing of a trade system designed during colonialism to extract resources, not build African prosperity. It lets African countries set their own economic terms, reduce vulnerability to volatile global commodity prices, cut reliance on exploitative foreign aid and conditional loans, and lift living standards for their people.
