[P1.1] Read the motion carefully, because its limits decide this debate. It protects only people with *no safe alternative shelter*, and it bars only *routine* fines, towing, and harassment for the act of sleeping in a vehicle. It does not legalize dumping trash, blocking driveways, drug activity, or threatening behavior — every one of those remains fully enforceable. So the only question is: when a person's sole options are a car or the sidewalk, should the state punish them for choosing the car?
[P1.2] Three reasons the answer is no.
[P1.3] First, punishing this is punishing status, not conduct. Sleep is a biological necessity. If someone literally cannot access shelter — and in most American cities shelter beds fall far short of the homeless population — then a ticket for sleeping in a vehicle is a ticket for existing while poor. American courts have repeatedly recognized this logic: you cannot criminalize an unavoidable act when no lawful alternative exists.
[P1.4] Second, enforcement is actively counterproductive. The vehicle is often the person's last asset: their shelter, their address for job applications, their transport to work, their storage for medication and documents. Tow it, and impound fees of hundreds or thousands of dollars — unpayable by definition for this population — mean permanent loss. We convert someone sleeping in a Honda into someone sleeping on concrete, which is worse for them, worse for public health, and vastly more expensive for taxpayers through emergency rooms, jails, and street outreach.
[P1.5] Third, the alternative already works. Cities running safe-parking programs and non-enforcement policies channel people toward services instead of churning them through citations they can never pay, which spiral into warrants that then block housing and employment — a debt trap manufactured by the state.
[P1.6] Any winning case against this motion must defend a specific claim: that fining and towing people with nowhere else to go produces better outcomes than leaving them be. That claim is indefensible, and everything in this debate flows from it.
