[P1.1] Peace on NATO's eastern frontier will not be preserved by promises made in Brussels or Washington. It will be preserved by armored brigades, air defenses, and supply depots physically sitting in Poland, the Baltics, and Romania. Deterrence is not a sentiment; it is a fact on the ground that a Russian planner cannot wish away.
[P1.2] A light tripwire invites exactly the miscalculation we should fear. A few thousand rotational troops that must be rescued after an invasion is deterrence by punishment — it tells Moscow it can seize Riga or Tallinn in days and then dare NATO to start a bloody liberation war. Permanent heavy forces reverse that calculus to deterrence by denial: you cannot take what is already strongly held. That is the only threat an opportunistic, risk-taking Kremlin reliably understands.
[P1.3] Geography makes this urgent. The Baltic states are small, flat, and separated from the rest of the alliance by the Suwalki Gap, with Russian forces in Kaliningrad and Belarus on either side. Reinforcement across the Atlantic under missile fire is a fantasy plan. Prepositioned equipment, hardened airfields, integrated air and missile defense, and troops who train on the terrain they would defend collapse warning time into fighting power.
[P1.4] The objection about escalation gets cause and effect backward. Russian aggression follows perceived weakness, not NATO strength. Restraint after Georgia, after Crimea, after the first Donbas war did not buy calm; it taught Moscow that red lines were negotiable. A permanently defended eastern flank raises day-to-day friction but lowers the catastrophic risk — a major war born of Russian belief that a quick fait accompli would succeed.
[P1.5] Finally, permanent basing cements alliance cohesion. Frontline democracies that lived under Soviet domination need more than reassurance visits; they need shared risk. When American, German, and British soldiers live alongside Polish and Lithuanian families, Article 5 ceases to be abstract and abandonment becomes politically impossible.

[C1.3] Combined with the multinational battlegroups already present, that posture makes a rapid seizure unachievable — a garrison that can fight within hours, backed by forces that arrive in days — without converting Estonia and Latvia into permanently militarized camps on Russia's frontier. [C1.4] Second, PRO waves away the escalation risk it concedes. [C1.6] Third, "even if" is doing the work of a blank check. PRO never prices the risk it accepts, because nuclear escalation is unpriceable. If equal deterrence is available at lower escalation risk, the motion loses on its own terms.
