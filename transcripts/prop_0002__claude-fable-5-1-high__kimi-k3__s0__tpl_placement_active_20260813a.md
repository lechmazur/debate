[P1.1] Housing prices are set by a simple arithmetic: how many people want to live somewhere versus how many homes exist there. American cities did not become unaffordable because of a mystery. They became unaffordable because, roughly from the 1970s onward, most of their residential land was legally reserved for one detached house per lot, while the jobs kept coming. The Bay Area spent the 2010s adding several jobs for every housing unit permitted.
[P1.2] When that happens, the price of the scarce thing rises until enough people are pushed out. That is not a market failure; it is a legal ceiling on supply doing exactly what a ceiling does.
[P1.3] Housing is a stock, not a flow. A city's affordability today depends on what was built over the previous generation, because most people live in homes that already exist. That is why two decades matters so much. Legalize density in 2005 instead of the mid-2020s and you catch the entire post-2008 recovery, when interest rates were near zero and capital was desperate for projects.
[P1.4] That decade should have been the greatest apartment-building era in American history. Instead, in the most in-demand metros, it was largely illegal.
[P1.5] We know this works because we can watch it work. Tokyo permits homes liberally at the national level and, despite a growing population, kept real housing costs roughly flat for decades. Houston shrank minimum lot sizes in the late 1990s and thousands of townhomes appeared where single houses stood, keeping it cheaper than any comparable boomtown. Minneapolis ended single-family-only zoning and cut parking mandates, then watched rents hold flat while the rest of Minnesota's climbed.
[P1.6] Austin permitted at high rates after 2021 and rents fell noticeably within two years. Every natural experiment points the same direction, and none of these places is unusual except in what it allowed.
[P1.7] The motion is comparative. Not "affordable," but "more affordable." CON must argue that twenty extra years of legal building would have left prices unchanged or higher. No city on earth has demonstrated that.
