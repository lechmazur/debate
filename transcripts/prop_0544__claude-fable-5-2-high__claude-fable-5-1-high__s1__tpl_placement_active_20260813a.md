[P1.1] Every illegal purchase and every substituted weapon the other side will point to is a leak in the policy, and a leak can only cancel a benefit if nearly everyone blocked finds another route that is just as deadly. That is the bar CON must clear, and it cannot be cleared for either half of the motion.
[P1.2] Take suicide first, since it accounts for the majority of American gun deaths. Suicidal crises in young adults are often brief and impulsive, and the method at hand decides who survives. A firearm attempt is fatal the overwhelming majority of the time, while pills or cutting are fatal only a small fraction of the time. Most people who survive an attempt do not later die by suicide.
[P1.3] So even if every blocked nineteen-year-old substituted another method, which will not happen, far fewer would die. A teenager in crisis also doesn't cultivate a black-market contact. Either he uses what is in reach or the moment passes.
[P1.4] Now homicide. Ages 18 to 20 sit at the peak of violent offending, with rates far out of proportion to their share of the population. Some will get guns illegally, as some do now. But illegal acquisition costs money and time, requires connections, and carries legal risk.
[P1.5] Each of those frictions screens out the impulsive, the unconnected and the marginal buyer. Recall that the shooters in Parkland, Buffalo and Uvalde were all 18 or 19 and bought their rifles legally over the counter. They chose the easy path because it existed. And a substituted knife is nothing like a rifle: the attacks are slower, the victims are fewer and many more survive.
[P1.6] We have run this kind of experiment before. Raising the drinking age to 21 did not end underage drinking, yet it measurably cut young traffic deaths. Imperfect compliance still produced a real reduction, because friction changes behavior at the margin, and the margin is where deaths occur.
[P1.7] So the question is not whether leakage exists. It is whether leakage is close to total and equally lethal. [clipped]
