[P1.1] A duplex is not a skyscraper. Start there, because this debate is really about whether it should remain illegal—on most residential land in most American cities—to build two homes where one is permitted. Single-family-only zoning doesn't protect houses; it prohibits everything else. Banning the creation of new districts of that kind simply ends a prohibition.
[P1.2] Nobody is forced to demolish anything or build anything. Homeowners keep every right they have today; they merely gain options their neighbors can no longer veto.
[P1.3] Why a full ban rather than density bonuses? Because bonuses are designed to fail politely. They are discretionary, project-by-project, and typically conditioned on affordability set-asides, hearings, and negotiations that add years and legal risk. The small builders who actually produce duplexes, triplexes, and backyard cottages cannot survive that process—only large developers on large parcels can, which is why bonus programs produce a trickle of units concentrated in a few corridors while the vast majority of residential land stays frozen.
[P1.4] A ban changes the default. Oregon and Minneapolis took roughly this approach—legalizing middle housing broadly rather than bargaining parcel by parcel—precisely because incrementalism had delivered almost nothing for decades.
[P1.5] The stakes are not abstract. Exclusionary zoning was built, historically and explicitly in many cities, to keep lower-income families and families of color out of opportunity-rich neighborhoods. Every new single-family-only district extends that machinery. It inflates land prices, forces sprawl onto farmland, lengthens commutes, and locks teachers, nurses, and firefighters out of the communities they serve.
[P1.6] A "density bonus" framework tacitly accepts that exclusion is the legitimate baseline and inclusion is the special exception requiring permission. That moral framing is backwards.
[P1.7] So the question for this round is simple: should exclusion remain the default that residents must beg exceptions from, or should ordinary, modest housing be legal by right? Bonuses tinker with the exception. A ban fixes the default. Only one of those actually solves the problem.
