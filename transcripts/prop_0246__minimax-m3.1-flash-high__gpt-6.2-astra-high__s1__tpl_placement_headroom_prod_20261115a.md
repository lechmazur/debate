[P1.1] The decisive difference is not whether platforms can display old posts; it is which posts they have an incentive to place before users. A chronological feed ranks content primarily by time, so reach follows from when something was posted rather than how predictably it provokes a reaction. An algorithmic feed ranks for predicted engagement, and because outrage and conflict reliably generate comments, reposts, and rapid reactions, it can learn that inflammatory material earns distribution.
[P1.2] That selection effect makes today’s feeds less toxic than a chronological alternative. The feed need not have an explicit hostility policy: a recommendation model can reward it indirectly while the platform advertises itself as neutral. Controversial posts reach otherwise indifferent audiences, their hostile comments attract further distribution, and apparent popularity makes them more prominent. Chronological feeds weaken that cycle by removing “engagement wins” as the principal basis for ranking.
[P1.3] Chronology would not create an online Eden. Users could still post abuse, and ads or trending tools could preserve incentives for sensationalism. But chronological ordering would stop one of the largest amplification systems from operating by default, while users could still opt into personalized recommendations.
[P1.4] Consider a provocative post about a public dispute. Under an engagement-ranked feed, it may be shown far beyond its original audience because its first replies are angry; each reply creates another reason to distribute the thread. Under a chronological feed, mainly followers and already-active users see it. The dispute may still be ugly, but it is less likely to become a mass spectacle.
[P1.5] Toxicity is not only abuse written; it also includes which comments become visible and whether fringe conflicts are repeatedly placed before people who did not seek them. Chronology directly reduces that manufactured visibility. Even if platforms moved some incentives elsewhere, it removes the default mechanism most directly rewarding outrage.
