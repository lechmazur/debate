[P1.1] A stamp on a map does not make a country safe. The motion states what justification requires: not prosperity, not perfection, but three enforceable guarantees that make "safe" more than a slogan.
[P1.2] Work first. A person barred from lawful employment is not protected; they are warehoused — pushed into black-market exploitation or destitution, and the "safe" country becomes an open-air waiting room. Livelihood is what turns refuge into a life rather than an indefinite pause.
[P1.3] Appeal second, because it is the right that protects all the others. Every asylum system errs, and some are pressured to reject. Britain's Supreme Court struck down the Rwanda scheme precisely because claimants there lacked any reliable way to contest refoulement — being sent onward to the very danger they fled. Without an appeal with teeth, "safe third country" is an unreviewable administrative guess, and a wrong guess can be fatal.
[P1.4] Family unity third, because separation is a harm all by itself, whatever the material conditions. A rule that deposits a mother in one country while her children remain in another manufactures suffering by design, not by accident.
