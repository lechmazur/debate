[P1.1] Official identity documents exist to verify who a person is, not to coerce conformity to a state-imposed mold. For people who do not identify as male or female, government-issued IDs featuring strictly binary markers force an unacceptable choice: endorse a legal falsehood on sworn paperwork, or face total administrative erasure. Recognizing a third-gender or nonbinary option—such as an "X" marker—is fundamentally a matter of individual liberty, basic dignity, and administrative accuracy.
[P1.2] The harm of withholding this recognition is concrete and pervasive. When an individual’s legal documentation conflicts with their lived identity, routine daily interactions become flashpoints for scrutiny. Boarding a plane, opening a bank account, applying for employment, or experiencing a traffic stop turns into an invasive interrogation. A forced binary document invites suspicion, exposes people to harassment, and denies them equal participation in public life.
[P1.3] Providing an accurate legal marker restores safety and functional privacy, ensuring citizens are not routinely penalized simply for existing.
[P1.4] Moreover, the administrative feasibility of this reform is an established fact. Dozens of jurisdictions worldwide—including countries like Canada, Germany, India, and Argentina, as well as numerous regional systems—already issue nonbinary passports and driver’s licenses. Society did not fracture, nor did security collapse. Civil registries, border control, and public institutions adapted easily.
[P1.5] In reality, state security depends on reliable personal verification—biometrics, photo identification, and unique registration numbers—not on forcing citizens into an inaccurate gender binary. Aligning legal documentation with lived reality makes identification records more reliable, not less.
[P1.6] When the state requires individuals to present identification to participate in modern society, it has an obligation to ensure those credentials reflect who they actually are. Compulsory binary classification serves no compelling administrative purpose; it only weaponizes bureaucracy against a vulnerable minority. Upholding equal dignity and genuine civil security requires recognizing a third option.
