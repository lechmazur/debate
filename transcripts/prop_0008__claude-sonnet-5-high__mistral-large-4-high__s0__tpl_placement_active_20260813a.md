[P1.1] Conventional age restrictions have had seventy years to work, and the result is a world where over a billion people still smoke. The policy's own logic is self-defeating: it bans sales to anyone under eighteen, but does nothing to stop that same person from buying legally on their nineteenth birthday, once addiction has already taken hold. Age restrictions regulate a birthday, not a product. A generational ban changes the target entirely: instead of policing who is old enough to buy, it locks the sale date itself, so the pool of legal customers shrinks every year and never refills.
[P1.2] Consider the mechanism concretely. New Zealand's model, raising the legal sale age by one year every year so that no one born after a fixed date can ever legally buy tobacco, doesn't ask retailers to guess an age across a counter. It asks them to check a date of birth against a line that only moves forward. That is actually easier to enforce than conventional age checks, which depend on fake IDs, lax carding at small shops, and social purchasing by slightly older peers, the dominant channel through which teenagers access cigarettes today.
[P1.3] Under a generational ban, there is no "slightly older peer" who can legally buy, because the whole cohort is locked out together.
[P1.4] The deeper point is about what each policy treats as the problem. Age restrictions treat youth smoking as a temporary lapse that resolves itself at eighteen. But nicotine addiction doesn't respect that boundary, nearly all adult smokers started as teenagers, and the industry's business model depends on recruiting that age group before the brain's reward circuitry fully matures. A generational ban attacks the supply side permanently rather than re-litigating the same age line every year while industry marketing adapts around it.
[P1.5] This is the comparison the rest of this debate should turn on: conventional restrictions manage tobacco's harm one birthday at a time, forever. A generational ban ends the market for new smokers entirely, converting a leaky, endlessly renewed loophole into a closing door.
