[P1.1] Public pension systems face a mathematical reckoning: the worker-to-retiree ratio is collapsing. When these systems were designed, retirees drew benefits for roughly a decade; today they draw them for two or three. The choice between raising retirement ages and relying mainly on higher payroll taxes is not merely technical—it determines who bears the burden and how our labor markets function.
[P1.2] Raising payroll taxes means taxing work itself. Each additional point makes hiring costlier, pushes marginal workers into informal employment, and shrinks the formal sector that funds pensions. It concentrates the cost on younger workers already burdened by housing costs and debt, asking them to subsidize retirements that may begin years before their own. It is a levy on job creation at precisely the moment economies need dynamism and innovation.
[P1.3] Linking retirement ages to life expectancy does the opposite. It acknowledges that modern sixty-five-year-olds are healthier, better educated, and capable of productive contribution far longer than the frail elders of the postwar era. Keeping experienced workers engaged expands the contribution base, sustains consumer demand, and preserves institutional knowledge in firms and public services. It honors the core logic of pay-as-you-go systems: working years and benefit years should remain roughly proportionate, not systematically tilted toward extended dependency.
[P1.4] Critics rightly cite physically demanding jobs and unequal life expectancy across income groups. But those concerns argue for targeted bridge employment, disability protections, and phased retirement—not for abandoning the structural adjustment. A system that automatically tracks longevity is fairer across generations and economically sustainable, whereas endlessly rising payroll taxes promise a shrinking tax base supporting a growing retiree population.
[P1.5] The alternative is not compassion; it is a deferred crisis imposed on the young. Raising retirement ages in line with life expectancy is the responsible path.
