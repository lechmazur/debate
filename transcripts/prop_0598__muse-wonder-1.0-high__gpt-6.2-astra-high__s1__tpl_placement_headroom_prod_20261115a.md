[P1.1] Mass deportations are expensive theater that makes irregular migration more dangerous, more informal, and harder to control. Legal labor migration corridors do the opposite: they bring movement into the open, match workers to real demand, and give states leverage they currently lack.
[P1.2] First, Gulf and North African economies already depend on migrant labor. Construction, agriculture, domestic work, logistics, fisheries, and care work cannot run on citizens alone, and North African states also rely on migrants in farms and services while their own workers move abroad. When demand persists but legal entry does not, workers do not disappear — they overstay, buy fraudulent papers, cross deserts and seas, and become indebted to smugglers. A deportation campaign clears a street for a week; the job that pulled the worker remains, so another worker fills it through a riskier channel.
[P1.3] Corridors solve that mismatch. Seasonal and multi-year work permits tied to verified employers, with portable rights, transparent recruitment fees, and renewal based on employment, allow states to set numbers, screen entrants, collect taxes and fees, and require departure or regularization through known systems. Instead of police hunting people underground, governments know who entered, where they work, and when their status expires.
[P1.4] Second, corridors improve security and public order more than sweeps do. Irregular workers avoid police, hospitals, and labor inspectors, creating blind spots exploited by traffickers and abusive employers. Legal entrants can be biometrically registered, medically screened, and directed away from overcrowded informal settlements. Deportations, by contrast, overwhelm detention centers, strain relations with origin countries that must accept returns, and often end in re-entry because no legal alternative exists.
[P1.5] Third, corridors protect citizens better than chaos does: enforceable wage floors and documented contracts reduce undercutting, while orderly returns for violations are credible because legal entry is possible.
