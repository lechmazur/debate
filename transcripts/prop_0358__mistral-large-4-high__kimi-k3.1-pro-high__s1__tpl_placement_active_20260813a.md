[P1.1] Traffic deaths are not solved in courtrooms; they are prevented in the street. The proposition asks cities to choose between designing safety into the road network and waiting to punish drivers after someone is killed. The former works; the latter is moral theater that arrives too late.
[P1.2] Post-crash penalties operate after the irreversible harm. A harsher sentence cannot restore a pedestrian's life or undo a traumatic brain injury. More importantly, they deter poorly because most fatal crashes are not premeditated crimes but tragic errors—momentary inattention, a blind spot, a wet road. The threat of prison does little to change split-second human fallibility.
[P1.3] Punishment is retrospective; safety must be prospective.
[P1.4] Lower default speed limits attack the physics of death. Kinetic energy increases with the square of velocity, meaning a car at 50 km/h carries nearly three times the destructive energy of one at 30 km/h. At typical urban speeds, a pedestrian struck at 30 km/h usually survives; at 50 km/h, death is likely. Reducing default limits does not rely on police presence or driver virtue—it changes the environment so that inevitable mistakes become survivable rather than fatal.
[P1.5] Street redesign goes further by making dangerous speeds physically uncomfortable. Narrow lanes, chicanes, raised crossings, and protected intersections force drivers to slow down without constant enforcement. Cities embracing Vision Zero principles—moving toward 30 km/h urban defaults and self-enforcing streets—have seen pedestrian fatalities collapse. The road itself becomes the traffic officer, working around the clock.
[P1.6] Harsher penalties also risk selective enforcement, hitting marginalized communities hardest while leaving the dangerous road design untouched. A city that redesigns its streets protects everyone equally; a city that merely promises longer sentences protects no one until the ambulance arrives.
[P1.7] The choice is stark: we can build cities where crashes are rare and survivable, or cities that punish efficiently after the body is cold. Only prevention reduces the body count.
