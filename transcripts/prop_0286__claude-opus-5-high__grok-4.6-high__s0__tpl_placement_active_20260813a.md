[P1.1] The proposition is modest in one crucial respect: it says space tourism is *hard to justify*, not that it should be criminalised. So the question before us is whether a defender of joyride spaceflight can discharge the burden of explanation that unresolved climate breakdown and severe poverty place on any luxury. I say they cannot, for three reasons.
[P1.2] First, the emissions are not incidental — they are the product. A suborbital hop for six passengers burns propellant and injects soot and water vapour directly into the stratosphere, where the climate effects per unit are far worse than at ground level, and where the atmosphere clears slowly. A single ticketed passenger generates, in minutes, a carbon and radiative burden that an ordinary person in a poor country would take a lifetime to match. There is no efficiency path here.
[P1.3] Making rockets cheaper makes the flights more frequent, which makes the harm worse. This is the one industry where success and mitigation point in opposite directions.
[P1.4] Second, the opportunity cost is real, not abstract. The scarce input isn't just money; it's world-class propulsion engineers, launch capacity, regulatory attention, and the finite political willingness to subsidise and licence aerospace. Every one of those inputs has an alternative use — earth-observation satellites that monitor deforestation, climate modelling, vaccine cold chains. When the marginal engineer is optimising cabin windows for the view rather than payload for a climate satellite, something has been traded, and the trade should be defended, not waved away.
[P1.5] Third — and this is where CON's likely reply fails — the innovation argument justifies *spaceflight*, not *tourism*. Satellites, science missions, and reusable launch technology all stand on their own merits and would proceed without paying customers taking eleven-minute pleasure trips. Tourism is the segment where the entire value delivered is a wealthy individual's experience.
[P1.6] That is the vivid picture: a few hundred thousand dollars buys one person a view, in a world where the same sum buys thousands of others clean water. Justifying that requires an argument. CON must supply one.
