[P1.1] Literature defined the nineteenth century and cinema defined the twentieth, but interactive software defines ours. Today, landmark video games, transformative mobile apps, and digital artworks carry as much cultural weight as any novel or feature film. Yet while legal deposit ensures that books and movies enter national archives, our digital heritage is vanishing in real time. When a platform like Flash retires, a studio goes bankrupt, or an authentication server shuts down, culturally vital software disappears overnight.
[P1.2] Treating print and film as permanent heritage while treating software as disposable code guarantees that future generations will inherit a hollowed-out record of modern life.
[P1.3] Voluntary preservation cannot solve this crisis because commercial incentives and copyright law actively block it. Publishers have little financial reason to maintain obsolete formats, and anti-circumvention laws routinely bar independent archivists from bypassing digital rights management, even to rescue abandoned works whose creators no longer exist. Worse, outside volunteers can never capture closed server logic or unencrypted builds locked inside corporate vaults. Only a statutory mandate solves both barriers: it obligates creators of culturally significant software to deposit functional, DRM-free archival builds, especially before sunsetting live services, and grants national archives clear legal authority to emulate those works on future hardware.
[P1.4] Crucially, this mandate is selective and commercially safe. Requiring deposit only for culturally significant software excludes routine enterprise utilities and disposable clones. Curators already make selective judgments for film and broadcast heritage; applying that model here keeps technical preservation focused and affordable. Nor does legal deposit threaten intellectual property.
[P1.5] National archives are secure vaults, not public download portals. Depositing compiled executables, paired with embargoes and on-site research terminals, protects trade secrets and prevents market substitution while a product remains commercially active. Film studios did not collapse when movies entered national libraries, and software studios will not collapse here. Without a mandate, however, the defining culture of the digital age will simply be deleted.
