[P1.1] A family's faith does not stop at the schoolhouse gate, but neither does a child's right to be left alone. The proposition protects both.
[P1.2] First, access. Public schools already host chess clubs, debate teams, and political student groups. [P1.3] Banning it doesn't make religion disappear; it makes access depend on income.
[P1.4] Now the conditions. "Clearly voluntary" means opt-in with parental consent, no grades, no teacher pressure, no penalty for staying out. [P1.5] The secular alternative closes the remaining gap. Picture a small-town school where the only offering at 3 p.m. is Bible study, and the other option is sitting alone in a hallway. [P1.6] Germany has long paired confessional classes with an ethics option on this logic.
[P1.7] So the opposition must pick a lane. Either ban outright, and tell religious families their convictions are uniquely unwelcome, or allow without safeguards, and tell minority children their discomfort is an acceptable price. The proposition refuses both. It's the only rule under which no child is pushed in and no child is pushed out.
