[P1.1] A state that knows people are here, knows they are working, and still refuses to classify them is not enforcing order; it is manufacturing illegality. This is not blanket amnesty or automatic citizenship. It is a conditional pathway for long-term undocumented residents who pass background checks and meet work or community criteria, turning an unmanaged fact into recorded duties.
[P1.2] The realistic alternatives are mass removal or permanent limbo. Removing settled people would break families, disrupt workplaces, and spend enforcement resources on neighbors with years of local ties instead of genuine threats. Limbo sustains a shadow labor market: bosses underpay, workers fear reporting abuse, victims avoid police, and agencies lack reliable records. Registration and screening work only when people have reason to come forward; a pathway supplies that reason and brings tax compliance, labor standards, licensing, and police cooperation inside the law.
[P1.3] Background checks answer the security objection. They sort people who should be pursued from people who can safely be documented. A parent who has lived in one town for years, worked steadily, paid rent, raised children, and has no serious criminal record is not a danger to be hunted; treating that person as one distracts from violent offenders and exploitative employers. Refusing to sort is not toughness; it leaves no verified status and no closure.
[P1.4] The criteria answer fairness. Work, community ties, clean checks, and long residence make this earned adjustment, not a prize for recent unlawful entry. It rewards the conduct government claims to want: stability, contribution, and willingness to be checked. The real beneficiaries of illegality are not compliant residents but employers, smugglers, and landlords who use fear as leverage.
[P1.5] Legal status weakens that black market.
[P1.6] Compare endpoints. A screened pathway creates documented, taxable, inspectable members of the community. Rejecting it preserves the shadows that a law-and-order case cannot defend. Conditional regularization is not the concession to illegality; permanent limbo is.

The durable remedy is enforcing the law at the border and the workplace through mandatory verification and strict employer penalties, while maintaining narrow avenues for crime victims to contact police without turning unlawful residence itself into an entitlement to stay.
