[P1.1] Modern warfare has fundamentally inverted the relationship between cost and lethality. For decades, American defense strategy prioritized a small inventory of exquisite, multi-billion-dollar platforms—aircraft carriers, fifth-generation stealth fighters, and advanced destroyers. Today, cheap precision munitions, loitering swarms, and commercially enabled sensors make these centralized assets glaring single points of failure. Sticking to an exquisite-only model puts the United States on the wrong side of cost, scale, and time.
[P1.2] First, consider the economics of modern combat. In recent conflicts, from Ukraine to the Red Sea, we see multi-million-dollar interceptors expended against twenty-thousand-dollar drones. In a high-intensity peer conflict, particularly across the vast distances of the Indo-Pacific, an adversary relying on massed missile salvos will exhaust our magazines and attrit our finite inventory of exquisite platforms long before our industrial base can replace them. When a single lost destroyer takes seven years and billions of dollars to rebuild, casualty aversion paralyzes strategic decision-making.
[P1.3] Second, attritable systems solve the vulnerability of concentration. Shifting procurement toward uncrewed surface vessels, collaborative combat aircraft, and distributed autonomous sensors creates combat power that is resilient, modular, and rapidly replenishable. These systems do not require zero-defect survivability; their military value lies in their ability to absorb losses, saturate enemy defenses, and disperse combat power across thousands of nodes. This collapses the adversary's targeting advantage by presenting too many lethal targets to neutralize simultaneously.
[P1.4] Accepting a reduction in exquisite spending is not a sacrifice of capability; it is a strategic necessity. Exquisite platforms suffer from severe diminishing returns, consuming vast portions of the budget for marginal gains in survivability that modern sensors rapidly negate. Shifting a significant share of procurement toward mass, modularity, and attritability is the only way to build a credible, sustainable deterrent for the twenty-first century.
