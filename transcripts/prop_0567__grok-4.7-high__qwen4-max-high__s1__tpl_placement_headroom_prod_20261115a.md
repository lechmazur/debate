[P1.1] A public trial that almost no one can see is public in name only. The gallery holds a handful of seats. Everyone else learns the case through headlines, clipped quotes, and the incentives of reporters racing a deadline. Courts should permit cameras in most trial proceedings because open justice is empty unless ordinary people can observe it, and a modern camera makes that possible without turning the room into a stage.
[P1.2] The principle is old: justice must be seen to be done, not merely described afterward. What changed is scale. A murder trial, a fraud case, or a challenge to government power can affect thousands who will never pass a metal detector. A fixed, silent camera extends the gallery to them.
[P1.3] It does not invent publicity. It replaces a privilege of proximity with equal access.
[P1.4] Rules can do the work opponents assign to a ban. "Most" is not "all." Proceedings involving children, sexual-assault complainants, undercover officers, or genuine secrets can stay dark. Judges already control decorum, exclude prejudicial material, and sequester juries.
[P1.5] Those powers cover camera placement, delayed feeds, and no close-ups of jurors. The claim that a lens must corrupt the trial assumes courts cannot govern a tool they already manage in the hallway.
[P1.6] The alternative is worse than the risk. Secondhand coverage selects the dramatic line and drops the qualifying one. Jurors are told not to follow the news; the public is left with little else. Direct footage is harder to spin than a paraphrase, and it disciplines the people inside.
[P1.7] A judge who cuts off a defendant, a prosecutor who overreaches, a witness who hedges: each is easier to excuse when the only record is a transcript few will read. Visibility restrains power. It is not a spectacle machine.
[P1.8] Fairness does not require invisibility. Defendants already face photographs, courthouse scrums, and commentary on the steps. The legally decisive moments happen inside, where evidence is tested. Hiding them protects reputation more than verdicts.
