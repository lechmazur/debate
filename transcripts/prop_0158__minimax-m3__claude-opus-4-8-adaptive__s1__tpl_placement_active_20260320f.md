[P1.1] Thousands die every year waiting for kidneys they will never receive. That is not a metaphor for a policy failure; it is the literal outcome of the system the opposition defends. A compensated donation regime under strict regulation is not a surrender to commercialism. It is the morally serious answer to a crisis that altruism alone has demonstrably failed to solve.
[P1.2] Altruistic-only donation rests on a noble ideal and produces terrible results. Demand for transplantable organs vastly exceeds supply in every country that relies solely on voluntary giving. The inevitable consequence is a waiting list that functions as a death list, and wherever legal supply is suppressed, an illegal market fills the gap. Prohibition does not eliminate organ sales; it relocates them to brokers who exploit the desperate, with no medical oversight, no follow-up care, and no recourse when donors are cheated or harmed.
[P1.3] The choice before us is not between commerce and virtue. It is between regulated transparency and an off-the-books trade that preys on the poor.
[P1.4] Compensated donation under strict regulation can directly answer the objections raised against it. Independent medical boards verify donor competence and screen out coercion. Standardized payments, set by government rather than negotiated by brokers, prevent exploitation and reflect the genuine burden of donation. Bounded, time-limited financial neutrality removes the incentive for vend organs while restoring the incentive that the present system has failed to provide.
[P1.5] Iran, for all its imperfections, has eliminated its kidney waiting list through a regulated compensated framework, while countries with much greater wealth and stronger medical systems still watch patients die.
[P1.6] The opposition asks us to preserve a system whose predictable result is preventable death. I ask this chamber to defend a future in which regulation, not prohibition, protects both donor and recipient. The lives at stake belong to people who cannot wait for the perfect ethic to arrive.
