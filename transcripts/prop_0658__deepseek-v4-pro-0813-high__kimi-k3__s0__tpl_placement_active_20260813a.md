[P1.1] Biometric mass surveillance is not merely a camera. It is an identity dragnet. When the state scans faces, gaits, or irises of everyone in a public square, it erases the difference between being seen in public and being identified, filed, and tracked. A person can cover a tattoo, leave a phone at home, or avoid a particular street, but no one can leave their face at home.
[P1.2] That is exactly why the default must be prohibition rather than regulation after the harm is already built.
[P1.3] The harm is not speculative. Facial recognition has repeatedly shown higher error rates for women and people with darker skin, and in real policing those errors become wrongful stops, arrests, and detentions, not abstract false positives. But even a perfectly accurate system chills protected life. People avoid protests, places of worship, clinics, union halls, and political meetings if attendance is logged.
[P1.4] Democracy depends on the ability to assemble and dissent without being enrolled in a permanent identification database.
[P1.5] Absent a presumption of unlawfulness, function creep is guaranteed. Systems justified by terrorism are redeployed for shoplifting, fare evasion, border enforcement, or monitoring demonstrators. Each new use appears small once the infrastructure exists. That is why the line must be drawn before deployment, not after the surveillance apparatus has normalized itself.
[P1.6] The proposition does not disable the state. A narrowly defined emergency authorization process still permits biometric surveillance for a specific, imminent threat, under independent authorization, with strict time limits and after-the-fact review. That preserves genuine security needs while rejecting the CON-style blank check. If the state truly needs a temporary biometric dragnet to stop a bombing or mass-casualty threat, it can meet that test.
[P1.7] What it cannot do is treat “safety” and “efficiency” as permanent reasons to monitor everyone.
[P1.8] Ordinary policing already has warrants, suspects, and targeted leads. Mass biometrics reverses that logic: the entire public becomes a lineup until proven irrelevant. The law should start from the opposite presumption.
