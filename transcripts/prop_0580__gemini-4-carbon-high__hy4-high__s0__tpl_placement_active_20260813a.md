[C1.1] The question is not whether cash lowers a poverty statistic. Give a family cash and measured income rises. The proposition is that monthly child credits do so more cost-effectively than equivalent spending on childcare subsidies. That is backwards: cash principally changes the income line by the transfer; subsidies change both the budget constraint and the parent's ability to earn.
[C1.2] The concession that "cost-effectiveness depends on time horizons, parental labor supply responses, and how poverty itself is measured" [P1.1] is decisive. The temporary expansion and post-expiry rebound [P1.2] show a transfer counted as income while it lasted. They do not show a durable escape from poverty. A one-year measured drop is weaker than permanently enabling stable hours, a better job, or training.
[C1.3] Childcare subsidies attack a recurring cause of hardship in working families: low wages are swallowed by care costs before rent, food, and utilities are paid. Reduce that cost and another shift becomes worthwhile, missed work from unstable care falls, and earnings compound. More work also generates taxes and lowers reliance on other benefits. A broad monthly credit, by contrast, goes to many households whose poverty status will not change and to families without that barrier; those dollars are less targeted per unit of poverty reduction.
[C1.4] Reach objections—non-filers, stay-at-home caregivers, irregular shifts, rural shortages [P1.3]—are design problems, not a reason to prefer cash. Subsidies can include automatic enrollment, nonstandard-hour care, higher reimbursement in shortage areas, and provider supply support. Each dollar still buys care plus earning capacity.
[C1.5] Monthly timing gives cash no special advantage: rent, food, utilities, and care bills are all monthly. But a check can be absorbed by rent, debts, or ordinary consumption without altering productive capacity. Childcare subsidies turn public spending into income after care and access to work. On durable poverty reduction per public dollar, they beat a cleaner but thinner short-run statistic.
