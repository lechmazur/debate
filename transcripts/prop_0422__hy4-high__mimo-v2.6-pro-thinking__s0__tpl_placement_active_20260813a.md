[P1.1] A young child’s ticket is not a ticket for an unaccompanied passenger. It is a ticket for a child who needs the accompanying adult within reach. During turbulence, sudden braking, crowding, or an emergency instruction, that adult is part of the safety system. Crews serve the whole vehicle; they should not become substitute caregivers because a seat map split a family.
[P1.2] Separation by several rows is not a minor inconvenience. It degrades the care the journey presupposes.
[P1.3] Nor is sitting together a premium luxury. Extra legroom, a quiet carriage, or a better view are upgrades. A parent beside a young child is the ordinary completion of the service sold: two tickets, one supervised child, one responsible adult. When an airline or rail company sells the cheapest fare but charges later for adjacency, or pushes families into a higher fare bucket, it monetizes a need the customer cannot responsibly decline.
[P1.4] The objection that families “chose” the cheap ticket assumes real choice. A parent cannot choose to make a four-year-old self-sufficient; the actual choices are pay, beg strangers to move, accept separation, or cancel.
[P1.5] The current practice also shifts costs outward: gate agents and conductors repair assignments during boarding, crews manage distressed children, and other passengers are pressured to surrender seats. A rule requiring free family seating makes the operator internalize those predictable costs through its own allocation system.
[P1.6] That rule can be narrow. Seat young children with their accompanying adults in the same cabin or class, within immediate reach, with no add-on seat fees or premium fare penalties, using advance assignment or automatic grouping before departure. It need not guarantee windows, bulkheads, or premium cabins. If grouping has administrative costs, include them transparently in the base fare.
[P1.7] Operators that can price seats by the inch can also reserve adjacent pairs; what is missing is obligation, not technology. A child’s need for supervision should not be converted into a checkout penalty.
