[P1.2] The motion concerns the timetable, not the destination. Returning slowly still returns. Returning quickly requires a recession — not as an accident but as the instrument: raise borrowing costs until firms stop hiring, households stop spending, and enough people sit idle that wage growth cools. CON must defend that purchase: months shaved off an overshoot, paid for in jobs that never come back.
[P1.3] Consider who pays. Moderate inflation is diffuse and survivable: wages renegotiate, benefits index, fixed-rate borrowers are quietly helped, and the pain fades as contracts catch up. [P1.4] That persistence has a name — hysteresis: lost skills, lost firms, lost investment, a permanently lower path. Overshoots are a flow you outlive. Destroyed capacity is a stock you cannot recover.
[P1.5] The tool is also mismatched to the problem. When inflation comes from energy, food, war, or broken supply chains, crushing domestic demand does not produce more gas or ships. [P1.6] Credibility is about the destination, not the stopwatch. The 1970s warning concerns double-digit inflation in economies with formal wage indexation, not three percent under a central bank still publicly committed to two.
