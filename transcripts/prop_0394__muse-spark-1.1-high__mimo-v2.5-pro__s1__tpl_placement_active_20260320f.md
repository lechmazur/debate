[P1.1] Accessibility should be the default, not a favor you have to beg for. Right now a deaf student, a student with ADHD, or a student who processes language more slowly must disclose, document, wait weeks for an office to approve, and then hope the professor complies. [P1.2] Provide captions, recordings, and readable materials unless you can articulate a strong pedagogical reason not to.
[P1.3] That default reflects how people actually learn. Lectures are dense, fast, and ephemeral. A student who misses a sentence because of an accent, a cough, or a momentary lapse can't rewind reality. Captions let you search for a term instead of scrubbing an hour of audio.
[P1.4] Recordings let you pause, repeat, take notes properly after, not frantically during. Accessible documents — real PDFs, not photos of pages, slides with actual headings, videos with descriptions — let screen readers work and let everyone else copy, translate, or study on a phone. [P1.5] The cost objection is obsolete. Every laptop records, auto-captioning is built into the platforms schools already pay for. The expensive part is the current bureaucracy: individual requests, appeals, manual retrofitting. A presumption is cheaper.

"Students won't come" or "it's my intellectual property" isn't a pedagogical reason, it's a preference for captive audiences.
[P1.7] Put the burden where justification belongs: on withholding access, not on needing it. That is what a university that takes learning seriously does — make the learning itself accessible first, and justify exclusion only when teaching truly demands it.
