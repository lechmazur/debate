[P1.1] Transparency rules already exist for content; the motion asks us to extend the same logic to targeting. When a platform shows me an AI-generated video, disclosure tells me the artifact is synthetic. But when an AI system decides that I, specifically, should see a message framed around my insecurities about money, while my neighbor sees the same policy framed around patriotism, no existing label tells either of us that the persuasion itself was machine-optimized. That gap is the motion's target, and it matters for three reasons.
[P1.2] First, persuasion that is invisible in its mechanism is more manipulative, not less. A billboard is seen by everyone; a micro-targeted campaign exploits what a system has inferred about me—my impulsivity, my fears, my voting history—often more accurately than I could articulate myself. Studies of behavioral advertising and the Cambridge Analytica episode made clear that the harm isn't only in the content but in the asymmetry: the persuader knows me, and I don't know the persuader. A label restores symmetry.
[P1.3] It converts covert exploitation of personal inference into a disclosed choice.
[P1.4] Second, the machinery already exists. Platforms already track who paid for an ad, what audience parameters were used, and which models optimized delivery. Requiring a "personalized by AI" tag is an engineering disclosure of information the platform already holds—not a demand to build surveillance or invent new law from nothing. We label genetically modified food, we label sponsored posts, we disclose political ad funders in many jurisdictions.
[P1.5] This is a modest extension of a settled norm.
[P1.6] Third, the counterargument—labels will be ignored or will clutter the interface—cuts the wrong way. We do not abandon ingredient lists because some shoppers skip them. Disclosure protects the attentive without harming anyone, and it creates accountability trails for regulators and researchers after the fact.
[P1.7] The principle is simple: people are entitled to know not just what is being shown to them, but why them. A democracy can survive persuasion it can see; it struggles with persuasion engineered in the dark.
