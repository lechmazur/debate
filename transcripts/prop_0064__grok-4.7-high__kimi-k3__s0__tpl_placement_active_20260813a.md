[P1.1] Employers should be prohibited from requiring always-on AI companions that monitor, prompt, or coach workers throughout the day. The issue is compulsory, continuous oversight, not technology as such.
[P1.2] Ordinary management already reviews results, sets priorities, and gives feedback. An always-on companion is different: it stays present across the workday, scores pauses and keystrokes, interrupts with prompts, and treats judgment itself as something a machine must continuously correct. Making that a job requirement converts employment into a license for real-time surveillance.
[P1.3] Consent under that pressure is largely fictional. A worker who refuses can be denied the role, marked uncooperative, or quietly replaced. When the alternative is lost income, “you agreed” does not describe a free choice. A prohibition leaves room for optional tools while forbidding the coercive version.
[P1.4] The harms are practical. People under constant scoring produce visible busyness instead of better work. They skip necessary pauses, avoid hard questions, and hide the uneven process of thinking because an algorithm is watching. Real-time prompts also break concentration, the resource the employer claims to want.
[P1.5] Permanent coaching treats experienced adults as trainees who cannot be trusted with their own attention, and over time that dependency weakens skill rather than building it.
[P1.6] The data trail makes the problem worse. Productivity logs can feed rankings, discipline, scheduling cuts, or inferences about mood, health, and loyalty. Workers seldom see the model, seldom contest the score, and seldom control how long the record lives. Once continuous capture is normalized as a condition of work, those uses expand quietly.
[P1.7] Companies may still offer companions. Workers may still choose them. What must be prohibited is the rule that says submit to an always-listening coach or forfeit your livelihood. Dignity at work requires at least that line.
