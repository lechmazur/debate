[P1.1] Aging combined sewers do not have a manageable overflow problem. They have a design flaw that guarantees failure: rainwater and sewage share the same pipe, so every storm forces a choice between flooding streets and basements or dumping untreated waste into rivers and harbors.
[P1.2] Continued overflow management accepts that choice forever. Storage tanks, tunnels, screens, and disinfection reduce how often overflows happen, but they do not eliminate them — they are built to spill when capacity is exceeded. That capacity is becoming less reliable as storms grow more intense and pavement leaves rain nowhere to go. A system designed to pollute less often is still designed to pollute.
[P1.3] Separation removes the cause. Sewage flows to treatment in a steadier, smaller stream, without the shocks that overwhelm plants, bypass treatment, and drive up energy and chemical use to treat mostly rain. Stormwater can be slowed, filtered, infiltrated, or reused where it falls, easing flooding instead of concentrating it in one pipe. Beaches stay open, drinking-water intakes face fewer pathogen spikes, and homes stop backing up with sewage because it rained.
[P1.4] The upfront price looks worst only if the comparison pretends the old system is cheap to keep. Aging pipes must be dug up and replaced anyway; rebuilding them as combined sewers means paying for infrastructure and then paying again for tunnels, penalties, repairs, flood damage, and public-health costs for a lifetime. Separation concentrates spending into one rebuild, done once while streets are open, instead of locking a city into fifty more years of managing spills.
[P1.5] That lock-in is the decisive cost. The neighborhoods hit by backups and contaminated waterways are the lowest-lying and least able to recover. Replacing a combined system with another combined system asks them to endure the harm so the city can defer the bill. Separation is expensive because it ends the problem — and for infrastructure meant to last, ending it is the cheaper and responsible choice.
