[P1.2] Without presumptive sealing, a single juvenile mistake remains just a Google search away for employers, landlords, and universities, effectively extending a youth's sentence for a lifetime.
[P1.3] We must establish presumptive sealing of these digital records at adulthood for three reasons.

[P1.6] Third, neuroscience and developmental psychology demonstrate that the adolescent brain is fundamentally different from the adult brain. Recklessness in youth does not predict adult criminality. A presumptive seal honors this biological reality by ensuring that adult lives are judged on adult conduct.

The proposal treats a shoplifting charge and a pattern of predatory violence identically, defaulting to invisibility for both. That's not rehabilitation.

[C1.4] The PRO side frames this as a fix for a broken petition system. [C1.5] And consider the mechanism: once records are presumptively sealed, the burden flips. Instead of the individual showing why their record should be hidden, the state must scramble to identify and contest every inappropriate sealing before the deadline passes. [C1.6] The PRO side invokes neuroscience, but adolescent brain development is an argument for lighter sentencing and for *available* sealing pathways, not for abolishing case-by-case review entirely.
