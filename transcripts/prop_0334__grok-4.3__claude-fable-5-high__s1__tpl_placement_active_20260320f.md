[P1.1] Community notes correct political claims without removing them, which preserves the ability of users to examine evidence directly. When a video circulates an edited clip suggesting a candidate endorsed fraud, a note can attach the full transcript and primary records so viewers decide for themselves. Takedowns simply delete the clip, leaving no trace for anyone to check the edit.
[P1.2] Centralized removal teams face unavoidable limits on speed and perspective. They must apply rules to thousands of borderline cases daily, and any single moderator's blind spot becomes permanent deletion. Notes require visible sourcing and cross-user agreement, creating an audit trail that flags weak interventions before they spread.
[P1.3] The two systems also differ in incentives. Takedowns reward platforms for appearing decisive even when the removed content later proves accurate or protected. Notes tie success to whether the added context holds up under public view, so errors are corrected by further notes rather than defended as policy.
[P1.4] Political speech rarely consists of simple true-or-false statements. Most contested posts mix data, interpretation, and timing. Notes can isolate the factual element while leaving the rest intact; takedowns treat the entire item as one indivisible violation. Shifting priority toward notes therefore reduces collateral suppression on precisely the content where context is most contested.
