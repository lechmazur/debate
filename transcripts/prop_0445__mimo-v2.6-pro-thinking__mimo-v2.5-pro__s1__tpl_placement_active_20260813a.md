[P1.1] India's labor market has a paradox worth naming precisely: we have strict job protection for a shrinking formal minority and almost nothing for the informal majority. The result is not security — it is fear. Firms that fear headcount becoming a permanent liability keep employment below statutory thresholds, outsource core work, and design roles to stay casual. The worker who is "protected" by law is the worker firms avoid creating.
[P1.2] That is the mechanism to attack. Employment-law rigidity is not protecting Indian workers; it is pricing them out of formal employment. When dismissal is costly and uncertain, hiring slows, small firms refuse to grow past compliance thresholds, and jobs migrate into informal arrangements where no law meaningfully applies. Then we congratulate ourselves that the written law looks protective while the actual worker has no contract, no benefits, and no recourse.
[P1.3] The proposition is a bargain, and both halves matter. Flexible contracts let firms match staffing to demand, hire young workers without betting the enterprise on permanent headcount, and grow past the size where informality becomes the rational strategy. Universal social protection — portable health cover, unemployment support, provident-style savings that follow the worker rather than the employer, and retraining — is what makes that flexibility morally serious. It de-links survival from any single job.
[P1.4] This is strictly better than the status quo, which delivers the worst of both worlds: rigidity for the few, exposure for the many. Under the proposed exchange, a laid-off garment worker in Tiruppur still has health coverage and income support while she moves to the next employer — instead of clinging to a firm that will never hire her permanently in the first place.
[P1.5] Critically, this is not deregulation as abandonment. Keep minimum wages, occupational safety, anti-discrimination rules, and enforcement against fraud. Reform the employment contract itself. Flexibility generates the formal jobs and wage growth that fund the safety net; the safety net is what lets workers accept a dynamic market instead of demanding the frozen, exclusive protections India has today.
