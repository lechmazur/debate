[C1.2] Consider representation. Professional leagues have roughly thirty teams playing one sport under one ruleset. College athletics has over 350 Division I schools, dozens of sports, and revenue models ranging from nine-figure departments to programs operating at a loss. Who bargains for whom?

[C1.4] Then there's Title IX. If revenue athletes bargain collectively for compensation packages, those agreements implicate the entire athletic department's budget. [C1.5] Collective bargaining's central promise—workers setting floors—becomes a ceiling for everyone outside the revenue sports.

[C1.7] The motion frames bargaining as merely giving athletes "a voice." But collective bargaining isn't just voice—it's a legal regime that makes athletes employees, with everything that follows: dues, lockouts, grievances, and the end of the collegiate model most athletes actually chose.
