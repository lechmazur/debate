[P1.1] When an economy enters a downturn, the single greatest threat to fiscal solvency is not a temporary widening of the deficit—it is the collapse of the real economy. Automatic fiscal stabilizers, such as progressive tax relief and unemployment transfers, cushion private demand precisely when market activity contracts. Refusing to let these stabilizers expand because public debt ratios are already elevated commits a fatal economic error: the denominator trap.
[P1.2] Fiscal sustainability is fundamentally a ratio of debt to output. During a downturn, private consumption and investment sharply decline. If the state suppresses automatic relief to contain borrowing, it drains liquidity directly from households with the highest marginal propensity to spend. The resulting collapse in aggregate demand deepens the recession, shuttering solvent businesses, driving up structural unemployment, and eroding the tax base.
[P1.3] Under these conditions, nominal GDP falls faster than debt can be restrained, perversely driving debt-to-GDP ratios higher rather than lower.
[P1.4] Automatic stabilizers are uniquely effective because they are timely, targeted, and self-correcting. Unlike discretionary stimulus packages, which are vulnerable to legislative delay and political distortion, automatic stabilizers inject liquidity instantly where the marginal economic impact is largest. Crucially, they automatically withdraw as recovery takes hold and incomes rise. They do not create permanent structural deficits; they provide temporary counter-cyclical support that prevents a cyclical downturn from hardening into long-term stagnation.
[P1.5] Elevated public debt cannot be sustained by an impoverished, scarred economy. Long-term unemployment and capital destruction permanently lower potential growth, ensuring a smaller revenue base for decades. The correct response to high sovereign debt is disciplined consolidation during economic expansions, not procyclical austerity during contractions. To protect the productive capacity of the economy and secure true long-run solvency, automatic fiscal stabilizers must expand when a downturn strikes.
