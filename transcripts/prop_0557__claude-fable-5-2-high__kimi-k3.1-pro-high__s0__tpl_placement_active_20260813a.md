[C1.1] The decisive flaw in reinstatement is that it turns a sometimes useful data point into a universal barrier. PRO must show not merely that scores can add information, but that every applicant should be compelled to supply one. Test-optional admissions already capture the benefit: students with helpful scores submit them, while universities still evaluate transcripts, course rigor and school context. A requirement adds no new way to read scores; it only excludes or deters students who cannot obtain a useful one.
[C1.2] A common scale is not a common opportunity. Preparation quality, repeated attempts, transportation, test-center access, disabilities and familiarity with the exam all shape performance. Fee waivers and free practice materials reduce some costs; they do not equalize time, coaching or access. PRO is right that essays and activities can be bought.
[C1.3] That argues for discounting purchased polish, not making another wealth-sensitive component compulsory.
[C1.4] Nor does incremental prediction settle the question. Grades reflect years of sustained work across courses, not one sitting, and can be interpreted alongside the opportunities a school actually offered. Even if scores improve statistical prediction for some institutions, better prediction is not automatically better admissions: graduation also depends on affordability, academic support and whether a university admits students it is equipped to serve. A marginal forecasting gain cannot justify a blanket barrier when optional submission preserves that gain without the barrier.
[C1.5] PRO’s rural student actually exposes the contradiction. If her score is strong relative to her circumstances, she can submit it under test-optional admissions and benefit. If students mistakenly withhold helpful scores, universities should publish clearer guidance and improve counseling. Compelling every other applicant to test is a remarkably indirect remedy for a communication problem.
[C1.6] MIT and Dartmouth are private, highly selective institutions; one flagship’s experience cannot establish policy for public universities with radically different selective, regional and access missions. Those examples show that requirements may fit particular enrollment strategies, not that reinstatement is generally justified.
[C1.7] Keep the yardstick available. Do not make it a tollbooth to consideration.
