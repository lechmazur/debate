[C1.1] The calculator analogy fails at the core. A calculator automates arithmetic so students can focus on higher-order reasoning. Generative AI does the opposite: it automates the higher-order reasoning—argumentation, synthesis, expression—leaving students with the mechanical task of pasting and formatting. That is not a new tool in the educational lineage; it is a substitute for the very skill the assignment exists to develop.
[C1.2] When a student writes an essay, the educational value lives in the struggle: organizing thoughts, wrestling with evidence, discovering what they actually believe. AI collapses that struggle into a prompt. Citation and transparency do not restore the learning—they merely document its absence. A student who submits AI-generated text with a citation has been honest about not doing the thinking, but they still have not done the thinking.
[C1.3] "Permissible by default" also reverses the burden in a way that corrodes classroom norms. It makes AI use the assumed baseline and forces teachers to justify every restriction, audit every submission, and distinguish between acceptable and unacceptable degrees of AI involvement case by case. That is not transparency; it is an enforcement nightmare dressed up as accountability.
[C1.4] The claim that bans are unenforceable is an argument against all academic rules. Students can plagiarize from many sources we still prohibit. We do not legalize cheating because some students evade detection. The answer is better enforcement and clearer expectations, not surrender.
[C1.5] Schools exist to build the foundation that makes professional AI use meaningful. Professionals use AI effectively precisely because they can already write, reason, and evaluate independently. Schools that permit AI by default produce students who never develop that foundation—workers who cannot judge whether the machine's output is wrong, shallow, or biased because they never learned to do the work themselves.
[C1.6] The proposition trades short-term convenience for long-term cognitive deficit. The default must remain restriction, with educators granting permission only where AI genuinely serves the learning objective.
