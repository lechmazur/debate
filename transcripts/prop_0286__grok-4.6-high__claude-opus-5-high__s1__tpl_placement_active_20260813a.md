[P1.1] Space tourism is not exploration. It is a luxury product sold to people already drowning in surplus, while climate breakdown and severe poverty still kill, displace, and trap millions. Ethical justification fails here not because pleasure is sinful, but because duties are ordered. When the worst-off still lack the basics, and the climate they inhabit is further loaded for entertainment, "because we can" describes power, not right.
[P1.2] The carbon objection is decisive. Rocket tourism is among the most emissions-heavy activities money can buy, injecting greenhouse gases and soot high in the atmosphere for minutes of spectacle. Climate ethics now turns on a thin remaining carbon budget. Every other sector is told to cut, delay, or transform.
[P1.3] Creating a new market in luxury combustion, while the vulnerable still lack cooling, housing, and reliable food, spends scarce atmospheric room on status.
[P1.4] Resources follow the same logic. Capital, engineers, and political glamour absorbed by tourist rockets are not costless. They could speed clean energy, adaptation, public health, and debt relief—gains that fall first on people with no surplus. The spin-off defense is a bait-and-switch.
[P1.5] If rocketry has public value, fund that value. A joyride does not inherit the moral credit of a weather satellite.
[P1.6] Then the spectacle. The richest leave the atmosphere while coastal workers lose homes to storms they did not cause. A photograph of Earth from a capsule does not discharge duties to the people living on it. Ethics is comparative: preventable suffering stacked on luxury emissions is harder to justify than either fact alone.
[P1.7] Until climate stability and extreme poverty stop being open wounds, space tourism remains a confession that we will burn the future for those least at risk.
