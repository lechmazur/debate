[P1.1] Start with what the motion actually says. It bans no tourism, no photography, no sharing. Only this: when viral traffic is verifiably damaging resident life and the sites themselves, destinations may turn down the amplifier. To oppose, one must argue that even once the damage is plain — trail eroded, neighborhood hollowed out — the geotag must stay up and the promotion must roll on.
[P1.2] Opposing the motion means defending that.
[P1.3] The mechanism is simple. A geotag turns vague interest into an exact coordinate; an influencer's post turns a coordinate into a surge. Fragile spots are sized for dozens, not thousands in a weekend — no parking, no toilets, no trail crews. Maya Bay closed outright to recover from its own fame; Iceland's Fjaðrárgljúfur canyon was fenced off after a music video sent visitors flooding in; a California town's geotagged poppy fields were trampled by the crowds drawn to photograph them.
[P1.4] These places did not lack admiration; they lacked a brake.
[P1.5] The economics are just as lopsided. The influencer monetizes the post; the platform monetizes the attention; the resident absorbs the congestion, the rent pressure, and the cleanup; the landscape absorbs erosion it cannot undo. Day-trip crowds chasing a photo often spend little locally — gains privatized, costs dumped on people who never consented. Limiting commercial-scale promotion of fragile coordinates is not censorship; it is refusing to let one business model run on someone else's home.
[P1.6] The remedies are modest: geotag-removal requests, as Jackson Hole's tourism board made; de-marketing campaigns like Amsterdam's; permits and timed entry, as at Angels Landing. This manages flow; it does not close doors.
[P1.7] The weighing is decisive. A delayed post, a blurred tag, a redirected crowd — all reversible. A trampled meadow, a displaced community, a dead reef — not. When one side of the scale risks inconvenience and the other irreversibility, you limit the reversible harm.
[P1.8] Fragile places, and the people who live in them, deserve that defense.
