[P1.1] Societies emerging from dictatorship or civil war face a choice about what kind of peace they are building. Amnesty buys a peace built on silence, where perpetrators keep their freedom and victims are told to move on without ever being told the truth. Truth commissions with public testimony buy a different, sturdier peace: one built on an acknowledged record that the whole society can stand on. That difference is why public truth commissions should usually be the preferred instrument.
[P1.2] Start with the mechanism of durability. Amnesty deals typically depend on the continued goodwill of the very actors who committed the abuses. Silence is fragile because it requires everyone to keep agreeing to forget, including people with grievances that never went away. South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission, with its televised hearings, is the model precisely because it converted private trauma into public, contested, but ultimately shared fact.
[P1.3] Chile's Rettig Commission similarly established an official record of disappearances that blanket amnesties in Argentina or Brazil could not produce, which is part of why those countries later reopened prosecutions once amnesty laws lost legitimacy. Buried truths tend to resurface, and when they do, they destabilize the very stability amnesty was supposed to secure.
[P1.4] Second, public testimony serves victims in a way silence structurally cannot. Denying victims a forum to be heard does not heal a society, it just relocates the wound. Public hearings give victims standing, an official acknowledgment that what happened to them was wrong and mattered. That recognition, not forgetting, is what allows people to actually live alongside former enemies without simmering resentment poisoning the next generation.
[P1.5] Third, truth commissions do not require abandoning stability concerns; they can be paired with conditional amnesty, reduced sentencing, or reparations, capturing amnesty's stabilizing benefits while adding legitimacy that silence alone cannot buy. That combination, not blanket silence, is the real comparison CON must beat.
