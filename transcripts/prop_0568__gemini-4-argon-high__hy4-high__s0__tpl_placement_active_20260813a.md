[P1.1] Picture a parent at a major pharmacy counter holding a sick child and a twenty-dollar bill—legal tender—being told they cannot buy medicine because they do not have a bank card. When access to food, water, and medicine requires approval from a private bank or tech platform, we have privatized the basic right to survive.
[P1.2] First, requiring major retailers to accept cash for essentials protects the most vulnerable from exclusion. Millions of people remain unbanked or underbanked because of poverty, minimum-balance fees, age, or disability. For others, cash is a matter of physical safety: survivors of domestic abuse often rely on untraceable cash to buy groceries or medication without alerting an abuser who monitors every digital transaction. Governments cannot print legal tender only to let major grocers and pharmacies tell the poorest citizens their money is worthless.
[P1.3] Second, cash is irreplaceable infrastructure when digital systems fail. Power grids go down, storms knock out cell towers, and cyberattacks or routine software glitches regularly freeze payment networks across entire regions. During an emergency, people urgently need food, water, and prescriptions. If major retailers dismantle their cash infrastructure for marginal savings, a single network outage instantly locks whole communities out of survival goods.
[P1.4] Maintaining cash capability at major retail hubs is basic civic resilience.
[P1.5] Finally, look at the proportionality of this mandate. It does not burden tiny coffee shops, solo vendors, or luxury boutiques. It targets only major retailers selling essential goods—large supermarket chains, big-box stores, and national pharmacies. These corporations already possess the revenue scale, security systems, and armored-car logistics to handle cash at a negligible cost per transaction.
[P1.6] On one side of the scale is a minor operational convenience for massive retail conglomerates. On the other is the guarantee that every person can buy food and medicine, whether they lack a bank account or the local network is down. Essential goods must remain accessible to everyone.
