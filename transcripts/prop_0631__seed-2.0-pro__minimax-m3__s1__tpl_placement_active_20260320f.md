[P1.1] Copyright exists for one core bargain: society grants a temporary monopoly over expression only to reward the human creative labour that produced that expression. That is not an ideological add-on, that is the foundational logic of every United States copyright statute since the beginning.
[P1.2] Right now, parties are trying to carve out an exception for AI. They want to type a three line prompt, hit generate, and walk away with 90 years of exclusive rights over the output. This is not a new edge case: we already have settled rules for this. If you tell a human artist "draw a sad fox on a ferry at sunset" you do not own the copyright to the resulting painting.
[P1.3] Nobody argues this. We only debate it now because the artist executing the instruction is code.
[P1.4] This proposition does not ban AI from creative work. It does not even bar copyright for works that use AI. It only requires that a human actually exercised substantial creative control over the final expression, and can document that. If you adjust every composition choice, rewrite every paragraph, paint over the base output, you qualify.
[P1.5] This is not a hostile restriction, it is the same standard applied to every other tool.
[P1.6] Without this line, copyright stops performing its function. We will hand out long monopolies to people who did no creative work at all, just pressed a button. That rewards rent-seeking, not creation. That harms every actual human creator, and breaks the bargain the entire system rests on.
