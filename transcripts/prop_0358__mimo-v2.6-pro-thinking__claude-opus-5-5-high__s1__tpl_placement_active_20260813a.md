[P1.1] A city that wants fewer bodies has two levers: change how crashes happen and how bad they are, or punish people harder after someone dies. The first lever works. The second mostly makes us feel decisive.
[P1.2] Start with physics. The energy released in a crash rises with the square of speed. A pedestrian struck at 30 mph is many times more likely to die than one struck at 20 mph. Lower default limits do not just reduce crash frequency; they convert fatal crashes into survivable ones.
[P1.3] No penalty passed after a collision changes that arithmetic for the person already hit.
[P1.4] Now consider what harsher post-crash penalties actually reach. They can only deter drivers who are weighing consequences at the moment of risk. But the large share of serious crashes involve distraction, fatigue, impairment, misjudgment of a gap, or plain error — not calculated gambles. You cannot deter a mistake with a longer sentence.
[P1.5] And deterrence research has long told us what matters is the certainty of being caught, not the severity of the punishment after the fact. Traffic enforcement is sparse and intermittent; raising penalties for a rare event that a driver does not expect to experience adds very little behavior change.
[P1.6] Street redesign is stronger still because it is self-enforcing. A narrowed lane, a raised crosswalk, a curb extension, a road diet does not require a patrol car, a citation, or a judge. It works at 2 a.m. on a Tuesday with no officer present and works on the driver who never reads the ordinance. It works on visitors who never learned the local penalty schedule.
[P1.7] It works on the driver having the worst day of their life.
[P1.8] Lower limits plus redesign attack the cause of death — kinetic energy and conflict points — while harsher penalties attack only one pathway to recklessness and only after the harm is done. If the stated goal is fewer traffic deaths, prevention beats punishment. Prevention measurably moves the fatality rate; punishment mostly moves the sentencing guidelines.
