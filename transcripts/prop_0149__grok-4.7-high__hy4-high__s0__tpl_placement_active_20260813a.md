[P1.1] Cities exist for the people who live in them, not for owners who convert houses into unregulated event venues. Stronger nuisance rules against party houses and disruptive short-term rentals are warranted because the damage is concentrated and recurring: sleepless nights, blocked driveways, overflowing trash, late fights, and streets that stop feeling like neighborhoods. Residents absorb those costs; hosts keep the booking fee.
[P1.2] The tourism objection confuses ordinary visitors with commercial blowouts. Cities can welcome tourists through hotels, licensed stays, occupancy caps, local contacts, and quiet hours. What “flexible hosting” often protects in practice is the right to hand a residential house to a crowd for a weekend party and leave the fallout on the block. Hotels already handle large groups under commercial rules.
[P1.3] A cul-de-sac was never built for that load, and tourism does not collapse when the worst rentals are constrained.
[P1.4] The core problem is an externality. Profit is private; noise, parking chaos, safety calls, and lost quiet are public. Weak enforcement invites the worst operators, because the party house is more lucrative precisely when it ignores the costs it dumps next door. Clear permits, occupancy limits, noise penalties, and swift revocation do not abolish hosting.
[P1.5] They stop rentals that function as nuisance businesses in disguise.
[P1.6] Housing pressure makes the case stronger. High-turnover party use pulls units out of the long-term market that workers and families need. A city that treats every house as a potential festival site chooses visitor volume over a stable population. Tourism revenue is real.
[P1.7] It is not a blank check. Cities already regulate bars, hotels, and nightlife. Applying the same discipline here is consistency, not hostility to guests.
[P1.8] Residents should not have to renegotiate their right to sleep every weekend with a revolving set of strangers. Enforceable nuisance rules draw a simple line: revenue from a property ends where it systematically destroys the livability of the properties around it. Tourism that depends on erasing that line is not worth protecting.
