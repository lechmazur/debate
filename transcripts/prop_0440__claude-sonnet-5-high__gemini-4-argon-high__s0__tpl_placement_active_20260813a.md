[P1.1] Gun violence in American cities is concentrated: a tiny number of people and a tiny number of blocks drive most shootings. That single fact decides this debate. A strategy that targets the actual drivers of violence will always outperform, dollar for dollar, a strategy that spreads resources across an entire city regardless of where the risk actually lives.
[P1.2] That's the structural case for focused deterrence and violence interruption over blanket police staffing increases. Focused deterrence programs - identify the small number of high-risk individuals or groups responsible for most shootings, deliver a direct message combining swift consequences with genuine offramps like jobs and social services, and concentrate enforcement only where it's needed. Violence interruption programs, the "credible messenger" model, put former gang members and formerly incarcerated outreach workers on the street to mediate conflicts before they become shootings, reaching people who would never trust or cooperate with police.
[P1.3] Both approaches have been implemented in cities like Boston, Chicago, Oakland, and Richmond, with evaluators repeatedly finding double-digit percentage reductions in shootings or homicides in the targeted areas.
[P1.4] Compare the cost structure. Hiring one additional patrol officer costs a city well over a hundred thousand dollars a year once you include salary, benefits, training, and equipment, and that officer's time is diluted across an entire precinct, responding to calls that have nothing to do with gun violence. A violence interruption worker costs a fraction of that and spends a hundred percent of their time on the highest-risk relationships and locations. Focused deterrence doesn't require new headcount at all; it requires redirecting existing police and prosecutorial attention toward the right targets, which is a management reform, not a budget expansion.
[P1.5] More officers, deployed generically, mostly produce more stops, more arrests for minor offenses, and more contact between police and communities already distrustful of them - with weak and inconsistent evidence that staffing alone moves gun violence rates. Precision beats volume. That's the fight CON has to win and can't.
