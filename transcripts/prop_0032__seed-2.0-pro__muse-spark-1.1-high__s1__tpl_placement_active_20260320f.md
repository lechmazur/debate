[P1.1] When a customer opens an email that says they carry a high predictive risk for early onset Alzheimer’s, they do not just process information. They call their partner to cancel plans for a second home. They decline a promotion they worked ten years to earn. They stop bothering to save for retirement.
[P1.2] All of this happens before they stop to ask what the risk number actually means. Most people cannot correctly interpret these results. Consistent survey data shows most ordinary adults will read a "2x increased risk" result as certainty they will develop the condition, not that their baseline risk rose from 1% to 2%. This is not ignorance, this is specialized statistical and clinical context no one brings to a consumer website.
[P1.3] This rule does not block anyone from accessing their data. It does not forbid results, it does not override individual choice. It only requires that before customers receive these high-impact numbers, they are given access to a certified counselor who can explain what the result means, what it does not mean, and what reasonable next steps actually are. We already accept this standard for every other high-stakes medical test.
[P1.4] No one argues you should get a positive HIV result mailed to you with no support. No one calls that paternalism. They call it basic duty of care. Genetic testing companies will never do this voluntarily.
[P1.5] Counseling costs money, cuts into profit margins, and slows the checkout flow. That is exactly why it must be required. The alternative is allowing companies to sell life-altering information with zero guardrails, and leave ordinary people to clean up the damage alone.

[C1.1] PRO tells a tragic story about life plans canceled on a misread email [P1.1] and then proposes a gate to prevent it. But requiring certified counseling *before* anyone can see high-impact results doesn't prevent that misreading. It just puts a tollbooth in front of it.
[C1.2] There are only a few thousand certified genetic counselors in the US serving millions of DTC customers. A mandate to provide access to one before results are released means one of two things: a months-long queue, or a $200+ fee passed to the customer for a five-minute check-the-box video call with a contractor hired by the company itself to clear liability. That destroys the only thing that made DTC testing democratizing — cheap, direct access without needing an academic medical center.
[C1.3] This rule prices out low-income users and recreates the specialist bottleneck DTC was invented to bypass. Less harm for the privileged, no access for everyone else, is not duty of care.
[C1.4] It also misunderstands how people learn [P1.2]. Forcing an unwanted lecture at the moment of peak anxiety, right before the results drop, is the worst time for comprehension. People will click through it like Terms of Service. Real understanding comes from good plain-language report design, risk presented in absolute terms, and optional support you can use when you're actually ready — with your own doctor, not a stranger employed by the testing company reading a compliance script.
[C1.5] The HIV analogy [P1.4] collapses on contact. An HIV test is diagnostic, immediately actionable, and consequential for others. An APOE or BRCA result from a consumer chip is probabilistic, often weakly predictive, and deeply personal. We don't require certified counseling before a concerning mammogram hits your patient portal, or a high cholesterol result, or many clinical exomes today.
[C1.6] Medicine moved to opt-in for a reason.
[C1.7] You should not need permission from a licensed intermediary to read your own genome. Require transparency and offer counseling. Don't mandate a gatekeeper.
