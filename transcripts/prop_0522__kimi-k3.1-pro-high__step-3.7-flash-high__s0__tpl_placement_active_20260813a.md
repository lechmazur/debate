[C1.1] The motion mistakes haste for compassion: expanding climate-linked humanitarian pathways before global displacement law is settled will create cascading, irreversible harms that far outweigh any short-term protective gains. First, ungoverned pathways fail the people they are meant to serve. Without settled, uniform eligibility criteria tied directly to climate-specific displacement, not general economic migration, ad hoc programs either exclude the most vulnerable—such as subsistence farmers in the Sahel who cannot meet host countries’ demanding documentary requirements—or divert limited spots to migrants fleeing general hardship.
[C1.2] The EU’s 2015 ad hoc refugee response, lacking clear rules, left hundreds of thousands in limbo for years, many deported after building roots in host communities, a harm no policy tweak can undo. Second, pre-law expansion erodes the global efforts that would prevent displacement in the first place. When high-income countries roll out resettlement pathways unilaterally, they gain cover to shirk their binding climate finance and mitigation obligations. The U.S. has repeatedly cited its narrow Temporary Protected Status designations for climate-affected Caribbean nations to justify blocking international climate aid, leaving more people displaced as climate impacts worsen.
[C1.3] Third, PRO’s claim that pilot programs let institutions learn ignores that temporary, revocable pre-law pathways create permanent limbo for admitted migrants. Without settled legal status, people cannot access long-term housing, healthcare, or education for their children, as their presence is tied to political whims rather than binding rules. Thousands of climate-displaced people in the U.S. have lived under TPS for decades with no path to citizenship, their lives disrupted permanently even as the program that admitted them can be scrapped overnight.
[C1.4] These irreversible harms far outweigh the speculative benefits of acting before law is settled. High-income countries should wait for a binding global framework that protects the most vulnerable, rather than rushing half-measures that fail everyone.
