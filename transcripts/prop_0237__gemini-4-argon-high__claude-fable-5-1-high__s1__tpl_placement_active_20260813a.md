[P1.1] Right now, U.S. border policy makes a perverse demand: to seek protection or opportunity, you must pay a cartel thousands of dollars, brave the Darién Gap, and physically reach U.S. soil. Because our border asylum process serves as a catch-all for both persecuted refugees and economic migrants, immigration courts are buried under a multi-year backlog. Adopting a “safe third country” rule in exchange for expanded legal pathways replaces this deadly physical lottery with an orderly, humane system.
[P1.2] Neither enforcement nor legal pathways can work alone. Expanded legal pathways—such as regional processing hubs, humanitarian parole, and expanded work visas—create a safe front door that allows people to apply near home without embarking on a perilous journey. Yet as long as irregular border crossing yields a multi-year release into the U.S. interior, smugglers will keep filling buses. A sharp “safe third country” rule closes that back door.
[P1.3] If a migrant passes through countries where they can safely find refuge, or bypasses accessible regional application channels to cross the southern border unlawfully, they forfeit U.S. asylum eligibility. Pairing a firm border consequence with real legal alternatives starves transnational cartels of billions in smuggling revenue and spares families from extortion, kidnapping, and death in transit.
[P1.4] Crucially, limiting border asylum is the only way to save protection for those who genuinely need it. When millions of workers use the asylum queue simply because no other legal avenue exists, a political dissident fleeing torture waits years in limbo. Channeling economic migrants into labor pathways and screening refugees before they travel clears the docket for urgent claims.
[P1.5] Finally, visible disorder at the southern border corrodes the public’s willingness to welcome newcomers at all. Trading unmanaged border chaos for structured, lawful pathways is the only way to protect vulnerable lives while sustaining the political consensus required to keep America’s doors open.
