[P1.1] When a university writes an athlete a check directly from ticket sales, broadcast deals, and licensing revenue, it has already crossed the line from amateur education to paid performance. Pretending otherwise through name, image, and likeness deals brokered by booster collectives does not preserve amateurism; it preserves deniability. The athlete generating the revenue has no seat at the table where its value, risks, and conditions are decided.
[P1.2] Formal collective bargaining replaces that fiction with enforceable representation. A certified association can negotiate what ad hoc contracts never reliably provide: a minimum share and timely payment, limits on required hours and travel, guaranteed medical coverage for sports injuries that outlasts eligibility, due process before discipline or loss of pay, and clear rules on transfers and use of likeness.
[P1.3] Instead of thousands of individual athletes signing opaque, unequal deals under pressure to commit, standards are transparent, grievable, and identical in their basic protections.
[P1.4] That structure also protects schools. A bargained agreement can set caps, roster rules, contract lengths, and eligibility terms with athlete consent, giving programs planning stability that endless antitrust lawsuits and state-by-state NIL laws cannot. Compliance offices currently police athletes on behalf of institutions; they do not and cannot represent athletes against those same institutions when money, playing time, or health is at stake. A conflict of interest is not a representation system.
[P1.5] The proposition is deliberately narrow. It does not unionize every intramural player or college without revenue sharing; it applies where schools have chosen direct payment. Once they profit jointly with athletes from a commercial product, the workers producing that product deserve the ordinary tool every other revenue-generating campus and professional workforce uses to avoid exploitation and chaos: collective bargaining, not improvised deals and self-interested compliance.
