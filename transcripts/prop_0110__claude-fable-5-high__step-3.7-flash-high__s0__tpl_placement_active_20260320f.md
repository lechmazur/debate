[P1.1] The motion asks a simple empirical question: which system design buys more health per dollar? The answer is written in the world's largest natural experiment. The United States, the only rich country built around private insurance, spends roughly seventeen percent of GDP on healthcare — about double what most peer nations spend per person — yet has the lowest life expectancy among wealthy democracies, the highest infant and maternal mortality, and the highest rate of avoidable deaths. Countries with public systems — Britain, Canada, Australia, Japan, the Nordics — spend half as much and live years longer.
[P1.2] If private insurance delivered efficiency, this is precisely where it would show. Instead it shows the opposite, at scale, over decades.
[P1.3] Three mechanisms explain why this is structural, not accidental.
[P1.4] First, administrative waste. Private insurance multiplies billing departments, prior authorizations, marketing, underwriting, and profit margins. Administrative overhead in the US consumes several times what single-payer systems spend. That money buys paperwork, not health.
[P1.5] Second, bargaining power. A public payer negotiates as a monopsony, driving down prices for drugs, devices, and procedures. Fragmented private insurers cannot. The same insulin, the same MRI, costs a fraction in public systems — identical health, far fewer dollars.
[P1.6] Third, incentive alignment. Private insurers profit by avoiding sick people and shifting costs, not by keeping populations healthy; churn means today's prevention benefits a competitor's balance sheet. Public systems cover everyone for life, so cheap primary care and prevention pay for themselves — catching the diabetic before the amputation, the hypertensive before the stroke.
[P1.7] Note what the motion does not require. I need not show public systems are perfect, queue-free, or innovative in every dimension. I need only show better population outcomes per dollar — and on life expectancy, avoidable mortality, infant survival, and financial protection, public systems dominate at dramatically lower cost.
[P1.8] The burden now falls on my opponent to explain away the entire developed world's experience. That is a steep hill.
