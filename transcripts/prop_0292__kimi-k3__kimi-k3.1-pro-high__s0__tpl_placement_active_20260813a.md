[P1.1] A deepfake takes one photo and a few minutes. Undoing one takes years—if the law allows it at all. That asymmetry is why this motion wins.
[P1.2] The harm is concrete. Sexually explicit deepfakes target overwhelmingly women, are made without consent, and do lasting damage: jobs lost, harassment triggered, severe psychological harm. The victim consented to nothing, yet her face becomes raw material for content that follows her forever. Counterspeech cannot fix this; you cannot "answer" a fabricated video of your own body.
[P1.3] Existing law leaves these victims stranded. Defamation suits crawl for years and stumble over falsity and fault. Harassment statutes require patterns, not one viral upload. Copyright belongs to the photographer, not the depicted.
[P1.4] Criminal laws, where they exist, wait on prosecutors who rarely act. A right that exists on paper but cannot be invoked is decoration, not protection.
[P1.5] A private right of action fixes precisely this. It hands agency to the victim: she can sue directly, win injunctions, force takedowns, unmask anonymous uploaders through discovery, and recover statutory damages without proving impossible financial loss. It also deters—creators and hosting platforms respond to liability, not to principles. Civil enforcement scales where prosecutors never will.
[P1.6] Now the conceded cost. Yes, this narrows some speech protections. But look at what is actually restricted: fabricated sexual imagery of an identifiable, non-consenting person. Law already tolerates exactly such limits—defamation, true threats, nonconsensual intimate imagery—because some content's value is near zero while its harm is near total.
[P1.7] The freedom lost here is not political debate or satire; it is the freedom to fake someone's sexuality against her will. No democracy needs that freedom.
[P1.8] So the clash is weighing, not absolutes. On one side: real victims, real harm, and currently no remedy. On the other: the marginal interest of fabricating intimate content. The motion asks which a legal system should protect.
[P1.9] That choice is easy.
