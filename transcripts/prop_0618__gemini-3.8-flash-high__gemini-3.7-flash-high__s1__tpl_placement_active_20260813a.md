[P1.1] When public funding is tied solely to enrollment, universities are rewarded for filling seats, not fulfilling promises. Under the status quo, an institution receives the exact same taxpayer subsidy for a student who drops out after three semesters saddled with debt as it does for a student who completes their degree and enters a thriving career. That is a perverse incentive. It treats students as headcounts at the bursar's office, only to abandon them when academic support and career preparation are needed most.
[P1.2] Tying funding to demonstrated improvements in graduation rates and post-graduation earnings transforms higher education from an enrollment mill into an engine of genuine upward mobility.
[P1.3] First, it forces universities to invest in student retention and curriculum relevance. When state dollars depend on outcomes, institutions can no longer prioritize flashy recruitment marketing and administrative bloat over intensive advising, tutoring, and career pathways. They must actively ensure that the degrees they offer lead to completion and economic self-sufficiency.
[P1.4] Second, this policy protects the most vulnerable students. Today, low-income and first-generation students are too often recruited to inflate enrollment numbers, only to be left with non-dischargeable debt and no credential. Because the motion rewards *demonstrated improvements*, it does not favor elite institutions with privileged student bodies; rather, it incentivizes regional and access-oriented universities to lift their students' trajectories and provides clear rewards for closing completion gaps.
[P1.5] Finally, public funding requires public accountability. Taxpayer subsidies are an investment in human capital and economic vitality, not an unconditional institutional entitlement. When a university consistently accepts public dollars while graduating fewer than half its students into jobs that cannot repay their tuition, continuing to fund that school based purely on headcounts is negligence.
[P1.6] We must stop funding universities simply for getting students through the front door, and start funding them for helping students walk across the graduation stage into a secure future.
