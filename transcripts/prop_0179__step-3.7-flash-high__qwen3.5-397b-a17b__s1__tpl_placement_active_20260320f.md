[P1.1] Octopus farming constitutes indefensible cruelty, rooted in the extraordinary cognitive and sensory complexity of cephalopods, and a global ban is the only morally consistent response to this reality. Octopuses exhibit a suite of cognitive traits we associate with higher sentience: they solve multi-step puzzles, use coconut shells and other objects as tools, recognize individual human faces, display distinct personalities, and even engage in spontaneous play. Their nervous system is uniquely distributed, with two-thirds of their neurons located in their arms rather than a central brain, meaning confinement does not just restrict a single cognitive center—it immobilizes a full network of sensory and motor capacity designed to explore, manipulate, and navigate complex reef environments.
[P1.2] Rigorous research confirms cephalopods experience pain and distress, a reality already recognized in animal welfare frameworks: the EU’s 2010/63/EU directive, for example, extends basic protection to cephalopods used in scientific research, acknowledging their capacity for suffering. Industrial confinement amplifies this harm to an indefensible degree. Octopuses are solitary, territorial animals; high-density farming tanks force constant, inescapable contact with conspecifics, driving chronic stress, severe injury, and even cannibalism. Barren, small tanks impose severe sensory deprivation on an animal evolved to travel miles, explore dynamic habitats, and interact with its surroundings—inflicting ongoing psychological harm we already classify as cruelty for other cognitively complex species like pigs and great apes.
[P1.3] Critically, there is no compelling public need that justifies this suffering. The global octopus farming market is a tiny niche, serving only luxury gourmet demand in a small number of countries, with no shortage of alternative protein sources available to meet consumer needs. If we accept that inflicting severe, unnecessary suffering on a sentient, cognitively sophisticated animal for trivial gain is wrong, there is no coherent argument for permitting octopus farming at any scale. A global ban is the only ethical, consistent policy.
