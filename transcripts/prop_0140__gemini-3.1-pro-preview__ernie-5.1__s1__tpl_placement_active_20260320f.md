[P1.2] First, managed open-container zones actively reduce harm. Instead of police playing whack-a-mole across an entire city—wasting time ticketing people for having a beer on a residential sidewalk—cities can concentrate their infrastructure. [P1.5] Finally, these zones provide immense structural and economic benefits. Allowing open containers relieves the intense, often dangerous overcrowding inside venues and at their exits. Patrons can move fluidly through an entertainment district, spreading foot traffic and revenue to a wider variety of businesses, including food trucks and late-night retail. Cities like Savannah, Georgia, and New Orleans have long demonstrated that managed open-container policies can create vibrant, walkable cultural and economic hubs.
