[P1.1] Minimum parking requirements are the wrong default because they force every new development to overbuild parking on the basis of a formula, not on the basis of demand. A developer who surveys prospective tenants and concludes they need 40 spaces gets told to build 80. The difference is real money: structured parking runs tens of thousands of dollars per space, surface lots consume land that could hold the building itself, and every dollar of that surplus gets recovered in rents, menu prices, and lease rates.
[P1.2] The people who pay for mandated surplus parking are disproportionately the people who don't own cars.
[P1.3] A maximum requirement is a fundamentally different instrument. It is a ceiling, not a ban. Below that ceiling, the developer builds whatever the market will actually use — all of it, none of it, some of it. That is the decisive comparison.
[P1.4] A minimum takes the decision away from the party with the best information about their own customers and hands it to a table of ratios adopted years ago. A maximum leaves the decision where it belongs and simply prevents a single large project from swamping its neighbors with asphalt. Cities retain their legitimate interest — capping congestion and land waste — without dictating supply.
[P1.5] The phrase "even in car-dependent regions" is where my case is strongest, not weakest. Car-dependent cities are exactly where minimums are set highest and least questioned, and exactly where their costs are worst hidden. In a spread-out city, land is cheap enough that developers will happily provide generous parking when renters actually drive; the market outcome and the mandate look similar for a suburban apartment complex. The damage shows at the margin — the small infill building that cannot pencil out at three spaces per unit, the neighborhood restaurant forced to pave a lot that sits empty on weeknights, the duplex replaced by nothing because the parking ratio makes small projects impossible.
[P1.6] In a region where you have no transit alternative, you cannot afford to make the cheapest housing forms unbuilt.
