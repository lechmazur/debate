[P1.1] Management has always held the right to evaluate what workers produce and how a job is done. Mandatory always-on AI productivity companions cross a qualitatively different line: they move from assessing work to hijacking the worker’s moment-to-moment cognition. When an algorithm continuously watches a screen, analyzes vocal tone, tracks micro-pauses, and injects real-time nudges throughout the shift, the employer is no longer just buying labor; they are claiming ownership over the worker’s unmediated attention and nervous system.
[P1.2] Consider what this looks like in practice. A support representative receives flashing warnings to speak faster and sound more cheerful mid-sentence; a claims adjuster or coder gets prompted by a sidebar coach the moment they pause for ninety seconds to think through a complex problem. This relentless feedback loop induces chronic hyper-vigilance. Humans are not machines designed for uninterrupted algorithmic pacing; we require mental breathing room, self-directed rhythm, and quiet reflection to function without burning out.
[P1.3] Worse, continuous prompting degrades the quality of work itself. By penalizing the pauses where deep analysis happens and steering employees toward standardized micro-behaviors, always-on companions reduce skilled professionals into anxious executors of an algorithm’s script.
[P1.4] Prohibiting employers from requiring these tools is a precise, proportionate intervention. It does not ban AI assistants that workers voluntarily invoke when they want help, nor does it stop managers from setting output targets or reviewing finished work. It simply establishes a necessary floor under workplace dignity, much like laws governing physical safety or mandatory rest breaks. Because bargaining power between a worker and an enterprise is profoundly unequal, leaving this to employer discretion guarantees that invasive, real-time surveillance becomes the non-negotiable price of keeping a paycheck.
[P1.5] Labor law must draw a bright line: employers may manage our labor, but they cannot mandate a digital tether on our minds.
