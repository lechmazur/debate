Entertainment is not a utility delivering identical gallons of water or kilowatt-hours; it is an ecosystem of wildly divergent creative formats, distribution models, and contractual risk allocations. Imposing a rigid, government-mandated standard will homogenize creative output, destroy flexible financing, and ultimately harm the independent artists it promises to help.
[C1.2] First, standardization is economically incoherent across incompatible models. A 30-second music play on a pro-rata subscription platform, a six-hour prestige miniseries licensed upfront by an SVOD service, and a fifteen-minute ad-supported video operate on completely different commercial logic. Music pools revenue across total plays; television often relies on guaranteed upfront buyouts that insulate artists from viewership risk; ad-supported video depends on dynamic impression values. Forcing these distinct architectures into a standardized ledger creates a misleading metric that distorts real value and destroys the bespoke contracts that protect creators from catastrophic downside.
[C1.3] Second, mandatory standardized metrics actively threaten artistic diversity. Today, platforms can cross-subsidize risky, niche, and prestige content because value is measured holistically through subscriber retention and brand prestige, not just raw volume. When every creator is forced onto a standardized scoreboard, platforms face overwhelming pressure to optimize exclusively for mass-appeal, algorithmic engagement. Long-tail indie films, regional music, and experimental art lose their funding when raw, standardized stream counts become the sole benchmark of worth.
[C1.4] Third, standardization creates a blueprint for fraud and rigidity. When payout calculations and engagement metrics are standardized by regulatory fiat, bot farms and bad actors gain an open playbook to game the system, siphoning money from legitimate creators. Organic transparency is already developing through market competition; a top-down mandate freezes yesterday’s metrics into law and stifles innovation.
[C1.5] True creator empowerment requires flexible valuation, not a clumsy regulatory mold that turns artistic variety into a commodified numbers game.
