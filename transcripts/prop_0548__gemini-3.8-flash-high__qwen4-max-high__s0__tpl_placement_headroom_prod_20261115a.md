[P1.1] Violent crime is an immediate, localized crisis, not an abstract calculation about distant courtroom penalties. [P1.2] First, human behavior responds to the certainty and immediacy of apprehension, not the deferred severity of punishment. An individual carrying an illegal weapon into a dispute does not calculate whether a conviction carries eight years instead of five; they discount the distant future heavily. What deters them is the visible certainty of immediate detection. A disciplined, accountable police presence on a high-risk block alters the immediate calculus, making the risk of carrying or firing a weapon palpable right now.
[P1.3] Second, violent crime is hyper-concentrated. Increasing sentence lengths, by contrast, is purely reactive: it requires a victim to suffer and a crime to occur before the mechanism even engages.
[P1.4] Third, stacking years onto repeat offenders runs directly into diminishing returns and the age-crime curve. Most violent crime is committed by young people who naturally age out of high-risk behavior. [P1.5] Crucially, tight accountability prevents heavy-handed abuses, ensuring that focused policing builds civic trust rather than community resentment. By prioritizing immediate deterrence over delayed retribution, hot spot policing prevents violence at the source.
