[P1.1] The motion’s core premise is clear: when parents and qualified clinicians agree that gender-affirming puberty blockers or hormone therapy are appropriate for a minor with gender dysphoria, denying that care inflicts preventable, catastrophic harm. The dual requirement of parental and clinical approval is a robust, targeted safeguard that eliminates any risk of reckless or inappropriate intervention, while leaving life-saving care accessible to youth who need it.
[P1.2] Gender dysphoria in minors is a serious, well-documented medical condition, not a transient phase. Untreated, it carries drastically elevated rates of depression, anxiety, and suicide attempt—up to 4 times higher than cisgender peers, per longstanding public health data. Puberty is the single greatest driver of this distress: irreversible secondary sex characteristics that do not align with a youth’s gender identity cause profound, lifelong psychological suffering if they are forced to endure them.
[P1.3] Gender-affirming care is endorsed as safe, effective, and medically necessary by every major relevant medical body, including the American Academy of Pediatrics, Endocrine Society, and World Professional Association for Transgender Health. Puberty blockers pause development to give youth time to confirm their gender identity without the distress of unwanted physical changes, while hormone therapy aligns their physical development with their identity. Studies show this care drastically reduces rates of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation in trans youth.
[P1.4] The alternative to early, timely care is far more harmful: forced puberty causes permanent physical changes—facial hair, deepened voice, bone structure—that are impossible to fully reverse even with extensive, costly surgery later in life. For a trans girl forced through male puberty, these changes will mark her body as incongruent with her identity for the rest of her life, even if she pursues transition later. Regret rates for gender-affirming care in youth are less than 1%, a fraction of the harm caused by denial.

[C1.1] The motion asks us to treat parental and clinical agreement as a magic shield that transforms an invasive medical intervention into an obviously safe one. It does not. When the treatment involves halting a minor’s puberty or flooding their body with opposite-sex hormones, agreement alone cannot settle the question of whether the benefits outweigh the permanent costs.
[C1.2] Start with the physical reality. Puberty blockers are not a harmless “pause button.” Administered during the years when bones mineralize and the brain undergoes critical development, they carry documented risks to bone density and potentially to cognitive and sexual function. Cross-sex hormones are more drastic still: they routinely render minors permanently sterile and alter cardiovascular risk profiles. These are not incidental side effects.
[C1.3] They are the elimination of fundamental biological capacities, imposed before a young person can possibly understand what they are surrendering.
[C1.4] The evidentiary foundation is far weaker than PRO pretends. The interventions at issue lack robust long-term randomized trials. Systematic reviews of the pediatric literature consistently grade the evidence for mental-health outcomes as low or very low certainty. We are not debating a well-established treatment with minor risks; we are debating interventions whose long-term safety profile is genuinely unknown.
[C1.5] To sterilize a child or stall their development on the strength of that evidence is not cautious medicine—it is a leap of faith.
[C1.6] Nor does the diagnosis itself guarantee permanence. A substantial portion of minors presenting with gender dysphoria see their distress resolve without biomedical intervention. The motion’s framework channels children toward permanent physical changes at precisely the moment of maximum developmental flux, assuming that a momentary identity crisis must be locked in with hormones and surgeries-in-waiting.
[C1.7] Denying immediate hormonal intervention is not denying care. Supportive psychotherapy, treatment of co-occurring depression and anxiety, and watchful waiting remain the only responses that preserve every future option. Caution is not cruelty. It is the minimum owed to a minor who cannot consent.

[P2.4] Third, CON’s desistance claim relies on outdated data for pre-pubertal children with non-persistent gender expression, not the adolescents with formally diagnosed, persistent gender dysphoria covered by this motion. There is no “momentary identity crisis” being locked in here—only youth who have already endured months or years of severe distress, and who have passed multiple clinical and parental gatekeeping steps to access care.
