[P1.1] Imagine pulling off a desolate highway on an empty tank, only to find the nearest fuel pump refuses your car because you bought the wrong brand. In traditional fueling, that would be unthinkable. In electric vehicle infrastructure, letting proprietary systems dominate threatens to make it the norm.
[P1.2] Public charging is not a luxury consumer-tech ecosystem; it is a critical public utility essential to decarbonizing transportation. Mandating open interoperability standards—both for physical connectors and communication protocols—is vital for three clear reasons.
[P1.3] First, interoperability eliminates artificial scarcity and accelerates mass EV adoption. Range anxiety is fundamentally charging anxiety. When proprietary walled gardens carve public roads into corporate fiefdoms, drivers face fragmented networks and unpredictable access. A single, interoperable standard guarantees that any driver can plug into any public charger, creating the ubiquitous reliability required for consumers to leave fossil fuels behind.
[P1.4] Second, open standards prevent massive capital waste. High-voltage grid connections, physical real estate, and municipal permitting are scarce public goods. Allowing proprietary networks leads to wasteful duplication: two adjacent, half-empty charging stations serving exclusive customer bases rather than a single, high-utilization public hub. Interoperability maximizes the return on every megawatt of capacity and every dollar of public subsidy.
[P1.5] Third, open standards foster genuine market competition. When an operator relies on exclusive access to lock in drivers, it has little incentive to compete on price, maintenance, or reliability. Open standards shift competition to where it benefits the public: network uptime, charging speed, and fair pricing. Just as standardized rail gauges and universal fuel nozzles enabled the expansion of modern transport, open protocols ensure that charging networks compete on service quality rather than proprietary capture.
[P1.6] Proprietary moats serve corporate monopolies, not the energy transition. Governments must mandate open interoperability to ensure our public infrastructure serves every driver, everywhere.
