[P1.4] The company captures the value of pooled readiness; the worker absorbs the dead time. Paying only for active task minutes is like a warehouse paying a forklift operator only while a pallet is moving, ignoring the hours they are required to sit at the controls waiting for orders.

It stops platforms from rebranding labor exploitation as flexibility and forces them to align worker supply with genuine demand. Excluding wait time does not protect innovation; it subsidizes inefficient matching algorithms by pushing effective hourly pay below the legal minimum.

[C1.2] Mandating minimum-wage compliance for idle app time ignores the undeniable economic reality of multi-apping. If platforms must pay for logged-in hours, workers will simply open Uber, Lyft, and DoorDash simultaneously, park in a busy zone, and collect multiple minimum wages for the exact same hour of unperformed labor. No business model can sustain paying overlapping wages for idle time. To prevent this massive moral hazard, platforms would be forced to implement rigid, pre-scheduled shifts and strictly cap the number of online workers.
[C1.3] This regulatory shift destroys the flexibility that makes gig work uniquely valuable. Millions of drivers rely on this model precisely because they can log on for a spare hour between classes or after a primary job, without needing manager approval. If platforms are forced to pay for wait time, they will exclusively onboard workers willing to commit to fixed, managed shifts, effectively locking out students, parents, and part-timers who need true on-demand access to earn income.
[C1.4] Furthermore, the PRO side's claim that workers are "trapped" by algorithmic penalties is highly exaggerated. Acceptance rate drops usually result in fewer priority perks, not deactivation, and workers can instantly toggle offline without penalty. The task-only model accurately compensates actual labor performed. Forcing platforms to subsidize idle time doesn't protect workers; it transforms a flexible, open marketplace into a rigid, exclusive traditional job, pricing out the very people the gig economy was built to serve.
