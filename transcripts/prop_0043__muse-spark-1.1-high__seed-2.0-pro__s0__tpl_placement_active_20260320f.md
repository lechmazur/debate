[P1.1] Democracies don't collapse because citizens can't name a trustworthy outlet. They collapse because citizens can't think.
[P1.2] Media literacy tells you whom to trust. Logic, probability, and statistics teach you how to think, regardless of whom you encounter. That distinction is everything for democratic resilience.
[P1.3] First, durability. Media-literacy curricula chase a moving target: last decade it was Photoshop, then Twitter bots, now AI video. Any list of "red flags" or "trusted sources" obsolesces in a year and can be gamed. Formal reasoning does not obsolesce.
[P1.4] If a seven-year-old learns that "all A are B" does not imply "all B are A," that fallacy stays broken for life, whether it appears on a pamphlet or a TikTok. If a ten-year-old learns what a random sample is, she will not be fooled by "I asked 100 people in my town."
[P1.5] Second, depth. The most dangerous anti-democratic claims are not cheap fakes. They are bad arguments built from real data: crime anecdotes inflated into trends, correlation presented as cause, absolute risks confused with relative risks. Spotting a fake image does nothing against that.
[P1.6] Knowing base rates, selection bias, conditional probability, and the difference between necessary and sufficient conditions does.
[P1.7] Third, independence. Media literacy inevitably becomes "trust these institutions, distrust those." In a polarized democracy, that instruction itself becomes partisan and triggers reactance. It outsources judgment to authorities whose authority is precisely what's contested.
[P1.8] Teaching inference rules does not tell you what conclusion to reach; it gives you tools to evaluate any conclusion, including your own side's. That builds the epistemic humility that lets democracies tolerate disagreement without fracturing.
[P1.9] Finally, democratic decisions are probabilistic trade-offs — budgets, pandemics, climate, war. A citizen who can weigh evidence, uncertainty, and expected value is resilient. One who only knows which logo to trust is dependent.
[P1.10] Teach children that truth is not a brand, but a method.
