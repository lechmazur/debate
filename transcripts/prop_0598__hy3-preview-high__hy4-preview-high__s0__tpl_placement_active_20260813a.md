[P1.1] Gulf and North African states face irregular migration not because they have too many workers, but because they have mismatched legal pathways and persistent labor demand. Prioritizing legal labor migration corridors directly confronts that mismatch, while mass deportations merely treat a symptom and inflame the underlying pressure. The path to victory is clear: judge each approach by whether it durably shrinks irregular flows while keeping economies functioning and respecting basic rights.
[P1.2] Legal corridors turn an uncontrolled flow into a managed asset. When a Gulf construction sector or a North African agricultural export zone needs labor, a formal channel with visas, contracts, and protections pulls workers out of smugglers' networks and into tax rolls and inspection systems. A pragmatic corridor—say, a monitored recruitment linkage between Egypt or Morocco and Gulf employers—replaces the chaos of irregular crossings with verifiable supply matched to real jobs. North African states gain steadier remittances and fewer border emergencies when their citizens move through law rather than on rickety boats.
[P1.3] Mass deportations, by contrast, drain state budgets and deepen instability. Repeated Gulf expulsion campaigns have shown that removed workers are often replaced by new irregular entrants drawn by the same unmet demand, while deportees face destitution that fuels further displacement. In practice, mass deportations create revolving-door dynamics: a worker expelled from one Gulf state simply re-enters another or returns later, wasting enforcement resources. The human and diplomatic costs are high, and the deterrent effect is weak because the economic gravity remains.
[P1.4] Deportation-first policies also erode rights protections and poison the cross-border trust needed to fight trafficking.
[P1.5] The decisive comparison is durability. Legal corridors reduce irregularity at its source by satisfying labor needs under law; deportation prioritization spends political capital to move people without closing the gap. For Gulf and North African states, the smarter investment is a corridor-first strategy that aligns migration with markets, protects workers, and builds regional stability.
