[P1.1] Low Earth orbit is a finite natural resource currently facing an impending tragedy of the commons. As thousands of commercial satellites launch each year, private operators reap immediate commercial profits while externalizing the catastrophic risk of orbital debris onto the global public. Existing regulatory guidelines requiring post-mission disposal are functionally toothless without financial leverage. When a spacecraft nears its end of life, operators face an economic incentive to squeeze out marginal revenue until the craft suffers system failure, stranding an uncontrolled, multi-ton hazard in high-velocity orbit.
[P1.2] Requiring deorbit bonds solves this market failure at its root through three decisive mechanisms.
[P1.3] First, deorbit bonds align economic incentives with orbital sustainability. Mirroring the proven model of environmental reclamation bonds in mining and energy extraction, deorbit bonds require operators to internalize the full lifecycle cost of their assets upfront. Because the bond is fully refunded upon successful disposal, it is not a punitive tax on innovation.
[P1.4] Instead, it creates a powerful financial imperative to engineer redundant deorbit hardware, maintain strict propellant reserves, and retire spacecraft while propulsion and communication systems are still active and reliable.
[P1.5] Second, the policy establishes absolute accountability. When an operator cuts corners, deploys unreliable bus designs, or gambles with end-of-life fuel margins, forfeited bonds ensure they pay for the risk they inflict on the orbital environment. These forfeited funds directly finance public tracking infrastructure and active debris removal missions, ensuring commercial neglect is remediated at the operator’s expense rather than the taxpayer’s.
[P1.6] Commercial spaceflight cannot survive in an orbital minefield of its own making. Without upfront financial guarantees, the exponential growth of mega-constellations will inevitably trigger cascading collisions that could render critical orbits unusable for generations. Requiring deorbit bonds ensures that those who profit from the orbital commons preserve it.

[C1.1] PRO's case stands on a single analogy: the "proven model" of mining reclamation bonds [P1.3]. That analogy collapses on inspection, and it takes the whole motion down with it.
[C1.2] First, the bond punishes outcomes, not conduct. A mine that leaves its pit unreclaimed does so by choice; reclamation is a deterministic, controllable task. A satellite usually fails to deorbit because of anomaly — a dead battery, a failed thruster, a solar storm, a collision with debris someone else abandoned. Forfeiture on outcome punishes bad luck, not negligence.
[C1.3] Worse, once a spacecraft is crippled and the bond already lost, every incentive to fund further mitigation vanishes. And desperate operators may attempt marginal deorbit burns with tumbling, dying craft to save their money — risking breakups that seed more debris than a quiet passivation ever would.
[C1.4] Second, the money cannot fix the harm. Reclamation bonds work because cash can actually refill the pit. Cash cannot retrieve a two-ton husk tumbling at 550 kilometers. Removing one specific failed satellite is experimental and costs far more than any plausible bond; forfeited funds spent on "tracking infrastructure" [P1.5] merely watch the hazard circle for centuries.
[C1.5] PRO's "absolute accountability" is a receipt, not a remedy.
[C1.6] Third, calibration is a trap. Set the bond low and it becomes a pay-to-pollute fee, absorbed into constellation business models as a routine cost. Set it high enough to bite and you freeze billions in escrow across thousand-satellite fleets — crushing startups, universities, and emerging spacefaring nations while the largest incumbents absorb it as a moat.
[C1.7] Fourth, the regime leaks. "Governments" will not move in lockstep; operators will simply launch from bond-free jurisdictions, exporting oversight along with the launches.
[C1.8] Better instruments already exist and target engineering rather than financial proxies: disposal timelines as license conditions, passivation and propellant-reserve mandates, demonstrated disposal reliability before launch approval. PRO wants to price the risk. We should engineer it away. A forfeited bond has never brought a satellite down.
