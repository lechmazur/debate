[P1.1] The word "net-zero" was supposed to mean a company stops heating the planet. In practice, it has too often come to mean a company keeps emitting at home and buys a stack of cheap certificates abroad to paper over the gap. The motion fixes that by forcing the truth back into the term: cut your own emissions first, and let offsets cover only a small remainder.
[P1.2] Here is the core argument. Offsets and real reductions are not equivalent, even when the tonnage matches on paper. A ton you never emit is certain and permanent. A ton you "offset" depends on a forest that may burn, a project that may have happened anyway, and an accounting baseline a seller chose for you.
[P1.3] Multiple investigations into voluntary carbon markets have found that large fractions of credits—particularly avoided-deforestation credits—delivered little or no real climate benefit. When you let companies treat shaky paper tons as equal to hard physical reductions, you don't get neutrality. You get the appearance of neutrality and the continuation of the harm.
[P1.4] Second, unlimited offsetting creates a delay machine. If a firm can hit "net-zero" by writing a check this year, it has every reason to postpone the expensive, structural work—electrifying fleets, decarbonizing suppliers, redesigning products—that actually bends the curve. Cheap offsets are a sedative for exactly the investment we most need. A legal cap removes the escape hatch and channels capital into in-value-chain change that compounds over decades.
[P1.5] Third, this is a consumer-protection and market-integrity issue. When every laggard can buy the same green label as a genuine decarbonizer, the label stops meaning anything, and honest firms get punished for doing the hard thing. A cap restores the signal.
[P1.6] So the question before us is simple. Should "net-zero" describe a company that actually stopped polluting, or one that merely bought permission to keep going? The motion insists on the former. I'll defend that the offsets belong at the margin—not as the main event.
