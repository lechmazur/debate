[P1.1] Too many overdose deaths are not unpreventable tragedies but lonely ones. A person uses alone in a stairwell, a park bathroom, or an abandoned building, stops breathing, and nobody is there to intervene. A supervised consumption site changes that single decisive fact: consumption happens in front of trained staff with oxygen and naloxone who can act within seconds. That is why facilities of this kind have operated for years without a death occurring inside.
[P1.2] Street overdose kills because help arrives too late or never. Inside a regulated room, breathing problems are spotted immediately, staff intervene, and emergency services are called when needed. People who would otherwise use hurriedly and alone, hiding from police, get time, clean equipment, and monitoring. Fewer infections, fewer fatal overdoses, and more survivors who can later enter treatment.
[P1.3] The same logic reduces public disorder rather than increasing it. Open drug use concentrates where there is no alternative: doorways, plazas, transit stations, with discarded syringes left behind. A regulated indoor site pulls that activity off the street under clear rules — no dealing, no sharing, no loitering outside, fixed hours, neighborhood agreements, cooperation with police and health services. Residents see less public injecting, fewer needles in playgrounds, and less frantic behavior driven by fear of arrest.
[P1.4] Connection to care happens precisely because nurses and social workers meet people where they are instead of waiting for a crisis.
[P1.5] This approach fits European and Latin American cities especially well. Many European cities already run needle exchange and low-threshold health services; adding supervision completes that chain. Many Latin American cities face overcrowded prisons, overstretched police, and poor neighborhoods that bear the worst of street use; a health facility offers relief without requiring a national overhaul. Permitting regulated sites is modest, local, and reversible — a practical way to save lives and reclaim public space at once.
