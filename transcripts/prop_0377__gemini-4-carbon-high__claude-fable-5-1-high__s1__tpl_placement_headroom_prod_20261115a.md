[P1.1] A top surgeon or senior engineer earns an enviable annual salary, yet their advantage has natural limits. Their pay reflects scarce skills in an active market, is taxed heavily every year, and stops the day they stop working. Crucially, human talent cannot be placed in a trust fund: even after enjoying great schools and debt-free tuition, a high earner’s children must still study, pass exams, and prove themselves to stay ahead. Dynastic wealth breaks that bargain completely.
[P1.2] Vast inherited fortunes compound passively across decades, severing economic power from any living person’s effort, risk, or competence.
[P1.3] That compounding makes dynastic wealth a far deeper threat to equality of opportunity than most high earners’ salaries. Dynastic privilege does not wait until a parent’s funeral to rig the race. Backed by family trusts, lifetime transfers, and the certainty of an immense fortune, heirs enter adulthood with risk-free seed capital, prime real estate, elite donor access, and political clout that salaried professionals cannot match. High earners buy better preparation for the competition; dynastic wealth lets heirs buy out the competition altogether, monopolizing scarce assets and insulating mediocre insiders from failure.
[P1.4] While labor income naturally regresses toward the mean across generations, lightly taxed fortunes snowball into a hereditary caste.
[P1.5] Taxing large inheritances much more aggressively attacks this bottleneck directly. High exemptions protect ordinary family homes and modest nest eggs, while closing trust and lifetime-gift loopholes ensures multi-million-dollar dynasties actually pay. Economically and morally, this beats squeezing earned income further. Steep taxes on salaries penalize the very climbers using talent and hard work to rise, whereas taxing an heir’s unearned windfall preserves founders’ incentives to build and innovate.
[P1.6] Channeling revenue from dynastic windfalls into early education and broad opportunity ensures success depends on what people create, not the fortune they were born into.
