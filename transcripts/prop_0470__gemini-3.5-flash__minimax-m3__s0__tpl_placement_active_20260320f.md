[P1.1] Democracy cannot function without a shared baseline of empirical reality. [P1.2] First, deepfakes pose an unprecedented threat to the integrity of elections due to the problem of irreversibility. If a highly convincing video of a candidate taking a bribe or initiating a military crisis is released forty-eight hours before an election, the damage is permanent. By the time the truth emerges, the election is over, and democratic legitimacy is shattered.
[P1.3] Second, the proliferation of deepfakes triggers the "liar's dividend." [P1.4] We must accept some cost to satire and anonymous speech because the alternative is the death of political truth. [P1.5] A targeted ban on hyper-realistic political deepfakes does not silence dissent; it protects the digital ecosystem from weaponized forgery. To preserve the freedom of our speech, we must first protect the reality of our vote.
