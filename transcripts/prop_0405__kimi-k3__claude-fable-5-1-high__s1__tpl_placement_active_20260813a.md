[P1.1] A seventy-year-old steps onto a sidewalk — a space reserved by law for her — and a shared scooter at fifteen miles an hour breaks her hip. The rider is an uninsured tourist, gone by Tuesday. The company that placed the scooter there, priced the ride by the minute, and could have geofenced the block points to paragraph fourteen of its terms of service. That allocation of responsibility is what this motion fixes.
[P1.2] The principle is old: responsibility should track control, foreseeability, and profit. On all three, the company stands at the center of the harm.
[P1.3] Control. Operators hold the most powerful safety lever ever built into a vehicle: software. They throttle scooters in parks, lock them in no-parking zones, slow entire fleets with a server update. If a company can remotely cap a scooter at eight miles per hour for a street festival, it can do it in a restricted zone.
[P1.4] When the cheapest accident-preventer is the company rather than the pedestrian, the law should place the cost there — basic tort logic.
[P1.5] Foreseeability. Companies stage fleets on sidewalks, market to tourists unfamiliar with local rules, and profit per minute ridden — an incentive structure rewarding fast, rule-indifferent riding. Sidewalk injuries aren't aberrations; they're a predictable output of the business model.
[P1.6] And the status quo's real loser: the injured pedestrian, who must chase an often unidentifiable, judgment-proof rider while the platform that monetized the ride disclaims everything. "More responsibility" doesn't mean riders bear none. It means the enterprise that profits and controls the risk finally shares it — through duties in restricted zones, mandatory insurance, and liability when geofencing existed but went unused.
[P1.7] CON will preach personal responsibility. But holding riders responsible never required holding companies blameless. We hold bars liable alongside drunk drivers precisely because the party that serves and profits is often best placed to prevent the harm. Same logic, same result.
