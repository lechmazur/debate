[P1.1] New antibiotics face a market designed to make them fail. A drug that works against resistant infections must be used sparingly to preserve it, tested against infections that are still uncommon, and sold in competition with cheap generics that doctors reach for first. The better stewardship works, the less revenue the innovator earns, and predictably firms have left the field while resistance spreads.
[P1.2] Advance market commitments fix that broken signal without forcing anyone to buy a bad drug. A country announces in advance: develop a drug meeting a defined target — activity against priority resistant pathogens, suitable safety, reliable supply — and we will commit to purchase or subscription payments at a pre-agreed reward. Payment follows success. That shifts risk from impossible sales forecasts to a clear public prize for solving a public problem.
[P1.3] The objection to high unit prices mistakes the pill for the product. What health systems actually need is availability on the worst day: a stocked option when first-line drugs fail, a child in intensive care with sepsis, a transplant patient with no working prophylaxis, a hospital outbreak. Those courses will be few by design. A high price per course multiplied by deliberately low, tightly controlled use is a modest total bill compared with prolonged ICU stays, failed surgeries, and lives lost — and compared with the cost of discovering too late that no treatment exists.
[P1.4] High unit price can even serve stewardship. It discourages casual overuse, while the commitment can be written with access and conservation conditions: hospitals get the drug when diagnostics justify it, supply is guaranteed, and marketing that pushes volume is barred. The price is not permission to profiteer unchecked; it is the lever that lets governments demand those terms in advance.
[P1.5] Permitting this tool does not commit any country to any single deal. It simply refuses to let unit-price optics veto an insurance policy against resistant infection. When the alternative is an empty pipeline, that refusal is the expensive choice.
