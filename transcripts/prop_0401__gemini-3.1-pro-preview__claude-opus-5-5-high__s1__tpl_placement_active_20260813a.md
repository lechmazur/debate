[P1.1] European countries must immediately end golden-visa and citizenship-by-investment programs. Selling residency and passports to the global elite is not a harmless revenue stream; it is a systemic threat to European security, local economies, and the fundamental integrity of citizenship.
[P1.2] First, these programs act as Trojan horses for corruption and money laundering. Golden visas offer a legal backdoor into the European Union for oligarchs, kleptocrats, and organized crime figures. Because the EU operates on freedom of movement, a passport or residency bought in one member state grants unchecked access to the entire Schengen zone. This is not a theoretical risk; individuals implicated in massive financial crimes and sanctions evasion have repeatedly utilized these exact schemes to shield their assets and move freely across Europe.
[P1.3] One country’s lucrative visa scheme compromises the security and financial integrity of all its neighbors.
[P1.4] Second, citizenship-by-investment fundamentally distorts local economies, particularly the housing market. The "investments" required by these programs rarely go into productive, job-creating industries. Instead, they are overwhelmingly parked in real estate. This floods cities with speculative capital, artificially driving up property values and rent prices.
[P1.5] In major European hubs, working-class and middle-class locals are aggressively priced out of their own neighborhoods to make room for empty apartments owned by absentee foreign investors. The supposed economic benefits are concentrated in the hands of a few developers and consultants, while the devastating costs of the resulting housing crises are borne by everyday citizens.
[P1.6] Finally, selling access commodifies human rights and democratic participation. It creates an inherently unjust, two-tiered immigration system where desperate refugees and highly skilled workers face years of grueling bureaucratic hurdles, while the ultra-wealthy simply buy their way to the front of the line. Citizenship and residency should be rooted in a genuine connection to a society, shared values, and meaningful contributions to the community—not treated as luxury assets available to the highest bidder. These programs erode public trust, compromise European sovereignty, and must be abolished.
