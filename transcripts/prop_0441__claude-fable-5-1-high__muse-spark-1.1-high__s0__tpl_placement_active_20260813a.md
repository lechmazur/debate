[P1.1] Cash bail asks one question about a defendant: can you pay? Risk assessment asks the question we actually care about: are you likely to hurt someone or skip court? Once you see that difference, the motion almost proves itself.
[P1.2] Money bail sorts by wealth, not danger. A well-financed defendant facing a violent charge posts bond within hours. A single mother arrested for shoplifting sits in jail for weeks because she can't raise a few hundred dollars. That jail cell isn't making anyone safer; it's holding the wrong people.
[P1.3] Meanwhile the dangerous defendant who bought his way out is under no supervision at all. Cash bail is not a public-safety tool. It's a wealth test with a safety label glued on.
[P1.4] Now look at what happens when jurisdictions actually make the switch. New Jersey effectively eliminated cash bail in 2017, moving to a validated risk tool plus tiered supervision. Its pretrial jail population fell by roughly forty percent over the following years. Violent crime did not spike; it continued the downward trend it was already on, and follow-up reviews found rearrest and court-appearance rates essentially unchanged.
[P1.5] Washington, D.C. has released the overwhelming majority of defendants without money for decades, with appearance rates around ninety percent and violent rearrests in the low single digits. Kentucky banned commercial bail in the 1970s. Illinois abolished cash bail statewide in 2023, and its jail populations dropped while crime kept falling. Every large-scale test points the same direction.
[P1.6] There's a mechanism behind these results. Pretrial detention itself is criminogenic. Studies comparing similar defendants who were detained versus released find the detained group loses jobs, housing, and custody, and reoffends more afterward. Holding low-risk people for poverty manufactures future crime; releasing them under supervision prevents it.
[P1.7] Note what the motion requires: validated tools, plus supervision. CON must show that this specific package, not some strawman of releasing everyone unconditionally, raises violent crime rates. The real-world record says the opposite: fewer people caged, and streets no less safe.
