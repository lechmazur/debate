[C1.3] A "women-only" filter does nothing to improve background checks, in-ride monitoring, or accountability — the mechanisms that actually reduce harm. [C1.4] Then there's the cost, which PRO calls "bounded" friction. Women are a minority of ride-hail drivers — often well under a third of the pool in most markets. A women-only filter means every woman requesting one competes for a dramatically smaller supply of cars. In dense urban centers at off-peak hours, maybe that's tolerable.
[C1.5] But ride-hail's safety problem is most acute late at night, in low-density neighborhoods, in areas where driver supply is already thin — exactly the conditions under which a women-only match becomes a long wait or a no-show. The women most at risk get the worst version of the product.
[C1.6] Meanwhile, universal improvements — stricter background checks, in-ride audio, real-time deviation alerts, permanent bans for offenders — protect every rider without carving up the driver pool. PRO frames the alternative as a "distrusted baseline," but the real alternative is investing in safety measures that scale to everyone rather than a segregation workaround that helps some women some of the time and signals to the rest that the standard service isn't for them.
