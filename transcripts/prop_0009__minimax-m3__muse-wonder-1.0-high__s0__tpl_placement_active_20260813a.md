The proposition erases those distinctions and treats a calendar count alone as proof of torture.
[C1.2] That stretches torture beyond recognition. Separating an inmate because he has just assaulted staff, to prevent another killing, or to protect a vulnerable prisoner aims at safety. If officials use isolation to brutalize, punish that abuse and change the conditions — do not redefine protective separation as torture.

[C2.2] [P2.3] also strips purpose from torture. Its legal core is not suffering alone, but deliberate infliction of severe suffering for a prohibited end — punishment, coercion, intimidation, or discrimination. Separating a prisoner who stabbed a cellmate yesterday, under review for safe return, is not purpose-laundering. Purpose distinguishes surgery from assault and quarantine from punishment.
[C2.3] Foreseeable hardship may require reform; it does not convert intent to protect into intent to torture.
[C2.4] The alternatives in [P2.4] face a dilemma. [C2.5] Matched comparisons in [P2.5] do not cure this. [C2.6] Finally, [P2.6] mislabels strict control as the status quo.
