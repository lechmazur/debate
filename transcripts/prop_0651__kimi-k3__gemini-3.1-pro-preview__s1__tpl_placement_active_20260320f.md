[P1.1] The question is not whether a common language is useful. Of course it is. The question is whether the specific way English dominates — as gatekeeper, default, and prestige marker — costs non-anglophone cultures and knowledge systems more than it returns. Three mechanisms say it does.
[P1.2] First, the toll booth. The overwhelming majority of indexed scientific literature appears in English, and global business runs on it. A researcher's career, a firm's reach, a student's prospects now depend not on the quality of their ideas but on their command of one historically contingent language. Non-native scientists spend years acquiring English, pay for editing, and face reviewers who mistake awkward prose for weak thinking.
[P1.3] Knowledge published in Japanese, Arabic, or Portuguese is treated as local; knowledge in English is treated as universal. Billions pay a tax that native speakers never notice — one that filters out talent the world needed.
[P1.4] Second, erosion of knowledge systems. Languages are not interchangeable containers. When universities across Europe, the Gulf, and Asia shift teaching to English, their own languages lose scientific and technical registers — domain loss — and a language that cannot do chemistry gradually stops being a full language. More sharply: indigenous ecological, medical, and agricultural knowledge is encoded in specific tongues; when those languages retreat under the prestige of English, the knowledge dies with them.
[P1.5] Science itself is impoverished — different languages carve the world differently, from spatial reasoning to how evidence is marked, and a single lingua franca narrows the questions researchers think to ask.
[P1.6] Third, the asymmetry that decides this debate. The benefits of English — coordination, access — are real but substitutable: translation technology, multilingual publishing norms, and deliberate language policy could deliver most of that good at a fraction of the cost. The harms are not reversible. A dead language, a lost register, a generation of scientists filtered out of the pipeline: none of that comes back.
[P1.7] The ledger here is permanent loss weighed against temporary convenience — and convenience cannot repay it.
