[P1.1] Immigration court is currently a system where delay does the work of decision. People wait years for a hearing, then lose not because a judge found their claim weak, but because a notice went to an old address, a witness could not travel, or a detained respondent was transferred and missed the date. That is the real engine of both noncompliance and unfairness. Expanded staffing and remote hearings attack that engine directly.
[P1.2] The core case is simple: delay produces worse evidence, worse participation, and worse outcomes. A person with a genuine protection claim may have witnesses scattered across countries, records that degrade, and a child aging in limbo. When a hearing finally arrives years later, the evidence is stale and the harm is already done. More judges, clerks, and interpreters mean cases are reached while memories and documents are still usable.
[P1.3] Remote hearings mean a witness abroad or a respondent who moved for work can still appear. That is not a shortcut around due process; it is due process made possible.
[P1.4] Compliance improves for ordinary, practical reasons. Missed hearings are often not disappearances but logistical failures: long distances, unaffordable travel, inflexible jobs, childcare burdens, or detention transfers. A remote appearance removes many of those barriers. Shorter timelines also reduce the problem of notices going stale as people move.
[P1.5] When people can actually appear, they are more likely to hear the next steps, understand the legal obligation, and return.
[P1.6] The wrongful removal concern runs the wrong way. The greatest source of wrongful removal today is the in absentia order: someone is ordered removed without ever presenting their case because they could not attend. Remote hearings reduce those defaults. Expanded staffing also reduces the overload that forces judges to rush.
[P1.7] The proposal speeds up processing by adding capacity, not by subtracting safeguards. The choice is not between careful and fast; it is between a backlog that decides by default and a system that decides on the merits.

[C1.8] That looks like participation. It is a faster wrongful removal.
