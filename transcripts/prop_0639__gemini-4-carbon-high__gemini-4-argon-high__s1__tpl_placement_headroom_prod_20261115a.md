[P1.1] Advisory citizens' assemblies are too often democratic theater. Governments convene them to project responsiveness, cherry-pick convenient ideas, and quietly bury any recommendation that threatens major donors, party leadership, or short-term electoral math. Elected legislatures suffer from two permanent structural flaws: myopia driven by the next election cycle, and direct conflicts of interest when regulating politics itself. Asking career politicians to enact strict campaign finance limits, fair electoral systems, or painful long-term climate transitions means asking them to jeopardize their own power.
[P1.2] Keep assemblies strictly advisory, and the exact political pathologies they were designed to overcome retain the final veto.
[P1.3] Granting sortition-selected assemblies binding authority on specific policy questions breaks this deadlock. Unlike ordinary ballot initiatives, where voters are bombarded by partisan slogans and deceptive advertising, a citizens' assembly pairs demographic representativeness with serious deliberation. Randomly selected citizens from every walk of life receive the time, balanced briefing materials, and expert cross-examination needed to master complex trade-offs. Because assembly members face no re-election campaign, court no lobbyists, and answer to no party whip, they possess a freedom elected officials never do: the freedom to change their minds in light of evidence and choose long-term public interest over partisan survival.
[P1.4] Crucially, binding authority applies to specific policy questions, not as a blank check to replace parliament or override constitutional rights. Where legislatures are paralyzed by polarization, trapped by generational short-termism, or regulating their own electoral rules, ethics, and pay, a binding assembly is the only institution both democratically representative and structurally impartial. Binding power also transforms participation itself. Working-class and disillusioned citizens will not sacrifice weekends to serve as an ignored government focus group.
[P1.5] When their verdict becomes law, participants deliberate with sober responsibility, the public pays attention, and democracy finally acts where electoral politics stalls.
