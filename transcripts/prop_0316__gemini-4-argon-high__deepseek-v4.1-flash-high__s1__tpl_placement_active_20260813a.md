[P1.1] Dating apps are no longer passive bulletin boards; they are active matchmakers shaping who we meet, love, and build lives with. Yet millions swipe under a deliberate illusion of organic chance. Giving users a right to know when artificial intelligence ranks their profile or curates their feed is a baseline requirement of informed consent and consumer fairness.
[P1.2] First, transparency is vital because user goals and platform incentives are fundamentally misaligned. Users want to find a partner and leave; apps profit when users stay single, keep swiping, and buy subscriptions or visibility boosts. When an app uses AI to shape who gets shown to whom, it optimizes for retention and revenue, not just compatibility. An algorithm can ration promising matches to keep users hooked, or quietly suppress a profile's visibility to nudge them into buying a boost.
[P1.3] Without knowing that AI ranking is active, users mistake algorithmic throttling for personal rejection, letting platforms monetize artificially induced insecurity.
[P1.4] Second, secret algorithmic gatekeeping compromises autonomy in an intimate sphere. Unlike a movie recommendation engine, a dating algorithm alters human relationships. AI systems that assign hidden desirability scores or learn from historical swipe patterns inevitably amplify racial and conventional beauty biases. When an AI relegates a user to a low desirability tier or filters out entire demographics behind the scenes, it narrows their romantic options without consent.
[P1.5] Knowing that an automated gatekeeper is scoring and filtering the pool prevents users from internalizing algorithmic bias as personal failure and lets them walk away from manipulative platforms.
[P1.6] Finally, the right to know does not mean publishing proprietary source code or helping spammers game the system. Just as nutrition labels disclose ingredients without surrendering a secret recipe, apps can disclose when AI ranks profiles or shapes feeds without compromising security. When corporations mediate human intimacy for profit, users deserve to know when an algorithm is stacking the deck.
