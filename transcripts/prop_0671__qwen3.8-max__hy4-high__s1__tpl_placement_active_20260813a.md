[P1.1] A device-light policy in the early grades is the safer and stronger educational default because the central task of K–3 is not familiarity with screens; it is building sustained attention, decoding, number sense, and the ability to complete a task without constant redirection. Paper-based learning directly supports that task.
[P1.2] Young children learn best when the learning environment minimizes unnecessary cognitive load. A tablet is never only a worksheet. It contains animations, prompts, other apps, and the constant possibility of switching attention. A paper page gives one bounded task: read, mark, answer, show your work.
[P1.3] For a six-year-old still learning to control impulses, that simplicity is not quaint; it is instructional design.
[P1.4] Paper also supports the physical act of learning. Handwriting letters, drawing number lines, underlining a sentence, and showing work in margins are low-tech but powerful. They slow children down in useful ways, make thinking visible to teachers, and reduce the temptation to guess rapidly and move on. Early reading comprehension benefits when children can track text without pop-ups, battery alerts, or interface friction.
[P1.5] This policy is not anti-technology. The proposition explicitly preserves one-to-one tablets later. That sequence matters: first establish durable reading, writing, and attention habits; then introduce devices as tools for research, coding, accessibility, and practice under clearer rules. A seventh grader with strong literacy can use a tablet purposefully.
[P1.6] A first grader without those foundations is more likely to be managed by the device rather than learning from it.
[P1.7] The strongest case for device-light is opportunity cost. Every early hour spent navigating interfaces, recovering from distraction, or troubleshooting accounts is an hour not spent on the foundational skills that make all later learning possible. That standard protects classroom time for instruction, not interface management. Schools should adopt device-light early grades because they must prioritize the preconditions of learning before adding the complications of technology.
