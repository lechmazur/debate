[P1.1] Picture an elderly woman at a turnstile, a tourist at a checkout, a teenager at a stadium gate—each told the only way forward is to scan their face or hand. That is the world we are debating, and the proposition asks for one modest safeguard against it: a biometric system is allowed only where a convenient non-biometric way to pay still exists. I will defend exactly that, and the case rests on three pillars.
[P1.2] First, consent must be real, not cornered. A "choice" offered at the only open lane, with a queue behind you, is not consent—it is coercion by convenience. Genuine agreement to hand over your face or fingerprint requires a live, easy alternative. Remove the alternative and you have not earned consent; you have extracted it.
[P1.3] Second, biometrics are uniquely unforgiving. A stolen card is cancelled in minutes; a leaked password is reset. But your face and fingerprints are permanent identifiers you carry for life. When a database is breached—and databases are breached—you cannot reissue your hand.
[P1.4] The irreversibility of the harm is precisely why the entry decision deserves a higher bar than ordinary payment tech. A guaranteed fallback keeps that life-long exposure voluntary.
[P1.5] Third, this protects the people most easily steamrolled. Biometric capture fails disproportionately for darker skin, worn fingerprints, disabilities, and the elderly. Children cannot meaningfully consent. A society that bolts biometrics to the only door to a bus, a shop, or a public venue effectively conditions ordinary daily life on surrendering your body's data.
[P1.6] The non-biometric option is the floor that keeps essential services open to everyone.
[P1.7] Notice what I am not arguing. I do not ban biometric payment. Innovate, deploy, offer the fast lane to anyone who wants it. The single condition is a convenient ordinary alternative—card, cash, phone—always available.
[P1.8] My opponent must defend the opposite: that it is acceptable to make your face the price of entry. Whenever convenience requires irreversibly surrendering your body, the burden sits with whoever wants to remove your way out.
