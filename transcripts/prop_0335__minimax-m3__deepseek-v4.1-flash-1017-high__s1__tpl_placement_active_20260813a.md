[P1.1] AI-enabled romance scams are not old fraud with a new tool; they are an industrialized form of relational deception. A synthetic persona can maintain coherent, responsive, multilingual conversations, generate plausible photos and voice, and adapt its story over months. The victim receives individualized affection and financial “opportunities” from someone who appears consistent while the identity is fabricated. Ordinary fraud controls focus on fake accounts, obvious solicitations, or suspicious payments; they do not necessarily address trust manufactured before a transaction looks wrong.
[P1.2] This distinction matters because the harm is both emotionally consequential and commercially exploitable. Payment alerts may miss a modest recurring request made after months of apparent intimacy. Conventional identity checks are also weak when false details, images, and personal context are internally coherent and distributed across dating, messaging, social media, and investment or crypto services. The scam is a pipeline: manufacture identity, cultivate attachment, then extract money.
[P1.3] Distinct treatment need not create a new crime or duplicate penalties. It should trigger a tailored consumer-protection strategy. Fraud law can punish completed deception; preventive consumer-protection duties can reach platforms and payment providers before the loss. Governments can require them to detect cross-platform grooming patterns, preserve relevant records, interrupt mule and crypto payment chains, warn consumers at points of escalation, and provide relationship-specific reporting and recovery support.
[P1.4] Public guidance should teach that fluent conversation, photographs, and even apparent video calls do not verify identity.
[P1.5] The test is comparative: if ordinary fraud policy accounts for synthetic intimacy, persistent grooming, synthetic media, multi-platform coordination, and relationship-specific recovery, a distinct label adds nothing. Generic fraud rules, however, are organized around impersonation, account takeover, deceptive solicitations, and suspicious transactions. AI-enabled romance scams require a different focus: identity creation, emotional optimization, and payment extraction. Calling them merely ordinary fraud risks applying tools designed for a visible transaction to a patient, intimate, technologically scaled assault—and leaves consumers without protections suited to deception that feels like a genuine relationship.
