Transforming an unelected room of citizens into a sovereign body with binding policy power overturns the foundational prerequisite of democracy: accountability.
[C1.2] In representative democracy, power is leased, never granted outright. When elected officials pass destructive regulations or unfair rules, the public can vote them out. A sortition assembly dissolves the moment its vote is cast. If a few dozen randomly selected individuals impose a binding policy that harms the economy, infringes rights, or defies public consensus, who answers for it?
[C1.3] Nobody. The vast majority of citizens cannot recall them, vote them out, or contest their judgment at the ballot box. Power without accountability is not an upgrade to democracy; it is an oligarchy chosen by lottery.
[C1.4] Furthermore, PRO’s reliance on Ireland disproves their own case. Ireland's assemblies on marriage equality and abortion succeeded precisely because they were advisory. They clarified complex issues, but the final, binding authority was exercised by the entire electorate through national referendums. Replacing universal public votes with the binding decree of a tiny, unelected sample does not empower the people—it disenfranchises the millions of citizens who never sat in that room.
[C1.5] Finally, binding power invites unaccountable institutional capture. Advisory assemblies allow bad briefings or biased witness pools to be filtered downstream. But if an assembly's vote immediately binds the nation, immense power shifts to the unelected staff who control the agenda: selecting the "expert" panels, framing the briefing materials, and drafting the ballot choices.
[C1.6] Policy should remain accountable to the entire public, not handed over to an unaccountable room chosen by chance.
