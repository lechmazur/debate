[P1.1] A country emerging from civil war does not need more rival martyrologies; it needs a common civic grammar for memory. Public memorials should teach one lesson everywhere: every resident, whatever their ethnicity, had an equal right to live in peace, and the violence was a betrayal of that shared civic order. The alternative—state-backed, fully separate ethnic narratives—does not merely preserve diversity. It freezes each community’s worst losses into a permanent claim of exclusive innocence and exclusive guilt.
[P1.2] This is dangerous because public memory is not private therapy. A shared square, a national day, a school curriculum tell citizens whom to trust and whom to fear. If Serbs, Croats, and Bosniaks each get their own sanctioned version in which enemies authored all suffering, the state has built an ethnic scoreboard into its institutions. In Northern Ireland, the same logic is visible in rival murals and commemorations, which often keep the Troubles alive as two irreconcilable victim-calendars; a civic memorial to all victims does not flatten responsibility.
[P1.3] It insists the dead of every community are equally mournable and that no paramilitary fought for the community it claimed to defend.
[P1.4] A shared civic account can name specific crimes—Srebrenica, Bloody Sunday, militia or state murders—without turning remembrance into tribal indictment. It condemns the act, not the ethnicity. That distinction separates accountability from revenge. Those crimes are not traded against a rival tally; they are judged by one standard.
[P1.5] When memorials mark all victims as full members of the same political community, they deny spoilers their best fuel: the belief that future violence is owed to the dead.
[P1.6] The choice is not memory versus amnesia. It is tribal memory versus civic memory. After civil conflict, the scarce public good is not each group’s separate catharsis but trust across groups. Only a shared civic account converts remembrance from a weapon into a shared rule: no citizen is disposable, and no community’s suffering licenses another’s destruction.
