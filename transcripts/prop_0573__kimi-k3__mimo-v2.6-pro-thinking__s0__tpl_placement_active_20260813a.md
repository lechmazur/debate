[P1.1] A musician's song racks up two million streams. Her royalty statement arrives as one number, computed by a formula she cannot see, from data she cannot audit. She supplies the product, yet she is the only participant forbidden to know what it earned. That asymmetry is the case for this motion.
[P1.2] Markets run on information. Wherever one party holds the data and the other bears the risk, we mandate disclosure: companies file standardized reports, food carries labels, paychecks are itemized. Streaming is the rare industry where the platform acts as distributor, accountant, and auditor of the very revenue it shares, answering to no standard but its own. Each platform defines a "view" or "stream" differently and bundles figures so that comparison is impossible.
[P1.3] That is not an accident; it is leverage. A creator who cannot see completion rates, regional breakdowns, or per-stream payout math negotiates blind against a counterparty holding every card.
[P1.4] Standardization is what makes the data useful. A raw dump in fifty incompatible formats is theater. Common definitions — what counts as a view, how revenue pools are split, what was deducted and why — let artists verify their pay, let rights holders detect fraud and misallocation, and let the market actually price content. Transparency also disciplines platforms, because accounting errors and quiet formula changes thrive in opacity.
[P1.5] Anticipate the objections. Privacy? The requirement runs to creators and rights holders about their own works, with ordinary aggregation safeguards — pay-stub logic, not surveillance. Trade secrets?
[P1.6] Disclosure regimes across finance and labor law show you can compel transparency without publishing source code. Cost? These platforms already generate this data to run their businesses; sharing it is a reporting obligation, not a research program.
[P1.7] The question is not whether platforms earned their advantage. It is whether the people whose work generates the revenue are entitled to see the scoreboard. Every functioning market says yes.
