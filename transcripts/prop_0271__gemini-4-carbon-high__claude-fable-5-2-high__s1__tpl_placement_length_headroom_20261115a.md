[P1.1] Today, parents face a rigged choice: isolate their child from modern social life, or surrender them to behavioral engineering. Current parental controls are deliberately blunt.
[P1.2] Parents can block an entire app, set crude daily timers, or toggle imperfect content filters, yet on most major platforms they cannot simply turn off algorithmic recommendations while leaving messaging, subscriptions, and intentional search intact.
[P1.3] That omission is not a technical hurdle; it is a business model. Platforms profit by selling attention, and algorithmic feeds are their primary engine for keeping young users hooked. Expecting app makers to voluntarily hand parents a clean kill-switch for their most lucrative feature ignores their core financial incentive.
[P1.4] Instead, companies offer cosmetic supervision hubs that let parents monitor screen time or nudge usage at the margins, while keeping the autoplay slot machine locked at the center of the child's device.
[P1.5] Giving parents enforceable power to block or delay these recommendation engines—through device-level permissions and mandatory in-app toggles—aligns authority with responsibility. Adolescents have still-developing impulse control, making them uniquely vulnerable to systems that adapt in real time to keep them scrolling past bedtime or pull them down rabbit holes of outrage and body insecurity.
[P1.6] Crucially, this power does not exile kids from digital life or sever peer connections. It lets families phase in technology safely: direct communication and intentional search first, algorithmic feeds years later—or not at all.
