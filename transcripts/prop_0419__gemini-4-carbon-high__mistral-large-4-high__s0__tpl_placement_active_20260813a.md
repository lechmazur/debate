[P1.1] When you ask where your package is, a company wants to help you, so a chatbot works fine. When you try to cancel a subscription or reverse a bogus charge, the company’s financial interest flips: every customer who surrenders in frustration is pure profit. Trapping users in circular chatbot loops is not a temporary technical glitch; in billing and cancellations, friction is a revenue strategy. Because firms profit directly from exhausting customers at the exit door, market forces will not clear the path.
[P1.2] Regulation must.
[P1.3] Consider the asymmetry of modern commerce. Signing up takes a single click. Yet when simple self-service is unavailable or fails, contesting an overcharge or ending a contract routinely means battling an automated gatekeeper engineered to deflect and loop through irrelevant help pages. Holding a consumer’s money hostage behind an algorithmic maze violates basic contractual fairness.
[P1.4] Consumers deserve a fast escape hatch to a human empowered to stop the billing or refund the error.
[P1.5] Safety complaints raise the stakes from stolen money to physical harm. Chatbots rely on scripts and pattern matching; they cannot exercise real judgment in novel crises. Picture a rideshare passenger reporting a threatening driver or a buyer reporting a sparking appliance, only to be trapped by a bot offering FAQ links or a small credit. Safety emergencies are urgent and unpredictable.
[P1.6] Forcing users to guess the magic phrase that unlocks escalation delays human action when minutes matter.
[P1.7] Crucially, this requirement is surgical. Businesses remain free to use AI for routine tasks like order tracking, password resets, and basic troubleshooting, preserving the vast majority of their automation savings. Mandating a fast human path in three high-stakes areas does not ban chatbots; it bans weaponizing them. Companies should use automation to serve customers, not to barricade the exits when money or safety is on the line.

[C1.1] The proposition confuses the channel with the harm. The harm is deception: hiding a cancellation, burying a refund, ignoring a safety report. A blanket mandate for a fast human path in every billing dispute, cancellation, and safety complaint does not outlaw deception; it outlaws chatbots, including systems that resolve routine issues faster than any human queue.
[C1.2] Start with speed. A mistaken subscription charge can be verified, refunded, and credited by a well-built bot in minutes at 3 a.m. A mandated human path often means a ticket, callback, or business-hours hold. Humans are not automatically the fast path; the fast path is instant automated resolution plus guaranteed escalation when the bot cannot solve it.
[C1.3] The motion requires the second without the first, and the second is slower and costlier.
[C1.4] The mandate is not surgical. It hits a two-person startup, a nonprofit processor, a marketplace seller, and a local utility alike. Staffing and supervising humans for every disputed charge or cancellation around the clock is a fixed cost incumbents absorb and small rivals cannot. The predictable response is narrower hours, paid priority access, more identity hurdles, or dumber bots that deflect to a queue.
[C1.5] Consumers pay more for less access.
[C1.6] Safety complaints need immediate action, not a generic conversation. A gas leak, threatening driver, or sparking outlet should trigger emergency routing, lockout, or dispatch in seconds, with an auditable log. Automated triage can do that now; a mandatory human line can become the after-hours bottleneck. The target should be failed escalation and suppressed reports, not automation itself.
[C1.7] The better rule is to ban the trap, not the tool: outlaw dark-pattern loops, require a one-step opt-out, guarantee escalation when a user asks for a human or the bot fails, preserve records, and penalize buried cancellations or safety reports. That reaches the misconduct without a one-size staffing mandate. As written, the motion makes service slower, costlier, and less available for the people it claims to protect.
