[P1.1] The motion turns on a distinction too often blurred: exposure is not expression. Privacy-by-design for minors means private accounts by default, discoverability off, direct messages from strangers restricted, location sharing disabled, friend lists hidden, minimal data collection, and no behavioral advertising unless a minor affirmatively opts in. Those are architectural settings, not judgments about what ideas are permissible. No post is deleted, no account is silenced, and no viewpoint is favored; the state is regulating design and data flows, not speech.
[P1.2] A teenager’s post can still exist, be seen by friends, and be made public with consent. But the default stops the most common harm pathways before they start: grooming through open DMs, doxxing through exposed locations and networks, harassment amplification through public discoverability, and commercial profiling that follows minors across the web.
[P1.3] This is measurable because platforms already track the relevant events: stranger contact, unwanted solicitation, account visibility, data sharing, and ad targeting. Governments can require audits of those indicators and compare default-protected cohorts with opt-in public cohorts. If the defaults work, harms fall; if they do not, the mandate can be revised. That is a stronger accountability loop than asking platforms to voluntarily moderate content after damage occurs.
[P1.4] And it does not significantly increase censorship. Censorship is suppressing expression because of its content or viewpoint. Privacy defaults suppress no viewpoint: they apply uniformly, and they leave publication available through an informed opt-in. Indeed, they reduce pressure for blunt content bans, because the state can protect minors by limiting data extraction and stranger access rather than by deleting speech.
[P1.5] The real choice is not between speech and safety; it is between smart defaults that protect the vulnerable and default exposure that profits from them. Vote PRO.
