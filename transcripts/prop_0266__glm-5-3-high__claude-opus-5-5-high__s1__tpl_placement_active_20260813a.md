[P1.1] The proposition is modest, targeted, and long overdue: when a delivery app's total—food price markup plus fees plus tip—materially exceeds what a customer would pay by ordering directly from a nearby restaurant or walking in to pick it up, the app should say so. Not ban the order. Not hide its own service. Just show the alternative.
[P1.2] Start with what everyone in this debate already concedes: apps routinely charge restaurants commissions around 15 to 30 percent, and restaurants recoup that by marking up menu prices on the app, sometimes 20 percent or more above the in-store menu. Add service fees, delivery fees, and small-order charges, and a $12 burrito routinely becomes $25 before the driver is tipped. The customer is not paying for transparency; they're paying for opacity. A person comparing two screens sees one total and never sees the counterfactual.
[P1.3] That information gap is the entire business case for the disclosure.
[P1.4] The objection you'll hear is that this is a "regulatory burden" or that apps shouldn't advertise competitors. But we demand comparable disclosures in adjacent markets without blinking. Airlines must show the full fare, fees included. Lenders must show APR, not just the teaser rate.
[P1.5] Brokerages must disclose that a "no-fee" trade is paid for elsewhere. The principle is settled: when the headline price conceals the true cost, disclosure follows. Delivery apps are the rare corner of commerce where the concealed markup is larger and the concealment more complete.
[P1.6] Note also what the proposal does not do. It triggers only when the app's price is *materially* higher—so trivial differences don't force clutter. It points to the *nearest* option, so it's actionable, not theoretical. And it preserves choice: someone who values couch convenience at $10 extra can still pay it.
[P1.7] What it removes is the ability to profit from customers not knowing.
[P1.8] The real question isn't whether apps *should* show the cheaper option. It's whether there's any defensible reason to keep hiding it. There isn't.
