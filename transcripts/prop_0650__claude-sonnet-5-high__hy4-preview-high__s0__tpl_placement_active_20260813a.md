[P1.1] Type 2 diabetes doesn't announce itself. It builds for years through insulin resistance and post-meal glucose spikes that a standard annual blood test, fasting glucose or even HbA1c, can miss entirely because those tests capture a single snapshot or a blurry average, not the pattern. By the time someone is diagnosed prediabetic, the metabolic damage is often already underway. A continuous glucose monitor closes that visibility gap for ordinary people, not just diagnosed diabetics, and that is the crux of why widespread adoption is a net public health win.
[P1.2] Consider what a CGM actually shows a non-diabetic wearer: that the bagel and orange juice breakfast spikes their glucose to levels associated with insulin resistance, while eating the same calories with protein and fiber first blunts the spike dramatically. That is not abstract nutrition advice, it's personalized, immediate, visual feedback. Behavioral science is clear that feedback loops change behavior far more effectively than generic guidelines ever have. "Eat less sugar" has been public health advice for decades with limited traction; watching your own glucose curve spike and fall is a different kind of persuasion, one that works because it is individualized and immediate rather than statistical and distant.
[P1.3] Scale that mechanism across a population and the case becomes overwhelming. Prediabetes affects roughly a third of American adults, and most of them don't know it. A tool that makes metabolic dysfunction visible before it becomes diagnosed diabetes is a prevention tool of exactly the kind public health has struggled to build. We already accept that home blood pressure cuffs and step counters, despite occasionally producing worried patients, are net positives because they catch problems early and reinforce healthy habits.
[P1.4] CGMs extend that same logic to the metabolic axis, arguably the more consequential one, since diabetes drives heart disease, kidney failure, and dementia risk.
[P1.5] The anxiety concern is real but bounded and correctable: it's a coaching and education problem, not an inherent flaw in the technology. The prevention benefit, by contrast, compounds over a lifetime. That asymmetry is the heart of my case.
