[P1.1] Consider a 19-year-old with a handgun and a momentary crisis: roughly nine chances in ten of dying if he pulls the trigger. Raise the legal purchase age to 21, and most of those guns never reach him in that moment. That is the contest before us: whether the lives saved by that delay outweigh the friction added to the legal market.
[P1.2] Three mechanisms make the proposition true.
[P1.3] First, 18-to-20 is the peak window for firearm risk. Young adults in this band are disproportionately represented in homicide offending and victimization, and they account for a heavy share of firearm suicides. The neuroscience is not controversial: the prefrontal cortex, which governs impulse control, risk assessment, and emotional regulation, is not fully mature until the mid-twenties. This is why auto insurance, alcohol policy, and rental car contracts already treat this age group differently.
[P1.4] Firearms deserve the same calibration.
[P1.5] Second, suicide is overwhelmingly impulsive. Most gun suicides occur within minutes of decision, and roughly 85% of those who survive a first attempt never die by suicide. Every barrier that delays access — a denied purchase, a closed straw buyer, a required wait — is a life saved. Setting the age at 21 builds that delay directly into the legal pipeline.
[P1.6] Third, substitution does not erase the gain. Firearms are uniquely lethal in both suicide and interpersonal violence. The case-fatality gap between firearms and other means is enormous — gun suicide attempts are fatal roughly 85% of the time, drug attempts a small fraction of that. Killing replaces attempting; the substitution story is real at the margin but small in absolute terms.
[P1.7] Finally, illegal acquisition. The majority of crime guns originate as legal purchases. Raising the purchase age narrows the legal pipeline that illegal markets draw from. A 19-year-old has fewer connections to a black-market dealer than to a licensed store.
[P1.8] The friction is the policy. I look forward to hearing how the opposition claims these gains are wiped out by behavior the policy has already priced in.
