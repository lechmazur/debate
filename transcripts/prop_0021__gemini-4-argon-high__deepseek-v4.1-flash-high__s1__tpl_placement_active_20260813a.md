[P1.1] Across the world, forests fail to regenerate and riverbanks erode because we broke the food web from the top down. Without wolves, lynx, and other apex predators, unchecked herbivores strip landscapes bare. Restoring these predators is not an aesthetic luxury; it is essential ecological infrastructure.
[P1.2] Human culling cannot substitute for a living predator. Hunters fill a quota and go home. Wolves and lynx create a continuous "landscape of fear" that keeps herds moving, preventing them from overgrazing fragile valleys and devouring every young sapling. When wolves returned to Yellowstone, they did not merely reduce elk numbers; they changed elk behavior, allowing streamside trees to recover, stabilizing riverbanks, and restoring habitats for birds and beavers.
[P1.3] You cannot replicate a twenty-four-hour ecological regulator with seasonal hunting permits.
[P1.4] Why must countries proceed even when rural communities and livestock farmers object? Because a functioning ecosystem is a national public good, and the stakes are fundamentally asymmetric. Healthy watersheds, regenerating forests, and biodiversity belong to the entire country, not solely to the industries operating beside them. In no other realm do we grant a local interest group an absolute veto over vital national infrastructure.
[P1.5] Crucially, the farmer's problem is solvable with money and tools, while ecological collapse is not. Livestock predation is a narrow, quantifiable economic cost. States should pair reintroduction with generous compensation for lost animals, alongside subsidized electric fencing, range riders, and guardian dogs. Where predators return, these proven measures keep predation to a tiny fraction of overall livestock mortality.
[P1.6] We can always make a rancher financially whole for a lost calf, but we cannot write a check to replace a broken food web.
[P1.7] Supporting rural communities means funding their adaptation, not leaving national ecosystems permanently degraded. Because we can compensate economic friction but cannot manufacture a synthetic apex predator, countries must reintroduce the species that keep nature alive.
