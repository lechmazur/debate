[P1.1] A research paper cannot push a rock. Today, planetary defense remains largely a research niche: astronomers catalog what telescopes catch, and agencies run rare experiments like the DART mission. Yet DART proved kinetic deflection works, ending the excuse for keeping planetary defense in the laboratory. Staying in research mode gambles whole cities on a fatal illusion: that we will always get decades of warning to fund, design, build, and launch a bespoke spacecraft from scratch.
[P1.2] Orbital physics and aerospace timelines shatter that gamble. While most extinction-level giants are tracked, vast numbers of city-killing asteroids, objects capable of flattening a metropolitan region just as the 1908 Tunguska blast leveled Siberian forest, remain uncatalogued. Many approach from the sun’s glare, like the smaller 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor, or follow trajectories where even improved telescopes may spot them only a few years before impact. Yet custom deep-space missions routinely take five to ten years from political approval to liftoff.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, orbital mechanics punishes delay exponentially: nudging an asteroid early takes tiny velocity changes, whereas waiting years to build hardware multiplies the required force until safe deflection becomes impossible. With short warning, starting from blueprints means watching our launch window close.
[P1.4] Deploying permanent deflection infrastructure closes that gap. This requires neither sci-fi battle stations nor orbital nuclear weapons. It means operationalizing an end-to-end shield: space-based infrared tracking arrays, standardized kinetic impactors deployed on continuous rapid-launch and orbital-staging readiness, and shared international targeting protocols. Because asteroids range from dense metal to fragile rubble piles, a single improvised shot risks missing or fragmenting the threat; permanent readiness enables immediate reconnaissance followed by calibrated, multi-craft nudges.
[P1.5] Built with modular aerospace hardware, this standing capability costs a tiny fraction of routine military space budgets while insuring against catastrophic urban loss. Asteroid strikes are the only major natural disaster humanity can completely prevent, provided we build the firehouse before we smell smoke.
