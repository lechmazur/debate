[P1.1] Nightlife disorder and drunk driving fester inside a common urban mismatch: weekend bars stay open late, while trains and frequent buses shut down around midnight. Cities can answer that risk either by pulling venue closing times earlier or by extending late-night transit. Transit extensions win decisively because they work with human behavior instead of fighting it.
[P1.2] Earlier closing times confuse changing the clock on a bar wall with ending the night. Shutting venues an hour or two earlier triggers predictable adaptation: patrons pre-load beforehand, rush drinks before last call, and carry after-hours parties into private apartments and parking lots without bouncers, trained bartenders, or security. Making one commercial strip quiet at 2 a.m. is not safety when intoxication is merely compressed earlier and displaced into unregulated spaces.
[P1.3] On nightlife disorder, street-level mechanics favor transit. Fights and public nuisance spike when intoxicated strangers are trapped together competing for scarce exits. Early curfews create a synchronized cliff, dumping every venue's crowd onto sidewalks at the exact same minute. Rideshares surge, queues stall, and frustrated crowds linger, turning last call into a flashpoint.
[P1.4] Weekend rail and bus extensions do the opposite: high-capacity transit absorbs staggered departures and clears thousands of patrons swiftly from entertainment districts before friction builds.
[P1.5] On drunk driving, the contrast is even sharper. Impaired driving is usually locked in at 8 p.m., when someone drives downtown because transit will stop before their night ends. Closing bars earlier leaves those keys in an impaired driver's pocket, bottlenecks rideshares at closing, and pushes drinking toward car-dependent house parties, putting impaired drivers onto busier earlier-evening roads. Guaranteed night transit changes the choice before the first drink: leave the car at home because a cheap, reliable ride back exists.
[P1.6] Early curfews compress crowds and relocate risk; late-night transit clears the street and takes the car out of the night.
