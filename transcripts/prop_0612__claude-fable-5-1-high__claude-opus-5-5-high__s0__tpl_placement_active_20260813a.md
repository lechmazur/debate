[P1.1] Academic independence is not a slogan; it is a set of incentives. A scholar is free when the person paying for her lab has no stake in her conclusions and cannot punish her for reaching them. Foreign governments fail both tests. Unlike a private donor, a state has a foreign policy, an intelligence service, and the patience to buy influence over decades.
[P1.2] That is why this motion isolates one category of money—government-linked—and asks for two modest things: sunlight and a ceiling.
[P1.3] Start with disclosure, because it is nearly impossible to argue against. No one designed that concealment as a plot. It happened because institutions with no obligation to disclose simply didn't.
[P1.4] Self-regulation was tested and failed.

[P1.6] Dependence produces self-censorship without anyone issuing an order. A cap is the only tool that removes the dependence itself, and with it the temptation.

[C1.3] Once a government decides which funders are acceptable and how much a university may take from them, it holds a lever over scholarship. That lever will not stay pointed outward.
[C1.4] Second, "government-linked" swallows modern science. Most research money on earth is public. A European Research Council grant is government-linked. [C1.5] PRO boasts that the rule "applies identically to every government" [P1.7]. Then it caps Horizon Europe exactly as it caps an authoritarian ministry. If PRO carves out friendly governments, the rule is no longer neutral. It becomes foreign policy run through university budgets.

[C1.7] Swapping a distant patron for the one that controls your accreditation, visas, and legal status is not independence.
[C1.8] Finally, the real harms PRO describes already have sharper tools: export controls, research-security reviews, espionage law, and conflict-of-interest rules for individual projects. Those target specific conduct. A blanket ceiling punishes every collaboration to prevent a few bad ones.
[C1.9] PRO spent its best material on sunlight. The motion demands a ceiling. And the ceiling hands governments exactly the power over universities that PRO claims to fear.
