[P1.1] The strongest path to victory is simple: population health is produced by universal access, prevention, and price discipline, not by billing complexity or selective coverage. Public healthcare systems do three things predominantly private insurance systems do poorly. First, they spread risk across the whole population, so care is financed by broad taxation rather than by premiums that deter the sick and low-income.
[P1.2] Second, they can set global budgets, negotiate drug and device prices, and steer resources toward primary care instead of duplicative administration. Third, they remove point-of-care cost barriers that cause patients to delay treatment until conditions become severe and expensive.
[P1.3] Consider the common international pattern. Countries with tax-funded or social-insurance models achieve higher life expectancy and lower infant mortality while spending a much smaller share of GDP than the United States, the clearest private-insurance-heavy outlier. America spends roughly twice as much per person as many peer nations, yet has worse outcomes on measures like avoidable death, maternal mortality, and unmanaged diabetes. That is not because American clinicians are less skilled; it is because a fragmented payment system rewards billable procedures over continuity, leaves millions underinsured, and generates enormous administrative overhead.
[P1.4] A public system’s advantage per dollar is not merely moral; it is mechanical. Vaccinations, prenatal visits, blood-pressure control, and cancer screening are cheap when used early but ruinous when deferred. Private insurance often profits by avoiding the costly sick, narrowing networks, or shifting costs to patients. Public systems gain by keeping the whole population healthier.
[P1.5] Even where public systems face waiting lists, the question is population health per dollar, not unlimited immediate access for every nonurgent procedure. The better system is the one that converts finite resources into longer, healthier lives for the whole community. That is the public model.
