[P1.1] Strip away the branding and look at what these platforms actually do. They take custody of customer money. They match buy and sell orders. They extend margin.
[P1.2] They run their own market-making desks against their customers' order flow. They decide which assets get listed and often hold those assets themselves. Every one of those functions is exactly what a securities exchange or broker-dealer does, and every one has a century-old rulebook attached to it because each function, unsupervised, has already blown up ordinary savers before. Regulation should follow function, not vocabulary.
[P1.3] The "distinct, lightly supervised category" is not a hypothesis; it has been the operating reality for most of the industry's life, and its results are in. FTX moved customer deposits to an affiliated trading firm, Alameda, while running the exchange, the market maker, the custodian, and the issuer of its own token under one roof. Celsius and Voyager took retail deposits, lent them out, and left customers as unsecured creditors. Mt. Gox lost customer coins for years before anyone noticed.
[P1.4] Notice the pattern: none of these were exotic crypto-native failures. They were commingling, self-dealing, undisclosed leverage, and missing assets, precisely the failures that customer-segregation rules, net-capital requirements, conflict-of-interest separation, and regular independent examination exist to catch. The rules already fit the disease.
[P1.5] The retail buyer's position confirms it. Most tokens are marketed with a team, a roadmap, and an implied promise that the founders' work will make the price go up. That is the classic investment contract. A person buying a token on Coinbase experiences the same information asymmetry, the same dependence on intermediaries, and the same downside as someone buying a small-cap stock; giving them fewer protections because the asset lives on a blockchain is arbitrary.
[P1.6] Finally, a lighter regime is not neutral. It rewards whoever tokenizes fastest and pulls activity out of supervised markets, a race to the bottom that erodes the rules protecting everyone. Bitcoin as a commodity may be a narrow exception. The motion says "most," and most is plainly right.
