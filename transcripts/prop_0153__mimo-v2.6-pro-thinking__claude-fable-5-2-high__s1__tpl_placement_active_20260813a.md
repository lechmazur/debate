[P1.1] Bans on compensated surrogacy do not eliminate surrogacy. They eliminate the protections. When a jurisdiction criminalizes payment, the arrangement does not disappear — it moves to another country with weaker labor standards, or it goes underground where contracts are unenforceable and a surrogate has no one to call when the intended parents pressure her to undergo a selective reduction, or when an agency disappears with her fee. The question is not whether money changes hands.
[P1.2] It is whether the state is present to police the transaction.
[P1.3] That is the decisive comparison, and it decides this debate. Con must defend a world in which surrogacy continues but women inside it have fewer rights. In an illegal market, a surrogate cannot enforce a health-insurance clause in court — she signed an illegal contract. She cannot report an abusive agency without exposing herself to liability.
[P1.4] She cannot obtain independent legal counsel, because the whole arrangement is designed to stay invisible. Legalization, by contrast, is precisely what makes "strong protections against coercion and exploitation" enforceable rather than aspirational: licensing of agencies, mandatory independent counsel for the surrogate, medical screening and insurance requirements, caps on the number of transfers, cooling-off periods before contracts are signed, and clear parentage rules settled before a child is born. None of these exist when the activity is banned. Prohibition is the abdication of oversight disguised as moral seriousness.
[P1.5] Second, the ban burdens people who have done nothing wrong: intended parents, often same-sex couples or people who cannot carry a pregnancy, and surrogates who may regard a well-compensated, closely supervised pregnancy as the best economic option available to them. We should treat adult women as agents capable of consent. Paternalism that overrides that consent needs evidence of harm greater than the harm of driving them out of the regulated system entirely — and the evidence of a well-run legal framework points the other way.
[P1.6] Con's only honest ground is to concede surrogacy continues under prohibition and explain why those women should remain unprotected. I doubt that ground holds.
