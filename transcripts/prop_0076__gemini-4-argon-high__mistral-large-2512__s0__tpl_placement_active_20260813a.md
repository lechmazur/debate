[P1.1] Needing long-term elder care is not a predictable expense like groceries; it is a catastrophic, roulette-wheel risk. One retiree dies peacefully at minimal cost; their neighbor develops severe dementia and requires years of round-the-clock care costing hundreds of thousands of dollars. Middle-class families cannot efficiently self-insure against that variance through private savings—preparing for the worst case requires massive over-saving by everyone else. Nor can private insurance fix this: the market is notoriously broken by adverse selection and the impossibility of pricing care risks decades in advance.
[P1.2] When private options fail, means testing is both cruel and economically perverse. A means-tested safety net demands pauperization as the price of admission. It tells a retired teacher or machinist with Parkinson’s that they must liquidate their life savings, strip their spouse of security, and spend down into poverty before receiving help. That penalizes middle-class thrift and turns frailty into a bankruptcy machine.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, leaving care to families does not eliminate costs; it just hides them off the government's books. When a daughter leaves her job to care for an ailing parent, the economy loses a skilled worker, the treasury loses tax revenue, and the caregiver forfeits wages and retirement savings, seeding the next generation’s old-age crisis.
[P1.4] Universal or near-universal social insurance—used in countries like Germany and Japan—solves these failures at the root. By pooling risk across the entire population, a ruinous shock for one household becomes a manageable, predictable contribution for all. Universal systems also unlock systemic efficiencies: they hold the purchasing power to restrain provider prices and the incentive to fund early home support, keeping seniors out of far costlier emergency rooms and acute hospital beds. Because societies already pool the predictable costs of aging through pensions and medical care, leaving the single most ruinous risk of old age to luck, spend-down poverty, and family exhaustion is indefensible.
