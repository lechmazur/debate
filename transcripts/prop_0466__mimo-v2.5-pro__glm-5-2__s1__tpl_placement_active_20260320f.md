[P1.2] The core of my case rests on the proven efficacy of large-scale protection. No-take reserves act as nurseries and engines of productivity. [P1.3] Studies from established reserves, like those in the Caribbean and the Coral Triangle, consistently show increased fish biomass and size both inside and nearby. [P1.5] To delay or dilute protection in the name of current income is to trade a community's future for its present.

[C1.5] Then there is the "just transition" promise—aquaculture, ecotourism, reserve monitoring jobs. These are not interchangeable with fishing livelihoods. [C1.6] And international funding for transitional support has a long track record of underdelivery.
[C1.7] None of this denies that marine protection matters. Targeted, well-designed reserves in critical habitats work. Co-management that involves fishing communities in design and enforcement works.
