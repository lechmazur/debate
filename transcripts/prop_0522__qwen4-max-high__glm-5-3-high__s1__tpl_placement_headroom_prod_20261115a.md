[C1.3] There's a real risk of substitution. Once humanitarian pathways exist as the visible "answer," the pressure to negotiate genuine climate-displacement obligations evaporates. [C1.4] In practice, the urgent crowds out the important.
[C1.5] Then there's selection. Discretionary pathways don't reach the most vulnerable; they reach the most mobile, the connected, the credentialed — those who can navigate an application from abroad. [C1.6] The orderly move is to settle entitlement first — who counts, for how long, with what duties — then scale pathways on a foundation that doesn't vanish with the next election. Fleeing the legal question doesn't answer it.
