[P1.1] To increase long-run household financial security, policy must reach the households actually at risk of retiring broke, and it must solve the real reason they fail to save. Increasing tax deductions fails on both counts; automatic enrollment succeeds because it removes behavioral friction for ordinary workers rather than subsidizing existing wealth.
[P1.2] Tax deductions for voluntary contributions are fundamentally upside-down. Because a deduction reduces taxable income, its financial value scales with a household's marginal tax bracket. Expanding deductions hands the largest windfall to high earners who already enjoy financial security. Worse, for affluent households, larger tax breaks rarely generate new net savings; wealthy savers mainly shift money they would have invested anyway from standard brokerage accounts into tax-sheltered ones.
[P1.3] Meanwhile, a working-class employee in a low tax bracket gets pennies on the dollar—or zero benefit—from an expanded deduction. You cannot build broad household security by paying affluent families to rearrange their portfolios.
[P1.4] For the workers who actually need a safety net, the main barrier to saving is not an insufficient tax write-off; it is inertia, present bias, and administrative complexity. When a twenty-four-year-old warehouse worker must navigate HR portals, calculate contribution rates, and choose among dozens of funds just to get started, he puts it off—often for a decade. Automatic enrollment flips human inertia from a liability into an asset. By making participation the default while preserving the freedom to opt out, enrollment surges among younger and lower-earning workers.
[P1.5] Capturing those early years unlocks two transformative engines of security: an extra decade of compound growth and immediate access to employer-matching contributions that workers otherwise forfeit by default.
[P1.6] Long-run financial security depends on turning non-savers into savers, not padding the tax returns of those already maxing out their accounts. Automatic enrollment generates genuine new savings exactly where vulnerability is highest, making it decisively more effective than expanding tax deductions.
