[P1.1] A twelve-year-old can't buy a scratch card at a corner shop. He can spend his mother's card on a hundred randomized crates in a game rated for children, watch the reel spin, hear the jackpot chime, and lose every time. The proposition simply says: treat those two acts the same way.
[P1.2] Start with the structure, because that is what gambling law actually regulates. Gambling has three elements — you stake something of value, the outcome turns on chance, and the prize varies in value. A paid loot box has all three. You pay real money, an algorithm decides, and you might get the rare skin or the duplicate junk.
[P1.3] Everything else is set dressing. The industry's defence is essentially "you always receive *something*" — but that is true of a slot machine too, if you count the noise and the flashing lights as a consolation prize.
[P1.4] Then look at the design. These aren't neutral vending machines. They borrow the whole psychological toolkit of the casino floor: variable-ratio reward schedules, rarity tiers, near-miss animations, limited-time offers, currencies that obscure real prices so that £4.99 becomes "500 gems." That toolkit was refined to defeat adult self-control.
[P1.5] Aiming it at a fourteen-year-old brain, still years from mature impulse regulation and risk assessment, is not a coincidence of game design — it is the business model.
[P1.6] The empirical picture reinforces this. Across many independent surveys in different countries, loot box spending correlates with problem-gambling measures, and the association shows up in adolescents too. Reasonable people dispute the causal direction. But regulation of minors has never required proof of causation — we age-gate alcohol, tobacco, and betting on precautionary grounds, precisely because we do not run experiments on children to see what sticks.
[P1.7] Note what this motion asks for: not prohibition, not the end of gaming. Age verification, disclosure, spending limits — the ordinary furniture of consumer protection. If a mechanic can only survive by being sold to children without the safeguards we apply to every other bet, that tells you what it is.
