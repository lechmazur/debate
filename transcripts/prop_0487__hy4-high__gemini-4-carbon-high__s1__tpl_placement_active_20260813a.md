[P1.1] Governments should fund and deploy permanent asteroid-deflection infrastructure and treat planetary defense as civil protection, not a research niche. The key fact is lead time. A hazardous asteroid is not deflected by a clever paper or an emergency grant after discovery; it needs early detection, orbit refinement, characterization, mission design, launch, and years for a small nudge to become a miss. If those steps begin only after a threatening orbit is confirmed, the window may already be gone.
[P1.2] Permanent infrastructure means a layered capability: wide-field surveys, including space-based infrared to catch objects hidden in solar glare; rapid follow-up tracking and radar; maintained models for orbit and composition; pre-negotiated launch contracts and spacecraft platforms; tested kinetic impactors and other deflection payloads; rehearsed command authority and international notification. This is not a monolithic “asteroid shield” or a blank check. It is a public good governments are uniquely suited to sustain across decades: the practical equivalent of fire stations, levees, and air-traffic control—boring capacity used daily for monitoring, and available before the emergency makes improvisation deadly.
[P1.3] Research remains necessary, but a research niche is structurally mismatched. Grants rise and fall, teams disperse, instruments go stale, and no one is accountable for shaving months off a response. DART showed kinetic deflection can work; Chelyabinsk showed a modest object from a blind direction can injure people without warning. The gap is not merely ignorance.
[P1.4] It is the absence of an integrated, maintained pipeline from detection to action.
[P1.5] The objection that the odds are low and money has other uses hides the asymmetry. The chance may be small; the loss from a large impact is continental to global, and city-scale airbursts are not trifling. We insure houses against rare fires not because fires are frequent, but because recovery cannot be negotiated afterward. The choice is a modest steady program now versus an avoidable catastrophe, preceded by panic, improvisation, and geopolitical blame after a late detection.
