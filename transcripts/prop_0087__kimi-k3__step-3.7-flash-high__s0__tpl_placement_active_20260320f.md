[P1.1] Every form of gambling can hurt someone. But only some products are engineered so that the harm arrives faster than reflection. That is the distinction at the heart of this motion, and regulators ignore it at the public's expense.
[P1.2] The mechanism is simple. Gambling addiction runs on a reinforcement loop: stake, suspense, outcome, repeat. Ultra-fast online slots complete that loop in two or three seconds; in-play betting compresses it further, offering a fresh wager every ball, every point, every corner. Slower forms—weekly lotteries, a Saturday accumulator, a day at the races—have friction built in: waiting.
[P1.3] And waiting is when the brain catches up, registers losses, and decides to stop. Remove the pause and you remove the natural braking system. Psychologists describe the "machine zone," a dissociative state in which rapid play dissolves awareness of time and money. That state is a design outcome, not a user defect.
[P1.4] The harm profile is correspondingly distinct. High event frequency is consistently the strongest product-level predictor of problem gambling severity. In-play bettors show elevated rates of loss-chasing and impulsive staking, because decisions are made in seconds, mid-match, mid-emotion, often mid-drink. A lottery ticket cannot bankrupt a family between dinner and midnight.
[P1.5] A turbo-mode slot can.
[P1.6] Nor is differentiating by risk radical—it is what regulation already does. We tax spirits harder than beer. We restrict automatic weapons more tightly than hunting rifles. Britain capped fixed-odds betting terminal stakes precisely because speed plus stake plus easy availability earned them the nickname "the crack cocaine of the betting shop."
[P1.7] This motion simply asks that online products be treated with the same proportionality: spin-speed floors, mandatory breaks, stake and loss limits, and curbs on in-play micro-markets.
[P1.8] The opposition will preach personal responsibility. But responsibility presumes a moment in which to exercise it—and these products are engineered to eliminate exactly that moment. Regulation should match the speed of the harm.
