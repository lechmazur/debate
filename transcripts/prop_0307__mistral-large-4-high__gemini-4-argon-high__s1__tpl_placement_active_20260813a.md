[P1.1] The proposition carries the weight of common sense and human dignity. When an older adult can remain safely at home, making institutionalization the default choice is not merely a policy preference—it is a failure of imagination that strips away autonomy and often accelerates physical and cognitive decline.
[P1.2] Consider what institutionalization typically means: rigid schedules, shared rooms, rotating staff who barely know your name, and a loss of control over basic daily choices like when to eat or bathe. This environment can trigger what clinicians recognize as institutionalization syndrome—a spiral of passivity, depression, and cognitive deterioration that often outpaces the very conditions that brought the person there. Home care, by contrast, preserves the routines, relationships, and environment that constitute a meaningful life. A familiar kitchen, a garden, a neighbor who checks in—these are not mere amenities but therapeutic infrastructure that supports both mental and physical health.
[P1.3] The economic case aligns with the moral one. Community-based care is frequently less expensive than residential facilities, and it avoids the downstream costs of hospitalization that often follow the falls and infections common in understaffed institutions. But cost is secondary to human outcomes. Clinical observation consistently shows that older adults who receive skilled support at home maintain functional independence longer and report higher life satisfaction than those in congregate settings, even when their medical needs are comparable.
[P1.4] Critics will claim that institutions offer necessary medical supervision and safety. But the proposition carefully says "when possible"—acknowledging that home care requires adequate support services, not that it is universally feasible. The error lies in reversing the burden of proof. We should not ask families to justify keeping an elder at home; we should require institutions to demonstrate why removing that person from their community is medically necessary.
[P1.5] Absent that specific demonstration, home care is not the fallback alternative—it is the ethical and practical standard.
