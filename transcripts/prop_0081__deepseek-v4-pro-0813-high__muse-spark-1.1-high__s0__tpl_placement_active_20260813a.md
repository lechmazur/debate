[P1.1] Dating apps have already decided which users get the fastest help, the strongest blocks, and the badge that says “this person is real”: the ones who pay. [P1.2] Safety features are not luxury add-ons; they are the minimum floor for a service that sends strangers into each other’s lives.
[P1.3] The clearest reason identity verification should be free by default is that verification only protects the whole network when it is widespread. If only paying users get badges, free users are still exposed to unverified profiles, and paying users still match with them. The paywall does not create a protected club; it creates a two-tier pool where the risk remains shared but the tools to manage it are not. Universal free verification shrinks impersonation and catfishing for everyone, including the platform, because more profiles become accountable.

Making scam response faster for paying users teaches fraudsters to target free accounts, who are often younger, newer, or less able to absorb the loss. That is not security; it is triage by income.
[P1.5] A free user who reports a blackmail threat deserves the same escalation as a subscriber who reports a bad date, not a slower queue.
[P1.6] The cost objection misses the point. Apps can charge for visibility, unlimited likes, advanced filters, or profile boosts. Those are genuine enhancements. Charging for the ability to verify someone is real, to block an abuser, or to get help after a scam converts ordinary risk into recurring revenue.
[P1.7] If the free tier cannot be safe, the app should not be offering it.
