[P1.1] Minimum parking requirements are not neutral road rules—they are government mandates forcing every new building to pave far more land than anyone will ever use, and this is just as damaging in car-dependent suburbs as it is in dense cities. Everyone has driven past the half-empty strip mall parking lot off the suburban highway, the vacant asphalt fields surrounding new apartment complexes that never fill even on the busiest holiday weekend. That is not poor planning by developers. That is minimum parking law requiring that overbuild.

[P1.3] Maximum parking requirements do not ban parking. [P1.4] This is not an attack on car owners. It is stopping the government from mandating a system that forces every single resident to subsidize driving, even if they walk, bike, or take the bus. It breaks the vicious cycle where mandatory parking creates more driving, which then is used to justify even more mandatory parking. Even in the most car-reliant regions, this is the first step to building places that give people a choice, rather than trapping everyone in sprawl by law.

[C1.2] First, equity collapses for the very people PRO claims to help. Maximum caps force residents to compete for scarce parking, turning access to housing, jobs, and basic services into a daily hardship. [C1.4] Third, capped parking kills local businesses: a new café with 5 spots instead of 10 will lose half its weekend customers to the older strip mall with ample free parking, stunting the very local growth PRO claims to support.
