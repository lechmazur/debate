[P1.1] Decades of prohibition have failed to curb cartel power in Latin America; instead, they have entrenched a violent black market that funds organized crime. [P1.2] Regulation enables price competition through economies of scale and tax policy, making legal products more attractive. Law enforcement resources currently wasted on prosecuting non-violent cannabis offenses can be redirected to dismantling more dangerous criminal enterprises. [P1.3] In Colorado, legalization led to a measurable decline in cannabis-related arrests and a shift in consumption patterns away from illicit sources. Additionally, the legal market can be structured to undercut illegal prices, ensuring that cartels cannot compete on cost.
[P1.4] Moreover, the shift from prohibition to regulation removes the very conditions that make the cannabis trade so lucrative for cartels: the absence of legal competition and the high risk premium that drives up street prices. Legalization is a targeted strike at the financial heart of organized crime, and it offers a realistic path to weaken their grip on the region.
