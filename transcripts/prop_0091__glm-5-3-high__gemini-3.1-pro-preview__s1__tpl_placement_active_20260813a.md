[P1.1] When you buy a car, you buy a machine — its steel, its motors, its wiring, its screens, its heated seats. The proposition is simple: once hardware is installed and paid for in the purchase price, the owner should keep permanent access to it. What automakers are doing instead is locking functionality behind monthly fees for equipment already sitting in your vehicle. BMW famously tried subscription pricing for heated seats; other makers have paywalled remote start, extra horsepower from the same electric motors, and adaptive cruise features.
[P1.2] The hardware is identical — only the software gate differs. That gate is a choice, not a necessity.
[P1.3] Three arguments carry this case.
[P1.4] First, ownership. If a seat heater, motor, or sensor is physically in my car and I paid for the car, the manufacturer has already been compensated for that hardware. Charging again to *use* it is double-dipping — like selling you a house and then renting you the rooms. Ownership that can be revoked by a lapsed subscription is not ownership at all; it's a lease dressed in a bill of sale.
[P1.5] Second, the vendor lock-in problem. Subscription features are tied to cloud services and manufacturer accounts. When a company changes strategy, shuts down servers, or goes out of business, features you rely on simply vanish. Buyers of certain connected cars have already seen remote services discontinued when companies pivoted.
[P1.6] Permanent access means your car's capabilities survive corporate decisions; subscriptions mean your car degrades on someone else's schedule.
[P1.7] Third, safety and consumer harm. Paywalling safety-relevant capabilities — improved braking response, driver assistance — means your car is deliberately less safe unless you keep paying. And resale values suffer: a used car whose features expire with each new owner is worth less to everyone.
[P1.8] The honest alternative exists: charge for genuine ongoing services with real costs, like live connectivity or streaming, and charge once — upfront — for onboard hardware capabilities. That model funds innovation without holding your own property hostage.
