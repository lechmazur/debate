[P1.1] A rule that bans both the rich and the poor from sleeping under bridges is only neutral on paper. The same is true of school and workplace dress codes that ban headscarves, face coverings, or modest attire. These policies treat majority cultural habits as the neutral baseline and turn minority conscience into a punishable offense.
[P1.2] Genuine safety is not in question here. If a loose garment poses a hazard near factory machinery, or a hospital ward requires sterile gear, restrictions are entirely justified. Even brief identity verification at a security gate is a valid safety measure. Once true safety is accounted for, the only bans left to defend are those where nobody is in danger—rules enforced purely for corporate branding, visual uniformity, or forced assimilation.
[P1.3] Such bans are indefensible because schools and workplaces are not private clubs; they are the gateways to education, independence, and survival. Consider the profound asymmetry behind a supposedly neutral appearance rule. Telling a student he cannot wear a baseball cap in class merely restricts a casual fashion preference. Telling a Muslim woman to remove her hijab, an Orthodox Jewish student she cannot wear a long skirt, or a cautious worker they cannot wear a face covering forces them to choose between their deepest convictions and their future.
[P1.4] Treating a core tenet of faith or bodily autonomy the same as a fashion accessory is not neutrality; it is willful blindness.
[P1.5] Worse, forcing that choice does not foster integration; it engineers exclusion. People rarely abandon their beliefs to satisfy an HR manual. Instead, qualified workers are pushed out of public-facing careers, and students are driven from mainstream classrooms. You cannot empower or unify people by locking them out of a school or a paycheck.
[P1.6] Corporate aesthetics and bureaucratic conformity never outweigh the right to learn and earn a living. Unless safety is genuinely at risk, institutions should judge what is inside a person's head, not what is covering it.
