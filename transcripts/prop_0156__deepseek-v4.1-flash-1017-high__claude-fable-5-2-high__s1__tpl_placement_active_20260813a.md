[P1.1] The eurozone did not respond to the crisis as a union of equals pooling risk; it responded as a creditor cartel supervising debtors. That design choice is why political distrust deepened more than solidarity was preserved.
[P1.2] Consider the core mechanism. When markets attacked, the response was not common unemployment insurance or mutualized debt, but conditional loans, memoranda, troika supervision, and austerity imposed on deficit countries. Greece is the emblem: repeated bailouts paired with deep cuts, external monitors, and a 2015 referendum whose result was quickly overridden by a better deal that never came. Whether or not one thinks the economics were necessary, the politics were corrosive.
[P1.3] Voters in debtor states learned that democratic choice could be disciplined by creditors; voters in creditor states learned that their money was rescuing feckless partners. Both lessons are distrust.
[P1.4] The solidarity that survived was thin and conditional. The ECB’s backstop and rescue funds prevented collapse, yes—but they were framed as emergency containment, not shared insurance. And the price was visible: deposit haircuts in Cyprus, mass youth unemployment in Spain and Greece, and the rise of anti-establishment parties from Syriza to the Five Star Movement to nationalist parties in creditor countries. These are not random protest votes; they are durable signals that crisis management was seen as hierarchical and undemocratic.
[P1.5] The relevant test is not whether the euro survived. It is whether citizens believe the union is legitimate. Financial solidarity can be preserved by a lender of last resort and legal improvisation; political trust cannot be decreed. Once citizens believe the union protects creditors from debtors rather than members from shocks, every later compromise is read through that lens.
[P1.6] The currency may have survived, but the political bond was damaged more deeply. A rescue that saves the euro while teaching voters that solidarity means supervision has deepened distrust more than it preserved solidarity.
