[C1.1] The proposal sounds appealing until you examine what it actually measures. A progressive levy tied to flight count is built on a fundamental category error: it conflates the number of trips with harm caused and ability to pay. Those three things are not the same, and designing tax policy around that conflation produces a scheme that is simultaneously unfair, environmentally misdirected, and nearly impossible to administer honestly.
[C1.2] Start with the metric. A short hop between two nearby cities produces a fraction of the emissions of a long-haul intercontinental flight. Under this levy, both count as one flight. An executive taking five European shuttles triggers the escalating charge; someone who takes two long-haul return journeys to visit family on another continent pays the lower rate.
[C1.3] The levy actively mismeasures the problem it claims to solve. If cutting aviation emissions is the goal, you price emissions—through fuel levies or carbon charges—not trip count.
[C1.4] The fairness argument collapses under similar pressure. PRO assumes high flight frequency signals wealth and discretion. But frequency is a remarkably crude proxy. A nurse rotating between facilities in a dispersed health system, a consultant whose clients require physical presence, a migrant worker visiting elderly parents—these people can accumulate flights without the leisure the proposal assumes.
[C1.5] Meanwhile, private jet passengers—arguably the most carbon-intensive flyers per seat—may never appear in a commercial airline's ledger at all. The levy misses the genuinely privileged and punishes the structurally mobile.
[C1.6] Then there's enforcement. This scheme requires tracking every individual's flights across all carriers and all countries. That is either a surveillance infrastructure of considerable intrusiveness, or a system full of gaps that sophisticated frequent flyers—precisely those with resources to restructure bookings through employers, affiliates, or foreign entities—can exploit most easily.
[C1.7] The real alternative to the status quo isn't this levy. It's an emissions-based charge that prices every flight according to actual carbon output. That approach is simpler, more honest about what it taxes, and harder to game.
[C1.8] PRO needs to explain why counting trips beats pricing carbon. That case hasn't been made.
