[P1.1] Every climate summit for fifteen years has ended the same way: a communiqué promising to "phase down" coal, applause, and then a new coal plant breaking ground somewhere the following month. The problem was never knowing what to do. The problem is that a promise with no consequence is not a commitment; it is a press release.
[P1.2] Coal is the single largest source of energy-related carbon emissions and the dirtiest fuel per unit of electricity, by a wide margin. It is also the one with the clearest, cheapest replacement. New solar and wind now undercut new coal almost everywhere, and in many markets they undercut running existing plants. Ending unabated coal power by 2040 is not a technological moonshot.
[P1.3] It is the lowest-hanging fruit in the entire climate problem. If the world cannot bind itself to this, it cannot bind itself to anything.
[P1.4] Enforceability is the whole point. Decarbonization is a textbook free-rider problem: every country benefits if others quit coal, and every country is tempted to keep burning cheap power while others pay. Voluntary pledges reward the defector. Trade and finance penalties flip that math.
[P1.5] The European Union's carbon border adjustment already shows the tool works and is legally viable: price the carbon at the border, and exporters start cleaning up at home. Extending that logic to coal power, alongside conditions on multilateral lending, turns a wish into a rule.
[P1.6] Penalties also unlock money, not just punish. Capital flows to credible commitments. When South Africa and Indonesia signed binding-looking phaseout partnerships, billions in transition finance followed. A global, enforced deadline gives every grid planner, pension fund, and turbine manufacturer a fifteen-year investment horizon.
[P1.7] Uncertainty is what keeps coal alive.
[P1.8] Finally, the stakes are measured in lungs as much as degrees. Coal smoke kills on the order of a million people a year, mostly in the countries told they "cannot afford" to stop.
[P1.9] The choice is not sovereignty versus coercion. It is enforced orderly transition versus chaotic, unfunded collapse later.
