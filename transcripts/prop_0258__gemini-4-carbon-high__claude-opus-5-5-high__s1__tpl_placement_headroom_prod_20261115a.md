[P1.1] A law that requires hiring an attorney just to read it is not a genuine public rule; it is a tollbooth on citizenship. Because courts enforce the maxim that ignorance of the law excuses no one, basic fairness demands statutes ordinary people can actually understand before the state penalizes them. Yet when a tenant checks eviction protections, a small contractor reviews licensing rules, or a citizen reads a criminal code, they hit a wall of archaic terms, nested provisos, and tangled cross-references.
[P1.2] Requiring legislatures, through binding drafting standards and mandatory pre-vote review, to write statutes in plain language returns the law to the people bound by it.
[P1.3] Plain drafting also restores democratic accountability. Impenetrable prose does not merely confuse citizens after enactment; it blinds voters, journalists, and even rank-and-file lawmakers before a vote occurs. Dense statutory jargon is the ideal hiding place for special-interest carve-outs, regulatory capture, and quiet subsidies. When legislators must write in accessible everyday terms, they cannot disguise who receives a benefit and who bears the cost.
[P1.4] Even where plain language sacrifices some legal precision, that trade-off overwhelmingly favors the public. First, the certainty promised by traditional legalese is largely a myth. Labyrinthine statutes do not prevent disputes; they breed technical loopholes that reward wealthy litigants able to hire specialized counsel while trapping ordinary citizens acting in good faith.
[P1.5] Second, replacing arcane terms of art with clear general standards anchors judicial interpretation in unmistakable legislative purpose, leaving courts and agencies only narrow boundary questions at the margins. Shifting occasional edge-case disputes to judges is vastly better than keeping core legal commands illegible to millions of non-lawyers every single day. A legitimate democracy requires laws written for citizens to follow, not puzzles written for lawyers to monetize.

Swap them for everyday words and you erase that settled meaning, so citizens lose fair notice at precisely the point where liberty is at stake.
[C1.6] Accessibility has a better route. Keep the operative text precise, and require official plain-language summaries, explanatory notes, and public guides beside it. Citizens get comprehension without the binding rule going soft.
[C1.7] So the clash is simple. PRO offers laws that feel readable but mean less. We offer laws that mean exactly what they say, explained in language anyone can follow.
