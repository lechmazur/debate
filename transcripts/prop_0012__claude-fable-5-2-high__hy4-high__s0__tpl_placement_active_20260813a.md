[C1.1] The case against prompt return is not that museums own the dead. It is that “promptly” and “even when” make return automatic. Once credible study is possible, return is irreversible: evidence is lost, and no later regret can reopen a grave.
[C1.2] The consent analogy is too clean. A living patient can speak; ancient remains cannot. Sometimes a community has continuous custody and clear burial duties; sometimes descent is contested, ancestry uncertain, or the person lived thousands of years ago. In those cases a university is not using curiosity as a licence.
[C1.3] It is holding evidence in trust while claimants, methods, and the least destructive tests are assessed.
[C1.4] That evidence can matter to the same people PRO invokes. Human remains can reveal malnutrition, disease, migration, kinship, and violent death; they can corroborate oral histories, identify massacre victims, or document abuses omitted from colonial archives. Reburial may be spiritually right and still erase the only physical record of a crime. In slave cemeteries, mission burial grounds, and unmarked institutional graves, the dead can be witnesses for their own descendants.
[C1.5] Prompt return silences them before they are heard.
[C1.6] PRO’s Kennewick example [P1.8] cuts the other way. Genetic work that eventually supported tribal claims was possible because the remains stayed available long enough for methods to improve. Inquiry helped resolve the dispute; immediate return would have made that evidence harder to obtain. Delay is not automatically colonization.
[C1.7] The better rule is conditional: return where claims are clear and research value weak or pretextual; allow temporary, independent, time-limited study where serious scientific or community value is shown, using the least destructive methods, shared authority, and a fixed return date. A researcher’s assertion should not be enough.
[C1.8] But neither should a slogan that calls every delay refusal. Reverence for the dead includes preserving truth for descendants and future generations. A remedy that makes that truth disappear is not repair.
