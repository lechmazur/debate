[P1.1] Most harm to minors on social media does not come from a political post a moderator missed. It comes from architecture: public-by-default profiles, adults permitted to message children they don't know, precise geolocation on by default, and personal data harvested to feed engagement profiling.
[P1.2] That is why default privacy-by-design is the right intervention. Require large platforms to flip the baseline for under-18 accounts: private by default, follower approval required, direct messages from strangers blocked unless the minor opts in with informed consent, location and contact-list sharing off unless affirmatively turned on, and no behavioral profiling for recommendations or ads by default. Platforms remain free to offer features, but the starting point protects the user who never touches settings.
[P1.3] Defaults matter enormously because most users never change them. Child-safety groups and regulators in Britain and parts of Europe pushed similar defaults in recent years, and the major platforms responded by making teen accounts private by default, curbing adult-to-teen messaging, and limiting targeted ads. The mechanism is obvious: predators, harassers, and sextortion schemes depend heavily on discovery and unsolicited contact. Shut that door by default and unwanted contact, viral pile-ons, and large-scale data collection fall, even without deleting a single lawful post.
[P1.4] That answers the censorship fear. This proposal does not tell government what speech is allowed, does not ban topics, and does not require pre-screening posts. A teenager can still speak, organize, joke, dissent, and view lawful content. It regulates who can reach the child and what data can be taken from the child — access and extraction, not expression.
[P1.5] Voluntary action will not get there because engagement-driven business models reward maximum disclosure and contact. Only a clear rule for large platforms, where most minors actually are, can align incentives. It is narrow, feasible, and aimed at measurable vectors of harm, not ideas.
