[P1.1] A person should not be followed for life by a score they cannot see, correct, or leave. The motion protects a simple but urgent right: if an algorithmic reputation system tracks you across jobs, platforms, schools, and housing, you may disconnect—opt out, stop portability, and seek opportunity through a fair human alternative without punishment.
[P1.2] These systems are not ordinary reviews. A bad rating from one customer, a disputed eviction filing, a canceled delivery shift, or a school discipline flag can be aggregated into a portable risk profile that shapes whether you get hired, approved for an apartment, admitted, or trusted next month. The harm is not one bad decision; it is permanent portability. Because access to work, education, and housing is not optional, consent is often coerced: accept tracking or lose the opportunity.
[P1.3] A legal right to disconnect restores basic liberty and procedural fairness. Many reputation algorithms are opaque, trained on biased or noisy signals, and difficult to contest. If a tenant-screening model confuses you with someone else, if a platform rating is manipulated, or if old data no longer reflects your life, the fair remedy must include exit—not just an endless appeal into a black box. Disconnecting lets people reset, limits the blast radius of errors, and forces institutions to justify decisions with relevant, current evidence.
[P1.4] This right does not abolish references, criminal-record checks, credit checks, or safety screenings. It prevents a single, cross-domain reputational ledger from becoming inescapable. That is not radical. The law already recognizes fresh starts in bankruptcy and sealed juvenile records; this applies the same principle to algorithmic reputation.
[P1.5] If a landlord or employer has a specific, legitimate concern, they should rely on relevant evidence tied to that role—not on a hidden lifetime score. The stronger society is not one where every stumble is permanently carried forward, but one where people can move on, prove themselves, and remain free.
