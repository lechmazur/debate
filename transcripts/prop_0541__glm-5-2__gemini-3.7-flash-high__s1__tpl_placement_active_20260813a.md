[P1.1] The United States faces a procurement crisis hiding in plain sight. Each generation of exquisite platforms costs more, takes longer to build, and arrives in smaller numbers than the last. A handful of ultra-capable aircraft, ships, and submarines must now cover a global footprint against adversaries who can produce, replace, and lose systems at a pace our industrial base no longer matches. The proposition is not to abandon exquisite platforms entirely but to shift a significant share of procurement toward cheaper, attritable systems, accepting tradeoffs in capability for gains in quantity, resilience, and cost discipline.
[P1.2] The core problem is cost-exchange ratio. If a ten-million-dollar cruise missile sinks a two-billion-dollar destroyer, or a drone swarm disables a hundred-million-dollar aircraft on the ground, capability per dollar collapses regardless of how exquisite the platform is. Modern warfare is punishing concentration. Sensors are ubiquitous, precision is democratized, and peer adversaries are building exactly the kind of massed, layered fires that make small numbers of premium targets vulnerable.
[P1.3] Attritable systems flip that math. They force the adversary to spend expensive interceptors on cheap targets, preserve crews and command infrastructure, and allow commanders to accept risk and take losses without collapsing an entire campaign.
[P1.4] Ukraine has made this concrete. Exquisite systems still matter, but the war has been sustained by drones, loitering munitions, artillery, and platforms cheap enough to lose by the hundreds. The side that can replace losses faster and field new designs in weeks rather than decades gains operational momentum. The United States currently optimizes for the opposite: spiraling development timelines, marginal upgrades at exponential cost, and fleet sizes too small to absorb attrition in a sustained conflict.
[P1.5] Attritable procurement also rebuilds the industrial base. Cheap systems built at scale sustain production lines, keep suppliers alive, and create the surge capacity that exquisite programs smother. A defense industrial strategy built around small batches of perfection is a strategy for losing a prolonged war. Mass, replaceability, and cost discipline are not consolation prizes; they are how you win.
