[P1.3] Industrial confinement makes suffering inevitable, not accidental. Octopuses are territorial loners and cannibals. [P1.6] Promising bigger tanks is like promising bigger cages for tigers and calling it kindness.

[C1.2] Complex cognition is a reason for stronger welfare obligations, not automatic prohibition. We keep and regulate pigs, parrots, and dogs despite their intelligence, because the moral question is not whether a mind is complex but whether a practice causes avoidable suffering. PRO describes a crowded concrete tank: fighting, cannibalism, self-mutilation. If those conditions are real, ban those conditions.
[C1.3] That is not the same as banning the enterprise. PRO’s examples indict bad husbandry, not the possibility of good husbandry.
[C1.4] Octopuses’ solitary nature actually makes low-density, individually housed systems more plausible than feedlot-style crowding. Modular tanks, shelters, live or puzzle feeding, controlled water quality, and species-appropriate stunning can be required and inspected. Those are engineering and welfare problems, exactly what regulation exists to solve. PRO treats the worst industrial model as inevitable; it is a design choice, not a biological law.
[C1.5] A global ban is also strategically self-defeating. Prohibition does not stop demand for luxury seafood; it pushes production into unregulated jurisdictions, black markets, or continued wild capture, where nobody inspects stocking density, slaughter, or water quality. International standards, import rules, and certification create leverage over actual practices. A ban creates a race to the bottom and calls it virtue.
[C1.6] Finally, “we don’t need it” is not a ban argument; it is an argument against excess consumption. Many foods are unnecessary. The consistent response is to permit farming only under strict, enforceable welfare rules, and prohibit specific cruel methods. PRO must prove that no regulated octopus farm can be humane.
[C1.7] They have not. Until they do, the evidence supports regulation, not global prohibition.
