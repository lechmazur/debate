[P1.1] The modern economy demands continuous upskilling, yet our current system tethers a worker's professional development to a single employer. This creates a lose-lose scenario: workers are trapped in roles to keep their training benefits, and industries suffer from a stagnant talent pool. Allowing workers to keep employer-funded training accounts when moving within the same sector is the necessary solution.
[P1.2] First, portability eliminates "job lock" and empowers the workforce. Under the status quo, an employee midway through a valuable certification is financially handcuffed to their current company. If they face a toxic work environment, stagnant wages, or find a better opportunity, they must forfeit their training progress to leave. By making these accounts portable within the sector, we decouple educational growth from immediate employment status.
[P1.3] This gives workers the freedom to negotiate better conditions and navigate the labor market without sacrificing their future.
[P1.4] Second, this policy solves chronic underinvestment in worker training. Currently, companies are hesitant to invest in broadly applicable skills, fearing they will spend thousands training an employee only to see them poached by a competitor. When training accounts are portable within a sector, it acknowledges that a rising tide lifts all boats. The industry as a whole benefits from a highly skilled talent pool.
[P1.5] Sector-wide portability encourages collective investment in human capital rather than a defensive approach, where employers only train workers for hyper-specific, non-transferable tasks that do little for the worker's long-term career.
[P1.6] Finally, portable training benefits build economic resilience. As technology and automation reshape entire industries, workers must constantly adapt. Tying learning funds to a single employer forces workers to start from scratch every time they change jobs, severely slowing down their ability to adapt to new market demands. A portable account acts as a continuous engine for career development, ensuring that a worker’s skill progression is dictated by industry needs rather than arbitrary corporate boundaries.
[P1.7] For genuine worker mobility and sector-wide innovation, training benefits must travel with the worker.
