[P1.1] Pretrial liberty was never an absolute right. Courts already detain for flight risk, witness tampering, and dangerousness because no constitutional principle requires releasing someone foreseeably likely to shoot, stab, or beat another person while awaiting trial. Public safety is a core court function, not an afterthought.
[P1.2] Validated risk tools make that judgment better, not worse. Unstructured intuition varies by judge, mood, and docket pressure. A validated instrument forces the same transparent factors — prior violence, age at first offense, pending charges, history of violating release — to be applied consistently and opened to challenge. Broader use means fewer dangerous releases driven by luck of the courtroom draw.
[P1.3] The tradeoff is real but lopsided. Detention before trial is temporary, time-credited, reviewable, with counsel and a hearing. A murder, rape, or shooting committed on release is permanent. It cannot be credited back to the victim.
[P1.4] A system that treats those harms as equal is morally blind. When prediction points to serious violence rather than missed court dates or petty reoffending, the weight shifts decisively toward protection.
[P1.5] Higher detention rates alone prove nothing. If tools correctly identify more genuinely high-risk defendants, then detention should rise. The relevant question is accuracy and threshold: detain the narrow band flagged for serious violence, release everyone else. Consider a defendant with multiple prior violent felonies and a new armed assault charge flagged as highest risk.
[P1.6] Releasing him to keep aggregate numbers low sacrifices a neighbor's safety for a statistic.
[P1.7] Tools are not perfect, but perfection is not the standard. Judges detain imperfectly today; validated tools reduce arbitrariness and create a record that defense counsel can contest.
[P1.8] Courts should not abdicate to cash bail or blind release. Allow broader, disciplined use of validated violence prediction, with hearings and time limits, because the state's first duty is to prevent foreseeable grave harm.

[P2.5] When the tool flags serious violence, refusing to detain to hold numbers down chooses predictable victims over predictable headlines about jail rates.
