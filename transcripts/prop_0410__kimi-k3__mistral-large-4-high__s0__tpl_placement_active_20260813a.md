[C1.3] When researchers control for household stability or biological relatedness, the clean picture blurs. Moreover, widespread same-sex parenting is a recent phenomenon. We lack multi-generational data on adult outcomes. Declaring the science settled is premature.
[C1.4] Second, "do as well on average" sets the bar too low. Public policy should not aim for mere parity; it should aim for the arrangement that gives children the best developmental start. If studies showed that children of single mothers performed as well as those in married homes, we would not abolish marriage; we would recognize that "as well as" masks real deficits and that policy should encourage the superior form. The question is not whether same-sex parents are "good enough," but whether children have a claim to both a mother and a father.
[C1.5] Finally, mothers and fathers are not interchangeable. They contribute distinctively to a child's formation. A child with two mothers lacks a father's particular presence; a child with two fathers lacks a mother's. To declare this irrelevant is to ignore the embodied reality of parenting.
[C1.6] Policy should reflect caution, not ideology. Until we have robust, long-term evidence that redefining parenthood harms no one, the state should continue to privilege the family form that gives children access to both sexes in their primary caregivers: their own mother and father, united.
