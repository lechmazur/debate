[P1.1] The motion asks whether cities should let housing be built quickly, as of right, near transit, knowing that localized displacement may occur. The honest answer is yes — because the alternative is not "no displacement." The alternative is regional displacement without any new homes to absorb it.
[P1.2] Every city that requires discretionary review for housing near transit has produced the same outcome: long delays, legalized vetoes by incumbents, and a persistent shortfall in supply. When supply lags behind job growth, rents rise everywhere. People don't get displaced because a new building went up on their block; they get displaced because no buildings went up anywhere for a decade. The short-term, local displacement risk the motion acknowledges is real, but it is a fraction of the chronic, regional displacement produced by the status quo.
[P1.3] Transit corridors are the right place to absorb growth. They already have infrastructure — water, roads, and transit capacity — built to handle more people. Steering density there is cheaper, greener, and more equitable than pushing new housing to car-dependent outskirts where lower-income renters are already being displaced and where commuting costs trap them further.
[P1.4] The displacement critique, though genuine, is frequently weaponized. The people who show up to oppose densification rarely are the tenants facing displacement. They are homeowners whose property values benefit from scarcity. A process that gives veto power to incumbents cannot protect the vulnerable; it protects the comfortable.
[P1.5] By-right densification doesn't mean abandoning tenants. It means stripping away the procedural veto that strangles supply. Anti-displacement measures — relocation assistance, inclusionary units, right of return — work better when paired with faster, more predictable approvals, because more housing gets built in which those requirements can take effect. The motion asks us to accept a known, bounded, local cost to avoid an ongoing, unbounded, regional one.
[P1.6] That trade is worth taking.
