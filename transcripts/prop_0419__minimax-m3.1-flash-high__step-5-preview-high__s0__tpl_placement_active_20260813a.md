[C1.1] The proposition mistakes a design problem for a technology problem and then answers it with a blunt legal mandate. Not every billing dispute, cancellation, or safety complaint needs a live agent, and forcing companies to route them all to humans adds friction where automation works better.
[C1.2] Consider the ordinary case. A customer notices a duplicated subscription charge, or wants to cancel a streaming service after a free trial. A chatbot can verify the account, spot the duplicate, refund it in seconds, or process the cancellation immediately. Requiring that person to leave the app, navigate a phone tree, and wait for an agent converts a two-minute fix into a fifteen-minute burden.
[C1.3] The mandate treats speed as a synonym for humans, but for high-volume, low-complexity issues, automation is often the faster and more accurate path.
[C1.4] The categories are also too broad to regulate cleanly. "Fast path" and "reasonable period" are undefined. Does a two-hour callback satisfy a safety complaint? What about a business-hours-only line for cancellations?
[C1.5] These gaps invite litigation, compliance consultants, and defensive over-staffing—costs that get passed to consumers, especially at small firms that cannot absorb them. The one-size-fits-all rule ignores the wide gap between a contested $9 charge and a serious product-defect report.
[C1.6] Even on safety, the proposal overpromises. A human on the other end of a phone line does not automatically produce competent escalation. A bot that instantly routes injury reports to a triage team may outperform a call-center script. The real failure is deceptive design—hidden buttons, misleading flows, and retention loops that obstruct exit.
[C1.7] That is already the target of consumer-protection law.
[C1.8] The better rule regulates outcomes, not channels: mandate accurate resolution within strict timelines, ban dark patterns, and penalize companies that bury complaints. Automation is not the enemy; obstruction is. A mandated human path is the wrong tool, and it will make the customer experience worse, not better.
