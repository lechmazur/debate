[P1.1] The unit of judgment should be the campaign, not the sentence. Coordinated harassment uses a platform's machinery—tags, replies, quote-posts, DMs, follows, mass reports—to concentrate pressure on one person. Doxxing exposes private locating information without consent. In both, danger lies beyond any single post's wording; a rule that inspects each brick for a written threat will miss the wall.
[P1.2] Picture a raid: one account posts a home address, others add an employer, family photos, and directions; hundreds tag the target, flood replies with "just asking questions," urge "polite visits," or mass-report her into silence. No line says "I will hurt you." Yet the combination supplies a map, an audience, and permission. Waiting for an explicit threat makes the target absorb the risk until a stranger says the quiet part aloud.
[P1.3] Review after the fact cannot un-doxx her, restore safety, or recover the speech drowned out.
[P1.4] Immediate suspension is a circuit breaker, not a conviction. It freezes the ability to keep tagging, uploading, and coordinating while context is reviewed. Notice, appeal, logs, and reinstatement can correct mistakes; they are useless if the mob finishes first. Nor is this guesswork: platforms can observe sudden bursts, shared links, repeated targets, recycled private data, and synchronized reports.
[P1.5] Those account-level patterns are better evidence of abuse than a magic threat word.
[P1.6] The free-expression objection reverses the interests. The speech value in a swarm of deniable posts is thin; the target's interest in speaking, working, and living without becoming a destination is enormous. If "no explicit threat" is enough, harassers simply launder danger through coded blandness: "public record," "interesting location," "who wants to talk?" That rule rewards disciplined mobs and pushes likely targets out of public life.
[P1.7] Coordinated harassment and doxxing should therefore be sufficient grounds for immediate account suspension: reversible exclusion may be wrong in an edge case; letting a coordinated exposure run to completion is wrong in the case that matters.
