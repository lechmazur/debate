[C1.1] The proposition asks us to believe that strapping a continuous sensor to millions of healthy people and feeding them a live stream of numbers about their blood chemistry will, on net, make the population healthier rather than more anxious and more medicalized. It will not, because the central premise—that glucose spikes in non-diabetics are meaningful signal—is false, and false signal at scale is exactly the recipe for anxiety and over-medicalization.
[C1.2] First, the noise problem. In people without dysglycemia, blood glucose fluctuates constantly and normally: it rises after a banana, after stress, after poor sleep, after coffee on an empty stomach. These spikes are not pathology; they are physiology doing its job. But CGM does not label them "normal variation"—it presents them as a personal data point demanding interpretation.
[C1.3] Without clinical training, users will treat noise as signal. That is the textbook mechanism of health anxiety: continuous, ambiguous data from a body that cannot be argued with, generating compulsive checking and worry, the same pattern seen with home symptom-tracking apps and genetic testing kits.
[C1.4] Second, the behavior CGM actually produces looks less like PRO's cheerful "swap the pastry, walk after dinner" and more like disordered eating. Non-diabetics will start avoiding fruit, whole grains, and starchy vegetables—foods that spike glucose but are nutritionally protective—because the line on the app went up. Unlike a step counter, where "more" is safely almost always better, glucose has no such simple direction; optimizing against spikes without clinical guidance pushes people toward restrictive, fear-based eating.
[C1.5] Third, PRO's screening story is unproven. A single postprandial spike is not a validated predictor of future diabetes in someone with normal fasting glucose and A1c; the diagnostic tools that already flag prediabetes exist and are cheap. What widespread consumer CGM adds is not better prevention but a bigger population of the "worried well," funneled into an industry that monetizes anxiety and produces medical visits and interventions with no proven mortality benefit.
[C1.6] That is over-medicalization: converting normal metabolic variation into a lifestyle disease to be managed, at real psychological cost, for benefits [clipped]
