[P1.1] Universal fuel subsidies in MENA are a policy that fails on its own terms. They were meant to share resource wealth and protect the poor, but they do the opposite. When oil is high, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Algeria and Libya write a blank check covering the gap between world prices and the pump. When oil crashes, Egypt, Tunisia and Morocco still carry the bill through debt.
[P1.2] This leaves budgets hostage to commodity swings, crowding out health, education and infrastructure that actually build prosperity.
[P1.3] They are also brutally regressive. A wealthy household in Doha or Casablanca with two SUVs and air conditioning running all day burns ten times the fuel of a poor family using a minibus. The subsidy follows consumption, so the richest quintile captures the majority of the benefit. A universal price cut is the most expensive way to help the poor, because you have to pay everyone else to get to them.
[P1.4] Replace the price distortion with targeted cash and you keep the protection without the waste. A direct monthly transfer to low and middle-income families preserves purchasing power, but does not reward burning more fuel. That shifts incentives overnight: less smuggling across the Algerian border, less traffic choking Cairo, less energy intensity in Gulf economies that already consume several times the world average per capita. It also stops taxing the transition these states claim to want.
[P1.5] You cannot diversify away from oil while making oil artificially cheap at home.
[P1.6] The five-year phase-out makes it feasible. These states now have national IDs, bank accounts, and digital payment rails built for VAT and COVID relief. Egypt cut energy subsidies sharply after 2014 while expanding Takaful and Karama. Morocco, Jordan and Saudi Arabia all have cash-transfer experience.
[P1.7] Phase the price over 60 months, recycle a fraction of savings into the transfer, and households adjust gradually while the state gains fiscal space.
[P1.8] Subsidies feel stable. They are not. Real stability is a budget that survives low oil prices and a safety net that goes to people, not petrol.
