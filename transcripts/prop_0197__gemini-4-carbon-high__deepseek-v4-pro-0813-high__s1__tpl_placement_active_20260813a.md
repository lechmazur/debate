[P1.1] Orbit is a shared commons where one nation’s target practice becomes every nation’s minefield. When a kinetic anti-satellite weapon slams into an orbital target at hypervelocity, it shatters a single object into thousands of lethal fragments and untrackable shards moving faster than a rifle bullet. China’s 2007 test created a debris belt satellites still dodge nearly two decades later; Russia’s 2021 test forced astronauts aboard the International Space Station to shelter for their lives.
[P1.2] Crucially, there is no "clean" kinetic test. Even low-altitude strikes, like those by the United States and India, fling fragments onto eccentric higher orbits before atmospheric drag pulls them down. In congested low Earth orbit, every kinetic test ratchets collision risk toward a self-sustaining cascade, where debris strikes active satellites and spawns further debris until weather forecasting, navigation, communications, and missile warning are crippled for generations.
[P1.3] A treaty banning all kinetic tests is uniquely workable because it prohibits an unmistakable act, not a hidden capability. Broad space-weapons treaties stall over verification because dual-use hardware on the ground or in quiet orbit looks civilian. Kinetic tests face the opposite reality: no nation can secretly pulverize a satellite in space. Global tracking networks immediately detect orbital breakups and trace their origin, enabling independent verification without intrusive inspections, much as the 1963 Limited Test Ban Treaty curbed nuclear fallout without policing warhead stockpiles.
[P1.4] Nor does this ban demand unilateral disarmament. Major powers depend far more on safe orbital access for intelligence and commerce than on live-target spectacle, and militaries can still validate interceptors against suborbital targets or virtual coordinates without generating permanent shrapnel.
[P1.5] Voluntary unilateral moratoria prove diplomatic appetite exists, yet revocable promises collapse during crises and fail to deter emerging powers from conducting prestige tests. Partial altitude caps likewise invite miscalculation over unpredictable impact physics. Only a bright-line, legally binding treaty locks in reciprocal restraint before one reckless test triggers irreversible orbital ruin.
