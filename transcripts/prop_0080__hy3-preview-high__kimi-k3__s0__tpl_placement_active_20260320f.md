[P1.1] The current patchwork of state-level NIL laws, school-specific compliance carveouts, and unregulated booster collectives has left revenue-sharing college athletes with no predictable, enforceable rights. Ad hoc systems mean a football player at a school in a state with weak NIL protections can lose access to agreed revenue shares for speaking out about unsafe practice conditions, while a peer at a neighboring program with stronger rules retains full pay. This fragmentation creates arbitrary winners and losers, with no consistent standard for what athletes are owed.
[P1.2] Collective bargaining solves this by establishing uniform, binding terms across all revenue-sharing programs. When athletes bargain as a single unit, they can secure baseline guarantees: minimum revenue share percentages, injury protections, academic support funding, and transparent dispute resolution processes that do not rely on a single school’s self-interested compliance office. Individual athletes have no leverage to demand these terms from multibillion-dollar athletic departments under ad hoc NIL rules, where schools and boosters hold all negotiating power.
[P1.3] The alternative, preserving ad hoc NIL and compliance systems, entrenches this power imbalance. Schools can change compliance rules mid-season without athlete input, threaten to withhold revenue shares to silence dissent, and shift costs like long-term medical care onto individual players. Collective bargaining is the only structure that gives revenue-sharing athletes a formal seat at the table, rather than leaving them as passive recipients of whatever terms administrators and boosters choose to grant.
