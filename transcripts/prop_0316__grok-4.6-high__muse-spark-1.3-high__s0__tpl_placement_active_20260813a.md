[P1.1] A dating app that ranks your profile or chooses who you see is not shuffling a deck of cards. It is deciding whether you exist as a potential partner. Users should have a right to know when AI is doing that.
[P1.2] Ranking is power. An unseen model can bury a profile, starve it of matches, or feed someone a narrow stream of faces designed to keep them swiping and paying. That shapes who people meet, how they spend months of their lives, and what they conclude about their own desirability. Those are not small stakes.
[P1.3] Without notice, there is no real consent. Agreeing to "see matches" is not agreeing to be scored on photos, activity, or inferred traits you never volunteered to a ranking engine. A person getting silence cannot tell whether other users rejected them or the machine simply never showed them. They cannot leave on purpose, change course, or refuse a product that treats them as inventory.
[P1.4] The right is modest. Not the formula. Not every weight. A clear statement that AI ranks profiles and shapes who appears.
[P1.5] That is enough for people to evaluate the market they are in.
[P1.6] People already expect notice when they are scored for credit or jobs. Dating is more intimate than those, not less. Closeness of the domain is a reason for the right, not an excuse to hide the ranking.
[P1.7] Apps already harvest photos, messages, location, and behavior. The user is left with a black box. That imbalance is the point. A right to know exists so intimacy is not processed in secret by a system optimizing for engagement rather than for the person using it.
[P1.8] If ranking did not matter, hiding it would not matter. It does. When an AI can decide whether you are seen at all, people have a right to know it is happening.

[C1.4] What it does add is harm. Tell millions of insecure users they have been machine-scored for desirability and you do not create consent, you create obsession. [C1.5] Unlike credit or hiring, there is no appeal and no fix. You cannot pay down a balance to raise attractiveness. Intimacy makes disclosure crueler, not kinder. [C1.6] Gamers are the real winners from a right to know. Once apps must announce when and where AI shapes visibility, manipulators, spammers, and harassers get a map of what to probe. [C1.7] Accountability should target conduct, not tools.
