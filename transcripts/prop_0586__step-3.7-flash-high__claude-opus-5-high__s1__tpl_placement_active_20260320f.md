[P1.1] Across-the-board minimum wage hikes routinely fail low-wage workers by applying a single, rigid cost shock to every industry and local labor market, ignoring vast differences in sector profitability and regional cost of living. A $15 federal minimum might be sustainable for a high-margin retail chain in a wealthy suburb, but it would force small rural restaurants, seasonal farm operations, and local independent retailers to cut staff, hours, or close entirely to absorb the sudden cost increase. That’s the core flaw of blanket hikes: they trade pay gains for some low-wage workers for job losses for others, hitting the most vulnerable workers in the lowest-margin sectors first.
[P1.2] Sectoral bargaining and industry-specific wage boards fix this by tailoring wage standards to each sector’s actual capacity to pay, with input from employers, workers, and government representatives. These bodies set floors calibrated to local cost of living, sector productivity, and profit margins, so wage hikes don’t outpace what businesses can realistically afford. A fast food wage board could set a $14 floor in low-cost rural areas and $19 in high-cost coastal cities, delivering meaningful pay gains without forcing job cuts.
[P1.3] Real-world evidence backs this up. Countries with widespread sectoral bargaining like Germany and Australia have far higher low-wage worker pay and lower youth unemployment than the US, with no mass job losses from wage increases. Empirical research on US minimum wage increases consistently finds modest but significant employment losses for low-skilled workers in low-margin sectors, while tailored sectoral standards avoid that drop by aligning wage growth with sector-specific productivity. Even domestically, California’s 2023 fast food wage board delivered a 25% pay bump for 700,000+ low-wage fast food workers, with no measurable decline in employment or hours, while states that passed similar across-the-board minimum wage hikes in the same period saw small but statistically significant job losses in low-margin hospitality and retail sectors.
