[C1.1] Judge this debate on one question: what did advocates actually expect, and did cameras deliver it? The answer requires an honest baseline, and the honest baseline was never "cameras will end police impunity." Read what the reformers, the Obama policing task force, and civil-liberties groups actually asked for in 2014: an objective record of contested encounters, a deterrent on the margins, faster resolution of complaints, and evidence when word-of-badge previously decided everything. On every one of those axes, cameras delivered.
[C1.2] Start with the evidentiary revolution, because it is not deniable. Before cameras, a fatal encounter came down to an officer's report against a dead or discredited civilian. Today, footage routinely ends careers within days. The Tyre Nichols case is the clearest picture of the new world: officers fired and charged with murder within roughly three weeks—a speed that was simply unimaginable in the pre-camera era, when comparable cases took years or never surfaced at all.
[C1.3] That is not "a few high-profile prosecutions." That is a structural change in what a department can plausibly claim happened.
[C1.4] Second, complaints. Across many departments and repeated studies, civilian complaints fell substantially after camera adoption—often dramatically. And crucially, complaints that are filed now get resolved on evidence rather than credibility contests. That includes exonerating officers falsely accused, which advocates explicitly expected and wanted: accountability means accurate consequences in both directions, not a conviction quota.
[C1.5] Third, transparency norms shifted. States and cities now mandate footage release on short timelines. Families of the dead can see what happened. That legal architecture did not exist a decade ago and exists only because cameras do.
[C1.6] The proposition survives only by inflating "expectations" into a fantasy of automatic justice, then declaring the fantasy unmet. Measured against the real, stated expectations of real advocates—evidence, deterrence at the margin, faster truth—body cameras met and in places exceeded them. That is the record.
