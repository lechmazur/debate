[P1.1] When a consumer purchases a vehicle, they are buying a physical asset, not signing a perpetual lease for the hardware physically bolted to the chassis. Automakers locking pre-installed hardware—such as heated seats, maximum battery capacity, or rear-wheel steering—behind recurring software subscriptions is an exploitative practice that fundamentally undermines property rights and consumer fairness.
[P1.2] First, this model transforms ownership into extortion. There is a critical distinction between a true software service and a hardware paywall. A subscription for live traffic routing or satellite radio makes sense because it requires ongoing server maintenance and continuous third-party data provision. However, a physical heating coil in a seat requires zero ongoing effort or cost from the manufacturer.
[P1.3] By locking this hardware behind a paywall, automakers are not providing a continuous service; they are simply deploying artificial scarcity to extract perpetual rent from a captive customer. Once the hardware is sold and driven off the lot, the manufacturer’s claim to its utility must end.
[P1.4] Second, pre-installing dormant hardware in every vehicle creates massive economic and environmental waste. To make a subscription model work, automakers must install the maximum hardware configuration in every car rolling off the assembly line, regardless of whether the buyer intends to use it. This adds unnecessary weight to the vehicle, directly reducing fuel efficiency and electric vehicle range. Furthermore, the cost of manufacturing and installing these dormant components does not disappear; it is quietly passed on to all consumers in the base price of the car.
[P1.5] Buyers are effectively forced to subsidize the cost of hardware they are locked out of, only to be charged a second time if they wish to turn it on.
[P1.6] Consumers should not have to pay a monthly ransom to access the physical components they already hauled out of the dealership. Protecting permanent access to installed hardware is essential to preserving true ownership, preventing corporate rent-seeking, and stopping the wasteful mass production of captive technology.
