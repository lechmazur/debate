[P1.2] It's a business model.
[P1.3] This is drip pricing, and it corrupts the entire market. Shoppers compare the number they see first, so a host who hides $200 in fees looks cheaper than an honest host charging the same total upfront. Deception wins the click; honest hosts must hide fees too or lose bookings. Platforms profit per completed stay and won't fix this alone—Airbnb added a "total price" toggle only after years of complaints and left it off by default.

[P1.6] The burden is trivial. Platforms already store every fee and every rule in their databases; this only requires rendering what they have, one screen earlier. No price controls, no caps—just truthful display. Regulators from the FTC to the European Union have moved against junk fees because disclosure works where competition alone fails.
[P1.7] The other side will call this paternalism. But stating the full price and the real terms before commitment is the oldest rule of fair dealing. Transparency isn't a burden on markets; it's what makes a market.

[C1.8] The consumer-protective principle is that the true total and material terms must be visible before final payment. On the major platforms at issue, they already are.
