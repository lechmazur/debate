[P1.1] Unauthorized migration is mostly a search for work. Remove the job, and you remove the reason to come and the reason to stay. That is why the proposition asks for something modest and achievable: a meaningful reduction, not perfection.
[P1.2] Today's I-9 system is an honor system with a filing cabinet. An employer glances at documents he is not trained to authenticate, and the law is satisfied. [P1.3] One verifies, pays payroll taxes, and hires legally. The other looks away and underbids him by twenty percent. Under a voluntary system, we punish the honest firm. A universal mandate ends that race to the bottom — that is precisely why universality, not the technology alone, does the work.
[P1.4] On discrimination, the abuse we actually observe is employers demanding extra papers from people who look or sound foreign. That is discretionary ethnic profiling, and it thrives under the current paper regime. A uniform electronic check run on every new hire — including native-born citizens — removes the discretion that makes profiling possible. Tentative nonconfirmations are contestable; workers keep working while errors are resolved.
[P1.5] Mistakes will happen. The claim to beat is a *large increase* in discrimination, and standardization points the other way.
[P1.6] On shortages, the argument assumes wages and legal supply are frozen. They aren't. When employers say no American will do the job, the honest translation is usually: not at a legal wage.
[P1.7] The real choice is between a rule everyone follows and a rule only the honest follow.
