Increasing penalties does not deter traffickers lured by astronomical profit margins; instead, it triggers the "iron law of prohibition," incentivizing the distribution of even more concentrated, lethal, and hard-to-detect synthetic analogues.
[P1.3] Second, harm reduction and MAT directly intercept death where it happens. Naloxone distribution reverses active overdoses in minutes, keeping people alive today so they have a tomorrow. By stabilizing individuals and eliminating cravings, MAT directly shrinks the very demand that fuels the illicit market.
[P1.4] Third, the five-year timeframe dictates our strategy. Scaling up distribution of life-saving tools like fentanyl test strips, syringe services, and low-barrier MAT clinics can be achieved almost immediately with targeted funding. In contrast, supply-side enforcement and prosecution require years of bureaucratic coordination, court backlogs, and international diplomacy, all while doing nothing for the person currently using alone in a room.
[P1.5] To save the most lives by 2030, we must fund the interventions that treat the disease, not wage an expensive, decades-failed war on a highly elastic supply chain.
