[P1.1] Global supply chains are undergoing their most decisive realignment since the end of the Cold War. For Latin America, nearshoring represents a once-in-a-generation escape hatch from the trap of raw-material extraction and low-growth vulnerability. But manufacturing networks do not relocate by geography alone; they require aggressive, state-led industrial policy to anchor them. Accepting targeted protectionism and fiscal burdens is not a reckless gamble—it is the indispensable price of admission.
[P1.2] First, this window is fleeting. Multinationals diversifying away from East Asia are deploying decades' worth of capital expenditures right now. If Latin America relies on passive market forces, that investment will bypass the region for Southeast Asia or be re-shored entirely within North America. Attracting advanced manufacturing requires immediate, state-backed investment in clean energy grids, multimodal transport corridors, and specialized technical institutes.
[P1.3] These fiscal commitments are not structural consumption deficits; they are front-loaded capital outlays that generate expanding, permanent tax bases through formal, high-productivity industrial clusters.
[P1.4] Second, strategic protectionism is essential to avoid the extractive maquiladora trap. Simply inviting foreign assembly plants without local-content rules or domestic preferences leaves host nations stranded at the bottom of the value chain. Latin America has seen this outcome before: imported components arrive duty-free, low-wage domestic labor performs basic assembly, and profits and technological know-how immediately flow abroad. Targeted protectionist measures and domestic sourcing requirements force multinational corporations to integrate local suppliers, license technology, and cultivate indigenous industrial capabilities.
[P1.5] Orthodox fiscal austerity and market neutrality have spent decades locking Latin America into boom-and-bust commodity cycles while eroding domestic industry. Accepting short-term fiscal strain and defensive trade policy today transforms nearshoring from an enclave of low-wage assembly into an engine of sovereign technological upgrading.
