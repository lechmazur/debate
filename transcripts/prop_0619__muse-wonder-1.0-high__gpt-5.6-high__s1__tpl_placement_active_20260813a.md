It is a foreseeable pipeline to stalking, swatting, vandalism, and assault. Platforms can see that pipeline forming in real time. The law should require them to act on what they can plainly foresee.
[P1.2] This is a duty of reasonable care, not censorship of unpopular opinions. Small forums would face a lighter standard than global networks, but neither may ignore a known danger it helps create.

Product makers, venues, and employers live under that principle. Social media should not be a liability-free exception because harm is assembled digitally before it lands physically.
[P1.4] Voluntary promises fail because incentives point the other way. Outrage spreads and pile-ons retain users, while the cost falls on a victim who cannot out-report a mob. That process-based standard protects a platform that acts proportionately even if an attacker persists — and suppressing speech without those signals is not safer compliance, just different conduct.
