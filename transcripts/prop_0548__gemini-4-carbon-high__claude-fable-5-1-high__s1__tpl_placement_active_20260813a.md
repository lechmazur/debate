[P1.1] Violent crime rarely follows a calm calculation about whether a prison sentence lasts ten years or fifteen. [P1.2] Deterrence depends on immediacy, not distant severity. Retaliatory shootings, robberies, and street assaults are driven by immediate provocation, peer pressure, and opportunity. Offenders heavily discount the distant future; five extra years added to a long prison term is an abstraction in the heat of the moment. What stops someone from carrying an illegal gun or escalating a dispute is certainty: knowing an officer is visibly stationed on that block right now.
[P1.3] Because urban violence concentrates intensely in specific intersections, transit hubs, and nightlife strips, focusing presence on those exact footprints removes criminal opportunity without spreading officers thin. [P1.5] By contrast, longer sentences for repeat offenders hit steep diminishing returns. Repeat violent offenders already face substantial prison terms. Adding extra years mostly keeps prisoners locked up into middle age, long after people naturally age out of violent crime. [P1.6] Controlling the hot spot prevents violence up front; extending sentences merely warehouses aging offenders after the damage is done.
