[C1.2] Minors cannot simply buy what an influencer praises. Procedures happen in clinics, not in a feed. Licensed providers must screen patients and obtain informed consent, and for minors that generally means parental involvement; undisclosed sponsorships and deceptive health claims are already regulated. [C1.3] An pop-up before a video adds theatre, not equivalent protection.
[C1.4] The proposed tools are weak where it matters. [C1.5] Finally, it singles out one contributor to insecurity while leaving the larger machine untouched. [C1.6] Target deception directly: punish false before-and-after claims, require sponsorship disclosure, ban marketing aimed specifically at children, and preserve parental consent and controls. That protects minors at real points of risk without treating every mixed audience as a children's ward and every adult as incapable of hearing about a legal choice.
