[P1.2] The proposal is not to replace referees with robots. It is to assign objective geometry to the tools that measure it best: goal-line technology, automated strike zones, offside tracking, and ball-out sensors. A pitch is either in the zone or it is not. A player is either onside or he is not.
[P1.3] A ball crosses the line or it does not. These are facts, not opinions, and semi-automated systems determine them with greater accuracy and consistency than any crew working under stadium lights and crowd noise.
[P1.4] The human element remains exactly where it belongs. [P1.5] Reaction time, viewing angle, fatigue, and unconscious bias all degrade accuracy in ways technology does not.
[P1.6] Traditional officiating asks fallible perception to adjudicate high-stakes geometry in real time. That is not romance; it is a design flaw. [P1.7] Sports thrive on fairness. When fans believe the score reflects skill rather than officiating luck, the product survives. Objective calls should be objective in fact, not just in name. Adopt semi-automated officiating with limited human override, and let the game be decided by the players.
