[P1.1] Unregulated ticket resale is a rigged system that fleeces ordinary fans. Scalpers use automated bots to snatch up virtually all high-demand inventory the second it drops, then list tickets at two, five, even ten times face value within minutes. Regular attendees, from parents taking kids to first concerts to diehard fans of local theater, are priced outof events they planned months to attend, while opportunistic resellers pocket unearned windfalls. Artists lose out too, as scalpers capture revenue that could support tour production, fair wages for crew, or lower primary ticket prices.
[P1.2] This is not a functioning market: it is exploitation of a supply-constrained product that has no substitute for the fan who wants to see that specific artist, that specific game, that specific show.
[P1.3] The proposition fixes this by forcing platforms to choose one of two simple, proven fixes for most tickets. Platforms already have the technical infrastructure to implement either fix, so compliance costs are negligible. Either cap resale markups at a modest level that covers only legitimate costs like transfer fees, eliminating the profit incentive for bulk scalping. Or run face-value resale channels where fans who can no longer attend can pass tickets to others at the original price, with no middleman markup.
[P1.4] Both options preserve resale for legitimate cases—a sudden illness, a schedule conflict, a travel cancellation—without letting predatory actors hijack the entire market.
[P1.5] Opponents will claim this violates free market principles, but there is no free market when bad actors use unfair tools to corner supply. The only people harmed by this rule are scalpers and platforms that profit from inflated resale fees and opaque pricing; everyone else—fans, artists, venues—wins. Requiring these minimal, low-cost protections for most tickets is the only way to restore fairness to an industry that has let exploitation run rampant for far too long.
