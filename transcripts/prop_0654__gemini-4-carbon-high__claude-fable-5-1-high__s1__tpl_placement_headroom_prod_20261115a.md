[P1.1] Structural failure does not wait for a scheduled appointment. Relying mainly on periodic manual inspections means governing critical infrastructure through rare, surface-level snapshots. Human inspectors cannot see steel tendons corroding deep inside concrete bridge decks, internal seepage weakening an earthen dam after heavy storms, or cumulative thermal and wind fatigue straining power-grid hardware. When floods, heatwaves, or tremors strike between inspection cycles, hidden damage compounds until failure is sudden and catastrophic.
[P1.2] Embedded structural-health-monitoring sensors replace blind intervals with continuous visibility, detecting micro-strains, vibration shifts, and moisture intrusion in real time so engineers intervene before bridges collapse, dams breach, or failing lines spark wildfires.
[P1.3] Crucially, requiring sensors in all new assets captures major lifecycle savings at modest upfront cost. Retrofitting aging structures is disruptive, but integrating fiber-optic, strain, or acoustic sensors during initial construction and manufacturing represents a small fraction of capital budgets. Once embedded, continuous monitoring transforms maintenance economics and worker safety.
[P1.4] Instead of closing highway lanes, de-energizing lines, and suspending inspectors from ropes just to check intact structures on a rigid calendar, operators dispatch crews precisely when and where structural data flags genuine degradation. Human expertise becomes targeted rather than blind, fixing early defects long before they force costly emergency rebuilds.
[P1.5] Finally, a universal government requirement is essential because builder and utility incentives skew dangerously short-term. Left optional, cash-strapped municipalities and private contractors strip out long-term monitoring to trim upfront bids, dumping catastrophic flood, collapse, and blackout risks onto the public. Nor should smaller assets be exempted: a washed-out rural bridge severs emergency routes, one failed distribution pole can ignite a wildfire, and sensor packages naturally scale from simple tilt or load gauges to dense dam arrays. Requiring embedded monitoring across all new construction standardizes data, lowers unit costs, and locks in resilience from day one.
