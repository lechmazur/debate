[P1.1] Selling citizenship is selling what should not be sold. That is the core of this debate, and from it every other harm follows.
[P1.2] A golden visa or citizenship-by-investment program takes a political status — the right to live, work, and vote in a country, and the passport that grants mobility across Europe — and markets it to the highest bidder. The unit of account is not contribution, character, or connection. It is capital. Once you accept that premise, the program is already corrosive: it tells citizens that their membership is worth less than someone's down payment.
[P1.3] The harms are not hypothetical. In multiple European countries, investigations have linked these programs to inflows of wealth whose origins were never seriously verified. Cyprus, Malta, and Portugal each saw scandals in which applicants under criminal investigation, politically exposed persons, or individuals of unexplained wealth obtained residency or citizenship. When the price of entry is high enough, the gatekeepers' incentive is to look the other way.
[P1.4] Security vetting collapses into marketing.
[P1.5] The second harm is concrete and domestic. In Lisbon, Athens, and parts of Barcelona, real estate linked to golden-visa inflows has priced locals out of their own cities. Ending the programs stops the future supply of corruption-laundered permits and removes a fuel line from housing bubbles in cities already under acute pressure.
[P1.6] The economic-benefit rebuttal is weak. The investments are small relative to national economies, often property purchases that would have occurred anyway or low-yield government bonds. The fiscal gains are a rounding error; the reputational and security costs are not. Whatever revenue is collected can be replaced through ordinary, transparent taxation of high earners, without the side effects.
[P1.7] A state that auctions its passport does not exercise sovereign power; it relinquishes it to the wallet of the applicant. Ending these programs is not a restriction on national freedom. It is the recovery of the idea that citizenship is a public good, not a private commodity.
