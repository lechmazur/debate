[P1.1] A training account an employer funds is not a gift. It is part of what the worker earned, paid in exchange for labor, just like wages or a retirement match. When a worker changes jobs and that account vanishes, the worker hasn't lost a perk. They've had compensation clawed back for the crime of switching employers.
[P1.2] That is the core of this debate: who owns value the worker already earned?
[P1.3] First, forfeiture is a mobility tax that suppresses wages. Picture a welder halfway through a funded certification, or a nurse with a balance earmarked for a specialty credential. A competitor offers better pay. If accepting means forfeiting that balance, many will stay put.
[P1.4] That lock-in is exactly what employers count on. It weakens workers' bargaining power and keeps people in jobs where they are less productive and less paid. We stopped tolerating this with retirement savings. Workers roll over their 401(k)s rather than surrendering them.
[P1.5] Training money deserves the same logic.
[P1.6] Second, the "same sector" limit answers the strongest objection before it is made. The usual worry is free-riding: why should one firm pay to train a worker who walks to a rival? But under sector portability, every firm is both sender and receiver. The hospital that loses a nurse gains another nurse arriving with her own funded skills.
[P1.7] The sector as a whole keeps the investment. This is how multi-employer training funds in the building trades have long worked. Contributions follow the electrician from contractor to contractor, and the industry gets a deeper skilled workforce rather than a thinner one.
[P1.8] Third, portability fixes underinvestment rather than causing it. Today firms already train too little, precisely because they fear poaching. Workers can't fill the gap because training they don't control can disappear overnight. Give workers ownership, and they plan multi-year skill paths instead of restarting every time they move.
[P1.9] So the question for CON is simple. If the money was earned, and the skills stay in the sector, what justifies confiscating it at the door?
