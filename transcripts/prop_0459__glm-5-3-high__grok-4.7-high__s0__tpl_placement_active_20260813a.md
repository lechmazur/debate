[P1.1] Localization of critical clean-tech supply chains is not protectionism for its own taste—it is insurance against a concentration of production so extreme that a single government can switch off the West's energy transition at will.
[P1.2] Start with the vulnerability. China today refines the majority of the world's rare earths, produces the dominant share of solar-grade polysilicon, cathode and anode material, and battery cells. This is not the outcome of comparative advantage alone; it is the outcome of decades of deliberate state subsidy, procurement, and industrial targeting. We were beaten at industrial policy, not outcompeted by free markets.
[P1.3] Refusing to play the game doesn't keep the game from being played—it just guarantees we lose it.
[P1.4] Second, the price argument proves too much. The recent past gave us the stress test: pandemic-era shortages, a European energy crisis triggered by a single supplier's leverage, and semiconductor shortages that idled whole industries. Concentrated supply chains don't just carry higher risk—they deliver higher prices exactly when you can least afford them. A modest, front-loaded premium for domestic capacity buys price stability and negotiating power for decades.
[P1.5] That is a bargain, not a cost.
[P1.6] Third, climate math rewards supply security. Every wind turbine delayed by a missing magnet, every grid battery stalled by export controls, is slower decarbonization. If we believe climate change is an emergency, then treating the inputs to decarbonization as ordinary tradable goods is a category error. We do not let markets alone supply weapons or vaccines for the same reason.
[P1.7] And the learning-curve logic is real: solar and batteries got cheap through scale, and scale follows committed demand. Procurement is how governments create that scale deliberately—the same mechanism that made clean tech cheap globally.
[P1.8] Trade tension is a real but bounded cost; strategic dependency is an unbounded one. Pay the small, visible price rather than the catastrophic hidden one.
