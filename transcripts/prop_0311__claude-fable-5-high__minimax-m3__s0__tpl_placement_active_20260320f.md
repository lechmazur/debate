[P1.1] When most couples in Britain, Canada, Australia, and the United States now live together before marrying, calling that choice a red flag means declaring the majority of happily married people suspect. The motion asks a simple question: should we treat this ordinary behavior as ordinary? Two arguments say yes.
[P1.2] First, cohabitation is an act of commitment, not an evasion of it. Signing a joint lease, merging grocery budgets, splitting utility bills, negotiating whose furniture survives — these are concrete investments in a shared life, made without the safety net of legal formality. A couple that dates for five years while keeping separate apartments has committed less, behaviorally, than a couple that has spent two years running a household together. Commitment is revealed by what people build, not by the sequence in which they file paperwork.
[P1.3] Second, cohabitation is a rational information-gathering step before an irreversible-ish decision. Marriage is one of the highest-stakes choices most people make. Living together exposes exactly the things dating conceals: how someone handles money under stress, illness, chores, conflict at 7 a.m. We would never call a company careless for running a pilot before a merger.
[P1.4] Entering marriage with better information is prudence, not weak resolve.
[P1.5] The old worry — the so-called "cohabitation effect" linking premarital cohabitation to divorce — rested largely on selection, not causation. People who cohabited decades ago were disproportionately those already skeptical of marriage or facing economic strain; as cohabitation became universal, that gap has substantially narrowed, and researchers have found that couples who move in with clear intent, especially after engagement, look essentially like everyone else.
[P1.6] So here is the frame for this debate: to win, my opponent must show that cohabitation itself weakens commitment — not that it once correlated with divorce among a self-selected minority. Absent that causal story, stigmatizing what most stable, married couples actually did is not caution. It is prejudice against the normal.
