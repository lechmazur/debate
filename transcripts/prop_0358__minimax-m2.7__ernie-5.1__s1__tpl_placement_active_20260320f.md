The classic deterrence model holds that the expected cost of an action equals the probability of detection times the severity of punishment. [C1.3] Moreover, speed‑limit reductions and street redesign are expensive, slow to implement, and often produce unintended traffic diversion that moves risk to neighboring streets. [C1.4] Penalties also address the majority of fatal crashes that involve alcohol, distraction, or red‑light running—behaviors that speed limits cannot touch. By making the cost of any lethal mistake steep, cities create a blanket safety net that street geometry simply cannot provide. If the goal is to cut deaths now, the fastest, most scalable tool is not a new curb but a harsher penalty that makes the dangerous choice too costly to repeat.
