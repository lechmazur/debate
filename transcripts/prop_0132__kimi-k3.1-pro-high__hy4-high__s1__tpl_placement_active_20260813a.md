[P1.1] The central distinction is between access and intrusion. Under this policy, no political post is banned, no account is silenced, and no user is prevented from searching, following, or sharing. What changes is who initiates the encounter: platforms may not insert politics into a user’s recommendations until that user asks. Political speech remains free; algorithmic political solicitation requires consent.
[P1.2] That consent matters because recommendation is not a neutral mirror. It is a ranking choice made at vast scale, usually to serve engagement goals. Politics gives those systems powerful levers: anger, threat, group loyalty, and humiliation. The system need not intend to polarise anyone for the incentive to favour content that provokes reaction over content that informs.
[P1.3] A user who opens an app for football or family photographs has not agreed to let an opaque ranking system turn politics into an endless stream of outrage bait.
[P1.4] Default opt-in corrects that without pretending platforms can or should eliminate political disagreement. Interested citizens opt in and receive recommendations; everyone retains ordinary access through subscriptions, search, and sharing. The burden is a choice screen, not censorship. It also protects the people least able to audit algorithmic influence: casual users, new users, and those simply exhausted by conflict.
[P1.5] CON’s likely defence—incidental exposure creates informed citizens—proves too much. Compelled exposure to whatever maximises engagement is not civic education. If platforms genuinely believe their recommendations serve users, they can explain the benefit and ask. A meaningful opt-in can describe the category, remain reversible, and avoid nagging.
[P1.6] What CON defends is not freedom to speak, but a commercial privilege to keep interrupting people who never accepted the invitation.
[P1.7] The test is therefore simple: preserve political participation while withdrawing unconsented algorithmic power. Default opt-in does exactly that. It makes political discovery a citizen’s choice rather than an engagement system’s entitlement.
