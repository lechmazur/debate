[P1.1] A means-tested school lunch program is not actually a program that feeds poor children. It is a program that feeds poor children whose parents fill out the right form, in the right language, before the right deadline, and who are willing to be identified in front of their classmates as the kid on the list. Every one of those filters leaks. The families most likely to miss the paperwork are the ones in crisis: moving apartments, working two shifts, undocumented and afraid of any form with a government seal, or simply ashamed.
[P1.2] So the honest comparison in this debate is not "help everyone versus help the needy." It is a program that reliably reaches hungry kids against one that predictably misses a large share of them.
[P1.3] Second, the eligibility line itself is arbitrary and cruel at the margin. A cafeteria cashier ends up enforcing a cutoff where a family earning a dollar over the threshold pays full price while the family a dollar under pays nothing — and the family just above the line is often the one with no cushion at all. Universality abolishes the cliff. Nobody falls through a line that doesn't exist.
[P1.4] Third, means testing has real costs that its defenders quietly ignore. Someone must collect the applications, verify income, audit the paperwork, chase unpaid balances, and stigmatize children with lunch debt — sometimes by stamping their hands or handing them a cold cheese sandwich. Those administrative dollars buy no food. Strip out the bureaucracy and a meaningful share of the "savings" from targeting evaporates.
[P1.5] Fourth, and decisively: we already accept this principle everywhere else in school. We do not means-test the textbook, the chair, the heating, the bus, or the teacher's attention. We treat those as the conditions of learning. A child cannot read on an empty stomach any more than in the dark.
[P1.6] Food during the school day is instructional infrastructure, not charity — and infrastructure is properly universal.
[P1.7] The wealthy family's free lunch is not the scandal. The hungry child nobody flagged is.
