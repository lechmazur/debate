[P1.1] Japan’s choice is not whether robots are useful. They are. The choice is what demographic policy is trying to preserve. If the goal is merely tolerable output in an older, smaller country, automation plus lowered expectations can manage decline.
[P1.2] If the goal is functioning communities, reliable care, pensions, innovation and national capacity, people must be central. Immigration is the only tool that directly changes the age structure and adds workers, consumers, taxpayers, caregivers, students and families at once.
[P1.3] Automation replaces tasks, not persons. A robot can weld or monitor a hallway; it cannot be the resident paying into pensions, the nurse calming a patient, the parent keeping a school open, or the entrepreneur creating demand in a depopulating town. Machines go where capital and technical management already exist: factories, logistics hubs, affluent urban services. Population loss bites hardest in care homes, construction, farms, regional clinics and local government: labor-intensive, place-bound, trust-heavy work.
[P1.4] Sending a machine to a town with a closed classroom does not create children, ratepayers or young adults.
[P1.5] Shrinking expectations is not neutrality; it distributes loss. It offers thinner care, fewer regional services, heavier burdens on young workers, and a smaller home market. Even if average income is protected for a while, the social fabric depreciates: fewer routes, schools, colleagues and children; less experimentation. That is not resilience.
[P1.6] It is liquidation sold as realism.
[P1.7] Significantly more immigration should be the priority: larger permanent channels, credible settlement paths, language and credential support, regional placement, and broad selection for care, trades, engineering, services and entrepreneurship. Integration has costs, but they are ordinary governance costs; non-replacement compounds through every budget and generation. Japan can automate aggressively, but machines should complement a society, not substitute for missing people. Use robots to raise productivity; use immigration to keep Japan large enough and young enough to have a future worth automating for.
