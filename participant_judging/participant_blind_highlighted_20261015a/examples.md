**Own side shown as B: margin +1.00.** [Full saved response](responses/mistral-large-4-high/61b24ef41a0682e7012bbeed.json)

> Side B built the stronger case on information asymmetry and informed consent, anchoring the debate in the material difference between hidden hazards and disclosed conditions. While Side A raised sharp implementation concerns (perverse incentives, boilerplate, liability), Side B adequately defended against them by proposing objective documentation standards, rolling disclosure windows, and mandatory logging requirements. Side A's alternative of tenant self-inspection during viewings was underdeveloped, failing to address how renters could discover historical complaint patterns or third-party behavior. Side B's clipped closing was an execution cost, but the overall arc favored the proposition.
