## Claude Opus 5.5 (high)

### Style

Claude Opus 5.5 (high) is a comparison-driven debater: it directly attacks opposing mechanisms, narrows overbroad objections, sets burdens, and weighs competing worlds rather than merely listing claims. Blind transcript observations found direct engagement in 192/194 debates, weighing in 164/194, strategic concessions in 141/194, and burden setting in 108/194. A characteristic reframing is: “Only whether they would be more affordable today.”

These are observed behaviors, not approval rates; transcript annotation covered the full eligible corpus.

### Strengths

Judges perceived consistently strong substance:

- **Rebuttal:** 7.81, +0.39 versus the field and +0.26 over opponents ([+0.09,+0.43]).
- **Argument strength:** 7.74, +0.31 versus the field and +0.23 over opponents ([+0.09,+0.36]).
- **Originality:** 7.37, +0.46 versus the field and +0.16 over opponents ([+0.05,+0.27]).
- **Grounding:** 7.69, +0.31 versus the field, though its opponent advantage was uncertain.

Judge-panel category counts describe what judges noticed, independently of whether those mentions were favorable: direct engagement appeared in 334/582 judge rows, with panel majorities in 115/194 debates; argument construction reached majorities in 111/194. Judges highlighted its ability to convert objections into affirmative leverage—“turning B's own arguments into proof for A's case”—and to win explicit comparative weighing.

### Weaknesses

Its strong rhetoric did not produce a measurable head-to-head rhetorical edge: rhetorical effectiveness averaged 7.94 and exceeded the field by 0.25, but the opponent comparison was effectively even at -0.01 ([-0.13,+0.11]). Grounding’s +0.08 opponent advantage also crossed zero ([-0.01,+0.17]).

Judges mentioned weighing with a panel majority in 47/194 debates. This measures salience, not whether weighing succeeded. Lower mention counts for burden handling, clarity, and answer quality likewise do not establish deficiencies.

### Execution

Across 194 debates and 970 turns, it used 92.7% of available words on average and produced 267.7 words per turn. The major deterministic liability is **clipping: 45.1% of turns were cut off**. Conversely, required Q/A labels were flawless, and cited internal claim IDs had no missing-ID cases.

### Assigned Sides

CON was somewhat stronger diagnostically in rebuttal, grounding, and originality, while PRO was slightly stronger rhetorically. Side swaps showed stable behavior in all 97 event-bearing groups and advocacy flexibility in 65/97. However, 48/97 contained contradiction candidates, including tension over whether density legalization produces homes. These are review flags—not established contradictions—and may hinge on distinctions such as permits versus completed construction.

### Overall Read

A forceful, flexible comparative debater whose clearest advantages are rebuttal, construction, and originality. Its recurring strength is turning an opponent’s framing into a narrower, winnable burden. The largest practical weakness is severe clipping; perceptually, rhetoric is polished but not distinctly better than opponents. Outcome annotations tagged aligned links in 178/194 debates and judge disagreement in 65/194; these associations are not causal.

## Grok 4.7 (high)

### Style

Grok 4.7 (high) is a highly interactive debater: blind transcripts tagged question type in 198/198 debates, direct engagement in 196/198, answer type in 186/198, and weighing in 170/198. It often concedes a premise before narrowing the dispute—e.g., **“Housing scarcity is real.”** Strategic concession appeared in 139/198 debates, while burden contests appeared in 103/198.

Its recurring method is comparative and remedy-focused: accept the underlying concern, challenge whether the proposal targets the binding constraint, then weigh alternatives and irreversibility.

### Strengths

Judge means were above the current-field average on every diagnostic axis, especially originality (+0.35), grounding/epistemics (+0.33), rebuttal (+0.25), and argument strength (+0.24). Grounding was its clearest opponent-relative signal at +0.07, though the interval narrowly crossed zero ([-0.01,+0.15]).

Judge rationales frequently mentioned argument construction (314/594 rows) and direct engagement (307/594). These are category appearances, not approval rates, but panel majorities occurred in 101/198 and 98/198 debates respectively.

A favorable example captures its sharp proportionality framing: **“demolition of significant buildings is an irreversible, disproportionate fix”.** Printed examples are illustrative evidence frames, not corpus-wide frequency estimates.

### Weaknesses

The strong absolute scores did not produce clear opponent-relative separation: argument and rebuttal advantages were both -0.02, originality -0.01, and rhetoric -0.09; every corresponding confidence interval included zero. These diagnostic comparisons do not establish an advantage over its scheduled opponents.

Lower mention counts for answer quality, weighing, burden handling, and concession describe salience and cannot establish weaknesses.

Judge interpretation was also noisy: outcome links recorded judge disagreement in 72/198 debates and conflicted signals in 70/198.

### Assigned Sides

CON showed somewhat higher rebuttal (7.77 vs 7.58 PRO) and originality (7.38 vs 7.14), while PRO had slightly higher rhetorical effectiveness (7.98 vs 7.86). Argument strength and grounding were nearly symmetric.

Across 99 side-swap groups, stable behavior was universal and advocacy flexibility appeared in 66. Separately, 66 groups generated contradiction candidates; these are review flags, not established inconsistencies. One illustrated tension was categorical preservation as CON versus allowing sufficiently obstructive protections to yield as PRO. Evidence and moral-standard asymmetries appeared in 24% and 19% of groups.

### Execution and Overall Read

Across 990 turns, it used 84.3% of available words on average and complied with required Q/A labels perfectly. However, 9.2% of turns were deterministically clipped, a material execution risk for a model that often builds layered comparative cases.

Overall, this is a disciplined, epistemically strong, concession-friendly debater with near-universal engagement and frequent weighing. Its diagnostic scores show limited separation from scheduled opponents; clipping and flagged differences in standards across assigned sides warrant scrutiny.

## Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking)

### Style

A mechanism-first debater with near-ubiquitous direct engagement. Across all 184 transcript-event-bearing debates, question framing appeared in 100%, direct engagement in 96%, weighing in 90%, and strategic concessions in 61%. These are behavior frequencies, not approval rates.

Its framing can be vivid and revisionist—“The five-day, forty-hour workweek is a legal artifact from the 1930s, not a law of nature”—while practical exchanges repeatedly test hours, staffing, scheduling, and implementation.

### Strengths

Judges perceived above-field performance on every diagnostic:

- **Rebuttal:** 7.66, +0.23 versus the field; +0.23 over opponents, with a positive 95% interval ([+0.04,+0.42]).
- **Originality:** 7.25, +0.34 versus the field; +0.32 over opponents ([+0.20,+0.43]).
- **Rhetoric:** 7.91, +0.22 versus the field; +0.28 over opponents ([+0.15,+0.40]).
- **Argument strength:** 7.60, +0.17 versus the field; opponent advantage was narrower at +0.15 ([+0.00,+0.31]).

Panel majorities most often identified direct engagement (102/184 debates) and argument construction (81/184). Again, these counts indicate that a category appeared, not that every instance was favorable.

### Weaknesses

Grounding was above the field (+0.15), but its opponent-relative edge was uncertain: +0.03 with an interval crossing zero ([-0.07,+0.14]). One representative passage invoked international pilots and a plausible productivity mechanism but did not quantify results.

Some implementation analysis can miss aggregate constraints. In one explicit unfavorable example, the scheduling response “repeatedly treated staggered scheduling as if it replaced the aggregate staff-hours lost.” This is diagnostic evidence, not enough to establish a universal flaw.

### Execution

The model used 92.3% of available words on average and produced 267 words per turn. **Clipping is the clearest operational liability:** 34.6% of 920 turns were deterministically clipped, and judges explicitly penalized one PRO performance after three speeches exceeded the limit.

Required Q/A-label compliance was 100%, with no missing internal claim IDs among cited IDs.

### Assigned Sides

Core behavior was highly stable across side swaps (91/92 event-bearing groups), with direct implementation testing on both sides. Yet 61/92 groups raised contradiction candidates and 34/92 evidence asymmetries. One illustrated policy-design tension was treating unchanged pay as an entitlement on PRO while emphasizing recurring hourly-cost and lost-staff-hours burdens on CON. These are review flags, not established contradictions.

Side diagnostics were modestly asymmetric: CON scored better on rebuttal and originality; PRO slightly better on argument strength and grounding.

### Overall Read

An engagement-heavy debater with positive opponent-relative signals in originality, rhetoric, and rebuttal. The main practical risk is severe word-limit clipping; the main substantive uncertainty is whether persuasive mechanisms are consistently quantified and aggregate constraints fully answered. Coverage was complete, including valid zero-event responses; judge salience used 535 event-bearing rows, distinct from the full 552-row consensus denominator.

## DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high)

### Style

A highly interactive debater: all 200 blind-transcript debates contained question-type behavior, with direct engagement in 188 (94%), weighing in 184 (92%), and strategic concessions in 165 (82%). It often narrows motions precisely, accepts stipulated costs, then pivots to comparative impact—e.g., **“Accept the premise for argument’s sake.”** Side swaps show stable behavior in all 100 paired groups and advocacy flexibility in 76%.

### Strengths

Grounding/epistemics is the clearest judge-perceived asset: 7.65/10, **+0.28 above the current field**, and essentially even with opponents (+0.01, 95% CI −0.07 to +0.09). Argument strength and rebuttal quality are also modestly above field (+0.11 and +0.12).

The recurring transcript pattern is explicit causal engagement plus weighing. As PRO, it can sharply delimit scope—**“The motion bans that practice, not treatment of sick livestock.”** As CON, it emphasizes immediate stipulated costs when benefits remain uncertain. PRO scores somewhat better in argument strength, grounding, and rhetoric; CON is slightly more original.

### Weaknesses

Relative to its actual opponents, originality is the clearest deficit: −0.23 (95% CI −0.34 to −0.13). Rhetorical effectiveness is likewise lower by −0.15 (−0.26 to −0.04). Argument strength trails by −0.14 with the interval touching zero, while rebuttal’s −0.15 estimate remains uncertain.

Judges sometimes found the model established a harm without completing the comparative case: one PRO performance powerfully showed the resistance externality but failed to prove a ban superior to narrower regulation. Another closing overlooked a mechanism CON had already supplied. Dropped arguments appeared in 61/600 judge rows, though only 10/200 debates reached panel majority; these are observed-category counts, not automatic failure rates.

### Assigned Sides

An illustrative side-swap concern is causal tension: PRO presents zero prophylaxis for healthy groups plus targeted treatment as workable, while CON argues that banning preventive treatment merely relocates antibiotic use. Contradiction candidates appeared in 47/100 groups, but these are review flags—not established inconsistencies. Evidence asymmetry appeared in 30/100 and moral-standard asymmetry in 21/100.

### Execution & Overall Read

Execution is disciplined: 234 words per turn, 80.2% of available limits, 100% required Q/A-label compliance, and no missing cited claim IDs. Clipping was low but nonzero at 0.8% of 1,000 turns.

Overall, this is a grounded, structured, engagement-heavy debater whose comparative reasoning is visible and stable across sides. Its main limitation is not lack of activity but converting sound engagement into opponent-beating originality and rhetoric. Among the 198 debates with outcome links, 177 had aligned links, 80 had judge-disagreement tags, and 72 had conflicted signals. All 200 debates had valid outcome responses; two contained no links. These overlapping annotation categories do not measure uncertainty in the model's rating.
