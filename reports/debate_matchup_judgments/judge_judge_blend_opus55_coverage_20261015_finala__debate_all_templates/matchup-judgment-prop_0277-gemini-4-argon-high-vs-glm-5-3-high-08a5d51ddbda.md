### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won the central comparison: dispersal only relieves overloaded districts if visitors meaningfully substitute away from peak trips, while induced off-season visits merely increase total tourism and spread its costs. Side A identified flexible travelers who could substitute, but then weakened that answer by defending February trips that would not otherwise occur in July. B also answered the attack on caps by framing capacity limits and pricing as direct congestion management that preserves residential refuges. A remained persuasive on seasonal employment and concentrated harm, but never adequately proved that its policy would reduce the peak rather than fill the trough and widen exposure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response to A4.2 was decisive: a newly induced February visitor who was never coming in July cannot relieve July congestion. That turned A’s own clarification against its dose-control claim and gave B the cleanest reason to prefer direct peak caps over citywide promotion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing that dispersal across seasons and neighborhoods largely induces additional trips rather than redirecting peak demand from overloaded historic cores, undermining the proposition's central mechanism. B consistently grounded its case in real estate economics and infrastructure scale, while effectively reframing caps and fees as intelligent boundary management rather than failure. Side A's admission in Rebuttal 2 that off-season visitors are "induced trips at the margin" who would not otherwise come during peak season gave B a decisive lever to show dispersal increases total dose without relieving the worst congestion. A's opening clip and inability to recover from the induced-trip concession left B with the cleaner comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's seasonal dispersal argument by seizing on A's admission that off-season visitors are induced marginal trips who would not otherwise come during peak season, proving dispersal adds total tourism rather than flattening the curve that matters.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the central substitution clash. PRO claims dispersal gives dose control and relief for overloaded cores, but in Rebuttal 2 admits February visitors are induced not substituted [B4.2], which CON weaponizes [A4.3][A5.1] to show dispersal leaves July peak intact while adding total load elsewhere. CON's rent-gap logic - cheap outlying housing is most vulnerable because low nightly rates still outbid local wages [A2.1][A3.2] - survives PRO's regulation-plus-dispersal reply, and CON answers PRO's revenue-loss pressure by reframing caps/fees as filtering low-spend visitors [A4.1]. PRO's flatter-curve and underused-corridor benefits never overcome the intact-peak problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's use of B's own A1 in [B4.2] - 'why a February festival-goer would otherwise have come in July' and 'induced trips at the margin are the point' - turned into fatal admission in [A4.3] and [A5.1] that dispersal adds dose rather than relieves peak, directly undermining PRO's core dose-control route.
