- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by decisively flipping Side A's core 'proprietary roadmap' argument, pointing out that keeping a map of failed trials secret necessitates experimenting on human subjects twice for the same failed outcome. Side B also effectively exposed the contradiction in Side A's claims that regulators protect patients while simultaneously maintaining the secrecy required to hide poor efficacy from prescribing doctors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly turned Side A's 'roadmap of failure' point against them, arguing that hoarding the map just guarantees future patients will be experimented on twice and small firms will burn cash repeating the exact same dead ends.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on prescribing-level information asymmetry: regulators do not prescribe, selective publication still shapes journals/guidelines, and patient harm is wrong prescriptions from incomplete evidence. B answered pressure more cleanly (trivial admin cost vs. loss of spin) and crystallized the choice better.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s property/roadmap rebuttal (failed endpoint is not a copyable formula; the map that saves rivals also stops patients being experimented on twice) against A’s free-ride/expropriation claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger path to victory by showing that confidential regulatory review cannot fix publication bias in real-world prescribing, making honest disclosure a legitimate condition of market privilege.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's calorie-label analogy in Rebuttal 2, which answered Side A's Q1 by showing that uploading a results summary is administratively trivial and only "expensive" if the business model depends on selective deception—sharply neutralizing the triviality-versus-ruin contradiction.
