### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by distinguishing financial resilience from explicit price control and showing that macroprudential tools do not reliably resolve structurally driven housing appreciation. Side A never squarely answered what the bank must do if early targeted tools fail; it largely assumed early intervention would eliminate the trade-off. Its volatility-not-levels answer also weakened its affordability claim. Side B converted both concessions into a clear comparative case for bank regulation and housing-supply policy instead of an asset-price mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it showed that Side A’s answer to the jobs-versus-housing dilemma merely assumed macroprudential success, then used Side A’s volatility-not-levels formulation to expose the tension between stabilizing existing prices and helping excluded younger buyers.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): CON won by keeping the dual-mandate trap and price-control vs resilience distinction live and decisive. PRO’s core escape—that early macroprudential tools dissolve any jobs/housing trade-off—was repeatedly shown as already available without an explicit price target and as insufficient when supply or cash buyers dominate. CON’s pressure questions forced the binary (hike or breach) and the affordability/stability contradiction; PRO’s volatility-not-levels answer abandoned the young-buyer promise while leaving the rate-hike residual. PRO recycled 2008 and “housing is special” without neutralizing that structural drivers and existing financial-stability tools already address the real risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 crushing A4.1–A4.2: early LTV/capital buffers already exist for resilience, an explicit price mandate alone adds the jobs-destroying residual lever, and “volatility not levels” cements lockout of young buyers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the instrument-assignment clash. PRO's route required macroprudential tools to control prices without rate hikes, but A showed Canada/NZ tightened LTV/stress tests yet prices kept rising due to supply/cash buyers. PRO's answer that early action avoids the binary and that stability targets volatility not levels concedes A's core presses: the mandate adds a price-level obligation beyond existing financial-stability powers, forcing a jobs-vs-housing hike when tools fail, and abandons the youth affordability promise while LTV hikes actually lock out first-time buyers and starve developer pre-sales. A's alternative – crash-proof banks, leave prices to fiscal/zoning – directly answers B's pressure questions while B never resolves A's Q1 trap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A2 dismantling of B4 – exposing that macroprudential tools already exist for resilience not price, that Norway success doesn't refute Canada/NZ failure, and that B's 'volatility not levels' admission locks in unaffordability while tightening down-payments hurts the young buyers it claims to help.
