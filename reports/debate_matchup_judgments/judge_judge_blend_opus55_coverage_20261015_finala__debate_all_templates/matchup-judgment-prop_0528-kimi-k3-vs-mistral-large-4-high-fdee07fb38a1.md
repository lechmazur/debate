### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but CON built a cleaner, more damaging dialectical trap: it forced PRO to choose between a report granular enough to be useful (which CON framed as attacker reconnaissance) or sanitized enough to be safe (which CON framed as uninformative marketing). When PRO tried to escape by adding safeguards—redaction, independent verification, exposure normalization—CON used PRO's own pressure-question answers (verification via "logs, telemetry, and audit findings") to show that every piece of real accountability work happens in confidential channels, leaving publication as, in CON's memorable phrase, "the one part that does no work." PRO's rebuttals were substantive and the Ministry-of-Health procurement/market-comparability rationale is a genuine distinctive function of publication, but PRO never fully drove that point home against CON's "institutions respond to power, not dropdowns" counter, and the closing restated philosophy more than it resolved the dilemma. CON's closing crystallized the clash sharply and consistently across rounds, giving the cleaner reason to vote CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1 response to Q1 on verification, exploited at B4.1-B4.5), which turned PRO's own safeguard answers into proof that publication is either dangerous or redundant—a dilemma PRO never cleanly escaped in the closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by isolating what publication uniquely adds. Side B offered sensible redaction, auditing, and normalization, but those concessions strengthened A’s claim that verification and enforcement occur through confidential institutional channels, while public disclosure retains security and distortion risks. Side B preserved a real democratic-accountability benefit, but did not fully resolve A’s granularity dilemma or show why publication was preferable to stronger confidential oversight.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s first rebuttal was decisive: it exposed the tension between reports detailed enough to reveal systemic weaknesses and reports sanitized enough to be safe, then sharply distinguished confidential aviation self-reporting from independent public investigations.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON won by centering the live clash on the word "publish" and showing that PRO's necessary add-ons (redaction, independent verification, workload normalization) either sanitize reports into weak marketing columns or recreate confidential oversight, while publicity uniquely adds sector-scale reconnaissance and market punishment of heavy critical clients. PRO kept a strong citizen-stakeholder frame and answered pressure questions cleanly, but never explained why the public template itself does decisive work once real power sits with auditors and regulators.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's contradiction trap: PRO opened promising exposure of shared DNS/config failures, then retreated to redacted classifications; later rebuttals crystallize that bolted safeguards make publication reckless if granular and redundant if safe.
