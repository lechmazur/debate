### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by maintaining the motion’s limited scope and making the correct comparison: imperfect, auditable technology versus fallible unaided perception, not machines versus perfect officials. Side A raised legitimate concerns about anchoring, calibration, and embedded judgment, but overreached by claiming that no objective calls exist and repeatedly conflated defining a rule with measuring facts under that rule. B directly answered both pressure questions, offered constrained override triggers, and showed why residual interpretation does not erase the comparative gains in accuracy and reviewability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s distinction between defining the relevant event and measuring positions at that event was decisive. It undercut A’s central claim that any prior human rule definition makes line, clock, or positional measurements fundamentally subjective, while preserving humans for genuinely contextual judgments.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly dismantling Side A's premise that objective calls are purely subjective interpretations. Side B successfully argued that while finding the exact moment of an event (like a pass) is difficult, it remains a factual determination best served by testable technology rather than unaided human perception. Side B's closing crystallization that 'responsibility after an unseeable error is not fairness; it is ritual blame' decisively defeated Side A's accountability argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side A's strongest point about the subjectivity of 'identifying the moment of the pass' by pointing out that even if it's hard, doing it with synchronized, freezable cameras is inherently more reliable than asking a human to perceive two different locations on the field simultaneously at full speed.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's scope intact - measurable facts like line, crossing, clock - while CON tried to dissolve that category by calling timing itself subjective. A answered pressure directly: synchronized multi-angle video can freeze the release frame and flag low confidence, which is still more checkable than a single full-speed glance, and override limited to malfunction/conflicting video with a stated reason creates a reviewable record versus ritual blame. CON never offered a more reliable human method and relied on structural anchoring and black-box claims without proving unaided eyes are more accountable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 on timing: showing the 'moment played' is a factual determination best settled by synchronized, auditable video versus invisible split-second perception, and that requiring officials to cite what the system missed turns override into a reviewable safeguard not blind deference.
