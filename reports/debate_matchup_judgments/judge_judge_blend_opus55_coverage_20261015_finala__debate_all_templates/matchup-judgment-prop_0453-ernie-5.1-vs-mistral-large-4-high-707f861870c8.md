B's closing crystallized the clash more cleanly around the mandate and the false dichotomy, while A's closing largely reasserted its asymmetry framing without engaging B's strongest new mechanism (rapid credit restriction). The result is a clear, competitive win for B.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-argued cases, but A sustained a cleaner central weighing device—permanent structural exclusion (housing) versus reversible, insurance-cushioned cyclical pain (recession)—and defended it consistently from opening through closing. A's answers to B's pressure questions (A1/A2) directly engaged the hysteresis and "managed correction" objections rather than dodging them. B landed strong points (macroprudential tools as substitutes, the 2008 analogy, hysteresis effects), but under A's pressure question about Canada/New Zealand needing rate hikes anyway, B's answer ("not because tools failed... but because overheating proved severe") subtly conceded that rate hikes become necessary when targeted tools are insufficient—undercutting B's own claim that the tradeoff the motion describes is avoidable. B's broader strategy of denying the necessity of the tradeoff, while legitimate, left its direct comparative weighing (recession harm vs. housing harm) slightly thinner than A's. The historical dueling over 2008 causation was a wash, but A's reversible/irreversible framing proved more resilient under cross-examination.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's Pressure Q1 (A4.1), distinguishing temporary reemployment from permanent housing-driven exclusion, versus B's answer to A's Pressure Q1 (B4.1), which inadvertently conceded that rate hikes are used precisely when macroprudential tools prove insufficient—undermining B's claim that the proposition's tradeoff is avoidable.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing housing inflation as a permanent, structural harm compared to the cyclical, reversible nature of recessions. B also effectively dismantled Side A's reliance on macroprudential tools by pointing out they are insufficient on their own to cool aggregate demand.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal and subsequent pressure question exposing that Canada and New Zealand had to use rate hikes anyway, proving macroprudential tools are complements rather than complete substitutes.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A established a clearer moral and structural hierarchy—temporary cyclical pain versus permanent wealth exclusion—and successfully turned B’s 2008 and macroprudential examples into evidence that delayed or insufficient action worsens outcomes, while B’s precision-tools alternative was undermined by its own Canada/NZ admission. A’s weighing of reversible recession against irreversible housing damage remained the live route to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer to B’s first pressure question (A4.1), drawing the decisive asymmetry between temporary joblessness and permanent housing exclusion.
