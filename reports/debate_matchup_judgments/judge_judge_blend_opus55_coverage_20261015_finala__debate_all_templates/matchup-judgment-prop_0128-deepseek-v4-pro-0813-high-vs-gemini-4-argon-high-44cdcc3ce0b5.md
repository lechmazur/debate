### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and complied with the pressure-question format, but CON built the more durable mechanism. PRO's strongest move was forcing CON to concede the degree is only a "probabilistic proxy," then arguing that proxy can't justify a hard bar. CON absorbed that by reframing the clash as "which proxy performs better at scale," and then landed the decisive blow: when PRO's own rubric examples (self-reported home troubleshooting, unpaid volunteer work, unproctored prompts) were examined concretely, CON showed they are unverifiable and trivially gameable, meaning they fail to actually narrow the applicant pool and instead push hiring back toward referrals and insider networks — the exact inequality outcome PRO was trying to prevent. PRO's rebuttal to this ("not every rejection reason is equally fair") restates the necessity framing but never rebuilds a concrete, scalable alternative that survives CON's gameability critique. PRO's closing point that CON's leadership-pipeline standard risks becoming unfalsifiable is a fair hit, but it doesn't erase CON's unanswered structural claim that stripping a transparent credential at scale predictably produces worse, more opaque discrimination. That mechanism, sustained and sharpened from opening through closing, gives CON the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 attack on PRO's own rubric examples (B4.3–B4.4), showing that self-reported troubleshooting/volunteer claims and unproctored prompts are unverifiable and automatable, directly undercut PRO's central claim that job-relevant alternatives can replace the degree at scale, and PRO's closing never fully recovered from it.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won by keeping the motion focused on whether degree completion is genuinely connected to performance. Side B created serious pressure over application volume, gaming, and informal networks, but repeatedly treated hiring convenience and a rough general signal as sufficient grounds for an automatic exclusion. Its claims that degree filters are the only scalable meritocratic option were asserted too strongly, while A supplied plausible job-relevant alternatives and explained why imperfect proxies are not interchangeable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.5 was decisive: A answered that all screens may be probabilistic, but a task-specific cutoff rejects candidates for a performance-relevant reason whereas a degree requirement often rejects them for unrelated educational history. This directly undercut B's attempt to equate every imperfect filter.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) kept the debate anchored to the proposition's necessity standard. Side A (CON) effectively conceded the degree is only a "probabilistic proxy" and shifted to scale/convenience. B's Pressure Qs exposed that shift - Q1 bar vs optional factor and Q2 leadership pipeline making the motion impossible - and B's R2 answered A's pressure concretely with job-relevant rubrics (certifications, scenario prompts) while drawing the key comparison: not all proxies are equal, job-relevant vs background advantage. A's R2 conceded technical roles rightly drop degrees, supporting the motion, and never rehabilitated necessity for the remaining generalist roles.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 (B4.3-B4.4) - distinguishing imperfect screens by job-relevance and supplying a non-title-locked rubric to break the catch-22, turning A's "every screen is a proxy" into a justification for arbitrary filtering.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO built a clear causal story (degree inflation, hidden turnover/wage costs, cheap alternative screens) and used concrete, relatable examples throughout. The decisive exchange was the Pressure Questions round: PRO's Q1 forced CON to defend "bachelor's required; equivalent experience considered," and PRO's closing correctly exposed the tension in that phrase—if equivalent experience can satisfy the posting, the degree is not truly "required," which is functionally what the motion asks for. CON's distinction (default-with-waiver vs. elimination) is conceptually coherent but reads as a retreat that concedes much of PRO's ground, and CON never fully neutralized the turnover/wage-premium harms from degree inflation raised in A1.3. CON's strongest material—the thin-file/early-career applicant problem and the "snapshot vs. trajectory" framing—was original and pressed well, but PRO's answer (alternative signals like certs, portfolios, retention, plus the degree remaining usable as one signal) adequately blunted it without fully collapsing under CON's follow-up. Overall PRO narrowed the clash more effectively in the final rounds and gave the cleaner reason to vote for it, while CON's closing mostly reasserted earlier points without fully resolving the "required vs. waived" contradiction PRO had surfaced.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 pressure question ("haven't they stopped requiring the degree—conceding the motion?") and its exploitation in Rebuttal 2/Closing, forcing CON into the semantically strained "default with narrow waiver" position that PRO then framed as a concession.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won the central distinction between considering a degree as useful evidence and requiring it as an exclusionary condition. Side B raised a credible concern about cheaply screening thin-file applicants, but repeatedly treated ending the mandate as removing the degree from consideration altogether. Side A showed that employers can still credit degrees while admitting alternative signals, directly advancing the proposition. Its unsupported turnover and wage claims, along with several incorrect reference IDs, reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest turn was that “bachelor’s required; equivalent experience considered” effectively concedes that degree completion is not indispensable. Its cloud-support example also exposed how Side B’s default can reject proven capability while an optional degree remains available as a positive signal.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won the core clash on signal vs mandate. It showed degree filters act as automated gates causing shortages and deterrence, while a degree can still be credited as one signal without being required. Side A’s middle-ground of “required; equivalent considered on review” was exposed by B’s Q1 as conceding the motion, and A never explained why a requirement preserves predictive information better than a preference. The thin-file concern was directly answered with alternative signals and finalist-only testing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Q1 that fixing the auto-reject equals stopping the requirement, and A4's attempt to defend “bachelor's required; equivalent considered” as still a requirement collapsed logically and anchored B's closing.
