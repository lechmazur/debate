### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a clear but competitive debate by forcing a clean decisive frame: scientific verification and reuse do not require irrevocable commercial open licenses. CON’s non-commercial/academic-license alternative answered PRO’s strongest goals (read, check, extend flood models and conclusions) while preserving university leverage for tech transfer, preferential startup terms, and reversible commercial rights. PRO’s burden-of-justification framing and productivity/accountability case were strong and disciplined, but were damaged by the timing catch-22 in computational fields (code/data as core asset before commercial partners exist) and by the inconsistency between attacking “speculative” exceptions and then accepting time-limited “credible reason” carve-outs. Across later rounds CON converted pressure into a sharper irreversibility-vs-managed-friction comparison; PRO never fully killed the non-commercial alternative or the claim that a permission gate can still keep scientific access immediate. That left CON with the cleaner route to vote against the mandate as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3–A4.4 / A5.2: CON’s catch-22 on PRO’s exceptions—lenient “credible reason” carve-outs recreate CON’s world with extra bureaucracy, while strict ones force irreversible surrender of code/data before commercial value can be known—most decisively undercut PRO’s default-plus-exceptions path.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by driving a wedge between scientific openness and irrevocable commercial open licensing. PRO wanted verification and reuse, but CON showed academic/non-commercial sharing delivers that while preserving tech-transfer leverage and avoiding irreversible giveaways of code-as-asset. PRO's time-limited exception answer to B3 Q1 contradicted its own warning against speculative exceptions, and CON's reversibility asymmetry – a permission gate can be waived, an open license cannot – left PRO's default looking riskier than tailored sharing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 collapse + reversibility: that PRO's credible-reason exception either recreates CON's world with bureaucracy or forces premature surrender, and that non-commercial sharing is reversible while open licenses are not.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A successfully bifurcated "scientific access" from "commercial open licensing," demonstrating that the mandate forces an irrevocable commercial giveaway that harms tech transfer and favors large incumbents. Side A's "timing trap" argument—highlighting that basic researchers cannot predict commercial viability upfront to secure exceptions—was decisive and effectively neutralized Side B's default mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's "credible reason" exception as a catch-22 and contrasted the reversibility of non-commercial licensing with the irrevocability of open licenses.
