### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): B won a clear but competitive debate by maintaining the crucial distinction between evidence and the legal standard. Certified readings need not conclusively identify the source or independently prove nuisance to provide useful evidence of timing, intensity, and pattern alongside contextual judgment. A exposed genuine concerns about cost, privacy, and B's inconsistent account of affordable certification, but its claims that greater weight necessarily creates a de facto bar and that monitoring must be either useless or invasive were too categorical. A's neutral-officer alternative also did not establish why private certified evidence should remain comparatively restricted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response that the meter supplies a factual foundation rather than replacing contextual nuisance analysis most decisively answered A's central attack. A's memorable trilemma damaged B's accessibility claims, but it did not defeat the narrower case for treating imperfect yet probative evidence more seriously.

- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B won by decisively dismantling Side A's core mechanism. Side B effectively demonstrated that a noise monitor capable of source attribution in a multi-unit building inherently requires audio surveillance, while a "dumb" decibel meter fails to prove nuisance or identify the source. Side B also won the evidentiary clash, proving that granting "more weight" to certified logs creates a de facto bar for poorer tenants relying on diaries—a point Side A inadvertently conceded by admitting diaries would lack a "presumption of reliability." Side A's closing retreat to "the motion is permissive" failed to address the practical realities of comparative evidentiary weight and landlord behavior.
