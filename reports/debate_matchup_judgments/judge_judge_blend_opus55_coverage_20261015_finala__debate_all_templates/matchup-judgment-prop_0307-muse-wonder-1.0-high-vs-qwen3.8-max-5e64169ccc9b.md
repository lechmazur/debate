### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by showing how a home-first presumption can delay necessary placement until foreseeable risks become actual injuries. B also sustained the caregiver-backup and service-coordination burdens more effectively than A answered them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s “that model learns by injury” response was decisive. It directly used Side A’s own placement thresholds to undermine the claim that home-first safely escalates before crisis, then weighed preventable physical harm against the acknowledged but generally less irreversible costs of an earlier planned move.

Side B also maintained a strong, unrefuted argument about the permanent "on-call" burden placed on families, ultimately winning the risk asymmetry weighing in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was the decisive moment, specifically the sequence from B4.3 to B4.4 where B dismantled Side A's threshold by pointing out that waiting for a "pattern" of falls or medication errors means the model "learns by injury," since the first event can easily be catastrophic.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): PRO wins by grounding the motion's "when possible" clause as a burden-of-proof rule: displacement must follow a specific, unmanageable risk, not merely foreseeable risk. CON's "learning by injury" and family-caregiver critiques stay live but are neutralized by PRO's demonstration that institutions impose their own severe harms and that home-first means robust state support, not family sacrifice alone. PRO's closing crystallizes the clash cleanly, while CON, despite strong rhetoric, never fully justifies why autonomy should be provisional on proving a risk-free existence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The clash over whether home-first means "learning by injury" until patterns of harm emerge (CON) or whether institutions merely trade one risk for another while PRO's escalation criteria catch problems prospectively (PRO), resolved by PRO's closing burden-of-proof frame.
