### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by making irreversibility the decisive comparison: imprisonment supplies incapacitation while preserving the possibility of correcting error, whereas execution adds permanence mainly for retribution. Side B's proportionality case was forceful but repeatedly assumed correct application or better safeguards without resolving inevitable error, politically abused terrorism charges, or why execution's marginal benefit outweighs those risks. A's occasional incorrect reference IDs were an execution flaw but did not undermine the substantive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the “perfectionism” charge was decisive: fallibility does not require abolishing every sanction because imprisonment is necessary and potentially reversible, while execution provides no additional incapacitation and makes mistakes irreparable. Side B never supplied an equally strong answer to that distinction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) built the decisive live route: irreversibility. Maximum-security LWOP already incapacitates, so execution adds zero safety while making panic-driven wrongful convictions and authoritarian abuse of 'terrorism' laws permanent. B exposed CON's (A) contradictions - distrusting prisons to stop letters yet trusting them to kill under 'safeguards' - and answered retribution with the torturing-torturers symmetry point. CON never overcame the reversible vs irreversible distinction and relied on 'comfort' framing that PRO neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4/B5 hammer on irreversibility: 'You cannot release a corpse' vs A's car-mechanic analogy, turning A's A2.1 'judge it when applied correctly' concession into proof PRO's policy fails in the real world of fallible, panicked courts and weaponized terrorism statutes.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by building a tighter, more logically consistent case and successfully trapping Side A in a fatal contradiction regarding state competence. Side B sharply pointed out that if a state is too incompetent to stop prison correspondence, it cannot be trusted with irreversible lethal power. Side A's attempt to defend this was weak, and Side A made a critical unforced error by comparing wrongful execution to a "sloppy mechanic" denting a car, which Side B devastatingly dismantled in the closing. Side A also misattributed its own opening claim to Side B during the rebuttals. While Side A's rhetorical framing of proportionality was strong and memorable, Side B's superior rebuttals, cleaner execution, and decisive handling of the irreversibility clash secure the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's "dented car" analogy in the closing, highlighting that a wrongfully executed human cannot be repaired like a car, perfectly crystallized the irreversibility clash. Additionally, Side B's pressure questions exposing the contradiction in trusting the state to flawlessly execute inmates while simultaneously claiming the state is too incompetent to stop them from sending letters was the most decisive tactical exchange.
