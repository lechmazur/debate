### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but CON landed the single most damaging blow of the debate: the claim that random mutagenesis is already screened by generations of phenotypic selection, while a precise, targeted edit bypasses that natural winnowing and can silently alter biochemistry undetected. This directly undercut PRO's central supporting analogy (that mutagenesis exemptions justify lighter treatment of characterized edits), and PRO's rebuttal to it (A4.2, "characterized directly") was a thin restatement that never actually engaged the selection-winnowing mechanism. CON also maintained a more internally consistent position throughout ("product-based" means biological consequence, not tool, with no method-based exemption at all), which survived pressure better than PRO's framework, which relied on method as a proxy for predictability even while claiming to be purely product-based. PRO's clever reframe in A1 ("not regulating lighter, just not regulating heavier") was rhetorically sharp but did not resolve the deeper tension CON exposed. PRO's points on access for small breeders and climate urgency were reasonable but contested to a rough draw, leaving CON's core rebuttal as the decisive, unanswered thread carrying the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's selection-winnowing argument (B2.2, reinforced in B4.1) against PRO's mutagenesis-exemption analogy, which PRO never adequately answered in Rebuttal 2.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively framing the debate around regulatory proportion, distinguishing between the necessary safety testing of a product and the irrelevant transgenic-specific data burdens currently applied to native gene edits. Side B's insistence that Side A wanted lighter regulation than conventional breeding was expertly dismantled in A's Rebuttal 2, leaving Side B's core comparative claim severely damaged.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A1 in Rebuttal 2, which flipped Side B's question about conventional breeding to show that current GMO rules are method-based overregulation, while Side A's proposal simply seeks regulatory parity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by framing the issue as regulatory proportion: non-transgenic gene edits lack the foreign DNA and vector sequences that justify the heaviest GMO dossier, so forcing them through the transgenic pipeline is method-based overregulation. Side B raised valid concerns about silent systemic effects and regulatory creep but overstated by calling this "deregulation," since A consistently affirmed a limited pathway would still require molecular characterization, allergen screening, and environmental testing. A's mutagenesis comparison exposed a real asymmetry in current rules, and A answered B's pressure questions more cleanly by showing the alternative is an arbitrary penalty on precision. The edge is clear but narrow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 answer to the mutagenesis comparison, arguing precise edits in conserved regulatory genes can silently rewrite biochemistry in ways random mutations cannot because they bypass phenotypic winnowing, was the most damaging exchange, though Side A partially recovered by noting the limited pathway still requires safety screening.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides executed the format well, but CON (B) built a tighter, more damaging line of attack. B's core move—forcing PRO into a dilemma where either the streamlined track retains every substantive test (so nothing is actually "streamlined") or it cuts time, and the cut time is precisely where low-frequency ecological harms like gene flow, dormancy, and weediness surface—was never fully resolved by PRO. PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing kept asserting that environmental risk assessment remains mandatory, but never explained how a faster "proportionate" process can still capture the multi-year, field-exposure evidence B identified as the actual function of the "decade-long dossier." PRO's strongest moves (product vs. process reframing, Japan/Argentina/Brazil examples, equating native deletions to mutagenesis) were met with B's sharp counter that using conventional breeding's near-zero baseline as the ceiling undercuts PRO's own claim of rigor, and that "substantial equivalence" is an inference, not a positive safety finding. B also sustained the unresolved irreversibility/indistinguishability point (no recall once released) through to the closing, while PRO's answer (treating it as a "trade-coordination" issue) didn't fully neutralize the biosafety angle. PRO's case was coherent and well-presented but left the decisive tension live; B's case progressively narrowed the debate onto that tension and closed on it cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1–B4.6), crystallized in the Closing exchange: the "if tests stay, only time is cut; if time is cut, the missing evidence is exactly what matters" dilemma, which PRO never squarely answered.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a fatal dilemma in Side A's proposal: if the streamlined track retains all necessary safety tests but removes the "decade-long dossier," it is cutting out the essential time required for longitudinal ecological observation. Side B also effectively turned Side A's point about indistinguishability into a permanent safety hazard regarding recalls.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal brilliantly redefined Side A's "bureaucratic delay" as the actual ecological release study, proving that cutting time inherently means cutting vital safety evidence.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a central tension in PRO's case: defending a process-defined "streamlined" lane for gene-edited crops undermined PRO's claim to product-based regulation. CON successfully showed that if regulation truly followed the product, no technique-specific track was needed, and that "streamlined" effectively cut the multi-year ecological observation time required to detect low-frequency environmental harms. CON also sharply reframed indistinguishability from conventional crops from a safety feature into an irreversibility and recall problem. PRO's climate urgency and category-error arguments were coherent and well-supported, but they never fully resolved why a gene-edited lane was necessary under genuine product-based logic, nor how retained "environmental risk assessments" replaced the longitudinal field exposure CON demonstrated was being eliminated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (B4), which identified the bypassed pre-market ecological release study and the instability of "substantial equivalence" as an operational threshold, directly damaging PRO's safety assurances and boundary definition.
