### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: whether firm-level four-day trials scale to a binding economy-wide mandate without raising unit labor costs. A’s continuous-demand arithmetic (cover the fifth day via overtime or new hires; weekends already use reduced services/premiums) outweighed B’s “output stayed flat so costs don’t rise” claim and hidden-turnover offset. B never fully neutralized selection bias, non-random trials, or macro effects on marginal employers. B’s “every trial worked” refrain and exact-percentage claims were overconfident for closed-book conditions, while A kept pressure on price-of-labor mechanics and global competition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.2 / A4.1–A4.2: even if per-worker output holds, continuous Friday demand forces 25% more paid hours (OT or hires); weekend rotation is not equivalent to full-service Friday coverage.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): B wins on the coverage/macro clash. PRO’s claim that same per-worker output means no cost increase was squarely hit by CON’s total wage-bill arithmetic: in time-bound sectors demand on Friday still requires overtime or new hires. PRO’s answers about idle capacity and turnover savings were assertive but did not explain full Friday staffing, while CON’s pressure on weekend skeleton staffing vs full Friday coverage and voluntary-trial selection bias remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 time-bound vs task-bound distinction and 25% total-wage-bill point — that identical per-worker output still raises total cost to cover a 5-day economy — directly undercut PRO’s core “no cost increase” line and survived PRO’s Rebuttal 2.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by decisively breaking Side B's reliance on micro-level trials. Side A's distinction between compressible (task-bound) and continuous (time-bound) sectors effectively neutralized PRO's claim that productivity gains universally offset the 25% hourly cost increase. Side A also won the macroeconomic clash by correctly pointing out that the absence of inflation in voluntary, localized trials does not disprove the macroeconomic shocks of a universal legal mandate. Side B's responses, particularly the claim that acute hospital wards have idle capacity that can be simply "rotated away," were analytically weak and easily dismantled by Side A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's claim about hospital idle capacity by distinguishing between scheduled elective care and unpredictable emergency demand, and nailed the micro-to-macro extrapolation fallacy.
