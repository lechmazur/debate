The result is a close but clear edge to CON on rebuttal quality and the decisiveness of the final clash.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but PRO stayed more tightly aligned with the motion's actual wording ("many" knowledge workers, not all), while CON's closing strategy repeatedly recast PRO's proof of gains for a substantial subset as merely a "subset gain, not the net increase the motion requires" — a move that quietly raises the bar beyond what the proposition asks. PRO's pressure question exposed a genuine tension in CON's case (office focus is both "fixable via quiet rooms" and the reason questions get "answered now"), and PRO's answer to pressure (A1/A2) cleanly reasserted a workable productivity metric (completed, defect-free deliverables) and a defensible reading of "many." CON's rebuttals were strong on coordination costs and apprenticeship, and effectively forced PRO to narrow toward hybrid arrangements, but CON never fully reconciled its own contradiction or decisively showed that coordination costs erase gains for the specific job categories (senior engineers, actuaries, compliance officers) PRO repeatedly cited as clearing the "many" threshold. PRO's closing crystallized the stakes more cleanly and kept the comparison anchored to the proposition as written, giving it a clear if not overwhelming edge.

CON's "subset gain" argument remained live but could not overcome PRO's clear burden that a substantial share of knowledge workers plainly benefit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal exposing the contradiction between CON's "quiet rooms and focus blocks" defense and their claim that offices win because questions get "answered now," showing that CON's ideal office reproduced remote delays while adding a pointless commute.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by effectively framing the debate around the marginal utility of the 4th and 5th days of a full-time office mandate. PRO's rebuttals sharply exposed the contradictions in CON's defense of office focus versus immediate availability, and PRO's rhetorical framing ("hallway lottery") made the comparative weighing decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's dismantling of CON's "quiet room vs. immediate answer" contradiction in Rebuttal 2, highlighting that an expert in a focus block cannot simultaneously be available for immediate triage, rendering the commute pointless for those waiting.
