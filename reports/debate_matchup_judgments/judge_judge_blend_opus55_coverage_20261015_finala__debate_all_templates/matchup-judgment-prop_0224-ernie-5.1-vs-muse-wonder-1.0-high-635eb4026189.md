### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): The decisive clash was whether "preventing backfill" counts as an emissions cut per dollar, and whether nameplate-capacity cost comparisons are fair without including firming costs. B pressed this directly with sharp pressure questions (Q1/Q2) forcing A to admit its $300M figure was "nameplate capacity" reflecting "today's prices" without full system costs [A4.2], which B then exploited: "CON offers no equivalent system-level proof: their figures are generation price and battery cost, not the delivered cost of replacing firm power" [B4.2]. B also turned A's own concession about "flexible gas peakers" [A2.3] into an admission that renewable-heavy grids still rely on fossil backup, which is a genuinely clever and damaging rebuttal move [B4.3]. A's response—that refurbishment "may not deliver additional output" and is "counterfactual accounting"—never fully answered why a plant that would otherwise close and be backfilled by gas doesn't count as emissions avoided; A kept asserting the distinction without dismantling B's gas-backfill mechanism. A's case rested heavily on falling renewable costs and fast deployment, which is a reasonable throughline but didn't engage as precisely with B's firm-power/intermittency framing in the final two rebuttals. Both sides executed the Q&A format correctly with proper A1/A2 labels. B's closing crystallized the clash more cleanly ("Speed is not how fast a panel is installed. Speed is how fast fossil generation actually falls") while A's closing largely restated earlier points without fully neutralizing B's gas-backfill and system-cost framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question exploiting A's "three to four gigawatts of solar-plus-storage" figure, forcing the admission in A4.2 that this was nameplate capacity without full transmission/storage costs, which B then leveraged in B4.2-B4.3 to argue CON never offered a true system-level cost comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won decisively by exposing the flaws in CON's framing of life-extension and firmness. PRO successfully argued that preventing a baseload nuclear plant from retiring is functionally an emissions cut compared to the gas backfill that would replace it. CON struggled to counter this, relying on the weak premise that the plant's continuation is a "hypothetical" counterfactual. Furthermore, PRO capitalized on CON's admission that renewable grids rely on "flexible gas peakers," brilliantly tying it back to PRO's core thesis about the hidden emissions and costs of intermittent power.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2, where they caught CON admitting that high-renewable grids rely on "flexible gas peakers," perfectly proving PRO's point that solar/wind leave evening fossil plants running.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by decisively controlling the definitions of "emissions speed" and "per public dollar." A effectively dismantled B's cost comparisons by highlighting the massive gap between nameplate renewable capacity and actual generation, alongside the hidden system costs of transmission and firming. A also neutralized B's semantic argument about "counterfactual" emissions by correctly framing the prevention of nuclear closures as direct avoidance of gas backfill. B's case was fatally undermined when it admitted that high-renewable grids still rely on "flexible gas peakers," which A exploited to prove that renewables merely work around fossil plants rather than replacing them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it pounced on Side B's admission of using "flexible gas peakers" to prove that high renewable penetration merely works around fossil plants rather than replacing them, shattering B's per-dollar emissions displacement claim.
