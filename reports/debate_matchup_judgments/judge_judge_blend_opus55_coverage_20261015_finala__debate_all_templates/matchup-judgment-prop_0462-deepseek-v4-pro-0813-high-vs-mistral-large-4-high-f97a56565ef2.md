### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Both sides opened with clear, well-structured cases—A on the equity/causal-story of tracking as early rationing, B on budget feasibility and the cumulative nature of math. The decisive exchange came in Rebuttal 1/2: B exposed a real arithmetic flaw in A's "redeploy the tracking staff into tutoring" mechanism—tracking doesn't multiply the number of teachers needed, it just sorts the same students into different rooms, so merging sections doesn't free capacity for genuinely "intensive" small-group tutoring. A's answer retreated to "phased rollout," which is an honest but damaging concession that the full-scale replacement envisioned by the motion may not be deliverable. B also landed the sharp reframe that tutoring aimed at filling prerequisite gaps functions as "a low track wearing a tutoring badge," undercutting A's claim that the proposal eliminates sorting rather than just relabeling it. A's strongest point—that age-11 tracking encodes prior privilege rather than true readiness—was never squarely refuted by B, but B's feasibility and sequencing critiques were more load-bearing because they attack whether the alternative can actually function as described, which is prior to any equity benefit materializing. A's closing reasserted hidden status-quo costs but never quantified or neutralized B's "order of magnitude" staffing gap or the "parallel track" reframe. B therefore ends the debate with the cleaner, less-answered route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 point that "tracking does not triple the teaching staff... you haven't freed up bodies, you've concentrated the spread," later reinforced in Rebuttal 2 by calling redeployed remedial staff "the same shortage" relabeled—this exposed the central mechanical flaw in A's funding/reallocation argument and was never fully resolved by A's "phased rollout" answer.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that detracking does not itself free teachers: the same number of students still require classroom staffing, while intensive small-group tutoring adds substantial labor. Side A never established that existing remedial resources could cover its promised dosage. B also persuasively argued that A's late reliance on out-of-class compacting shifted both the instructional and resource burden rather than preserving in-class acceleration. A made a strong equity case against rigid early sorting, but did not adequately prove that the proposed replacement could maintain advanced sequencing at scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal directly dismantled A's reallocation mechanism: merging tracked sections changes student composition but does not release the teachers still needed for those students. This left A's intensive-tutoring promise without a credible staffing path, and the later phased-rollout answer did not repair that deficit.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling the practical mechanics of Side B's proposal. Side A used simple arithmetic to prove that merging tracks does not free up teachers for tutoring, and effectively demonstrated that Side B's pedagogical solutions (parallel tutoring in class, compacted out-of-class work) amount to a hidden remedial track and unpaid student labor, respectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 decisively exposed the flaw in Side B's reallocation argument, pointing out that merging tracks concentrates the spread of students but does not reduce the number of teachers needed.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON built a tighter causal case: math is cumulative, teacher bandwidth is finite, and "intensive tutoring" is the scarcer, recurring resource while fair-entry tracking fixes are cheaper and bounded. CON's pressure question (Q2) exposed a real tension—PRO's "flexible differentiation within one classroom" for advanced students looks functionally like tracking under another name—and PRO's answer ("no separate track, no gate") was more assertion than resolution. CON also repeatedly weaponized PRO's own concession that tutoring, not heterogeneous grouping, does the equity work, turning it into a recurring "why not combine tutoring with fixed-gate tracking" attack that PRO never fully neutralized, instead falling back on normative framing about snapshots and ceilings. PRO's case remained strong on equity rhetoric and the "sorting vs. building readiness" frame, and its rebuttals were substantive, but Rebuttal 1 was clipped for length (a real execution cost), and the closing mostly recrystallized equity framing without re-engaging the cost/staffing or "tracking by another name" points CON had pressed. CON's closing was sharper, centering the debate on the unresolved concession and giving a cleaner reason to vote CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question Q2 ("what's the concrete path for an advanced student—doesn't compacted/accelerated work inside the class amount to tracking by another name?") combined with PRO's A2 answer, which conceded flexible internal grouping without fully explaining why this isn't functionally tracking, was the most decisive exchange, exposing a structural tension in PRO's model that was never fully closed.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won by isolating the proposition’s central implementation problem: tutoring could broaden access without eliminating advanced sections, while heterogeneous classrooms must continuously serve sharply different readiness levels. Side A offered a strong equity frame but never made its differentiation model convincing or explained why fair-entry tracking plus tutoring could not capture the same benefits. Its first rebuttal was also clipped. B slightly overstated that A admitted tutoring often fails, but this did not undo B’s superior resource and instructional-time comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure question asking why tutoring could not accompany fair-entry tracking was decisive. A replied that tracking still imposes exclusion, but did not establish why universal screening, retesting, bridges, and multiple entry points necessarily retain that exclusion. B then showed that its model concentrates support at the boundary while preserving advanced course sequences.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proposing a counter-model (fair-entry tracking via universal screening and summer bridges) that resolved Side B's equity concerns without sacrificing advanced instruction. Side A cleanly won the pressure question exchange by demonstrating that boundary fixes are cheaper and more efficient than recurring daily differentiation, while Side B's responses failed to fully engage Side A's actual model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 answers to the pressure questions (A4.1 and A4.3) decisively contrasted the temporary, localized cost and time of a summer bridge with the permanent, systemic cost of heterogeneous instruction.
