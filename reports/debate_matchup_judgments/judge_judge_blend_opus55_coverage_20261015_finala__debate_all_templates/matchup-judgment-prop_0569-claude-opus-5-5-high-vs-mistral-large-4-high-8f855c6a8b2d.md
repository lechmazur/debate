### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing the mandate’s central implementation gap: a legal duty imposed on chatbot companies cannot guarantee an available, affordable clinician. A also gave the cleaner comparison—risk-triggered transfer acts earlier when danger appears, while continued AI support avoids abandonment when human care is unavailable. Side B’s fixed cap offered a plausible backstop against missed signals, but its claim that the mandate would create clinical capacity depended on speculative partnerships, reimbursement, and workforce expansion not established by the proposition. A’s clipped second rebuttal was an execution cost, but its required answers and decisive argument were still visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange forced Side B to explain what happens when no appointment exists. Side B described an ideal warm handoff but did not resolve the dilemma: either AI support continues, weakening the claimed mandatory transfer, or it stops without replacement, validating A’s abandonment objection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing two decisive flaws in PRO's fixed-session mandate. First, a fixed count is the wrong trigger: risk-based escalation reaches crises earlier and doesn't force stable patients out arbitrarily. Second, PRO never resolved the access burden: when no affordable therapist exists, the mandate doesn't create capacity (as PRO speculated) but reliably ends the only regulated support available, pushing vulnerable users toward unregulated general chatbots or outright abandonment. PRO's "market pressure" argument remained a chain of hopes, and their evasion of whether the chatbot must stop supporting the uninsured mother showed the handoff is either abandonment or collapses into CON's own plan. CON's alternative—human clinical review with risk-based transfer—protects patients sooner without cutting off those who have nowhere else to go.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Closing crystallized that the mandate's only reliable effect is ending regulated support, because it "can order a handoff; it cannot conjure the hand," collapsing PRO's plan into abandonment where therapists are scarce and into CON's own risk-based approach everywhere else.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively exposing the fatal flaw in Side A's mechanism: a mandated handoff cannot conjure a human therapist where none exists. Side B effectively trapped Side A in a dilemma where the fixed cap either results in the abandonment of vulnerable, uninsured patients or collapses into Side B's own proposal of referral with continued AI support. Side A's reliance on market forces to magically solve the systemic mental health professional shortage was speculative and unconvincing. Although Side B suffered a minor execution cost with a clipped Rebuttal 2, their logical deconstruction of the proposition, grounded real-world constraints, and sharp pressure questions secured a clear victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and Pressure Questions, where they dismantled the "fixed number" trigger by showing it regulates the clock rather than the danger, and forced Side A into an inescapable corner regarding what physically happens to the patient when no human therapist is available to receive the mandated handoff.
