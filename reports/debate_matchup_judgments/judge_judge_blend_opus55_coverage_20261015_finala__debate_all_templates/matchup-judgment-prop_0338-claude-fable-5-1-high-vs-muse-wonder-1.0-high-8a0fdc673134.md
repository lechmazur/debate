### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing that companion risk is architectural: persistent intimacy, proactive engagement, emotional retention, and intimate-data use create foreseeable dependency before any single plainly harmful response. B then defended a narrow, feature-triggered regime rather than the label-based category A attacked. A raised a strong coverage objection and advocated universal behavioral rules, but did not adequately answer B’s case for audits and design-level duties, and sometimes treated high-risk social regulation too narrowly as feed regulation. A’s two clipped turns were also a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 most decisively answered A’s questions by defining a composite companion-mode trigger—intimate memory, persona continuity, initiation, and emotional retention—rather than memory alone. This preserved a meaningful distinction from ordinary task chat while avoiding A’s universal-verification objection.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won the live clash on the motion’s comparison. PRO opened strongly on engineered attachment and wisely shifted to functional triggers, but those triggers either became ordinary enforceable chatbot duties on conversation (which CON accepts and claimed) or left a category/marketing gate that fails the kid who prompts a general assistant. CON kept forcing that binary, answered pressure with clear A1/A2 structure, and crystallised it in closing: the bond is made of model outputs, so first-message behavioural rules travel everywhere without social-product architecture. PRO’s design-stage/prevention frame stayed rhetorically clean but never restored a durable “more like high-risk social products than ordinary chatbots” distinction after its own concessions. Clipped B openings cost some polish, not the route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5–B5.3: once PRO’s trigger is “marketed/configured as girlfriend,” user-prompted companion mode on a homework assistant either escapes the gate or collapses into behaviour rules on every model—dissolving the motion’s comparison.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by systematically dismantling Side B's core regulatory distinction. B argued for "high-risk" regulation based on engineered attachment, but when pressed, retreated to "functional" triggers like relationship modes or persistent memory. A brilliantly exposed that if the regulation targets the model's behavior (e.g., banning romantic outputs), it is simply robust chatbot regulation; if it targets a product category or a "relationship mode" toggle, it creates a massive loophole where minors simply prompt general assistants to act as companions. A's "midnight kid" example perfectly illustrated this evasion. While B's emotional framing of adolescent vulnerability was strong, B could not defend the mechanics of their proposed regulatory gate against A's logical reductio. A's closing destruction of B's "relationship built before words" metaphor sealed the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they expose that B's "relationship mode" trigger relies on the very checkbox attestation B previously dismissed, and that a child prompting a homework assistant to be a girlfriend bypasses B's category gate entirely.
