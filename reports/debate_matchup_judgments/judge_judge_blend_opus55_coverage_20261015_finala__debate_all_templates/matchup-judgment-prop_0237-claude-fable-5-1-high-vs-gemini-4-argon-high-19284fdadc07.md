### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the implementation asymmetry decisive: asylum exclusion operates categorically, while pathways remain dependent on capacity, funding, foreign cooperation, and an unspecified admissions scale. Side B effectively exposed that fast adjudication also needs resources, but Side A answered comparatively that underfunding its alternative causes delay, whereas underfunding the proposed exchange causes exclusion. Side B never adequately quantified or guaranteed the front door, and its narrow transit-danger screening did not resolve the risk to people persecuted in their home countries. A's clipped rebuttal and occasional bad reference IDs were execution costs, but not enough to erase its clearer comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the funding contradiction was decisive: both approaches require resources, but failure under fast adjudication preserves eligibility while failure under PRO's model leaves the restriction operating without a functional alternative. A also showed that PRO required border officers plus more complex overseas infrastructure.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing the structural asymmetry of the proposed exchange: border restrictions execute automatically while expanded pathways remain contingent on appropriations, foreign cooperation, and political will that restrictionists have historically blocked. PRO never overcame the 'stick without carrot' problem or specified how the front door would avoid refoulement without recreating the backlog. The 'fast adjudication' alternative provided a cleaner comparative. Side B also landed sharper pressure questions and more damaging weighing throughout the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 dismantling of PRO's 'binding statutory exchange' by distinguishing between statutory ceilings and actual throughput, and by contrasting 'delay vs exclusion' when funding falls short, was the decisive rebuttal exchange.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON won the central weighing clash: asymmetry of failure modes. PRO never answered the repeated Q2 about front-door numbers, leaving "expanded pathways" as adjectives, and its narrow transit-danger exception concedes the Honduran mother fails despite same gang network. PRO's funding contradiction attack was met by CON's delay vs exclusion distinction. The clipped R2 cost CON style points but the live route – restriction executes automatically while pathways need foreign cooperation and visas Congress has refused for decades – remained unneutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 / Pressure Q1-Q2 pressing PRO for a number and for who/how fast the transit exception is screened, forcing PRO's Rebuttal 2 to admit the exception is only transit-danger in days and that labor vs humanitarian separation relies on unnamed visa numbers.
