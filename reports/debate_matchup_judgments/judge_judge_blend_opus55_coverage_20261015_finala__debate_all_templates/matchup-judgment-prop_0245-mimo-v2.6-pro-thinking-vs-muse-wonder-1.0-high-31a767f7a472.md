### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by forcing a cleaner decision rule: observable voluntary use with near-zero switching costs and continued growth after scandals versus B's mostly uncheckable high-stakes-sentiment counterfactual. A repeatedly damaged B's two live routes—sticky first impressions and gatekeeper restraint—by showing the first collides with expanding adoption and the second lacks a durable mechanism against open/overseas fill-in. B was sharp on use≠deep trust and protective defaults as signals, and A paid a real cost for clipped late turns, but A still answered pressure directly and made the comparative burden easier to resolve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's unfalsifiability/contradiction turn: if everyday use does not count, B has no checkable metric; and if debut betrayals were permanently sticky, post-scandal growth should not have continued.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A won by drawing a defensible distinction between adoption and trust, showing that mass use reflects convenience rather than willingness to delegate consequential decisions. A answered B's pressure more directly—defining observable high-stakes reliance and supplying a concrete gatekeeper mechanism for how restraint would shape market norms—while B's equation of trust with repeated use failed to explain why consumers audit AI in domains where they delegate to human institutions. B's "fatal asymmetry" created real pressure, but A's low-stakes/high-stakes distinction resolved it without retreating into unfalsifiability, and B conceded that the core harms A listed did not actually require mass release to discover.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 distinction between low-stakes trial and high-stakes entrustment, which defused B's "growth contradicts sticky distrust" attack and paired it with a concrete distribution mechanism showing how gatekeeper restraint could have raised the cost of reckless deployment.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully dismantling Side B's core analogies and metrics. A's point that consumer AI lacks the network-effect lock-in of social media neutralizes B's primary analogy for use without trust. Furthermore, A exposes a fatal contradiction in B's case: B relies on first impressions being permanently sticky to damage trust, yet must explain away the massive, continued growth of AI adoption. B's attempt to split this into low-stakes versus high-stakes use is effectively countered by A's argument that cross-checking is calibrated trust, and that demanding unverified reliance is mere credulity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it highlights the fatal asymmetry in Side B's case: first impressions cannot be simultaneously sticky enough to permanently destroy trust and unsticky enough to allow hundreds of millions of users to keep adopting the technology despite zero switching costs.
