### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with discipline and no fabricated evidence, but CON built a structural dilemma that PRO never fully dissolved: if market prices are merely an optional input officials can override, publication of internal dissent does the same work without the market's manipulation and accountability risks; if the price is consequential, then contract framing, wealth-backed positions, and strategically motivated money gain power over policy without being contestable the way expert testimony is. CON pressed this dilemma from the opening through Rebuttal 2 and the closing, and PRO's "comply-or-explain" answer in Rebuttal 2 actually sharpened rather than solved it, since CON's closing (A5.3) showed the proposed rule still collapses into one of the two horns. CON also landed a clean, unanswered point in A4.5 that position caps constrain informed traders too and that coordinated manipulation can mimic broad market movement, leaving PRO's manipulation-correction story weaker than claimed; PRO's closing did not return to rebut this specific point, effectively dropping it. PRO's incentive-based case (financial bounty for contrarians, cheap capture of closed committees) was strong and well-argued, and its pressure questions were genuinely incisive, but CON answered them directly with A1:/A2: and reframed the comparison credibly (contestable reasoning vs. an unexplained price). Overall CON left fewer open wounds and ended with the cleaner, more resolved path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's recurring dilemma about what "formal input" actually means (A2.1, pressed again in A4.4 and A5.3) was the most decisive exchange: PRO's comply-or-explain mechanism either makes the market dispensable (publication does the work) or lets price-moving money and contract framing drive policy without accountability, and PRO's closing never resolved which horn it accepts.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive clash on what a formal input must do under the proposition. A made a strong incentive-and-outsider-information case and forced useful symmetry on counterfactuals and capture, but B repeatedly showed that prices remain unexplained, contract framing embeds judgment, and strategic money can move decisions before correction. A's best patch—comply-or-explain plus auditor investigation—ultimately returned the real work to accountable evidence institutions, so B retained the cleaner reason not to privilege markets over expert processes as the main reliance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 turn on comply-or-explain: either publication (not the price) prevents burying, or reductive framing and uninspectable money acquire power without responsibility.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON successfully defended that major policy requires causal reasoning, accountable judgment, and contestable explanation that market prices cannot supply. PRO’s comply-or-explain mechanism was turned into a dilemma: if officials can override the market, publication rules—not prices—do the accountability work; if prices bind, unaccountable contract-framers and strategic money distort policy. CON’s manipulation and counterfactual objections survived the exchange, showing markets should supplement, not replace, expert institutions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal framing that a price cannot be cross-examined, and that PRO’s own “demand hard proof” concession shows evidentiary institutions—not market movement—must resolve disputes, undercutting the claim that betting odds should be a privileged formal input.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON (A) landed the decisive blow by exposing an internal contradiction in PRO's case: PRO's opening sold prediction markets on the premise that they let insiders, engineers, and junior analysts bypass hierarchy with local knowledge, but PRO's own manipulation safeguards (excluding "direct beneficiaries," barring "controlling" insiders, requiring internal disclosure first) gutted exactly that informational advantage, leaving only outside speculators setting the price. CON pressed this cleanly across rebuttals and the closing ("who is left to set the price?"), and PRO's answer ("targeted, not a purge") never fully resolved it. CON also won the counterfactual-markets exchange: voided bets on unchosen policies mean no price-discipline against reality, while panels can at least be audited on explicit assumptions pre-decision; PRO's "panels can't observe the counterfactual either" reply didn't answer the accountability asymmetry. CON's closing "mute alarm"/"blindfold" framing crystallized why an unexplainable price that persists even after expert investigation finds no flaw is a weak basis for overriding transparent, auditable reasoning. PRO's expert/market/official division-of-labor framework was conceptually clean and its points about hierarchy and self-grading institutions had force, but it conceded ground by walling off the very insiders it needed, and its closing was clipped before completing the thought. CON's execution was also cleaner, with no clipped turns, while PRO was clipped three times, costing it potential load-bearing content.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 (A2.1–A2.2), showing that PRO's manipulation safeguards (excluding beneficiaries/controlling insiders, forcing disclosed-identity reporting) directly contradicted PRO's opening claim that markets let local-knowledge holders bypass institutional hierarchy — a tension PRO never cleanly resolved through the rest of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): CON won by turning PRO’s safeguards into a live contradiction: the rules needed against manipulation and moral hazard strip the very insiders who were supposed to beat hierarchy, leaving a mute, hard-to-police price that officials still need experts to interpret. PRO answered pressure with a narrower “targeted” carve-out and an alarm-plus-check story, but never restored a clean insider channel or a way for the market to lower confidence after a clean expert audit without becoming a cheap veto. That left CON with the cleaner decision rule: reform explainable panels rather than mainly lean on a signal that neither reasons nor settles well on counterfactuals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal (and follow-through in B4) showing PRO’s identity/control bans and pre-trade reporting recreate hierarchy and empty the market of local knowledge while still forcing expert adjudication of unexplained prices.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing that PRO's safeguards against manipulation and moral hazard stripped the market of the very insiders who possessed local knowledge, leaving only speculators and sector-wide interests, while the supposedly independent price signal remained a mute alarm that still required experts to interpret—and would override those experts even when they found no flaw. CON's counterfactual and causal-mechanism burdens stuck through the exchange, while PRO's repeated word-limit clipping further undermined its ability to close gaps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 turning PRO's safeguards into a dismantling of the market's informational engine, culminating in the "mute alarm" and "cheap veto" arguments that exposed the emptiness of an unexplained price overriding transparent causal models.
