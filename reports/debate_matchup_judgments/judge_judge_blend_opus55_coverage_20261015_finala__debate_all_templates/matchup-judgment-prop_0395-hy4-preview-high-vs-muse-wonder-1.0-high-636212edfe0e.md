### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by systematically decoupling Side A's "succession and turnover" justifications from the tool of age-based retirement. Side B proved that for general roles, forcing exits in shortage fields loses capacity (treating jobs as a fixed lump), and for leadership chairs, fixed terms target the actual problem without ending a person's occupation entirely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side A's succession argument by pointing out that age-based rules eject a newly-appointed 68-year-old while sparing a 15-year incumbent 55-year-old, proving age is an arbitrary proxy for "turn-taking."
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the motion’s “most occupations” scope live and showing A’s succession/dignity case never justified age over post-specific fixed terms or ordinary performance tools. A’s stigma-vs-honor weighing remained real but did not neutralize the overbreadth clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 answers to A’s pressure turn: time-in-post targets the chair without ending the occupation, and non-chair jobs need no compulsory birthday vacancy amid ordinary turnover/shortages.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing a persistent mismatch in CON's case: mandatory retirement by age does not actually target the problems CON identified—specific tenure in finite posts or declining capacity. A's pressure questions and Rebuttal 2 showed that a fixed term addresses post-occupation directly, while age arbitrarily removes recently appointed 68-year-olds and spares long-tenured 55-year-olds. A also successfully limited CON's diffuse "whole stock" bottleneck argument, noting that most teachers, nurses, and administrators face shortages, not queues. CON's administrative-convenience and dignity points were real but insufficient to justify a routine blanket rule when the calendar so poorly tracks the institutional needs CON cited.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and pressure-question answers, which showed that age-based removal is an incoherent turn-taking mechanism compared to fixed terms in specific posts, and that CON never justified applying the rule to the majority of workers who hold no bottleneck chair.
