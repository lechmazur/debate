### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): B built a tighter, more disciplined case around the motion's actual metric—global emissions cut within five years—and sustained pressure on two fatal weaknesses in A's plan: the absence of any defensible border price (exposed sharply when A's $190/ton "social cost of carbon" figure was shown to create exactly the discrimination it claimed to avoid), and the "shuffling/bifurcation" problem, where exporters can simply redirect clean output to the U.S. while continuing to run dirty furnaces for other markets, leaving global emissions unchanged. A never squarely answered the bifurcation point, instead asserting that market pressure would eventually force cleanup without explaining why exporters wouldn't just split supply chains, which B had already identified as the profit-maximizing response. B's closing crystallized this clash cleanly, while A's closing largely repeated earlier assertions rather than rebutting the devastating bifurcation rebuttal. B's pressure questions also forced A into the brittle $190/ton claim that backfired, while B's own answers to A's questions (measurement burden distinction, output-based vs. extraterritorial accounting) were direct and coherent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A2), explaining that exporters can bifurcate supply chains—serving dirty furnaces to other markets while sending only clean output to the U.S.—directly gutted A's central "forces global cleanup" mechanism, and A never recovered from this point in the closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B won by locking the debate to the five-year global-tonnes test and keeping two burdens live: CBA implementation (no ready national price, measurement/litigation drag, discrimination risk) and mechanism failure (instant shuffling leaves total emissions flat).
