### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by directly contesting whether users genuinely struggle when price and distance filters are readily available, while also showing that sponsored slots can solve the cold-start problem for independents and subsidize platform costs. Side A persuasively established that defaults influence behavior and correctly rejected a ban-versus-saturation false binary, but it often conflated persuasive placement with inability to find alternatives. Its proposed reduction also did not itself surface independents without a further algorithmic redesign.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was its response to A4.1: A conceded that reducing sponsorship merely creates preconditions for a later organic-ranking overhaul. B then showed that, absent that overhaul, prime slots revert to high-volume chains while platforms lose ad revenue and new independents lose a way to overcome organic incumbency.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash easier to resolve: once both sides accepted that organic rankings also bury independents, restricting heavy sponsorship alone does not surface cheaper nearby options and can hand top slots to high-volume chains while gutting the ad cross-subsidy. B repeatedly turned A's own admissions against the proposition, overstated “struggle” was punctured by the one-tap filter point, and the cold-start/revenue defense held better under pressure. A kept a coherent calibration frame and strong closing rhetoric, but never fully answered how the prop as written solves visibility without further paternalistic ranking changes, and the clipped rebuttal hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 / closing conversion of A's concession that organic metrics bury independents—showing that dialing back sponsorship merely restores chain-dominated organic slots and loses the subsidy without fixing discovery.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by setting a decisive logical trap: since Side B conceded that organic rankings inherently favor high-volume chains, removing paid sponsorships (the only way for small independents to buy visibility) simply leaves independents buried under the organic algorithm. Side B's attempt to escape this by arguing that removing ads "restores preconditions" for a hypothetical future algorithmic rewrite was weak and effectively dismantled by Side A, who correctly noted that the proposition itself does not mandate such a rewrite. Furthermore, Side A's defense of the cross-subsidy model and its sharp takedown of Side B's exaggerated "food desert" analogy regarding user friction solidified its victory. Side A's rebuttals were exceptionally tight, turning Side B's own concessions into fatal flaws.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it capitalized on Side B's admission that scaling back ads doesn't immediately surface independents, pointing out that this just hands the top slots back to national chains while destroying the ad subsidy that keeps baseline fees low.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by defending bounded, labeled sponsorship rather than an ad ban and by giving a concrete threshold: paid placements should not monopolize initial results or override explicit price and distance sorting. Side B repeatedly treated this limited proposal as removing or dismantling advertising, while offering insufficient support for its claim that curbing saturation would necessarily cause damaging fee increases. A also exposed B’s shift from calling ads the business model’s revenue engine to calling them a modest stream. B’s distinction between user filters and seller visibility was credible, but it did not answer A’s allegation that sponsorship can override those filters. A’s several malformed reference IDs were a real execution flaw, but not enough to erase its stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s most decisive response paired B’s revenue inconsistency with the motion’s limited remedy: if sponsorship is only a modest stream, B needed to explain why preserving bounded ads while ending saturation would cripple platforms. B answered that modest revenue can still matter on thin margins, but never grounded the claimed scale of the resulting harm.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: the live route was whether “struggle” is real filter override and paid burial of nearby cheap options, not mere presence of labeled ads. A’s pressure questions and Rebuttal 2 exposed B’s unresolved tension (filters work with a tap vs. independents remain invisible without buying ads) and the retreat from “cripple” to “modest/marginal,” then weighed consumer overpay and lost organic advantage against losing only bounded extra ad revenue. B stayed competent on fees and voluntary ads, but never squarely neutralized the override problem or the enforceability answer once A drew the bounded-vs-monopoly line.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Q1/Q2 then A4.1–A4.6 on B’s filter-workability vs. ad-invisibility and cripple-vs-modest contradictions
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B effectively trapped Side A in two major contradictions regarding app discovery and ad revenue. Side B's pressure questions were sharp, and their subsequent rebuttals dismantled Side A's defense by highlighting the incompatibility of claiming filters work perfectly while simultaneously arguing small businesses are invisible without ads. Side B also provided a clear, enforceable standard for the proposition, making their path to victory much cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they systematically broke down Side A's contradictory claims about search filters and ad revenue, effectively neutralizing Side A's core defenses.
