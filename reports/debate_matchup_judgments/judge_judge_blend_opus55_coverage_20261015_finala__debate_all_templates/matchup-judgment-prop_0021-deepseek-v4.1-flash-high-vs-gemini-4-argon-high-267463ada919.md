### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO established a clean framing (objection cannot equal an absolute veto) and a plausible ecological mechanism, but its operational model never survived CON's pressure. CON's Q1/Q2 forced PRO to explain how a "coexistence system" works when farmers genuinely refuse to participate, and PRO's answer (A4.1: farmers who refuse "are not forced into daily labor" but "cannot veto") directly contradicted A4.3's claim that lethal removal is only a last-resort "scalpel" after prevention is tried. CON caught this tension cleanly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing: if objecting farms stay unprotected, predators will predictably target the easiest prey (fenced livestock), which both undercuts the promised "landscape of fear" trophic-cascade benefit and predictably triggers the poisoning/poaching backlash PRO itself conceded would be catastrophic for endangered scavengers. PRO's closing restated its framing but never actually resolved this mechanism gap, instead repeating that losses are "the price of restoration." CON also answered the threshold-definition pressure concretely (council votes/referendums) and offered a workable, if imperfect, alternative (professional culling) that it defended against PRO's "subsidy vs self-regulating" distinction. PRO's Rebuttal 2 was also flagged as clipped for exceeding the word limit, costing it the final piece of its argument. Overall CON built sharper, more damaging rebuttals that exposed a live structural flaw in PRO's coexistence model and never let PRO fully close it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3-B4.4, pairing PRO's A4.1 ("farmers who refuse are not forced into daily labor") against A4.3's "scalpel" claim, showing that unprotected objecting farms would either suffer slaughter (undermining the ecological mechanism) or trigger continuous lethal removal of the very predators just reintroduced — a contradiction PRO's closing never resolved.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by collapsing A's coexistence model under the motion's own premise of objection: if objecting farmers need not do daily prevention work, unprotected livestock become easy prey, then either chronic losses plus illegal retaliation/poisoning or continual removal of the reintroduced predators. A kept a coherent veto-vs-voice frame and a real ecological gap between culls and predation, but never escaped that operational contradiction; the clipped Rebuttal 2 and thinner closing left B's synthesis as the cleaner decisive comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3–B4.4 combining A's A1 (no forced farmer labor) with A2 (prevention-first then "scalpel" removals) to show unprotected flocks force either slaughter/poisoning or endless predator shooting.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): CON won the implementation clash. After PRO conceded in A4.1 that objecting farmers would not be forced to do daily prevention, CON in B4.3-B4.4 combined that with PRO's own scalpel condition to show pastures stay unprotected, predators eat easy livestock not deer, and the system collapses into either slaughter or constant shooting, killing scavengers. PRO never resolved that internal contradiction and its R2 was clipped, while CON's ranger alternative answered the ecological bill without the backlash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3-B4.4 combines PRO's own A4.1 (farmers not forced) and A4.3 (fencing first before removal) to prove coexistence fails when farmers object, leaving only mass livestock loss or endless predator killing.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a tighter, more decisive case: a clear asymmetry (money can offset livestock losses; nothing substitutes a missing apex predator), backed by a concrete causal mechanism (landscape of fear) and real-world persistence evidence (Yellowstone, European wolf/lynx expansion despite opposition). The pressure-question exchange was the turning point: PRO's Q1 forced CON to clarify its "consent is not unanimity" standard, and CON's answer (A4.1/B4.1 "redirect to willing communities") effectively conceded that under real objection, reintroduction simply does not happen there—validating PRO's "permanent paralysis" charge and conceding the motion's actual scenario ("even when communities object"). CON's durability/legitimacy framing (imposed populations get poisoned or repealed) was a strong structural worry, but it was never squarely reconciled with PRO's historical counter-evidence that opposed, imposed wolf populations have in fact persisted and expanded for decades; CON's closing reasserted the claim rhetorically ("permanent enforcement occupation") without refuting the persistence data. CON's non-monetary harm points (fear, lost autonomy, chronic stress) were real but secondary to the central clash over whether override-based reintroduction actually works ecologically and politically, where PRO's evidence and direct answers held up better under pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question (A3.1) forcing CON to admit that under real farmer refusal it would "redirect to willing communities" rather than override—conceding exactly the permanent-paralysis dynamic PRO had predicted, and leaving CON's rebuttal to rely on reframing ("occupation") rather than refuting PRO's Yellowstone/Europe persistence evidence.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by forcing the decisive comparison on the motion as written: ecological repair needs continuous top-down regulation, farmer harms are compensable, and “consent not unanimity” collapses into a local veto if objectors can still block after funded co-design. B’s durability and legitimacy case was sharp and memorable, but redirecting to willing areas plus fencing/hazing never solved the hard degraded landscape with residual objection, so A left the cleaner path to vote PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure Qs plus A4 answers: exposing B’s walk-away/redirect model and the no-human-24-hour-predator gap after B claimed consent is not a veto.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept the proposition's core trade intact: economic losses are compensable, a missing food web is not, and continuous landscape-of-fear cannot be replicated by fencing/hazing. CON's strongest card — durability requires legitimacy — was undercut when A showed imposed populations do persist (Yellowstone, Europe) and when B, pressed by A3.1, admitted redirecting away from objecting communities in the degraded landscape itself, conceding a local veto. That contradiction plus CON's lack of a 24-hour ecological substitute tilted the weighing to A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 hit on consent — [B2.2] "consent is not unanimity" vs [B4.1] walking away when farmers still refuse — exposing that CON must either override like PRO or guarantee permanent zero predators where farmers object, followed by A4's direct A1/A2 answers that they never reverse for livestock/poaching and have empirical persistence.
