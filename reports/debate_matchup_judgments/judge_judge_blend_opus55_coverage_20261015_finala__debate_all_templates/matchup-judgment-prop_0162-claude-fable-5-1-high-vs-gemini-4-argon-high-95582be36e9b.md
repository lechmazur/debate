### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by framing priority as a marginal investment choice rather than claiming reuse must supply the entire city. Its central comparison survived: every gallon reused reduces exposure to drought-prone, contested imports, while treatment risks are more locally controllable than basin hydrology and water rights. Side A sharply raised return-flow, brine, and contamination concerns, but overgeneralized them across inland cities and never fully answered why reuse must be independent or lossless to deserve priority. B's clipped opening was a modest execution cost, keeping the debate competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal most decisively answered the closed-loop objection: reuse need not be the whole supply, because each recovered gallon displaces an imported gallon. Its comparison between engineerable treatment risks and uncontrollable hydrological or legal risks gave the clearest reason to prefer prioritization.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because it built a cleaner comparative case around reliability and control, showing that direct potable reuse provides a drought-proof local supply while imports depend on shrinking, overallocated basins. B’s “planned versus accidental” reframing neutralized A’s safety fears by demonstrating that imported river water already contains upstream effluent treated less rigorously. A’s strongest objections—legal return-flow constraints and agricultural efficiency transfers—were plausible but partially mitigated by B’s “next dollar” framing and the effective closing argument that seniority ranks claims without putting water in the river. B’s closing crystallized the clash more decisively, leaving the reader with the memorable choice between engineering problems a city can fix and hydrologic problems it can only pray about.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 answer A2, which reframed the safety dispute as planned multi-barrier purification with engineered storage versus the unplanned, single-barrier reuse already occurring in imported river water, capped by the line “Dilution is hoping. Multi-barrier treatment is removing.”
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a clearer, more resilient decision rule: prioritizing supply reliability and local control over hydrology and interstate law. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B won by framing priority as the marginal choice facing a city that already has baseline water: capture a large local supply multiplier before seeking new distant imports. Side A’s fixed-multiplier argument was sharp, especially on safety and indirect reuse, but dependency does not establish that additional inflow must come first. Its claim that imports scale with need also sat uneasily with basin scarcity. Side A’s clipped rebuttal and Side B’s erroneous reference labels were minor execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s most decisive move was accepting Side A’s roughly 1.67-times arithmetic and turning it: a two-thirds expansion of existing supply is substantial enough to prioritize before costly, contested imports. Side A replied that this was only a one-time multiplier, but never showed why cities should forgo that available expansion first.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON built the stronger path to victory by showing that direct potable reuse is a fixed, one-time multiplier of existing inflow rather than an independent source of water. PRO’s 1.67× expansion figure, accepted by both sides, turned into CON’s evidence that reuse is a derivative supplement: it scales with and depends on the baseline supply, shrinks if inflow falls, and cannot be run harder year after year to meet growing demand. PRO never squarely answered why a static multiplier should be prioritized over scalable new volume from agricultural transfers and markets, and PRO’s distinction between direct and indirect reuse collapsed when the aquifer argument was abandoned under pressure. CON’s closing crystallized the clash cleanly: priority belongs to the lever that adds inflow, not the amplifier that depends on it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and closing, which turned PRO’s own 1.67× multiplier against PRO by demonstrating it is a non-renewable ceiling on baseline inflow, not a scalable strategy, and used the dependency relationship to show imports must come first.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by establishing a clear logical hierarchy: reuse is a multiplier of inflow, meaning securing base supply must be the priority. B also effectively exploits the motion's specification of direct potable reuse, highlighting that PRO failed to adequately defend the lack of an environmental buffer against the high consequence of a pathogen breach in a 100% sewage source. While Side A landed strong punches on the vulnerability of senior water rights during dead-pool droughts, Side B's structural framing of dependency and risk provided a more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 on safety, explaining that upstream river effluent is diluted and aged, whereas direct reuse starts as 100% sewage, making the lack of a time buffer for confirmatory lab tests a critical failure mode that PRO's reliance on surrogate sensors actually exacerbated.
