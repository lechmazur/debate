### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by challenging both the causal proof of learning loss and Side A’s assumed counterfactual. B showed that equal instructional hours complicate the claimed one-fifth loss, that before-and-after comparisons may reflect preexisting district crises, and that teacher vacancies can directly undermine instruction. A offered plausible mechanisms but repeatedly treated learning damage as permanent and retention as temporary without establishing either comparison. B’s failure to answer A’s first pressure question directly and its own unsupported certainty about retention kept the margin moderate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was its response that districts adopt four-day weeks amid funding and staffing crises, so declining scores after adoption do not by themselves isolate the calendar’s effect. Coupled with the vacancy and long-term-substitute counterfactual, this undercut A’s claim that the benefits were merely marginal.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by making the decisive comparison clearer and sticking: the realistic alternative in adopting districts is staffing collapse, not a healthy five-day school, and PRO never cleanly established learning harm large enough to outweigh that. A dismantled the “one-fifth hours lost” premise, exposed before/after confounding, and forced the asymmetry that PRO’s case stayed mechanism-and-metaphor while CON’s harm (vacant rooms, subs, swollen classes) was treated as observable. B had a strong opening and memorable mortgage framing, but later rounds deflected pressure, reversed burdens on retention data, and left magnitude unanchored.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.3): PRO failed the turnover-data burden, spacing assumes the wrong model, and mechanism-without-measurement loses to an observable instructional floor collapse.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by establishing a much stronger counterfactual (staffing collapse versus a rearranged calendar) and effectively pressuring Side B to quantify the learning loss. Side B relied heavily on rhetorical metaphors and theoretical mechanisms without measurement. When pressed on the comparative harm of an unfilled teaching position, Side B weakly claimed the staffing crisis was "fixable," which failed to neutralize Side A's argument that a vacant classroom halts learning entirely. Side A's direct rebuttals, disciplined weighing, and exposure of Side B's evasions made the decision clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's "spacing effect" argument by comparing four-day extended sessions to successful block scheduling, and sharply highlighted Side B's failure to provide turnover data or quantify the learning loss, contrasting B's theoretical mechanisms with the observable reality of unfilled classrooms.
