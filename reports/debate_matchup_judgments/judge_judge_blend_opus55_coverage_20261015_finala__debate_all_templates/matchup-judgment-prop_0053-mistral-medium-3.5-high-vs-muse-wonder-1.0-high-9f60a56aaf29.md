### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): B consistently exposed the central weakness in A's case—the undefined, unenforceable "likely to include minors" trigger—and pressed it relentlessly through rebuttals and pressure questions. When A finally tried to answer (A4.1, "significant teen reach," "verifiably adult audiences"), B's second rebuttal cleanly showed the circularity: if platform age data is reliable enough to identify teen-heavy audiences, it also misclassifies disguised teens as adults, and if unreliable, compliant creators default to over-gating regardless. B also offered a concrete, enforceable alternative (targeting conduct like ads to known minor accounts, disclosure rules, parental/clinical consent) that answered the "children are still harmed upstream" objection by redirecting enforcement to identifiable points of risk. A's case leaned on emotionally resonant but repetitive claims about upstream psychological harm and normalization, yet never resolved how age-gating would actually reach the "real" harm (filtered images, tutorials, peer pressure) that both sides agreed drives insecurity, nor did it meaningfully rebut B's point that gating would disproportionately remove compliant, accountable voices while leaving deceptive sellers untouched. A's opening and first rebuttal were also clipped for exceeding word limits, a real execution cost that weakened the completeness of its framing. B's closing crystallized the clash sharply: the rule either overreaches or underreaches, and either way misses the stated target.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (B4.3–B4.5), which turned A's attempted narrowing of "likely to include minors" into a self-defeating dilemma—unreliable age data either fails to catch disguised minors or forces over-gating of adults—was the most decisive exchange, and A's closing never answered it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's proposed mechanism and turning Side A's own analogies against them. Side B successfully argued that age-gating mixed audiences is overly broad, easily bypassed by the very teens it aims to protect, and fails to address the root cause of the psychological harm (filtered ideals). Side B's alternative—targeting known minor accounts and enforcing clinic consent—provided a cleaner, more practical solution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 turning the alcohol analogy was decisive: pointing out that society age-restricts the sale of alcohol (the transaction), not adult videos discussing wine (the speech).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger and more logically consistent case. Side A successfully exposed the core contradiction in Side B's mechanism: relying on easily bypassed age data to enforce audience-based gating, which either fails to protect disguised minors or forces compliant creators to gate lawful adult speech. Furthermore, Side A decisively won the clash on upstream harm by pointing out that Side B's remedy walls off accountable information while leaving the actual visual drivers of insecurity (filters, transformations) completely ungated. Side B relied heavily on repeating that the harm occurs in the feed, but never effectively answered why gating specific procedure mentions solves this when the underlying beauty ideals remain freely accessible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they answered the pressure questions by proposing a conduct-based alternative (targeting known minor accounts) and dismantling Side B's reliance on flawed demographic data, perfectly highlighting the mismatch between the proposed remedy and the actual feed-level harm.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): A built a coherent, adaptive causal story—persuasion happens upstream via gifted treatments, discount codes, and parasocial trust, long before any clinic consultation or paid-ad restriction kicks in—and offered a concrete, bounded trigger (sustained ~20% under-18 analytics or deliberate youth targeting) plus a principled line against slippery-slope expansion (physical risk mechanisms like vessel occlusion/bone reshaping distinguish cosmetic procedures from skincare). B's core objections (vagueness of the trigger, existing safeguards suffice, slippery slope, warning desensitization) were reasserted across rounds but never squarely engaged A's strongest point: that paid-ad restrictions and clinic consent arrive after the recruitment has already happened through unpaid/gifted content and affiliate links. B's pressure-question answers (A1/A2) largely restated 'unworkable' and 'existing safeguards work' without grappling with A's specific threshold proposal or the timing gap, leaving B's rebuttal 2 and closing feeling repetitive rather than progressive. A's closing crystallized the clash around 'timing' effectively, while B's closing mostly recapped the vagueness objection. This gives A the clearer, more resolved path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 point [A2.3] that existing ad restrictions and parental consent "arrive after months of persuasion have produced the desire and chosen the provider" was never squarely answered by B, who kept retreating to 'existing safeguards already address the core concern' without addressing the gifted-treatment/affiliate-link gap—this unanswered timing argument became the decisive throughline of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won decisively by directly answering all of Side B's pressure while Side B mostly repeated its opening claims. Side A introduced highly effective, unrefuted concepts like 'friction stops algorithmic feeding' to counter the bypass argument, and 'persuasion in the feed vs. consent at the clinic door' to defeat the existing safeguards argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's distinction that skincare does not occlude vessels or permanently reshape bone completely dismantled Side B's slippery slope argument, which Side B then repeated verbatim anyway.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B built a much stronger and more responsive case. B effectively neutralized Side A's primary attack on the "unworkable trigger" by proposing a concrete, conjunctive standard (roughly 20% demographic threshold OR deliberate youth targeting) and explaining why it wouldn't ensnare adult content. More decisively, B's "timing" argument—that parasocial persuasion happens in the feed long before a minor reaches the clinic door—demolished A's reliance on in-person consultations and existing ad restrictions. B also cleanly defeated the slippery slope argument by distinguishing irreversible medical procedures from topical skincare. Side A relied too heavily on generic claims of futility, bypass, and censorship without adequately answering B's specific mechanisms or pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's crystallization of the "timing" clash in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, effectively arguing that clinical consent at the end of a sales funnel cannot undo months of algorithmic persuasion in the feed, which dismantled Side A's core defense of existing safeguards.
