### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's policy mechanics. B successfully trapped A on the "essential work" contradiction and neutralized A's core enforcement advantage by pointing out that a vulnerable worker who cannot enforce a shade mandate is equally powerless to enforce a paid-shutdown mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal decisively exposed the contradiction in A's stance on essential work, forcing A to bite the bullet that disaster recovery would simply stop, and thoroughly deconstructed the idea that a paid mandate solves the enforcement gap for undocumented workers.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing a hard structural contradiction in Side B's (PRO) advocacy and showing that the proposed tool misfires where it matters most. CON demonstrated that if task-specific controls like rotation, cooling, and hazard pay can keep essential work safe, a universal mandatory stop is unnecessary; if they cannot, critical work is banned when most needed. This dilemma was never resolved by PRO, whose closing simply repeated that essential work would be "properly resourced" or stopped. CON also effectively rebutted the pay-retention/unemployment argument by showing that output still vanishes and small employers will price idle days into fewer hires or off-books work, while an underground employer is not reached by the statute in the first place. PRO's moral framework remained powerful, but its practical coherence crumbled under CON's sustained pressure on essential work and enforcement reach. After the full exchange, CON had the cleaner live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dismantling of the essential-work exception: showing that PRO's reliance on rotation, cooling, and hazard pay for critical tasks either makes the universal stop redundant or proves the stop is prohibitively blunt, crystallized in the closing line that those controls "either keep necessary work inside safe limits or they do not."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won the central mechanism clash. A framed stop-work with pay as empowerment, but B showed it is blunt (thermometer vs workload/humidity/acclimatization), costly (paid idle days priced into smaller crews/more off-books work harming the informal workers A invoked), and self-contradictory on essential work. A's answers in A4.1 that the stop forbids work regardless of task conceded B's Q1 that line repair/concrete/disaster recovery would stop when most needed. B's answers A1/A2 directly answered A's pressure about refusal rights and funding while keeping task-specific controls tied to how heat actually kills.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 + B4 essential-work contradiction: rotation/cooling/hazard pay either suffices (so mandatory stop unnecessary) or fails (so necessary work banned), exposing that the mandate must be waived where it matters most.
