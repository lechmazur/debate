### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON identified and sustained the decisive structural tension in the motion: a "guarantee" that contracts with local capacity cannot simultaneously be bankable for multi-year planning and responsive to strain. PRO's staged-tranche mechanism (protect current cohort, adjust future tranches) was a thoughtful attempt to resolve this, but CON's closing sharpened the critique precisely where it hurts most—future tranches are exactly what universities market and hospitals build pipelines on, so making them contingent undercuts the very planning certainty the guarantee was supposed to provide, while leaving current strain unresolved. PRO never squarely answered this "too late for the cap, too early for the guarantee" framing. CON also better sustained secondary threads (incumbent advantage in capacity-building, the nurse/GP-school example) across rounds, while PRO's rebuttals to those points were comparatively thinner by the end. PRO's clipped opening was a minor execution cost but not decisive on its own; the deciding factor is that CON's core dilemma survived the full exchange while PRO's main defense mechanism did not fully escape it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) and Closing (B5.1-B5.3), which showed that PRO's staged-tranche fix merely relocates rather than resolves the stability/responsiveness contradiction: protecting the current cohort means local strain persists exactly as the cap was meant to prevent, while making future tranches contingent means the guarantee cannot support the multi-year commitments (degree pipelines, hospital training programs) that recruiters actually need certainty for.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing an unresolvable contradiction in Side B's model: if allocations are guaranteed, they cannot strictly bind to fluctuating local capacity, and if they bind to capacity, they cease to be guaranteed. Side A's closing brilliantly dismantled Side B's temporal compromise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal and closing, which expertly showed that Side B's 'future tranches' delay mechanism meant pipelines were ultimately not guaranteed, and that keeping current cohorts despite housing delays meant the cap failed to prevent local strain.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully exposed an unresolved structural tension in PRO's model: a multi-year guarantee to universities and employers is incompatible with a cap that tightens when local housing and services lag, and PRO's temporal fix merely schedules the conflict rather than dissolving it. PRO's staging mechanism was clever but confirmed that future pipeline tranches remain exposed to local constraints, making the guarantee illusory for degree programmes and training pipelines. Meanwhile, CON offered a coherent alternative—national criteria, stable process, and direct recruiter contributions—without stipulating to the motion's contradictory architecture. While PRO's framing of political weather was rhetorically powerful, CON's rebuttals and closing crystallization provided the cleaner reason to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's closing crystallization that PRO's safeguard arrives "too late for the cap and too early for the guarantee," combined with the earlier demonstration that staged issuance merely "schedules" rather than resolves the capacity-guarantee conflict.
