### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by preserving the clearest principle: institutions retaining benefits created by specific injustices owe material repair, and general present need does not erase that distinct obligation. Side A raised serious opportunity-cost, targeting, and governance concerns, but treated funding as categorically zero-sum and did not overcome B's proportional programs, targeted fundraising, and community-governance answers. B's clipped rebuttal and some overstated claims narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's proportionality response was decisive: the motion does not require identical spending from every institution, so budget differences affect scale rather than whether acknowledgment alone is sufficient. A's reply that a modest fund must be symbolic did not follow, since even limited recurring transfers materially differ from words alone.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by demonstrating that PRO's funding mechanism either imposed costs on current students who bore no responsibility, or collapsed into ordinary present-need aid that made the historical ledger arbitrary. The decisive exchange came when CON showed that PRO's "creditors" of segregation were simply disadvantaged local high schools—indistinguishable from general need-based support—thereby undermining the core distinction between historical obligation and current educational mission. PRO's clipped Rebuttal 2 and retreat on creditor specificity left their moral principle without a coherent beneficiary class or clean funding mechanism, while CON consistently sharpened the clash that universities should tell the truth about the past but distribute resources based on present need.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4), particularly the demonstration that PRO's identification of segregation "creditors" as disadvantaged local high schools and neighborhoods conceded CON's central argument that financial resources should track present need rather than institutional biography.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A wins a highly competitive debate by maintaining a stronger moral and comparative framework. A's core argument—that inherited assets carry inherited obligations and that acknowledgments without material repair are merely "reputation management"—directly and forcefully answers the proposition. Side B offers excellent analytical pushback, particularly the sharp turn that A's redefinition of "creditors" as local disadvantaged high schools collapses into general present-need aid, severing the link to historical wrongs. However, A successfully weighs this by arguing that B's "present need" principle effectively allows the university to keep the compounded wealth of its specific historical injustices while telling the harmed communities to seek public assistance elsewhere. A's rhetorical crystallization ("inherited assets carry inherited press releases," "a receipt without payment") makes the stakes clearer and more memorable. While A suffered a minor execution cost from a clipped final sentence in Rebuttal 2, the substantive answers to pressure were fully delivered, and A's overall path to victory remained intact and more persuasive to a third-party reader.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which sharply attacked Side A's pressure answers by pointing out that a "modest named scholarship" is the exact cheap reputation management A condemned in the opening, and that redefining creditors as local high schools concedes that aid should be based on present need rather than historical lineage.
