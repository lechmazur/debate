### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but A's economic mechanism (vanished DVD/secondary-market economics, marketing-floor math, behavioral shift toward "event-only" theatergoing) was more concrete and sustained under pressure. When B's Q1 pushed A to quantify average profit per dollar, A's answer was somewhat asserted, but A's parallel counter—that mid-budget misses lack the "billion-dollar ceiling" that offsets sequel losses—went largely unanswered in B's final rebuttal and closing. B's strongest moves (portfolio asymmetry, "paying rent" on outside IP, ownership of libraries) were persuasive in theory, but B never cleanly closed the loop on how modern windows actually recoup a $90M mid-budget outlay once flat-fee streaming replaced DVD margins, and A exploited an apparent inconsistency between B's "testing $250M ideas" claim and B's later admission that only certain genres compress to micro-budget. B also suffered a real execution cost: both the opening and first rebuttal were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off concluding synthesis exactly where the core thesis was being stated. A's closing crystallized the clash more cleanly by turning B's own concession about genre limits into the decisive point, while B's closing, though sharp, mostly reasserted the "predictability isn't the question" frame without resolving the live downside-ceiling and DVD-replacement gaps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to B's asymmetric-downside argument (A2.1: "five mid-budget misses destroys just as much capital... except the mid-budget slate lacks the billion-dollar ceiling") was the most decisive exchange; B never directly neutralized the "lacks ceiling" point in later rebuttals, leaving a live hole in the core portfolio-theory claim.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by showing why the mid-budget tier has a structurally poor risk-to-reward profile: substantial fixed marketing costs, weakened secondary windows, and genres audiences increasingly reserve for home viewing. A offered a compelling portfolio and future-IP story, but never established that smaller individual losses produce better expected returns, and B identified credible substitutes—micro-budget films and in-house television—for talent development and IP creation. A’s two clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange answered A’s pipeline case by arguing that micro-budget films and conglomerate-owned television can develop talent and retain IP without mid-budget theatrical risk. It then exposed that A’s examples—character dramas and romantic comedies—were especially vulnerable to the audience shift toward home viewing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A built a significantly stronger and more grounded case based on the current economic realities of the film industry. A successfully dismantled B's portfolio theory by highlighting structural shifts like the death of the DVD market, the fixed floor of marketing costs, and audience streaming behavior. The decisive moment was A's brilliant turn in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, where A used B's own examples (character dramas and romantic comedies) to prove that the mid-budget tier consists entirely of non-event movies that modern theatrical audiences actively reject. Side B relied on slightly outdated industry assumptions and struggled to overcome A's sharp, specific rebuttals regarding secondary revenue windows and IP ownership.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where A weaponized Side B's concession that mid-budget originals are used for "character dramas and romantic comedies" to prove that the mid-budget tier is exactly the type of non-event content audiences now wait to watch at home, thereby destroying B's justification for theatrical spending in that tier.
