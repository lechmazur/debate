### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by reframing the clash as mandate design rather than action versus inaction, then offering a binding carbon-price and intensity-standard alternative. A never resolved B’s supply dilemma: strict lifecycle rules either create shortages that bar ships or require waivers that undermine the timetable. B also showed that its policy recycles revenue to vulnerable importers, while A’s late surcharge fund effectively borrowed B’s mechanism. B’s repeated clipping and mistaken reference labels were execution costs, but its comparative case remained substantially stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s pressure-question answers was decisive: A explicitly accepted barring ships if compliant fuel was unavailable, allowing B to connect the mandate’s supply risk directly to disrupted poor-country routes. B then showed that A’s proposed surcharge fund resembled the levy model A had criticized.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by turning the live clash into a clean choice: fuel-prescription deadline risk versus binding price-plus-intensity path. PRO never neutralized the supply/deadline dilemma or the equity hole; answering “bar non-compliant ships” and adding a surcharge fund conceded CON’s strongest objections while PRO kept asserting that mandates create missing infrastructure. Clipped CON turns were a real cost but did not erase the sharper comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2/closing use of PRO’s A1 (bar ships) and A2 (surcharge fund) answers, converting them into cliff-edge trade disruption and a levy concession.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B decisively won by dismantling Side A's core analogies and exposing the physical and economic flaws in a strict fuel mandate. Furthermore, Side B brilliantly neutralized Side A's late attempt to add an equity fund by pointing out it was functionally identical to Side B's carbon levy proposal. Side A's case relied on the unsupported assumption that regulatory deadlines can magically conjure massive new infrastructure, whereas Side B offered a more physically plausible and equitable alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it capitalized on Side A's Pressure Question answers. Side B highlighted that Side A's choice to "bar non-compliant ships" would strand feeder vessels carrying essential goods to poor nations, and exposed that Side A's proposed "surcharge on laggard operators" to fund an equity mechanism was simply a repackaged version of Side B's carbon levy.
