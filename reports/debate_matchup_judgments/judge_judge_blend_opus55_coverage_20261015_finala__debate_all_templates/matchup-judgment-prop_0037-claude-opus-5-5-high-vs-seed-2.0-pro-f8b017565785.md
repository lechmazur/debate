### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively capitalizing on Side A's concessions. When Side A defended the ban by explicitly permitting ride-hails and pivoting the core harm to "hoarding" space via parking, Side B cleanly demonstrated that Side A's policy would not stop wealthy drivers, would generate more congestion via empty return trips, and was fundamentally a parking policy rather than a driving ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly synthesized Side A's concessions, pointing out that under A's "ban," the same rich driver uses the same street via a ride-hail, but the public loses the toll revenue that could have funded transit.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by exposing that PRO's ban cannot be "entire" without exemptions for taxis, trades, and mobility vehicles, and that those exemptions let wealthy users simply switch to ride-hail—undermining the claim that the ban removes cars or privilege from city centers. CON also turned PRO's Oslo example into evidence that parking removal and tolling work without a total ban, and sustained a decisive funding advantage: pricing generates revenue for transit and reclaimed public space, while a ban adds enforcement costs and removes that revenue. PRO's moral commodification frame never recovered from CON's comparative argument that the needy are treated similarly under both systems, while the rich still enter under a ban but now pay a platform instead of the public purse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's demonstration that PRO's exemptions for taxis and trade vehicles allow the wealthy coffee-run millionaire to simply switch to ride-hail, producing an empty return trip and zero public revenue, collapsing the claimed fairness and spatial advantages of a ban over pricing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive clash on what 'entirely' means. PRO conceded broad exemptions for vans, trades, taxis [A2.1, A2.3] and then that ride-hails still enter and don't park [A4.1], so the millionaire coffee-run still occupies the street plus an empty return trip that pays nothing, while needy users need permits. B showed pricing handles the same need-based exemptions with discounts and turns the wealthy's payment into night buses, bus lanes and plaza conversions, with a dial (Singapore) to hit flow. PRO never answered the funding pressure [B3.2] except with the ungrounded claim that farebox alone would cover new service [A4.4], and relied on brittle claims like 40 sq km Oslo and retail/asthma improvements within half a year [A1.5, A2.3].
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 + B4 empty-return ride-hail trap and parking-policy reframe: showing PRO's ban is not zero-vehicle but an anti-parking rule achievable with pricing + curb repricing, while losing revenue and adding permit bureaucracy — directly neutralizing PRO's commons/space-reclaim route.
