### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the tighter causal case: tariffs hit import-dependent inputs immediately while domestic factories (which themselves need those same taxed inputs) take years to materialize, if at all. PRO's rebuttals systematically dismantled CON's "tariff revenue = investment" framing and the "learning curves accelerate with protection" claim by pointing out that learning depends on cumulative volume, which tariffs suppress. The pressure-question exchange was decisive: PRO's Q1 (how does capacity arrive faster than deployment costs hit, given factories need taxed imports too) and Q2 (why wouldn't the feared export cutoff happen during the pre-capacity window tariffs create) exposed a real circularity in CON's resilience argument. CON's answers (A4.1-A4.3) largely reasserted that "the tariff is the market signal" and that cutoffs would "trigger emergency construction," without ever explaining the mechanism by which taxed inputs don't also hamstring the promised domestic factories. PRO's closing crystallized this gap sharply ("CON's case requires the tariff to behave like a factory, and it does not"), while CON's closing restated the resilience framing without closing that hole. PRO did suffer clipping penalties in two speeches, a real execution cost, but the substantive content delivered before clipping was already sufficient to carry its main lines, and its answers in the required A1/A2 format were clean and responsive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Turn 3 rebuttal (B2.1–B2.6), which reframed CON's "tariff as market signal" into a self-defeating circularity (domestic plants also need taxed imported inputs) and reframed "tariff revenue as investment" as a deadweight-loss fallacy — a critique CON never fully answered even after being pressed again in the questions round.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A won by locking the proposition to a timing and instrument weighing CON never neutralized: deployment losses are immediate and certain, while capacity is delayed, partial, and still taxed via upstream inputs. A’s pressure questions exposed the core gap—tariffs do not guarantee factories before the cost hit, and the pre-capacity cutoff remains uninsured—and B’s answers stayed circular (tariff = capital commitment; cutoff “triggers” emergency builds). B’s resilience/hostages framing was rhetorically strong and kept the debate live, but repeatedly substituted the desired factory outcome for the tariff mechanism and treated cancelled near-term deployment as merely recoverable delay. A made the cleaner comparative path: on the motion as written, broad tariffs slow deployment enough that narrower tools capture more of the manufacturing benefit without the front-loaded tax.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1–2 line that CON treats a tariff as if it were a factory, then worsens the exact pre-capacity vulnerability window the resilience case fears—especially after Q1/Q2 forced the mechanism gap.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) established a crisp structural asymmetry: broad tariffs impose immediate, certain deployment losses (canceled projects, stranded emissions, suppressed learning-by-doing) in exchange for capacity gains that are delayed, partial, and undermined by the same tariffs on imported inputs. Side A (CON) never overcame the burden of showing that a price wedge reliably becomes a factory faster than the damage accumulates; its answers to B’s pressure questions increasingly relied on circular claims that the tariff “is” the capital signal. A also failed to engage B’s live comparison showing that targeted procurement and narrow duties can secure strategic depth without taxing every panel and chip today. B’s closing crystallized the decisive clash—CON needs the tariff to behave like a factory, and it does not—while A’s closing repeated the resilience frame without resolving the timing gap or the alternative-instrument objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal [B2.1–B2.7] was the most damaging exchange, dismantling CON’s “reallocation,” “deadweight-loss-as-investment,” and “learning-curves” claims by showing that tariffs tax the inputs domestic plants need, that canceled projects never become factories, and that protected domestic volume suppresses rather than accelerates cost declines.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A built the stronger comparative frame (stock vs. flow, fragility of unprotected supply chains, and—crucially—repeatedly pressed PRO on the motion's own threshold word "enough," which PRO never quantified or even approximately bounded). A's sharpest moment was exposing an internal tension in PRO's case: PRO wanted subsidies/mandates to cushion deployment against tariff costs while simultaneously claiming tariffs would gut the market for the very factories they fund—A's "both ways" rebuttal in Rebuttal 2 went unanswered in PRO's closing, which simply reasserted the dilemma rather than resolving it. PRO's pressure-question answers (A1 in particular) sidestepped the magnitude burden by reframing it as unnecessary, which is a live weakness given the motion's explicit "enough to outweigh" language placing that burden on PRO. PRO's case was coherent and had a genuine throughline (timeline mismatch, self-defeating demand destruction, finite subsidies), and its closing was tight and well-organized, but it never closed the quantification gap A kept pressing. A's major cost was repeated format violations—three of its four main speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning some of its argument was literally cut off before the judge saw it. This is a real execution failure that tempers the win but does not overcome the substantive edge A built through tighter rebuttal work and better exploitation of the motion's own wording.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 point that PRO cannot simultaneously claim deployment is cushioned by subsidies/mandates and that tariffs will gut the market for protected factories — PRO's closing never resolved this tension, leaving it as A's most damaging unanswered blow.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): B won the decisive clash on burden and counterfactual: PRO never showed a slowdown large enough to outweigh a permanent base, while CON made “factories do not get financed without the tariff signal” the live alternative to idealized cheap deployment. A’s sequencing/self-defeating-demand dilemma was real and sticky, but B neutralized it by exposing A’s both-ways move—deployment cushions work when convenient, markets collapse when not—and by treating alternatives as bridges that leave the motion’s capacity benefit unrealized. Three clipped B turns are a real cost, yet the completed core still made the proposition easier to reject than accept.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.7: A cannot treat subsidies/mandates as real cushions against price pass-through and also claim tariffs shrink the demand base to nothing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won by exposing that PRO (B) never met the motion’s core burden: proving deployment slows *enough* to outweigh permanent domestic capacity. B repeatedly asserted an immediate, compounding slowdown but never quantified it, eventually describing it as merely “modest” [B4.1]. A provided plausible buffers—subsidies, mandates, input exclusions, and long-run demand floors—that undermined B’s causal chain, and A’s counterfactual that capacity simply would not get financed without tariff protection gave the manufacturing side real weight. B’s elegant “self-defeating demand signal” theory was never grounded in evidence that the market actually collapses under tariffs, and B’s alternatives (stockpiling, diversification) conceded the value of domestic capacity while failing to deliver it. A’s sharper burden analysis and cleaner weighing controlled the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s first rebuttal and pressure question that capital will not finance domestic fabs without a tariff signal to prevent undercutting, combined with the follow-up demand that PRO quantify the deployment loss, exposed that B was weighing speculative, modest near-term delays against a permanent capacity gain that tariffs alone secure.
