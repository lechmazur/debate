PRO's responses (distinguishing eligibility from competence, proposing shall-issue safeguards) were reasonable but never fully recovered from the concession or matched CON's concrete framing of costs. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly with no fabricated evidence, but CON executed the sharper strategic narrowing. In the pressure round, CON conceded support for dealer-run checks on all private transfers, which neutralized PRO's signature "private-sale loophole" argument without requiring a license at all, leaving mandatory training as the real point of contention. CON then relentlessly pressed the unresolved cost: an eligible first-time buyer facing sudden danger cannot get trained in time, and PRO's "provisional license" fix only addresses bureaucratic processing delay, not the separate problem of training-appointment availability. PRO never squarely closed this gap in Rebuttal 2 or Closing, instead reasserting that "eligibility and training are settled once" without explaining how an urgent buyer clears training when none is available. PRO also conceded that, holding coverage and database accuracy constant, licensing "adds little new eligibility information," which CON exploited to argue the remaining justification (compulsory training) doesn't outweigh the access burden. CON's closing crystallized this cleanly ("protection against delay starts after the barrier that matters"), while PRO's closing mostly recapped its mechanisms rather than directly answering that final pressure point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's answer to A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2 (B4.1–B4.5), where it conceded universal dealer-run checks for private sales (undercutting PRO's coverage argument) while pointing out that PRO's provisional-license fix solves government delay but not training-appointment unavailability, a gap PRO never closed in its final speeches.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively narrowing the clash to the marginal access burden of compulsory training. By accepting universal dealer-run checks, Side A neutralized Side B's private-sale loophole argument, and then successfully exposed that Side B's "provisional license" did not solve the waiting time for training class availability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 response that "a short class is not a short wait for a class" decisively dismantled Side B's attempt to solve the urgent-buyer delay.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B successfully separated universal background checks from licensing and sustained pressure on the unresolved training-access delay for first-time buyers. Side A conceded that licensing adds little new eligibility information beyond point-of-sale screening, leaving the case resting heavily on mandatory training, yet never fully justified why that safety gain outweighed the real access costs Side B identified. Side B’s sharper rebuttals and cleaner weighing of the decisive tradeoff earn the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s rebuttal that a short course is not a short wait for a course, exposing that Side A’s provisional-license safeguard did not resolve training-access delays for first-time buyers.
