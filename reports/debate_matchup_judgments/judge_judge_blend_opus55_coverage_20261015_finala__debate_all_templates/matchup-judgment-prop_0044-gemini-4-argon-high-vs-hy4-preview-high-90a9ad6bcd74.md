- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won the decisive mechanism clash: physical packaging cannot reliably inform the large online “before purchase” market, and a printed guarantee functions as express-warranty liability that compresses promises to a uniform short floor rather than lengthening support.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 / close (B4.1–B4.7, B5): converting A’s warranty claim into the race-to-floor dynamic while showing the carton arrives after online checkout and misleads on remaining life.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing a central tension in Side B's case: B argued printed guarantees would create feared breach liability that drives firms to lowball promises, yet simultaneously claimed the mandate offered buyers no enforceable remedy when broken. A leveraged this to show the guarantee carries real legal weight, making the physical label a genuine warranty rather than a mere sticker. While B's floor-becomes-ceiling argument and online-shopping mismatch were strong, A handled pressure questions more directly, gave cleaner answers on gifts and enforcement, and crystallized the debate around the consumer's right to know hidden device fragility before paying. B's alternative of digital disclosure was undermined by B's own admission that online listings are easily edited, leaving A's physical-box anchor as the more credible commitment mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1, where Side A demonstrated that Side B could not coherently claim printed guarantees create strict liability while also arguing they lack any enforcement mechanism or remedy for buyers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B wins the central weighing: information asymmetry and risk-shifting to buyers. B exposed A's contradiction between fearing breach liability in [A1.3]/[A2.1] and claiming in [A3.1] the guarantee has no teeth, establishing it as an express warranty with remedy. A's race-to-bottom claim that premium brands would cut 6-year support to 2 years was asserted but not weighed against B's point that even a 2-year floor beats zero and that a $1000 TV cannot hide behind 2 years. ### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by converting A’s own pressure answers into decisive mechanism failures: the required mitigation/refund duty for upstream chip abandonment plus inventory relabel/pull rules make long honest guarantees commercially impossible, so the shelf comparison rewards shell brands’ long lies over compliant short floors. A kept a strong externality frame and blank-box asymmetry, but never neutralized that distortion or the activation/fleet-timeline trap; B’s later speeches narrowed the clash to “does this label reverse the truth?” and answered it more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 turning A4.1’s “mitigate/refund” and A4.3’s “relabel or pull” into proof that rational firms must print extreme lowballs while shells print 7 years and vanish.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by demonstrating that the mandate creates perverse incentives: honest manufacturers facing third-party supply-chain liability and fleet-wide infrastructure costs will rationally lowball guarantees, while judgment-proof shell brands exploit the mandated label to lend credibility to inflated claims. B effectively converted A's concessions—particularly the admission that makers must "make the buyer whole" for upstream failures and must relabel or pull aging retail stock—into proof that the burden is substantial and the printed number becomes a pre-negotiated exit ramp. A's transparency narrative never recovered from B's sustained comparison showing the blank box, for all its flaws, at least invites skepticism, whereas a government-mandated label replaces skepticism with official-looking misinformation that steers buyers toward bad actors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's pressure question on upstream chipmaker liability. When A conceded in Rebuttal 2 that device makers must mitigate or "make the buyer whole" with repair, replacement, or refund if a supplier abandons a component, B immediately turned that concession into a lethal argument: fleet-wide refund liability for third-party silicon makes long guarantees economically irrational, effectively mandating the defensive lowballing B had predicted and cementing the shell-brand distortion problem.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the core design clash. PRO offered transparency and competition, but couldn't resolve the fleet-wide vs individual warranty problem: activation dating creates open-ended server duty for one dusty box, fixed dating cheats late buyers or loses value. PRO's answers that makers must refund/replace or relabel/pull stock like perishables conceded massive liability and waste, proving CON's lowballing and shelf-gaming point – honest firms print 2 years to survive third-party chip risk while judgment-proof shells print 7 years and win comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 dismantling B4.1/B4.3 – showing the mitigation duty means bricking or fleet-wide refunds and the perishables analogy means manufacturers don't control retail inventory, turning PRO's burden is trivial into mandatory defensive lowballing.
