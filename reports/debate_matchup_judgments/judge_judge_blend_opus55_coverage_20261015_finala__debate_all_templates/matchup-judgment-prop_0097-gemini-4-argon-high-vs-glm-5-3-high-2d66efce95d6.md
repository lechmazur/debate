- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A built the more durable case by exploiting two things B never fully resolved. B's answer (A2, Turn 7) just reasserted the dormant-spending claim without rebutting that the decision to build the apparatus itself is never tested against evidence. A pressed this again in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, correctly framing it as "a position immune to disconfirmation." Second, A's closing identified a real bait-and-switch: the motion calls for retraining, wage insurance, and income support as substantive programs, but B's later speeches retreated to calling preparation merely "pennies of administrative plumbing," a narrower and cheaper claim than what the proposition demands. B's own strongest material—distinguishing structural downward mobility from cyclical unemployment, and the deployment-speed argument against trucking/spreadsheet analogies—landed well and was never fully erased, but it wasn't enough to offset the unresolved epistemic and scope problems A kept pressing. A's answers to B's pressure questions (A1/A2 in Turn 8) were also cleanly formatted and substantively concrete (naming an observable metric: wage/employment trends in routine cognitive occupations), while B's rebuttals tended to repeat the contradiction charge against A's "existing stabilizers vs weak bespoke programs" distinction without landing a decisive blow, since A had already drawn that distinction clearly in Rebuttal 1.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases—A on epistemic humility, moral hazard, and the risk of locking workers into dead-end retraining, B on the insurance-against-asymmetric-risk framing with concrete examples of current AI deployment. The pivotal exchange came in the pressure-question round: B forced A to name a concrete trigger for "definitive proof," and A answered with "sustained net white-collar job destruction with unemployment above cyclical churn." B's closing then weaponized this against A's own earlier admission that "hiring quietly stops before firing starts," showing A's chosen threshold is inherently a lagging indicator that certifies the crisis only after the damage is done. A's final closing never directly rebutted this specific contradiction, instead reasserting the market-distortion argument, leaving B's sharpest hit unanswered. A did land strong points of its own—especially the "false alarms from leading indicators" critique and the irony of paying idle graduates to "wait out" a market that may never return—but B's answers (A1/A2) adequately covered the graduate case via the income-support leg of the three-part package, blunting A's strongest trap. On balance B's closing argument was more decisive and better resolved the central "wait vs. prepare" dispute, giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing move exposing that A's own definition of "definitive proof" (net job destruction) is a lagging indicator, directly contradicting A's earlier claim that "hiring quietly stops before firing starts"—a contradiction A's final speech did not squarely address.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping the motion’s core comparison live: prepare-now insurance vs wait-for-definitive-proof. After B was forced to name “sustained net white-collar job destruction above cyclical churn,” A cleanly showed that standard is lagging given B’s own slow-restructuring/hiring-freeze points, so CON’s rule certifies damage after careers are already scarred. A’s package reply (income support + worker-following retraining for graduates; wage insurance for mid-career; leading indicators) was imperfect but enough to preserve asymmetry—early false alarms waste money; late alarms lose the house—while B’s strong distortion/moral-hazard/fiscal-priority hits never fully flipped that cost comparison under the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 contradiction: displacement-triggered wage insurance cannot help the entry-rung graduates A kept citing, which forced A’s later package/leading-indicator move and set up B’s false-alarm/trap-workers counters.

A2 exposed the contradiction between "hiring stops before firing" and "triggered by job loss" — wage insurance can't help the never-hired graduates B centers, and leading-indicator triggers fire on every normal tech/finance cooldown. B's package answer in B4 didn't resolve the second-order harms A raised: paying graduates to wait for vanished jobs creates scarring, and wage insurance lets firms cycle talent on taxpayers' dime while diverting scarce funds from low-income hardship.
