### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON (A) consistently held PRO to the motion's comparative, causal burden and never let the debate drift into a mere "engagement algorithms are addictive" narrative. CON's opening reversal-of-causation point and its reframing of chronological feeds as the most potent comparison engine (classmates, not strangers) were never fully neutralized. In the pressure round, CON's question ("what actual evidence...rather than screen time in general") exposed the core weakness of PRO's case, and PRO's answer in Rebuttal 2 simply re-asserted the attention→sleep→depression chain rather than supplying independent support, which CON explicitly and effectively called out ("two conversions, zero evidence"). CON also turned PRO's own "globally filtered to match exact insecurities" line into a self-defeating admission that the same engine must be equally capable of surfacing recovery content. PRO's case was vivid and well-organized, and its closing correctly spotted apparent tension in CON's position, but CON's closing cleanly resolved that tension (user-drives/algorithm-follows) and reiterated that no demonstrated comparative link was ever established. Overall CON made the clash easier to adjudicate and left a cleaner, more defensible final position.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to A1 ("A recommender that permanently flooded users off a single pause would be a broken product—monotony kills watch time too") combined with its closing use of [B4.6] against PRO ("an engine omniscient about despair yet blind to joy") decisively undercut PRO's sleep/spiral mechanism without PRO ever supplying independent evidence for the causal chain.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by keeping the comparative causal burden central. A offered plausible amplification and sleep-loss mechanisms, but repeatedly moved from greater engagement to clinical anxiety and depression without adequately establishing those conversions or showing they were unique to personalized feeds. B also supplied credible comparative harms from chronological feeds and effectively challenged A’s assumption that recommenders detect despair but not positive interests. B’s broad empirical claim was insufficiently grounded, but its victory did not depend solely on that claim. A’s nonexistent C-reference IDs in the closing were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively separated the conceded increase in watch time from the proposition’s required increase in anxiety and depression, while explaining that refreshing, notifications, rumination, social comparison, and sleep displacement can also persist under chronological feeds.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): PRO maintained a comparative, causal path: engagement turns a fleeting pause into an automated flood of distress content, concentrates extreme peers versus bounded chronological reality, and removes stopping cues, converting watch time into sleep loss and rumination. CON conceded the key premises - algorithm reads lingering as preference [B1.2] and buys watch time [B2.1] - then tried to reclassify sleep loss as a phone problem and claim the feed retrains equally to joy, creating the contradiction PRO weaponized in closing. CON's constant demand for demonstrated clinical evidence overweighted burden for a closed-book mechanism debate and never neutralized the asymmetry and friction arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 turn of CON's own concessions - that dwelling=preference [B1.2] indicts engagement, and that billions spent on personalization proves chronological does not hold attention equally - was decisive and was never recovered.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely clever cases, but B's mechanism story proved more durable under pressure. B's central claim—that watch-time optimization cannot distinguish healthy engagement from rumination, and that distress content is itself highly "engaging" (shared, saved, rewatched)—was never fully disarmed. A's answer relied on an unsupported assertion that long-term retention signals favor "relief" content, and when B closed with "pain content gets shared, saved, and rewatched... relatability is its currency," A's final speech pivoted to a "steering wheel" framing rather than directly answering that specific rebuttal. A scored real points elsewhere: the proximity argument (classmates hurt more than strangers), the discovery-mechanism problem (an isolated teen can't search for a subculture she doesn't know exists), and the timeline challenge to B's opening correlation evidence all landed and were never squarely answered by B. But these wins on secondary fronts didn't offset B's stronger hold on the core causal mechanism the motion turns on. B also used the Pressure Questions round more incisively, forcing A into a concession-laden answer about swiping/retraining that B then exploited effectively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing line that "pain content gets shared, saved, and rewatched" directly undercut A's claim that retention/sharing signals push the algorithm toward relief content, and A's final speech did not squarely answer it.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won a close, substantive clash by isolating the distinctive risk of engagement ranking: it learns which material holds a particular adolescent and can amplify distress because attention signals do not reliably encode well-being. Side B effectively showed that deactivation evidence does not isolate ranking and that non-personalized feeds can also be infinite, weakening A’s compulsion pillar. But B’s positive case depended on speculative claims that harmful scrolling causes avoidance, beneficial content dominates long-term retention, and feeds can be retrained instantly. A more convincingly explained why those same retention signals can reward rumination and why harm occurs before eventual disengagement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned whether optimization can distinguish delight from distress. B answered that long-term retention, shares, saves, and return visits favor humor and hobbies; A countered that painful content produces those signals too and can cause anxiety well before burnout or app avoidance. That left B without a reliable mechanism ensuring personalization functions as a refuge rather than targeted amplification.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won the key comparative by forcing Side A to concede in [A4.1] that non-personalized feeds are also bottomless, neutralizing the sleep/compulsion pillar, and by reframing harm around social proximity – that one video of real classmates excluding you hurts more than strangers. A never showed what harm ranking removes rather than keeps; B answered it removes the monopoly of the high-school graph and gives a steering wheel via interest-based re-training. That left A arguing personalization worsens comparison, but without answering why watch-time would prefer relief, while B's refuge logic survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's proximity-vs-volume attack in [B2.1]-[B2.2] that chronological peer exclusion is more wounding than global stranger volume, plus later burial claim in [B4.3], directly undercut A's amplification story.
