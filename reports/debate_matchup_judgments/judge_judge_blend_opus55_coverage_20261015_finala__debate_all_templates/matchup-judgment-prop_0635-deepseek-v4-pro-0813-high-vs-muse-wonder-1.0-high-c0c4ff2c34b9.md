### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won a close, high-quality clash by keeping the motion on product-liability-style responsibility, not mere accountability. CON’s strongest move was accepting PRO’s best cases as ordinary negligence/failure-to-warn/recall while showing why the PL frame overreaches, chills open release, and misplaces many concrete deployment choices. PRO answered pressure honestly with a process checklist, but that still left no workable safe harbor against CON’s jury/hindsight and always-present-category pressure. Edge to B for the cleaner comparative reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2: concede the known unpatched jailbreak warrants liability, but as negligence/recall—not as product defect for residual general-purpose misuse.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): PRO established that providers hold unique, non-delegable control over base-model design and can fix risks once for millions of users, while CON’s deployer-focused alternative collapsed when CON conceded that no other actor could patch a provider-discovered jailbreak. CON’s attempt to relabel the standard as mere negligence did not defeat the substantive case for upstream responsibility, and PRO’s proportionality response on open weights showed reasonable safeguards need not ban release. PRO’s rebuttals were sharper, its pressure questions more damaging, and its closing more tightly crystallized the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s pressure question on jailbreaks forcing CON’s concession that no other actor is better placed to fix provider-level failures, which PRO then leveraged to anchor its unique-control thesis.

B showed that A's strongest examples (known, unpatched jailbreaks) are already covered by negligence and recall doctrines, whereas applying product liability to all foreseeable categories leads to hindsight bias and chills open-weight releases. A's dismissal of the legal distinction as merely a "label" in the closing weakened their defense of the specific motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they answered A's pressure questions by conceding that providers are best placed to fix known jailbreaks, but pivoted to explain why that specific failure supports negligence/recall rather than the broader product-liability standard proposed in the motion. This neatly neutralized A's strongest offensive point while preserving B's core critique of the motion's mechanism.
