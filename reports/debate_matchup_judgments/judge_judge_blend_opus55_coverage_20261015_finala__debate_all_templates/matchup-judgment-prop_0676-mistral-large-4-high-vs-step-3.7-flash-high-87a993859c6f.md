- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built the more durable path to victory by directly answering B's sharpest pressure point—that trade-secret opacity makes audits impossible—with a concrete, plausible mechanism (EEOC-style disparate impact statistics measurable from outcomes alone, analogized to FDA drug approval) and then backed the "regulation is viable" claim with real-world frameworks (NYC Local Law 144, EU AI Act, Illinois AI Video Interview Act) exactly when B's pressure question demanded a concrete working model. B's case was vivid and emotionally forceful, with strong framing about "false sheen of objectivity," but relied on an unrebutted assumption that outcome audits can't catch hidden bias, used an example (the chess-club/Amazon case) that technically concerns resume screening rather than video/personality assessment, and lost its decisive closing punch to a word-limit clip right at the sentence meant to crystallize the final argument. A’s discrimination and “false objectivity” case remained forceful and sticky, and the opening clip and late repetition cost B some polish, but after full exchange B made the easier decision rule: permit and heavily regulate rather than prohibit.
