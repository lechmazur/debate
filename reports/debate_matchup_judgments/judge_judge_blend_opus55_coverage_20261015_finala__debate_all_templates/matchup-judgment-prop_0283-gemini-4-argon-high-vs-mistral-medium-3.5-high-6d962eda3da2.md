A's US-demand and fragmentation points never neutralized that precondition frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure Q1 plus B4.5/B5.2-3 on how unshielded judges/police cannot project coercion against armored/drone paramilitaries if the military first refuses to disarm them.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by exposing a decisive sequencing problem that CON never resolved: you cannot build credible police, courts, and witness protection while cartels field armored convoys and assassinate officials with impunity.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s central rebuttal that "you cannot subpoena a paramilitary army" and its accompanying security-first sequencing argument. PRO demonstrated that CON’s institutional-reform solution is circular—courts and police cannot function if cartels outgun the state and assassinate officials with impunity, so physical security must precede rather than follow civilian institution-building. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by separating rejection of the status quo from rejection of renewed militarized decapitation. It repeatedly showed that B’s proposed targeted leadership operations resembled the failed kingpin strategy and forced B to explain splintering, succession violence, and who governs cleared territory. B established that restraint has not delivered security, but its crucial claim that military action could “direct” fragmentation was asserted rather than demonstrated, and temporary military occupation did not resolve the institutional vacuum. A’s outside-in financial and institutional alternative was imperfect but comparatively clearer. A’s nonexistent P-reference was a minor execution flaw.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s use of the 2024 Culiacán violence was decisive: if leadership removals precipitated the succession conflict, B could not straightforwardly cite that conflict as proof that non-confrontation caused the chaos. B’s answer—that fragmentation already exists and can be directed—did not adequately solve the causal objection.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won by converting PRO’s own exhibit list (Culiacán 2024, high post-2006 death toll, kingpin captures) into proof that the harder approach itself generates the chaos PRO attributes to “restraint,” while PRO never cleanly escaped the contradiction of demanding 2006-style leadership strikes yet denying a return to 2006 and never solving the “who holds the ground” institutional gap beyond temporary occupation. A’s sequencing/financial-first alternative stayed live and comparatively cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s real-time use of the 2024 Culiacán war as kingpin fallout (A2.1–A2.2 / A4.3), which flipped B’s central example against the harder approach.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by turning Side B's key examples against it and collapsing the solvency of the "harder approach." B vividly described cartel atrocities, but A showed the 2024 Culiacán violence and broader fragmentation were the direct result of the kingpin decapitations B advocated, not of restraint. A also demonstrated that without functioning local courts and police, military occupation becomes a permanent trap rather than a path to state control. B never overcome this causal contradiction or explain why targeted operations would not simply repeat the 2006 fracture-and-metastasise pattern. A's financial and institutional alternative, though contested, provided a cleaner comparative path to reclaiming the state.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal (A2), where it inverted the Culiacán blockade and 'El Mayo' capture to show that the violence B decried was caused by the very decapitation strategy B proposed, exposing a fatal solvency contradiction in PRO's case.
