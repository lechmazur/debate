### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the decisive clash on the proposition as written: whether social-insurance ledgers should count unpaid care rather than record zeros. A showed services/allowances and a universal floor can help without making care visible in the ledger, answered B's token-or-expensive and targeting traps without collapsing, and treated prevention and counting as complements. B had sharper timing/prevention rhetoric and exposed A's modest-credit concession, but repeatedly reframed the motion into "help now instead," and clipping hurt delivery; A's comparative case remained cleaner to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A1/A2 answers dissolving B's token-or-expensive dilemma and the two-child targeting trap by tying credits to the same carer-identification records B's own allowance needs, then weighing residual ledger zeros when services fail.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won the recognition clash. CON's prevention and floor arguments are strong but PRO showed counting now and counting later are not mutually exclusive, and that CON's own alternatives need the same carer identification. CON clipped twice and never answered why a ledger should stay zero when services fail, while PRO directly answered token-vs-expensive and the two-children targeting trap with capped, flat-rated, hours-thresholded credits funded like unemployment credits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's targeting self-refutation — B2.5/B4.3: if a carer allowance can identify the primary carer via assessment/hours threshold, a pension credit can use the same record, so CON's alternative collapses if its objection stands.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won decisively by forcing Side B (PRO) into a dilemma regarding the size and impact of the proposed pension credits. CON effectively demonstrated that if the credits are capped and cheap, they fail to prevent poverty any better than a universal basic pension, but add significant bureaucratic friction to verify past care hours. PRO's concession that the credit merely lifts caregivers "above destitution" allowed CON to argue that its alternative (UBP) achieves the same anti-poverty goal without the ledger's blind spots. PRO's retreat in the closing to defending the symbolic value of "the ledger" rather than the caregiver's immediate welfare ceded the practical high ground to CON, whose focus on immediate, tangible support (respite, leave, UBP) was far more compelling. Side A receives a minor execution penalty for two clipped turns, though the core arguments were fully delivered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it capitalized on PRO's concession that the credit only lifts the caregiver "above destitution." CON sharply pointed out that a universal basic pension already achieves this without the invasive, error-prone bureaucracy of assessing "hours thresholds" decades later, exposing the credit as a redundant and delayed symbol.
