Overall PRO built the clearer, more resilient path to victory and better resolved the live clash by the end.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but CON more effectively exploited a genuine tension in PRO's case. PRO initially insisted "strong pedagogical reason" was the only valid exception and that cost concerns did not qualify, but under pressure conceded that cost-driven substitution (cheaper transcript for captions) and consent-driven omission of recordings were compatible with the presumption, framing them as "the presumption operating intelligently" rather than exceptions. CON's rebuttal 2 and closing sharply forced the dichotomy: either these substitutions validate CON's "effective access" standard (conceding CON's framework) or the motion's literal text, which names specific tools with only a pedagogical-reason escape, imposes an unjustified hurdle even when access is already secured by other means. PRO never squarely resolved this textual/structural tension in its closing, instead reasserting the burden-shift rhetoric without explaining why consent- and cost-based substitutions fall inside the presumption as written. CON also preemptively neutralized PRO's central burden-shifting argument by offering its own proactive, non-diagnostic institutional accountability model. PRO's case remained principled and rhetorically strong on the stakes of exclusion, but CON did the sharper comparative work and left the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 2 (A4.4–A4.6), pressing that PRO's pressure-question answers (allowing transcript substitution and consent-based omission without invoking "strong pedagogical reason") effectively conceded CON's "effective access" framework rather than defending the motion's literal tool-specific presumption, was the decisive exchange; PRO's closing did not fully resolve this tension.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal brilliantly weaponized Side B's answers to the pressure questions, showing that allowing omissions for consent or cheaper equivalent formats meant abandoning the motion's strict "pedagogical reason" requirement.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by rigorously attacking the specific text of the motion. Side A successfully demonstrated that Side B's model only works if it ignores the restrictive "strong pedagogical reason" exception and substitutes it with a broader "effective access" standard that permits exceptions for consent, cost, and equivalent alternatives. Side B's attempt to frame these departures as "the presumption operating intelligently" effectively conceded Side A's core point that the motion as written is overly rigid, counterproductive, and requires rewriting to be defensible. Side A's rebuttals and closing crystallized this textual and logical clash perfectly, leaving Side B without a coherent defense of the actual proposition.
