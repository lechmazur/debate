### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing that information asymmetry turns leases into traps and, crucially, undermining CON's economic and incentive critique. CON could not resolve the contradiction between claiming disclosure stigmatizes buildings and claiming landlords profit by raising rents on exposed defects; PRO showed the "lemons discount" instead forces owners to fix problems to restore value. CON's enforcement-only alternative offered no mechanism to protect tenants before slow bureaucratic action, and PRO successfully analogized the disclosure standards to existing housing law concepts. By the end, PRO's path—that transparency creates market pressure for real repairs—remained intact while CON's central economic objection had retreated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's exposure of CON's economic self-contradiction: demonstrating that a landlord cannot charge higher rent for a building just exposed as defective, that disclosure instead imposes a lemons discount, and that this market pressure makes fixing the building cheaper than hiding it—a point CON effectively conceded by retreating to portfolio-wide offsets in Rebuttal 2.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): PRO won the central economic clash. CON argued disclosure is vague and only stigmatizes without fixing. PRO answered that "recurring/severe/chronic" can be operationalized like "material defect" via multi-household complaints, police logs, and tenant-retained emails, neutralizing the erasure/extortion claims. PRO's pressure Q2 exposed a contradiction – CON claimed disclosure both forces a discount [B1.4][B2.3] and lets landlords raise rents to offset it. In R2 CON retracted to a portfolio-offset theory [B4.2], which PRO showed is economically implausible. PRO's frame – who bears cost of known defect locked into a year lease – remained live, while CON's enforcement-only alternative was shown to allow concealment during slow bureaucracy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 pairing that landlord paper-trail erasure fails because emails, public STR listings, and police logs are tenant-controlled, and that stigma is the incentive to fix (parcel locker vs discount), forcing CON to concede the lemons discount in B4.2.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B built a significantly stronger causal and economic case. Side B successfully argued that the "lemons discount" created by mandatory disclosure aligns landlord incentives with physically fixing the problems (e.g., installing parcel lockers). Side A's attempt to counter this by claiming landlords would simply raise rents to offset the discount was economically incoherent and effectively dismantled by Side B, who rightly pointed out that a seller cannot charge a premium for a newly exposed defect. Side B also sharply exposed Side A's internal contradictions regarding the paper trail—specifically, Side A's simultaneous claims that landlords can unilaterally erase complaint records and that they are helpless victims of tenants manufacturing fake complaints. While Side A's "information does not equal safety" argument had rhetorical punch, Side B's economic weighing proved that disclosure is the mechanism that forces safety improvements. Side A's fallback to existing municipal enforcement was less convincing given the reality of slow bureaucracies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side A's economic claim that landlords would raise rents on exposed defective units, and exposed the logical contradiction in Side A's claims about landlords erasing paper trails while simultaneously being vulnerable to tenant extortion.
