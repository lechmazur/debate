### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A built a consistent lifecycle framework (cost it over decades, don't let one side's full system cost be compared against the other's "existing infrastructure plus chargers") and used the pressure-question round decisively: A's Q1/Q2 forced B to admit no commercially available electric aircraft exists at scale and, critically, that electrification "need not invent time savings from propulsion changes alone." A then converted that concession into the closing's sharpest line — preserving the status quo baseline is not the same as reducing travel time, which the motion explicitly requires. B's case (modularity, sunk-infrastructure reuse, capital-risk citations like California HSR/HS2) remained strong on cost-risk but never recovered from this definitional trap: B's own answers conceded electrification delivers no travel-time reduction, only non-degradation, leaving half the proposition's burden unaddressed by B's own admission. B's rebuttals repeated the modularity frame rather than directly neutralizing A's "you're crediting existing airport/jet-speed benefits to electrification, not caused by it" critique. A's progression across rounds was tighter and exploited the pressure-question exchange more effectively than B did.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Question 2 ("holding airport locations and procedures constant, what door-to-door time does electrification itself save?") and B's resulting concession in A2 of Rebuttal 2 that electrification "need not invent time savings from propulsion changes alone" — which A then weaponized in closing to argue B had conceded away half the proposition's required burden.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing that Side A could not demonstrate travel-time reductions under the proposition's terms—only preservation of an existing baseline—while rail offered a concrete mechanism to shorten door-to-door journeys and cut emissions through proven mode shift. Side B's sharper framing of the dual burden, stronger answers to pressure questions, and decisive closing crystallization gave it the clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's rebuttal and closing that CON had "relabelled maintaining travel time as saving it," directly answering Side A's pressure question by showing that electrification leaves airport access, security, and boarding unchanged, and therefore cannot claim cost-effective travel-time reductions.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO won the decisive clash on travel time. The proposition requires reducing both emissions AND travel time more cost-effectively. CON conceded in R2 it has no commercial e-aircraft at major-city volumes and admits electrification only preserves existing time – zero minutes saved. PRO's Pressure Q2 and R2 A2 exposed that, making dollars per minute saved infinite for electrification and leaving CON with evasive reframing. Rail's lifecycle spreading of costs over high-capacity frequent service remained unneutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q2 in Pressure – holding airports constant, what time does electrification itself save – plus B4 A2 answering that it saves none, turning CON's "preserve time" defense into a failure on half the proposition.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A built a clear, consistent causal story grounded in physics (energy density gap) and concrete examples (Madrid-Barcelona, TGV/AVE), and pressed it effectively through rebuttals, forcing B to concede at A3's pressure questions that no battery-electric airliner serves these routes today. That concession was significant: it handed A the central comparative claim—one option delivers measurable service now, the other delivers none. B's strongest move was insisting on full-cost accounting and a shared evaluation horizon, and correctly noting A never priced rail construction against aviation electrification costs, but B also admitted it could not provide its own cost estimate (A2 answer), which blunted the force of demanding precision from A. B's case increasingly became 'the proposition isn't proven for the whole category' rather than 'CON has a stronger affirmative case that electrification is comparably or more cost-effective,' which is a weaker rebuttal posture given the motion's framing. A's closing better crystallized the decisive asymmetry (delivered benefits vs. zero delivered benefits) while B's closing mostly restated the burden-of-proof objection without landing a independent comparative claim of its own. A also answered the PQ round more concretely and directly (A1/A2 answers with specifics) than B, whose answers conceded ground without recovering it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Questions (Q1/Q2) forcing B to concede no battery-electric aircraft exists for these routes and that B could not provide a cost estimate — this concession anchored A's closing argument about delivered vs. undelivered benefits and was never fully neutralized by B's subsequent 'shared horizon' reframe.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B established a clear positive case: high-speed rail is already operating on exactly these corridors and demonstrably collapses short-haul aviation demand, while battery-electric aviation faces fundamental energy-density limits that prevent commercial payload at 400–500 miles and remains speculative on shorter major routes. Side A delivered rigorous methodological critiques about capital-cost recovery, unequal comparisons, and category breadth, but conceded that no electric airliner serves the long end of the range and never provided a concrete major corridor where electrification would actually outperform rail. After the full exchange, B’s “proven construction project versus physics-limited laboratory promise” remained the cleaner path to victory, because the motion asks which option would deliver emissions and time savings more cost-effectively, and only one option is delivering them at all.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal, which exposed Side B’s redefinition of the evaluation period as merely “time to first passenger,” rejected the demand for invented price tags on epistemic grounds, and showed that B had not actually demonstrated capital-cost recovery or a consistent full-cost framework for new corridors.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping focus on the proposition's cost-effectiveness test. PRO proved rail is technically mature and that batteries are weak at 400-500 miles, but CON showed PRO counted only catenary vs full airport upgrades, treated operating surplus as capital recovery, used non-domestic examples, and used an 'infinite cost because zero service today' trick that would also fail unbuilt rail. CON conceded no current 400-500 mile electric airliner, preserving discipline, and forced a common-horizon comparison PRO never supplied.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2 catenary-only comparison and operating-surplus != capital-repayment, plus reclaiming Paris-London as non-domestic and reframing evaluation period.
