### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won a competitive debate by making the stronger net comparison: added, plural watchdog capacity under transparent safeguards versus existing news deserts, opaque donor influence, and absentee ownership. Side A compellingly exposed tensions between objective eligibility and editorial judgment, plus the risk of correlated self-censorship. However, it did not show that these residual risks would outweigh the direct deterrent value of reporters, records requests, and published local facts. It also repeatedly treated public support as replacing the fragmented private ecosystem rather than supplementing it. B more persuasively showed that corruption deterrence does not require universal trust and that even partisan-leaning recipients can produce useful accountability when bound by viewpoint-neutral structural duties.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange answered the trust objection by separating affection for an outlet from exposure of verifiable records: officials can still fear a published contract or records request even when some residents distrust the publisher. This materially weakened A’s claim that disputed legitimacy cancels corruption deterrence, though A retained force on whether assessing meaningful follow-through inevitably becomes editorial judgment.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively establishing that public funding replaces fragmented, outlet-specific private capture with correlated, systemic capture across all funded media. Side A successfully demonstrated that a firewall cannot prevent anticipatory self-censorship when the legislature retains ultimate control over appropriations and formulas.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 answer to Q2 decisively dismantled Side B's firewall defense by pointing out that newsrooms will preemptively protect the funding program itself, as the state acts simultaneously as funder, rulemaker, and subject of coverage.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON demonstrated that the firewall’s structural benchmarks either fund stenographic compliance or smuggle in editorial judgment, and that a common state funder creates correlated anticipatory self-censorship worse than fragmented private capture. PRO’s "deterrence by exposure" and "follow-through" tests were left vulnerable to CON’s trust-polarization linkage and the impossibility of truly neutral criteria.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 (A4), which exposed that legislative retaliation need not target individual grants to induce self-censorship, and that PRO’s "follow-through" benchmark collapses the supposed firewall between structural funding and editorial judgment.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B offered the stronger comparative: existing news deserts produce immediate losses in oversight and shared local facts, while capture requires overcoming transparent, general, multi-year safeguards. A identified a genuine anticipatory-obedience risk, but repeatedly treated statutory capture as nearly inevitable and overstated local officials' control over a higher-level formula. Its proposed vouchers and tax credits also resemble public funding with firewall governance, partially conceding the proposition, while private regranting was not shown sufficient to restore deserted newsrooms. B's failure to answer that alternative in closing kept the debate competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive response was that the relevant comparison is not perfect independence against captured public media, but formula-funded reporting against present absence and invisible private vetoes. Its explanation that retaliation would require a visible, generally applicable rule change substantially weakened A's claim that officials could readily punish one critical newsroom.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won by systematically defeating PRO's structural analogies (libraries, courts/auditors) and perfectly exploiting PRO's Pressure Question to introduce a viable alternative (citizen-directed funding/vouchers) that PRO's closing failed to address.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 effectively dismantled the courts/auditors analogy by pointing out that judges have fixed tenure and non-renewable shields, whereas journalism under PRO's model only gets a repealable statute.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON wins because it established a live structural asymmetry that PRO could not neutralize: journalism’s core function is continuous scrutiny of current officeholders, creating an ever-present incentive for anticipatory obedience that statutory, repealable firewalls cannot durably stop. CON’s pressure questions exposed that courts and auditors enjoy non-renewable shields like tenure and contempt powers, while PRO’s newsrooms rely only on general rules that the same legislators they cover can rewrite. CON also answered PRO’s “present cautious one versus none at all” framing by sketching a citizen-directed funding alternative that achieves the early record PRO wants without making the watched the paymaster. PRO’s closing never engaged that alternative, leaving PRO’s comparative as a false dichotomy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8) answer to PRO’s pressure questions: the distinction that judges and auditors hold tenure, fixed terms, and contempt powers while a newsroom gets only a repealable statute, and the introduction of citizen-directed vouchers and transparency infrastructure as a safer way to produce the same early record without giving the state a renewal lever over its watchdogs.
