### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by tying targeted industrial policy to the actual conditions of preferential US market access: regional-content rules, supplier development, infrastructure, and coordination failures. A also limited the policy through export conditions, phased requirements, and narrow tariffs, weakening B’s portrayal of indiscriminate protectionism. A’s repeated clipping was a meaningful execution cost, and its fiscal answer remained somewhat optimistic, keeping the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing response to B’s customs-enforcement alternative was decisive: policing rules of origin cannot itself create domestic suppliers. B’s drawback critique was rhetorically sharp, but it did not defeat A’s broader case for selective protection combined with export discipline and regional sourcing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by more convincingly grounding nearshoring industrial policy in the actual geopolitical trade environment, including U.S. tariff pressure on Chinese transshipment and the rules-of-origin logic embedded in North American trade agreements. A successfully turned CON's own Mexico auto success story against CON by showing it relied on NAFTA's regional-content rules, a protectionist-by-design mechanism that favored regional suppliers over outside inputs. While CON maintained persistent pressure on fiscal risks and debt constraints, PRO better crystallized the comparative choice: conditional protectionism and targeted coordination as the price of market access versus a passive model that polices compliance without building the supplier networks needed to qualify. PRO also answered pressure questions more directly, whereas CON evaded the direct yes/no on Mexico's tariffs and saw its best example reframed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing demonstration that CON's cited Mexico auto boom actually depended on NAFTA's regional-content rules—a protectionist mechanism discriminating against non-regional inputs—which turned CON's own evidence into support for PRO's managed-trade model.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins the central weighing: in a US market screening Chinese content and reviewing USMCA, regional-content protection is re-framed as entry ticket not self-sabotage, and fiscal spending is re-framed as grid/skills that CON conceded investors need. - **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that tariffs and fiscal risk can directly undermine nearshoring competitiveness, while offering active alternatives such as infrastructure concessions, training, certification, and governance reform.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing, which showed that PRO's pressure-question answers (duty drawbacks and sunset clauses) conceded that costs matter, thereby collapsing into CON's position and leaving the motion's unconditional language undefended.
