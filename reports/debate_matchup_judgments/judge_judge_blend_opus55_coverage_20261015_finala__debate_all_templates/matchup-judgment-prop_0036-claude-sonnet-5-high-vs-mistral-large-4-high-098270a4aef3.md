### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash on burden and comparison: PRO needed a live coercion/harm case strong enough to justify default prohibition, but kept describing structural surcharges and dystopian uses without grounding them in an actual market practice beyond ordinary discount design. CON repeatedly forced the distinction between “no bonus” and “penalty,” showed that ACA/targeted underwriting rules already cabined the worst uses, and weighed demonstrated voluntary-discount upside against speculative downside plus litigation chill from vague “strict limits.” A’s life-insurance and dual-power points stayed live and rhetorically sharp, but never fully neutralized B’s cleaner path that the motion overcorrects.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4’s answer that declining a wellness discount is the status-quo base rate rather than a surcharge, paired with the blood-panel analogy: regulate pregnancy/racial proxies directly instead of killing the voluntary channel.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully showed that PRO's coercion and discrimination harms were speculative and better addressed by targeted regulation than by blanket prohibition. PRO never substantiated a concrete case where declining wearable data raised premiums above the standard rate, mischaracterized CON's voluntary position as mandatory data-sharing, and failed to explain why banning the entire data channel was necessary when existing anti-discrimination rules and market calibration already address the concrete risks raised. CON's framing of wellness discounts as genuine voluntary benefits, the blood-panel analogy demonstrating that regulating use outperforms prohibiting the channel, and the closing burden-of-proof argument provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal [B4.1]-[B4.4], which exposed PRO's "surcharge" framing as a relabeling of standard base rates, answered pregnancy and racial bias concerns with the blood-panel analogy to show that targeted regulation outperforms blanket prohibition, and crystallized that speculative harms cannot justify deleting a concrete voluntary benefit.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively dismantling Side B's core coercion argument. A effectively distinguished between a "surcharge" and the "absence of a discount," repeatedly challenging B to provide a single concrete example of a premium rising above the standard baseline rate for refusing data—a challenge B could not meet. A also provided a more practical regulatory alternative (existing targeted anti-discrimination rules) for B's bias and pregnancy concerns, whereas B's reliance on vague "strict limits" was successfully framed by A as a litigation risk that would kill consumer-benefiting innovation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically [A4.1] and [A4.3], where A cleanly refuted the "surcharge" framing by comparing it to standard market loyalty discounts and neutralized the bias argument by pointing to existing targeted anti-discrimination laws that already police protected-class proxies regardless of the data source.
