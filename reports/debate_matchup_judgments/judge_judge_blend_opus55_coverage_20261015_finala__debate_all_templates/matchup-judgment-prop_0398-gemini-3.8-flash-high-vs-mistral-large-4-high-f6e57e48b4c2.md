### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by distinguishing the value labor creates from the market price required to obtain it, then linking headquarters premiums to replacement costs and competitive sustainability. Side A sharply challenged whether a remote worker really enters a local labor market, but its answer depended too heavily on assuming relocated workers are scarce, headquarters-caliber talent with unchanged global outside options. That exception did not defeat B’s broader claim about what workers should generally expect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s value-versus-price response was decisive. It directly answered Side A’s repeated claim that identical output requires identical pay and exposed why unchanged employer revenue does not by itself establish an entitlement to the original wage.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won the market-definition clash. PRO's live route required that relocating = entering a cheaper local labor market with lower replacement cost. CON directly damaged that with the outsourcing paradox [B2.2/B4.3]: if identical HQ-caliber talent were available locally at half price, firms would have already outsourced. PRO never answered, merely re-asserted local hiring. CON also won asymmetry: PRO says pay isn't a guarantee of purchasing power when rent rises [A2.3] but defends cuts when personal costs fall, exposing opportunistic one-way adjustment. PRO's sustainability warning was neutralized by CON's scarcity answer to A3 Q1.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's outsourcing paradox in R1/R2 - if Lisbon local rates set replacement cost, why haven't enterprises outsourced entire HQ workforce - collapsed A's marginal local-cost premise and was never squarely answered.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively dismantling Side B's core premise of "local replacement cost." Side A effectively argued that if headquarters-caliber talent were easily replaceable at local municipal rates, firms would have already offshored their entire workforce. By distinguishing between the borderless enterprise market for elite talent and local domestic labor markets, Side A neutralized Side B's market-pricing argument. Side A also leveraged the asymmetry of cost-of-living adjustments to frame location-based pay cuts as corporate rent-seeking, a point Side B failed to adequately overcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered the pressure questions and then collapsed Side B's "local replacement cost" foundation by pointing out that the scarcity of elite talent means the true replacement market is borderless, not municipal.
