### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by tying burnout and isolation to workplace conditions—unreliable recovery and thin relationships—then showing that time, coverage, and paid contact address those causes more directly than solitary coping tools. B conceded that meaningful reform can require real spending, which was consistent with the proposition rather than fatal to it.

When Side A conceded that its plan required additional money beyond the trade-off, Side B decisively framed this as stripping away affordable, immediate relief for an unfunded fantasy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first and second rebuttals attacking the budget math, pointing out that shifting per-seat app pennies cannot buy salary and benefits for coverage staffing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing the debate around root causes versus symptom management. While Side B made a strong logistical argument that wellness apps are cheaper than hiring extra staff for coverage, Side A effectively parried this by distinguishing between predictability that requires headcount and predictability that merely requires management discipline (e.g., publishing rotas early, enforcing boundaries). Side A's rhetoric was sharper, and their closing crystallized the core trade-off memorably: paying for structural recovery versus paying workers to tolerate unchanged, exhausting conditions. Side B's rebuttals became somewhat repetitive on the budget math, failing to fully engage with Side A's discipline vs. headcount distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1), where they clarified that the budget shift isn't a magical 1:1 license-to-nurse swap, but rather funding marginal cover while relying on management discipline for the rest of the predictability, effectively neutralizing Side B's main budget attack.
