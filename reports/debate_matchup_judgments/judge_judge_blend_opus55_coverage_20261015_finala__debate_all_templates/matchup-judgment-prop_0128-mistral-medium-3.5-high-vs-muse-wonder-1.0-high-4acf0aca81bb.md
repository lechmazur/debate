- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively weaponizing the resolution's wording, consistently isolating the difference between a credential being a 'useful proxy' and an 'absolute gate.'   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and Closing effectively dismantled Side B's 'bet on potential' argument by pointing out that a mandatory degree requirement is not a bet, but a refusal to look at any other evidence of that potential.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B won by locking the debate to the proposition’s necessity standard and forcing A’s concession that degrees should remain mandatory even when not needed for day-one performance.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Pressure Q1 plus the later crystallization that a candidate who already passes work samples and shows adult persistence is still excluded only for lacking a credential—not for lacking job capacity.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A successfully controlled the central clash by distinguishing between a degree as a "useful" heuristic and a "necessary" requirement. Side B relied heavily on the convenience and scalability of the degree filter but struggled to justify why it must remain a mandatory requirement rather than a strong preference, ultimately conceding that degrees are useful even when not strictly necessary for day-one performance, which Side A expertly capitalized on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's lack of a limiting principle by showing that justifying a degree requirement purely on "unbounded potential" renders the concept of "genuinely necessary" meaningless, and clearly answered the pressure questions with practical alternatives like job analysis and tiered screening.
