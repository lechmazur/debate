### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the decisive clash on targeting: a trip-count escalator systematically misprices carbon (two long-haul luxury trips vs many short hops) and is gamed by rotating staff, while fuel/distance/cabin taxes cut low-value marginal trips without that proxy failure. PRO’s best frame—make the 40th flight dearer—never overcame that mechanism problem; once PRO conceded base carbon/distance charges from flight one, the distinctive personal counter looked like pure collateral punishment plus tracking cost. PRO’s pressure questions were sharp, but CON answered them coherently; PRO’s own “near-zero first flights” vs layered base-tax story stayed sticky and undercut the occasional-flyer benefit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A1/A2 on corporate travel: total meeting demand can be inelastic while which employee flies is elastic—so a per-person escalator changes boarding-pass names, not emissions—paired with the long-haul-vs-short-hops carbon mismatch.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON's live route survived: frequency poorly proxies emissions (six short economy hops vs two long-haul first-class), per-person counting is gamed by rotating staff among employees, and layering a distance/cabin base undercuts PRO's own "first flights near zero" progressive promise. PRO never answered corporate splitting and conceded base taxes must stay, making the escalator redundant; CON showed a high uniform carbon price already cuts lowest-value marginal trips first. Privacy/tracking costs reinforced CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1 distinction — total corporate trips inelastic but who flies is elastic, so firms send 4 people x10 trips not 1x40 — resolved B's charge of contradiction and left PRO's per-person levy with no emissions reduction.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won decisively by exposing a fatal flaw in the frequent-flyer levy: corporate tax avoidance via staff rotation. Side A brilliantly distinguished between inelastic total demand for meetings and elastic per-employee trip counts, showing that companies would simply rotate staff to avoid the escalator without reducing emissions. Side B mistakenly labeled this a contradiction and failed to resolve it. Side A also maintained stronger philosophical grounding by insisting on pricing the carbon rather than the passenger, and effectively highlighted the equity flaws in Side B's layered tax model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's clarification in Rebuttal 2 that corporate travel demand is inelastic but the identity of the traveler is elastic, completely neutralizing Side B's pressure question and exposing the staff-rotation loophole in the frequent-flyer levy.
