### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): B identified and relentlessly exploited the motion's core structural demand: a "headline target" must be a single, steerable, fundable number. B's pressure question ("what single weighting becomes the headline target?") forced A into answering with "a transparent dashboard" with no pre-set weighting — effectively conceding that PRO's actual proposal is the supplement CON already endorsed, not a replacement headline target. B returned to this concession in both Rebuttal 2 and Closing, making it the decisive, unresolved fault line. A's strongest material (opioid crisis, UK austerity, insulin/yacht framing) scored rhetorical points on GDP's blind spots, but never resolved the funding-capacity rebuttal (a shrinking GDP base can't sustainably pay for the services a well-being score promises) or the weighting/accountability problem, instead repeating "it makes trade-offs visible" without showing how a multi-dimensional dashboard functions as a single primary target. A's case also suffered real execution costs: both the opening and closing were clipped mid-argument, weakening its framing and denying it a clean final crystallization, while B's closing was complete, sharp, and directly tied to the live clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Question Q1 ("what single weighting would become the primary headline target, and who decides it?") and A's subsequent answer retreating to an unweighted dashboard, which B then used in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to show PRO had effectively conceded the motion's central requirement.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 3 (PRO)): Side A won by exposing that Side B never supplied an operational replacement for GDP. When pressed on conflicting well-being dimensions, B retreated to an unweighted dashboard, substantially conceding A’s proposed model of GDP as anchor plus supplementary indicators. B also failed to answer the recurring-resource objection: reallocating existing output may improve distribution, but does not explain how sustained services are financed amid shrinking productive capacity. A converted both pressure answers into clear comparative reasons for accountability and policy steering. B compellingly showed GDP’s moral and informational limits, but overstated some historical examples, had both its opening and closing clipped, and did not establish why well-being should function as the primary target rather than an essential constraint or dashboard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used B’s own admission that well-being would be an unweighted dashboard to argue that PRO had rebranded CON’s supplement as a headline target, then showed that B’s yacht-to-insulin reallocation did not answer how a smaller economic base sustains recurring public services.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 3 (PRO)): Side A showed that Side B's well-being "headline target" was actually a weightless dashboard—the very supplement Side A advocated—while proving GDP provides the accountable, timely fiscal anchor that funds well-being. Side B never solved the weighting problem or the attribution lag, and its clipped speeches and unconvincing answers to funding pressure cost it further ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2 turning the OECD Better Life Index against Side B, demonstrating that an 11-dimension dashboard with user-defined weights is a supplement, not a primary headline target, which Side B effectively conceded in its Rebuttal 2 answer.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 3 (CON)): A built a consistent, clean framework (GDP as instrument, well-being as goal) and sustained it across every round, turning B's strongest card—GDP's "objectivity"—back on itself by showing GDP's conventions, gameability, and silent weighting are just as political. A's pressure questions forced a decisive moment: B's answer to Q1 (A4.1 in B's Rebuttal 2) conceded that GDP rising while health, pay, and satisfaction fall "is not success—it is failure by any meaningful standard," which directly undercuts B's entire case for keeping GDP as the primary headline. A's closing seized on this concession with surgical precision, while B's closing largely repeated the discipline/finite-resources argument without repairing the contradiction. B also suffered real execution costs: both the Opening and Rebuttal 1 were clipped mid-argument, losing content (including the "oxygen" metaphor payoff) that the judge never got to see in full, which is a legitimate penalty under the judging criteria. B's pressure questions were sharp and exposed a real tension in A's discipline claims, and A's answers, while adequate, did not fully resolve how a well-being target self-polices against unsustainable borrowing beyond asserting that debt stress is "inside" the metric. Still, on balance A controlled the central clash more effectively, forced the more damaging concession, and closed with the cleaner, more memorable framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing highlighting B's own concession in Rebuttal 2 (B4.1: "that is not success—it is failure by any meaningful standard") was the most decisive moment, since it used B's direct answer to A's pressure question to collapse B's case for GDP primacy from the inside.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won the core ranking question: GDP is an important resource constraint but cannot be the primary test of success when output rises alongside worsening lives. The closing returned to fiscal discipline but never established why GDP growth itself reliably supplies that discipline.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the fiscal-constraint argument was decisive: borrowing can raise current GDP, so GDP growth does not inherently prevent unsustainable spending. B then used A’s admission that GDP can rise while lives worsen to show that the instrument cannot outrank the outcomes it serves.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A clipped two speeches, losing developmental space, and then delivered a fatal concession in Rebuttal 2, admitting that GDP-driven “failure” when lives worsen is exactly “the problem we are solving by replacing it.” Side B seized that admission, showed well-being frameworks can incorporate fiscal discipline rather than abolish it, and turned A’s “oxygen” analogy to reinforce that instruments serve ends. B’s cleaner comparative framing and direct answers to pressure questions leave a clear route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2, where B answered A’s pressure questions directly, neutralized the “gaming” objection, and turned the “oxygen” analogy to show that clinicians judge the patient, not the gas tank.
