### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won because third-party harm supplied a clear case where genuine bilateral consent cannot establish overall ethicality. Side A's appeal to pollution law did not answer the moral question; legal remedies themselves reflect standards beyond the parties' consent.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's river-pollution challenge was decisive. Side A called externalities an adjacent legal issue, but never explained how an agreement causing foreseeable harm to nonconsenting people becomes ethical merely because its two parties agreed.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A (CON) won by forcing the decisive clash onto who decides and what remains after existing duress/externality law is stripped out, then holding that ground through both rebuttals and the close. A’s opening clip was a real cost, but later clash management was sharper and made the vote easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s separation of the PRO case into three already-distinct issues (procedural consent defects, third-party externalities, and leftover bilateral “fairness”)—especially in Rebuttal 2—left B defending outsider veto over ordinary adult bargains.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's recursive categorization in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 distinguishing procedural consent defects, third-party externalities, and ordinary adult exchanges, which revealed Side B's fusion of separate issues and showed no non-arbitrary way to limit outsider veto over competent adult transactions.
