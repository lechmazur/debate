### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON won by narrowing the live clash to the proposition’s real burden: default/priority status, not whether privacy-preserving wallets should exist. PRO kept winning the easy comparison against crude passport-upload honeypots and made strong points on unlinkability, dark patterns, and startup per-check tolls, but CON repeatedly forced a harder metric—regulated availability of anonymous verification with fair presentation and no steering—and showed that reusable wallets, local auth/passkeys, and outcome rules capture much of PRO’s friction and privacy upside without making other compliant routes secondary. PRO never cleanly separated cryptographic advantages of wallets from advantages of default status, while CON’s enforcement-symmetry answer (fallbacks need the same scrutiny PRO dismisses for equal choice) left PRO’s bright-line closing undercut. Competitive debate; CON made the decision rule easier to apply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 / closing: availability plus anti-steering already yields reusable anonymous checks; default only adds steering, and enforcing “equally usable free fallbacks” requires the interface/cost scrutiny PRO claims only defaults can avoid.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won because it established that default status is necessary to overcome platform incentives: data-hungry services will steer users toward identity-exposing checks unless a privacy-preserving wallet is the primary gateway, and policing fair presentation across thousands of sites is less practical than a bright-line default rule. Side B effectively separated availability from default, but never resolved why platforms would prominently offer anonymous verification without default priority. Side A also better crystallized the cost and privacy comparison, showing that cryptographic handshakes avoid repeated per-check broker tolls and repeated identity exposure across dozens of sites. Side B’s credential-sharing concern was neutralized when both sides conceded that local biometrics protect either system equally. Side A’s execution was marred by nonexistent reference IDs in its closing, but this did not erase its live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s dark-pattern argument in Rebuttal 2: it showed that mere availability would be undermined by platform design choices nudging users toward data-rich ID checks, making a default the only enforceable way to make privacy real. Side B’s counter that regulators could police fair presentation was exposed as weaker than a bright-line rule in Side A’s closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won the default-vs-availability clash. PRO's strongest privacy volume claim (30 exposures) was answered in B4.1: reusable wallets work under guaranteed availability, so no 30x check needed. ### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON (A) wins a competitive debate by keeping pressure on two live practical comparisons PRO never fully neutralizes: under broad EU minor-protection liability, frictionless wallets remove the only effective brake on age-gating ordinary speech, and PRO’s answer (ban unjustified gates / no safe harbor) is more wish than workable sorting rule; and “default” still leaves platform ID fallbacks intact for desktops, non-phone users, and unsupported devices, so identity collection is stacked rather than replaced. PRO is sharper on pure principle—don’t use invasiveness as the regulatory brake; separate when checks are allowed from what is disclosed—and correctly exposes CON’s overstated “alternative = free bypass of age proof” error, but those wins do not erase CON’s stronger path on real deployment and overreach. Engineering (attestation/OS gatekeeping vs unlinkable proofs) ends roughly mixed; household sharing is not decisive for either side once both concede cooperating adults and weak local auth. After full exchange, CON makes the motion easier to reject on comparative grounds than PRO makes it easy to affirm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s liability/friction rebuttal chain (A2.1–A2.2, A4.4, A5.1–A5.3): frictionless wallets plus vague “harmful content” duties drive papers-at-every-door overuse; PRO’s companion legislative fix does not square the circle.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): CON wins by showing that the wallet default does not replace platform-specific ID checks but instead stacks a frictionless, continent-wide credentialing layer on top of them. CON's scope-creep and fallback arguments remain live: because millions lack compatible devices or documents, platforms must maintain the old verification pipelines indefinitely, and the removal of friction under broad EU liability incentives would push age-gating onto ordinary news, forums, and search tools. PRO's response—to simply prohibit unjustified checks—was exposed as an optimistic legislative fix that underestimates how risk-averse platforms behave under vague duties of care. PRO successfully framed the moral high ground that privacy should not depend on making verification unpleasant, but CON neutralized this by demonstrating that a wallet default normalizes cryptographic identity checks without retiring the fragmented system PRO attacks, leaving users with more infrastructure, not less surveillance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's demonstration that accessible fallbacks permanently preserve platform-specific checks while a frictionless default incentivizes broader age-gating, creating a dual-layer surveillance system rather than a replacement.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO kept the decisive comparison on what ordinary compliance costs: narrow threshold proof vs repeated identity exposure. CON's strongest cards - frictionless scope-creep and hardware attestation - were answered by separating when checks are justified from how they are satisfied and by requiring issuer-checked, unlinkable proofs. CON failed to answer Pressure Q2: clicking "alternative" still requires adult proof, so it bypasses the wallet format but not age assurance, undermining the claim that fallback = defeat. Friction-as-brake argument that privacy should stay unpleasant to limit gating lost to direct regulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1/Q2 pressure plus R2 handling of household sharing and fallback: exposing unequal standards for wallet PIN vs platform account and showing fallback reduces aggregate disclosure even if maintained.
