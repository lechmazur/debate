### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued coherently, but B built the more damaging and sustained critique. B's opening identified the structural flaw (labels can be stripped, metadata doesn't survive real virality, and most authentic content—voicemails, forwarded chats, screen recordings—is naturally unlabeled, so "missing label = suspicious" inverts reality). A's rebuttal leaned on a spam-filter/HTTPS analogy that B convincingly dismantled by noting those mechanisms work against attacker will, whereas a "synthetic" tag is a voluntary self-report that only the honest will provide. B also exploited A's own concession ("never promised to hunt down...anonymous posters") to reframe the regime as a "confession system," and pressed the enforcement paradox: since liars can't be caught, liability falls on platforms, which then over-block and quietly rebuild identity-tracking pressure — the very outcome the proposition claims to avoid. A's answers to pressure questions retreated to "platform-uploaded content" without ever resolving the core B objection about content that travels through texts, calls, and messaging apps outside any platform's upload gate, leaving a central vulnerability unaddressed through the final rebuttal and closing. B's closing crystallized this double-bind cleanly, while A's closing largely restated its original framing without directly neutralizing B's strongest line. B's answers to A's own pressure questions (A1/A2) were also clean and format-compliant, offering a coherent alternative (enforce existing fraud/defamation/election law) that went unanswered in A's closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 turning A's "we're not hunting ghosts" concession into "a confession regime that relies on liars to confess," combined with the point that authentic real-world content (voicemails, forwarded clips) is normally unlabeled, which undercuts A's core "missing label = suspicious" claim and was never fully resolved by A in later rounds.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that the mandate binds compliant platforms and creators more reliably than deceptive actors. Side A preserved a plausible transparency benefit, but its platform-upload solution never explained how a platform can distinguish an authentic unlabeled recording from a laundered synthetic one without self-reporting, aggressive filtering, or greater tracking. B also directly answered both pressure questions and crystallized the tradeoff more clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s distinction between infrastructure imposed against an attacker and a synthetic label dependent on truthful declaration decisively weakened A’s spam-filter analogy. Its follow-up that ordinary authentic footage also lacks provenance exposed the central problem with treating missing signals as suspicious.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B systematically dismantled Side A's mechanisms, proving that since bad actors can easily strip labels, the mandate would train voters to trust unmarked deepfakes. Furthermore, Side B successfully argued that placing liability on platforms at upload would inevitably lead to backdoor identity verification, directly violating the spirit of the resolution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal effectively neutralized Side A's HTTPS/spam filter analogy by pointing out that unlike infrastructure-enforced encryption, a synthetic tag relies on the malicious actor to self-declare.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Both sides executed the format well, correctly labeling Q1/Q2 and A1/A2. A built a coherent initial case (provenance-not-identity, the "deny camouflage" mechanism, apt analogies to browser certificates and spam filters) and kept a clean rhetorical throughline. But B's "two-tier" critique evolved more sharply across rounds: it correctly resolved A's "theater vs. surveillance" pressure question by distinguishing effects on honest compliant users versus evading bad actors, rather than being caught in contradiction, and it landed a decisive, unanswered point in Rebuttal 2 — that under A's own "origin unknown" mechanism, an anonymous whistleblower's leak and a fraudster's clone look identically suspect, while a public figure's professionally captured denial gets the trusted "captured" label. This directly undercuts A's central claim that labeling without ID protects the vulnerable while exposing the powerful; A's closing simply reasserts "it is her cover" without explaining how audiences would distinguish the two cases it had just conceded look alike. That unresolved asymmetry, plus B's sharper and more adaptive rhetoric ("surveillance architecture with a security placebo," "worst of both worlds"), gives Con the cleaner path to victory, though the race stayed competitive throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 point that under A's mandate a whistleblower's genuine leak and a fraudster's clone both arrive as "origin unknown" while a public figure's authentic denial carries a trusted "captured" label — directly undermining A's claim that labeling protects the vulnerable without ID, and left largely unanswered in A's closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by drawing a credible distinction between identifying a file’s provenance and identifying its uploader, then explaining how distribution-level labels still impose friction when bad actors strip metadata. Side B’s scarlet-letter concern remained meaningful, and its asymmetric answer to the tracking-versus-toothlessness challenge was strong. However, B repeatedly assumed that provenance necessarily reveals creator identity or requires centralized verification without establishing that link, while its voluntary-standards alternative was underdeveloped. A therefore offered the clearer comparative benefit without conceding centralized identity controls.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answers in A4.1-A4.2 were decisive: platforms can display file-level provenance without demanding passports, while stripped adversarial media loses the ability to masquerade as verified capture. This directly answered B’s enforcement questions and preserved A’s main middle-path claim.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by systematically dismantling the opposition's dilemma between a surveillance honeypot and a toothless placebo. Side A effectively used practical analogies (spam filters, counterfeit bills) to prove the policy's mechanism and won the comparative weighing on whistleblowers by showing that Side B's alternative leaves them competing against indistinguishable fakes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was the decisive point of the debate, effectively neutralizing the liability/honeypot argument with the spam filter analogy and turning the "toothless" argument by showing that an "origin unknown" label actively removes the camouflage needed for mass fraud.
