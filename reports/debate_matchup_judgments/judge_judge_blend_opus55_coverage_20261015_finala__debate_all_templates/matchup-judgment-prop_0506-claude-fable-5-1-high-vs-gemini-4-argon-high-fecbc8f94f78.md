### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the unresolved gap between an allegation being repeated and a synthetic scam being reliably verified. Side B established a strong moral and incentive case for liability, but its operational test ultimately relied heavily on the depicted person disavowing the solicitation. Side A showed why that creates an asymmetric incentive to remove contested authentic content and then distinguished prospective regulatory standards from retrospective, potentially large tort damages. Side B answered causation plausibly, but never fully neutralized the ex ante chilling and adjudication-cost arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange came in A4.1-A4.6: it directly answered Side B’s claim that only actual fraud creates risk, explaining that platforms make removal decisions before fraud is adjudicated, then used the genuine endorsement later disavowed by its subject to show how Side B’s verification standard could collapse into self-certification. Side B’s regulator-versus-court contradiction was sharp, but Side A’s distinction between bounded takedown enforcement and individualized causation and damages substantially answered it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A won by making the motion's load-bearing term—"verified"—collapse under realistic pressure. B's opening promised that repeated verified notice makes ambiguity vanish and cabins free-speech harm to scams, not satire; A answered that platforms face reports alleging fraud, not adjudicated fraud, then forced B to admit fully generated/voice-clone cases verify by self-certification plus attachments. A's asymmetry point (remove free / leave-up risky) and the genuine-but-disavowed celebrity solicitation example better explained chill without needing a politician plaintiff. B scored on fraud requiring actual loss and on victim recovery vs fines alone, but never fully rescued proximate cause when money closes off-platform, while A kept a cleaner alternative path: defined regulatory clocks plus payment friction. Comparative edge to A after full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (esp. A4.2–A4.6): celebrity self-disavowal counts as "verified," regulator clocks ≠ unbounded causation/damages, and no-original scams become self-certification.

CON's regulatory-clock vs tort-causation distinction answers PRO's pressure and leaves PRO's compensation story without a workable limiting principle.
