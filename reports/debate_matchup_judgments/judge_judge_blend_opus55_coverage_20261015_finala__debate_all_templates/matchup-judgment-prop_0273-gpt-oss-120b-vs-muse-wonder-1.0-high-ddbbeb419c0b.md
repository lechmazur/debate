### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): The debate turned on whether baggage and seat-selection fees are genuinely "mandatory" (A's load-bearing premise) or merely optional purchases families can decline (B's counter). B's mandatory-vs-optional distinction was conceptually cleaner and B exploited it relentlessly: a bag fee you can decline is not a tax, and seats can be assigned together without paid selection. A's attempt to define a "baseline bundle" (A4.1) collapsed under its own weight when it then introduced toggles and personalized totals (A4.3) to handle light travelers — B's rebuttal (B4.3-B4.4) correctly identified this as a fatal dilemma: if the total changes per family it is no longer a common pre-comparison price, and if it doesn't change it is fictional. A never resolved this bind in its closing, instead reasserting the baseline-bundle claim. B's pressure questions forced this contradiction into the open, and B's answers to A's own questions (A1/A2) were direct and format-compliant, while A's Rebuttal 1 was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, costing it a full counter-argument. B's closing crystallized the clash memorably ("a price everyone must pay" vs "a purchase someone assumes they will make"), giving the clearer decision rule. A had a reasonable opening and cited EU precedent, but never neutralized the core conceptual flaw B identified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3-B4.4), which exposed that A's "baseline bundle" definition is either fictional (if fixed) or no longer a common pre-comparison standard (if personalized via toggles) — a contradiction A never resolved in its closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO)): Side A won dominantly by establishing a critical distinction between truly unavoidable fees and optional purchases. Side B was forced into the untenable position of defending optional checked bags and paid seat selection as universally mandatory, which Side A easily dismantled. Side B also committed an unforced error in its first Pressure Question by essentially asking Side A to defend Side B's burden, which Side A effortlessly capitalized on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 expertly neutralized Side B's EU comparison by pointing out that the EU mandates inclusion of truly unavoidable charges, not optional baggage or seat selection, perfectly cementing Side A's core distinction.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO)): Side A (CON) decisively won by drawing a sharp, unassailable distinction between unavoidable mandatory fees (like taxes) and optional ancillary fees (like checked bags and paid seat selection). Side A effectively demonstrated that forcing a "full practical cost" upfront either requires inventing a fictional "standard family" (which misleads light travelers and status holders) or moving the entire checkout questionnaire to the search page (which destroys quick comparison). Side B (PRO) struggled to defend the premise that checked bags and paid seats are universally mandatory for all families, and their pivot to "personalized totals" essentially conceded Side A's critique that they were just moving the booking flow earlier. Side A's rhetoric was also exceptionally crisp, culminating in a highly memorable closing framing ("A mandate that must invent the buyer cannot claim to reveal the price").
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.4), which sharply exposed the contradiction in Side B's defense: if the upfront total is a standardized "baseline," it is fictional for many families; if it is personalized via toggles and inputs, it is no longer a common pre-comparison price but simply the existing booking calculation moved earlier with more chances to go stale.
