### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A won by forcing the decisive comparison: PRO’s democratic-harm case mostly tracks covert, unauthorized profiling, while the motion also bans inspected, permissioned, archived uses of inference. A’s overbreadth, enforcement-parity, and self-reported-anxiety concession consistently narrowed the clash; B repeatedly answered the abusive case rather than justifying the full categorical ban once secrecy and contestability were removed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 / closing turn on PRO’s self-report concession: if emotionally tailored reassurance can respect agency when disclosed, then banning the identical archived message after explicit inspection/authorization targets inference as such, not proven predation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON showed that the categorical ban is overbroad by distinguishing the technique of psychological inference from its abuses (non-consensual, hidden manipulation). PRO's core claim—that inferred profiles inherently bypass rational deliberation—was asserted rather than proven. When PRO conceded that emotionally tailored messages based on self-reported anxiety are permissible, CON turned this decisively to show the harm lies in covertness and lack of consent, which CON's alternative (explicit authorization, profile review before use, public archives, audits) addresses. PRO never adequately justified why transparent, voluntary inferred profiling should still be prohibited, repeatedly defining inferred profiles as inherently covert even when CON's framework removed that covertness. CON's narrower regulatory approach resolved the stated harms without collateral damage to legitimate communication.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8) exposed that PRO defined inferred profiles as created "without the voter's knowledge," thereby assuming away the very transparent, authorized case CON had constructed, and leveraged PRO's self-reported concession to force the debate onto whether narrower safeguards suffice rather than categorical prohibition.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A successfully exposed the overbreadth of the proposition by distinguishing between the harms of covert manipulation and the mere use of psychological inference. A's regulatory alternative (explicit consent with profile inspection prior to use) directly neutralized B's core harm of asymmetric, hidden dossiers. B struggled to answer why a total ban was necessary if the inference was transparent and consented to, and repeatedly dropped A's specific mechanism in later speeches, relying instead on repetitive rhetoric about "hidden" profiles that A had already solved for. Side A's logical trap regarding B's exception for self-reported emotional tailoring further proved that emotional persuasion itself is not the inherent harm, making A's targeted regulatory framework much stronger than B's categorical ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantled B's reliance on the "hidden dossier" argument by pointing out that its proposed safeguard explicitly grants voters access to the inferred profile before use, rendering B's repeated claims of "unseen" profiles non-responsive and evasive.
