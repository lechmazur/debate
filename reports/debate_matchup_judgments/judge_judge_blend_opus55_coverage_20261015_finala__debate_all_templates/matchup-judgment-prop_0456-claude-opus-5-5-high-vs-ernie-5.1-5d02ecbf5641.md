### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by cleanly leveraging the exact text of the motion against Side A's narrative, proving that the proposition actively empowers dominant buyers rather than protecting small creators. Side B also dismantled Side A's core mechanical license analogy, effectively using the historical reality of frozen rates to show the dangers of removing exit rights.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal and subsequent pressure questions brilliantly exposed the fatal contradiction in Side A's case: Side A claimed the motion protects independents from powerful studios, yet the motion explicitly gives those very studios the right to train on creators' works without asking.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive clash: the motion itself transfers the right to train without permission to publishers, labels, and studios, so PRO’s “protect independents from leverage” story conflicts with the text. CON better showed refusal is the source of real bargains, covers ≠ replacement training, and exitless rate boards historically freeze under buyer pressure. PRO’s logistics/payment-floor case was competent but never neutralized that structural contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R1 turn exposing PRO’s leverage claim as inverted by the motion: the same big players get guaranteed training rights while independents lose refusal.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON kept the decisive clash on consent as bargaining power. PRO's case for guaranteed payment collapsed into a contradiction CON exposed: the motion gives publishers/labels/studios the right to train without asking [B2.5][B5.1], while PRO claimed it targets them for their leverage. CON also won the analogy fight (cover vs adaptation/substitute) and the capture fight (frozen mechanical rate as structural result of no-exit), while PRO's answers to pressure on rate-setting and illustrator exit were generic "independence and review" and mischaracterized CON on opt-out chaos.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 reversal on leverage: showing PRO's own premise that big players have leverage means compulsory licensing hands them a guaranteed option and strips the small creator's only lever, plus correcting PRO's false dilemmas on voluntary collectives as "a list, not chaos" and aggregation solving logistics.
