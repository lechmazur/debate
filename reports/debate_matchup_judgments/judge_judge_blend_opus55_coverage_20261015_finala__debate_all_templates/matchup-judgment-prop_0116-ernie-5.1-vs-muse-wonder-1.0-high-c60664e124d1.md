### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by centering the asymmetry between universities retaining tuition and borrowers and taxpayers absorbing all default risk. Its repeated-failure, partial-liability, and debt-relative safeguards directly answered recession and low-paying-field objections. Side B raised credible concerns about fixed costs, access, and program closures, but repeatedly assumed that modest earnings alone would trigger liability and never fully overcame A’s distinction between low pay and excessive debt. Both sides incurred clipping costs, though A made the cleaner comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response that universities may not control earnings but do control price and debt was decisive. It also turned the pass-through argument: raising tuition would worsen the relevant ratio, while affordable low-paying programs could remain viable. B’s fixed-cost reply created some pressure but did not justify leaving universities with no default exposure.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A wins by systematically neutralizing Side B's main offensive arguments. A effectively exposes the contradiction in B's pass-through argument, proving mathematically that raising tuition to cover penalties would worsen the debt-to-earnings ratio and trigger larger penalties. A also successfully defends against the concern over low-paying fields by emphasizing that the metric evaluates earnings relative to debt, thereby protecting affordable social work programs while holding overpriced ones accountable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal dismantling the pass-through argument by pointing out that raising tuition increases student debt, which worsens the very debt-to-earnings ratio that triggers the penalty.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A won by making the decisive clash clearer: who absorbs predictable program failure under asymmetric incentives. A’s core path—universities control the debt/price/admission/support side; “part” + “repeatedly” + “relative to debt” filter macro noise and protect modest low-pay programs; pass-through fails because higher tuition worsens the same ratio—survived pressure and stayed operational. B raised real collateral-damage worries (aid cuts, social-work/education chilling, gaming) but never resolved A’s tuition contradiction (locked vs ready pass-through), over-demanded perfect control before any liability while students already face total liability, and treated safeguard vagueness as fatal without answering A’s ordinary multi-year operationalization. Word-limit clips hurt both, but A still adapted more cleanly from openings through pressure answers into a sharper closing crystallize.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure Q1 plus R2 A1/A5: tuition cannot be both immovable from fixed costs and a frictionless valve to pass penalties through; once raising price worsens debt-to-earnings, the escape is price cut, better support, or closing loan-dependent programs—not permanent externalization.
