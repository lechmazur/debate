### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A built a strong opening on cost-efficiency arithmetic (Voyager/Cassini/JWST vs. Apollo/ISS), but its rebuttals mostly restated that arithmetic rather than engaging B's structural challenge that reallocating "budgets" isn't a clean dollar-for-dollar swap. B's "accounting fantasy" and "anchor tenant" framing—arguing that crewed programs build the infrastructure and political will that make cheap robotic missions possible—was never squarely answered; A's pressure-question response instead conceded that even if total funding and total discovery shrink, it would still back the policy because the per-dollar ratio stays favorable. B seized on this as a "fatal concession," correctly noting that a policy recommendation can't rest on an efficiency ratio applied to a smaller pie. B also properly used the A1:/A2: format to answer A's pressure questions directly, while A's Rebuttal 2 blurred its own question-answering with claims (e.g., overclaiming Perseverance-class rovers can do "sustained deep drilling," which B exposed as unrealistic). A's Rebuttal 1 was also cut off by the word-limit clip, leaving its response to the ISS/life-support point incomplete. B's closing tied the clash together (infrastructure dependency, the fatal concession, human-depth vs. robotic-breadth) more effectively than A's closing, which largely re-asserted the opening math without engaging the live rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's turn exposing A's pressure-question answer ("even if political shifts adjusted total budgets...") as a fatal concession that reduces the policy to optimizing a ratio on a shrinking, possibly collapsed, budget was the most decisive single exchange.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that the proposition is a practical budget policy, not merely an efficiency-ratio exercise. Side A established that robots are cheaper and can reach more environments, but repeatedly assumed crewed funds would convert directly into vast numbers of probes, understated shared infrastructure, and made a vulnerable claim that Perseverance-class rovers could conduct sustained deep drilling. Side B directly challenged those assumptions, defended distinct human scientific capabilities, and explained why reduced political support could mean less total science despite higher science per remaining dollar. Side A also suffered from a clipped rebuttal and an overstated closing arithmetic.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it used Side A's concession that total funding might shrink to expose the weakness of optimizing only a ratio, then concretely refuted the claim that many Perseverance-class rovers could replace human-tended deep drilling.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by exposing that PRO's cost comparisons relied on accounting freeriding and by highlighting the fatal concession that optimizing science-per-dollar on a decimated total budget is poor policy. CON answered pressure questions directly, rebutted the JWST and commercial-launch points, and crystallized the clash in the closing, while PRO's first rebuttal was clipped and its closing merely repeated opening numbers without resolving live burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal exposing PRO's accounting freeriding with the railroad-tracks analogy and the marginal-efficiency-on-a-decimated-budget trap, which undermined PRO's entire comparative framework.
