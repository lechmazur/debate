### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (A) built a tighter, more cumulative case. Its core liquidity/valuation objection was sharpened across rounds into a decisive dilemma—"PRO needs the tax to be simultaneously painless and transformative. It cannot be both"—and when PRO was pressed for specifics (2% rate, $50M threshold), CON used those exact numbers against PRO, showing a 2% levy on a 1% cash-yield business consumes 200% of its income, forcing dilution or debt. PRO's rebuttals largely reasserted that banks and the IRS "already value" assets without squarely answering CON's distinction between a lender's discretionary, hedged appraisal and an annual, legally binding tax assessment, and PRO's closing never engaged the math CON exposed in Rebuttal 2, settling instead for rhetorical labels ("weaponized administrative pessimism") rather than resolving the live contradiction. CON also offered a clear, consistently maintained alternative (tax at realization/transfer, close the loan loophole) that gave the judge a clean standard to weigh against PRO's self-admittedly patchwork formula-plus-true-up-plus-anti-abuse-rules system, which CON correctly framed as conceding the very valuation problem PRO denied existed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.4), turning PRO's own concrete 2%/$50M proposal into proof that the tax either forces liquidation/dilution on low-yield holdings or is too weak to curb the dynastic compounding PRO cares about—paired with PRO never directly rebutting this math in Closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by making valuation/liquidity and the rate dilemma decisive rather than peripheral. PRO’s equity/buy-borrow-die frame and dynastic-power urgency were strong openers, but after pressure CON showed bank lending is not a tax base, property-tax analogies fail, and PRO’s 2%/$50M plus index/true-up/debt-cap answers concede annual guessing corrected only at realization—undercutting the case for an annual net-worth levy versus targeted realization and loan rules. PRO never resolved “painless yet transformative.”
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 (esp. A4.1–A4.5): bank haircuts ≠ IRS assessments; 2% forces borrow/sell/dividend on low-yield firms; formulas/true-ups recreate the valuation problem and point back to realization.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by decisively dismantling Side B's valuation and liquidity mechanisms. A's observation that B's proposed "true-up" formula essentially admits the annual tax is just a guess corrected at realization perfectly neutralized B's case and validated A's alternative. A also cleanly distinguished bank loan appraisals from IRS tax assessments, leaving B's analogies broken.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically pointing out that Side B's "true-up" mechanism concedes the annual valuation is a guess that only becomes accurate at realization—the exact moment Side A's alternative policy would tax it.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): The decisive exchange was B's repeated pressure on A's concession that estate-death taxation requires valuing unrealized, illiquid wealth—B used this to argue A's objection is one of frequency, not principle, and A never fully escaped that trap, instead reasserting "autopsy vs. chronic hemorrhage" without explaining why the valuation difficulty that's acceptable once becomes impossible when done annually at a high threshold with deferral. B also handled the PQ on the equity-lien mechanism cleanly (A1: non-voting lien, not board control) and the capital flight question (A2: residency-based attachment, exit costs exceed portfolio moves), while A's answers to B's PQs leaned on the same "annual is different from once" claim without much new mechanism. A scored real hits on the "foreign investors exempt" admission, which is a genuine structural tension B never fully neutralized (B only said existing income taxes would already cause flight if the disadvantage were severe, which is a reasonable but incomplete rebuttal). Still, B's framing—tax the stock while it's conferring advantage vs. wait until the advantage is spent—was clearer and more consistently defended across rounds, and B's closing crystallized the central clash better than A's, which repeated the autopsy metaphor and the foreign-investor point without fully closing the loop on why annual valuation is categorically unworkable rather than merely costly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's turn in Rebuttal 2 (B4.5) pointing out that CON's death-tax fix "concedes wealth can be valued without cash changing hands" and merely postpones collection, directly exploiting A's own concession from A1.6/A2.3, was the most damaging single rebuttal, and A never fully recovered from having to defend why frequency alone makes valuation impossible.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive comparison live: whether large fortunes should be taxed while they compound, borrow, and confer power, or only at late realization/death. CON’s feasibility, flight, and lien attacks were serious and the foreign-founder disparity punch landed, but never fully neutralized PRO’s core burden once CON accepted valuation of unrealized wealth at death. PRO’s high-threshold/deferral design work and pressure answers made the residual “decades of untaxed advantage” gap clearer than CON’s cash-flow alternative, which treated timing as principle rather than delay.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s death-tax concession flip (Pressure Qs through R2/closing): if unrealized wealth can be valued and taxed once at death, CON’s objection collapses to frequency—and frequency is exactly why annual taxation reaches living advantage that death-only reforms leave untouched.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B built a more philosophically cohesive case, effectively trapping Side A into conceding that unrealized wealth can be valued (via the estate tax alternative) and pivoting the debate to the necessity of taxing the annual utility of wealth. While Side A landed sharp practical attacks regarding debt overhang and foreign investor disparities, Side B's core framing of "manageable imperfection versus permanently ignoring accumulated wealth" and the tight rebuttal of the death-tax alternative secured the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's exploitation of Side A's "tax at death" alternative, pointing out that it concedes the viability of valuing unrealized wealth without cash changing hands, effectively reducing Side A's core objection to mere frequency rather than principle.
