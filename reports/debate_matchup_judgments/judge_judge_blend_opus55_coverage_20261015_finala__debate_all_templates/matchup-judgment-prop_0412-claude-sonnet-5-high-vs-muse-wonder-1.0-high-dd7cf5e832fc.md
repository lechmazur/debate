### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by defining equal strictness as equally unmistakable disclosure and meaningful consequences, not identical broadcast infrastructure. Side A repeatedly treated licensing, pre-clearance, and mechanically identical penalties as essential to the motion without establishing that premise. B directly answered the livestream and enforcement challenges, preserved proportional treatment of mistakes, and showed why blended influencer content makes strong disclosure more necessary. A's scammer objection was also weakened by B's point that imperfect compliance does not negate deterrence, provability, or real-time audience protection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response that strictness concerns timing, prominence, clarity, and consequences rather than copying television's licensing machinery was decisive. It turned A's strongest category-error argument into a strawman and left A defending little substantive difference in the disclosure protection followers should receive.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing the motion’s actual wording—“as strict as”—to do decisive work. PRO’s substance case on parasocial disguise and high-stakes products was stronger at the open, but every later adaptation (no licence/pre-clearance, platform enforcement, multi-moment livestream rules, intent-scaled fines) collapsed “broadcast parity” into a tailored effectiveness regime CON already endorsed. CON’s R2 and close crystallized that abandonment cleanly; PRO never restored a distinct “as strict” surplus that survivors of the pressure exchange still needed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 (A4.3–A4.5): using B’s own livestream answer to show format-specific re-disclosures, banners, and intent-scaled penalties are CON’s framework, not television parity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON demonstrated that PRO could not defend the literal motion without abandoning it. The proposition demands rules "as strict as" traditional media, which structurally includes licensing chokepoints, uniform mechanical penalties, and compliance departments. PRO rightly conceded those mechanisms do not map onto influencers, but then claimed the "spirit" or "standard" of strictness could still transfer. CON sharpened this clash through the pressure questions, forcing PRO to specify enforcement and livestream handling. PRO's answers—platform-administered scaled penalties, format-tailored re-disclosure moments, and persistent banners—were indistinguishable from CON's proposed tailored, platform-native regime. CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing crystallized this turn effectively: PRO had silently replaced "as strict as" with "as effective as," which is a different motion. While PRO offered a compelling consumer-protection narrative, they never squared their practical framework with the text of the proposition. CON's path to victory—showing the motion was a category error and that PRO's necessary concessions proved it—remained live and unneutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [A4.3], which showed that PRO's answer to the livestream pressure question—requiring opening disclosures, re-disclosure for each new paid product, persistent on-screen banners, and intent-scaled penalties—was not broadcast parity at all, but exactly the tailored, platform-native framework CON had proposed. This exposed that PRO had functionally abandoned "as strict as television" while claiming to defend it.
