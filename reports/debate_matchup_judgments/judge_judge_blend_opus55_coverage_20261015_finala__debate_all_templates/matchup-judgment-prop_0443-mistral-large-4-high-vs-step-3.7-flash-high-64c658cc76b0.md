### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): B built a case grounded in concrete mechanisms (embargoes, carveouts, data management plan funding) and consistently forced A to engage with specifics rather than hypotheticals. When pressed in Q1/Q2 for a documented case of chilling effects, A conceded none exists and retreated to an "invisible by design" argument—an unfalsifiable claim that is rhetorically clever but epistemically weak, since it can justify any unsupported harm. B's A1/A2 answers were properly formatted and directly engaged the patent-timing question with the embargo mechanism, which A never fully dismantled (A's rebuttal that "effective exclusivity collapses even with embargoes" is plausible but speculative and asserted without clear mechanism beyond "competitors design around it immediately"). B also successfully reframed the default/mandate distinction that A tried to erase, repeatedly pointing out that carveouts exist in real policies, forcing A to argue they are "bureaucratically risky" without evidence. A's case had genuine strengths (sovereignty, dual-use risk, prior art concerns are real policy tensions) but by the end relied heavily on structural/invisible harms that are hard to verify and were directly challenged without strong empirical pushback. B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly: proven ongoing harms vs. speculative harms, which is the stronger framing given the unanswered pressure question.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q1 asking for a documented case of chilling effects, Q2 on patent embargo timing) forced A into an unfalsifiable "invisible by design" defense and a less concrete rebuttal on patent exclusivity, which meaningfully weakened A's two core pillars (chilling effects and commercialization harm) for the remainder of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly contrasting the concrete, ongoing harms of the status quo (e.g., $28 billion in waste, COVID-19 paywalls) against Side A's structurally "invisible" and speculative harms. While Side A made an excellent and original logical pivot to explain why chilling effects are unprovable, Side B effectively weaponized that concession in the closing to demonstrate that Side A's impacts were purely hypothetical.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response (A1) explaining that chilling effects occur upstream at the grant/IRB stage, thus remaining "invisible by design," was a brilliant way to handle Side B's demand for empirical evidence, even if Side B ultimately won the weighing of those impacts.

CON's strongest remaining route — invisible chilling at proposal stage — was exposed by A's Pressure Q1 and answered in B4 with an admission of no documented cases, relying on speculation. PRO's harms (paywall delays during COVID, $28B irreproducibility waste, HGP spillovers) remained concrete and unrefuted, while B2/B4 collapsed exclusivity to lead-time loss even after valid patents. Clipping cost A some completeness but did not erase decisive rebuttals.
