On balance, CON's rebuttals did more direct damage to PRO's live route to victory, and CON's closing sharpened the decisive clash more cleanly than PRO's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.4), which turned PRO's own Pressure Question answers (consent-gated edges, opaque incumbent routing, emergency suspension) into a self-defeating trilemma that PRO's closing never fully untangled.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON's opening was clipped (a real execution cost), but CON recovered and progressively sharpened a structural dilemma that PRO never fully resolved: either "portable social graph access" means non-consenting third parties' (Bob's) identity and associations move before they consent, or it is pared down to mere endpoint-switching that leaves the incumbent's lock-in intact. PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to the pressure question ("the third party may not receive Bob's... relationship metadata before Bob's service authorizes") inadvertently conceded this, narrowing the mandate's substance, and CON's final rebuttal and closing drove that contradiction home ("gains an endpoint, not a portable social graph... PRO's closing reasserted its "veto" framing rhetorically but did not directly re-engage this empty-endpoint/consent dilemma, leaving CON's sharpest point live and unanswered at the end. PRO's case (lock-in, qualified gatekeeping, consent-preserving revocable access, strong analogies) was well-constructed and rhetorically polished throughout, but CON's late-round pressure exposed a genuine structural tension PRO could not close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.4-A4.8) pointing out that PRO's answer to Q1 either yields "no portable contacts, groups, or usable social graph" (an empty endpoint that doesn't solve lock-in) or requires Bob's identity/association to cross before his consent — a dilemma PRO's closing never directly resolved.

Side B never recovers from this dilemma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which brilliantly catches Side B abandoning "social graph portability" to save consent, trapping them in a dilemma between meaningless portability and privacy violations.

CON crystallized this double bind in the late rebuttals and closing, showing that PRO makes interoperability safe only by defining away graph portability, or makes portability meaningful by defining away consent. PRO’s "who gets a veto" reframing was eloquent but never escaped the dilemma, and CON successfully undermined PRO’s analogies by distinguishing routing identifiers from relational social graphs. The opening clip was a minor execution cost for CON, but the visible arguments were sufficient to build the decisive clash.
