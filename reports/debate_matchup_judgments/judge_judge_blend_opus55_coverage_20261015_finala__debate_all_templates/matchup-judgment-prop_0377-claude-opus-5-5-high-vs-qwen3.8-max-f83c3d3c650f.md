### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won the motion's central comparison. Side B persuasively argued that advantage operates continuously and that a death-triggered tax can be avoided, but its preferred remedies largely targeted capital returns and intergenerational transfers rather than high earners' salaries. A repeatedly exposed that mismatch and explained why taxing gifts alongside estates is a coherent anti-avoidance design, not a concession. B showed inheritance taxation is insufficient alone, but not that large inheritances should remain lightly taxed. A's repeated clipping was an execution cost, though its decisive arguments remained visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was its response that B's own examples—lifetime gifts, trusts, tuition, and seed capital—concern accumulated wealth being transferred to descendants, while none establishes annual salary as the greater threat. This directly secured the proposition's comparative claim and weakened B's attempt to turn alternative capital taxes into a reason against inheritance taxation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by forcing the debate onto the motion's actual comparative: whether dynastic wealth is a bigger threat than high earners' annual income. CON never defended the surgeon's paycheck as the core problem; instead, CON argued that wealth-building and continuous transfers before death matter most. PRO deftly absorbed this into its own framework, showing that CON's preferred remedies—taxing capital gains, dividends, and lifetime gifts—were all taxes on accumulated wealth and its transfer, not on wages. PRO's "unavoidable checkpoint" argument (death cannot be deferred, while annual capital taxes can be avoided through unrealized gains, borrowing, and step-up in basis) provided a decisive mechanism for why inheritance taxation is the proper lever. CON's timing objection had force, but PRO demonstrated that taxing the stock at transfer shrinks the stream for future generations, and that including lifetime gifts is simply inheritance taxation done properly rather than a concession. PRO incurred real execution costs from three word-limit clippings, keeping the margin moderate, but the argumentative path to victory was cleaner and more intact after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing reframing that CON's proposed alternatives—taxing capital gains, dividends, and lifetime gifts—target wealth transfers rather than salaries, effectively collapsing CON's case into PRO's framework and showing CON had tacitly accepted that transferred wealth, not earned income, is the bigger threat to equal opportunity.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the motion's comparison alive while CON conceded it. CON admitted wealth buys continuous advantage and ended arguing “transferred wealth matters all along the timeline” [A4.5] with a remedy of taxing gifts, trusts, and dividends – none of which taxes high earners' salaries harder. PRO showed why that helps PRO: annual capital income can be deferred via borrow-against-stock and erased by step-up at death, making death the one unavoidable checkpoint, and that shrinking the stock at transfer shrinks every future year of schooling, internships and seed money. CON's timing and liquidity critiques were answered by exemptions/installments and by parity of avoidance, leaving no live reason to prefer taxing salaries over taxing large bequests.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B3.1/B4.6 pressure that CON's own fix – lifetime gift taxes and trust distribution taxes – is inheritance taxation under another label and never touches wages, proving CON had accepted that dynastic wealth, not annual income, is the bigger threat.
