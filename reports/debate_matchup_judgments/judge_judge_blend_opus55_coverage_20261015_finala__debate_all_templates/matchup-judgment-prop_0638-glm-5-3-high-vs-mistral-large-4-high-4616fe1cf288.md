- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a sustained, structurally decisive critique: every time PRO defended block grants, it reintroduced competitive mechanisms (entry review, exit review, individual awards, early-career carve-outs, internal competitions), leaving only the "silence between reviews" as the actual distinguishing feature of the motion. A pressed this "what survives the patching" question repeatedly and in the pressure round, and PRO's closing never resolved it—it reasserted the freedom/decade narrative without explaining why its boundary-only accountability model differs meaningfully from CON's proposed "longer competitive horizons" solution. PRO's rebuttals on hierarchy and drift (reputational accountability, market of ideas) were plausible but suffered from an unaddressed lag problem that CON exploited cleanly. CON's answers to pressure questions (A1/A2) were sharp and directly responsive; PRO's answers were adequate but didn't neutralize the collapse argument. This made CON's comparative case easier to follow and more resilient across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal moment — "Look at what PRO actually defended this round... Strip the label off and that is a long-horizon competitive grant system... What survives the patching? Only the ten-year silence between reviews" — which PRO's closing never squarely answered.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by maintaining a consistent focus on how short-cycle funding structurally changes the type of research proposed (chasing renewals vs. chasing anomalies). Furthermore, Side A subtly pivoted to defending "longer horizons," effectively conceding the short-cycle half of the prompt to Side B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 directly neutralized Side A's "shell game" attack by clarifying that the structural block grant provides freedom from fragmenting work into grant-sized deliverables, which is distinct from entry competition.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: after A layered on entry competition, individual awards, carve-outs, internal contests, and exit review, the motion’s distinctive residue was mainly the long mid-period silence, which B framed as costly drift lag rather than productive freedom. B’s path—hierarchy risk under true blocks, repeated market entry as feedback, and the patching critique—stayed live and progressive through the close.
