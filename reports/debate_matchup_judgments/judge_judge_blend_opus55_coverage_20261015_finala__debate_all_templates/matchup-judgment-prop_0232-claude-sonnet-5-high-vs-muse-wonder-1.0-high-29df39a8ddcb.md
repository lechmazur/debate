### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by consistently enforcing the motion’s modest burden: organized crime need only be weakened, not eliminated. A plausibly showed that shifting domestic demand to legal sellers removes voluntary recurring revenue, reduces cartel control over growers, and makes substitution through extortion costlier and riskier. B’s capture and diversification arguments established that cartels could survive and infiltrate legal markets, but did not prove they would retain the same total power or revenue. B also repeatedly characterized exports and territorial leverage as fully intact despite A’s narrower argument that legalization disrupts supporting domestic networks. The clipped opening was an additional execution cost for B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was its answer that extortion cannot replace legal-market losses one-for-one: voluntary customers repeatedly pay dealers, whereas extortion requires coercive collection, creates resistance and exposure, and can drive businesses away. This directly answered B’s diversification claim and clarified why cartel survival remains compatible with meaningful weakening.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the live comparison on net weakening under Latin American conditions. PRO’s monopoly-breaking and “weaken ≠ eliminate” frame was coherent, but CON steadily forced concessions on intact export margins, residual extortion capacity, and substitution into other steady local streams, then tipped the clash with the avocado/capture mechanism: visible licensed farms become better extortion targets and potential laundering cover. PRO never fully escaped that state-capacity trap; “contestable paper trails” assumed auditors and whistleblowers that CON showed are the very people killed in cartel territory. A’s opening was clipped, but later rounds recovered and crystallized a cleaner reason to vote: imperfect legalization under capture risks formalizing cartel power rather than defunding it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A5 on avocado-capture: legalization does not remove a cartel franchise so much as attach bankable paperwork and a government seal to a market cartels can still extort, turning “weakening” into formalization.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully undermined PRO's core mechanism by showing that cannabis is a replaceable revenue stream for diversified cartels, that the highest-margin export market survives intact, and that in captured states legalization converts hidden illicit markets into visible, extortable legal assets—citing the avocado and lime precedent—while captured licensing authorities risk giving cartels legal cover. PRO never adequately answered why visible licensed farms would escape extortion in cartel territory or how losing domestic retail would materially weaken organizations that retain trafficking, extortion, and now gain laundering channels. CON's closing framing that legalization would formalize rather than weaken organized crime crystallized the clash decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer A2 distinguishing captured enforcement from captured licensing, arguing that captured licensing is laundering with a government seal, and showing that visible legal assets become easier rather than harder extortion targets in weak-state regions.
