A therefore built the cleaner, more decisive path through the exchange.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases and followed format cleanly, including proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure. The decisive exchange centered on two load-bearing claims: A's distinction between plain stated deadlines (which survive the ban) and manufactured urgency formatting (which doesn't), and B's closing weighing argument that default cues guard against irreversible loss while removing them only risks small, reversible regret. A directly attacked that weighing claim by pointing out that many pressure-induced purchases—nonrefundable rooms, fares, add-ons locked at checkout—are themselves irreversible, not reversible like 24-hour-cancelable insurance. B's rebuttal and closing answered this only with the narrow "insurance she cancels" example and never engaged the nonrefundable-fare/room point, leaving B's central closing framing (reversible vs irreversible) damaged and unresolved. A also offered a workable observable-design test (does it reset, omit scope, vanish after upsell) that blunted B's vagueness objection better than B's rebuttal (legitimate resets) actually engaged with it, since A's test targets quiet/deceptive resets specifically. B's accessibility argument (grey text gets missed, emphasis is how real warnings work) was vivid and intuitively strong, and A's reply to it was somewhat assertive rather than deeply argued, but this gap was smaller than the unaddressed irreversibility symmetry on B's side, which undercut B's own clinching argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's "irreversible purchases" point in Rebuttal 2 (A4.2)—noting that nonrefundable rooms, fares, and add-ons are not neatly reversible—critically undercut B's central reversible-vs-irreversible weighing claim, and B's closing never squarely answered it, only reasserting the narrower "cancelable insurance" example.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by preserving truthful, prominent scarcity information while distinguishing it from cues mainly engineered to induce haste. That directly matched the qualified proposition and undercut B’s central claim that rejecting pressure cues means hiding urgency. B’s irreversible-loss comparison depended on portraying A’s alternative as buried grey text and the pressured purchases as readily refundable, neither of which A conceded or B adequately grounded.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal decisively answered B’s error comparison: real deadlines and inventory counts remain visible, so removing manipulative presentation does not create the alleged blindness, while pressured purchases can themselves be costly and irreversible. B’s response largely reinstated the false choice between flashing countdowns and unseen information.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the motion's real distinction live: plain perishable facts stay visible; pressure packaging (resetting races, pulsing scarcity, crowd cues) need not be default. B repeatedly collapsed that into “unseen truth = no truth,” so its irreversible-miss weighing never fully landed against A’s surviving plain-deadline/inventory alternative. A answered B’s pressure more cleanly and made the vote path easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answers: truth need not be weaponized; plain held-until/rooms-at-price survives, so B’s reversible-regret vs irreversible-miss frame fails once the race format is stripped.
