### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that cheap attritable systems either lack the range, payload, sensing, and resilience needed for Pacific operations or acquire those capabilities and cease being cheap. Side A compellingly emphasized replacement and concentration risks, but repeatedly assumed that distributed systems would survive jamming, identify targets, and rapidly reconstitute without adequately proving mission effectiveness. Its proposed cuts to surface combatants and F-35s exposed an especially serious gap in missile defense, anti-submarine warfare, and penetrating strike.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used Side A’s specific proposed cuts to show that XLUUVs, missiles, and sensor webs could not replace the escorts, shooters, and complex missions being sacrificed, while sharpening the contradiction between cheapness, networking, survivability, and capability.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (A) won because it kept the proposition’s real trade—“significant share… even if it reduces exquisite investment”—at the center and made that trade look strategically costly. B’s opening case on mass, expendability, and surge capacity was coherent, but A’s dependency chain (range/endurance/payload/enablers) and mission-gap argument forced B to name cuts (surface combatants/F-35s) and weak substitutes (XLUUVs/sensors/missiles). A then showed those substitutes do not perform ASW, fleet missile defense, or penetrating strike on day one, and that a “cheap yet diverse, networked, autonomous, long-range” swarm is internally contradictory. B scored on attrition math and irreplaceability of carriers, but never squared how reduced exquisite investment still supplies the kill chain the swarm needs. Across pressure answers and late weighing, A narrowed the clash to combat effect after first contact versus inventory lost before effect, and that frame was cleaner and more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4.5–A4.6): uses B’s named cuts against B, then exposes the cheap/networked/hardened swarm contradiction as fatal to the proposition’s tradeoff.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by exposing a fundamental contradiction in Side A's case: attritable systems capable of surviving and operating across the vast Indo-Pacific require the size, power, and complexity that makes them expensive, while keeping them cheap renders them entirely dependent on the exquisite logistics and enabler platforms the proposition would cut. Side B's deconstruction of Side A's specific proposed cuts (surface combatants and F-35s) effectively demonstrated that attritable systems like XLUUVs cannot replace the missile defense and ASW missions of those platforms, which paradoxically leaves the remaining exquisite forces more vulnerable. While Side A presented a strong structural argument regarding industrial surge and the math of attrition, Side B's grounding in the physics, geography, and logistical realities of the theater proved more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, specifically the dismantling of Side A's swarm and XLUUV claims. Side B sharply pointed out that adding the necessary hardening, diverse sensors, and autonomy to make the swarm effective strips it of its "cheap and attritable" nature, and that cutting surface combatants removes the very escorts needed to protect the remaining fleet.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by presenting attritables as complements that preserve and enable retained heavy platforms, not substitutes for every exquisite capability. Its mid-tier spectrum, pre-positioning, saturation, and wartime replacement arguments answered the geography and lethality objections while keeping the motion’s tradeoff intact. Side B raised a serious Pacific-range concern but repeatedly assumed, rather than demonstrated, that any significant shift must eliminate too many motherships. Its electronic-warfare response was also overly categorical, and its answer to prolonged attrition largely reduced to asserting that exquisite systems would survive or the war was already lost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both questions, distinguished long-range attritable systems from fully exquisite platforms, and reframed effective firepower as surviving platforms multiplied by usable sorties. Side B never established the specific procurement bottleneck needed to defeat that comparison.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the decisive metric—force survival and sustained firepower after opening losses—and answering CON’s mothership/geography trap without collapsing into either pure cheap junk or a full exquisite rebuild. CON’s Pacific-range and industrial-base hits stayed live and forceful, but too often restated the zero-sum binary instead of beating PRO’s “significant shift keeps enough heavy hitters while making them usable” weighing. PRO’s clearer attrition logic and better pressure answers made the vote easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s B4 A1/A2 plus spectrum break: retained exquisite platforms still deliver heavy munitions once attritables open corridors, and long-range unmanned need not become gold-plated exquisite if crew, multi-mission, and irreplaceability are stripped out—this most damaged CON’s mothership contradiction.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's core binary. Side B repeatedly argued that long-range systems must be exquisite and expensive, while attritables must be short-range, cheap, and vulnerable to electronic warfare. Side A's second rebuttal cleanly broke this frame by explaining how removing crew, life support, and multi-mission versatility creates a spectrum of long-range but attritable systems. Furthermore, Side B's concession that losing the exquisite fleet means "the war is already lost" was a fatal strategic blunder that Side A brilliantly exploited in the closing to demonstrate that CON's model is fragile and unprepared for a war of attrition. Side A's comparative weighing on survivability, mass, and industrial replenishment was decisively stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the "engineering paradox" pressure question (A4.3-A4.4), which deconstructed CON's false binary between cheap/short-range and expensive/long-range platforms by isolating the specific cost drivers (crew protection, life support, multi-mission versatility) that can be removed to maintain range at an attritable cost.
