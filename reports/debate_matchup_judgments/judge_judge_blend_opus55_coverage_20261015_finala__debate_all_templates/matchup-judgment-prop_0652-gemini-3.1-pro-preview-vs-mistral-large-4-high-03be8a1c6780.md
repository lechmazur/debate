### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A won by consistently showing generative AI is not a blank tool: the platform supplies protected expression from its model, making shared liability the workable incentive fix when user-level enforcement fails at scale. B's volitional-act and chilling-effect lines stayed live but were weakened by the screening concession and the clipped Rebuttal 2, leaving A with the cleaner decisive comparison on the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 use of B's own real-time screening claim to flip the incentives question: capability without liability leaves filters unfunded, so shared liability is required to force deployment.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by proving that generative AI platforms are active co-authors of infringement rather than passive tools, decisively breaking Side B's analogies to cameras and printing presses. Side A effectively turned Side B's own concession that platforms possess real-time screening capability into an argument that shared liability is the only mechanism that creates incentives to actually deploy those filters. Side B's volitional-act framework retained intuitive appeal but never recovered from A's distinction between supplying a tool and supplying the copyrighted expression itself, and B's clipped rebuttal compounded the execution gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the "passive tool" analogy by showing that generative AI supplies the copyrighted expression itself (unlike Word or a camera), combined with turning Side B's concession about real-time screening into a decisive incentive argument for shared liability.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B wins by dismantling Side A's core "printing press" analogy through the crucial copyright distinction between unprotectable ideas (prompts) and protectable expression (outputs). While A argued the user makes the volitional choice, B effectively demonstrated that the user only supplies the idea, while the platform's engine supplies the infringing expression. Furthermore, B brilliantly weaponized A's concession that platforms possess real-time filtering capabilities, using it to prove that platforms have the technical capacity to prevent infringement but lack the economic incentive to do so without shared liability. A's arguments regarding fair use and regulatory capture were strong, but A failed to provide a compelling alternative mechanism to force platforms to deploy the filters they conceded were possible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they turned Side A's concession about real-time screening capabilities into a proof of technical capacity, contrasting it with the lack of economic incentive to deploy those filters absent shared liability, and deploying the "idea vs. expression" distinction to fatally wound the printing press analogy.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): CON won the decisive clash: shared liability forces platforms into preemptive filtering of user-prompted content, yet copyright (unlike CSAM) is contextual and fair-use-dependent, so risk-averse algorithms will erase lawful parody/satire. PRO's strongest route—control-plus-profit and the "pre-loaded" machine vs blank-cassette distinction—was real but never solved that filter problem or the over-censorship/monopoly costs. CON's Sony/substantial-non-infringing-use framing, user-intent answers, and existing-inducement fallback held up under pressure and gave the cleaner reason to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's fair-use contextuality attack (R1 then R2/closing) on A's claim that platforms can simply block high-confidence "Mickey Mouse" prompts without destroying protected parody.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B won by showing that the costs of shared liability are prohibitive and the benefits largely redundant. B’s live route—copyright is inherently contextual, so pre-generation automated filters cannot distinguish fair use from infringement, guaranteeing blunt-force censorship—remained standing after A could only offer vague appeals to “reasonable care” and implausible human review at scale. B also landed active inducement in Rebuttal 2 to defuse A’s strongest premium-feature examples, a point A never answered in Closing. A’s benefit-and-control narrative and VCR distinction were strong, but B successfully narrowed the debate: existing law already handles bad actors, and sweeping shared liability would crush decentralized innovation and fair use without solving a distinct unsolved problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sustained attack on filtering feasibility, showing that copyright is contextual (unlike CSAM) and that algorithms cannot adjudicate fair use, paired with the active-inducement answer that neutralized A’s “premium feature” pressure.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively exploiting the practical impossibility of automated copyright filtering without destroying fair use. Side A effectively points out that even direct prompts require context for parody or critique, meaning Side B's "reasonable care" filters would result in blunt-force censorship. Furthermore, Side A neutralizes Side B's worst-case scenarios by noting that existing "active inducement" laws already cover platforms that explicitly market infringement, and successfully counters Side B's attempt to carve out open-source models by highlighting the chilling effect on cloud hosts and repositories.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling Side B's "reasonable care" filter proposal by explaining that even direct prompts like "Mickey Mouse" require context for fair use, making automated adjudication impossible and guaranteeing over-censorship.
