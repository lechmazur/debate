### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. B offered a plausible free-riding concern and correctly distinguished spent training, which may produce some return, from an unspent balance transferred at departure. But B repeatedly escalated that marginal cost into the unsupported certainty that accounts would vanish. A answered with stronger comparison: employers already tolerate transferable skills, training remains useful for recruitment, and portability removes a concrete mobility penalty. A also showed that B's claimed retention benefit ultimately depended on workers fearing forfeiture. The clipped rebuttal and reliance on sector pooling were costs for A, but neither displaced its central case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange challenged why employers tolerate losing fully funded certifications but would abolish accounts over the smaller unspent balance. B's return-on-investment distinction had force, yet A persuasively showed that the difference established only some additional cost, not B's claimed collapse of training accounts.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the stronger path to victory by exposing a decisive asymmetry in CON's empirical prediction. By asking why employers keep funding training when workers can already leave with fully paid certifications, PRO undermined CON's central claim that portability would trigger account elimination. CON never satisfactorily explained why losing an unspent balance would deter employers more than losing a worker immediately after a completed certification. PRO's "smallest slice of risk" turn, cleaner handling of pressure questions, and effective crystallization that CON's retention model depends on holding earned benefits hostage gave PRO a solid edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question 1 asking why employers do not already abandon training when workers walk away with fully funded certifications, which CON failed to answer cleanly, causing CON's "employer exodus" argument to collapse under its own logic.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won the ownership and incentive clashes. B framed accounts as earned, restricted benefits like HSA/401(k), answered CON's Q about cash-out restriction, and showed unspent balances can only buy sector training, not rival profit. CON's core that portability = guaranteed subsidy/elimination was undercut by B's Q1: employers already tolerate losing fully spent training when workers leave yet keep funding. CON's retention defense collapsed into forfeiture-as-hostage, which B crystallized in closing. Clip cost for B but path remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - earmarked = still earned (HSA/401k analogy) and free-riding already exists for spent human capital, making unspent balance the smallest risk and portability a recruitment advantage, not a death knell for accounts.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won the central incentive clash. Side A never adequately explained why voluntary employer contributions would remain robust when each firm could benefit by funding less and hiring workers carrying competitors' balances. Side B also answered the recruitment objection by distinguishing zero training from shifting toward cash, in-house courses, or other nonportable forms. Side A's late acceptance of adjustable vesting weakened its job-lock remedy. B's clipped turns and erroneous C-reference labels were execution costs, but did not erase its stronger causal case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it showed that competition need not eliminate development but can change its form, explained why the new employer has the incentive to fund training it will benefit from, and used Side A's retirement-account analogy to expose the unresolved vesting dilemma.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by keeping the free-rider and incentive-substitution problems alive. PRO never showed who would keep funding portable accounts once waiting to hire already-funded workers became the smarter strategy. CON demonstrated that rational firms would shift to firm-specific training, repayable tuition, or cash wages rather than subsidize rivals, undermining the sector-wide upskilling PRO promised. CON also turned PRO’s 401(k) analogy against PRO by showing such benefits rely on vesting schedules, and exploited PRO’s closing concession that vesting adjustments are acceptable—collapsing the motion toward the status quo. PRO’s job-lock story remained intuitively resonant but could not overcome CON’s live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal 2 turning PRO’s 401(k) analogy against PRO by revealing that portable benefits typically rely on vesting, then showing that firms would rationally substitute portable accounts with non-portable training or cash—so workers end up unable to carry benefits they were never given.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins the incentive clash that decides the debate. PRO's circulation and 401(k) analogies assume firms keep funding, but never answers A3.1 / who fills the pool when filling last pays best. A shows competition doesn't prevent free-riding, it changes form toward firm-specific training, repayable tuition, or cash - the exact narrow training PRO wants to end in B1.5. A's pressure answers distinguish new-employer investment (captures return) from old-employer subsidy to rival, and turn the 401(k) point into vesting = status quo, creating PRO's dilemma in the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 dismantling B's 401(k) analogy and Q2: vesting explains current practice and new-employer finishing training proves CON's premise because return is captured, while making old employer pay breaks that link and pushes funding into non-portable forms.
