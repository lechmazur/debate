- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won the motion’s actual comparison decisively. B’s unsupported scale claims and reliance on market discipline never answered that prudential case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing exploited B4.1 most effectively: B described a run caused by illiquid reserves as an immediate, self-correcting mechanism, while A showed that preventing precisely such first-mover-loss dynamics is the purpose of bank and money-market regulation.

B’s scale, e-money, and innovation points never neutralized the substance test or the SVB/USDC evidence that concentrated bank deposits and yield-seeking create classic financial risks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 + closing exploitation of B’s own admissions that issuers are “not ordinary software,” that off-chain reserve rules leave on-chain transfers permissionless, and that runs are the supposed safety valve—turning B’s market-discipline claim into the exact harm regulation exists to prevent.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B won decisively by exposing Side A's concessions and dismantling its core impacts. Side B correctly pointed out that Side A's proposed oversight (audits, money transmitter licenses) already treats issuers as financial entities rather than ordinary software, effectively conceding the motion's core comparison. More importantly, Side B neutralized Side A's "permissionless DeFi" impact by distinguishing between off-chain centralized issuance and on-chain permissionless transfers—a distinction Side A eventually conceded. Side A's fatal error was framing a financial run as a "self-correcting" market mechanism, which Side B brilliantly attacked by highlighting the catastrophic consumer harm of runs, proving exactly why bank and money-market guardrails are necessary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing attack on Side A's claim that a financial run is a "self-correcting feature," effectively turning Side A's main defense into the ultimate proof for why prudential financial regulation is required.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A won because the motion requires regulation more like financial regulation, not mandatory bank charters or taxpayer insurance. Its reserve-adequacy, liquidity, diversification, audit, and run-risk case remained applicable to dominant centralized issuers. B sharply exposed heterogeneity and weaknesses in the Terra example, but relied on a false choice between ordinary software law and publicly insured banking. Contract and anti-fraud law do not themselves impose or continuously verify liquid one-to-one backing. B also overstated that regulated failures prove regulation ineffective and used nonexistent reference IDs in its closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 most effectively answered B's bailout framing: bank-like liquidity, diversification, and audit requirements can reduce USDC-style concentration risk without deposit insurance. That preserved A's core proposal while showing that B had attacked an unnecessarily maximal version of it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash by converting PRO’s own exhibits against it: SVB (a heavily regulated bank) caused the USDC de-peg, and Terra’s collapse stayed contained with zero bank failures or taxpayer costs precisely because it was treated as software. B’s maturity-transformation distinction and 1:1 warehouse-receipt framing undercut A’s “function is banking” premise, while A retreated under pressure from an earlier deposit-insurance demand and tried to both brandish Terra and dismiss non-custodial code as an outlier. A’s case remained coherent on systemic scale but never neutralized the socialization-and-contagion weighing B kept live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3–B4.4: exposing A’s simultaneous retreat on deposit insurance and contradictory use of algorithmic Terra while calling non-custodial protocols non-systemic.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won decisively by turning Side B's own examples against it. Side A brilliantly highlighted the irony of Side B using the SVB collapse to argue for bank regulation, pointing out that USDC de-pegged precisely because its reserves were trapped in a heavily regulated fractional-reserve bank. Furthermore, Side A exposed major contradictions in Side B's case, specifically Side B's retreat from deposit insurance to avoid the taxpayer bailout burden, and its concession that decentralized protocols are outliers while still relying on Terra (an algorithmic protocol) to prove systemic risk. Side A's rebuttals were sharp, logically cohesive, and effectively neutralized Side B's core arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's retreat on deposit insurance and exposed the contradiction of using Terra to justify custodial bank regulation after Side B conceded decentralized protocols are outliers.
