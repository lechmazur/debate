### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): A won by defining a workable, target-area standard and exposing that B’s preferred policy—no approval without independently audited, pre-impact baselines—substantively accepts the proposition. B’s wall-versus-door distinction was rhetorically strong, but depended on reading the pause as global and indefinite despite A specifying multi-year, site-specific baselines certified by the ISA Council. B also never established that pausing commercial approvals would halt existing exploration, while its claim that independent verification required amendment conflicted with its later claim that the ISA could impose it immediately. A had minor grounding and reference-ID errors, but retained the clearer precautionary case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s timing fork was decisive: if independent baselines must precede extraction, approvals must be withheld until they exist; if auditing follows approval, the pristine baseline may already be lost. B’s case-by-case licensing response created a real semantic distinction, but did not overcome that functional sequencing argument.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): PRO won the sequencing clash. CON conceded independent pre-impact baselines and a needed regulatory amendment [B1.4], and PRO showed that until those rules exist, withholding commercial approvals is exactly the pause sought. PRO answered both pressure questions - naming ISA Council certification and escrow funding - while CON left the irreversibility and unmapped reference zone + plume timing problems unanswered. The door vs wall distinction collapsed because zero areas currently meet CON's own audited-baseline test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 expose that CON's own condition - "independently audited, pre-impact baselines" requiring an unwritten amendment - implies withholding approvals now, which is the motion's pause; plus A5 framing withholding as pause vs premature approval under conflicted status quo.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A successfully distinguished between a categorical moratorium and conditional licensing, forcing Side B into a defensive position where B either had to defend an undefined, open-ended threshold or collapse their model into A's. A's arguments regarding the defunding of baseline science under a pause were highly effective and poorly answered by B's ad hoc "escrow fund" proposal. A's closing crystallized the clash memorably, exposing the logical contradictions in B's attempt to equate case-by-case gating with a blanket halt.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where A dismantled B's attempt to equate conditional licensing with a categorical pause, highlighting that the motion's unique addition is an unstatable, categorical bar that defunds the very science it demands.

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by supplying a workable answer to A's central objection: pre-extraction plot surveys, independent verification, and adjacent untouched control zones. A also blurred a temporary baseline condition into an ethical objection to any nonrecovering disturbance, allowing B's disguised-moratorium criticism to remain live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A1 answer was decisive: specific pre-extraction surveys and untouched reference zones directly answered what the first permit would be measured against. A's closing treated the existence of a reference as conceding a blanket pause rather than explaining why B's independently verified site baseline was inadequate.

B's distinction between exploration and exploitation contracts dismantled A's "Catch-22" regarding science funding, and B's emphasis on the lack of imminent commercial deep-sea mines blunted the immediacy of A's terrestrial counterfactual. B's weighing of irreversible, invisible abyssal harm against visible, partially remediable terrestrial harm was highly persuasive, and B's closing crystallized the clash beautifully without overreaching.
