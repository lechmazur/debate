### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by preserving the motion's natural distinction between the same basic commercial terms and identical technical rules. A consistently allowed process-specific inspections while defending equal market access, truthful cultivated-beef labeling, and ordinary hazard-based regulation. B sharply exposed A's acceptance of special controls, but never established that those controls require a separate regulatory caste once safety is met; its closing effectively substituted identical terms for the motion's same basic terms. A also answered the medium-change challenge by distinguishing genuinely novel inputs from routine adjustments to approved ingredients. The win is moderated by A's unsupported late claim about conventional lot-release and lineage tools and its erroneous C-reference IDs, while B incurred a clipping cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned whether tailored controls concede B's case. B forcefully argued that A kept describing distinct batch testing, labeling, and authorization while calling them equal treatment. A answered that process-specific controls already exist within conventional hazard-based regulation and that only genuinely novel inputs require renewed approval; this better matched the motion's qualified phrase same basic terms.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won a competitive, high-quality clash by forcing the dispute onto what “same basic terms” actually requires. After both sides accepted process-specific hazard controls and “cultivated beef” labeling, A showed those concessions still leave a distinct standing regime—especially change-triggered re-authorization—because cultivated cells have no biological buffer and the medium is the product. B kept strong principles (neutrality, anti-protectionism, externalities) but never fully neutralized that comparison, and late attempts to shrink the residue to mere nutrient-ratio tweaks underplayed A’s reformulation point. Mild cost to A for a clipped Rebuttal 2, but the path to victory remained clearer on A’s side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s [A2.1–A2.2] “item for item” trap of B’s own hazard/label concessions, sealed in [A4.5–A4.7] by turning B’s additive rule into re-authorization once the medium-is-product biology is applied.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively exposing the semantic and practical flaws in Side B's interpretation of "same basic terms." Side A's argument that conventional meat regulation relies on the biological assumption that an animal's digestion buffers its inputs, whereas in cultivated meat the growth medium directly constitutes the product, was a brilliant and unanswerable point. Side B was forced to concede in closing that "same basic terms" merely meant "same principles," effectively conceding Side A's core premise that a distinct regulatory framework (with lot-release testing and change-triggered re-authorization) is necessary. Side A's rebuttals were sharp, direct, and systematically dismantled Side B's analogies, while Side B struggled to defend the mechanics of their proposed regulatory equivalence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's claim that adjusting growth media is a "routine operation" by explaining that unlike a cow's feed, the medium *is* the product in cultivated meat, making any change a fundamental reformulation requiring re-authorization under standard hazard frameworks.
