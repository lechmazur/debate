### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A (CON) won by keeping the decisive clash clean: need explains who receives care, not who is billed. B’s reciprocity/status framing never fully showed why entitled childhood support becomes a collectible debt, while A repeatedly showed unequal bills among equally capable adults as targeting parental misfortune, not capacity. A’s handling of abuse burdens and resource-creation claims did more damage than B’s replies, and B’s clipped R2 plus edge-case-heavy closing left A’s comparative route clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2/closing use of B’s own A2—default duty stands when abuse is unproved—plus the equal-income/parental-poverty comparison, collapsing B’s reciprocity justification into unjust liability selection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by exposing that A's core "reciprocal duty" premise assumed what it needed to prove. B showed that childhood support was owed to the child, not a loan creating debt, and that parental need does not establish why a specific adult child should owe an additional bill that an equally affluent peer does not. B also landed a damaging, unanswered blow on abuse: under A's presumptive duty, survivors who cannot prove abuse in court may be forced to pay their abusers, a predictable injustice A could only normalize as "ordinary burden of proof." A's "taxing strangers" frame lost force because B demonstrated that progressive financing still collects from affluent children—just by capacity rather than by family misfortune. A had strong emotional rhetoric, but B made the decisive clash clearer and left A's central justification circular.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal demonstrating that parental poverty selects debtors rather than ability to pay, combined with the exposure that A's abuse safeguard collapses when evidence is scarce—forcing survivors to pay abusers under a "default status-based duty."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively separating the identification of need from the assignment of liability. While Side A relied on the rhetorical appeal of "beneficiaries vs. strangers," Side B systematically dismantled this by showing that progressive taxation already captures the child's capacity to pay without imposing an arbitrary penalty based on parental poverty. Side B also forced Side A into damaging concessions regarding the abuse exception, highlighting the profound injustice of compelling unproven abuse victims to fund their abusers. Side A's execution was slightly marred by a clipped turn, but the substantive loss was due to Side B's superior conceptual clarity and sharper rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which capitalized on Side A's answers to the pressure questions. By highlighting Side A's concession that equally capable adults face different bills solely due to parental circumstances, and exposing the harsh reality of Side A's burden of proof for abuse victims, Side B effectively neutralized Side A's core equity and moral claims.
