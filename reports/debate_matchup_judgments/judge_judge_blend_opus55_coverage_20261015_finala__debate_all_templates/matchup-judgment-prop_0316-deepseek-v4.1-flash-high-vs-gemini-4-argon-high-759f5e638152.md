### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the motion focused on baseline knowledge rather than individualized explanations. A sharply showed that notice alone cannot diagnose bias, but that burden exceeded the proposition. Once B excluded real-time suppression alerts, A's scammer harm largely disappeared, leaving only futility and consent-theater objections. B plausibly established that truthful notice still supports autonomy and accountability even when it cannot prove why a particular profile was buried.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to A's dilemma was decisive: disclosure that AI governs visibility need not reveal thresholds or trigger account-specific alerts. A proved such notice is limited, but not that ignorance is preferable or that the right itself is harmful.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B maintained a tight dilemma (generic notice is white noise; specific notice enables scammers) and forced A to shrink the right into a static universal banner. A's "It doesn't" answer to whether that banner helps the buried/biased user neutralized the opening harms and left only a thinner consent-baseline claim that B framed as notice theater. A kept a live normative residual on consent-not-in-the-dark, but B made the decisive comparative easier: the defended right fails the case's own metrics without creating real user choice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and closing using A's "It doesn't" concession: static notice escapes the scammer horn only by emptying the right of the personal suppression/bias knowledge A's case needed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins because it successfully trapped Side B in a dilemma: a generic notice is either too vague to help the very users invoked in Side B's opening (as Side B conceded when pressed) or too specific to be safe. Side A showed that a static universal label would be identical across platforms, offering no comparative choice, and would serve mainly as "notice-and-consent theater" that lets platforms claim user approval without changing practices. Side B's principled appeals to autonomy and consent could not overcome the demonstrated emptiness of the proposed remedy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 rebuttal of the 'ingredient label' analogy by showing every mainstream app uses the same three 'ingredients,' making the label uniform and useless, and its exploitation of Side B's concession that the static notice does not help the buried user Side B originally invoked.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B narrowly wins because Side A established that AI disclosure is insufficient, but not that users should therefore lack this baseline right. B correctly framed material-treatment rules and AI disclosure as compatible rather than competing remedies, while A’s claimed costs of disclosure remained comparatively speculative. A was sharper on the limits of a generic label and successfully answered B’s alleged contradiction, keeping the debate close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest response was that demanding disclosure to solve every abuse commits a nirvana fallacy: a right to know can be a transparency baseline even if throttling and paid visibility also require separate regulation. A’s insufficiency argument did not fully defeat that modest proposition.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won by keeping the motion narrow and live: a right to know when AI ranks/shapes can be fully satisfied while leaving throttling, boosts, retention optimization, and filter overrides hidden. PRO’s strongest harms and later “contextual” reading keep sliding into material-outcome disclosure, which CON already endorsed and cleanly separated from an AI-method trigger. PRO landed real pressure on the theater/fatalism tension and the foundation point, but never made the written proposition the cleaner remedy than no-lies plus outcome transparency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sustained “label can be true while every harmful practice continues” line, crystallized in Rebuttal 1 and Closing against PRO’s attempt to turn filter-override concessions into victory for the AI trigger.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): CON demonstrated that the motion’s AI-disclosure trigger is a hollow method label: it can be fully satisfied by a vague banner while every harmful practice continues, and its unstable boundary invites compliance theater. PRO’s strongest harms—throttling, paid boosts, filter overrides—are better cured by outcome-based transparency, which CON consistently endorsed without conceding the motion. PRO never resolved why knowing *that* AI is used matters more than knowing *how* one is treated, and their closing mischaracterized CON’s position as concession. The decisive clash resolved cleanly for CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained attack across Rebuttal 1 and 2 showing that the motion’s “AI” trigger provides only a shallow label, whereas outcome disclosure directly answers the user’s actual need; this culminated in the pressure question that exposed PRO’s inability to specify what beyond a bare label the motion requires.
