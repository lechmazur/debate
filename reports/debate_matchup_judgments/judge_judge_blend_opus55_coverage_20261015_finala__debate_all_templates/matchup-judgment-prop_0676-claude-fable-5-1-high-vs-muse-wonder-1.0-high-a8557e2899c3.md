  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing synthesis of CON's timeline argument, framing the post-hoc liability regime as one where vulnerable applicants "are the experiment" before any evidence is generated or evaluated.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by isolating the crucial validity gap between established questionnaires and software that infers personality from face and voice, then defending a proportionate heavy-restriction regime rather than an indiscriminate ban. Side B compellingly showed that humans are biased and algorithms can create auditable records, but its safeguards largely converged with A's policy while never adequately explaining why employers should deploy unvalidated tools before proving job relevance. The displacement-to-human-bias objection kept the debate competitive, but it did not defeat A's core burden-shifting case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response that evidence for self-report personality questionnaires cannot be borrowed by facial and vocal inference was decisive. It undercut Side B's main validity defense while allowing genuinely validated tools through A's proposed gate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by relentlessly pressing the comparative advantage of automated tools over human interviewers. Side B successfully argued that while machines leave an auditable log, human recruiters rely on opaque, unrecorded biases. Side A's attempt to argue that humans "must meet the same test" was fatally exposed by Side B as falling outside the motion's text, which only restricts automated tools.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 decisively capitalized on A's answers by pointing out that PRO's "symmetry" applied only in speech, not in the motion's text, proving employers would simply route applicants to ungated, unlogged human bias channels.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the decisive target clear: opaque face/voice inference lacks transferred validity, industrializes first-impression rejection before any exchange, and leaves candidates with no notice or appeal. CON’s twin-baseline, inspectability, and channel-substitution replies were sharp and left a real cost of asymmetric gating, but repeatedly slid into defending validated questionnaires, hypothetical transparent models, or “regulate all hiring,” which PRO answered with a coherent prove-first gate and the pre-interview screening point. CON’s clipped R1 was a mild execution drag. Slight edge to A on the cleaner path to the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's [A2.1] split: structured self-report personality evidence does not transfer to automated face/voice inference, collapsing CON's main validity pivot.
