### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by preserving the key distinction between receiving proportional representation and capturing all representation in a district. A directly answered B's strongest dilemma: STV need not moderate every individual winner because narrow factions receive only their share and still need broader support for additional seats and governing power. B raised serious concerns about fringe leverage, coalition accountability, and persistent media-driven sorting, but repeatedly treated a proportional seat as equivalent to a plurality faction monopolizing a district. B also conceded that geographic sorting defeats competitive-redistricting solutions. A's cleaner comparative mechanism and B's two clipped turns produce a clear but limited A victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's challenge that multi-member STV guarantees disciplined blocs seats without requiring second choices. A answered that this is legitimate proportional representation, while transfers matter for additional power and legislative control still requires broader coalition support. B's reply highlighted kingmaker risks but did not overcome A's central comparison between a faction receiving its share and receiving the district's entire representation.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively exposing a tension in Side B's case: that a proportional system granting seats to a 20% cohesive bloc removes the electoral incentive for moderation. Side A successfully argued this relocates polarization into unaccountable, post-election backroom coalition bargaining, whereas single-member districts force broad coalition-building before the election.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2 brilliantly turned Side B's 5-seat STV math against them, showing that a 20% threshold guarantees extreme blocs a seat without requiring crossover appeal, thereby undermining Side B's core promise of electoral moderation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO won by showing that winner-take-all creates a structural total prize allowing narrow factions to seize full control through safe-seat primaries and split fields, while CONs targeted alternatives could not solve geographic sorting and were conceded to be incomplete. CON landed sharp internal critiques, but the safe-seat dilemma, reliance on list-PR horror stories not proposed, and two clipped speeches left a cleaner route for PROs proportional payout framework.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PROs second rebuttal (A4) was decisive: it dismantled CONs single-winner vs. multi-member trap by explaining that multi-member STV caps narrow control at a proportional share while still forcing breadth for additional seats and governing coalitions, directly answering CONs pressure questions and setting up PROs closing crystallization.
