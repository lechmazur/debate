### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) identified and relentlessly pressed a genuine internal contradiction in PRO's case: granting residents priority specifically during peak season gives them every incentive to use that access at peak times, undermining PRO's claim that resident flexibility would spread demand to off-peak periods. PRO's answers to this (B2.2, B4.2) never resolved the logic—they simply reasserted that residents "will still prefer off-peak" without explaining why a peak-only priority pass wouldn't be used at peak. CON also landed a sharp structural point that physical erosion depends on foot traffic/humidity, not visitor etiquette or residency status, which PRO answered only with an unsupported assertion that "respectful locals cause less wear." CON's reframe of price as a "climbable ladder" versus residency as "a wall no visitor can scale" effectively flipped PRO's equity argument. PRO's closing largely recapped moral framing (heritage belongs to residents) without directly resolving the demand-spreading contradiction or the structural-erosion rebuttal, making it feel like an evasion of the decisive clash rather than a resolution of it. CON's closing, by contrast, crystallized the contradiction and offered a clear alternative (timed entry, capacity caps), giving a cleaner path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated pressure (opening A1.1, rebuttal A2.1-2.2, and Pressure Q2) on the contradiction between "peak-season priority" and "residents will voluntarily shift to off-peak" was never adequately answered by B, who in B4.2 simply reasserted the claim without resolving the logical tension—this was the most decisive exchange in the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing a fatal mechanism contradiction in PRO's case: granting residents priority specifically during peak seasons rationally incentivizes them to visit during those peaks, intensifying rather than reducing crowding, while off-peak capacity remains underutilized. CON also successfully rebuts PRO's "respectful locals" claim by showing that structural erosion depends on foot traffic and physics, not etiquette. PRO's moral framing of local belonging remains resonant but never overcomes the technical failure of the proposed policy to actually manage demand or conserve fragile sites. CON's alternative framework—genuine capacity limits, timed entry, and means-tested pricing—provides a more coherent path to the shared goal of sustainable heritage preservation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (A2) exposing the contradiction between peak-season priority and demand-spreading, and dismantling the "respectful locals" conservation claim with structural wear mechanics.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: granting peak-season priority cannot both reserve peak slots and expect residents to voluntarily move off-peak, and foot-traffic damage is physical, not moral. PRO's "respectful locals cause less wear" hurt grounding and its answers to Q1/Q2 were evasive, while CON directly answered pressure and offered superior comparatives (ladder vs wall, proximity advantage, timed entry alternative).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 exposure of the peak-priority / demand-spreading contradiction and the erosion-is-foot-traffic-not-etiquette rebuttal that neutralized PRO's core mechanism.
