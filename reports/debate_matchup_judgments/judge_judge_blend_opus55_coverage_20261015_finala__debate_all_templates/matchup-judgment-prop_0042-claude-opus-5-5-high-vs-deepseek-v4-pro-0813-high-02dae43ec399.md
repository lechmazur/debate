- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that lower-income households already bear substantial hidden costs and that a lump-sum rebate preserves the marginal price signal while protecting budgets. Its closing also treated higher prices as the policy's entire effect without weighing reduced external harms. Side A lost some ground through two clipped turns and by understating Side B's progressive-funding alternative, but retained the clearer comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the rebate dilemma was decisive: a fixed payment does not reduce the price of the next unit of meat, so compensation and behavioral incentives can coexist. Side B ultimately conceded that marginal point and shifted to imperfect coverage, leaving implementation concerns rather than a fundamental contradiction.

Side B effectively demonstrated that Side A's proposed regulation would also raise prices for the poor, but without generating revenue for compensation. Side A's attempt to trap Side B on the "even if" clause of the motion yielded a strong bullet-biting response from B, who convincingly argued that the poor are already paying these costs invisibly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal and subsequent pressure question perfectly dismantled Side A's dilemma regarding the rebate weakening the price signal, forcing Side A to walk back the economic claim in R2.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by establishing that the status quo is not costless for low-income households; the poor already pay meat’s environmental and health costs through disease, polluted neighborhoods, and climate shocks. This reframing made the motion’s conceded price hike a transfer of an existing hidden burden rather than a new harm. PRO also decisively dissolved CON’s central “rebate dilemma” by explaining that a lump-sum dividend preserves the marginal price signal—every additional kilo still costs more—while restoring total budgets. CON never engaged this mechanism, instead repeating the dilemma. PRO further turned CON’s regulatory alternative by noting it raises prices too yet generates no revenue to return. While CON landed real pressure with PRO’s admission that they would defend the tax without a rebate, PRO successfully argued that even then the policy makes costs visible and avoidable while creating revenue options that regulation lacks. PRO’s superior crystallization and cleaner comparative path outweigh their word-limit execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 1 dissolution of the rebate dilemma (B2.2–B2.3), clearly explaining that a lump-sum rebate restores total budgets without weakening marginal price signals because every extra kilogram of meat still costs more at the margin. CON reiterated the dilemma in later rounds but never answered this mechanism, collapsing their main offensive.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by isolating what the proposition uniquely requires rather than disputing that meat production causes harm. B showed that targeted methane pricing, subsidy removal, and direct regulation can price pollution, raise or free revenue, and support poor households without stacking a disputed health surcharge onto all meat. A weakened its fidelity to the motion by redefining “full” externalities as a conservative lower bound and could not explain how a farm-gate production tax would avoid charging protein-deficient children for health harms associated with excess consumption. A also repeated in closing that only its tax generates protective revenue after B had directly shown that methane pricing does too. The clipping costs B some execution credit, but the visible case still resolved the central comparison decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s pressure answers was decisive: it noted that methane pricing raises revenue, then exposed the contradiction between A promising not to add health costs to protein-deficient children’s meat and advocating a production tax imposed before the consumer is known.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won decisively by outmaneuvering Side B on the practical mechanics of the policy. Side A trapped Side B into claiming that health externalities would "not be added" to the meat of protein-deficient children, and then cleanly dismantled this by pointing out that a farm-gate production tax cannot selectively exempt specific consumers. Side A also successfully neutralized Side B's revenue advantage by showing how alternative policies generate funds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.6-A4.7, where Side A points out that a production tax is levied at the farm gate and mathematically cannot exempt a poor toddler from the health surcharge, destroying Side B's primary mitigation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing that the motion's production tax is indiscriminate by design—it is levied at the farm gate and cannot distinguish excess consumption from necessary protein for poor children—while PRO's retreat from "full" externalities to a conservative lower-bound estimate conceded the measurement problem. CON further demonstrated that broad carbon pricing, ending feed subsidies, and targeted regulation achieve environmental pricing and generate revenue for poor households without imposing the motion's unique, regressive health surcharge. PRO's admission that it would support the tax even when rebates miss vulnerable families underscored the motion's blunt burden on the poor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 exposing that a farm-gate production tax cannot distinguish an overweight executive from an anaemic toddler, thereby gutting PRO's promise that protein-deficient children would be shielded, and showing that methane pricing and subsidy repeal generate equivalent protective revenue without the motion's indiscriminate health stack.
