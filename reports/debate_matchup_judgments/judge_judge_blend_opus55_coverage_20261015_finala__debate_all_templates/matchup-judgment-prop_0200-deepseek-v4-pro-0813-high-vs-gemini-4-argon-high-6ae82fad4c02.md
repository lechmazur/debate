### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON landed the decisive blow. PRO's opening relied on a "lose the license, lose the right to sell" market-hook mechanism, but in Rebuttal 1 PRO admitted that without recognition, private firms "cannot finance or insure" missions—CON immediately turned this into a concession that withholding recognition already restrains premature commercial mining, undercutting PRO's "unlit gray-zone race" framing. CON's Pressure Question (Q1) then forced PRO into an awkward pivot: the real gray-zone actor is a self-financing state agency that doesn't need a commercial sales license at all. PRO tried to rescue this by claiming the recognition hook still applies "as the price of lawful sale or transfer," but CON correctly flagged this as incoherent in Rebuttal 2 and closing: a state extracting for domestic strategic use needs no sales license, so PRO's regulatory hook has "zero leverage over the only gray-zone actor they can identify." PRO never cleanly resolved this contradiction in its final closing, instead reasserting the same floor-vs-no-floor framing without addressing the state-actor loophole CON exposed. CON's case progression was tighter: it kept returning to the scarcity/chokepoint framing and the financing-bottleneck-as-leverage argument, and its closing crystallized the clash cleanly ("their regulatory hook cannot catch the only threat they identify"). PRO had some strong counters (reversing causation on treaty leverage, distinguishing launch licensing from title/sale rights) but didn't fully capitalize on the mutual concession that states bypass any policy regardless, leaving CON's rebuttal chain more damaging and more memorable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exploitation of PRO's admission in [A2.1] ("unable to sell...finance...insure") combined with the Pressure Question forcing PRO to identify the gray-zone actor as a state agency, followed by CON's charge of incoherence in Rebuttal 2 ("A state agency extracting for domestic strategic use does not need a market sales license... PRO's regulatory hook has zero leverage over the only gray-zone actor they can identify")—a contradiction PRO never resolved.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash: A admitted private capital is throttled without recognized sales, then pivoted to state-backed gray-zone operators as the real threat—yet conditional commercial recognition cannot bind foreign/strategic extractors who do not need a sales license. That mismatch undercut A's regulatory-hook case, while B's leverage/race weighing on scarce polar ice remained cleaner after exchanges.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 / Closing on the incoherence: private firms cannot mine without recognition (A's admission), but the only gray-zone actor A can name (state agencies) is untouched by a commercial-sale hook.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): CON won the leverage clash. PRO conceded private firms cannot finance without recognized sale, which supports CON's point that withholding recognition restrains the race, while launch licensing already covers transparency/debris. PRO then pivoted to state-backed operators as the gray-zone threat, but B showed a commercial sales license adds zero leverage over a rival state extracting for domestic/strategic use, yet it uniquely unlocks private capital to exhaust scarce polar ice. Transparency as early warning does not prevent depletion, leaving PRO with a visible rush rather than a governed floor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 and B4 exposing the fatal mismatch: admission in [A2.1] that private capital is blocked without recognition proves withholding works, while the state champion [A4.1] that bypasses private finance is not bound by PRO's commercial sales hook [A4.2/B4.3-B4.5].

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides executed the format cleanly (proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure), but A built a more complete throughline. A's "bucket vs. mine" distinction and fishing-quota analogy directly answered B's expropriation worry, and A's monopoly argument—that banning commercial sale just hands craters to unaccountable state agencies with zero incentive to ever ratify a treaty—was never fully neutralized. B's closing conceded the key mechanism (A4's pressure question forced B to admit that banning sale sharply reduces private financing, B4.1), and A exploited that concession decisively in closing, framing it as proof that B's "safety without sale" alternative collapses into state monopoly. B's own alternative (licensing-based safety without legalizing sale) never explained how non-treaty site protections would bind superpowers any better than the treaty B is waiting for, leaving a live gap A kept pressing. B's distributive argument (first movers lock in value before global consent) remained a real point throughout but was weakened once A reframed extraction-without-sale as equally or more distributionally harmful.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q2 in Pressure Questions (why would banning commercial sale stop superpowers from draining craters for sovereign use, removing any incentive to ratify a treaty) combined with B's concession in B4.1 that banning sale "removes the ability to privatize" and sharply reduces private financing—A then weaponized this admission in closing (A5.1-A5.2) to argue B's own position guarantees the state monopoly outcome B claims to fear, and B's closing never directly rebutted this specific dynamic.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A won by converting B’s core alternative into a worse distributive outcome: banning sale kills private financing, leaves polar ice to superpower agencies that hoard and lack incentive to ratify, and thus locks developing states out of both treaty and market. A kept the bucket-vs-mine and fishing-quota distinction live against expropriation fears, answered verification/flag-of-convenience pressure with market and sensor gatekeeping, and crystallized that conditional recognition supplies the compliance leverage and open access B’s “licenses without sale” never fully funded. B stayed principled on nonrenewable scarcity and pre-treaty assignment, but never neutralized the monopoly/incentive trap or made safety-without-sale more than aspirational.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A1/A4–A5 line: title only after extraction (like catch quotas) blocks vested expropriation claims, while B’s admission that sale bans deter private missions hands the craters to non-selling state monopolies.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO wins the central fork: CON admits banning sales kills private financing (B4.1) leaving only state missions, but then cannot explain why that superpower-only world is better for equity or protects scarce ice. PRO's bucket-vs-mine distinction plus conditional leverage (transparency/debris as price of recognized title) preserves future treaty authority while solving the immediate vacuum that CON's safety-only licenses leave for sovereign extraction. CON's distributive harm is real but is reported, not prevented, by its own alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 A1-A2 plus A4.3-A4.4: banning commercial sale does not stop state draining of polar craters, it just removes the market that lets smaller states buy propellant, and buyer/insurer gatekeeping plus post-extraction title defeats flag-of-convenience and expropriation fears.
