### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Both sides built strong, well-grounded cases, but the decisive clash came down to whether shortened windows plus theatrical accessibility fixes can substitute for simultaneous release. A's strongest point—that bedbound, immunocompromised, and caregiving viewers can never be served by any in-theater fix, and are still excluded from the opening-week cultural conversation under a "shortened window"—was explicitly conceded by B in Rebuttal 2 ("You are right... I will not pretend otherwise"), and B's subsequent pivot to "a properly shortened window... achieves inclusion" never actually closed the gap, since a shortened window is still a delay, not day-one access. A exploited this concession cleanly in closing. Meanwhile, B's central economic objection (cannibalization via Mulan/Black Widow) was met by A with a plausible rebuttal (pandemic confound, zero marginal cost of already-produced accessibility assets, additive rather than substitutive digital revenue), leaving B's revenue-collapse claim weakened though not destroyed. B did land a sharp pressure question on the vagueness of PRO's "almost every big title" line-drawing, which A answered only loosely—an unresolved soft spot for A—but this didn't offset the core concession on the day-one-access issue. On balance, A more fully neutralized B's chief argument than B neutralized A's, giving A the cleaner path to the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 concession ("You are right that perfect captioning glasses do not get a bedbound viewer into a building") followed by A's closing exploitation of that concession to show the "shortened window" fix still leaves excluded viewers waiting past the cultural moment.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) established an irreducible accessibility gap—viewers who physically cannot enter theaters—that CON's shortened-window alternative could not close, while undermining CON's economic doom scenario by contextualizing pandemic-era hybrid releases and showing that day-one accessibility features add zero production cost. CON's financing argument remained abstract and speculative compared to PRO's concrete inclusion of excluded audiences.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4's pressure-question answers, especially the zero-cost accessibility point and the reframing of simultaneous release as adding a home option rather than stripping theaters, which neutralized CON's claim that PRO was destroying theatrical financing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B built a more compelling case by effectively narrowing the scope of the motion to exclude visual spectacles, thereby neutralizing Side A's "burn the theater" slippery slope. Side A's economic arguments relied heavily on pandemic-era examples that Side B successfully contextualized, and Side A struggled to defend why mid-budget films need an exclusive theatrical window when they are already struggling to fill seats.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they directly answered the pressure questions, clarified the higher margins of digital sales compared to exhibitor splits, and exposed Side A's failure to provide an opening-night solution for homebound viewers.
