### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that A’s harms stem primarily from poor scheduling rather than international travel itself, then offering targeted remedies that preserve global benefits. A’s case depended on suspiciously precise injury claims and the sweeping assertion that leagues never implement fixes, which B countered with ordinary examples such as post-trip byes and collective-bargaining leverage. B also exposed A’s institutional contradiction: A distrusts leagues to assess costs honestly yet relies on them to apply the proposed guardrail. B’s clipped rebuttal was an execution cost, but its decisive analysis remained visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange followed A’s concession that well-planned and compensated trips need not be reduced. B used that answer to narrow the dispute to bad planning and argue that mandatory byes, paired games, and fan credits address the actual failure without sacrificing international games or long-term growth.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a fatal contradiction in Side A's case: if leagues are too short-sighted to schedule a basic bye week, they cannot be trusted to execute the motion's cost-benefit math honestly. Side B also effectively used unrefuted examples of NFL byes to dismantle Side A's claim that leagues 'never' improve scheduling.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal and subsequent pressure questions, which trapped Side A by asking why we should trust incompetent leagues to run the motion's cost-benefit math.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON wins the central weighing and remedy clash. PRO's harms (fatigue, fan loss) were reframed as bad-planning harms, not location harms. PRO conceded under B's Q1 that fixed, compensated trips should NOT be scaled back [A4.1], collapsing the motion to punishing badly planned trips. CON then showed the motion's test is rigged because brand gains are long-term/unmeasured [B2.2, B4.7] and unenforceable if leagues are untrustworthy [B4.2, B5.2]. CON's targeted fix (mandatory byes, paired series, CBA fan credits) keeps the global upside while solving the same injury/fan concerns, which PRO never squarely defeated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2/B5 double-bind: if leagues can't be trusted to schedule a bye, they can't be trusted to run PRO's outweigh test — and PRO's own no answer to a fixed trip shows the real problem is planning, not the ocean, so surgery-to-cure-a-splinter scaling back is the wrong remedy.
