### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion's "best" language live and forcing PRO into repeated narrowing: free scam response toward algorithmic intake/triage, and proactive anti-discovery behind a paywall as a mere "matching setting." That undercut PRO's duty-of-care path while CON's adversary/unit-economics case (verification spam, scarce human credibility work, paid friction, niche-app cost shock) stayed comparatively clearer. PRO's wealth-vs-severity framing and pressure questions were sharp, but never fully restored a free "best" package without looking like the status quo plus rhetoric.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's retreat/definition clash (A2.1 then A4.3–A4.5/A5.1–A5.2): PRO's free tier collapses into baseline automation while paywalled invisibility remains "best" protection against stalkers.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that the motion’s core safety infrastructure—identity verification, anti-evasion blocking, and meaningful scam response—can and should be free by default without collapsing into unlimited concierge service. Side A cleanly separated block tools from optional discovery modes, exposed that payment does not reliably verify identity or deter organized scammers, and crystallized the moral distinction between funding safety capacity and selling priority access to it. Side B’s scarcity and vendor-cost arguments were live, but overreached by treating incognito browsing as a block tool and by repeatedly asserting that Side A had abandoned the motion when Side A’s position remained consistent throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s rebuttal demonstrating that funding a safety queue and selling priority in that queue are different choices, combined with the observation that stolen payment methods and disposable cards mean financial friction burdens genuine low-income users more than sophisticated scammers, decisively undermined Side B’s claim that paywalls are necessary security barriers.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by grounding the debate in the practical mechanics of adversarial environments and unit economics. While Side B presents a strong moral case against wealth-based safety allocation, Side A effectively demonstrates that making resource-intensive features (like biometric verification and human review) free by default removes crucial financial friction against bot farms and either bankrupts platforms or forces a downgrade to easily weaponized automated triage. Side A's sharp critiques of Side B's concessions on "proactive invisibility" and the vulnerabilities of algorithmic severity triage secured the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Turn 8 critique of Side B's "temporary restrictions pending review" model, pointing out that without immediate human review, abusers can weaponize coordinated false reports from burner accounts to automatically freeze innocent targets.
