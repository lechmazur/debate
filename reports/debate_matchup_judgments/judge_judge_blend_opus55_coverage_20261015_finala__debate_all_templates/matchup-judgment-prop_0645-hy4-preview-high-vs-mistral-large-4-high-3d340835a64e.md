### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and stayed on the resolution, but B landed the more decisive mechanical critiques. B's "alchemy" rebuttal exposed that a court seal cannot unscrape data already held by brokers, undercutting A's central claim that sealing "turns off the spigot." A's reply—that sealing creates an enforceable legal predicate against brokers—was asserted but never substantiated against B's direct challenge that courts bind clerks, not Equifax. More damaging still, B's "blind petition is a fiction" point (that a licensing board cannot petition to unseal a record it does not know exists) directly attacked the practical core of A's "narrow exceptions" safeguard, and A's closing never answered it, leaving a live, unresolved hole in the PRO mechanism. B also offered a coherent alternative (expunge minor records, ban commercial resale and algorithmic auto-rejection, keep serious adjudications open for human vetting) that absorbed A's own stated harms (algorithmic screening, data-broker permanence) without the practical cost of blinding safety-sensitive vetting. A's moral framing about adulthood and redemption was compelling and consistently delivered, but by the end B had done more to neutralize A's mechanism and sharpen the comparative stakes, making B's case the easier one to vote for.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's "blind petition is a fiction" (B4.4) devastated A's safety-valve mechanism by showing that presumptive sealing removes the very visibility a licensing board would need to know to petition for an exception—an objection A's closing never addressed.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won by showing that presumptive sealing is simultaneously incomplete against already-scraped private data and overbroad for serious adjudications. A offered a compelling rehabilitation principle, but its crucial claim that sealing enables effective deletion was asserted rather than established, while its proposed petition process never resolved how an institution discovers an invisible record. B supplied the cleaner comparison: directly restrict brokers and automated rejection while preserving human review for sensitive positions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s “blind petition” response was decisive. A said licensing boards could petition for access to sealed records, but B showed that a board cannot seek a record it does not know exists, undermining A’s claimed safety exception and reinforcing the burden problem.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B wins by exposing a fatal practical flaw in Side A's mechanism: a licensing board or school cannot petition to unseal a juvenile record it cannot see on a standard background check. Side B also provides a more direct and effective alternative (regulating data brokers and algorithmic screening directly rather than relying on court sealing to indirectly stop scraping) and successfully defends its graduated system against Side A's rehabilitation critiques.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing argument pointing out that "a school hiring a coach cannot petition to unseal a record it cannot see," which logically dismantles Side A's proposed safety mechanism for vulnerable-population vetting.
