### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently and the debate narrowed cleanly, but CON's framing burden proved more durable. CON established early (A1.1) that PRO had to prove categorical obsolescence, not mere inferiority, and sustained the point that PRO's own alternative—"selective sanctuaries"—still relies on captivity, insurance populations, and specialist infrastructure, meaning PRO never actually eliminates captivity, it only relabels and strips public access. PRO never squarely answered CON's "reserves cannot backstop their own failure" and "insurance populations don't require individual release" points, leaving a real functional gap in the conservation half of PRO's case. PRO's rhetoric was sharper (the "retreat to selective captivity as concession" move, "living ticket-booths" framing) and its use of pressure questions effectively forced CON onto the defensive, but CON's A1/A2 answers directly rebutted the "concession" characterization and CON's closing reasserted the unresolved burden point cleanly. PRO's claim that digital venues would generate "equal or greater net funding" for habitat protection remained an unsupported assumption that CON repeatedly flagged without a real answer. On balance, CON's structural and functional-gap arguments were the more durable live routes to victory, even though PRO's delivery and proportionality judo made this a close, well-matched debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange (B3.1/A3.1 into A1 at B4.1) was decisive: PRO tried to convert CON's "selective, high-welfare captivity" language into a concession of PRO's thesis, but CON's direct answer ("I concede only that unnecessary captivity should be reduced—not that modern zoos are unjustified") held the line, while PRO never answered CON's parallel point that PRO's own sanctuary/breeding-center model still constitutes captivity and cannot independently guarantee conservation insurance against reserve failure.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO won by making the motion’s “less captivity” clause decisive and forcing CON onto its own terms. After CON endorsed proportionality, selective captivity, and only “incremental” educational value, PRO showed that the commercial urban-display zoo—not essential rescue/breeding—is what remains unjustified once digital tools and habitat-backed sanctuaries are available. CON’s relabeling critique was real, and the absolute “no longer justified” bar is high, but CON never cleanly proved public spectacle is necessary rather than additive, so PRO left the cleaner path to the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4): answers B’s pressure questions head-on, converts CON’s “selective”/“incremental” concessions into a win on “less captivity,” and collapses the zoo-vs-sanctuary distinction back onto the motion.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A successfully isolated the commercial and public-facing spectacle as the defining, unjustifiable feature of the "modern zoo," while cleanly conceding the need for non-public ex-situ facilities to satisfy the "less captivity" condition of the prompt. Side A's answers to the pressure questions were exceptionally sharp, explaining exactly how public access warps institutional incentives and animal welfare. Side B relied too heavily on semantic arguments (claiming PRO was just relabeling reformed zoos as sanctuaries) and struggled to defend the keeping of non-endangered animals under their own proportionality test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically the A1 answer explaining how the imperative for public sightlines and gate revenue structurally forces modern zoos to prioritize charismatic crowd-pleasers over pure conservation, effectively neutralizing Side B's attempt to blur the line between zoos and sanctuaries.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases, but CON (A) landed the more decisive structural blow: in Rebuttal 2, CON pointed out that PRO's "targeted conservation centers" — which must hold related surrogate species, non-releasable rescues, and precautionary populations to avoid fatal trial-and-error — are themselves broad captive collections in all but name. This exposed PRO's model as reinventing the zoo while stripping it of its proven funding mechanism, a point CON hammered home memorably in closing ("bricks up the viewing windows," "an unfunded ark is a sunken ark"). PRO's closing attempt to rescue the distinction ("purpose and selection, not variety, defines a zoo") was logically available but thin and arrived too late to fully neutralize the funding-collapse charge, especially after PRO had already conceded "no mechanism ensures" replacement funding. PRO's asymmetry framing (captivity harm certain, conservation payoff conditional) was a strong counter-frame, but CON's closing inversion — that irreversible extinction is the true certain harm being gambled against hypothetical tax levies and screens — answered it adequately. Pressure Questions were well-executed on both sides with proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure. Overall, CON made the comparative stakes clearer and left PRO's central practical alternative looking structurally indistinguishable from the institution it sought to abolish.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1, A4.4) catching that PRO's "targeted centers" holding related/non-releasable/precautionary populations are themselves broad captive collections, undercutting PRO's core necessity-without-captivity claim.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B built the clearer real-world path: exhibition, continuous multi-species practice, and recreation revenue are interlocking, so PRO’s “targeted centers only” either starves capacity or reinvents the zoo without its funding engine. A’s purpose/necessity distinction and ethical asymmetry were sharp but never fully solved continuity, net funding, or mass access; B kept forcing that feasibility gap and crystallized it better by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 turning A’s “related/non-releasable/risk-based” answer into concession that broad captivity remains, just defunded and closed-door.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's alternative model. While Side A drew a clean philosophical line between "modern zoos" and "targeted conservation centers," Side B effectively demonstrated that A's centers would still require broad captivity for veterinary practice and assurance populations. B's rhetorical trap—that A's model simply "bricks up the viewing windows" while destroying the recreational funding that makes such captivity viable—was decisive. Side A struggled to answer the funding pressure, admitting that no mechanism ensures legislative support, which B rightly framed as an unacceptable gamble with extinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they trapped Side A's concession that "targeted centers" must hold related species and rescues, proving that A's model still relies on captivity but strips away the public funding that sustains it, reducing A's ethical high ground to a mere cosmetic change.
