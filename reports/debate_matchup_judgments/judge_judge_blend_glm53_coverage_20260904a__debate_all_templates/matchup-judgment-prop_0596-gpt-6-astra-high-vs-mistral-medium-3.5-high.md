### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by making the live decision rule clearer: PRO had to justify requiring this coercive pathway for complex gene therapies, not merely that high prices are bad. B’s lawful-versus-viable competition distinction repeatedly undercut A’s patent-as-binding-constraint claim, exposed the weak HIV-to-gene-editing transfer, and forced A into a royalty answer that subordinated investment returns to affordability. A stayed coherent on monopoly power and moral urgency, but repeated rather than resolved the manufacturing and incentive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 turn on compelled tech transfer plus the royalty conflict: A expanded the remedy and then conceded affordability priority, confirming B’s incremental-benefit burden rather than answering it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing that PRO conflated legal patent authorization with viable therapeutic access. For complex autologous gene-editing therapies, manufacturing validation and proprietary know-how are decisive bottlenecks that a patent license alone does not resolve. CON sharply distinguished the HIV pill precedent from gene therapies, showed that compelling technology transfer expands the intervention far beyond licensing, and caught PRO conceding that affordability would override investment-supporting returns when royalties conflict. Because PRO never proved that lawful competition would become credible competition in time to help patients, and because CON offered plausible alternative mechanisms (pooled purchasing, conditional public funding, coverage financing) that improve access without undermining innovation, CON demonstrated that requiring this specific coercive pathway is not justified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal distinguishing lawful competition from viable competition for gene therapies, and exposing PRO's concession that affordability would override investment-supporting returns in royalty-setting, thereby undermining the claim that innovation incentives are safely preserved.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B effectively dismantled Side A's core analogy (HIV antiretrovirals) by distinguishing the manufacturing and clinical realities of gene-editing therapies from small-molecule drugs. Side B's pressure questions exposed critical gaps in Side A's mechanism, particularly regarding technology transfer and the tradeoff between affordable royalties and R&D incentives for rare diseases. Side B's distinction between making competition "lawful" versus "viable" was the decisive clash, and Side A failed to adequately bridge that gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it capitalized on Side A's answers to the pressure questions, pointing out that compelling technology transfer is a massive expansion of the policy and that prioritizing affordability over R&D returns concedes the risk to future innovation for small patient populations.
