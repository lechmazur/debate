### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the debate focused on implementation. A made a persuasive moral case against exploiting minors, but its eventual mechanism covered designated youth accounts or unverified profiles. B convincingly argued that the former largely duplicates existing controls while the latter burdens privacy-conscious adults and reintroduces verification. A never resolved that dilemma and largely repeated its principled case in closing. B did overstate some claims about universal platform defaults and adult whales, but its policy critique remained the clearest live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively split A’s mechanism into two horns: youth accounts where protections already exist, and unverified profiles where caps spill onto adults. A’s closing did not answer that implementation mismatch.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing a lethal mechanism dilemma: PRO's policy either binds only self-declared youth accounts—making it the status quo with statutory letterhead—or it binds all actual minors, which requires the mass age-verification surveillance PRO spent the debate disavowing. CON further shows that PRO's named market failure, the "whale" extraction model, is overwhelmingly an adult phenomenon that a youth cap cannot touch. PRO's moral case against predatory design is forceful, but it never resolves how the mandated policy would actually reach new children without unacceptable collateral costs, and CON's "redundant where it binds, invasive where it bites" framing crystallizes this unrepaired burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (Turn 8), which springs the mechanism trap on PRO's B4.1 answer. CON demonstrates that "designated youth accounts" already require parental approval by default, while "unverified payment profiles" smuggles in the very surveillance apparatus PRO denied. CON then turns PRO's "whale extraction" market failure against them by noting whales are predominantly adults, so a youth cap misses its own target. This exchange collapses PRO's practical path to victory.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won on mechanism and definitional control. PRO showed youth vulnerability to loot-box design, but CON's enforcement dilemma stuck: youth caps either apply only to self-declared youth accounts that already require parental approval by default - making the mandate redundant - or to all actual minors, requiring universal age verification and capping unverified adults. PRO's answer in A4.1 embraced both horns, and its whale market-failure claim turned out to be adult-centered, which youth caps don't reach. That left PRO without incremental benefit to outweigh surveillance, sprawl, and displacement costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1 + B4.3-B4.5 trapping PRO on "designated youth accounts and unverified payment profiles" - showing the policy is status-quo where it binds and surveillant where it bites, plus mismatch that whale extractions are adult, not youth.
