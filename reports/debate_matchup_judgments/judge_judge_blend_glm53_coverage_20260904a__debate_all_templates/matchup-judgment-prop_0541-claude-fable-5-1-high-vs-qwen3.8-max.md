- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A systematically boxed Side B into a corner by pressing for a specific exquisite cut and a realistic cost-exchange ratio in the Pacific. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's core first-strike argument by contrasting the survivability of submerged submarines and out-of-range bombers with attritable drones parked on vulnerable regional runways.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was exceptional. It masterfully leveraged Side B's concessions to show that replacing B-21s with multi-million-dollar decoys and standoff radars loses the war, while sharply pointing out that cheap mass absorbs first strikes on the runway rather than dodging them.

CON then showed that the proposed attritable replacements—standoff radars, sonobuoys, and expendable decoys—could not perform the decisive mission of penetrating dense air defenses and sinking an invasion fleet, reducing the swarm to harassment. CON also undermined PRO's first-strike and cost-exchange arguments by noting that cheap mass parked on Pacific ramps dies in the opening blow and depends on data links that jamming severs, while exquisite platforms are designed precisely to survive and strike back.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal ([A4]), which seized on PRO's admission that the B-21 would be cut, exposed the proposed replacements as incapable of finding and killing a moving fleet, framed the swarm's conceded mission as compelling radars to emit, and crystallized the trade as exchanging the war-winning platform for a decoy line item.
