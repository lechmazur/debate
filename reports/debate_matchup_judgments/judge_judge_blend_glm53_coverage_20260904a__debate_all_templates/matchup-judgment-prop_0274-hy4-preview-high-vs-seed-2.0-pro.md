B directly explained why reduced conversion still matters and better matched the motion’s narrow limit. B’s erroneous C-reference was a minor execution cost, not enough to alter the result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions directly, rejected the demand for a preverified percentage, and showed that a same-hour alert can be useless as warning while still functioning as an accelerant through one-tap navigation and continued algorithmic discovery.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): PRO won the central clash on arrival rate vs discoverability. B conceded the spot won't stay secret, which undercuts its own harm story, and was caught in a contradiction - geotag as vital brake-light signal yet also same-hour useless warning - that B4 tried to patch as confirmation but still admits no lead time. B's scavenger-hunt and total-ban enforcement claims widen rather than disprove the narrow limit on commercial promotion.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it explicitly called out Side A's contradiction of treating the geotag as both a valuable early warning signal and a useless same-hour alert, effectively neutralizing A's "brake lights" analogy.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won because it framed limits as a complementary, time-sensitive harm-reduction tool rather than a substitute for visitor caps or infrastructure. Side A compellingly defended direct regulation and exposed enforcement problems, but its claim that promotion limits are either redundant or a placebo never defeated B’s central point that partially slowing future viral traffic can buy time even if other discovery channels remain. B’s unsupported Maya Bay timeline, repeated exact growth claims, and invented “90%” figure substantially weakened its grounding, while A’s closing also incurred a reference error by citing nonexistent C-series IDs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s seatbelt comparison crystallized the decisive response: a measure need not stop every visitor to be worthwhile alongside direct management. A’s answer—that if both measures exist the speech restriction is necessarily superfluous—did not establish why reducing one important source of additional traffic has no marginal value.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive impact vs expression comparison. PRO needed a fast brake after measurable harm but before peak surge; CON showed B2.1/B4.1 that once harm is measurable a conduct cap still cuts feet while a speech limit only edits the ad, easily bypassed via 'secret beach' posts, dropped pins, and AI itineraries, and that cheap speech rules invite governments to skip bins/parking/caps. PRO conceded in A4.2 it doesn't stop all visitors and leaned on an invented 90% cut, while CON answered both Pressure Questions directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: late cap still cuts impact vs late speech only hides label, plus the substitution/redundancy dilemma - if governments are too slow for real management they will take the cheap speech ban as cover, if diligent they don't need it.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by exposing the logical contradiction in Side B's trigger condition (waiting for measurable harm means the spike already happened) and effectively dismantling Side B's analogies. Side A also correctly capitalized on Side B's use of brittle fake precision ("two weeks," "90%") in the second rebuttal, while maintaining a stronger, more grounded case that physical conduct limits are the actual solution to overtourism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's seatbelt analogy in Rebuttal 2, pointing out that a seatbelt restrains the body in a crash, while a geotag ban merely restrains a description of the crash while the vehicle keeps moving.
