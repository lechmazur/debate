### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): CON won by effectively dismantling PRO's 'material harm' standard. By introducing the concept of proactive risk management—illustrated vividly by the gambling bookkeeper example—CON demonstrated that forcing employers to wait for 'demonstrable harm' before firing paralyzes prudent business judgment, while PRO's counter that risk management is just disguised prejudice failed to account for existing discrimination laws.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8) delivered a decisive blow by using the gambling bookkeeper example to show that employers must act on risk before harm materializes, directly answering PRO's pressure question and undermining PRO's 'demonstrable impact' standard.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO won by forcing the debate onto the motion's own terms: after materiality is stipulated, what remains is pure managerial power over non-job conduct. CON never produced a clean case of truly irrelevant lawful off-duty conduct that still deserved unprotected firing, defaulting instead to preemptive risk forecasts that collapse into the materiality exception or invite prejudice. PRO also exploited CON's concession that at-will already yields to race/religion/union/etc. statutes, making job-relevance a more coherent line than category lists, while existing state examples undercut the litigation-doom prediction. CON's reciprocity, small-employer cost, and vagueness arguments stayed live and competitive but never displaced the core normative burden of justifying livelihood destruction for non-performance reasons.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 plus A4.5/A5.1: CON could not name a pure irrelevant case and was forced into pre-materialization risk examples that PRO showed rationalize any disapproval (politics, identity) as "brand risk," exposing the whim CON was actually defending.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO won the central burden: the motion already permits firing when harm is material. CON never justified firing for genuinely irrelevant off-duty conduct, retreating to pre-material risk management, which B exposed as indistinguishable from the prejudice rationale used to fire gay teachers or interracial couples. B's contradiction argument – CON praises race/religion protections but calls job-relevance protection a one-sided mandate – and existence proof from Colorado/California/NY/ND remained live, while A's vagueness/litigation costs point was answered by citing parallel standards like undue hardship/BFOQ. A's clipped opening also hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B5.1 + B2.1 – turning CON's risk-before-harm principle into proof that any disfavored life becomes a "brand risk" and noting CON already accepts one-sided mandates in its praised civil-rights statutes.
