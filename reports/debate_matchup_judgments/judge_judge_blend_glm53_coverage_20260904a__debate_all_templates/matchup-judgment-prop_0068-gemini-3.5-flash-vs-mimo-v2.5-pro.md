### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): B won by making the central problem with the proposition clearer: the category of AI-optimized persuasion targeted to users or micro-groups is so broad that it captures ordinary modern advertising and benign public-interest campaigns. A defended real-time awareness and autonomy well, but ultimately answered the line-drawing challenge by saying the criterion is methodology, not intent, which conceded B's core point that the label would apply broadly. B then persuasively argued that such a label becomes generic, fatigued, and less informative than alternatives like targeting controls and transparency tools.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was pressing A on the methodology criterion: either the label applies to nearly all modern targeted campaigns and becomes wallpaper, or platforms must make discretionary judgments about manipulation. A's answer chose the broad route, letting B crystallize the decisive "label everything means inform nothing" rebuttal.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): CON built the clearer path by showing the mandate is unworkably broad and indistinguishable from ordinary advertising, with PRO unable to supply a workable line that avoids label fatigue or platform discretion; B's rebuttals and pressure answers sharpened this comparison more effectively than A's real-time awareness framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's rebuttal on PRO's methodology criterion collapsing into labeling everything, directly neutralizing A's distinction and exposing the binary-label emptiness.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B wins by successfully trapping Side A on the resolution's scope. B demonstrated that the motion's language captures virtually all modern digital advertising, making the proposed label ubiquitous and vulnerable to habituation. A's attempt to limit the mandate to "sophisticated" targeting collapsed when pressed, as A's own "methodology" criterion still swept in benign public health and advocacy campaigns, confirming B's overbreadth charge. B's cookie-banner analogy effectively neutralized A's claim that labels trigger real-time skepticism, while A's "Sponsored" tag comparison was cleanly dismantled in Rebuttal 2 as conveying clear, binary information unlike a vague AI warning. A never adequately solved the label-fatigue problem or offered a workable bright line. Despite B's closing being clipped (an execution cost), B's structural critique and superior weighing of compliance theater versus genuine user control provided a clearer, more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 dismantling A's "Sponsored" tag analogy by noting that "Sponsored" answers a specific, actionable question while "AI-optimized" is a vague categorical flag that breeds habituation, directly collapsing A's core mechanism for real-time user protection.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): A wins because it made the better comparative case that ubiquity strengthens, rather than defeats, the case for disclosure. B’s label-fatigue and overbreadth arguments were strong and kept the round competitive, especially by showing that dynamic optimization is already common. But A more effectively neutralized the censorship/intent objection by framing the label as disclosure of a technical mechanism, not a moral judgment, and B’s data-privacy alternative was not mutually exclusive with labeling and did not answer the value of point-of-contact awareness. A had minor execution flaws, including the clipped turn and bad reference IDs, but its core route survived more clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s most decisive rebuttal was turning B’s “optimization is everywhere” claim into a reason for transparency: if adaptive targeting is pervasive, users have more need to know, not less. B’s fatigue response was forceful but too dependent on treating all labels as useless warnings.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins by turning CON's pervasiveness concession into a decisive transparency argument, cleanly answering pressure questions on retroactive awareness and technical distinguishability, and maintaining sharper comparative weighing throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly neutralized fatigue and intent objections while exposing CON's privacy solution as blind at the point of influence.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins by successfully turning Side B's core claim—that AI optimization is ubiquitous—into a decisive argument for transparency rather than against it. A effectively analogized universal optimization to universal ingredient lists or "Sponsored" tags, neutralizing the label fatigue offense. A also landed a cleaner structural blow on B's preferred alternative, demonstrating that upstream data privacy cannot provide point-of-contact awareness once a tailored message has already been served. B's technical points about dynamic creative optimization were strong, but B struggled to overcome A's clarification that the mandate targets a verifiable technical mechanism, not subjective human intent, leaving A with the more coherent and actionable path to voter autonomy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turn of the ubiquity argument: by conceding that optimization is pervasive but arguing this makes disclosure more essential, not less, A flipped B's main offensive pillar into a liability while simultaneously clarifying that the label tracks technical mechanisms, not moral intent.
