### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a more disciplined and consistently decisive case across all five rounds. The cholera/seawall concrete image was introduced in the opening and maintained throughout without being neutralized—PRO's best answer (that menus can include health surveillance) was circular: it assumed perfect pre-certification before the crisis, precisely the condition CON said cannot be guaranteed when a disaster exceeds its categories. CON's use of the Grenada example was sharper: PRO cited it as proof of concept, CON correctly identified that tailoring was the operative success variable, and automaticity strips exactly that tailoring away. PRO never fully answered this. The "algorithmic probation" and "key sits abroad" framings were vivid and argumentatively grounded, not merely rhetorical. CON's pressure questions were better targeted—the "bargaining requires a right of refusal" line in Rebuttal 2 was the debate's cleanest crystallization. PRO's first rebuttal was clipped, which is a real execution cost that left CON's Grenada argument partially unanswered. PRO's A1 answer in Rebuttal 2 made a significant concession ("post-disaster treasuries retain other revenue instruments and donor emergency grants"), partially undermining the automaticity argument's urgency. PRO's sovereignty-inversion ("the real sovereignty thief is the spiral") was its strongest rhetorical move and kept the debate competitive, but CON had already framed the spiral problem as solvable through unconditional liquidity relief plus technical assistance, leaving the inversion with less purchase by the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 [A2.3]: "When every dollar of suspended debt service must pass through a climate-resilience filter before it reaches a finance ministry, the so-called menu becomes a muzzle." This directly answered PRO's "flexible portfolio" defense, repurposed PRO's own concession that debt service is the largest fiscal line, and produced the cholera/seawall framing that PRO never fully neutralized for the rest of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully dismantling Side B's 'country-led menu' defense, proving that pre-negotiated menus under the threat of default are coercive because the debtor lacks a right of refusal. Side A also decisively wins the flexibility clash by showing that unpredictable crises, such as currency runs, exceed pre-planned resilience categories, making automatic triggers a fiscal shackle.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's response dismantling the 'mutual bargaining' claim by pointing out that a devastated nation facing default has no right of refusal, cleanly proving the menu is still creditor conditionality.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins by successfully exploiting the structural tension between the motion's keyword "automatic" and the unpredictable reality of post-disaster governance. While PRO built a coherent case around breaking the debt-disaster spiral and preventing patronage, CON consistently demonstrated that hard-wiring relief to pre-approved categories strips treasuries of real-time discretion when crises cascade. PRO's defense relied on idealized "broad menus" and "joint governance," which failed to fully neutralize CON's core argument: automation inherently removes democratic bargaining power and fiscal flexibility at the exact moment they are most needed. CON's sharper weighing of adaptability over speed, combined with more memorable framing, made the path to victory clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing exchange, particularly the "cyclone brings cholera, automatic menu delivers a seawall" framing. This sharply crystallized the flexibility deficit of PRO's model and decisively answered PRO's claim that broad resilience categories solve the mismatch problem, exposing how automatic triggers lock in spending categories rather than real-world outcomes.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won on three reinforcing grounds. First, the emergency-liquidity vs. structural-relief distinction, introduced in B2.1 and defended throughout, was the debate's central pivot. CON repeatedly argued that relief must first restore state function—salaries, tax bases—but never squarely explained why that argues against tying structural debt forgiveness (a multi-year instrument) to pre-cleared resilience budgets, as opposed to addressing immediate cash needs through separate emergency facilities. The distinction held. Second, PRO's treadmill argument—that unconditioned structural relief enters general budgets, pays recurrent bills, leaves coastlines exposed, and produces identical default after the next cyclone—was never neutralized. CON's sequencing point (stabilize first, then adapt) implicitly concedes that adaptation comes second; PRO's reply is that it never comes at all under the status quo, which is the more damaging comparison. Third, PRO correctly noted in B2.5 that an automatic, transparent rule constrains creditor leverage rather than expanding it, a point CON called naïve but never actually rebutted. CON's strongest contributions were the state-capacity sequencing argument and the verification-dispute worry ("shifts the bottleneck from the boardroom to the verification office"), but neither was developed into a clean positive case: CON never explained what mechanism under flexible relief would actually prevent repeated defaults. PRO answered A1:/A2: with the required label structure; CON did the same, but CON's speeches were clipped in four of five turns, an execution cost that denied the judge finished arguments. PRO's closing crystallized the live clash without introducing new material; CON's closing was again clipped before completing its counter-syllogism. Overall, PRO made the decisive clash clearer, maintained its core distinction under pressure, and answered the pressure questions more completely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 [B2.1–B2.5] was the most decisive exchange. By sharply separating emergency liquidity from structural debt relief and showing that automaticity binds creditors as well as debtors, PRO reframed CON's two most damaging attacks—delay and paternalism—as features of the status quo rather than consequences of the proposition. CON's subsequent rebuttal challenged the distinction but never explained why a multi-year debt-forgiveness instrument cannot be pre-tied to a country's own published adaptation plan, leaving PRO's framing intact for the rest of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): Side B won by successfully distinguishing structural debt relief from emergency liquidity, which neutralized Side A's concerns about immediate disaster response. B effectively turned A's core argument—that relief must fund recurrent bills like salaries—by exposing it as the exact status quo cycle that leaves countries vulnerable to the next shock. Side A's sequencing arguments were logically strong, but they suffered significant execution costs by exceeding the word limit and being clipped in every substantive speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which neutralized A's delay and emergency arguments while framing A's defense of using relief for recurrent bills as the exact cause of the debt treadmill.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully distinguishing emergency liquidity from structural debt relief, which neutralizes CON’s core sequencing argument about immediate state capacity needs. PRO consistently frames the proposition as a multi-year guardrail that breaks the default-restructure treadmill, while CON’s warnings about verification bottlenecks and democratic discretion, though plausible, never fully overcome PRO’s point that ad hoc bargaining is slower and more politically weaponized. PRO also adhered strictly to word limits and format rules, whereas CON incurred multiple clipping penalties that truncated key extensions and weighed as a clear execution cost. Both sides deployed strong rhetoric, but PRO’s closing crystallized the central tradeoff more decisively, offering a cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over emergency liquidity versus structural debt relief. CON pressed that immediate post-disaster recovery requires fungible funds for salaries and basic services, arguing that automatic conditionality would collapse state capacity. PRO sharply rebutted by separating crisis liquidity from multi-year structural debt forgiveness, arguing that without a resilience guardrail, structural relief simply funds recurrent spending and guarantees the next default. This distinction allowed PRO to absorb CON’s sequencing pressure while maintaining the proposition’s core mechanism.
