### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B built a more concrete, testable case by anchoring both prongs to specific structural mechanisms (Arizona's observed shift to off-payroll work, H-2A's practical processing constraints, seasonal rehiring windows), and then used A's own concessions—"a material decline, not a numerical percentage" and "processing and logistics remain constraints"—to sharpen the clash in its favor. A's comparative framing ("imperfect verification vs. imperfect document inspection") was logically sound but stayed abstract, and its discrimination fix (statistical audits/paired testing) inadvertently conceded B's core point that harm is detected after the fact rather than prevented, which B exploited cleanly in the final rebuttal and closing. B's pressure questions extracted a genuine concession (H-2A's real-world limits), while A's questions were answered adequately but without denting B's structural claims. B's "uphill vs. downhill" framing in the closing crystallized the clash more memorably than A's closing, which mostly restated its comparative logic without new leverage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A4-response), which took A's concession that "processing and logistics remain constraints" on H-2A and reframed it as "the labor shortage, renamed," directly converting A's own answer into a decisive weapon against the shortage prong.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON (A) kept the stronger live route: AZ showed off-payroll absorption not geographic flight, ~half clearing via borrowed IDs shrinks any reduction, invisible prescreening cost makes discrimination large despite universal rule, and seasonal rehiring collapses PRO's turnover buffer. PRO (B) retreated to unfalsifiable "material decline" without measurable drop and to after-the-fact detection for harms that A showed are unobservable to victims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 pivot: closing the state border doesn't close the payroll/off-payroll border, plus mismatch cost attaches to perceived foreignness, making the callback that never comes undetectable — turning PRO's enforcement answer into a concession.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by consistently grounding its arguments in practical realities and empirical observations (Arizona's off-the-books displacement, H-2A logistical constraints, the invisibility of prescreening), which effectively dismantled Side B's more theoretical defenses. Side A's "downhill vs. uphill" framing in the closing perfectly crystallized the structural asymmetry of the policy's impacts, making the comparative choice clear and decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal to Side B's "paired-testing" defense for discrimination, sharply pointing out that statistical audits only measure harm after the fact rather than preventing the invisible injury of a lost callback, effectively neutralizing Side B's safeguard argument.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but PRO built a cleaner path to victory. PRO's turn of CON's "interstate relocation" concession into an argument for national coverage was rhetorically effective and never fully neutralized — CON's best counter (that simultaneous nationwide tightening removes the adjustment valve states enjoyed) remained a live but underdeveloped structural point rather than a decisive rebuttal. More importantly, under direct pressure questioning CON explicitly conceded it could not supply evidence of large, persistent shortages ("a warning, not clean proof... I will not overclaim it") or of increased discrimination ("I cannot give you a clean causal estimate"). Since the motion requires CON to show harms large enough to defeat the policy, these honest but damaging concessions handed PRO the clearest resolution point in the debate. PRO's closing exploited this directly and memorably by quoting CON's own hedges, while CON's closing ("my refusal to manufacture a causal estimate does not make the risk modest") was a fair but insufficient recovery — it reasserted the theoretical risk without new traction. CON's discrimination mechanism (pre-offer screening avoidance) was a genuinely strong, underexplored point, but PRO's response (reduced incentive to discriminate given ~98% frictionless confirmation, plus existing illegality of pre-screening) was adequate to blunt it. Overall PRO more effectively converted the exchange into a decision rule favoring its side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 turning CON's interstate-relocation concession into "the case for a national mandate," combined with CON's own pressure-question answers admitting no clean causal evidence for shortages or discrimination, which PRO then weaponized in closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO wins the reduction prong: CON's relocation point [A1.5] becomes the case for national coverage, and population fall undercuts pure off-books substitution. On harms, CON is more honest [A4.1, A4.3] but that honesty concedes lack of causal proof of large discrimination or persistent shortages, while PRO offers a plausible mechanism (uniform logged check, 98% auto-confirm, phased new-hire) and state experience of a decade without collapse. CON's split-market insight explains how evasion and shortages can coexist, but does not carry the burden of showing those shortages and discrimination are large under the motion's thresholds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1/2 split-market answer – compliant growers can face concentrated shocks while evasive contractors preserve unauthorized work – that dissolves PRO's contradiction charge [B2.3] and shows time (phasing) is not replacement workers and logs are not visibility into pre-offer rejection.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by effectively leveraging state-level evidence to support the proposition's claims and forcing Side A into damaging concessions regarding the magnitude of labor shortages and discrimination. Side B's pressure questions were sharp, and their closing crystallized the clash perfectly by highlighting Side A's lack of concrete evidence for the predicted harms and their explicit walk-backs in Rebuttal 2.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they directly answered the pressure questions while exposing Side A's failure to answer theirs, and capitalized on Side A's explicit admissions that they could not provide clean proof of persistent shortages or causal estimates for discrimination.
