### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the decisive burden live: after full recovery of network, balancing, backup, and platform costs, PRO never established residual public surplus uniquely created by neighbor contracts rather than by solar already on the wire. CON’s cleaner comparison—trading versus regulated time-varying export/import tariffs that reward the same generation and demand-shifting without a household marketplace—survived pressure and closings. PRO’s street-at-noon waste image and renter-leverage frame were rhetorically strong and forced good exchanges on incentives, but repeatedly double-counted physical local absorption and avoided distant supply that already arise (or can be paid for) without P2P billing. Regulatory consistency cut both ways; CON handled it more tightly by conceding feasible wire charges while denying that bilateral prices isolate system costs or prove added value.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 / closing line that if rooftop already exports and neighbor already consumes at noon, relabeling the payee creates no further saving—and a contract alone does not lift a network constraint—neutralized PRO’s core “created value from waste” path.

Side B’s alternative of regulator-set export payments remained vulnerable to A’s critique of utility monopsony power, and B never fully overcame the asymmetry between cost-based regulation (wires) and value-based local price discovery (energy). A’s “participant versus customer” framing and toll-road analogy gave the clearer path to victory in a close, competitive debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s opening Rebuttal 1 exposed the regulatory contradiction and reframed the debate around whether trading adds value beyond direct tariffs, but A’s second Rebuttal answered decisively with the toll-road distinction—arguing regulators can police cost-based wire fees but cannot centrally discover the minute-by-minute local value of solar, establishing the clearest comparative frame of the debate.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully distinguishing between cost-based wire fees (which regulators handle well) and dynamic value-based energy pricing (which P2P markets discover better than central planners). This effectively neutralizes Side A's main alternative of using regulated time-varying tariffs. Side B also maintains a stronger narrative about capturing wasted surplus and retail markups, whereas Side A relies heavily on the assumption that regulated tariffs can perfectly replicate these incentives without the utility protecting its margins.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's "toll road" analogy in Rebuttal 2, distinguishing between regulating the cost-based wire fee and letting the market discover the dynamic value of the energy, which dismantled Side A's "omniscient regulator" alternative.
