### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (CON); B = Mistral Large 3 (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins by a clear but competitive margin. The decisive advantage lies in mechanism. The motion asks which approach is a stronger anti-loneliness and anti-violence *tool*, and PRO consistently argued the more compelling causal story: loneliness is fundamentally a deprivation of human connection, which literacy skills can navigate around but not replace. The "managing vs. solving" frame—teaching kids to cope with a broken system vs. giving them an alternative—was introduced mid-debate and CON never genuinely answered it. CON's strongest asset was the scale/cost-efficiency argument, well-maintained throughout, but that argument addresses reach rather than effectiveness per recipient, and the motion is about comparative tool strength, not marginal cost. CON also never showed that literacy campaigns actually cure loneliness; it only showed they are cheaper and reach more students. PRO's counter-equity argument in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive single exchange: by pointing out that rural teens without reliable internet, neurodivergent students, and low-income kids sharing devices are equally excluded from digital curricula, PRO effectively neutralized what had been CON's sharpest live point, converting the equity clash into a wash. PRO's Minneapolis/Portland figure was suspiciously specific and discounted, but the underlying pattern it illustrated (cities investing in programs and seeing crime drops) was plausible and CON's response—attributing it to concurrent policing and housing investments—was fair but also applied equally to its own claimed literacy outcomes. CON's closing was cleaner and more focused than PRO's (which was clipped), and CON's disciplined repetition of the cost-effectiveness frame deserves credit. But discipline in arguing an insufficiently responsive thesis doesn't beat a less tidy argument that actually addresses the mechanism the motion cares about.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's counter-equity argument in Rebuttal 2: pointing out that digital-literacy campaigns also fail rural teens without reliable internet, neurodivergent students who can't parse abstract online lessons, and low-income kids sharing a single device—directly mirrored and neutralized CON's strongest equity attack and turned the equity clash into a draw rather than a CON win.
