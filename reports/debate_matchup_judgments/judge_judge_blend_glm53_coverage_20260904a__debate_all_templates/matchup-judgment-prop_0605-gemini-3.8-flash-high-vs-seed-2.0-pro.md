### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by separating legitimate employer obligations from coercive control over residency. Side A identified serious recruitment-cost and welfare risks, but repeatedly assumed that portability must abolish housing, healthcare, and repatriation duties rather than showing why those protections require employer permission to switch jobs. Side B offered plausible alternative administrative mechanisms and made worker exit the clearer priority. Its sweeping international comparisons and questionable Singapore claim reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest rebuttal was that employer accountability does not require deportation leverage: welfare duties can remain while workers gain mobility. Side A effectively challenged payroll deductions as debt bondage, but never established why contractual cost recovery or regulated transfers were impossible without preserving employer control over visas.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on whether coercive employer permission is necessary for cost recovery and welfare, and by answering A’s live practical burdens with concrete administrative alternatives (capped repayments, new-employer payroll remittance, industry levies). A raised real Gulf-specific risks on freeriding, low-wage garnishment, and missing public safety nets, but never justified why deportation leverage itself is required once those alternatives are on the table, and repeatedly retreated to “better enforcement” without neutralizing B’s claim that kafala manufactures the underclass it claims to prevent. B’s pressure answers and closing crystallized a cleaner no-unavoidable-tradeoff path; A’s replies damaged but did not defeat it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s A1/A2 (R2) neutralizing funding and repayment objections with levy + payroll-remittance mechanisms, forcing A into weaker “garnishment = new bondage / funds can’t scale” replies.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept the core trade-off on its terms: deportation power is not needed for cost recovery or welfare. Its capped repayment + new-employer payroll deduction and pooled levy fund directly answered CON's two strongest pressures (funding in downturns, free-rider poaching), while CON never explained why coercive leverage is indispensable. PRO's precariat-already-exists point undercut CON's safety-net defense, and CON's alternative of state-adjudicated mobility left the 40-year enforcement failure unanswered. Cleaner weighing and sharper rebuttals gave A the live path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.3-A2.7 / A4.1-A4.3 – PRO turns CON's safety-net and recruitment economics claims by showing the undocumented precariat is produced by kafala itself and that mundane mechanisms (capped repayment, new-employer withholding, industry levy citing Singapore) solve cost recovery without deportation leverage, which CON then could only re-label as penury without disproving feasibility.
