### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by showing that absorption capacity was partly endogenous to earlier decisions: training and integration could have started sooner, while delay gave Russia time to mobilize, recover, and fortify. Side B persuasively distinguished heavy armor from quickly absorbed systems and showed that earlier Russian mobilization could have changed the offensives, but it never established why aid could not have been accelerated in a sequenced way. A therefore retained the stronger counterfactual that at least some faster 2022 aid would have improved Ukraine’s position today.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer to the absorption challenge was decisive: the absence of ready Western-armored formations in 2022 did not prove earlier preparation was impossible, because approval itself unlocked training. Side B identified genuine integration floors but did not show they outweighed the strategic cost of Russia’s recovery window.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the burden modest – not outright victory, just stronger today – and anchored it to a visible ledger: decision delay = training delay, HIMARS/M777s proved fast absorption once approved, and the Surovikin line/mobilized army are the concrete products of the winter Russia was given. CON's best hits were real – distinction between HIMARS and heavy armor, and concrete penalties of early concentration and earlier backfill – but they remained conditional maybes against a demonstrated fortification cost, leaving B's window-of-opportunity mechanism as the cleaner live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 – naming aid-destruction at unhardened depots and early mobilization backfilling the overextended line as penalties beyond the losses that arrived anyway – the most direct answer to B's "what did hesitation buy?" pressure, matched against B Rebuttal 1's decision-delay-equals-training-delay counter.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A built a much more persuasive and rhetorically sharp case. A effectively neutralized B's main arguments by pointing out that absorption training only begins after political decisions are made, meaning earlier decisions would have yielded earlier readiness. A also devastatingly framed the "escalation phobia" argument, noting that the feared Russian escalations happened anyway, meaning the West paid the premium of delay without avoiding the costs. B's arguments about absorption limits and chokepoint strikes were plausible but remained speculative, whereas A successfully grounded the costs of delay in the visible reality of the Surovikin line and compounded casualties.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the absorption argument in Rebuttal 1 ("absorption begins only after decisions are made") and the stockpile/exchange rate argument in Rebuttal 2 (early inefficiency was caused by the lack of the very precision aid that was delayed).
