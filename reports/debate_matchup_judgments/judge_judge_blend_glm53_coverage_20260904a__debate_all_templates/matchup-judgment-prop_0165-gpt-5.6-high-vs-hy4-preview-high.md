### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): A won a close debate by keeping a cleaner comparative path: proprietary lock-in is rational, voluntary convergence yields bilateral islands not universal access, and CON’s own accepted conditions (connectors/cards/status) already concede interoperability logic that still fails if authentication, discovery, and live availability remain fenced. A’s pressure questions forced that tension, and A answered B’s layers/costs and utilization challenges more directly than B answered the incompleteness of point-of-sale-only remedies. B was strong on proportionality, freeze/compliance recursion, and the claim that PRO never proved incremental backend architecture beyond direct access, but the clipped opening and weaker crystallization left A’s roadside-utility frame easier to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Q1/Q2 on residual authentication/availability lock-in after CON’s “targeted” rules, carried through A4.5/A5 that those rules are unfinished interoperability rather than a true alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON successfully showed that targeted access rules—card payment, common connectors, transparent pricing, and truthful status disclosure—solve the stranded-driver problem without imposing the backend architecture mandate PRO defended. CON’s sharper rebuttals exposed that PRO’s "narrow handshake" still bundled discovery, identity, clearing, and roaming into a multi-party regulated stack, and CON’s proportionality principle provided a cleaner reason to prefer conditional public-funding rules over universal compulsion. PRO’s case remained plausible but never proved the incremental benefit of backend uniformity justified its costs and scope.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 answers to PRO’s Pressure Questions: demonstrating that payment-card activation and truthful-status rules solve exclusion without credential sharing or roaming, blunting PRO’s "stranded driver" leverage.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins by narrowing the clash from interoperability vs chaos to point-of-sale access vs backend architecture compulsion. It answers B's pressure directly: card payment authenticates payment not membership and truthful-status disclosure fixes hiding, without roaming/credential sharing, and draws a principled proportionality line based on taxpayer funding. It damages PRO's live route by showing a legally versioned multi-layer handshake (discovery, identity, clearing, roaming, status) carries rulemaking, certification, and recurring security costs that flow to drivers, and that PRO oscillates between all public-facing and only subsidized stations and between keeping connectors and guaranteeing any driver can use any charger. PRO never proves incremental benefit beyond CON's targeted fix.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 – showing payment-card activation solves authentication and status disclosure solves availability without full interoperability, and establishing necessity/proportionality/funding line to separate direct access conditions from sector-wide clearing mandate.
