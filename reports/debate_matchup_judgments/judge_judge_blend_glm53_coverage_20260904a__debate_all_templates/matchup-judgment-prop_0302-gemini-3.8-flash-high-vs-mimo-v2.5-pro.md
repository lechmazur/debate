Its reliance on successful men at the top did not answer broad literacy, discipline, dropout, and college disparities. Three clipped speeches also materially weakened A's execution, while B's inaccurate reference IDs were a smaller cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest response was that elite male success cannot offset widespread failure at the educational floor, and that within-class sex gaps leave gender as a relevant structural multiplier rather than an alternative to poverty. Side A never squarely showed why a persistent cross-class pattern should remain merely marginal.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by establishing a broad, compounding pipeline failure and decisively splitting Side A's class-reduction argument with the within-household divergence: girls outperform boys under identical socioeconomic conditions, so poverty alone cannot explain the gap. While CON landed a credible redundancy objection—arguing that the proposed remedies are good general reforms rather than gender-specific emergency measures—they conceded too much factual ground and could not convincingly sustain the "marginal" label against the scale of dropouts, discipline gaps, and college enrollment inversion. Execution costs from multiple clipped speeches further weakened CON's ability to close the comparative gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal dismantling CON's class argument by showing that within the same underfunded schools and households, girls consistently outperform boys, forcing the recognition that gender operates as an independent multiplier of disadvantage and cannot be dissolved into poverty alone.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B built a more cohesive and persuasive case, effectively neutralizing Side A's "elite men" defense by distinguishing between the educational floor and the economic ceiling. Side B's "sibling" argument powerfully isolated gender as an active catalyst independent of class. While Side A made a smart pivot in the closing—arguing that the solutions for the struggling brother are just good general reforms that don't require a "gender emergency" label—Side A was severely hampered by being clipped in three separate speeches (Opening, Rebuttal 1, and Closing). Side B followed all formatting rules, delivered sharper pressure questions, and maintained better epistemic discipline without relying on fake precision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where it dismantled Side A's "elite STEM" argument by pointing out that education policy must serve the broad public rather than just future corporate executives, effectively killing Side A's attempt to minimize the crisis using top-tier earners.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by showing that class and gender can be simultaneous structural axes: concentration of the worst outcomes among poorer boys does not make a persistent cross-class gender gap marginal. Side A effectively challenged the labor-market causal story and warned against blunt gender policy, but repeatedly treated the motion as requiring gender to be the exclusive or primary cause. It also mischaracterized intervention-triggering benchmarks as necessarily lowering expectations. Side B supplied a clearer gender-informed, not gender-exclusive, policy path, though repeated clipping and some sweeping unsupported generalizations narrowed the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest response was that disadvantaged boys are harmed by both poverty and gender-linked patterns, so class-targeted policy alone does not answer the motion. Its distinction between gender-informed and gender-exclusive reforms directly answered Side A’s challenge that broadly beneficial remedies cannot treat male underperformance structurally.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by demonstrating that the worst educational harms are concentrated among low-income and minority boys, making poverty the root structural driver rather than gender itself. CON also showed that PRO's proposed remedies are largely universal developmental practices that do not require reorienting policy around a distinct male-underperformance axis, and landed a damaging causal rebuttal that male labor-force decline stems from deindustrialization and automation rather than K-12 schooling. PRO never squarely neutralized these challenges, and repeated word-limit violations in three speeches created additional execution costs that left their structural-framing case incomplete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal showing that PRO's proposed structural remedies (expanded recess, adjusted assessments, reduced over-diagnosis) are universal developmental practices benefiting all students, and that male labor-force decline is driven by macroeconomic restructuring rather than schooling, which severed PRO's causal chain and collapsed the case for a gender-specific structural pivot.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO wins by effectively dismantling CON's primary defense. CON relied heavily on the success of affluent boys to argue that the education system isn't structurally hostile to males, framing the crisis as purely a class issue. PRO sharply pointed out that the affluent boy isn't the one suffering the catastrophic outcomes CON dismisses, and that using the top of the distribution to negate a crisis at the bottom is a flawed standard. PRO successfully maintained that a gap persisting across all income levels is inherently structural, and that treating it as merely a class issue renders the gendered dimension of the disadvantage invisible. While CON's policy arguments regarding universal reforms were pragmatic, PRO's theoretical framing of the clash was more cohesive, resilient, and directly responsive to the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3-B4.4) calling out CON's retreat to the affluent boy, pointing out that focusing on the top of the distribution to dismiss the crisis at the bottom is an excuse for inaction and a fundamentally flawed policy standard.
