### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases, but A more consistently forced the comparison the motion demands: not "is the euro flawed" but "does the accumulated damage and lost adjustment tool outweigh the rescue architecture it eventually built." A's pressure questions (Q2 especially) pinned B to a net-accounting burden, and A's answer to B's Q1 offered a concrete mechanism (import-cost disincentives, trade rules) constraining the "inevitable retaliatory spiral" B relied on. B's rebuttals largely reasserted that devaluation "spreads rather than avoids" pain and that euro institutions provide credibility, but never squarely quantified why that credibility outweighs the surrendered tools and accumulated political/economic damage A kept foregrounding, nor fully dismantled A's alternative backstop model beyond asserting it lacks ECB "scale." A's consent/political-corrosion line (creditor-debtor resentment) also went largely unanswered in kind, while B's closing repeated prior framing more than it advanced new comparative weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answers to B's pressure questions (A4.1-A4.3), where A directly rebuts the "inevitable retaliatory spiral" claim with the import-cost disincentive mechanism and proposes a concrete non-euro backstop model, forcing B into a less specific "credibility" defense rather than a fresh counter-mechanism.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): A won by keeping the decisive comparison live and clearer: strength as resilient economies plus durable consent, measured against never adopting the euro rather than against a caricature of currency-war Europe. A’s core path—mismatched one-size money, internal adjustment through unemployment/austerity, crisis-born political corrosion, and institutions that partly repair euro-created vulnerabilities—survived intact. B’s best pressure (devaluation spirals; common LOCR credibility) was real, but A answered both in format and substance without abandoning the counterfactual of floating monies under single-market rules and shared fiscal help. B repeatedly redefined strength as “shock management inside euro institutions” and treated repairs and hierarchy as proof of net strength, without a full netting of surrender costs, prolonged scars, and continuing rigidity; that left A’s weighing easier to vote on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R1/R2 strike that euro rescue/supervision capacities are incomplete balance-sheet wins (built to contain euro-created risks, not unique to monetary union) plus the parallel demand-damage reply after pressure: internal devaluation also hurts partners’ exports, so B needed a true comparative damage account rather than “negotiated inside the club.”
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the decisive net-gain weighing live: rescue/firewall capacity must be weighed against surrendered monetary tools, lasting investment/unemployment losses, and creditor-debtor resentment. CON (A) conceded unemployment also hurts partners but only asserted that inside-institutions pain is more containable, without showing it is less damaging today. B's answers to Q1/Q2 directly challenged the competitive-devaluation inevitability and offered a plausible non-euro backstop, while A never answered why institutional repair outweighs its own costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's double move — that depreciation costs vs internal devaluation costs need true comparison [B2.2] and that rescue institutions are repairs that don't prove net benefit [B2.4] — directly damaged CON's institutional-capacity route and forced the comparison CON never closed.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases: A anchored its story in the concrete 2010-2012 eurozone crisis (internal devaluation, bailouts, sovereignty fights), while B anchored its counter-model in pre-euro currency instability (ERM-style pressures, capital flight, foreign-currency debt) and in the euro's collective backstop capacity (ECB, banking supervision). The debate turned on whether a "coordinated national currency" counterfactual would actually be safer and more bounded than the euro. A's rebuttal 2 conceded that under its alternative, capital flight would simply be met by letting the currency move, calling the cost "a limited sacrifice" — and B's following rebuttal seized directly on this, asking what stops that fall once investors rush for the exit and noting that A had "acknowledged the trade-off while still minimizing its consequences." B repeated and sharpened this into the closing's decisive line: A's whole comparison depends on national adjustment being "bounded," but A never supplied a mechanism guaranteeing that boundary. A's closing did not answer this specific challenge, instead restating the boundedness claim. B also answered its own pressure questions and A's questions with direct A1/A2 responses, offering real falsifiability criteria for what would count as euro failure. On balance, B's rebuttals were sharper, better exploited an unanswered structural gap in A's counterfactual, and closed the debate on the strongest unresolved point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 exploitation of A's concession that currency depreciation would be "a limited sacrifice" ("What limits it when investors rush for the exit?"), reinforced in B's closing by naming "bounded" as the decisive, unsupported word in A's case.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A won by defining EU strength as durable cohesion and consent, showing the euro relocates shocks into internal devaluation, bailouts, and sovereignty fights, and by treating repeated ECB/fiscal rescues as design brittleness rather than net capacity. B pressed the impossible trinity and unbound currency risk effectively, but did not displace A's cleaner comparative claim that national tools keep adjustment more accountable and less systemically existential.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 answers accepting managed-band movement while weighing euro internal devaluation and conditionality as the worse trade-off against B's stability/backstop case.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON (A) won the central comparison. PRO (B) showed euro costs on sovereignty and unemployment but never solved the trilemma CON pressed: managed currencies cannot keep both stable rates for trade and independent easing when capital flees. B conceded to letting rates move and called the cost "limited," and claimed national central banks can stop shocks by borrowing in own currency. A then exploited that concession, showing depreciation overshoots, import-cost and foreign-currency banking stress, and that printing does not equal collective resilience. A's answers to pressure – conceding asymmetric shocks hurt under euro but arguing shared liquidity/supervision counts as strength today – were more direct than B's minimization, leaving A with the cleaner live route: stability + common backstop vs discretion that retransmits cross-border stress.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal attack on B's "let the currency move" answer – bands need defense, falls don't stop where intended, and national money creation doesn't preserve external purchasing power or foreign-currency obligations – which turned B's shock-absorber claim into a shock-transmitter.
