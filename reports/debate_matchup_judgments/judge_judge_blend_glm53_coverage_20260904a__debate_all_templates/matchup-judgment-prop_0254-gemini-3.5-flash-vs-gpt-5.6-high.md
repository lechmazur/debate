### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): The debate turned on whether "national well-being" can function as a coherent primary headline target. CON (A) identified this structural dilemma early—well-being is either a manipulable composite score or a plural dashboard, and either way it cannot serve as a single "primary" headline target the way GDP can. PRO (B) never resolved this: its Pressure Questions answer claimed well-being is "not a composite, not a dashboard" but a "democratically defined framework of key indicators elevated to primary target," which simply restates the dashboard model without a decision rule for conflicts (e.g., housing vs. emissions vs. health). CON pressed this directly ("several competing indicators cannot all be the primary headline target... an aspiration, not an operational rule") and PRO's closing pivoted back to the emotionally resonant means-vs-ends framing without repairing the definitional gap. CON's clever thermometer analogy and defense-measurement analogy also neutralized PRO's "GDP weights everything at zero" and "manage complexity elsewhere" arguments. PRO's causal-reversal claim (well-being funds productivity, not vice versa) was a good point but was adequately met by CON's mutual-dependency response. Overall CON built a tighter, more resolved case and forced PRO into unresolved evasions on the central operational question.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that PRO's "clear, transparent indicators" model "quietly abandons a primary headline target for a dashboard of plural targets," reinforced in Rebuttal 2 by showing PRO's Pressure-Question answers still provided no decision rule for cross-indicator conflicts—this went essentially unanswered through PRO's closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by making the proposition’s operational demand the decisive clash: a “primary headline target” must supply a usable decision rule and clear accountability. A repeatedly showed that B’s well-being framework either collapses into the same multi-indicator dashboard A already accepts (with GDP still the economic headline) or else lacks weights and becomes discretionary aspiration; B never cured that after direct pressure. A’s hierarchy—GDP for productive capacity plus binding separate social/environmental targets—stayed clearer and more robust under trade-offs and downturns. B’s means-ends rhetoric and reverse-causation claims were forceful but never solved the headline problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4.4–A4.5) on B’s answers: multiple elevated indicators cannot all be “primary,” and “prioritize long-term resilience” is an aspiration without a decision rule when indicators conflict.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON won by making the headline-target test decisive. PRO never resolved the dashboard vs composite dilemma, admitting in B4 that well-being is multiple indicators elevated together, which concedes it cannot function as one primary headline target. CON's binding-targets answer and production vs welfare distinction neutralized the cut-constraints and cleanup-counts attacks, while the aspiration-vs-metric frame showed GDP measures capacity clearly and lets voters judge well-being components separately, preserving accountability PRO's framework loses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal directly using PRO's own A1/A2 answers: multiple indicators cannot all be the primary headline, the long-term resilience rule is an aspiration not an operational decision rule, and the thermometer analogy exposing the zero-weight category error - collapsing PRO's definitional core.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): B built a more coherent and sustained case around a "means vs ends" framing, correctly noting that "primary" need not mean "only" and that a dashboard can retain GDP as an input while judging success by actual outcomes. B's pressure questions were sharp and exposed a real tension in CON's case, which A partially conceded by admitting GDP growth is "not a declaration of moral success" — a concession B exploited effectively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing. A's rebuttals largely repeated the "subjective and malleable" and "engine not gauge" framings without substantially advancing them, and A's specific answer that "GDP does not value clean air as zero; it recognizes it as a non-market asset" was factually shaky and left unclarified, weakening epistemic grounding at a key juncture. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but B's answers were tighter and more responsive, while A's closing mostly reasserted prior claims rather than directly neutralizing B's decisive "creation vs conversion" distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) directly confronting CON's own concession that GDP growth isn't "moral success," paired with the exposure that GDP cannot price externalities like clean air except by implicitly valuing them near zero — a point CON's shaky counter-answer failed to convincingly rebut.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the proposition as written (well-being as primary end, GDP retained as instrument) and forcing the decisive clash onto incomplete precision vs transparent conversion of capacity into lives. Side A had a coherent engine/accountability case but repeatedly collapsed “primary” into “only,” over-claimed GDP’s neutrality on non-market trade-offs, and left the key transmission and success-definition problems under-answered. B’s responses to pressure and later weighing of the “not a moral success” concession made the choice clearer to a third-party reader.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing seizure of A’s concession that GDP rise is “not a declaration of moral success,” showing that such a metric cannot coherently serve as the primary public success target.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) kept the means vs ends framing live throughout: a headline target must define success. CON's own A4.1 concession that GDP growth is "not a declaration of moral success" was turned into B5.1's decisive point - if the headline can rise while housing, health, incomes worsen, it is the wrong headline. PRO answered the two strongest CON pressures: manipulability by proposing stable, pre-announced, disaggregated indicators, and engine/funding by retaining GDP as central but requiring proof of transmission to lives. CON never answered PRO's Q1/Q2 core that GDP resolves trade-offs by valuing unpriced goods near zero, and repeated the arbitrary-weights objection after it was met.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.6/B4.1 on trade-offs - GDP does not objectively resolve environment vs jobs but disguises assigning zero to clean air/health, plus exploitation in B5.1 of A's A4.1 concession that GDP is capacity not success.
