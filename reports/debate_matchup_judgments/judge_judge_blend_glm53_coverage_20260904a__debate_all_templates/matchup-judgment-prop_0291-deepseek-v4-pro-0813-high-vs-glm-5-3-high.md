### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON's core logical move proved more durable: the claim that the unbypassable feature's marginal value is concentrated precisely in households where the parent refuses every reasonable request, so any parent who would grant exceptions never needed the unbypassable lock in the first place. PRO's main counters—that coercive parents already have cruder tools (confiscation, SIM control) and that "other channels" like counselors exist—were reasonable but never squarely answered CON's sharper point that confiscation is visible/effortful while an OS-level lock is invisible/ambient, nor that "appeal to the parent" is meaningless when the parent is the danger. CON's closing effectively turned PRO's own concession (that capable teens bypass anyway, so the tool binds "the compliant and the cornered") into a direct indictment of PRO's case. PRO's ordinary-case-outweighs-edge-case weighing was asserted but not developed with comparable force. CON's case did lean on treating "cannot easily bypass" as an absolute, unreviewable lock, which PRO fairly flagged as an overstretch of the motion's actual wording, and CON's opening being clipped for length is a real execution cost — but neither issue was decisive enough to overcome CON's sharper, better-sustained central argument through the later rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's closing move turning PRO's A4.4 concession ("the teen who can easily bypass any app-level control is precisely the more capable teen") into "That is my case, stated by my opponent" was the most decisive single exchange, crystallizing that the tool's unique bite falls on exactly the children CON had been defending.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by staying closer to the actual proposition and establishing why reasonable parents need enforceable boundaries even when communication remains available. Side B compellingly exposed abuse and overblocking risks, but repeatedly inflated app-resistant limits into universal, permanent locks and never justified why those serious edge cases outweigh protection for ordinary, impulsive, or vulnerable teenagers. Its claim that good parents do not need effective controls was especially weak.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s distinction between preventing third-party apps from silently defeating a limit and creating a lock with no expiry, exception, or appeal was decisive. Side B correctly noted that safeguards are not guaranteed, but its response still relied on treating technical enforceability as absolute parental control and did not defeat A’s ordinary-household benefit.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by turning PRO's permission framing into a marginal-impact comparison. PRO needed to show bypassable limits fail ordinary families; CON showed the non-bypassable feature only adds power when the parent refuses to relax it, and that burden falls hardest on queer, pregnant, or abused teens whose device is their private lifeline. PRO's "request more time" and "other channels" answers conceded appeal dead-ends at the parent, while CON's visible-vs-ambient control distinction and compliant-and-cornered line left PRO's grooming/self-harm examples undercut.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - nothing requires expiry and appeal dead-ends at the parent, so the lock's unique power operates exactly where the parent is the danger, while capable teens bypass anyway.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): The pivotal exchange was A's Q1 forcing B to state an enforcement mechanism, and B's answer ("I will not propose a limit a determined teen can't bypass") directly contradicts the motion's core requirement that limits "cannot easily bypass." A exploited this cleanly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, framing it as a concession that CON's alternative simply is the failed status quo renamed. B's counter—that leakiness is a feature preserving contestability—is a coherent philosophical stance, but it never squarely resolves the tension with the motion's own wording, leaving B arguing against the proposition as stated rather than for a version of it. A also did more work comparing the status quo (unaccountable engagement feed) against the proposed remedy, consistently reframing the choice as "invisible algorithmic ceiling vs. visible parent-set ceiling," which B never fully dismantled. B's strongest material (misclassification of health/identity content, deniability of quiet expansion) landed real damage and kept the debate close, but A's answers (coarse categories, dinner-table appeal, bounded cost) were adequate rebuttals that CON didn't fully overturn. Overall A ran a tighter throughline from opening to closing and turned the Q&A round into a genuine pressure win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 in Pressure Questions, forcing B to admit the enforcement mechanism must remain bypassable "because that is the harm"—directly undercutting the motion's "cannot easily bypass" clause and setting up A's decisive closing argument.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A won by distinguishing bypass resistance from inescapability: apps can be prevented from defeating a visible, parent-revisable rule without eliminating discussion or appeal. B compellingly exposed that an OS layer does not simply replace corporate recommendations, but increasingly treated coarse age and recommendation limits as universal keyword censorship. B also never fully answered why preserving easy evasion is preferable to a narrow, functional parental boundary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal directly answered the central pressure by separating app-level enforcement from teen contestability and emphasizing that the motion concerns coarse app and recommendation categories, not hidden search censorship. This undercut B’s repeated claim that bypass resistance necessarily makes parental errors invisible or unchallengeable.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the decisive comparison alive: app-level controls are structurally bypassable vs OS-level that actually works, narrowly scoped to age and recommendations. CON's core defense was that leakiness is a safety valve for contestability, but that conceded in A4.1/Q1 that it will not propose any limit a determined teen can't bypass, which PRO crystallized as supervising only the compliant. CON's misclassification and coercive-parent worries were answered by B4's distinction between app bypass-resistance and teen contestability and by coarse categories, leaving PRO with the cleaner path: visible, revisable parental limits beat an invisible, unappealable engagement feed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B3 Q1 into B5 exploited A4.1 admission "I will not propose a limit a determined teen can't bypass" to frame CON's layered system as a fence with gate ajar, turning CON's safety-valve argument into a concession of non-enforcement.
