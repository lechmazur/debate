### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a coherent, evolving case distinguishing blanket pre-compliance funding bars from enforceable, purpose-specific conditions, and used the Pressure Questions round decisively: PRO's own A1/A2 answers conceded that a monitored remedial grant could precede full compliance and that doctrinal teaching alone (absent discriminatory conduct) would not forfeit funding. These concessions directly undercut the strict "only when standards are met" reading of the proposition PRO was defending. PRO tried to rescue the rule by calling this a "limited exception," but never explained why a grant that funds a teacher's salary at the school isn't simply funding the school before compliance — exactly the tension CON kept pressing. PRO's rebuttal to CON's core science-teacher example also weakened its own position by changing the hypothetical (adding an LGBTQ ban) rather than answering it as posed, a move CON correctly called out. Compounding this, PRO's Rebuttal 1, Rebuttal 2, and Closing were all clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning key parts of PRO's argument (e.g., the promised answer to repeated-violation enforcement) were left incomplete — a real execution cost per the judging framework. CON's closing crystallized the concession cleanly, while PRO's closing largely repeated earlier claims without resolving the "limited exception" problem. Overall, CON provided the clearer, more resolved path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.4–A4.6), which seized on PRO's Pressure-Question concession that a monitored remedial grant could precede full compliance, showing this directly contradicts the "only when" framing PRO was defending — a concession PRO never fully neutralized in its clipped closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing and then crystallizing the decisive clash: the proposition’s “only when” prerequisite is too absolute. Under pressure, PRO explicitly accepted a monitored science grant before curricular parity and doctrinal teaching without discriminatory conduct—concessions CON used to show PRO defending accountability while abandoning the blanket condition. PRO’s repeated clippings and “limited exception / general discretionary funding” pivot weakened its route; CON’s replies stayed tighter on comparison and enforcement without swapping the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A2.1/A4 sequence: first calling out PRO’s shift from pure weak-science to added discrimination, then locking PRO’s Rebuttal-2 concession that a monitored science grant may precede full standards as proof the “only when” rule is overbroad.
