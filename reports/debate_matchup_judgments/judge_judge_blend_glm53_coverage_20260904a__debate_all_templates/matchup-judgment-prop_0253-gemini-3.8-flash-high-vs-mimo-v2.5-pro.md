CON raised serious contextual and safety objections, but PRO successfully showed that recipients retain discretion to weight incoming data, blunting the fungibility objection. CON's "false confidence" safety argument remained speculative, while PRO's "blank slate" problem was concrete. CON also incurred real execution costs with both its opening and closing clipped for exceeding word limits, leaving its final crystallization fragmented. CON's selective curation pressure question exposed the sharpest live tension, yet PRO's response that portability replaces fraud with authenticated records and preserves platform vetting discretion kept its route to victory intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A3.2 (CON's pressure question on selective curation of exported histories) versus B4.2 (PRO's response distinguishing authenticated credentials from a universal deactivation registry). This exchange exposed the sharpest unresolved tension: whether portability creates genuine transparency or a worker-curated mask.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO kept a live route that CON never neutralized: portability as verified facts (jobs, tenure, distribution) that receiving platforms can weight freely, solving the cold-start trap and monopsony lock-in without forcing identical scoring. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by defending the proposition's modest requirement: make verified experience transferable, while allowing receiving platforms to interpret it. Side A's sharp selective-versus-comprehensive dilemma exposed inconsistency in B's fraud response, but relied on a false binary that data must either be trusted automatically or be worthless. B persuasively showed that discretionary credentials can still reduce information loss and switching costs. The two clipped speeches narrowed B's margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer that employer discretion does not make resumes or references worthless most directly damaged Side A's claim that portability provides no mobility unless algorithms are forced to credit imported data.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by exposing a structural dilemma that PRO could not escape: mandating data portability forces a choice between selective worker-controlled transfers (which enable reputation laundering and fraud) and comprehensive mandatory transfers (which create a permanent cross-platform blacklist of disputes and penalties). When PRO conceded that workers could selectively present records like references and that receiving platforms retain full discretion over algorithmic weighting, CON successfully demonstrated that the mandate would either facilitate fraud or fail to deliver actual algorithmic mobility. CON’s distinction between human hiring—where references work—and automated gig dispatch—where algorithms cannot contextualize external data—remained unrefuted. PRO’s intuitive ownership framing and telecom analogies were rhetorically effective but could not survive the technical scrutiny of how gig platforms actually operate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 (A4), which converted PRO’s own concessions from the pressure-question answers—specifically that workers may selectively filter records and that platforms retain full discretion—into proof that the policy simultaneously gutted its fraud prevention and its promised worker mobility.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON wins on the selective-vs-comprehensive dilemma. PRO clarified portability as verified facts for receiving platforms to interpret, which answered incommensurability, and had strong telecom/open-banking on lock-in. But PRO then conceded in R2 that workers could choose what to present like a resume [A4.3]. B's pressure Q2 exposed this, and B's R2 A1/A2 turned A's contradiction charge into a fatal choice: selective transfer = reputation laundering of dispute histories, comprehensive = permanent cross-platform blacklist. PRO kept asserting leverage without showing why automated dispatch wouldn't discount foreign data, so mobility benefit collapses while fraud/surveillance costs remain. Execution clips cost A, and the core path to mandate efficacy was neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1-A2 — reframing PRO's alleged contradiction as a policy dilemma and using PRO's own concession that workers can curate records to show the proposal must either enable laundering or create a blacklist, gutting both safety and mobility claims.
