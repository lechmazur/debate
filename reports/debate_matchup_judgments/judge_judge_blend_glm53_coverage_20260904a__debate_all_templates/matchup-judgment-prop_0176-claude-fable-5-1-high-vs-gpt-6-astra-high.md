### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around the institutional reality of the specific countries named in the motion. While Side A made strong macroeconomic arguments about the need for a fiscal cushion during busts, Side B persuasively used empirical examples (Nigeria, Chad, Angola) to demonstrate that weak institutions render this cushion illusory. Side B effectively leveraged Side A's concession regarding captured institutions to prove that cash distributions are structurally safer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 argument that cash has one leak point (receipts) whereas a fund has three (receipts, investment, and withdrawals), effectively neutralizing Side A's defense that both systems require upstream transparency.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B anchored the debate in the institutional realities of resource-rich developing countries and showed that sovereign wealth funds in that category face an asymmetric downside: total loss through raiding versus cash’s worst case of some waste and inflation. Side B effectively turned Side A’s Chile example into proof that funds work only where strong institutions already exist, and it exploited Side A’s concession that captured institutions cannot reliably police a fund, forcing Side A’s own framework to favor cash in the modal developing-country case. Side A’s theoretical benefits of stabilization and intergenerational saving could not overcome Side B’s sustained argument that the promised cushion typically vanishes before the bust in the very countries the motion targets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 turn on Chile—arguing it had OECD-level institutions that make it the exception proving the rule—and the framework trap leveraging Side A’s admission that institutional capture eliminates any guaranteed stopper, showing that even on PRO’s own comparison the weaker-institution countries named in the motion should prefer cash.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won the contextual burden. For resource-rich developing countries, B anchored the expected outcome to Angola/Nigeria/Chad failures and framed funds as three leak points (receipts, investments, withdrawals) versus cash's one. A conceded capture leaves "no guaranteed stopper" and offered only Chile - which B turned into proof that funds need strong institutions already absent here. A's valid point that dispersed cash doesn't fund clinics in a bust was answered weakly by B, but B's reply that the fund was already gone in Nigeria so it didn't prevent borrowing anyway left A's cushion as hypothetical versus cash's guarantee that the windfall survives to citizens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's leak-point asymmetry and Nigeria example - cash has one leak point after disclosure, a fund has three, and Nigeria borrowed anyway after emptying its Excess Crude Account, so the promised fiscal insurance was absent where it was most needed.
