### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully linking Side A's distinct points into a single tradeoff "dial." By proving that the districts saving the most money are the ones cutting the most time (and thus harming learning), Side B trapped Side A. Side B also effectively called out Side A for stretching a concession about "near zero" learning loss in a minority of districts to cover all adopters, and successfully contrasted measured learning losses against Side A's admittedly unmeasured retention benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which pointed out that Side A was trying to claim the maximal savings of the time-cutting districts while defending the test scores of the time-holding districts, proving that harm and savings are on the same dial.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a close but clear decision by forcing and then exploiting PRO's key concession that losses are "small or near zero" where hours hold, while still conceding residual savings. That undercut the motion's burden that harm exceeds help, not merely that poorly designed adopters can lose learning. PRO's strongest live path—the "same dial" claim that time cuts, savings, and harm travel together, plus political stickiness over learning—was sharp and made the debate competitive, but never fully neutralized CON's middle-ground implementation (modest day extension, hours held, some savings) or the compounding claim that retention is itself a learning variable. CON's later speeches narrowed better to universal help vs. conditional/small harm; PRO closed vividly but still needed a cleaner measured rebuttal on retention and typical-hour practice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / closing use of B4.1: near-zero harm where hours hold plus "saves the least" still means help can arrive while harm nearly vanishes, collapsing PRO's comparative claim.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by decisively linking the magnitude of financial savings to the magnitude of instructional time cut. Side B's "dial" argument in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing proves that districts cannot achieve meaningful savings without incurring the learning harms Side A concedes occur when time is cut. Side A's attempt to separate the two relies on an idealized, time-neutral version of the policy that Side B shows saves almost nothing, thus failing the "help" burden of the motion. Furthermore, Side B successfully contrasts the measured learning losses with Side A's unmeasured, albeit logically sound, retention benefits. Side B's closing crystallizes the clash perfectly, leaving Side A's final "middle stop" defense unconvincing because it ignores that the middle stop yields negligible savings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 introduction of the "dial" metaphor, demonstrating that harm and savings are not independent variables but move together, effectively neutralizing Side A's strategy of counting universal savings against conditional learning harms.

- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by establishing a highly realistic counterfactual (a five-day week with unfilled vacancies and rotating substitutes) and systematically dismantling Side B's central "trap" that preserving instructional hours eliminates financial savings. Furthermore, Side A's point about test scores failing to capture unoffered courses brilliantly neutralized Side B's reliance on "near zero" achievement data, leaving Side B without a strong comparative metric for the harm of a vacancy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they clarified that a consolidated van and a single heated wing for targeted fifth-day tutoring preserves the bulk of operational savings, collapsing Side B's claim that learning mitigation and financial savings are mutually exclusive.
