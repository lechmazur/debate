### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate. A’s distinction between conservation breeding and public display was powerful, but B directly connected public zoos to reliable funding, expertise, genetic management, and public motivation. A’s proposed replacement funding remained largely hypothetical, so it did not establish that digital tools and habitat protection could perform the same conservation role at acceptable risk. B also weighed irreversible extinction against potentially mitigable welfare harms more effectively. A’s repeated clipping and erroneous reference IDs added execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered A’s separation argument: captive assurance populations require an ongoing financial and institutional engine, while A had not shown that virtual centers, philanthropy, and government support could reliably replace zoo-generated resources.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): B won by forcing and holding the decisive distinction between captive breeding science and public display zoos. After conceding acute-extinction assurance populations, B showed A still could not justify tourist confinement, glass-fronted enclosures, or circular gate-revenue logic. A’s ark examples and funding engine stayed live but never cleanly answered why that work requires public zoos rather than dedicated facilities, or how stress-behavior encounters reliably create empathy. B made the proposition easier to decide despite some clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 separation: ark/breeding necessity conceded, public display and circular funding still unjustified.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by isolating the decisive distinction that Side B never bridged: captive conservation science does not require public animal display. A conceded that acute extinction threats need assurance populations, then showed these already operate in dedicated non-public facilities. This collapsed B’s claim that zoos as visitor-facing institutions are ethically necessary. B’s defense rested heavily on a circular funding argument—we must display animals to generate revenue for conservation—which A exposed as a description of a business model rather than an ethical justification. B also failed to rebut A’s concrete description of brief, stressed zoo encounters with anything stronger than the assertion that modern welfare prevents stereotypies. While A incurred real execution costs from repeated word-limit clips and invented reference IDs, the live argumentative route to victory remained clearer after the full exchange because the core separation between breeding and spectacle stayed intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2, which conceded the necessity of captive assurance populations for acute extinction threats but immediately showed they do not require public zoo display, thereby severing conservation science from the spectacle of captivity and collapsing the central justification for Side B’s case.
