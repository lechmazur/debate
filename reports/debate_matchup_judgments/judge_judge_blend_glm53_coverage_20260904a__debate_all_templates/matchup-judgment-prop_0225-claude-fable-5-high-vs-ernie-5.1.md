### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won by making the cap itself the decisive issue and showing that the beneficial part of the proposal, funding bottlenecks, did not require enrollment caps. A also relied on a brittle late metric and a suspiciously precise computer-science figure, then badly mishandled the closing by citing B’s refutation as if it supported A. B’s forecasting, metric, equity, and bottleneck arguments all remained live, while A’s mechanism became less credible over time.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered A’s funding pressure by arguing caps likely reduce rather than create usable funds, separated direct investment from the cap, and exposed A’s twelve-month in-field metric as both late and poorly suited to flexible degrees.

Side A's cross-subsidization argument logically destroyed Side B's claim that the cap frees up funding, and Side B's closing fatally cited Side A's own rebuttal as evidence for its case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 masterfully refutes B's core funding argument by pointing out that cheap humanities programs cross-subsidize expensive fields like nursing, meaning a cap would actually lose money.

A also capitalized on B's concession in Rebuttal 1 that nursing shortages stem from clinical bottlenecks and direct funding solves the problem—making the cap unnecessary.
