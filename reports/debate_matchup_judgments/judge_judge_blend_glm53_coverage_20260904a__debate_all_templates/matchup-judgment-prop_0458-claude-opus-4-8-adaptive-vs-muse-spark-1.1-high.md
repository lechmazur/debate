- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by effectively neutralizing Side B's key global emissions arguments and exploiting a tension in B's demand logic. A showed that the liquefaction penalty applies equally to competing LNG suppliers and convincingly argued that short-term scarcity leads to coal use rather than clean energy transitions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal decisively answered B's pressure question by pointing out that Russian pipeline gas was not the alternative in 2022 because Russia cut the supply, making coal restarts the actual baseline.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by exposing a fatal tension in PRO's case: PRO could not consistently maintain both that U.S. LNG is "additive, not subtractive" (and thus doesn't displace coal) and that restricting it would reduce global fossil demand by making buyers build solar instead. CON showed that if gas is additive, restriction merely relocates emissions to dirtier Qatari, Russian, or coal suppliers; if it is substitutive enough to change behavior, then U.S. LNG undercuts coal at the margin and restriction backfires. PRO's closing concession that gas is "additive this winter" but "elastic at the investment margin" conceded the near-term relocation problem—the very timeframe where methane's 80x potency matters most. CON also neutralized PRO's liquefaction penalty by showing it is a wash across all LNG suppliers, leaving upstream regulation as the decisive variable where the U.S. has an advantage. On domestic decarbonization, CON's simple point that blocking a cargo strings no transmission line stood unrebutted against PRO's more speculative capital-diversion claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of PRO's contradiction between the "additive" and "elastic" framings ([A2.1]-[A2.3] and especially [A4.5]-[A4.6]), which forced PRO to concede in the closing that restriction at best works only at the distant investment margin while relocating emissions in the near term where methane matters most.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by setting a brilliant logical trap regarding the elasticity of gas demand. By forcing Side B to claim gas is "additive" rather than displacing coal, Side A proved that restricting exports merely shifts supply to dirtier competitors. When Side B tried to pivot to elasticity to claim restriction reduces overall demand, Side A highlighted the contradiction and used the 2022 energy crisis to prove that scarcity drives poorer nations to burn coal, not build solar. Side A's rhetorical control and structural clarity were exceptional.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they exposed Side B's contradiction between gas being "additive" in the short term and "elastic" in the long term, and used the 2022 Pakistan and Bangladesh example to destroy the claim that high prices spur renewable adoption instead of coal burning.
