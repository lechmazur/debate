### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling the economic mechanics of the mandate in labor-intensive sectors. By forcing Side B to concede that essential workers would largely just work the fifth day at an overtime premium, Side A successfully framed the policy as a 25% overnight cost shock that would lead to rationing and understaffing rather than a true four-day workweek.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's observation that calling a 20% staffing gap "job creation" mistakes a budget line for a labor supply, devastatingly exposing the flaw in Side B's argument that the mandate would magically solve chronic vacancies.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by locking the decisive clash onto labor-intensive sectors and the motion’s “no reduction in base pay” clause. After A’s overtime/hire escape valve, B showed the fifth day either disappears (service cut) or stays (cost shock), so the mandate is redundant where slack already exists and harmful where hours equal output. A’s equity/coordination case remained live but never neutralized the funding and delivery burden B forced into the open.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1–B2.2: turning A’s “just pay overtime or hire” into proof the 25% shock remains, merely rerouted—then crystallizing that the carer still works five days.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing that PRO's policy collapses into a cost shock rather than a schedule reform for the very workers it claims to help. When PRO conceded that employers needing a fifth day could simply pay overtime, CON demonstrated that this either raises wage bills in cash-strapped sectors or leaves workers still on five shifts—just more expensive ones—rather than delivering the promised four-day week. CON's closing crystallization that the motion is "redundant where it works and harmful where it bites" remained unanswered, while CON successfully distinguished historical hours laws from this motion's unprecedented statutory pay-freeze clause. PRO's coordination argument and trial evidence were powerful in the abstract but could not overcome the sectoral asymmetry problem or the funding vacuum in labour-intensive public services.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's takedown of PRO's overtime concession, showing that pricing the fifth day does not deliver a four-day week to carers and nurses but merely imposes a 25% cost shock on sectors that cannot absorb it, fundamentally undermining PRO's solvency.
