### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating 'reproducibility and research access' from 'broad, irrevocable open licensing'. Side A's concrete alternative (institutional deposits with immediately exercisable research permissions) neutralized Side B's strongest offense about disappearing labs, leaving Side B to defend why commercial and redistributive rights must be the default. Side A's point that "an exception protects nobody until the relevant interest is identified" decisively won the clash over the default mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal (A2) provided a highly specific, enforceable mechanism for institutional deposits and expiry dates, completely dismantling Side B's "disappearing lab / contact us" offense.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing that the default matters most for routine research outputs—methods papers, models, and code—where no legitimate restriction exists, and by showing that Side B's assessment-first alternative effectively preserves the status quo of discretionary enclosure. A's framing of "who waits when no exception is proved" crystallized the burden of proof, and B's concession that open licensing holds a friction advantage proved decisive when weighed against B's inability to name ordinary outputs that should be withheld by default. B's sensitive-data examples remained comfortably within A's exception framework and never justified making them the baseline.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (Turn 7), where A directly answered B's pressure questions and turned them into a demonstration that B could neither identify common non-sensitive outputs deserving enclosure nor specify an enforceable post-grant mechanism to release withheld rights, while reframing the central choice as "who bears the burden decides who waits."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully framing the debate around practical institutional incentives and the reality of academic friction. While Side B presented a theoretically sophisticated case for stewardship and assessment-before-permission, Side A effectively demonstrated that Side B's proposed bureaucratic apparatus (enforceable sharing plans, scheduled releases) would functionally default to enclosure. Side A also decisively neutralized Side B's examples of "ordinary outputs" requiring restriction by pointing out they fall under the privacy, safety, and legal exceptions already accepted in the motion. Side A's closing crystallization of "who bears the burden decides who waits" secured the victory by highlighting the practical asymmetry between an irrevocable open license and a promised future release of controlled data.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they answered pressure questions by exposing that Side B's "ordinary outputs" (health data, interview datasets) are already covered by the motion's exceptions, and sharply attacked Side B's "conversion process" as an idealized bureaucratic promise that fails in practice compared to the present right of an open license.
