### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by keeping the central clash on overbreadth, fit, and effectiveness. A built a plausible harm story about parasocial gambling promotion and defended commercial-speech limits well, but its late 25 percent threshold felt arbitrary and let B argue that A had retreated from the motion into an ad-targeting compliance regime. B also effectively pressed that access controls address the actual point of gambling and that A's law leaves unsponsored parasocial normalization largely untouched.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was forcing A to specify a demographic cutoff, then using the 25 percent answer to argue that A either defended an overbroad blanket ban or abandoned prohibition for B's narrower targeting-regulation framework.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by forcing PRO to retreat from the literal motion (a prohibition) into an ad-targeting threshold that matches CON's counterplan, while showing the law leaves its own causal mechanism (parasocial harm) untouched and hands traffic to offshore operators. This clash is sustained cleanly across rebuttals and crystallized in closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1 exposing PRO's redefinition of the motion plus A4.5 weighing the resulting 24% cutoff against the proposition as written.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): CON won by trapping PRO in a fatal dilemma over the motion's text. CON showed that "audiences likely to include minors" on the open internet is either a blanket ban on adult speech or, if softened to a demographic threshold, ceases to be a prohibition and becomes the ad-targeting mandate CON had advocated from the start. CON also landed a devastating turn on mechanism: PRO's core harm was parasocial normalization by trusted creators, but PRO conceded that identical unsponsored content survives the ban untouched, leaving the harm mechanism intact while only disabling the licensed, accountable half of the market. Combined with CON's persistent chokepoint argument—fixing leaky age verification stops all streams, while a speech ban stops only one and offshore actors fill the vacuum—PRO's case unraveled into a regulation-plus-penalty dressed as prohibition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [A4.1]-[A4.7], which crystallized that PRO had conceded both the motion's wording (via the invented 25% threshold that permits promotion to 24%-minor audiences) and its own theory of harm (by admitting organic, unsponsored normalization survives), leaving PRO with a criminalized version of CON's counterplan that fails to address the very developmental vulnerability it opened with.
