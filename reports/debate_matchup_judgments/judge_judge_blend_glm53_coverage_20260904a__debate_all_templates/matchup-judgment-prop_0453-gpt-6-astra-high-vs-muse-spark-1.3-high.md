### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): A (CON) built a more concrete, mechanistically grounded case: in supply-constrained housing markets with low vacancies, rate hikes cannot create the missing bedrooms, so weaker demand simply strips income from the same tenants while devastating rate-sensitive construction financing. This tool-mismatch argument was pressed consistently and sharpened across rounds. The pressure-question exchange proved decisive: A's Q1 forced B to explicitly admit that prioritization means tightening further even as unemployment rises, and B's honest but costly answer ("that is the sacrifice prioritization requires") handed A a direct quote to weaponize in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, framing the tradeoff as "firing the tenant to beg mercy from the landlord" for a payoff B itself limited to "slower rent growth, not empty apartments or immediate rent cuts." B never adequately neutralized this asymmetry; its closing largely restated the distributional-erosion frame without directly answering why a large, certain employment risk is worth an admittedly modest and uncertain rent-growth slowdown in scarce markets. B's demand-channel defense ("landlords cannot indefinitely raise rents faster than budgets") remained comparatively abstract next to A's concrete causal chain (buyer displacement into rentals, stalled permits, bankrupted builders). A's rebuttals were sharper, more responsive, and better exploited B's own concessions, giving it the clearer, more defensible path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.4), which directly quotes B's Pressure-Question answer ("tighten further despite increased recession risk") against B's own admitted limited payoff ("slower rent growth, not empty apartments or immediate rent cuts"), turning B's honesty into the debate's most damaging exhibit.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that interest rates are a mismatched tool for housing inflation. A's mechanistic explanation—that rate hikes disproportionately destroy housing supply while pushing buyers into the rental market—was never effectively neutralized by B, making B's accepted recession risk appear actively harmful to renters.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively exposed the asymmetry in B's tradeoff, showing that the demand effect B relies on is weakest precisely because shelter is inelastic, while the supply damage is brutally rate-sensitive.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B built a much stronger and more economically grounded case. B effectively highlighted the inelastic nature of housing demand and the specific mechanisms by which higher interest rates exacerbate housing affordability (stifling supply, pushing buyers into the rental market). B also successfully trapped A into admitting they would continue tightening despite rising unemployment, which B used to devastating rhetorical effect in the final speeches. A's reliance on the idea that landlords would be constrained by tenant budgets in a low-vacancy market was effectively neutralized by B's points about overcrowding and inelasticity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they capitalized on Side A's admission to "tighten further despite increased recession risk" to frame PRO's policy as "firing the tenant to beg mercy from the landlord." This crystallized the clash perfectly.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built the more disciplined case by consistently separating scarcity-driven housing inflation from speculative-bubble dynamics and insisting that rate hikes don't create supply. The decisive moment came in the Pressure Questions: A's Q1 forced B to admit it would prioritize tightening "even when overall inflation is at target and rents rise because homes are scarce" [B4.1]. CON exploited this concession devastatingly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, showing this reveals housing inflation as an independent veto over broad price stability and employment—exactly the extra recession risk the motion should have to justify, not merely assert. PRO's answer to A's second question (on price rebounds) also conceded the point and retreated to a narrower "buyer avoids peak debt" claim that CON correctly noted doesn't help the distressed renter PRO's opening centered on. PRO's closing reasserted its asymmetry framing ("a paycheck cut ends... housing lockout compounds") without repairing the sectoral-obsession problem CON exposed. PRO's opening and early rebuttals were vivid and emotionally forceful, but the case cracked exactly where pressure was applied, while CON's answers to B's questions were more measured and consistent with its overall framing (targeted safeguards, warranted tightening on broad inflation).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Question ("If overall inflation is at target but rents keep rising because homes are scarce, does your housing-first priority still justify further tightening despite risks to jobs and construction?") and B's affirmative answer, which CON then used in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to reframe PRO's priority as a sectoral veto imposing extra, unjustified recession risk.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by dismantling Side B's core asymmetry (temporary recession vs. permanent housing relief). A's Pressure Questions successfully forced B to concede that prices could rebound and that B would trigger a recession even if overall inflation were at target, which A used to prove that B's policy crushes incomes without permanently fixing the underlying shortage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's capitalization on B's PQ answers, exposing the danger of treating a sectoral shortage with blunt monetary tools when broad inflation is already at target.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core mechanism and asymmetry claims. While Side A offered powerful rhetoric about the permanence of housing lockouts versus the cyclical nature of recessions, Side B successfully proved that interest rate hikes do not solve housing scarcity, can actually stall construction by raising financing costs, and cause evictions through job losses. Side B's pressure questions exposed a critical flaw in Side A's case: A's willingness to trigger a recession to fight scarcity-driven rent growth even when broad inflation is at target. Side B capitalized on this to show that Side A's priority would inflict severe economic pain without delivering the promised housing relief, making Side B's comparative weighing much stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which directly attacked Side A's answers to the pressure questions, exposing that Side A would prioritize a sectoral housing indicator over broad economic stability and highlighting A's concession that prices could rebound, thereby neutralizing Side A's permanent versus temporary asymmetry.
