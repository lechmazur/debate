### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): CON built a cleaner, more durable through-line: the "either the grid charge reflects true network use and the spread vanishes, or the charge stays small and the cost-shift persists" dilemma, first raised in the opening and sharpened via pressure questions and rebuttals. PRO's answer—that fixed connection fees already separate infrastructure funding from energy pricing and that local delivery costs are "negligible"—was asserted but never fully substantiated, and PRO's own admission that bidirectional inverters, dynamic export limits, and smart settlement are needed directly undercut its opening claim that "no new infrastructure is needed," a contradiction CON exploited effectively in both pressure questions and closing. CON also maintained a sharper equity story (capped/controllable feed-in tariffs vs. open-ended private-profit P2P margins) that PRO's "proves too much" rebuttal only partially neutralized. Compounding this, PRO's turns were clipped for exceeding word limits in the opening, both rebuttals, and the closing—repeatedly cutting off arguments right at the weighing/conclusion stage, a real execution cost per the judging criteria. CON, by contrast, stayed within limits throughout and delivered a tight, complete closing that crystallized the central clash. The combination of sharper framing, an unresolved logical dilemma, and PRO's repeated clipping gives CON a clear, though not overwhelming, win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "you cannot fix it without killing it" dilemma (B4.2/B4.5-B4.6), which PRO never cleanly escaped, especially since PRO's fullest attempt to rebut it in the closing was clipped mid-argument.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by establishing a highly effective dilemma regarding grid cost recovery and the economic incentives of P2P trading. Side A repeatedly struggled with the word limit, leaving critical arguments clipped, and was caught in a contradiction regarding whether new infrastructure was needed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing pointed out the contradiction between Side A claiming local grid costs are 'negligible' while simultaneously admitting the need for smart meters and bidirectional inverters.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): CON locked the decisive clash into an inescapable dilemma: the pitched "below retail / above feed-in" spread is created by undercutting volumetric grid recovery, so either cost-shift remains or the incentive vanishes once honest charges apply. PRO correctly noted fixed charges and design fixes are possible and answered pressure questions cleanly, yet never showed residual value after true recovery or neutralized the "you legalize the loophole first" charge; clippings further limited closure. CON progressed the same core comparison from opening through closing with tighter weighing, making the no vote cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal 2 (and earlier B2) trap on the free-ride/spread dilemma: full volumetric recovery erases the trading margin that is the entire PRO pitch, so "pricing reform within P2P" kills the case.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built a coherent causal story and engaged directly with each other's core mechanism, but PRO's framing proved more durable across the exchange. PRO consistently tied cost-reflective wire fees to a surviving "middle price" (cheaper than retail, better than feed-in tariff) and used this to answer both the death-spiral analogy and the equity/renter objection, while directly exploiting CON's shift from "savings vanish" under fair pricing (B2.2) to "marginal but real, ~10c not 3c" (B4.5) as an internal inconsistency in the closing. CON's dilemma (fair fees kill the incentive; unfair fees cause cost-shifting) was a strong structural attack and its rebuttal on renter-matching mechanics (meters, platform access, transaction-cost clustering) landed real pressure, but PRO's simultaneous-seller argument (A4.1) and existing-infrastructure point (A4.3) blunted it, and CON never fully closed the loop on why avoided losses/congestion deferral wouldn't still leave a positive spread. CON's case was also weakened procedurally: both its Rebuttal 1 and Closing were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off its final synthesis exactly where it mattered most, while PRO delivered all speeches cleanly and closed with the sharper, more memorable frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The sharpest exchange was PRO's closing move exposing CON's shift from claiming fair fees make P2P savings "vanish" (B2.2) to conceding a marginal but real 10c seller margin (B4.5) — PRO used this drift to argue CON had effectively conceded the trap it built, and CON's clipped closing left it unable to fully rebuild the "marginal gains don't justify new institutions" line in response.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by successfully navigating Side B's central pricing dilemma. While B effectively proved that cost-reflective fees would shrink the P2P profit margin, A demonstrated that physical efficiencies (avoiding losses and feeder upgrades) guarantee a margin remains. B's concession that a tighter margin survives allowed A to win the comparative. A also decisively won the equity clash by pointing out the physical reality of solar generation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.1) decisively countered B's claim that homeowners would only trade with each other by pointing out that all local solar owners have surplus at the exact same time (1 PM), making non-solar renters the only viable buyers.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A wins by establishing that local efficiency (avoided losses/congestion + deferred upgrades) creates a genuine middle price that survives fair wire fees, directly neutralizing B's fee-dilemma trap while also flipping the equity claim: bans lock renters out of cheap local supply, P2P at least lets them bid. B's dilemma is sharp and forces useful narrowing, but overstates how completely full cost allocation erases the spread and under-responds to simultaneous-surplus matching; clipping on two B turns compounds the cost. Proposition is only "allow," so A's cleaner net benefits path decides it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.1/A4.5 market-matching reply + efficiency-survives-fees crystallization of B's dilemma
