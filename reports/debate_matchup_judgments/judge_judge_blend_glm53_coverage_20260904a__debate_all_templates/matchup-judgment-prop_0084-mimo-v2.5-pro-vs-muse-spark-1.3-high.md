### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won narrowly by establishing that distinct verified identities materially raise the cost of mass fake accounts and repeat entry, even if sophisticated criminals sometimes adapt. Side B made serious privacy, exclusion, and false-confidence arguments, but too often assumed avoidable implementations such as permanent centralized ID storage and public trust badges. It also never fully answered why higher costs would not deter amateurs or reduce professional scale. A's repeated clipping and dismissal of B's behavioral alternatives substantially narrowed the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest response was that professional evasion does not make verification useless: requiring fifty distinct identities still creates meaningful friction. B's reply that criminals would obtain fifty stolen identities exposed a real externality, but did not show that every criminal would absorb that cost rather than reduce activity.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by making the decisive trade cleaner: mandatory legal-identity linkage creates certain, irreversible exposure for vulnerable daters and a false-security badge, while professionals treat stolen IDs as a cost and first-time abusers pass anyway. A had the stronger industrial-scale harm story and landed solid perfectionist/door-lock rebuttals, but never fully neutralized the join-key/outing route, leaned on jurisdiction carve-outs that undercut a universal mandate, and left CON’s status-quo critique imperfectly answered relative to B’s sustained “who pays / certain vs bought” weighing. Clipped A turns also cost some polish.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1 answering A’s breach pressure: photos/messages are rotatable, but an ID check builds the permanent searchable join of legal name, face, sexuality, location, and chats—that is the breaking point, not marginal extra data.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B built a more compelling and resilient case by effectively reframing the privacy and safety risks. B's argument that linking immutable government IDs to highly sensitive dating data creates an irrevocable honeypot risk ("you cannot rotate your passport number") decisively countered A's claim that data breaches are mere implementation issues. B also successfully defended the necessity of anonymity for vulnerable users by highlighting that the platform itself (via breaches or subpoenas) poses a threat, not just other users. While A made a strong case for raising the barrier to entry for scammers, B's rebuttal that professionals will simply harvest more stolen IDs and that verification badges create a false sense of security neutralized A's efficacy claims. B's rhetoric was sharper, more memorable, and better addressed the specific trade-offs in the prompt.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the honeypot and data breach argument, specifically pointing out that while photos and chats can be deleted or changed, a leaked passport number linked to sexuality and location cannot be rotated, making the centralized database an irrevocable risk.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won a competitive debate by showing how persistent, platform-side verification makes bans enforceable and raises the cost of operating many fraudulent accounts. Its distinction between private verification and public identity, plus limited alternative paths, answered much of B's exclusion concern. B's breach argument remained serious, but it assumed extensive identity retention despite A proposing minimization or deletion, and B endorsed several stronger verification and fraud tools that moved toward the proposition. A also answered the pressure questions more directly, while B incurred repeated clipping costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to B's escape-hatch dilemma was decisive: alternative verification can remain demanding through liveness, phone tenure, review, and account limits, creating an asymmetry between an honest user needing one account and fraud operations needing scalable access. B correctly noted that patient single-account criminals remain, but that did not defeat A's narrower claim of substantial risk reduction.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping a cleaner live route to victory: without a persistent identity anchor, moderation and behavioral tools become a revolving door, and the safety stakes of access to bodies, money, and homes outweigh slower signup. A answered B’s strongest structural attack with the asymmetry claim—honest users need one limited account; predators need scale—while separating platform-side checks from public display (“Alex, 29”). B’s inclusion-or-escape-hatch dilemma and transformational breach risk were serious and well framed, but repeated clipping, some overclaim (including invented “PRO’s own sources”), and weaker resolution of the biometrics-only path left those objections incompletely paid off. After full exchange, A made the decisive comparison easier to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2: ID anchors unique personhood beyond mask-matching, and inclusive limited paths create predator/honest-user asymmetry that keeps the escape-hatch dilemma from collapsing the PRO case.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by successfully introducing the "asymmetry" argument to resolve the "escape hatch" dilemma posed by Side B. By explaining that scammers rely on high-volume account creation while honest users only need one, Side A demonstrates how inclusive alternative verification paths can be throttled to stop bad actors without excluding vulnerable populations. Side B's data breach argument regarding the linking of legal identity to dating profiles is strong, but Side A's weighing of immediate physical and financial harms against backend data risks is persuasive. Additionally, Side B suffered significant execution costs with three clipped turns, which disrupted its rhetorical flow and prevented it from fully crystallizing its final points, whereas Side A maintained crisp, well-structured speeches throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it uses the "asymmetry" argument to dismantle Side B's "escape hatch" dilemma, explaining that limiting alternative verification paths stops volume-based scammers without harming vulnerable users who only need a single account.
