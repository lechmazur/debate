### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON built the debate around one decisive, repeatedly pressed question: what legal mechanism reaches a sane, non-neglected child who commits a grave offense once criminal responsibility is removed? PRO's welfare/civil-commitment alternative was shown to trigger on illness or neglect, not wrongdoing, and PRO's Pressure Questions answer (B4.1) simply reasserted that such systems "already possess every necessary legal tool" without ever naming the triggering standard—a gap CON exploited cleanly in both Rebuttal 2 and Closing. CON also landed a strong methodological point that PRO's recidivism evidence conflates selection (serious cases prosecuted) with causation, and reframed the "at least fourteen" bright line as less scientific than an individualized capacity test, which directly undercuts the motion's chosen mechanism. PRO's case (developmental neuroscience, stigma harms, UN consensus) remained plausible but generic, and PRO never closed the mechanism hole CON kept hammering, nor fully grappled with the Bulger/diversion distinction CON drew. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but CON's answers were more substantive and its closing crystallized the unresolved burden more sharply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Turn 8 (A4.1-A4.2), showing that PRO's welfare/civil-commitment alternative never identifies a trigger for a sane, non-neglected culpable child, directly exploiting PRO's evasive Pressure Questions answer.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by showing that youth criminal responsibility need not mean adult punishment and can instead provide a narrow, safeguarded route to compulsory intervention. A repeatedly asserted that welfare systems offer equivalent powers and protections but never adequately established the legal trigger for sane, non-neglected serious offenders. B also exposed selection bias in A's recidivism comparison and showed why individualized capacity testing better fits developmental variation than an unexplained bright line at fourteen.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was its response to A's welfare alternative: if secure civil intervention is available, it may preserve stigma while offering weaker procedural safeguards; if it is unavailable absent illness or neglect, A creates a serious enforcement gap. A answered mainly by repeating that such powers exist, leaving B's central pressure live.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's proposed alternative (welfare systems). B successfully exposed that A's alternative lacks a clear legal trigger for sane, non-neglected dangerous youth, lacks equivalent due process safeguards, and suffers from the same stigma. Side A failed to adapt, merely repeating assertions about due process without answering B's specific comparative attacks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal, which brilliantly neutralized A's recidivism statistics by pointing out the selection bias (that diverted minor cases are compared to prosecuted serious cases), and effectively turned A's welfare alternative into a liability by highlighting its weaker safeguards.
