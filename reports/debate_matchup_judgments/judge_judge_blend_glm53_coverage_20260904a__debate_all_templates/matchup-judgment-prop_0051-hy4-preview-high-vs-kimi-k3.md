### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by forcing the decisive clash onto prevention versus waiting for harm, and by repeatedly damaging B’s alternative. A’s contradiction hit (ghosts who dissolve vs. fragile workshops that paperwork crushes) and pressure questions on post-harm tools needing the same identity/traceability machinery boxed B into a timing-only defense. A then crystallized that if payee KYC/shipping data is usable after injury, identity is not suddenly ruinous one step earlier, and equal-products/equal-rules carried the “even if it slows shopping” burden more cleanly than B’s regressive-access claim. B was strong on tiered supply chains, non-amortizing per-SKU testing, and delisting risk, and correctly narrowed A’s overread of the KYC concession in closing, but never fully escaped the trap that its “proportionate” tools either presuppose PRO machinery or fail against true ghosts. Net path to victory: A made the choice easier—baseline safety before the parcel, not recall after the child.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Pressure Qs plus Rebuttal 2/Closing on B’s ghost vs. reachable-payee tension: if platforms already hold payment/KYC for post-harm penalties, pre-listing identity is not a different instrument—and B’s alternative becomes delayed PRO machinery waiting for a burned child.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO wins the central timing clash. CON's chain (ghost storefront vs real workshop) partially fixes its contradiction, but its own admission in [B4.3] that the platform knows payment/KYC payee for each sale lets A collapse the ghost argument: if traceable after harm, traceable before. B's cost distinction (identity cheap, per-SKU testing expensive) answers Q1 but concedes the transparency pillar of the motion, and its evasion argument boomerangs because a flight to unpoliceable alleys hurts an ex-post recall regime even more. PRO's "equal products, equal rules" and prevention-before-harm framing gives a cleaner weighing that accommodates the conceded slowdown.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure Q1 in [A3.1] and closing use of B's [B4.3] concession — platform knows payee at transaction — to turn CON's ex-post identity/traceability alternative into an argument for ex-ante verification, neutralizing the ghost-seller defense.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by setting a highly effective logical trap in the Pressure Questions round regarding seller traceability. When Side B relied on KYC and payment data to justify ex-post recalls, Side A brilliantly pointed out in the closing that if a seller is traceable enough to be penalized after harm, they are traceable enough to be verified before listing. Side B's attempt to distinguish between identifying a payee and verifying product safety in the closing was too late and logically flawed, as the platform could simply demand the safety documentation from the verified payee ex-ante. Side A also maintained stronger rhetorical framing throughout, effectively neutralizing the affordability argument by asserting that poverty should not exempt products from baseline safety standards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it capitalized on Side B's Pressure Question answer about KYC data to collapse Side B's distinction between ex-ante verification and ex-post enforcement.
