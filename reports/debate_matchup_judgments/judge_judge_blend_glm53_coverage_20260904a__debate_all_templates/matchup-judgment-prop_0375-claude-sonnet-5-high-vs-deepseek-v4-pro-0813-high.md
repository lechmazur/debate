### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a clear, consistent definition of 'advantage' in weight-class sports (frame/reach and watts/kg) and successfully trapping Side B in a catch-22 regarding evidence and monitoring. Side B argued effectively that GLP-1s cause muscle loss and are thus a liability, but Side A neutralized this by pointing out that athletes will still make this trade-off for the structural scale advantage. Finally, Side B's concession that WADA could simply add GLP-1s to a watch-list effectively validated Side A's call for 'stricter scrutiny' over the status quo.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal and closing brilliantly pointed out that Side B's demand for proof of an advantage before implementing monitoring is an impossible catch-22, and that Side B's reliance on 'existing watch-lists' is exactly the stricter scrutiny being debated.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by collapsing PRO's central claim that GLP-1s create unfair competitive advantages. CON showed that the drugs' physiological liabilities—muscle catabolism, glycogen depletion, and nausea—undermine the very capacities needed to win bouts and races. When PRO conceded that "the advantage does not require users to feel stronger," they effectively abandoned the performance-enhancement premise the proposition required. CON also demonstrated that existing TUE and WADA monitoring mechanisms already suffice, making PRO's call for "stricter scrutiny" either redundant or an unjustified surveillance expansion. PRO's structural-access argument had some force but could not survive the liability framing and the lack of a coherent, net competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing locked in the decisive framing that PRO could not simultaneously argue GLP-1s were a liability and an unfair advantage, crystallizing the collapse with the line that PRO "needed unfair advantage" but "delivered a liability."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live route: weight-class sport already regulates scale manipulation (diuretic analogy), GLP-1s plausibly make a lower class / higher w/kg attainable via unequal prescription access, and current TUE rules don't collect off-label use data. CON's core liability argument (muscle loss/nausea) doesn't negate net scale advantage and its own remedy—add to monitoring—concedes PRO's ask. CON also left the access gap exposed under pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A4.1: diuretic as precedent that sport bans non-strength methods to protect the scale, plus falsifiable monitoring threshold that answers B3.1 and turns B4.1's "existing watch-list" into PRO's definition of stricter scrutiny.
