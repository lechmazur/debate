- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a more coherent path against the conjunctive motion by consistently attacking both the "routine within 15 years" claim and the "accelerate now" claim, and by turning PRO's own material against it. CON's closing pounced on this, arguing PRO had effectively admitted durability doesn't exist while describing what sounds like epidemiological biocontainment rather than routine medicine — a sharp reframe that PRO never adequately answered, instead repeating the "circular veto" framing without resolving the concession. CON also used PRO's own cited compassionate-use evidence (a virus evading screening, found only at autopsy) to undercut the claim that "stricter screening" fixes zoonotic risk. PRO's opening was strong and its "waitlist kills with certainty" framing landed well, but PRO's rebuttals leaned on an analogy (human heart transplant iteration) that CON persuasively distinguished from cross-species grafts, and PRO's own admissions in Rebuttal 2 handed CON its best material. PRO's opening was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost. Overall CON answered more of the load-bearing pressure and left a cleaner, more resolved case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.4), which exploited PRO's own admission that durable one-year survival is only an aspirational milestone and reframed PRO's proposed safeguards (isolation, contact tracing, registries) as evidence the practice is biocontainment, not routine medicine — a point PRO's closing never fully neutralized.

- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON wins by decisively dismantling PRO's redefinition of "accelerated regulatory pathways" and exposing the fatal flaw in PRO's risk calculus. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON built a disciplined, consistent case around the gap between "proof-of-concept" survival and the motion's demanding "routine within fifteen years" standard, and correctly identified that biological timelines (chronic rejection, latent infection) cannot be compressed by regulatory acceleration. CON exploited this collapse decisively in closing, showing PRO had "quietly abandoned the motion" by conceding that what's on offer is evidence-gated staged trials, not routine medicine. PRO's opening and moral urgency framing were strong, and the 1960s-transplant analogy was a good rejoinder to the "early deaths" point, but PRO never recovered from the definitional slippage, and its closing simply reasserted urgency without repairing the collapsed distinction between "accelerated" and "staged" pathways. CON's closing was sharper and more precisely targeted at the conjunctive burden of the motion (both "routine in 15 years" and "accelerate now"), making the debate easier to decide in CON's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.5), especially the observation that PRO's own description of "accelerated pathways" as milestone-based, registry-driven staged review is indistinguishable from the non-accelerated staged-trial alternative CON proposed—turned PRO's pressure-question answer into a concession that undercut its own case, and CON crystallized this in closing.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.2 / B5.2-B5.4: the catch-22 is false - evidence is required for routine, not for trials - and acceleration cannot compress chronic rejection / latent infection time, it only widens exposure. Longest heart = months directly undercuts 15-year routine claim.

Side A effectively demonstrates that the staged trials, interim approvals, and priority reviews Side B advocates for are, in fact, the exact mechanisms of modern accelerated regulatory frameworks. Side A's "regulatory catch-22" argument decisively exposes Side B's demand for long-term human data as a paradox that prevents the very clinical infrastructure needed to gather that data.
