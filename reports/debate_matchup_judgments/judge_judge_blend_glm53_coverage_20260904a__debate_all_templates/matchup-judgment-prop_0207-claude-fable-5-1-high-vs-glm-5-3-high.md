- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): A won because it forced the decisive clash onto design: a benefit that pays by fuel burned permanently misses non-burners, while B’s objections (registries, indexation votes, austerity) are implementation failures a government can be made to fix. B conceded inefficiency early and never fully neutralized that; A’s oil-doubles frame and A1/A2 answers turned B’s “built-in indexation” and durability claims into confessions of unvoted SUV insurance plus borrowed health budgets. B’s automatic-payout-in-crisis and silent-failure close were strong, and multiple clips cost B clarity, but they did not reverse A’s cleaner comparative path after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A5 converting B’s “minister borrows” and “built-in indexation” into the case that subsidy “stability” is unvoted SUV protection that doubles while the minibus rider gets nothing and the debt hits the poor twice.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by decisively turning Side B's strongest argument—that fuel subsidies provide "built-in indexation" and stability—into a fatal flaw.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "built-in indexation" argument in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, showing that automatic adjustment merely doubles the subsidy for SUV owners while forcing the state to borrow, effectively flipping Side B's stability claim into a proof of fiscal and regressive danger.
