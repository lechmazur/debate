### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's proposed mechanism and exposing a fatal contradiction. Side A argued that labels are insufficient to stop the harm of a fake once seen, yet conceded that labeling a fake as a dramatization makes it lawful to protect satire. Side B brilliantly exploited this to show that Side A's ban adds nothing but dangerous removal pressure over a mandatory disclosure rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing (B5.2) decisively weaponized A's concession about labels against A's own core harm claim, cleanly collapsing A's case for removal.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive comparison: after both sides agreed detection of synthesis is a shared bottleneck, PRO never showed reliable protection uniquely requiring contraband removal rather than mandatory disclosure plus targeted fraud/intimidation rules. CON repeatedly forced the labeling concession—if labels make hyper-realistic content lawful, disclosure already answers deception; if they do not, PRO’s own safe harbor preserves the harm—and showed removal safe-harbors plus liability-for-delay create irreversible over-suppression risk for genuine leaks. PRO’s “forging the electoral record” frame and bright-line synthetic/unlabeled rule were strong but lost the marginal-benefit clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1–B2.3 and B4.1/B4.4: forcing critics of disputed authentic leaks to relabel them as “dramatization,” then showing both regimes need synthesis findings while the ban mainly adds asymmetric removal pressure under imperfect detectors.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B wins by exposing a critical contradiction in Side A's framework and offering a cleaner comparative mechanism. Side A concedes that labeled hyper-realistic deepfakes are lawful (e.g., for satire), which proves the harm stems from the lack of disclosure rather than the synthetic realism itself. Side B effectively leverages this to argue that a mandatory disclosure regime achieves the exact same mitigation without the severe over-removal incentives created by Side A's "contraband" approach. Furthermore, Side B successfully neutralizes Side A's claim that a ban is inherently faster, pointing out that both regimes rely on the exact same technical detection of synthesis. Side B's warning about platform incentives to defensively over-remove genuine leaks under a safe-harbor ban is highly persuasive, and Side A's attempt to patch this by saying "inconclusive cases stay up" fatally undermines their own promise of stopping the most sophisticated fakes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantles Side A's distinction between a ban and disclosure by pointing out that if a label makes a synthetic video lawful, a disclosure mandate solves the deception without turning the artifact into contraband and incentivizing platforms to suppress genuine, disputed evidence.
