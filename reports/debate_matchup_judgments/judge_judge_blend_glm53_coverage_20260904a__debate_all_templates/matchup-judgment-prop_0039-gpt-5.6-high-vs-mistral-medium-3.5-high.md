### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by demonstrating that a rigid 8:30 a.m. mandate regulates bell time rather than actual sleep time. Side A's argument that an 8:30 start with a long commute can result in less sleep than an 8:20 start with a short commute effectively neutralizes Side B's biological justification for an absolute floor. Furthermore, Side A successfully proves that the displaced logistical costs are real and recurring, directly countering Side B's assertion that they are merely temporary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, using Side B's own biological arithmetic (11 p.m. sleep time + 8 hours = 7 a.m. wake time) to show why a rigid 8:30 bell time cannot guarantee sufficient rest across different commute lengths.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won because the decisive clash was bell-time proxy vs actual sleep and who bears costs. PRO proved later starts can help but never proved 8:30 uniquely necessary for every school. CON showed an 8:30 school with long bus ride can require earlier waking than 8:20 neighborhood school, and that mandated secondary delay often pushes elementary earlier, cuts budgets, or hits working students — recurring student harms, not temporary inconvenience. PRO's R1 was clipped for length and left B's uniqueness and local-weighing questions unanswered, repeating selective gains instead of weighing hard cases required by "even when harder."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 arithmetic that wake-time ≠ start-time — 8:20 walk zone beats 8:30 90-minute ride — plus recurring cost of earlier elementary starts and lost income, collapsing PRO's 8:30 floor and forcing the mandate to outrun its own biology.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively dismantling the universality of Side A's 8:30 bright line. B successfully reframes the logistical hardships not merely as "adult convenience" but as tangible student harms (e.g., elementary students losing sleep due to shared bus fleets, working teens losing income) and highlights that bell time does not equal sleep time when long commutes are involved. Side A relies too heavily on average gains and repeats biological facts without adequately resolving the hard cases explicitly demanded by the prompt's "even when" clause. Side B's logical precision and contextual weighing make for a much stronger and more decisive case against a rigid mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, specifically the point that an 8:30 start with a 90-minute bus route requires an earlier wake-up than an 8:20 start next door, perfectly exposing the flaw in treating a single clock time as a universal biological proxy.
