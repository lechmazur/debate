### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by separating military preparedness from the explicit political commitment required by the proposition. Side A plausibly argued that clarity could prevent a fait accompli, but relied on sweeping credibility claims and never showed why forward deployment, arms sales, and denial capabilities require an automatic promise of war. Side B also weighed the nuclear escalation risk and asymmetric stakes more clearly. Side A's brief closing repeated its deterrence thesis without answering B's decisive distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it turned Side A's proposed mechanism of forward deployment and integrated planning against the proposition by showing that those measures can strengthen deterrence without an unconditional war commitment. Side A never closed that gap.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won by sustaining a decisive asymmetry and tripwire critique across the full exchange: China treats Taiwan as existential while the US does not, so an explicit war commitment creates the nuclear path least compatible with rational deterrence. A’s opening chips-plus-credibility package was coherent but later relied on repeating that clarity simply makes attack “irrational,” without sufficiently answering B’s central separation of capability posture from a political blank check. B’s R2 and closing crystallized that gap cleanly—build denial, arm Taiwan, forward-deploy without swearing automatic war—leaving A’s route to victory depending on the very commitment whose failure risk B showed was more catastrophic. Clear comparative pressure and better late-round adaptation favored B in a competitive debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1–B4.2: recasting A’s “forward deployment” answer as a capability solution that does not require the resolution’s political blank check, collapsing A’s claimed necessity for explicit commitment.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a structural gap in PRO's case: PRO repeatedly justified the resolution through military posture and deterrence benefits that do not actually require the explicit political commitment the resolution demands. CON's distinction between building denial capability and signing a blank check over a nuclear-armed power's existential interest collapsed PRO's mechanism. CON's asymmetry argument—that Beijing views Taiwan as non-negotiable regime survival while Washington does not—also withstood PRO's attempts to elevate credibility to existential status. PRO's failure to answer why the formal commitment itself is necessary beyond capability, combined with a brief closing that never recovered from this distinction, leaves CON with the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's separation of military capability from political commitment in Rebuttal 2 (B4.1/B4.2), which showed that forward deployment and joint planning are separable from the blank-check guarantee PRO was defending, and therefore that PRO's deterrence benefits could be achieved without accepting the resolution.
