### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly within the rules, but B built the more incisive case. B's opening laid out a concrete causal mechanism (advance financing depends on subscriber revenue predictability) and directly attacked the motion's premise by noting streaming already lacks cable's contractual lock-in. Crucially, in Rebuttal 1 B turned A's "cancellation is already trivial" point into a contradiction — if churn is already easy, the reform either changes nothing or, more plausibly, normalizes casual monthly toggling that behavioral economics (friction/defaults) suggests would meaningfully raise churn frequency. A's answer to the corresponding pressure question relied on restating "aggregate-demand tools" and "temporary dips reverse" without engaging why frequency-driven volatility wouldn't compound, leaving B's mechanism largely unrebutted. B also answered A's Q2 with a coherent (if slightly dismissive) defense of per-household pricing, while A's rebuttal to B's Q2 was serviceable but repetitive of earlier framing. A's case was grounded and relatable (work travel, roommates, kids outgrowing content) but leaned on assertion and a weak shopping-cart analogy that B correctly dismantled. B's closing was clipped by the word limit, which is a genuine execution cost, but the substantive clash had already been established and largely favored B by that point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 move turning A's "cancellation is already easy" point into an admission that the reform either does nothing or specifically increases churn frequency — a contradiction A never fully resolved in later speeches.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by distinguishing easier monthly control from banning voluntary annual discounts and by showing that Side B never adequately established its dramatic leap from easier pausing to unfundable prestige content. Side B offered a plausible frequency-shift mechanism, but repeatedly treated subscriber inertia as indispensable without grounding the claimed magnitude. Its unsupported precision and clipped closing also imposed execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange answered the financing objection: rapid cancellation already coexists with long-term production, while pausing may turn permanent churn into temporary absence. Its clarification that voluntary commitments can remain also undercut Side B’s claim that the proposition necessarily abolishes consumer choice.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won the central financing clash. CON conceded 30-second cancellation already exists with shows still funded, then argued easy pause adds fatal volatility via frequency shift. B exposed the tension: if financing survives existing churn, removing punitive friction (lost history, ban threats) converts permanent resentful churn into reversible dips rather than new unmanageable risk. B also preserved voluntary annual discounts while targeting deliberate lossiness, making CON's defense of inertia as structurally necessary look like the cable lock-in the proposition rejects. A's closing clip added execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.1 collapse that streaming already tolerates 30-second cancels yet funds prestige shows, so pause is not novel fatal volatility but a shift from captivity to merit-based willingness-to-pay.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, competing causal stories — PRO's "friction as a hidden tax that betrays streaming's founding promise" versus CON's "financing collapse and free-rider cost-shifting in a multi-platform bidding war." The pressure round was sharp and honestly answered on both sides (A1/A2 format respected). CON's answer to Q1 in [B4.1] actually named concrete practices (intact bundles, limited/priced pauses, modest re-subscribe steps), which made PRO's closing claim that "CON never answered" ([A5.1]-[A5.2]) a mischaracterization that weakened PRO's final crystallization. PRO's Netflix precedent was strong but CON's "early-dominance, unmatched scale" rebuttal was never fully dismantled beyond assertion. Decisively, PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing were both clipped for exceeding word limits, costing it fully developed final arguments and a clean crystallization — a real execution cost per the judging frame, especially in the closing where the case needed to land cleanest. CON, despite a slightly repetitive macro-argument, delivered complete, well-targeted speeches throughout and directly answered pressure with specifics rather than evasion. This combination of a live, underrebutted CON mechanism and PRO's execution failures in the two most consequential late speeches gives CON a clear, if not dominant, edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [B4.1] answer to Q1, naming specific practices (intact bundles, limited/priced pauses, modest re-subscribe steps) the proposition would eliminate, directly undercut PRO's later closing claim that CON never identified a concrete threatened practice, exposing a mischaracterization in PRO's final speech.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): B won by engaging the proposition’s explicit pause, split, and monthly-reconfiguration language, while A increasingly narrowed its case to easy cancellation and user-friendly design. B gave a coherent cost-shifting mechanism and directly identified the bundle structures it sought to preserve. A’s Netflix example supported easy cancellation but did less to establish broad modularity across competing services, and its two clipped turns added execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s answer to A’s straw-man charge was decisive: it distinguished ordinary monthly cancellation from freely pausing, splitting, and rebuilding bundle components, then linked the latter to churn, weaker revenue stability, and costs shifted onto continuing subscribers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won the financing clash. PRO's strongest route was Netflix proving easy-cancel sustains content, but CON rebutted that as early-mover scale plus later moves to ad tiers/password limits show friction matters when bidding wars intensify. PRO's answers in Rebuttal 2 admitted costs are spread via pricing/ads and leaned on content quality, without refuting cost-shifting to loyal payers. PRO also paid an execution cost with two turns clipped for length, leaving crystallization incomplete, while CON kept a clear line: monthly cancel exists, mandated monthly pause/split/rebuild as baseline is different and shifts costs via churn to tight-budget households.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn [A2.1]/[A4.2] turning PRO's Netflix example against itself — arguing early dominance enabled easy-cancel and later ad tiers/password enforcement proved frictionless churn erodes margins in today's multi-platform bidding war.
