### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but CON built the more damaging comparative case. CON grounded its cost argument in concrete, plausible U.S. realities (California HSR overruns, Northeast Corridor speed limits) and generalized them credibly to any U.S. corridor, while PRO's rebuttal leaned on foreign precedents (Paris-Lyon, Tokyo-Osaka) that CON never had to fully engage because PRO's own chosen U.S. example backfired. When pressed to name a feasible corridor, PRO offered Dallas-Houston, and CON turned this directly against PRO by citing the real-world stall of that exact project (Texas Central, a decade with no track and land-suit gridlock), which PRO's closing never addressed. CON also successfully contested the motion's time prong: its detailed door-to-door comparison showed rail plausibly slower than electrified short-haul flight once downtown access and traffic are honestly counted, and PRO's closing rebuttal ("double-counting") was asserted rather than demonstrated. PRO's best point — that CON's "hybrid-electric" retreat concedes it isn't true electrification — was a fair hit, but CON had already preempted it in Rebuttal 2 by arguing it didn't need a fully electric aircraft to win the cost-effectiveness and scalability comparison. CON's rebuttal 1 was clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost, but the substance lost there was largely restated in later turns, so it did not offset CON's stronger overall comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's use of PRO's own Dallas-Houston answer against it — citing the real-world decade-long stall of Texas Central to show land-acquisition costs aren't unique to California — was the most decisive exchange, since PRO never recovered from having its own example turned into evidence for the opposing side.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing that Side A compared real rail costs with a fleet-scale electric aviation option that does not yet exist, after which A retreated to hybrid retrofits that still burn fuel. B also had the cleaner time comparison: A assigned unusually heavy access penalties to downtown rail while minimizing airport transfers. A made a strong infrastructure-reuse and construction-cost case, but its claims that retrofits could scale immediately and decarbonize many routes were insufficiently supported, and its clipped first rebuttal imposed a small execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s challenge on deployability was decisive. A conceded that no certified 76-seat battery airliner operates and substituted hybrid retrofits; B then showed that this weakens both the emissions claim and the promised immediate nationwide scale.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing a decisive asymmetry: it forced Side B to admit that no certified 76-seat electric short-haul aircraft exists and to retreat to hybrid-electric retrofits that still burn fuel. This blunted CON's emissions and cost-effectiveness claims, while PRO's proven-corridor examples and structural energy-efficiency advantage remained live. CON's U.S.-specific construction-cost argument was formidable, but its own alternative was equally speculative and only partial, and its clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost that PRO exploited. After the full exchange, PRO provided the cleaner comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Pressure Questions (Turn 5), which asked Side B to name its assumed certified electric aircraft and exact door-to-door times. Side B's admission that no such certified aircraft exists and its subsequent retreat to hybrid-electric retrofits conceded the high ground on full electrification and materially weakened its live route on emissions and cost-effectiveness.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and followed the pressure-question format correctly, but PRO's case rested on a mechanism CON never truly neutralized: the ~50x energy-density gap between batteries and jet fuel, which PRO used consistently to explain why rail is a mature, already-working technology while electric short-haul aviation is not merely "unbuilt" but physically unproven at relevant scale. CON's strongest structural move — that comparing a working (elsewhere) rail technology to a nonexistent aircraft is unfair because neither exists on the specific US corridors discussed — was a good reframe, but PRO's counter (mature tech deployed abroad vs. a technology facing an unsolved physics constraint) was sharper and went unanswered in kind. PRO also landed a clean, undamaged hit on CON's flexibility argument ("flexibility to redeploy a vehicle that cannot make the trip is meaningless"), which CON's closing never directly rebutted. CON's repeated demand for a dollars-per-ton figure exposed a real gap in PRO's precision, but PRO's response (that CON's own path yields an undefined/infinite dollars-per-ton today) turned that pressure back effectively. On balance, PRO built the clearer, more resilient throughline connecting physics, maturity, and cost, while CON's case leaned more on procedural risk-accounting than on rebutting the core mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 pressure question ("name a certified electric aircraft in service today") combined with the A4.5 rebuttal calling CON's flexibility "flexibility to deploy nothing" was the most damaging exchange — it forced CON to concede zero current electric-aviation capability and shifted the framing to "adoption paths," a defense that never fully recovered the initiative.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a more concrete path to actual emissions and travel-time reductions. Side A persuasively raised rail construction cost, delay, and demand risk, but never showed that near-term electric or hybrid aircraft could replace meaningful short-haul capacity, nor how electrification would improve aviation travel time. Its phased chargers and aircraft remained more an adoption strategy than demonstrated abatement. Side B sometimes relied on overly precise, unsupported projections, but its maturity, capacity, energy-efficiency, and door-to-door arguments made the motion easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was that flexibility has little value when no certified electric aircraft can perform the relevant missions at useful capacity. Side A fairly answered that both options require development, but did not neutralize the distinction between constructing proven rail technology and depending on aircraft capability that does not yet exist.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A wins because it established a concrete, proven mechanism for emissions reduction and time savings while exposing that Side B's alternative lacks any currently certifiable aircraft in the relevant category. A effectively turned B's flexibility argument by showing that "flexibility" to redeploy aircraft that cannot make the trip is simply flexibility to deploy nothing, making B's cost-effectiveness metric undefined. A answered B's pressure with specific corridors (Texas Central, Northeast Corridor) and reasonable door-to-door logic, whereas B had to concede zero electric planes exist at scale and never produced a comparable cost-effectiveness calculation for aviation. B's risk-discipline framing was competent but could not overcome the reality that one path delivers abatement with existing physics while the other requires decades of unproven incremental evolution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure-round exchange and subsequent turn: after B admitted no certified 50-seat electric aircraft flies 200 miles today, A crystallized that CON's "phased" and "flexible" aviation plan amounts to zero current abatement, so its dollars-per-ton is effectively infinite compared to a working rail corridor.
