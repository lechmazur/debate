### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A built a single, disciplined thread — that absorbing displaced households into existing inland cities costs only marginal infrastructure while a purpose-built refuge city must front-load a full urban backbone before anyone arrives — and hammered it consistently across every round. Crucially, A turned B's own concessions against it: when B argued ports could keep running with "workers commuting from inland" and that states can only "attract, not command" settlement, A converted this into a decisive fork (either jobs stay coastal, making the new city a dormitory, or jobs move, in which case existing functioning metros already absorb them, making the new city redundant). B never squarely dismantled this fork; its closing asserted a "third path" without actually answering why clustering beats marginal absorption in cities with existing employers and housing stock. B's case also suffered a real execution cost: its opening, first rebuttal, and closing were all clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off developing arguments (including the closing's attempt to rebut the fork) before they could land. A's speeches were tightly argued, stayed within format, and consistently narrowed the debate to the one comparison that mattered, which is exactly what the judging criteria reward.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's [A2.3]/[A4.2]-[A4.4] exchange, which seized on B's concession that port workers could "commute from inland" to construct the dormitory-vs-redundant-city fork, was the decisive clash; B's closing never resolved it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by landing the cleaner comparative case: full-price greenfield cities are costlier and unnecessary once people and firms can be absorbed or incentivized into existing inland metros at marginal cost. PRO never resolved the demand/economy fork or the absorption-cost comparison despite repeated pressure; clipping and assertion of guaranteed demand further weakened B's route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 fork (jobs immovable → dormitory; jobs movable → existing cities suffice) plus restated marginal-vs-full cost that PRO never costed.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A built a decisively stronger comparative case by introducing the alternative of expanding existing inland cities at marginal cost, which effectively trapped Side B. Side A's "fork" argument regarding job relocation and the "slow leak" absorption model neutralized Side B's claims of guaranteed demand and economies of scale. Side B struggled to justify the massive upfront cost and political friction of a greenfield megaproject when targeted buyouts and existing metro expansion offered a more realistic, fiscally sound path to the same resilience goals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which deployed the "fork" (if coastal jobs move, they go to existing inland cities; if they stay, the refuge city is a dormitory) and dismantled the "guaranteed demand" argument by contrasting sudden disaster spikes with the slow, manageable leak of climate migration that existing metros already absorb.
