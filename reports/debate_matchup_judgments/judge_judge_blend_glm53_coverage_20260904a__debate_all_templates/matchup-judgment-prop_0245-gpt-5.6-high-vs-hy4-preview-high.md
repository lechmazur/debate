### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive tradeoff live: PRO’s counterfactual needed comparable learning, broad adoption, and similar capability by today without the public exposure that generates bad first impressions, and CON repeatedly showed those claims cannot all hold. PRO’s baseline/sequencing theory (restraint vs cleanup) was coherent and memorable, and A answered pressure in format, but never accepted a real cost side and therefore kept describing an ideal product rather than proving higher trust under realistic constraints. CON’s competitive-gates and “defaults are hypotheses” attacks further damaged PRO’s floor-setting route. Opening clip cost B slightly; overall clash still favored B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3/B5.1–3: staging either expands into real public deployment (scandals return) or stays limited (less learning/usefulness/familiarity by today)—PRO cannot claim both comparable testing and a protected first impression.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing a structural contradiction in PRO's counterfactual: PRO claimed broad adoption, comparable cumulative testing, and similar capability while insisting the public would not have been the test set. CON showed that sufficiently broad staged deployment is functionally mass deployment, while genuinely contained deployment would mean less real-world learning and less demonstrated trustworthiness by today. CON also successfully rebutted PRO's competitive dynamics argument by demonstrating that platform gatekeeping is independent of a firm's own release speed, and that diversion to less moderated alternatives would likely worsen the category's reputation. PRO's path-dependence narrative remained intuitively plausible, but CON demonstrated it lacked a coherent mechanism to deliver its benefits without incurring the exposure costs that generate both failures and the evidence needed to correct them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4.1-A4.3), which collapsed PRO's staged-deployment theory by showing it required every upside of mass deployment without the tradeoffs, crystallized in the line: "Either deployment remains genuinely limited, sacrificing learning, usefulness, and familiar reliance by today, or it expands enough that failures and headlines still occur."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively exposing the central contradiction in Side A's counterfactual. Side A argued that companies could have used "staged pilots" to achieve "broad adoption" without making the public a "test set," but Side B effectively demonstrated that any rollout broad enough to generate real-world trust and utility would inevitably be subject to the same public failures, adversarial attacks, and scandals. Side B also successfully neutralized Side A's claim about platform gates by noting the web-based, API, and open-source nature of AI, which bypasses traditional app store control. Side A had excellent rhetoric and a compelling initial framework ("boring is trustworthy"), but Side B's analytical rigor in the later rebuttals dismantled the core mechanism of the PRO case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it highlights that Side A's counterfactual demands "broad adoption" and "comparable testing" while simultaneously avoiding public exposure, proving that the proposed "staged deployment" is either functionally identical to public deployment (and thus prone to the same scandals) or too restricted to build genuine trust.
