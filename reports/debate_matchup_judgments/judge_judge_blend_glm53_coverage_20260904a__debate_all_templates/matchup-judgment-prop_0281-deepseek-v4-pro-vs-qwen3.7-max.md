### Judge Notes

Side A directly answered the most important objections by separating interface ease from contract terms, authentication, and optional retention offers. That left A with the more convincing route that regulators can require symmetrical cancellation without banning subscriptions or useful support.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answers in Rebuttal 2 were decisive: annual contracts can still display fees and require acknowledgment, and security can use the same authentication used at sign-up.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's A1 and A2 in Rebuttal 2 cleanly dispatched Side A's pressure questions by distinguishing interface design from contract law (ETFs) and separating secure authentication from predatory friction.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): PRO established a coherent, morally compelling framework around "symmetry" that survived CON's strongest pressure. CON's case was hampered by a clipped opening, brittle minimizing claims about dark patterns, and a closing that mischaracterized PRO's security answer. While CON's perverse-consequences argument about human support channels remained live, PRO decisively reframed CON's "customer service" and "retention revenue" defenses as corporate hostage-taking, shifting the burden onto CON to justify why extraction-by-friction should be preserved. PRO's clean answers on annual contracts and security further cemented that the mandate targets predatory interface design, not legitimate verification or flexible deals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's reframing of CON's gym and phone-retention examples from "flexible customer service" into "psychological manipulation and corporate hostage-taking," which undercut CON's consumer-welfare appeal and exposed that CON was defending extraction-by-exhaustion.
