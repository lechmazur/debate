### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by showing that standardization changes optional sharing into a common hiring signal: workers who decline to port can be penalized through adverse inference. A established real benefits for workers with strong histories, but its consent safeguard did not resolve this externality, and its proposed non-discrimination protection was outside the motion and difficult to enforce. B also persuasively reframed the choice as local versus portable records, not merely platform versus worker control. The clipped closing slightly reduced B's margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was the answer to A's claimed dilemma: imported records can be powerful enough to determine ranking and hiring while remaining too weak to improve platform pay. Combined with the point that a selectively blank module becomes informative, this undercut both A's consent defense and its claim that abstainers face little harm.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the mandate as a "shadow dossier" that harms vulnerable workers, and systematically dismantling Side A's defenses against it. When A proposed module-level consent to hide bad marks, B sharply pointed out that selective silence on a standardized schema is a legible red flag. B also cleanly defeated A's claim that the "file already exists," proving that a localized file at one company is fundamentally less harmful than a universal, portable labor file.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 dismantling A's "module-level consent" by pointing out that a blank on the dispute module next to a full ratings module makes selective silence explicitly legible.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won a close, high-quality debate by clarifying the decisive comparative: a mandated universal schema turns local records into portable currency that markets will treat as an expected disclosure, so silence becomes legible harm and leverage accrues unevenly to clean-record workers. PRO’s ownership, independent-contractor fairness, and exit-power frames were strong and progressive, especially against rideshare-only multi-homing, but never fully escaped the simultaneous gatekeeping-harm / thin-wage-gain problem without leaning on non-discrimination tools outside the motion. CON’s local-vs-portable reframing and “currency minted from the weak” weighing made the live clash easier to decide, outweighing a clipped closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2: module-level consent makes selective blanks damning in a way universal blanks never were [A4.6], while imports can police ranking without moving pay [A4.2]—breaking PRO’s forced “pick one” dilemma.
