- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO built and sustained a coherent structural case throughout the debate: the "most wasteful" qualifier limits scope to materials that fail every metric simultaneously (litter, recyclability, local processing), the historical adaptation record was plausible and never effectively rebutted, and the asymmetry framework — permanent/distributed harm from inaction versus temporary/concentrated transition costs — was a live comparative that CON never squarely won. CON's best moments were the genuine lifecycle complexity argument and, especially, the concrete leakage-and-capture rule as a distinct alternative to material bans (the clearest piece of original argumentation in the debate). CON also landed the convergence point: PRO's own refined decision rule looked very close to CON's outcome-based test. However, these gains were partial. PRO had a ready answer — if lifecycle complexity blocks phaseouts it blocks CON's alternatives too, and outcome standards without material bans are less enforceable in practice — and that counter was never squarely answered. CON's A2 response to PRO's pressure question (identifying genuine substitution complications) was adequate but fell short of naming a common substitute that is clearly worse overall, keeping the objection partly theoretical. PRO's A2 answer to CON's cost-ceiling question was somewhat evasive, but CON needed to show costs plausibly exceed the harms of inaction and never attempted that comparison, which was PRO's sharpest redirect. Both closings were clipped or near-complete; CON's closing crystallized the convergence argument cleanly, but PRO's sustained narrative on asymmetric harms remained the most durable line in the debate. On balance, PRO's core route to victory — phase out the clear losers first, adaptation will follow, the comparison favors action — was never neutralized, while CON's strongest tool (performance standards as genuine alternative) was effectively complicated by PRO's enforcement-reliability counter.
