### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by making A price the law’s coordination, workload-compression, and relocation costs. A coherently distinguished a right against expected availability from a total ban, so B overstated the contradiction created by voluntary work and emergency exceptions. Still, A never adequately showed that process redesign and improved recovery would outweigh admitted coordination costs or employment risks, while B maintained the cleaner comparative ledger through the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was on process redesign: it argued that merely forcing work into fixed hours does not reduce workload and that calling coordination costs “internalization” does not remove them.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal expertly dismantled A's attempt to thread the needle on exceptions, noting that 'every exception is a doorway back into the low-level scanning state' because workers still have to monitor channels to determine if a message is an emergency.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B (CON) wins by exposing a fatal internal contradiction in PRO's law: it must both end evening monitoring and preserve valued flexibility/emergencies.
