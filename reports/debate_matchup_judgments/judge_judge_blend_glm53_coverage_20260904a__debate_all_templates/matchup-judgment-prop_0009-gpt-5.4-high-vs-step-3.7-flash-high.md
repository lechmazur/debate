### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a more durable path to victory across the full exchange. Three moves were decisive. First, PRO correctly identified and exploited CON's "manageable tradeoff" concession in B1: once CON admitted that serious psychological injury is real, foreseeable, and accepted for administrative purposes, the intent defense was structurally weakened—knowingly continuing a harmful practice is sufficient to satisfy the torture standard PRO advanced, regardless of the label officials attach. CON never fully recovered from this. Second, PRO consistently drew the "separation ≠ solitary" distinction: the motion does not require releasing dangerous prisoners; it only bans prolonged total social deprivation. CON's hypothetical (gang leader on day 16) only proves that continued physical separation may be needed, not that it must include sensory and social starvation. PRO answered this directly in A4—barrier contact with staff, clinicians, screened communications, and no-peer-access can contain the network threat without the added cruelty. CON's coded-messages rejoinder was speculative and did not engage the specific counter (contact with staff and clinicians, not fellow prisoners). Third, PRO's pressure question on the limiting principle was the debate's sharpest moment. CON's response in B4.1—proportionality plus foreseeable outcomes—actually pointed toward PRO's conclusion: if knowingly continuing a practice that foreseeably destroys mental capacity is the test, prolonged solitary beyond an emergency period fails that test by CON's own standard. CON's closing repeated the "unproven alternatives" argument without advancing it, while PRO's closing crystallized the core distinction cleanly. CON's strongest residual argument—that PRO never demonstrated alternatives are operationally sufficient for the highest-risk cases—remained alive but was insufficient to overcome PRO's combined structural advantages. Both sides were clipped in Rebuttal 2, representing equal execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's exploitation of CON's "manageable tradeoff" concession in A2.5 was the most decisive rebuttal exchange. PRO turned CON's own framing—that psychological injury is real, foreseeable, and accepted—into an admission that the state is knowingly choosing to inflict that injury for administrative convenience. CON's attempt to reframe it as "proportional security policy" in B2.4 did not adequately address the moral weight of PRO's point: the duty to protect people in custody includes the person being isolated, not just everyone else.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): PRO decisively won by conceptually separating physical containment (security) from total social and sensory deprivation (torture). Furthermore, CON failed to explain how PRO's proposed alternative of zero peer contact and barrier-only interactions with clinicians would still allow gang leaders to transmit codes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer (A2), which definitively neutralized the necessity argument by distinguishing "separation from the network" from "sensory and social starvation."
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully carving out a decisive distinction between physical security separation and total social deprivation. While Side B effectively pressed the necessity of containing high-risk threats, Side A consistently demonstrated that secure housing, barrier interaction, and screened communication can neutralize danger without imposing the severe psychological damage that defines torture. Side A's Rebuttal 2 directly answered Side B's pressure questions, dismantled the false binary, and maintained a clear limiting principle, whereas Side B's intent-based defense grew muddled and relied on speculative counter-examples to dismiss alternatives.
