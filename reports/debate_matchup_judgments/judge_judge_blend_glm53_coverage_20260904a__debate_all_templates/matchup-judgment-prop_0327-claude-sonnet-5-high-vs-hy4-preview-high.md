### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's comparative test and proposed remedy. Side A successfully distinguished the risk profile of adult content (criminal tail risk) from Side B's examples of gambling and violence (fines), proving the difference in treatment is not purely reputational. Furthermore, Side A decisively leveraged the supply-chain reality: if upstream banks refuse to fund adult content, a platform is acting out of operational necessity to retain its funding, not solely out of brand anxiety.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1 and A4.3) was the decisive exchange, proving that adult content carries unique catastrophic tail risks and that binding platforms without binding upstream banks creates an impossible operational bind.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won a close debate by turning the live clash onto whether PRO’s “solely reputational” rule is workable. After tools for fraud and legality are priced, PRO still needs a clean path from principle to access; CON showed the payment chain leaves upstream banks and networks free to walk away, so binding only the platform creates an impossible compliance position rather than access. Differentiated failure costs and ordinary risk segmentation also undercut pure-stigma and “economic death” claims. PRO’s heckler’s-veto framing and violence/gambling comparison stayed live and rhetorically sharp, but never neutralized the administrability and counterparty holes that CON crystallized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 A4.3: upstream banks/advertisers retain lawful discretion, so forcing the platform alone does not solve exclusion and leaves it unable to comply with its own funding partners.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's normative core live: after fraud, illegality, age/consent checks, a residual blanket ban vs reserves/age-gates/geo-blocks for gambling/violence is operative reputational exclusion. CON's strongest differentiated-risk claim of catastrophic failure [B4.1] collapses into illegality, which the motion already permits, and its impossible-compliance chain [B4.3][B5.2] fails because the rule also binds upstream payment processors. Specialized channels as adequate access [B1.6][B2.4] was turned into evidence of second-class citizenship.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.2 + A5.2 answering B3.1/B4.1: PRO's comparative, conduct-based test for "solely" and reframe that catastrophic verification failure is unlawful content already excludable, while total loss of banking is outsourced moral discomfort - the veto the motion bars.
