Its minimal-vs-genuine competence dilemma and active-recruitment answer neutralized B's equity claim, while B never provided a principled limit for core or a credible cost/cut answer, leaving the bundle unjustified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 category-error move (universal need does not entail universal personal performance, especially renters) plus the infrastructure dilemma – if minimal then no competence, if genuine then costly/unequal – directly undercut B's universal-necessity and equity routes.

While Side A presented a highly intuitive and rhetorically appealing case about equity and the necessity of life skills, Side B effectively distinguished between universal human needs (eating, shelter) and the specific prescribed means (cooking, drywall repair), noting that renters and those without kitchens cannot always utilize these skills. Side B also strategically conceded the value of financial literacy by proposing it be embedded in math and civics, isolating the weaker elements of Side A's bundle.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's "universal daily necessity" limiting principle in Rebuttal 2, sharply distinguishing between universal needs (eating, shelter) and the specific skills mandated (cooking, repairing drywall), which exposed the overbreadth of Side A's bundle.
