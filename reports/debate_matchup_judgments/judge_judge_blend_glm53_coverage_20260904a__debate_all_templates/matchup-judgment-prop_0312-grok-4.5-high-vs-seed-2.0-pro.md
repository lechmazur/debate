### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): A won by maintaining the motion's actual middle ground: a meaningful share of marquee games can remain free while substantial exclusive inventory still supports subscriptions. B repeatedly treated partial access as though every desirable game were being given away, and its claim that ad-supported viewing creates no attachment was implausibly absolute. B also weakened its credibility with the unsupported assertion that every major streaming service had confirmed immediate churn. A had a minor reference-ID error and did not quantify the optimal share, but its access, fan-pipeline, and long-term-value comparison remained more plausible and responsive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was that imperfect free access still beats complete exclusion and that social clips can spark curiosity while full live games build attachment. B's alternatives of bars, borrowed logins, and delayed replays did not answer households lacking money, connections, or broadband, while its response relied chiefly on asserting that viewers cannot tolerate advertisements.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by successfully proving that imperfect free access (with ads) is far superior to total exclusion, exposing Side B's alternatives (going to a bar or sharing logins) as unworkable for the truly disadvantaged. Side A also provides a more plausible causal mechanism for long-term revenue growth, while Side B relies on a discounted, unsupported empirical claim about immediate churn and the unintuitive premise that no one can become a fan if a broadcast has commercials.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R2 response that "Twelve minutes of ads beats zero minutes of the game," which definitively crushed Side B's strange insistence that commercial breaks make free broadcasts useless for low-income fans.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO showed that keeping a meaningful share of marquee games free sustains the public conversation and fan pipeline that make residual subscription packages desirable, while CON never proved that partial free placement collapses the whole funding model. CON’s brittle fake precision about streaming churn [A4.2], failure to answer why residual exclusives cannot coexist, and dismissal of imperfect-but-real access for low-income viewers weakened its economic case. PRO answered pressure questions directly and crystallized the long-term tradeoff more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 answers [B4.1-4]: the explanation that residual exclusives maintain value through prestige generated by free broadcasts, and that twelve minutes of ads beats zero minutes of the game for fixed-income households, directly neutralized CON’s core economic and access arguments.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by directly answering both of A’s pillars: stadium support purchases local benefits rather than broadcast access, and young fans have multiple pathways into fandom besides full free marquee games. A made the cultural-belonging case vividly, but repeatedly escalated it into unsupported absolutes about fandom disappearing and leagues having nothing left to sell. B’s revenue-loss claims were also somewhat overstated, but its comparative case was more consistently developed and responsive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange distinguished local stadium subsidies from a permanent national broadcast entitlement, then pressed A to justify why highlights, peers, schools, and social media could not sustain fan formation. A reframed this as an implicit moral bargain but did not establish the claimed entitlement or answer the alternative pathways with more than assertion.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively neutralizing Side A's stadium subsidy argument, successfully drawing a logical boundary between local infrastructure benefits and national broadcast rights. Side B also effectively countered the cultural belonging argument by showing that premium revenue is what funds the excellence required to make marquee games culturally significant in the first place.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 distinction that local stadium subsidies purchased local amenities (jobs, civic pride) rather than a national free-to-air entitlement, forcing Side A to retreat to an implicit moral claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins because it decisively dismantled PRO's central stadium-subsidy entitlement claim as special pleading and after-the-fact capture, and it provided a more complete causal story showing that mandated free-to-air shares drain the premium revenue needed for product quality. PRO kept a live emotional case about shared cultural belonging, but never adequately answered CON's pressure on the exact mechanism linking local stadium deals to national broadcast rights, nor did it rebut the economic dilution argument. CON's rebuttals were sharper, its pressure answers more direct, and its closing better integrated the comparative weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (Turn 4, A2.1-A2.3) exposing the stadium-subsidy-to-broadcast-rights leap as "special pleading" and "after-the-fact capture," which PRO never recovered from beyond asserting an unverified "implicit moral bargain."
