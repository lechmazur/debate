### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): A won by turning B’s central example: post-pandemic expectations remained anchored amid forceful tightening, so that episode does not establish that extended tolerance is safe. A also exposed B’s reactive standard—waiting for a proven, sustained spiral risks acting only after inflation is embedded. B powerfully emphasized concentrated recession harms, but relied on an unsupported forecast of a guaranteed unemployment increase and never adequately separated supply recovery from the effects of monetary resolve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was that B cited calm under “maximum resolve” as proof resolve was unnecessary, then showed that B’s proposed trigger for tightening would activate only after the cheap preventive option had disappeared.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B won decisively by turning Side A's core empirical example against them and brilliantly exploiting Side A's answers to the pressure questions. Side B successfully argued that the recent lack of a wage spiral was the result of aggressive central bank tightening, not proof that inflation is harmless, and sharply pointed out that Side A's proposed safety standard ("wait for a proven, sustained spiral") guarantees waiting until it is too late.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, which dismantled A's standard of waiting for a "proven, sustained" spiral by noting it structurally guarantees waiting until the spiral is deeply embedded, requiring a Volcker-style deep recession.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B won decisively by dismantling Side A's core premise. Side A's case relied heavily on the claim that returning to the 2% target necessitates an engineered recession with mass unemployment. Side B effectively neutralized this by pointing to the actual post-pandemic soft landing, where inflation fell sharply without mass firings. When Side A attempted to salvage this in Rebuttal 2 by claiming the recession "hasn't happened yet" and citing forecasts of a mere 1-point rise in unemployment, Side B expertly pounced on this concession, contrasting a modest 1-point rise with the severe costs of unanchored expectations. Furthermore, Side B brilliantly flipped Side A's strongest piece of evidence—the failure of a wage spiral to materialize—by arguing that the spiral was deterred precisely because central banks maintained a credible threat of aggressive tightening (the "fire department" analogy). Side B's rebuttals were sharper, more grounded in recent economic realities, and systematically dismantled Side A's trade-offs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response in Rebuttal 2 to Side A's concession that the predicted catastrophe had shrunk to a 1-point rise in unemployment, combined with B's dismantling of A's "the ghost didn't appear" argument by pointing out that the ghost was deterred by the very policy A opposes.
