### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON built the more comprehensive, sustained case: centralization risk, exclusion of the undocumented, mistargeted harms, and a device-vs-user vulnerability that a wallet default cannot solve. Its sharpest pressure question (Q2 on borrowed devices) exposed a real hole in PRO's mechanism that PRO could only answer comparatively ("no system survives this"), and CON's second rebuttal introduced a strong, largely unanswered analogy (defaults atrophying alternatives, e.g., cookie banners) that PRO's closing addressed only in passing. PRO answered capably—its zero-knowledge/selective-disclosure explanation and "fails safely" comparative framing were genuinely strong and well-grounded—but by the end several of CON's threads (fallback atrophy, protection of the specifically at-risk unsupervised minor) remained live and unresolved, while CON's own weak point (naming specific unverified country breaches) was a comparatively minor grounding lapse. On balance CON's case left fewer open questions for the judge to resolve in PRO's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question on the borrowed-phone/device-vs-user problem (A3.2), which forced PRO into a purely comparative "every system fails this way" answer rather than a real solution, and which CON then leveraged in closing as evidence the wallet fails the very at-risk population the motion is meant to protect.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won by maintaining a relentless comparative focus. While Side A successfully pointed out that wallets are vulnerable to circumvention and exclude the undocumented, Side B effectively neutralized these points by showing that platform-specific ID checks suffer from the exact same flaws—but with the catastrophic addition of proliferating raw passport scans across the internet. Side A's attempt to reject the comparison in closing ignored the explicit text of the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the 'borrowed phone' evasion argument in Rebuttal 2, conceding that both systems fail against this tactic but noting that the wallet 'fails safely' by leaving no reusable document residue on the platform.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON successfully reframed the motion around the structural consequences of a default mandate, showing it would become de facto infrastructure that excludes the undocumented, imposes costs on all adults, and—most damagingly—fails the unsupervised at-risk minors who borrow devices. PRO’s comparative privacy case relied on an idealized architecture that CON undermined by pointing to issuer-side correlation and verification logs. PRO’s concession that borrowed phones defeat any age check proved fatal once CON weighed it against the policy’s stated goal of protecting the most vulnerable, and CON’s historical analogy about the atrophy of “optional” EU defaults defanged PRO’s fallback-route promises.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s pressure questions (Turn 6) exposing the gap between binary age brackets and tiered youth protections, and bluntly asking why the default should be a tool that concedes failure against the unsupervised minor borrowing a device—the exact at-risk population the motion prioritizes. PRO’s answers recovered some ground on technical tiering but never escaped the comparative weight of that concession.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON (A) built and sustained the more damaging structural critique: the "enrolment is the outing" point (a wallet credential must be issued somewhere, so identity exposure is relocated to a checkpoint rather than eliminated) and the unlinkability-revocability-offline trilemma (you cannot have all three, so either a stable identifier becomes a cross-site tracking token or revocation fails). PRO (B) landed a strong flip in Rebuttal 1 (platform checks expose identity to every site; wallets restore pseudonymity to the relying party), but its later answers to the trilemma ("cached revocation lists," the IP-logging analogy) were hand-wavy and did not close the technical gap A kept pressing. CON's closing double-bind — the wallet default either is universal (forcing enrolment exposure on the most vulnerable minors) or merely a non-exclusive default (leaving bad-faith sites free to skip it, so the 12-year-old still gets in) — was never squarely resolved by PRO, whose closing repeated "issuer diversity" and "severs the knot" assertions without mechanism detail. Pressure Questions sharpened this: A's Q2 on the trilemma was answered by B with an analogy (IP logging) that understated the tracking concern, while A's answers to B's questions (graduated checks, direct trilemma restatement) were more responsive and precise. Overall CON provided the cleaner, more resolved comparative story by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.4), reframing B's "kill-switch" revocation defense as an inescapable trilemma between unlinkability, revocability, and offline tolerance — a technical point B's closing never actually dismantled, only asserted around.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A wins by systematically dismantling the privacy mechanisms of Side B's wallet architecture. A introduces a highly effective "unlinkability/revocation trilemma" that proves B's proposed technical solutions (short-lived tokens and cached revocation lists) inevitably reintroduce either a centralized activity log or cross-site tracking via stable identifiers. Side B fails to resolve this logical contradiction, merely asserting the mandate can sever the knot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.3) articulating the unlinkability/revocation trilemma decisively dismantled B's proposed technical solution.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON wins by exposing an unresolved technical trilemma in PRO's core architecture: truly privacy-preserving wallets cannot simultaneously be unlinkable, revocable, and offline-tolerant, yet PRO's case requires all three. Combined with the vulnerable-minor enrolment problem—where obtaining a wallet credential itself outs at-risk youth to an issuer—this undermines PRO's central "exposure geometry" claim. CON's alternative of graduated, hardened platform checks preserves pseudonymous access where risks are low while allowing targeted verification where they are high.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's articulation of the unlinkability/revocation/tracking trilemma in second rebuttal (A2.3/A4.3), which PRO never resolved substantively despite two rounds of pressure, instead repeating the same token-refresh and cached-list assertions without showing how they avoid either cross-site tracking or irrevocability.
