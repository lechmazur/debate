### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): A won by keeping the motion’s real burden live: not “should government respond fast,” but “must one person impose sweeping, economy-wide tariff burdens before Congress authorizes them.” A repeatedly separated emergency response from unilateral broad tariffs, showed CON’s safeguards arrive after sunk costs/retaliation, and turned CON’s “consensus is absent” claim into a reason against unilateral first moves. B’s speed/leverage and auto-expire bridge were competitive, but never fully established why that particular sweeping tool is indispensable versus targeted/non-tariff options and prior authorization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and closing on the “consensus is absent” problem: that is exactly when presidential emergency tariffs most risk imposing nationwide costs Congress would reject, while sunsets/reporting cannot recover already-imposed burdens.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by keeping the decisive clash focused on sequencing: even supervised emergency power lets the president impose sweeping costs and trigger retaliation before representative consent, a first-mover advantage that sunsets and reporting cannot undo. Side A (CON) landed a sharp delegation dilemma on pre-written triggers and an effective countdown frame, but never fully overcame the accountability asymmetry—PRO showed that delay harms are borne by specific actors while unilateral tariffs redistribute nationwide burdens without prior authorization. CON also failed to establish concrete, non-speculative scenarios where economy-wide (not merely targeted) tariffs were indispensable before Congress could act.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's argument that ex post safeguards like sunset clauses and congressional review only limit duration after costs have already been imposed and retaliation triggered, which exposed that CON's supervised emergency model still reverses the order of consent and consequence.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO maintained the tighter fit to the proposition: sweeping, economy-wide tariffs distribute costs across unrelated producers, consumers, and exporters exposed to retaliation, so they require prior congressional authorization. CON's emergency narrative never bridged the gap between a targeted shock (blockade, input cutoff, dumping) and the necessity of broad tariffs; PRO's point that broad duties can worsen scarcity and that targeted/non-tariff tools remain available went largely unanswered. PRO's safeguards-after-harm rebuttal — reporting explains harm after it occurs, sunset doesn't reimburse, veto makes repeal harder than prior approval — directly undercut CON's framework-checks defense, while CON's pressure was partly conceded (A4.1 delay costs fall on workers). That leaves PRO with the cleaner weighing: delay risk vs. unilateral imposition of nationwide tax burdens without agreement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 / Rebuttal 2 sequence on safeguards arriving after harm, veto blocking reversal, and administration vs. choice of broad policy (A2.5-A2.6, A4.5-A4.6) which CON answered only with future expiration.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO won a close, high-quality clash by making imposition timing decisive: even temporary sweeping tariffs immediately tax households, break contracts, and trigger retaliation before consent, review, or sunset can matter. CON’s coercion scenario and delay≠rejection distinction were strong and forced real comparison, but never fully neutralized the consent/accountability burden or the pretext problem of who defines the emergency before duties hit. PRO’s clearer rule—prior legislative authority for broad tariffs—survived better after both pressure rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.3–A2.5 / A5.1–A5.3: automatic expiration and findings answer permanence, not first-day irreversible tax harms; if Congress can write precise triggers and expedite, it can authorize before effect.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by successfully reframing the choice not as unchecked presidential power versus congressional supremacy, but as bounded advance legislative consent with automatic expiration versus a categorical ban that guarantees costly legislative delay. CON’s symmetry argument—that irreversible harms occur while foreign coercion continues during mandatory congressional waiting periods—was never squarely answered by PRO, who repeatedly conflated procedural delay with democratic refusal. PRO’s constitutional and accountability arguments were strong, but they did not establish why tightly cabined temporary authority is less safe than compulsory delay when alternative tools are inadequate. CON’s framework preserved congressional ownership of lasting burdens while bridging genuine procedural gaps, offering the cleaner comparative path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing crystallized that irreversibility cuts both ways: while temporary tariffs impose immediate costs that sunsets cannot reverse, mandatory congressional delay also produces irreversible losses from ongoing foreign coercion. This exposed PRO’s one-sided cost accounting and made the comparative choice clearer, forcing the recognition that the dispute was about which risk profile Congress should prefer, not which side avoids all harm.

After A3 pressure, B conceded in B4.1-4.3 that no independent check exists before tariffs hit and that sunset does not compensate importers/farmers/consumers. That leaves PRO's core [A1.3][A2.4][A5.1] unanswered: the injury is imposition. CON's strongest counter - eventual agreement vs immediate availability [B2.3] - was met by PRO's reply that a Congress capable of writing tight triggers can pre-authorize expedited approval [A4.4][A5.3], preserving legitimacy without ceding a one-person tax window.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's immediate-harm + no-pre-check clash in A2/A3/A5 which forced B4 to admit no pre-tariff policing and no compensation, undercutting CON's safeguard story.
