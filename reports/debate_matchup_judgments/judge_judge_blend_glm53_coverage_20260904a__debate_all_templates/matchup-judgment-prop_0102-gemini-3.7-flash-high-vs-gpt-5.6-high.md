### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON consistently forced the debate onto the correct burden: the motion requires comparative causal primacy, not merely a plausible mechanism plus a matching timeline. CON's rebuttals systematically attacked PRO's weakest joints — reverse causation, the bundling of heterogeneous "social media" uses, the unaddressed counterfactual (would sleep/activity/connection actually return without platforms?), and most decisively, PRO's inconsistent evidentiary standard of treating cross-national uniformity as proof while also treating demographic heterogeneity as proof. PRO's answers to CON's pressure questions (A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2) largely restated the mechanism/timing story rather than supplying the demanded comparative evidence, while CON's answers to PRO's questions offered concrete alternative mechanisms (homework, over-supervision, restricted local freedom) that could produce the same "displacement" pattern without social media being the primary cause. CON's closing crystallized this with the sharp "renaming is not causal proof" line, which PRO's closing never rebutted — it simply reasserted the same displacement claims. Both sides were rhetorically strong and avoided fake precision, but CON built the cleaner, more decisive path to victory by keeping the burden question central and exposing PRO's unresolved circularity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn 8 point that "PRO cannot treat uniformity as proof when trends coincide, then treat heterogeneity as proof when they diverge" (responding to B4.2's gender-based dose-response claim) was the most decisive single exchange, exposing an unaddressed structural inconsistency in PRO's evidentiary approach that PRO never recovered from in the closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won on burden. PRO showed plausible mechanisms and timing for harm, but never discharged the comparative "primary driver" requirement. CON repeatedly exposed that gap: temporal coincidence does not isolate cause, heterogeneity undercuts a universal dose-response, reverse causation and mixed uses are unaddressed, and displacement assumes the counterfactual. PRO's answers to pressure repeated the mechanism rather than weighing it against offline pressures. B's amplifier-vs-engine framing left a live route that PRO never neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.1-B2.7 and B4.5-B4.7: thermometer vs pathogen, confounders asumed away by "single ubiquitous change," and catalyst/spark vs fuel reframe that turns PRO's uniformity-and-heterogeneity moves into a contradiction and demands the missing counterfactual.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A wins by providing a more cohesive causal story and effectively turning Side B's heterogeneity attack into a strength. When pressed on why trends vary if social media is a universal driver, Side A pointed to the disproportionate impact on adolescent girls using image-based feeds, offering a compelling dose-response mechanism. Side B's dismissal of this as mere "group correlation" was less persuasive than Side A's specific application. Furthermore, Side A's rhetorical framing of social media as destroying "evolutionary shock absorbers" was highly effective in meeting the "primary driver" burden, whereas Side B's counterfactual arguments, while logically sound, lacked the same decisive punch. Side A made the debate easier to decide by anchoring abstract mechanisms to concrete demographic realities.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.2), where they used the disproportionate mental health decline among adolescent girls on image-based platforms to transform Side B's heterogeneity argument into a demonstration of a dose-response relationship.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, mechanism-based cases, but the decisive exchange came from A's pressure question forcing B to concede that, absent the offline vulnerabilities (autonomy, security, community), "algorithmic feeds alone might not produce a crisis of the same scale." A converted that concession into a clean asymmetry: remove offline stressors and (by B's own admission) the crisis fades, but remove phones while keeping offline stressors and A asserts the crisis persists — an argument A crystallized unanswered in the final closing since B's closing preceded it. B's methodological rebuttal (that "primary driver" need not mean independently sufficient, akin to smoking and lung cancer) was analytically sound and partially blunted the force of the concession, and B's mechanism specificity (sleep displacement, quantified comparison, algorithmic amplification) and reversal of the "third places" argument were genuinely strong. But B's response to the divergence/asymmetry critique remained somewhat abstract ("vulnerability identifies who is affected, not what converts it") rather than directly rebutting A's sharpened final framing, leaving A with the cleaner, more memorable closing logic and the last unanswered word on the central clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure question ("would algorithmic feeds alone have generated a generation-wide crisis?") forcing B's "No" concession, which A then converted into a decisive asymmetry argument in Rebuttal 2 and Closing that B never got to directly counter.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the definitional clash: primary as largest interactive amplifier that makes scattered vulnerabilities continuous, quantified, and sleep-displacing, not sole origin. CON demanded independent sufficiency and leaned on generic offline list without showing better fit for form/timing, and never answered the fair counterfactual of holding modern pressures constant and removing algorithmic feeds. PRO answered both pressure questions directly with the microphone analogy and a conceded-but-reframed counterfactual, while CON sidestepped the uniformity question.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - conceding feeds alone wouldn't create crisis in ideal conditions but showing amplification can be the biggest causal contribution (microphone/volume) and reframing the test to removing social media while holding modern adolescence constant.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully dismantling Side B's "mirror" and "merely a correlate" framing. While Side B's rhetorical push on "root cause" and the asymmetry of confiscating phones was strong, Side A decisively proved that social media is an active, engineered driver adding specific harms (sleep displacement, public ranking) rather than a passive reflection of offline issues. Side A's public health framing of "primary driver" as the main converting force was more robust than Side B's demand for independent sufficiency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal destroying the "third places" argument by pointing out that a physical park does not rank children publicly, interrupt them overnight, or preserve humiliation indefinitely, effectively neutralizing Side B's "digital mirror" analogy.
