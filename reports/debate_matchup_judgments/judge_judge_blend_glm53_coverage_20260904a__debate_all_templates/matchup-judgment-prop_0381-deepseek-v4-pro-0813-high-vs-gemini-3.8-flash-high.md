PRO's "people not cots" reframe and its safety-protocol answers were reasonable but never fully resolved the DV-vetting asymmetry CON raised (objective caregiver paperwork vs. subjective coercive-control screening), and PRO's opening being clipped for exceeding the word limit is a real execution cost that cut off some of its foundational support. CON's answers to pressure questions were sharper and more consistent with its overall case, giving it the cleaner, more resolved path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 point that PRO's own "secure kennel/animal-control partner" fix for dangerous pets [B4.3] is functionally identical to the off-site containment CON already proposed, exposing that PRO's practical solution abandons the broad co-living mandate the motion requires.

A sometimes overstated the motion as requiring bulk storage or unsafe cohabitation, and used a nonexistent reference label, but still supplied the clearer emergency-triage reason to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used Side B’s own five-cots-for-three-people hypothetical to show a net loss under binding capacity, directly undermining B’s claim that inclusion necessarily expands reach.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a durable capacity-tradeoff mechanism—finite floor space and staff time mean "far more" couple/pet/storage accommodation costs raw beds precisely on the highest-demand nights—and never let it die: it reappeared in every speech and was pressed hard in Pressure Questions ("what gets cut... what shows intake still nets positive?"). PRO (B) answered mainly with assertion ("nothing essential is cut") rather than arithmetic, repeating that empty cots are wasted capacity without engaging A's specific rebuttal that on peak cold nights those cots are not empty at all. A also neutralized PRO's core "either accommodate or freeze" framing by proposing outreach, vouchers, and day-center casework as an alternative pathway to safety and housing referrals—directly answering both of PRO's pressure questions with a coherent counter-model, which PRO's closing merely rhetorically dismissed as a "concession" rather than substantively rebutting. PRO's case was vivid and emotionally forceful (the "ultimatum" framing, freezing-outside-vacant-beds imagery) and its coercion-visibility answer was plausible, but it left A's sharpest arithmetic and false-dilemma challenges functionally unanswered. A's opening was clipped for length, a real execution flaw, but A recovered its key points fully in later speeches, limiting the damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated "assertion, not arithmetic" challenge to B's claim that lockers/partitions/crating add capacity "without cutting anything essential"—first raised in Rebuttal 1, sharpened via Pressure Questions, and never resolved by B's answer that "nothing essential is cut."
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won the central resource tradeoff. Side B plausibly showed that separation rules deter intake, but never established that far greater accommodation would produce a net gain once space, staffing, safety, and peak-night capacity were considered. Its answer that nothing essential must be cut was especially unsupported. Side A then supplied a credible alternative of outreach, warming centers, vouchers, and off-site casework, weakening B’s freeze-or-enter framing. A’s clipped opening and both sides’ erroneous C-reference labels were execution costs, but not enough to reverse the result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was its response to B’s claim that lockers, partitions, and crates require no essential cuts: finite space and supervision make some tradeoff unavoidable, especially during peak demand. B did not answer that arithmetic and instead continued assuming vacant rigid beds.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO won the central intake-vs-capacity clash. A reframed B's bed-count as paper capacity while real beds sit empty because couples/pet owners refuse separation, and met B's strongest safety pressure with concrete private intake and unilateral transfer to trauma beds. B's winter-night tradeoff was asserted not proved, and its answer to refusers - outreach/motel vouchers - conceded PRO's point that restrictive shelter repels people and relies on scarcer alternatives, plus B lost part of its opening to clipping.
