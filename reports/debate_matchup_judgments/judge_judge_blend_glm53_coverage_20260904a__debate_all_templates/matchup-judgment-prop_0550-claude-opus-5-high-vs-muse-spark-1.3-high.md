### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making expansion capacity decisive: employer-selected slots may produce excellent individual outcomes, but Side B never established that subsidies could create them at cohort scale. Side A also sharply exposed that Side B repeatedly treated college subsidies as debt-financed enrollment, weakening its central harm story. Side B gave plausible answers about intermediaries and credential stacking, but conceded smaller scale and did not fully resolve selection or cyclical employer demand. The clipped second rebuttal was a real execution cost, though its visible answers were direct and the closing crystallized the winning comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s first rebuttal was decisive: it challenged the claim that productive apprenticeships merely need public coordination, highlighted employer screening, and distinguished grants from loans. Side B’s later answer explained how subsidies could reduce supervision costs, but its concession that apprenticeship scale would remain smaller left Side A’s reach argument alive.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around scalability and the definition of "subsidies." B effectively argued that while apprenticeships have high individual returns, their expansion is bottlenecked by private employer willingness, limiting cohort-level impact. Furthermore, B neutralized Side A's strongest harm—student debt—by pointing out that the motion specifies expanding subsidies, meaning grants rather than loans. Side A's reliance on the debt narrative was thus systematically dismantled.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 point that Side A was "prosecuting loans in a debate about aid," which neutralized Side A's repeated claims about debt compounding against marginal students.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): A won by keeping the live comparative burden on expanding: gain per person times people reached. B built a strong micro case for paid pathways (early earnings, licenses, no debt drag, stacking), but never solved firm-controlled scale, selection residuals, or cyclicity. A turned B’s own “produce while training” and “doesn’t compel; it bribes” lines into the decisive point that public money cannot reliably expand slots one HR decision at a time, while enrollment subsidies are a pullable open-door lever—and correctly noted the motion compares subsidies, not loans. B’s completion critique landed, yet did not neutralize A’s long-run portability/slope and expandability frame; A’s clipped R2 was a cost but not fatal given the rest of the exchange and a tighter closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s use of B’s self-financing/bribe-not-compel admission plus employer selection vs “reach the majority left behind” to collapse B’s scale claim.
