### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (CON)): Side B won by correctly identifying and consistently defending the motion's actual question—whether states should proceed "even when" communities object—and then forcing PRO to abandon it. From the opening, CON set the decisive frame: benefits are diffuse and long-term, harms are concentrated and immediate, and the distribution problem is not solved by saying "the region gains." PRO's rebuttal 1 damaged itself with a cascade of brittle, suspiciously exact statistics (30% deer reduction, 1.5x compensation multiplier, €150/ha, 80% poaching drop) that CON correctly flagged as uncheckable precision in a closed-book round; stripping those away left only the thin claim that predators can sometimes help somewhere. CON's pressure questions were sharper and more decisive than PRO's: Q1 exposed the contradiction between "inclusive governance" and overriding objection, and Q2 forced PRO to articulate a limiting principle. In rebuttal 2, PRO answered with a "calibrated risk-benefit threshold" requiring diagnostics, mitigation floors, pilot failures of alternatives, and community revenue-sharing before proceeding—effectively retreating to CON's cautious, consent-sensitive position while claiming it still supported the motion. CON's closing crystallized this retreat cleanly: PRO now says proceed only "sometimes, after safeguards, pilots, exclusions, compensation, and participation," which is not the motion's sweeping rule of imposition but rather CON's own standard in softer language. PRO's closing failed to rebut this decisive framing. Side A's ecological case was substantively sound and the Yellowstone/Swedish lynx examples were appropriate, but the fake precision, a clipped rebuttal 1, and the self-undermining concession in rebuttal 2 left no live route to victory on the actual motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 2 crystallization that PRO had retreated from the motion was the most decisive exchange. After PRO's A2 answer introduced conditions (diagnostics, thresholds, pilot failures, exclusions for fragmented terrain, community participation) that effectively matched CON's own position, CON correctly identified this as a concession rather than a defense: "PRO has retreated from 'countries should reintroduce predators even when rural communities and livestock farmers object' to 'sometimes, after safeguards, pilots, exclusions, compensation, and participation.'" This moved the burden irreversibly: if legitimacy and local fit are necessary conditions, then sustained rural objection is evidence the policy may be the wrong tool, not a mere obstacle to override—making the motion as written indefensible on PRO's own terms.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (CON)): CON wins because it repeatedly forced the clash onto the motion's explicit "even when...object" clause, exposed PRO's retreat into thresholds and consent-building in Rebuttal 2, and maintained consistent grounding without brittle claims.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 directly answering the pressure questions then showing PRO's safeguards equal CON's position.
