### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by making the 2026–2036 clock and the bundled portfolio the decisive decision rule. PRO’s strongest live route—cheap, immediate life extensions that prevent gas backfill—was never fully neutralized on pure per-dollar terms, but CON repeatedly forced the comparison the motion actually writes: life-extension *and* new-build programs under equivalent scale-up. Once PRO conceded new-build dollars largely abate nothing before 2036, CON’s finite-fleet dilemma landed cleanly: if relicensing is cheap, it cannot absorb an equivalent public scale-up and most capital becomes deadweight; if overhauls are huge, the cheapness claim evaporates. PRO’s system-cost and capacity-factor case was real but weaker inside this decade’s window, and clipping on PRO’s opening and closing hurt closure. CON adapted across rounds, answered pressure, and left the cleaner vote: operational renewable abatement compounds inside the window while the nuclear package is dragged by non-producing new-build capital.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s portfolio trap (especially A2/A4): cheap finite life extensions cannot carry an equivalent scale-up, so bulk public dollars go to new builds that produce ~0 abatement before 2036—averaging nuclear’s per-dollar result down fatally.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a structural catch-22 in Side A's bundled case that Side A never resolved. Side A established that life extensions are highly cost-effective, but Side B showed that under an equivalent scale-up the finite existing fleet cannot absorb the full public budget, forcing most capital into new-build programs that Side A conceded would abate negligible emissions before 2036. Side A's attempt to reframe the motion as a pure per-dollar rate comparison without confronting the scale implication of "equivalent scale-up" left the portfolio-average math unanswered. Meanwhile, Side B's consistent temporal focus—renewables deploy in 12–36 months and compound displacement across nine years within the window, while nuclear new-build capital sits idle—provided the cleaner comparative weighing. Side A's clipped opening and closing further undermined its ability to close this gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8) created the decisive dilemma: if life extensions are as cheap as Side A claims, they cannot absorb an equivalent public scale-up and most funding must flow to zero-yielding new builds; if they require massive overhauls, their per-dollar advantage disappears. This catch-22 remained live through the close because Side A could only assert that the aggregate investment is "enormous" without quantifying how it could absorb scaled-up public capital comparable to a major renewable buildout.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a more rigorous analytical framework, particularly regarding emissions counterfactuals and grid integration costs. Side B's strongest rhetorical attack—that new nuclear builds produce zero emissions in the 2026-2036 window—was effectively neutralized by A's demonstration that life-extension dollars yield such massive per-dollar returns that they carry the blended portfolio. Crucially, B committed a fatal analytical error in Rebuttal 2 by claiming life extensions "displace zero additional fossil emissions beyond today's baseline," ignoring A's established counterfactual that retiring reactors are replaced by gas. B also hand-waved A's sharp pressure questions regarding renewable integration costs at high penetration. While B's "finite fleet" trap was clever, A's clarification on aggregate refurbishment costs and the per-dollar metric successfully defused it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered the "finite fleet" trap by explaining the massive aggregate capital required for uprates and refurbishments across 95 GW, while simultaneously exposing B's flawed assumption that the proposition requires an identical total budget rather than a per-dollar marginal comparison.
