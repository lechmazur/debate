### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A wins a clear but competitive debate. Side B had a plausible well-being and anti-herd case, but Side A more effectively exposed the unresolved contradiction that platforms would still optimize hidden engagement while users lose public cross-checks. A also landed the stronger default-status-quo point against B's claim that creators can simply toggle metrics back on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was pressing that hiding public counts does not stop the backend outrage engine, but does remove users' ability to compare likes, reposts, and reactions as imperfect public diagnostics. B's laundering response was strong, but A made the opacity tradeoff more decisive.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a clearer causal mechanism: default-off removes the public reward loop that drives outrage content and herd dismissal of new voices, while preserving private data and user toggles. B's transparency and power-transfer claims were addressed directly in pressure answers and rebuttals, showing metrics often launder rather than expose manipulation; B's creator lifeline argument was weakened by ignoring how visible low counts create immediate stigma that neutral defaults avoid. Later rounds narrowed effectively on incentives vs. hidden control without new evidence dumps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2's direct A1/A2 answers to B's pressure questions on toggle mechanics and algos reframed default-off as neutral ground that starves performative behavior upstream while B's ecosystem diagnostic was left partially exposed as still reliant on visible but inflated signals.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO wins by more convincingly dismantling CON's core thesis that visible metrics serve as a democratic check on algorithms. CON argued public counts expose manipulation, but PRO effectively showed that algorithmically-curated feeds generate those very numbers, meaning likes launder corporate promotion as organic consensus rather than audit it. PRO's framing—that you cannot audit the referee with the referee's own numbers—survived the exchange and materially weakened CON's transparency claim. PRO also landed a durable, intuitive benefit for grassroots creators by highlighting the "scroll-past" penalty of low visible counts under the status quo, which CON never fully dispel. While CON landed a sharp procedural hit on PRO's toggle mechanics and kept the debate competitive by pressing that hidden backend engagement would still fuel outrage, PRO's behavioral mechanism—changing the interface to reshape creator incentives—held up better under pressure. PRO's closing cleanly crystallized these live routes to victory, whereas CON's closing, while rhetorically strong, could not recover its central transparency argument after PRO's laundering rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that visible engagement metrics do not expose algorithmic manipulation but instead launder it, capped by the line "You cannot audit the referee using numbers the referee generates." This reframe directly neutralized CON's "democratic check" argument and remained unanswered in a way that decisively shifted the transparency clash in PRO's favor.
