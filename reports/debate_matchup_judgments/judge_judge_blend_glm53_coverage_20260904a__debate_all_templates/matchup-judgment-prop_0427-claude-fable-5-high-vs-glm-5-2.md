### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making effectiveness, not mere exposure, the decisive comparison. B showed that structured activities directly create relationships, supervision, identity, and alternatives to online belonging, while A never adequately established that recognizing manipulation reliably changes behavior. A’s self-selection argument was strong, but it relied on unsupported claims that programs chiefly capture the already-connected and that mandatory literacy reaches and influences nearly every detached youth. B also answered both pressure questions directly and gave the cleaner account of how limited program hours can reshape life beyond those hours.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sharpest exchange was its response that reach multiplied by negligible behavioral effect remains negligible, followed by the symmetry challenge: if detached students can be reached through schools for literacy, publicly funded programs can also use school-based recruitment and delivery. A distinguished mandatory lessons from voluntary belonging, but never overcame B’s deeper point that instruction offers awareness without an actual substitute for isolation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A (PRO) won because it established that effectiveness matters more than raw reach, grounded its case in a concrete model (Iceland), and repeatedly exposed that Side B never proved social-media literacy changes behavior. Side B's best argument—that voluntary programs miss the most isolated kids—was competitive, but it could not overcome the conceded fact that awareness alone failed to stop radicalization (the Buffalo shooter example) and that B offered only speculative upstream prevention. A's sustained pressure demanding evidence that literacy recognition produces behavioral change went largely unanswered, while A satisfactorily answered B's questions by showing the Iceland mechanism (restructuring time, identity, and adult contact) transfers across harms. A's closing crystallized the choice as a working mechanism versus a lecture with perfect attendance, making the comparative vote clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7) dismantling the "3 hours versus 24/7" objection by arguing that belonging changes what the other hours mean, paired with its sustained Q1 pressure demanding evidence that literacy recognition produces behavioral change—a burden Side B never met with concrete support.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): PRO established that effectiveness matters more than distribution (reach*zero=zero), turned self-selection into funding solvable via school-embedded free programs, and turned radicalization by distinguishing online delivery from offline vulnerability. CON never answered Q1 on evidence that recognition changes behavior and conceded the Buffalo shooter knew what he consumed, leaving its mechanism hypothetical versus Iceland-type proven mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 pairing reach-multiplied-by-zero plus mandatory-vs-voluntary symmetry: if school captures the withdrawn kid for literacy, same funding captures him for an intramural, and belonging changes the meaning of the remaining screen hours.
