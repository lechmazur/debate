- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a stronger cumulative case by the close. Its central burden—that nuclear is necessary because no proven firm zero-carbon alternative exists at grid scale—was maintained and sharpened across rounds, culminating in the closing's decisive challenge: "when you strip nuclear out, what proven firm zero-carbon technology remains?" Side B (PRO) kept a valid logical frame alive throughout ("firm capacity is valuable" does not entail "nuclear must be central"), but never adequately defended each element of its portfolio under CON's systematic pressure. CON exposed that hydro is largely built out, geothermal is site-limited, hydrogen and long-duration storage are unproven at scale, and bioenergy is supply-constrained—leaving PRO's portfolio looking speculative rather than ready. PRO's strongest move, the modular-vs-lumpy risk comparison in the closing, was genuine but undercut by not answering the core question of what keeps the grid firm in the interim. PRO was also clipped in three turns, a real execution cost that left key arguments incompletely on the record. CON's insurance-to-load-bearing-structure rebuttal was the sharpest single exchange, and the unanswered "what exactly is the clean backup?" pressure question remained live at the close. The margin is clear but competitive: PRO's framing discipline and the motion's "central strategy" qualifier prevented a wider gap.
