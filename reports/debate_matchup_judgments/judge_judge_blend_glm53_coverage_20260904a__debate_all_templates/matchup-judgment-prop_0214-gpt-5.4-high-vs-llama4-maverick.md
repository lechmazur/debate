- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Llama 4 Maverick (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won on two decisive analytical threads that PRO never neutralized. First, the limiting principle: the motion covers "descendants" broadly, but when pressed in Rebuttal 2, PRO retreated to "direct survivors and their children," which is an ad hoc cutoff that cannot explain why children count but grandchildren do not—an implicit concession that CON harvested effectively in its own Rebuttal 2 and closing. Second, the archives paradox: PRO conceded in Rebuttal 2 that redacted records reveal "scale, demographics, and policies" rather than personal information—but CON had already argued that this level of disclosure can be provided through institutional reports and memorialization without wider file access. PRO's own answer thus collapsed the distinction between what the motion demands and what CON's alternative already delivers. PRO's moral case had genuine force and was never falsified, but it rested on vague appeals to "genuine commitment" and "meaningful steps" rather than answering CON's core critique that the specific remedies (broader compensation, wider archival access) are inaccurate where harm was worst and privacy-violating where disclosure is richest. CON also offered a live alternative—apology, memorialization, forward-looking safeguards—that PRO never engaged as insufficient on its own terms. The pressure questions from CON were sharper and more targeted than PRO's, and CON's closing crystallized the live clash cleanly while PRO's closing largely restated earlier points without advancing the comparison.
