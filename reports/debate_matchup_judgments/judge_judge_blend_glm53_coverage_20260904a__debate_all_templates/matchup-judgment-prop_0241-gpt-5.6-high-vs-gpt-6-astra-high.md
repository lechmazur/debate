### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): The debate turned on a well-framed test: which aim governs in genuine conflict cases. B introduced this test early (low-risk murderer, wealthy fraudster) and pressed it relentlessly. Critically, A's own answers in Pressure Questions and Rebuttal 2 conceded both key conflict scenarios—that a reformed murderer still gets grave punishment because of retribution, and that rehabilitative sentences must yield when they "genuinely fail proportionate accountability." B's closing crystallized this: rehabilitation only wins when retribution is "indifferent" between equally fitting options, which is a tie-breaker role, not primacy. A tried to reframe this as retribution being merely a "minimum threshold" while rehabilitation supplies the "leading direction," but never explained why satisfying a necessary condition doesn't count as governing the choice, especially once A conceded retribution decides when the aims actually clash. B's procedural analogy rebuttal (fair process determines legitimacy but isn't itself an aim) was a clean parry of A's attempted counter-analogy. A's rehabilitation-as-equality argument was met effectively by B's point about equally culpable but differently "treatable" offenders, and A never fully resolved why unequal treatability doesn't threaten equal justice under an ostensibly rehabilitation-led regime. Both sides argued skillfully and stayed on-topic throughout, with strong adherence to format rules, but B's tighter logical structure and more effective use of A's own concessions gives it the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (B3.1/B3.2) forcing A to concede in A4.1/A4.2 that retribution governs the reformed-murderer case and that rehabilitation must yield when it fails proportionate accountability—concessions B then used decisively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to show rehabilitation only wins in cases where retribution is indifferent.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by establishing a clear metric for "most important aim"—which aim prevails in a direct conflict—and consistently demonstrating that Side A conceded these conflict cases to retribution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively reframed Side A's case, pointing out that Side A was merely "redescribing every victory for retribution as a constraint" and that winning only when retribution is indifferent makes rehabilitation a tie-breaker, not the primary aim.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won the definition of "most important" - PRO conceded that grave punishment is still required for a reformed murderer and that a rehabilitative sentence yielding inadequate condemnation must yield. B showed retribution determines who, why, and how much we punish and prevails in genuine conflicts, while rehabilitation only selects among equally fitting options like cost or mercy. PRO's "threshold + constructive aim" reframe could not survive those concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 framing that an aim that defines punishment's distinct action and wins direct conflicts is more important, plus exposing PRO's double concession on the reformed-murderer and inadequate-condemnation cases.
