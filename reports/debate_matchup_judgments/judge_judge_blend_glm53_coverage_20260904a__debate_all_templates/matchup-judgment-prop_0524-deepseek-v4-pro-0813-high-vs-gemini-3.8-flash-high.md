- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a more resilient mechanism across the exchange: it conceded the cap-vs-guarantee tension early, then used the pressure round to expose a genuine internal contradiction in CON's twin objections (that local caps both paralyze institutions via strict site-binding and are simultaneously meaningless because workers commute past them). The closings mirrored this: PRO crystallized why the capacity link is self-correcting, while CON repeated the stagnation-trap framing without directly closing the contradiction PRO had exposed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2.4/A2.5 catch of CON's self-contradictory commuting objection (paralysis assumes no commuting relief; "meaningless test" assumes commuting relief), reinforced in A4.2, was the most decisive exchange—CON never cleanly resolved it, instead restating the dilemma in B4.2 without addressing the internal tension PRO identified.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): B identified the resolution's central structural tension in the opening (a "capped but guaranteed" allocation cannot simultaneously track a moving capacity target) and sustained that attack across every round. A's attempted resolution via "fixed-cycle" allocations in Rebuttal 2 actually conceded the point B needed: within a cycle, the guarantee overrides the tie, meaning strain can deepen until the next cycle. B seized on this admission in Rebuttal 2 and the closing, turning A's own answer into the decisive rebuttal. B also landed originally strong points that were never fully neutralized: the nurse-as-both-demand-and-supply causal-order problem, the planner-capture/lobbying vulnerability in defining "usable headroom," and the NIMBY-veto incentive created by an explicit capacity-to-visa link. A's case was well-constructed and rhetorically clean (institutional responsibility, predictability, secondary-city channeling), but its central analogy (airport landing slots) was convincingly dismantled by B's two-sided-migration point, and A's closing simply reasserted the financing-certainty claim without engaging B's concrete counter that phased, milestone-triggered visas are a standard and sufficient financing mechanism. B's closing crystallized the one live issue—guarantee vs. milestone conditionality—more precisely and used A's own concession against it, giving B the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3-B4.4), which uses A's own pressure-question answer (visas issued mid-cycle are never revoked; the cap only adjusts next cycle) to show that the "guarantee" necessarily overrides the "tie" during any period of worsening capacity — the exact contradiction B raised in the opening, now proven from A's own mechanism.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing a fatal structural tension in PRO's plan. PRO concedes that if local capacity worsens mid-cycle, already-issued visas are not revoked and strain deepens until the next review. This proves that the "guarantee" overrides the "tie" to local capacity, converting it into a one-cycle entitlement that prioritizes institutional certainty over municipal health. CON's milestone-linked alternative captures the benefits of infrastructure coordination without this risk transfer, and PRO cannot show that financing requires an irrevocable guarantee rather than phased, enforceable milestones. The debate narrows cleanly to this point, and CON's closing crystallizes it with damaging clarity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over mid-cycle capacity deterioration. CON's pressure question (A3.1) forces PRO to admit (B4.1) that visas are not revoked even if housing or clinic capacity worsens. CON then weaponizes this concession across A4.3-A4.4 and the closing to show the guarantee necessarily severs the link to local capacity, destroying the scheme's core promise.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won the core logical clash. B's Q1 exposed that capped+guaranteed+tied cannot hold when capacity moves within a cycle, and A's answer [A4.1] conceded visas would not be revoked, letting strain deepen. B framed the decision as guarantee vs tie and showed phased milestone certainty achieves sequencing without risk transfer, undercutting PRO's finance necessity claim. PRO's airport-slot analogy and tangible-capacity claim were materially damaged and not recovered.
