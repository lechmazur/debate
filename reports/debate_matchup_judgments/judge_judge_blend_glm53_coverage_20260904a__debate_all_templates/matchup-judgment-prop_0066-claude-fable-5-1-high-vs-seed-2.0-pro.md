Execution drag: A’s Rebuttal 2 was clipped mid-sentence; still edged a competitive B.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by separating recognition from retention, export, and nonconsensual enrollment, offering narrower regulation for the harms Side B identified. Most decisively, Side B conceded that the motion as written criminalizes disability-assistance use, then tried to rewrite the professional exception rather than defend the proposition. Side B persuasively established privacy risks, but never showed why banning logs and databases would be insufficient or why the motion's overbreadth was justified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the perfect-memory concern by targeting retention and export, then used Side B's explicit concession about prosopagnosia to show that the proposed disability fix contradicts the motion's professional-only exception.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing decisive concessions and then weighing them cleanly: A admitted database bans are good, admitted the motion as written criminalizes non-professional disabled users, and could not show a unique residual glasses harm beyond logging/export (already covered by B’s retention rule) or stalking already illegal. A’s vivid anonymity case stayed intuitive but never recovered from the text-as-written burden, institutional asymmetry, and “amend later” repair. B made the ballot easier: after A’s own concessions, the motion’s distinctive effect is banning the pedestrian look while institutions keep scanning.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 after A’s A1/A2 answers: accepting A’s disability concession, collapsing remaining “perfect memory/export” harm into the database ban A already endorsed, and showing the motion uniquely criminalizes the wrong people while preserving institutional scanners.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger and more decisive case by attacking the exact text of the motion, specifically the "professional exception" which excludes disabled users who legitimately need the technology. Side A also successfully reframed the core harm as a database and retention issue rather than a hardware issue, forcing Side B into a weak "we can amend the text later" defense. Side A's closing crystallized this clash perfectly, highlighting the contradiction of Side B asking the judge to wait for future amendments while criminalizing vulnerable people today. Side B's reliance on fixing other things (databases, fixed cameras) outside the motion weakened their defense of the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's "memory vs. logging" distinction and capitalized on Side B's concession that disabled users would be criminals under the motion's text, contrasting it sharply with Side B's accusation that Side A was asking the judge to "wait" for future fixes.
