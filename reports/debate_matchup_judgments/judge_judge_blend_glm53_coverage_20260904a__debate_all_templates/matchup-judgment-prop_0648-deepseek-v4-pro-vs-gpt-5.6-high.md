### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but B's was more resilient across the exchange. B's early concession of a small permanent nucleus for classified/continuous missions looked risky, but B converted it into a strength in the closing by reframing the clash as "prepared dependence vs. improvised dependence": since A's own answers (A4.1) conceded that standing units still need civilians to execute inside actual infrastructure, B could argue that a trained, pre-vetted reserve is simply the organized version of a dependency A already admits exists. A never squarely answered this reframe in its final speech, instead re-running the tempo/institutional-memory argument without explaining why ad hoc civilian reliance beats a rehearsed reserve for the localized-access problem. A's structural conflict-of-interest argument and continuity/tempo points were substantive and were pressed well in Rebuttal 1 and the pressure round, but B's A1/A2 answers (governance of conflicts via disclosure, recusal, and rotation parity with full-time staff) neutralized much of that pressure. A also suffered a real execution cost: both its opening and Rebuttal 1 were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off supporting material (including an unfinished Cyber Command example and an unfinished insider-risk point), which weakens the completeness and persuasive force of its case relative to B's clean, fully delivered speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to A's pressure questions (A1/A2 in Turn 7) directly rebutting the "primary depth" and conflict-of-interest objections, followed by B's closing reframe of "prepared dependence vs. improvised dependence," which exploited A's own concession that standing units still rely on civilian execution inside infrastructure.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully reframing the core clash around how to integrate undeniable civilian expertise. While Side A pushed hard on the need for continuous operational tempo and institutional memory, Side B expertly weaponized Side A's admission that standing units would draw on civilian operators during emergencies, turning the debate into a choice between 'prepared' vs. 'improvised' dependence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing correctly identified Side A's Rebuttal 2 concession (that standing units rely on civilian experts in an emergency) to decisively prove that Side B's model of a formalized, pre-vetted reserve is structurally superior.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by developing a cleaner comparative frame: prepared civilian dependence beats improvised civilian dependence. CON conceded that civilian platform expertise is necessary in emergencies but never showed why ad hoc help matches pre-vetted, trained reserves with rehearsed relationships. A also neutralized B's security objections by showing full-timers face comparable conflicts, while B's clipped Opening and R1—plus repetitive assertions about structural risk—failed to fully answer A's scale, sustainability, and surge-capacity advantages. A's closing crystallized the decisive clash effectively: a command that cannot translate direction into safe action on private systems is coherent only on paper.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing reframing of CON's position as "improvised dependence" versus PRO's "prepared dependence," exposing that CON still needs civilians but reaches for them too late and without rehearsed trust or secure reporting paths.
