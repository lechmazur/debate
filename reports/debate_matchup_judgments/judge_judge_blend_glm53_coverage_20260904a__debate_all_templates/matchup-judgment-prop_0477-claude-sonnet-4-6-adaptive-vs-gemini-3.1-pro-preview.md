### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by making the tradeoff in the motion decisive. A persuasively established that cables are critical and vulnerable, and gave a strong chokepoint, redundancy, and attribution story. But B more effectively answered the key comparative burden: protection and attribution still require conventional forces, military command systems are not simply dependent on commercial cables, and A never showed why the needed investment must come at the expense of visible procurements rather than other budgets. B also exploited A's finite, bounded-project answer to argue that A had weakened the case for a major defense reallocation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was turning A's sensor and chokepoint argument back against the motion: sensors may help detect problems, but without conventional naval and retaliatory capacity they do not deter or stop state actors, so defunding that capacity breaks A's own deterrence chain.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by exposing a fatal tension in PRO’s case. PRO conceded that effective deterrence requires both seabed sensors and conventional forces to act on them, yet insisted on shifting funding away from those very conventional forces. CON showed that defunding one necessary link to strengthen another breaks the deterrence chain. Worse for PRO, when pressed on the “much more” threshold, PRO described the project as “finite” and “geographically bounded.” CON turned this into a powerful comparative argument: a modest, bounded infrastructure project does not require raiding defense procurement budgets, gutting the motion’s core trade-off. PRO never resolved this dilemma, leaving CON with the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal (Turn 8), where it weaponized PRO’s own “finite, geographically bounded” threshold answer to show that a limited infrastructure project cannot justify sacrificing visible defense procurements, collapsing the motion’s “even if” clause.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): CON wins by successfully turning PRO’s attempt to scale the project as “finite and bounded” against the motion’s explicit trade-off, showing that a targeted infrastructure fix doesn’t justify raiding conventional procurement. CON also landed a decisive blow by separating military command networks from commercial cable dependency, which PRO never fully answered. PRO’s case was strategically coherent but ultimately collapsed the required sacrifice, making CON’s framing of the deterrence chain and budget allocation cleaner and more responsive to the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 effectively dismantled PRO’s threshold concession, arguing that a “finite, geographically bounded” project belongs in infrastructure budgets, not defense procurement, thereby neutralizing the motion’s core trade-off while also debunking the commercial/military comms conflation.
