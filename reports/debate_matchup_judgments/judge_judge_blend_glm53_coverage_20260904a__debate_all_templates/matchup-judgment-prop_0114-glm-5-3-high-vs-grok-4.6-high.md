### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by distinguishing immediate, reviewable caregiver support from permanent pension liabilities and by showing that verification, targeting, and fiscal errors become more consequential when they create vested claims. Side B strongly challenged the idea that pensions are purely deferred wages, but its cost defense was internally weak: little behavioral change does not make credits cheap when many families receive them anyway, and credits are not equivalent to targeted old-age support. Side B also incurred modest clipping costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A decisively answered Side B’s marginal-savings argument: the existence of some families choosing between home and institutional care cannot justify pension credits for every eligible year, especially when most care may have occurred regardless. This exposed the gap between hypothetical avoided costs and guaranteed pension liabilities.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by exposing the incoherence in Side B's verification objection: B's own alternative of cash and respite requires the same care-verification infrastructure, yet B treats pension credits as uniquely unverifiable. While B's distinction between temporary benefits and permanent compounding claims is sharp, it does not fully explain why the same care assessments that gate cash support cannot gate credits, especially when A shows that B's preferred path leads to the same verification burden plus later means-testing. A's framing that the pension record already includes non-wage years and that the line is political rather than metaphysical withstands B's category-error argument, leaving A with the cleaner path to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing trap that Side B's alternative requires the same caregiver verification B calls impossible, and that means-tested old-age support is strictly more intrusive than piggybacked pension credits—forcing B into a "distinction of degree" defense that never fully recovered the practical objection.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the more live path: pensions already credit non-wage years (survivors, child-rearing), and caregivers substitute for state-bought care. CON's strongest card was verification impossibility [A2.6], but B's pressure Q1 exposed that CON's own alternative—cash, respite, services now [A2.9]—requires the same identification. CON's reply that error "dies with the payment" vs credits that "compound" [A4.1-A4.2] is a valid stakes difference but does not restore impossibility, and leaves the core incoherence unanswered: why a state-verified caregiver is paid in one column and erased in the other. That verification paradox plus B's margin/cheapness defense neutralized cost and fairness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 + B4.3-B4.5 showing verification needed for CON's cash/respite is the enrollment for credits, vs A's A4.1-A4.2 temporary-vs-vested distinction—the decisive exchange where CON's impossibility claim consumed its own alternative.

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate tied to the proposition: present-day support and pension credits are complementary, because cash or respite now does not repair the caregiver’s later contribution gap. Its insistence that the record must remain zero also reinforced B’s central charge that the system recognizes invoices rather than socially necessary work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s most decisive response rejected Side A’s cash-versus-credit framing: immediate aid addresses current hardship, while credits address old-age insecurity. B crystallized the choice around the caregiver’s actual wellbeing rather than ledger aesthetics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8), distinguishing general-revenue aid from pension credits as “dressing redistribution up as contribution” and demonstrating that credits fail caregivers at every life stage.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live path: insurance already credits unemployment/sickness/military despite no financing, care that substitutes for billed beds saves public money, and leaving the record at zero creates a predictable gendered old-age poverty trap.
