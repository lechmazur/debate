### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by preserving the central normalization and consent mechanism: an automatic cosmetic alteration establishes the edited face as the baseline before a minor chooses it. Side B offered creative exceptions and a strong denoising continuum, but repeatedly called normalization “zero mechanism” rather than neutralizing it, and its focus on harms the motion does not eliminate confused incompleteness with ineffectiveness. A also answered the campaign exception with a low-cost opt-in alternative, leaving B without a compelling reason these effects need to be defaults.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s response to the “spotlight” argument was decisive: presenting universal smoothing as protection from appearing raw effectively reinforced A’s claim that defaults make natural faces seem socially deficient. Side B’s distinction between mild correction and aggressive reshaping narrowed its defense but did not defeat that baseline-setting concern.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins the decisive clash on mechanism and scope. PRO asserts defaults create a synthetic baseline but never explains why toggle position, not filter use, drives dysmorphia, and concedes the high-risk deliberate editor is untouched [B2.2]. CON shows reality baseline does not exist, that mild softening can restore what the eye sees vs 4K exaggeration, and that the categorical rule bans a disclosed one-tap-off anti-smoking aging filter [B4.2] while waving through the 20-minute edit. PRO's workaround that the campaign can require a tap confirms the motion's overbreadth without answering the threshold collapse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2/A4 dismantling PRO's photon vs biology line by using PRO's own "what the eye actually sees" test to justify mild smoothing, and exposing that PRO declares the conscious nose-slimmer safe while targeting uniform live-video softening.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by decisively exposing the logical and practical flaws in the proposition's categorical ban on defaults. A successfully argued that the motion misses the actual driver of dysmorphia (deliberate, heavy, comparative editing) while banning harmless or beneficial applications (like mild video-call smoothing or educational aging filters). A's reframing of "opt-in" as a more psychologically damaging active choice of self-correction was highly persuasive and trapped Side B. B defended the motion well but was undermined by its rigidity, particularly when conceding it would ban an anti-smoking campaign filter, allowing A to paint the proposition as a blunt, slogan-driven instrument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's "biological reality" threshold by pointing out that phone cameras exaggerate texture (making mild softening a restoration of reality), and highlighted the absurdity of a rule that bans a disclosed educational aging filter while leaving 20-minute deliberate selfie editing completely untouched.
