### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO's fire-alarm framing—populism is the symptom of already-failed channels, never the cure—was introduced early, extended consistently, and never truly defeated. The core empirical observation that voters only turn to system-smashers after normal channels have stopped working was largely conceded by CON's own examples: both the 1890s Populists and Five Star arose precisely because of serious elite capture. PRO correctly used "usually" as a shield: two examples cannot overturn a general pattern, and CON never produced cross-national evidence establishing their cases were representative rather than exceptional. PRO's "legitimacy erosion" answer to Italy (A1 in Rebuttal 2)—that Five Star destroyed residual trust and cleared the path for actors who would break rules—was thin but functional, and CON never produced a compelling rebuttal to the longer-run Italian trajectory. CON's strongest move was the unfalsifiability charge (any outcome fits PRO's story) and the conceptual distinction between correctable elite unresponsiveness and terminal institutional decay. These were genuine scoring blows. PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 was evasive on the falsifiability question, and CON's institutional-decay definition was the sharpest conceptual contribution in the debate. However, CON never converted that conceptual advantage into a demonstration that "usually" most populist surges are healthy correctives—their entire evidential case rested on two examples. Two execution-cost clippings (CON's Opening and Rebuttal 2) also meant CON lost content at critical points. On balance, PRO's framing held because the central causal claim—that populism signals pre-existing failure rather than producing a cure—was more durable than CON's two-case rebuttal of a "usually" proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 counter-analysis of CON's own examples was the most decisive exchange. PRO argued that the 1890s Populists emerged only after twenty years of failed normal channels (supporting the warning-sign thesis) and that Five Star's collapse was followed by a far-right government (undermining the healthy-corrective thesis). CON pushed back with the post-pandemic causation argument for Italy, but PRO's legitimacy-erosion mechanism (cleared the field for norm-breakers) absorbed that rebuttal adequately, keeping the Five Star example in PRO's column rather than CON's.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): B won by making the sharper burden and falsifiability attack: A asserted “usually” and treated even successful reform cases as proof of decay, but never supplied a comparative basis beyond rhetoric. B’s concrete examples showed populist movements operating through democratic institutions, producing reforms, and peacefully leaving power. A’s fire-alarm frame was memorable and kept the debate competitive, but it became too circular and under-evidenced to carry the “usually” claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was pressing A on Five Star: A conceded it eroded no formal democratic rule, then relied on vague legitimacy erosion, allowing B to argue that A was converting any elite dissatisfaction into “decay” and making the proposition unfalsifiable.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): B built the stronger case by consistently defending the "usually" framing, exposing the circularity in A's examples, and maintaining the fire-alarm analogy through rebuttals without evasion. A's examples were neutralized as fitting the warning pattern; clipping hurt execution but did not change the core clash outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-2.6 and B4.1-4.6 together dismantled A's examples by showing they confirm prior decay and by correctly refusing to shift the "usually" burden.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A wins because it successfully defended both halves of the motion: that populism is a warning sign of decay, and that it is not a healthy corrective. Side B's "symptom vs. cause" reframing was genuinely interesting but strategically self-defeating—by arguing populism signals decay that already happened, Side B was essentially conceding the "warning sign" framing and never robustly defended the "healthy corrective" claim that is the critical comparison the motion demands. Side B's strongest point was the "usually" challenge—most populist movements never take power and many force elite adjustment—but Side A answered this in Rebuttal 2 by arguing that non-governing populist movements still erode democratic norms through anti-institutional rhetoric, mainstreaming conspiracy theories, and pushing mainstream parties to abandon guardrails. Side B never adequately neutralized this answer. Side B's "fire alarm" metaphor was rhetorically sharp but logically gave away the motion by framing populism as a passive signal rather than a corrective force. Side A's invocation of non-populist reform paths (post-Watergate, civil rights movement) to rebut the false dichotomy charge was not squarely answered. Two clipped turns for Side A are a real execution cost, but the core argument survived. Side B's rhetorical clarity and the "usually" framing in the closing were genuinely strong and nearly closed the gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's "most movements never take office" pressure question: even non-governing populist movements damage democratic norms by legitimizing anti-institutional rhetoric, mainstreaming electoral fraud claims, and pulling mainstream parties off democratic guardrails. This was the most decisive exchange because it directly neutralized CON's strongest structural argument without relying on the Orbán outlier, and Side B never adequately answered it.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): A won because B’s central “populism is only an alarm after decay has begun” framing largely conceded the motion’s warning-sign component, while A more persuasively showed that populist movements tend to convert elite failure into attacks on courts, media, minorities, and democratic norms. B had a strong “usually/movements not governments” challenge, but supported it mostly with assertion and an unsupported “9 out of 10” claim rather than concrete corrective examples. A’s case was somewhat clipped and also overgeneralized, but it had clearer mechanisms and better-grounded examples.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s most decisive rebuttal was that pre-existing elite corruption in Hungary did not make Orbán a healthy corrective; it showed how populist anger can be leveraged into permanent institutional capture, turning B’s symptom argument into support for the warning-sign framing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins because it better leverages the "usually" qualifier by highlighting that most populist movements never gain power and instead prompt elite reform, directly countering PRO's focus on rare victories. Both conceded decay precedes populism, undermining PRO's causal claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal 1 effectively flipped PRO's Hungary concession and highlighted the false dichotomy.
