### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by preserving the core Pigouvian case while answering uncertainty and leakage with plausible, if imperfect, mechanisms. Most importantly, B's central dilemma was economically incomplete: lump-sum rebates can protect household income without erasing relative-price or producer-side incentives. B raised serious nutrition, measurement, and implementation concerns, but its alternatives were not shown to address the full harms as effectively, and it underanswered A's point that low-income communities already bear substantial hidden costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to the compensation dilemma was decisive: targeted transfers need not restore meat's false price, while producers, importers, and uncompensated consumers still face incentives to reduce harm. This directly weakened the premise of B's closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by systematically dismantling Side A's compensatory mechanisms. B effectively uses A's responses to the pressure questions to prove that A's model would maximize price hikes for the poor while neutralizing the demand-side environmental benefits. B also successfully defends against A's "zero price" accusation by offering concrete alternative policies that do not punish consumers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal brilliantly synthesizes A's pressure question answers to show that A is proposing a regressive tariff on cheap protein while defunding the exact innovation needed to actually lower emissions.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive debate by making the decisive clash clearer: the motion’s “even if” clause plus leakage and uncomputable “full” pricing left PRO defending either a neutralized price signal (once rebates protect the most price-responsive buyers) or a real grocery hit on iron/protein with weak environmental payoff. PRO’s status-quo-externalities and compensation framing was strong, but repeatedly needed bolted-on border machinery and revenue recycling that CON turned into administrative and dual-use revenue problems; CON’s intermediate tools kept a cleaner path without routing the bill through poor households’ baskets. Opening clip cost CON some polish, not the comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s compensation dilemma (B4.1/B5.2): full protection kills the signal where demand is most elastic; thin protection activates the motion’s regressive food-price cost.
