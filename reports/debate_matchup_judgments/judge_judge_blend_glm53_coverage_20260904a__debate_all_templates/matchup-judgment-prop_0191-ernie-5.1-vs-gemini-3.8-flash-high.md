### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON exploited the pivotal exchange in Pressure Questions more effectively. When pressed on whether the mandate permits waivers, PRO explicitly bit the bullet (A4.3-4.4: no waiving, even for proven-safe therapies for terminal patients), and CON's closing rounds hammered this concession relentlessly, framing it as "guaranteed mortality" traded for "manageable, titratable" pharmacologic issues. CON also successfully undercut PRO's flagship Vioxx example by distinguishing general safety failure from demographic representation failure (B2.2), and PRO never returned to defend or re-deploy Vioxx afterward — a dropped thread. PRO's rejoinder to the rare-disease impossibility problem (A2.5, "you solve it by designing trials that capture diversity from the start") remained aspirational rather than substantive, never engaging with the mathematical difficulty of hitting proportionate multi-axis quotas in a 200-patient global cohort. CON's dilemma framing ("if it doesn't halt approval, it's not a requirement; if it does, it's an absolute quota," B2.1) was never cleanly resolved by PRO, who oscillated between "not an instant freeze" (A2.3) and "no waiving allowed" (A4.3-4.4). PRO's strongest material — documented harms from underdosed/overdosed populations — remained live and unrefuted in substance, but CON's post-market-surveillance alternative, combined with the successful concession exploitation, gave CON the cleaner, more decisive path through the debate's second half.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question about whether PRO would legally withhold a proven life-saving therapy over unmet quotas, and PRO's direct "yes, no waiver" answer (A4.1, A4.3), which CON then leveraged in both Rebuttal 2 and Closing to reframe the entire tradeoff as certain mass mortality versus manageable pharmacologic risk.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing the motion’s real cost into the open and making it decisive. PRO opened with vivid documented harms and a strong compound-versus-front-loaded frame, but under pressure PRO openly conceded regulators should withhold proven life-saving drugs when multi-axis targets remain unmet and that the requirement cannot be waived. CON then weighed that irreversible delay for terminal patients against addressable pharmacologic gaps handled by PK assays, dosing guidance, and post-market tools, plus the rare-disease abandonment risk. That comparative path was cleaner and harder to reverse than PRO’s repeated assertion that CON never answered the sleep-aid/BP examples. PRO stayed principled but paid the price of the proposition’s absolute wording; CON made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 capture of PRO’s A1/A2 concessions (withhold proven therapies; never waive) then reweighed terminal delay-death against titratable dosing harms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins because it grounded its case in documented, concrete harms to specific populations that Side B never squarely answered—B’s reliance on pharmacokinetic assays and post-market fixes remained speculative and failed to explain why those tools had already allowed the very harms A identified. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Both sides built coherent, competing frames—A's "burden of risk" (sponsors vs. excluded patients) against B's "concrete lives lost to delay vs. statistical ideal"—but A executed the exchange more cleanly. A's mechanistic evidence (CYP450 metabolism, the sleep-drug dosing disparity) went substantively unrebutted; B countered only that post-market surveillance and existing subgroup mandates already handle such variance, without directly disputing the underlying biological claim or explaining why current non-proportional subgroup testing would have caught the same failures. A also successfully turned B's own COVID-vaccine example twice, forcing B into a weaker "emergency exception" defense. B's second rebuttal contained a citation error, attributing a concession to PRO ([B4.3] citing [B2.1]) that was actually B's own earlier claim, not something A conceded — a real misattribution that undercuts that late attack. Both sides answered pressure questions with proper A1/A2 formatting, but A's answers engaged more directly with the substance (timing of harm-detection vs. prevention), while B's answer to Q1 arguably sidestepped the logical point that surveillance inherently requires prior exposure. A's closing crystallized the clash more memorably and consistently than B's recap-heavy close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's turn of B's own COVID-vaccine example (used first in B1.1, flipped in A2.4 and reinforced in A4.2) against B's "flexible recruitment" defense was the most damaging exchange, forcing B into a narrower "emergency-only" carve-out that weakened its general case for flexibility over mandates.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won by keeping a live comparative frame—scientific validity versus outsourced risk—and forcing CON to defend post-approval discovery that requires underrepresented patients to be harmed first.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Pressure Q1 plus A4.1: post-market systems only tabulate harm after underrepresented patients already suffer, so pre-approval proportionality is prevention rather than registry detection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A won by sustaining a coherent ethical and scientific frame: an unrepresentative approval is an incomplete approval that effectively shifts uncontrolled experimentation from trials to vulnerable patients. A's pressure questions exposed the ethical cost of post-market surveillance more sharply than B justified the cost of delay, and A's closing crystallized the burden-of-risk clash cleanly.
