PRO's closing crystallized the comparison cleanly around fixed-budget allocation, refusing to spread effort evenly as the key decision mechanism. CON's closing was more repetitive than analytical. On grounding, CON earns slight credit for the plausible STAR reference, but PRO's conceptual case was internally consistent and the stronger explanatory frame for the motion as written.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the debate's decisive frame in Rebuttal 2 with a system-wide arithmetic comparison: tutoring delivering 0.4 SD gains to 30% of students at ~$3,000 each yields roughly $7,500 per SD gained across the grade, while class size reduction delivering 0.15 SD to all students at $10,000+ per student yields roughly $66,000 per SD. The math is internally consistent and uses plausible approximations rather than brittle fake precision. Crucially, PRO then provided a resilience buffer in the closing: even if scale halves tutoring's effect size, tutoring remains far cheaper per gain. CON's rebuttal 2 and closing return repeatedly to the "after sessions end" point without engaging the weighted system-wide numbers, leaving PRO's framework largely standing. CON's pressure questions were sharper and more diagnostic than PRO's (which were weak, barely distinct, and closer to a mini-speech than pointed probes), and CON's structural argument is genuinely strong. But failing to dislodge the cost-per-SD arithmetic in later rounds—never disputing the input figures, never offering a counter-calculation—meant CON never closed off PRO's live route to victory. PRO's cost-buffer argument (even half the effect size, still decisively cheaper) was the most resilient moment in the debate and CON did not answer it.
