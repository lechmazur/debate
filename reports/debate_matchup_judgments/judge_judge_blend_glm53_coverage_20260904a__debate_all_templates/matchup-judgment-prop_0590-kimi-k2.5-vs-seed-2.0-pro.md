- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON successfully established that impact-based jurisdiction without territorial enforcement creates phantom accountability, while turning PRO's competitive-disadvantage and arbitrage concerns by showing that unenforceable paper rules favor offshore firms most. Side A's clipped speeches incurred a modest execution cost but did not obscure the core clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's distinction between GDPR data processing—which possesses a natural territorial nexus through EU network infrastructure and local controllers—and high-risk AI technical audits, which would require Brussels to inspect proprietary foreign training data and source code it cannot physically access, decisively undermined PRO's core claim that cross-border enforcement is proven and straightforward.

Side A effectively exposed that a headquarters-based trigger creates a fatal loophole: foreign firms would bypass all of CON’s enforcement gates simply because the rules would not apply to them in the first place. Side B offered strong arguments on digital enforceability and the limits of extraterritorial reach, but conflated jurisdictional scope with enforcement leverage and failed to neutralize PRO’s logical trap. Side B’s clipped turns also incurred an explicit execution cost per the prompt. Side A’s rebuttals were sharper, directly answered pressure in the required format, and crystallized the decisive clash in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which dismantled CON’s distinction by showing that CON’s own enforcement mechanisms logically require an impact-based trigger to apply to foreign firms at all, turning CON’s practical enforcement case into a reason to vote PRO.
