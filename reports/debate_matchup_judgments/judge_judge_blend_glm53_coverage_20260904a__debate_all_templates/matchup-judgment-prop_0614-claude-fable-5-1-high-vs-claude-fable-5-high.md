- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): CON showed the proposition is structurally misaligned with the harm it targets: most documented Pegasus victims were in democracies untouched by the ban, while a categorical ban would drive vendors to offshore shells beyond the reach of Entity Lists, lawsuits, and license revocation. Because the only hooks that have actually bitten depend on keeping vendors inside democratic jurisdiction, CON’s route held up as the cleaner path to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s opening salvo that Pegasus thrived under conditional licensing, and CON’s counter that the post-atrocity remedies only worked because the vendor sat within democratic jurisdiction—meaning a ban that pushes trade offshore eliminates the very leverage both sides rely on.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO won the verification and status-quo clash. CON answered Q1 with vendor-side phone-home controls but that still requires trusting the vendor and catches harm only after detection, while A answered the offshore-flight Q1 with durable chokepoints (US courts, cloud, dollar clearing, passport liability) that CON conceded are real. CON's scope point that many victims were in democracies is strong but PRO correctly framed it as a floor objection, not a justification to keep arming worst regimes. Clip penalty on B4.6 weakened CON's close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.3: licensing as failed status quo – Pegasus was licensed, not smuggled, and CON's best success (Entity List blacklisting) is itself a ban imposed after journalists were hacked and Khashoggi killed, so CON's toolkit concedes PRO's principle and only differs on timing.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the "Entity List" blacklisting in Rebuttal 1 and 2, successfully reframing Side B's best example of regulatory leverage as a post-atrocity penalty that actually concedes the failure of the status quo licensing regime.
