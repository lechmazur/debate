### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON built a cleaner, more internally consistent case: if a credit is unverifiable, no percentage should legitimize it; if it is verifiable and durable, its location outside the corporate boundary doesn't erase its climate value. This logical wedge, planted in the opening ("if fake, no percentage makes it acceptable; if real, an arbitrary cap needlessly discards it"), was never squarely resolved by PRO. PRO's strongest material—the additionality-is-unverifiable critique and the moral-hazard/lock-in story—was powerful early, but the Pressure Questions round exposed a real tension: if offsets are "permanently unknowable," why does PRO's own cap still permit them below a threshold? PRO's answer reframed the cap as being about the "seriousness" of a claim rather than physical tonnes, which CON then exploited decisively ("the value-chain boundary can matter for accountability, but it is not a climate metric"), turning PRO's own concession into a weapon. PRO also introduced a specific "5–20%" range without grounding, a minor brittle-precision slip, while CON stuck to generic, defensible regulatory mechanisms (metering, registries, buffer pools, liability). Compounding this, PRO's opening, first rebuttal, and second rebuttal were all clipped for exceeding word limits, costing PRO visible content in three of five turns — a real execution penalty under the judging rules. CON's closing crystallized the clash cleanly; PRO's closing restated its thesis without fully repairing the exposed inconsistency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question ("why does an allegedly unverifiable offset become acceptable below that line but intolerable above it?") and PRO's reframed answer, which CON then turned back with "it only shifts abatement to more expensive or less effective places... it is not a climate metric" — this exchange most clearly exposed the structural weakness in PRO's cap rationale.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won a close, high-quality clash by forcing the decisive comparison onto integrity versus geography. PRO’s structural case (unreliable counterfactuals make unbounded offset claims hollow; a residual-only share forces real in-chain transformation) was coherent and well crystallized, but never escaped CON’s core trap: if credits are too unknowable to count, a small allowance still counts fiction; if they can be made accountable via metering, registries, buffers, and liability, excluding verified out-of-chain tonnes is not a climate metric. CON pressed that contradiction through every round, answered pressure with concrete quality tools rather than magic certainty, and kept hard-to-abate residual scale live against PRO’s “small share” bridge. PRO’s multiple clipped turns and more repetitive late framing left CON with the cleaner path to decide the written proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained “cap only rations fiction” reply (esp. A2.1/A4.3) to PRO’s unverifiability premise—showing the allowance still rests on the same counterfactuals PRO says cannot be fixed.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO kept two independent pillars alive: additionality is structurally unverifiable at scale and permanence risk differs from long-lived industrial CO2, and unchecked offsets enable pathway lock-in. CON admitted “no mechanism can make every counterfactual certain” and its quality tools still let a net-zero label rest mostly on paper. PRO’s cap-as-firewall answers the arbitrariness charge – small share covers true residual and forces the bulk of the claim to be real in-chain cuts – while CON’s “if fake, 0% should count / if real, location doesn’t matter” ignores that residual must be tolerated but not allowed to dominate. Clipping cost for A keeps it razor-thin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Rebuttal 1 [B2.1-B2.2] – “rationing fiction” – sharpest turn, forcing PRO to defend why any unverifiable credit is tolerable; PRO’s A4.1-A4.2 firewall reply then becomes the deciding recovery.
