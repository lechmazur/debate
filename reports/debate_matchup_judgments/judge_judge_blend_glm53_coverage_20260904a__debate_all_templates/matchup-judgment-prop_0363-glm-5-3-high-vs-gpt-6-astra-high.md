### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully isolating the mid-budget tier as a unique structural dead zone due to P&A costs, and systematically dismantling Side A's pipeline arguments by showing that renewal is better sourced from low-budget films or acquisitions. Side A argued well that predictability does not equal higher expected value, but B cleanly demonstrated why the specific mechanics of mid-budget production carry uncompensated risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A2), which brilliantly flipped Side A's desire for "development control" by pointing out that acquisitions bound downside risk, making the lack of development control a feature you pay a premium to avoid, rather than a bug.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): CON won by keeping the burden on PRO to justify a spending shift and showing PRO never met it. PRO framed the debate around long-term portfolio value and planting vs. harvesting, but when pressed twice for the hit rate or expected value that would make a mid-budget original beat a franchise sequel after marketing exposure, PRO explicitly declined to offer one. CON turned this into the decisive clash: predictability is only a bad investment criterion if the uncertain alternative is actually priced, and PRO only offered "uncertainty is not worthlessness." CON also landed the acquisition trap—if buying finished films satisfies the motion, PRO's case for riskier theatrical production collapses—and showed renewal historically flows from low-budget discovery rather than the mid-budget dead zone. PRO's closing remained conceptually coherent but under-defined, allowing CON to crystallize that the reform ended at the status quo. The marketing-break-even logic stayed live on CON's side, while PRO's rebuttals, though nimble, never supplied the comparative arithmetic needed to make the marginal-dollar case credible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and following pressure exchanges exposed that PRO could not name the hit rate that would justify mid-budget expected value against sequels, and that PRO's acquisition concession either swallowed the motion or dissolved the production-spending case.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A built a highly persuasive economic case about the "commercial dead zone" of mid-budget films, effectively using the reality of marketing costs to show why the risk-adjusted returns are poor. Side A also successfully trapped Side B on the issue of acquisitions and micro-budgets, demonstrating that IP renewal is cheaper and safer outside the mid-budget tier. Side B argued well for the abstract need for new IP but struggled to justify why the mid-budget segment specifically warranted more spending, and their inability to counter the marketing arithmetic left their case vulnerable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's answers to the pressure questions, pointing out that Side B's "selective expansion" was just the status quo and that their refusal to price the hit rate left them with "benefits asserted, costs conceded, arithmetic declined."
