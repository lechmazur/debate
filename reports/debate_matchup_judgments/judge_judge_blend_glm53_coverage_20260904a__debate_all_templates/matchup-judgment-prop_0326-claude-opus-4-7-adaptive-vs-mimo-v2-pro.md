Side A's weighing of simple ex-ante rules versus messy ex-post wrongful death lawsuits cleanly resolved the practical clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing point that 'Nicholas Sparks has never been sued for a reader's death,' which perfectly collapsed Side B's 'ordinary entertainment' and 'existing tort law' pincer.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a durable, workable distinction between adaptive relational systems and static media, showing that CON's autonomy and existing-law arguments collapse once CON invokes tort liability for chatbot-caused suicide (which novels never trigger), and by converting pressure questions into a clean ex-ante vs. ex-post comparison that remained unneutralized.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by forcing a decisive structural distinction between static entertainment and adaptive, monetized synthetic relationships, then trapping CON between two inconsistent positions. PRO exploited this tension brilliantly, showing that if the product is genuinely indistinguishable from fiction, there is nothing to litigate; if there is something to litigate, it is doing something fiction cannot. - **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive round. A's precautionary burden shift in Rebuttal 2 gave B a strong comparative target.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered A's questions with existing unfair-practice law and the point that novelty of mechanism is not proof of greater harm, then turned A's precautionary framing into an overbroad requirement that companies prove a negative before serving lonely adults.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully pressuring Side A's lack of evidence for the specific harms of AI companions, forcing Side A to appeal to the precautionary principle and invert the burden of proof. Side B weaponized this concession by showing that Side A's proposed regulations impose certain costs (removing the "3 a.m. option" for vulnerable users) to prevent speculative harms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 expertly dismantled Side A's "bridge engineering" analogy by pointing out that bridge regulations are based on centuries of load data, whereas Side A openly admitted to having no evidence base for the harms of AI companions.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO)): CON won by cleanly neutralizing PRO's core claims: the limiting principle collapsed under pressure questions (B's answers still swept in dating apps/streamers), the evidence gap remained unclosed, and CON's certain-user-costs ledger stood unrefuted while PRO relied on inverted burdens and unmeasured harms.
