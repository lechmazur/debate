B also won the policy comparison through asymmetric transition risks, though its broad claims about fixed budgets and cheap preparation were somewhat overstated.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by distinguishing AI from earlier task-specific automation: when copyable cognition can also supply the additional output created by lower prices, demand expansion need not restore headcount. Side A offered strong historical analogies but never fully answered that distinction and repeatedly attacked a stronger “will” standard even though the proposition says “likely.” B also better weighed the policy lag: waiting for conclusive net-loss data undermines early preparation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response that cheaper output can be filled by more model inference, while a small number of verifiers does not restore the displaced junior workforce, directly weakened A’s elasticity and human-sign-off defenses.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by clearly distinguishing the mechanism of AI from past automation, successfully arguing that when cognition itself is cheap, the model fills the expanded demand rather than requiring proportionate human hires. Side B argued effectively from history but made an unforced error by repeatedly misquoting the resolution as requiring proof that AI 'will' eliminate jobs, which Side A rightly corrected.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 smoothly dispatched Side B's pressure questions by correcting B's misquote of the motion ('likely' vs 'will') and showing how B's demand for a completed census logically defeats the prompt's mandate to 'prepare early.'
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the likelihood burden from the proposition text and explained why this wave differs: cognition itself is copyable, so extra output can be filled by more inference not more hires. CON (A) relied on ATM/spreadsheet history but never answered who restores headcount for non-liability roles like copywriters/translators and why verification from 20 to 3 isn't still a net cut. Its demand for a finished census misreads "likely" and leaves the lag/policy prong intact for B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 [B2.1-B2.3] and Pressure Q1 — ATM automated a mechanical step leaving humans to sell mortgages, while generative AI writes the memo itself; cheap drafting therefore fills extra demand with models, not hires — which directly damaged A's core elasticity [A1.3-A1.4] and was never repaired in A4.1.
