### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON built a more durable case by exposing that the motion's plain wording ("any undisclosed AI-generated photo or AI-written assistance") is far broader than PRO's defended examples of outright fabrication, and by pressing the practical unenforceability of disclosure. PRO's answer to the metadata/detection pressure question conceded that platforms would rely on an honor-system self-report, which CON immediately turned into a devastating point: bad actors ignore honor systems while honest ESL/neurodivergent users get stigmatized as "deceptive" for using accessibility tools. PRO never adequately closed the gap between its "enhancement vs. fabrication" line and the proposition's actual breadth, and its rebuttals largely restated the same distinction rather than engaging CON's continuum analogies (makeup, friends drafting bios, professional photographers). CON's closing crystallized this clash cleanly, while PRO's closing reasserted its consent framing without resolving the enforcement and overbreadth problems CON had raised.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's answer to its own pressure question (B3.1/A4.1 exchange) forced PRO to concede that disclosure would rely on self-reporting rather than detection, which CON then leveraged in Rebuttal 2 to show the rule fails against bad actors while punishing honest accessibility users—this was the most decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing the proposition’s overbreadth: it brands ordinary, accurate aids (ESL polishing, lighting correction, neurodivergent scaffolding) as deception while real fabrication is already covered. PRO’s “can fabricate” examples never justified the universal presumption. Enforceability collapsed when PRO conceded self-reporting; CON cleanly weighed that this punishes honest users and leaves bad actors untouched. Continuum-of-curation framing and consent-to-the-person (not unaided self) gave the clearer decisive path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 turning PRO’s self-report concession against the case while rejecting the “unaided self” standard
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B effectively dismantled Side A's case by exposing the overbreadth of the proposition and the critical weakness of Side A's enforcement mechanism. Side B successfully argued that Side A's reliance on an honor system fails to stop bad actors while actively stigmatizing honest users who rely on AI for accessibility (e.g., ESL or neurodivergent daters). Furthermore, Side B clearly distinguished between fraudulent catfishing, which is already banned, and benign AI assistance, leaving Side A to defend a rule that practically only impacts well-intentioned users.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it capitalized on Side A's concession regarding self-reporting to demonstrate that the proposed enforcement mechanism would merely create a culture of paranoid suspicion that punishes honest users while bad actors easily bypass it.
