### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): CON built a more coherent and durable distinction (excludable/reversible noise vs. non-excludable/cumulative air pollution, stock vs. flow, event-based vs. ambient measurement) and sustained it under pressure. Crucially, when PRO answered the pressure questions by biting the bullet on night delivery restrictions and facade dB caps, CON turned that concession directly into proof of its central warning ("that's the truck ban I warned about," "fine-farming of the corner bar"), and PRO's closing never squarely answered that specific turn, instead retreating to a broader "equal seriousness" reframe. CON also pressed the equity point (enforcement inevitably lands on bars/contractors while state highways escape) more concretely, including a plausible mechanistic rebuttal about speed limits barely reducing tire noise. PRO's opening and health/equity framing were strong, and its pressure-question answers were direct and well-formatted, but its rebuttals relied more on assertion ("chronic noise accumulates like bad air") than on dismantling CON's physical/economic distinctions, and it left CON's sharpest late turn unaddressed. CON's vivid, memorable framing ("you can close a window on a jackhammer... you cannot close your lungs to PM2.5," "lungs have no door") also made the stakes easier to grasp without sacrificing substance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's move in Rebuttal 2/Closing turning PRO's own admitted sanction (night delivery restrictions after repeated breach) into confirmation of CON's predicted harm ("your answers prove why equal urgency fails... no concrete after 4pm, freight forced into morning school traffic") was the most decisive exchange; PRO never directly neutralized this in the following speech.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion's hard parts live: same urgency plus binding traffic limits. PRO proved real harm and under-enforcement, and forced CON to name workable construction/commercial limits, but repeatedly softened "same urgency" into "seriousness, not headcount/mortality" and admitted night-delivery/route sanctions that matched CON's economic prediction. CON's excludability, reversibility, stock/flow, and highway-physics/enforcement-incidence comparisons better explained why air-style urgency is the wrong regulatory transplant, and its closings crystallized triage without dumping new evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5 turning B4.1's 55 dB freight sanctions into an admission that "equal urgency" means night delivery bans and construction shutoffs.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by proving that the physical and public-health differences between noise and air pollution—reversibility, excludability, and vastly lower mortality—make "same urgency" a triage error. Side A effectively exploited Side B's admission that binding traffic limits would require night delivery restrictions and route bans, validating CON's economic-damage warnings and showing enforcement would hit small city-licensed targets rather than dominant highways. Side B's health-equity case remained live but could not overcome the comparative burden that equal urgency implies equal emergency response and shutdown power, which Side B could not justify without severe, conceded tradeoffs.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO built a coherent causal case (chronic acoustic stress mirrors chronic particulate harm) and translated it into concrete, differentiated tools (facade limits, cheap loggers, time-zoned caps), directly answering CON's insulation and equity points with the vivid "gas mask vs. smokestack" framing. The pivotal exchange came when CON conceded that logger-enforced midnight caps and permit-based source controls are legitimate binding measures, then tried to rescue the debate by insisting these are merely "targeted" rather than true air-quality-style "parity." PRO exploited this concession sharply, pointing out that air-quality law is itself zoned, timed, and source-differentiated, so the targeted/ambient distinction CON relies on does most of its work by definitional fiat rather than substance. CON's strongest surviving point—that noise doesn't aggregate across an airshed the way particulates do, so a point reading can't represent citywide exposure—was real and never fully dismantled, but by the closing CON was mostly restating this distinction without engaging PRO's rebuttal that air rules are equally source/zone-specific. CON's clipped opening (an execution fault under the stated rules) also cost it some ground. Both sides answered pressure questions competently in correct A1/A2 format, but PRO's answers were more concrete and load-bearing for its case, while CON's answers, though intelligent, ultimately reinforced a narrowing retreat rather than a rebuttal. PRO's closing crystallized the clash more effectively, making the "you already conceded the substance, only dispute the label" framing the cleaner one to adjudicate on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's move in Rebuttal 2 (A4.4) turning CON's own concession—that logger-enforced caps and permit conditions are legitimate binding controls—into evidence that CON had already granted "parity" while merely renaming it, was the decisive exchange; CON's closing never fully recovered from having to keep asserting a targeted/ambient distinction without rebutting PRO's point that air law is itself differentiated by source and zone.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): CON won by locking the debate to the motion as written: parity/same-urgency architecture with air quality. PRO’s health and polluter-pays case was strong, but later rounds kept conceding or collapsing into differentiated, time/zone/source-targeted tools that CON already accepted; CON’s exposure-mismatch and tools-vs-task rebuttals kept that gap live through closings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R2 (A4.1–A4.5): shared sensors do not equal air-style ambient task because noise does not mix/travel like particulates, so PRO’s endorsed loggers/caps remain targeted source enforcement rather than the motion’s parity claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won because it successfully absorbed CON's acceptable noise controls into its framework and showed they are the same architecture as air-quality regulation. CON's "category error" argument depended on treating air law as a flat ambient ceiling, which PRO showed it is not. B maintained the cleaner live route: binding limits, differentiated monitoring, and automated enforcement are parity in action, and CON never fully escaped the concession that these tools work. The exposure-mismatch point remained live but was dulled by PRO's "reference receptor" answer and the reframe that same disease pathway demands same polluter-pays urgency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing showing that air-quality law is itself differentiated by time, zone, and source, thereby collapsing CON's claim that targeted noise limits are not parity. This reframe turned CON's strongest distinction against them.
