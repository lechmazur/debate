### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but B built the more durable analytical frame: the distinction between "involvement" and "commitment," and the claim that the real alternative to cohabitation is not blind marriage but continued dating with clarified intentions. A leaned heavily on the "cohabitation vs. catastrophic divorce" comparison, but never squarely answered B's repeated point that this sets up a false dichotomy (B2.1, B4.6-4.7). Crucially, in Rebuttal 2, B turned A's own pressure-question answer against it: A listed "aligned goals and mutual timelines" as the markers distinguishing real commitment from convenience, but B correctly noted these markers don't require cohabitation at all, undermining A's foundational claim that cohabitation is the "only testing ground" (B4.4). A's final closing recapped its prudence framing but did not resolve this specific contradiction, leaving B's sharpest rebuttal unanswered. B's closing also sustained the decisive distinction more clearly, while A's closing mostly restated earlier points without new leverage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4, where B uses A's own pressure-question answer (aligned goals/mutual timelines as commitment signals) to show those signals don't require cohabitation, undercutting A's "only testing ground" claim and going unanswered in A's closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by keeping the decisive clash clean: involvement/dependence is not the same as reciprocal durable commitment, and “safer than divorce” answers the wrong baseline versus serious dating with separate households. B’s prudence/trial case was rhetorically strong but repeatedly underweighted inertia and treated cohabitation as if it itself established intent. A’s answers after pressure questions and the A4 weighing crystallized that gap more cleanly than B’s closing reframed it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4): firm plans, not the shared address, answer weak-commitment concern; B’s own commitment signals are either available without cohabiting or are the disputed entanglement, so the trial can create dependence while leaving the promise unresolved.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by analytically dismantling Side B's core "trial run" argument. A successfully distinguishes between increased dependence/involvement and actual commitment, pointing out that cohabitation creates exit barriers (inertia) that can trap incompatible couples. Crucially, A exposes B's false dichotomy—that the only alternative to cohabitation is a "blind leap" into marriage—by establishing that couples can clarify intentions and observe each other while maintaining separate households. A's closing line, "Becoming harder to leave is not the same as deciding to stay," perfectly crystallizes the debate and secures the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantles Side B's answers to the pressure questions, pointing out that B's signals of commitment (timelines, integrating finances) can be established without cohabiting, and that B's defense of the "trial" relies on the false baseline that the alternative is immediate marriage rather than continued separate dating.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases with vivid framing, but A landed the decisive conceptual move: separating "weaker than marriage" (a comparison of formal status) from "weak commitment" (a judgment about character/behavior), and using it to expose that CON's whole "institutionalized hedge" framing conflates degree of formal obligation with sincerity of intent. A reinforced this with the reductio that dating and even non-binding engagement share the same "conditional exit" structure CON condemns, which CON never resolved—its answer to the engagement question simply reasserted "zero-cost exit = hedge" without explaining why that logic doesn't equally indict engagement itself. CON's repeated "spousal privileges without spousal responsibility" line was rhetorically punchy and its structural-trap/inertia mechanism was plausible, but across rebuttals it mostly restated the same assertion rather than answering A's distinction between deliberate, honest cohabitation and drifting/deceptive cohabitation, dismissing A's safeguards proposal as "textbook fantasy" rather than engaging its mechanism. A's closing crystallized this gap cleanly; CON's closing repeated its core framing without closing that gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1) separating "weaker than marriage" from "weak commitment," which exposed CON's core definitional conflation and was never squarely answered—CON's Rebuttal 2 response ("distinction without a difference") merely reasserted the claim rather than refuting it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the proposition’s social-treatment standard live: cohabitation can be ordinary/reasonable preparation rather than proof of weak commitment. A’s “institutionalized hedge” frame was punchy and consistent but too absolute; it could not absorb the engaged, mutually intentional case without treating timing as more diagnostic than conduct. B answered pressure cleanly, separated stages from character, and left the cleaner comparative reason to reject blanket stigma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s A1/A2 on engagement-as-preparation and “fewer obligations ≠ weak commitment,” which neutralized A’s structural definition without conceding the drift/trap concern.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully exposing Side A's overly rigid definition of commitment. While Side A delivers highly polished rhetoric and a strong critique of the structural inertia of cohabitation, Side A's case falters when pressed on the common scenario of engaged couples moving in together. Side A's insistence that engagement lacks legal binding and therefore moving in is still a "hedge" feels disconnected from social reality. Side B effectively argues that an earlier stage of commitment is not a "weak" one, and that treating cohabitation as ordinary allows for responsible preparation. Side B's logical dismantling of A's all-or-nothing view secures the win, despite A's rhetorical flair.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the engaged couple example, pointing out that Side A conflates fewer formal obligations with weak commitment and mistakenly treats an earlier stage of a relationship as a defective version of the final one.
