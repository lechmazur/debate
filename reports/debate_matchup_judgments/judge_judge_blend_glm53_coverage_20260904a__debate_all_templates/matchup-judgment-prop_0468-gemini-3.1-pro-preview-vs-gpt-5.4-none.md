### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B built the stronger overall case by consistently pressing the manipulation paradox, anonymity problem, and "rather than mainly experts" language in the motion, none of which Side A fully neutralized despite repeated attempts at rebuttal. Multiple clips for Side A further weakened execution on key turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's point that even an "input" still forces reliance on experts to interpret motivated price moves, turning the motion's own wording against Side A.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO wins by a narrow margin because it successfully defended the motion’s actual wording—using markets as a formal input rather than mainly relying on panels—and established that markets add falsifiable, adversarial price discovery that internal forecasts lack. CON’s manipulation argument remained viscerally potent but never fully overcame PRO’s comparative that panel capture is cheaper, stickier, and less visible; meanwhile CON’s claim that expert interpretation of markets defeats the motion relied on an overly strict reading of “rather than rely mainly.” PRO’s repeated word-limit clips were a significant execution cost that keeps the margin narrow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal that deep-pocketed actors willing to take trading losses to secure policy outcomes will overwhelm profit-seeking traders, rendering the price a reflection of money rather than truth (B2.1/B4.3), and PRO’s counter that distortion in markets is at least costly and traceable while panel distortion hides behind institutional authority (A2.2/A4.2).
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): PRO wins by establishing a cleaner comparative framework on capture and accountability. While CON's manipulation and Goodhart arguments were forceful, PRO effectively mitigated them by clarifying that "formal input" does not mean automatic triggers, and by turning the capture debate: market distortion is costly, visible, and contestable by sophisticated counter-traders, whereas panel capture is cheaper, hidden, and institutionally sticky. PRO directly answered pressure questions, consistently weighed why a quantified external signal improves decision-making even when imperfect, and crystallized the clash around contestability versus bureaucratic opacity. CON repeated the deep-pockets lobbying point across rounds without fully answering PRO's comparative turn, leaving PRO with the more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 directly answers CON's pressure on manipulation by reframing it as a sustained financial tax that invites counter-trading from firms and specialists, then sharply weighs this against CON's idealized view of expert panels. This comparative turn—visible, contestable distortion versus cheap, hidden panel capture—materially damaged CON's live route and anchored PRO's closing.
