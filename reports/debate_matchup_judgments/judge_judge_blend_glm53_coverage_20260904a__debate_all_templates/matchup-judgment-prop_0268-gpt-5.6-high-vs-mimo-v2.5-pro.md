### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully proving that shrinkflation warnings require subjective, historical product-matching that is fundamentally more complex than Side A's analogies to existing inventory systems or ingredient labels. Side B also effectively argued that such flags would selectively dramatize one pricing tactic while missing standard price hikes, whereas universal unit pricing provides a more objective metric.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 distinction between manufacturer-supplied current facts (ingredient labels) and retailer-constructed historical comparisons completely dismantled Side A's attempt to equate the two compliance burdens.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by keeping the decisive clash on reliable information versus selective historical judgment and never letting it be neutralized. PRO opened with a clean, vivid market-failure case and light-touch framing, but CON repeatedly showed that "same product," baseline, duration, and reformulation tests turn the mandate into contestable commercial biography rather than objective facts; PRO's own late defaults (UPC/brand-line matching, six-month window, "substantive" ingredient exclusion) confirmed rather than dissolved the complexity. CON's answers to pressure and its closings cleanly weighed a universal current unit-price metric against selective flags that miss direct hikes, generate loopholes, and add retailer tracking costs without proving commensurate decision value. PRO's best pressure (failed real-world unit pricing, industry opacity) landed but was answered as an argument for better enforcement of the simpler rule, not expansion into a harder one; PRO never supplied a mechanism stronger than assertion that the new mandate would magically improve the old one. Net: CON made the choice easier after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON [A4.3]/A4.5] turning PRO's own proposed UPC/brand-line + "substantive reformulation" + six-month rules into proof that the flag requires insinuation and loophole-prone line-drawing rather than objective pricing data.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B wins by analytically dismantling the mechanics of the proposed mandate. While Side A effectively highlights the failure of current unit pricing enforcement, Side B successfully argues that "shrinkflation" requires subjective historical judgments (e.g., defining the "same product" or a baseline) rather than objective current facts. Side B's critique of Side A's proposed implementation (matching UPCs or brand lines) exposes how easily the rule could be gamed or how subjectively it would have to be enforced. Furthermore, Side B wins the "selection problem" clash, demonstrating that flagging only package reductions selectively dramatizes one pricing tactic while ignoring direct price hikes, thereby distorting rather than clarifying consumer choice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 on the "same product" and inventory tracking clash. Side B sharply distinguishes between tracking sellable SKUs for inventory and adjudicating economic "sameness" for a legal mandate, effectively neutralizing Side A's claim that the data already exists in retailer systems.
