- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A built a strong, mainstream economic case (supply mechanics, Tokyo/Minneapolis/Auckland examples, absorption logic) and landed a sharp pressure question exposing tension in B's exemption argument. Crucially, A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing were both clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning A never fully completed its response to B's "next month" pressure or its final weighing argument, while B delivered a complete, sharply worded closing that crystallized the clash in its favor. This execution gap, combined with B's more effective use of the concession extracted via pressure questions, gives B the edge, though the debate remained genuinely close on substance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Question ("why do reformed cities remain unaffordable for low-wage renters") forced A into a counterfactual-only defense ("less expensive than they would be without reform"), which B then exploited in Rebuttal 2 and Closing as a decisive concession that supply-side reform helps averages but not the people the motion should be judged by.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the motion's comparative test intact: which policy increases affordability more effectively at scale. A's supply mechanism, absorption logic, and use of B's own concessions (supply eventually helps averages; new construction must be exempted) cleaner path to victory. B's urgency/incumbent frame was rhetorically sharp and often memorable, but repeatedly redefined affordability as near-term incumbent retention rather than winning the written comparison. A's word-limit clips in R2 and closing were real execution costs and kept the margin competitive rather than large.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure Q1 on the exemption contradiction (new building harms yet must be exempted) forced B into design special pleading and crystallized the live clash over whether control free-rides on the supply it criticizes.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by establishing that housing affordability is most effectively addressed at the systemic level through supply expansion rather than incumbent protection. B successfully defined effectiveness as bending the cost curve for the entire market—including newcomers and future renters—and exposed the central tension in A's case: A simultaneously argued new market-rate construction is harmful yet conceded its necessity by exempting new construction from controls. While A mounted a compelling moral case for immediate tenant protections and delivered superior rhetorical crystallization in the closing, A never fully resolved the outsider problem or showed why freezing rents for current tenants outweighs the supply constraints and two-tier market dynamics that worsen affordability for everyone else. B's rebuttals cleanly absorbed A's empirical challenges, reframed CON's concessions as decisive, and maintained the comparative frame that expanding the pie outperforms rationing slices of a shrinking one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Pressure Questions (Q1/Q2) were the decisive exchange. By asking why CON exempts new construction if building is destructive, and how rent control helps a searcher who cannot find a unit, B exposed an unresolved burden in A's framework. A's attempt to answer in R2—arguing exemptions channel investment without purging tenants—admitted supply's necessity without reconciling it with A's earlier gentrification claims, leaving B's systemic scalability argument intact.
