### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A's "signpost vs. gate" framing was the decisive move of the debate: it accepted B's evidence (NHS 111, CE-marked tools) as proof of a navigation layer while arguing the motion's actual language demands official front-door status with funding and staffing consequences. A then exploited a genuine internal tension in B's case—that tools are simultaneously "high sensitivity/cautious" and "divert significant numbers" from care—which B never resolved beyond asserting "calibrated thresholds" and "safety-first parameters" without engaging the incentive mechanism A raised (savings get banked immediately; misses are unmeasured and unattributed). A's pressure question forced this contradiction into the open, and B's answer in Rebuttal 2 was largely conclusory. B's Rebuttal 1 also suffered a format penalty for exceeding the word limit, an execution cost. B maintained a coherent, examples-grounded case and correctly used A1/A2 formatting, but its closing repeated earlier claims without ever squarely defeating the signpost/gate distinction or the calibration contradiction, leaving A's route to victory live and B's answers incomplete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 point that a tool cannot be simultaneously "high sensitivity/cautious" and a major diverter of ER visits — "pick one" — which B never cleanly resolved in later rounds.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won because the motion permits approved AI as one front door rather than requiring it to become the exclusive gate. Side A offered excellent rhetoric and a plausible warning about staffing incentives, but repeatedly treated endorsement as mandatory displacement. B directly preserved human channels and explained what official integration adds. A also overstated the tradeoff between cautious thresholds and useful diversion: calibrated triage can plausibly achieve both. B's claims about real-world safety and regulation were too confident and weakly substantiated, keeping the debate competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response that patients may choose AI as one official access route, rather than being forced through it, most decisively undermined A's signpost-versus-gate framing. A showed integration can create pressure toward atrophy, but did not establish that the motion requires or inevitably causes that outcome.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): CON won by making the decisive clash easy to resolve: signpost (navigation layered on intact human systems) vs gate (AI as endorsed front door). PRO’s best evidence, especially NHS 111, validated the signpost CON already accepted, while CON exposed the unresolved tension between “high sensitivity / err on caution” and “significant diversion / savings,” plus the incentive path to atrophy and unmeasured misses. PRO’s permissive “let patients use” reading never recovered from CON’s defaults/endorsement pressure or weak PQs; Rebuttal 1 clipping and thin answers left those burdens live. CON adapted, weighed, and crystallized cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sensitivity-vs-diversion contradiction (B2.3–B2.4) and NHS 111 signpost/gate reframe (B4.3), which stripped PRO’s core evidence of its force for the motion as written.
