### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins on the central clash of the debate. The motion's decisive word is "before," and CON consistently demonstrated that a rigid sequencing rule withholds scalable, low-barrier access from the students most likely to be left out—commuters, caregivers, night workers, students with anxiety. PRO never answered this burden with a workable threshold. The "functional baseline" response in Rebuttal 2 was acknowledged even by PRO to lack a numeric formula, making "before" effectively open-ended delay rather than principled sequencing. CON's counter to the "directory to nowhere" argument was also strong: both sides explicitly agreed campuses already have some clubs, events, and spaces, meaning digital tools function as connectors to real community, not to emptiness. This concession materially damaged PRO's core metaphor. CON also reframed "digital" convincingly—not as passive apps but as discovery, messaging, accessibility information, and routing, all of which multiply the value of existing physical investments. PRO's strongest moments were the "legible emptiness" formulation and the Q2 pressure question (if the platform sends a student to a dead club, was she reached?), which CON answered competently but not fully neutralized. PRO was hindered by multiple clipped speeches and a closing that was also clipped, reducing the crystallization benefit. CON's unclipped closing landed the cleaner comparative finish: "a bridge you can cross now beats a late promise of better scenery." The margin is clear but competitive because PRO's substantive arguments—loneliness as community deficit, digital individualizing what is social—remained live and unrefuted on their own terms; CON only showed that "before" was the wrong policy instrument for delivering them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's response to "directory to nowhere" in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2: by noting that both sides concede campuses already have active groups and services, CON recast digital tools as connectors to real existing community rather than pointers to emptiness, directly neutralizing PRO's signature metaphor and shifting the burden back to PRO to explain why sequencing still matters when the physical foundation already partially exists.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully attacking the strict sequencing of "before." When Side A defended its physical threshold by proposing an extensive build-out (late cafes, childcare), Side B expertly weaponized this to prove that Side A's sequence would indefinitely delay scalable digital access for marginalized students.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 expertly turned Side A's list of required physical accommodations (like childcare and weekend programming) into proof that Side A's "functional baseline" was actually an open-ended delay for digital access.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by establishing a clearer, more decisive route to victory: the proposition's rigid "before" sequencing imposes an open-ended delay that withholds scalable access from commuters, caregivers, night workers, and anxious students who cannot use imperfect physical offerings now. Side B's physical-first case, while grounded in the "directory to nowhere" problem and the irreplaceable value of real contact, never supplies a workable threshold beyond a vague functional baseline and treats flexible physical expansion as an immediate substitute rather than another delayed project. Side A directly answered the live pressure questions by rejecting any blanket sequence in favor of marginal-need allocation and by showing that digital tools amplify existing (even partial) clubs and services rather than replacing them. Side B's responses restated the ideal but left the "why delay the first reachable contact" burden unneutralized. The clash on reach versus substance remained live through closing; Side A framed the tradeoff more cleanly as harm to the hardest-to-reach students.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 answers on thresholds and dead-club reach most damaged Side B's case by exposing the lack of any stopping point for "before" and by reframing digital as coordination that activates what already exists rather than a replacement.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won on the strength of a structural dependency argument that CON never fully neutralized. The "front door to what?" pressure question was the decisive exchange: CON's answer—that students self-organize digitally and platforms help cohere them and "pressure institutions to fund rooms"—inadvertently conceded that physical rooms are the ultimate goal, which is exactly what PRO was arguing. The "front door without a house is not inclusion; it is branding" line crystallized this clearly and was never squarely answered. PRO also landed cleanly on the "detection is still reaction" point: CON argued digital systems catch early withdrawal signals, but PRO correctly identified this as downstream, not upstream, of the isolation PRO's clubs prevent from forming in the first place. CON's strongest argument—that structurally excluded students (commuters, caregivers, anxious students) need digital as their primary belonging space—was genuine and created real pressure, but PRO handled it adequately by clarifying the motion means "before expanding" rather than "instead of," and noting basic digital infrastructure already exists. That answer is not fully satisfying for the hardest cases, but it is sufficient within the motion's scope. CON's repeated invocation of those students was compelling rhetoric but began to feel circular after PRO had already conceded digital tools can play a supporting role. PRO maintained tighter structural coherence across all rounds, and the closing crystallization was sharper and more decisive than CON's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Pressure Question 1 and its Rebuttal 2 answer together form the most decisive exchange. PRO asked "what community is the platform opening onto when clubs and spaces haven't been funded?" CON's answer—that students self-organize and platforms cohere them and help them pressure institutions to fund physical rooms—inadvertently confirmed that physical rooms are the ultimate good, making PRO's prioritization argument self-evidently correct. PRO then drove this home with "your case never escapes dependence on ours," which CON's clipped closing did not successfully rebut.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing digital platforms as secondary tools ('a front door without a house') that rely on the existence of a funded physical community. Side B made a strong case for non-traditional students who rely on digital spaces, but suffered severe word-count clipping in almost every speech, which hampered their ability to cleanly resolve A's central critique. A's sharp rhetoric and disciplined execution secured a solid win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they turned B's 'front door' metaphor against them by arguing that a front door to an unfunded, empty campus is 'not inclusion; it is branding.'
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by establishing that digital tools presuppose the irreplaceable embodied community created by in-person infrastructure, directly answering pressure questions on exclusion and upstream prevention while showing CON's sequencing risks a map to an empty campus. Side A counters with equity for non-traditional students but fails to show standalone digital durability, and clipped turns reduce clarity without neutralizing the core comparison on finite budgets and dependence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's rebuttal 2 answers: A1 notes basic digital already exists so next funds prioritize physical landing pads; A2 stresses gateway needs the in-person "house" for real belonging, exposing CON's reliance on self-organizing community without substance.
