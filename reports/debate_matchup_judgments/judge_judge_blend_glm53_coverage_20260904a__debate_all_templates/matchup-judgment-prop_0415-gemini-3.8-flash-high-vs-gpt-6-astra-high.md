- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO built the more precise mechanism: resident priority operates as a reallocation of bookings within an unchanged conservation-safe capacity, not an addition of bodies, which directly defused CON's central "wear and tear" and "peak-season starvation" arguments. CON never fully dismantled this framing; its best counter—that turned-away tourists simply spill into surrounding streets—shifts the argument from site-level fragility (the motion's actual subject) to city-wide congestion, and was met adequately by PRO's point that residents denied entry are also still in the city. CON's revenue-cannibalization claim also leaned on an unsupported generalization (that "local day-trippers never match" tourist ancillary spending), which PRO correctly flagged as unsupported, while PRO's own "or" clause reading (priority without discount) survived scrutiny. On the demand-smoothing exchange, PRO's Pressure Question about symmetric inflexibility (tourists' fixed windows vs. residents' school/work schedules) exposed a real asymmetry in CON's reasoning that CON's answers (residents have "365 days," "dozens of shoulder weekends") never squarely resolved, especially given PRO's opening point that school holidays are peak season for both groups. CON's closing mostly recapped the ecosystem-vs-spoils-system framing without directly answering PRO's sharper "conservation sets the ceiling, not the queue" reframe. Both sides argued cleanly with plausible, ungrounded-in-fake-citation reasoning, but PRO's tighter mechanism and more responsive rebuttals gave it the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 clarification that resident priority reallocates existing bookings under fixed conservation caps rather than adding visitors (B2.1), which neutralized CON's opening wear-and-tear argument and was never fully answered except by a tangential street-spillover claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins because it successfully showed that resident priority can operate within existing conservation caps without adding visitors or necessarily cutting prices, preserving tourism revenue while recognizing residents' reciprocal claims.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of B's spillover and schedule-flexibility arguments—showing that denied residents also remain in the city and that year-round proximity does not equal year-round availability—was the turning point that left B's core comparative objections unresolved.

Furthermore, PRO's pressure question regarding prioritizing wealthy tourists exposed the weakness in CON's reliance on ancillary tourist spending. PRO's closing crystallization that "conservation sets the ceiling; it does not settle the queue" decisively framed the clash.
