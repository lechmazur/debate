- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner, more durable case. The decisive structural point—that every regulatory tool CON invoked (thresholds, adaptive management, liability, monitoring) logically presupposes a baseline to function—was introduced in B2.4-B2.6, sharpened by B3.1's pressure question, and never squarely answered by CON. CON's response was that control sites and statistical replication already address patchiness, but PRO correctly identified that spatial controls cannot substitute for temporal before-and-after data at the actual site; CON never closed that gap. PRO also neutralized CON's most powerful counter (the land-mining tradeoff) on two independent grounds: the causal link from pause to expanded land harm is speculative rather than guaranteed, and the ISA's obligation to govern the commons responsibly is not absolved by harms on land. CON's use of specific projections (cobalt demand 4-6x by 2030, recycling meeting under 10% of demand) carried brittle precision in a closed-book setting and overstated certainty in ways that weakened epistemic credibility. PRO's functional clarification that baselines apply to specific contract areas rather than the entire Pacific effectively rebutted CON's "permanent ban by another name" argument. The clipping of PRO's Rebuttal 2 was a real format cost but did not materially reduce the live argument, since the key answers appeared before the clip. CON's closing largely recapped earlier points without sharpening the comparison. PRO's closing crystallized the core question cleanly: without a reference condition, the ISA cannot detect, attribute, or remedy harm—and that is reason enough to pause first.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's turn in Rebuttal 1 exposing that CON's own regulatory tools presuppose the baseline they claim is unnecessary—"A threshold relative to what? A plume is manageable compared to what background spread?"—was the debate's most damaging hit. It undermined CON's entire operational model in one move, and CON never produced a satisfying answer across the remaining rounds.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): The decisive clash was on two linked questions: whether CON's proposed regulatory framework can function without pre-operational baselines, and whether PRO's stopping condition is workable. CON identified the workable-stopping-rule gap in the opening and maintained it all the way to the closing. PRO's most specific answer—multi-year datasets across multiple trophic levels, habitat structure, ecosystem processes, certified by ISA expert panels—still left unanswered who decides when that checklist is finally complete rather than extended by one more season or taxon. CON exploited this in Rebuttal 2 by turning PRO's own A1 answer against them, arguing that describing such a broad multi-variable requirement in a vast, changing, underexplored ocean is a recipe for endless contestation, not a finite administrable threshold. CON also sustained the operation-specific argument throughout: some risks (real plume behavior in real sediments) are qualitatively different from mesocosms and validated models, meaning PRO's pause cannot end without the controlled pilot activity it forbids. PRO's counter-argument that "you cannot enforce a limit you cannot measure" has genuine logical force and was never fully neutralized, but CON adequately answered that directly observable physical metrics—plume extent, sediment deposition, equipment footprint—allow conservative regulation without requiring complete ecological attribution. CON's closing framing ("precaution with an exit vs. a parking brake") crystallized the core tradeoff memorably. CON had two speeches clipped for exceeding word limits (a real execution cost), though in both cases core arguments were delivered before the clip. PRO's speeches ran consistently under word limits, leaving some rebuttals underdeveloped. The land-mining counterfactual was never knocked down with more than the assertion that the precautionary principle does not weigh ecological risks against each other, which is a thin answer to a real supply-chain consequence. On balance, CON built the cleaner, more practical path to a policy conclusion—precaution with a defined exit—while PRO never closed the gap on who decides the pause is over.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [A4.3–A4.4]: CON turned PRO's own A1 answer (multi-year datasets across multiple trophic levels, habitat structure, ecosystem processes, ISA expert-panel certification) against PRO, demonstrating that this description is not an administrable finite threshold but a recipe for endless extension—always one more survey, one more taxon, one more process—especially in a dynamic ocean. CON then noted that PRO's A2 effectively conceded the operation-specific-risk point: if real plume behavior depends on actual machinery in actual sediments, models and lab studies cannot by themselves tell the ISA when the pause should end, meaning PRO's framework has no clear exit path even on its own terms.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by establishing a clearer, more workable path to precaution through staged pilots with observable controls and exit rules, while exposing PRO's baseline threshold as undefined and potentially endless in a dynamic environment. PRO's emphasis on legal mandate and irreversibility was undermined by failure to define finite, objective stopping criteria or address how limited activity could generate needed evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which directly answered B's pressure questions on harm detection and thresholds via measurable plume controls and conservative limits, while showing PRO's multi-year criteria enable indefinite contest and that operation-specific risks require limited trials to resolve.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by exposing that PRO's standard—pause until "robust, independently verified baselines exist"—is vague, inherently expandable, and likely paralyzing in a vast, underexplored, changing ocean. CON offered a concrete alternative (limited pilot approvals, contemporaneous controls, automatic stop rules on directly observable variables) that better satisfies precaution without an indefinite freeze. CON also landed the operation-specific point: real plume behavior and equipment disturbance can only be learned through controlled activity, yet PRO's pause forbids the very operations needed to generate that evidence. PRO's ethical and legal framing was strong, but they never adequately answered the land-mining counterfactual or provided a clear, non-arbitrary stopping rule, undermining the workability of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure questions and subsequent rebuttal (A3/A4) showing that PRO's baseline criteria are infinitely contestable and that operation-specific risks require actual pilot activity to resolve—directly undermining the feasibility of a blanket pause.
