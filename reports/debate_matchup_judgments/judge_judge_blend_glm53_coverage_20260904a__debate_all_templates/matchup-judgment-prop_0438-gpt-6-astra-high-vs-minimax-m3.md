### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's enforcement mechanism, proving that self-classification either creates loopholes or requires the exact same fact-intensive regulatory review Side B claimed to avoid.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5) brilliantly exposed Side B's scalability dilemma: regulators must either blindly trust a brand's self-classification or verify it by examining the actual message, defeating the triage benefit.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a dilemma at the heart of PRO's remedy: if categories are self-reported, brands will game the soft "wellness" bucket while keeping medical implications; if regulators verify the classification, they must still conduct the fact-intensive message-by-message inquiry PRO claims cannot scale. CON also showed that direct claim-specific disclosures (e.g., "not shown to treat acne") give teens more actionable guidance than broad categorical labels, and collapsed PRO's ownership boundary by noting that paid influencer partnerships can be equally persistent and parasocial. PRO's "operating system" framing was elegant but depended on a false timing distinction—classification is merely a seller statement, not pre-publication verification—leaving CON's cleaner alternative intact after the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's triage dilemma (B4.5/B5.2): self-classification invites gaming, while verification requires the same interpretive inquiry PRO says is unscalable, proving the category label does not actually move enforcement earlier.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON wins by showing classification is neither necessary nor sufficient: medical label doesn't prove evidence, cosmetic/wellness labels don't convey safe use, and ownership boundary misses identical parasocial harm from paid promotion. PRO's infrastructure/triage claim collapses under CON's dilemma that self-labels must still be verified for content. A also replaces "no info" strawman with more informative claim-specific disclosures, giving the third-party reader a cleaner path to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's dilemma — if regulators trust self-classification it can be gamed, if they verify it they must examine the message anyway — neutralizes PRO's scalability and timing advantage.
