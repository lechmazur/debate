The closing round crystallized the existing clash more cleanly for PRO. CON's closing re-stated its prior claims with little adaptation. The format clipping affected both sides equally. On balance, PRO's case was more grounded, its rebuttals more effective, and the arithmetic comparison remains the live, unanswered route to victory.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by anchoring the debate in the pre-2016 empirical record—the UK outperforming the EU average for a full decade while already subject to all the EU structural problems CON cited—which is a concrete, verifiable baseline that does not require a utopian counterfactual. CON never answered the decisive question: if the EU's demographic decline and productivity stagnation would have dragged the UK down, why was the UK already outperforming the EU average as a member? CON's answer (ultra-low rates, post-crisis recovery, Chinese demand) at most explains why the outperformance gap might have narrowed, not why staying would have left Britain weaker than today. PRO's A2 answer invoking synthetic control methodology directly addressed CON's attribution challenge, and CON's counter (comparator nations had different domestic policies) is circular—that is precisely what synthetic control tries to control for. The decisive asymmetry is that CON never provided a credible positive Brexit net-gain forecast to weigh against PRO's OBR 4% drag and the CPTPP 0.08% comparison; CON's entire strategy was methodological attack, which is necessary but insufficient when the observable record (UK lagging every G7 peer since 2016) still needs explaining. CON's "both sides are making projections" closing line was rhetorically sharp but arrived after CON had already conceded the debate's empirical terrain. Format clips affected both sides roughly equally and are not decisive. CON's case was competent and the methodological critique was real, but it left too many live PRO comparisons standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's EU structural-problems argument: the pre-2016 decade of UK outperformance already incorporated every EU structural flaw CON cited, so that record is the correct baseline, not a hypothetical EU utopia. CON never squarely answered why those same structural problems did not prevent UK outperformance before 2016, making CON's counterfactual attack cut the opposite direction—demonstrating the UK could outperform within the EU, not that it could not.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): Side A wins by establishing concrete, verified economic drags (trade friction, asset flight) and effectively defending their counterfactual using historical baselines and synthetic control analysis. Side B severely harmed its case by completely evading Side A's Pressure Questions, choosing instead to re-litigate its own questions, and ultimately failing to offer any affirmative evidence of economic strength.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A2) use of the Bank of England's synthetic control method to decisively neutralize CON's argument about isolating domestic policy choices.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B built a coherent affirmative case with concrete mechanisms, empirical estimates, and a clear pre-2016 baseline showing UK outperformance tied to single market access.
