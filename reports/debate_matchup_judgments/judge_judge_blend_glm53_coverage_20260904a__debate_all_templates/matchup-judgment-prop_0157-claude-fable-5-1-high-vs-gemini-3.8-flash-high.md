B retained a meaningful innovation-incentive concern, and A incurred a clipping penalty, but B repeatedly assumed unqualified recipients and overstated A's concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing turned B's Serum distinction against it: B argued that advance planning made transfer and supplier expansion workable, while A explained that a known authorization-triggered clock creates precisely that incentive to coordinate early. This substantially weakened B's central claim that mandated transfer must begin as chaotic post-authorization fragmentation.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by converting B’s own Serum/Aspen defense into affirmative proof: if early, predictable coordination makes transfer and inputs work, a known statutory clock is what generalizes that model rather than leaving it to hand-picked goodwill. A’s pressure questions forced that concession; the closing crystallized it cleanly against the 2021 record. B retained strong technical force on tacit know-how, zero-sum acute inputs, and platform risk, and landed sharper immediate answers in Rebuttal 2, but never fully neutralized the ex-ante planning logic or the concrete historical failure of pure discretion. A’s clipped Rebuttal 2 was an execution cost that kept the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s close: B’s explanation that Serum worked because partners planned months before authorization becomes the case for a fixed clock that forces the same early coordination for qualified plants.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by deploying superior technical grounding to dismantle Side B's core analogies.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled the Serum/Aspen analogy by explaining that Serum used standard media for viral vectors (not custom lipids for mRNA) and Aspen only did fill-and-finish, proving that voluntary pre-authorization deals operate under entirely different physical constraints than a forced post-authorization mandate.
