### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A wins a very tight debate by maintaining a crisp, defensible distinction between speech (utterance) and reach (platform amplification), and by executing a brilliant strategic maneuver on measurability. Side B introduced excellent mechanisms, particularly the one-way liability ratchet, but Side A successfully defused them by arguing that accidental default-privacy for adults is merely a classification error they can opt out of, not censorship. Side A's adoption of Side B's own falsification metrics to satisfy the proposition's 'measurability' burden cleanly closed off Side B's migration critique.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Pressure Question 1 and subsequent adoption of Side B's own falsifiability metrics in Closing to satisfy the measurability burden was a devastating and highly effective debate maneuver.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by controlling the definitional center of gravity. The proposition requires showing harm reduction without significantly increasing censorship, and A consistently maintained that content-neutral architectural defaults (private accounts, no behavioral ads, no open DMs) are not censorship because they delete no posts, block no viewpoints, and prohibit no utterance. B's attempt to equate reduced algorithmic reach with censorship was rhetorically powerful and exploited A's own concession about the power of defaults, but it never fully overcame the intuitive and linguistic gap between audience architecture and state suppression of speech. A also successfully absorbed B's pressure on measurability by adopting B's own proposed off-platform falsification tests (surveys, helpline data, law enforcement data) and pairing them with independent audit of platform telemetry, neutralizing B's "theater" accusation. B landed real blows on the sticky-default tension and the over-classification ratchet, but these were framed as costs or errors rather than as censorship, leaving A's core claim intact after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.3 and the subsequent A2.1-A2.4 exchange, where B developed the argument that reach is speech on modern platforms and A countered that withdrawing algorithmic amplification is not silencing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully challenging Side B's narrow definition of censorship. While Side B relied heavily on the semantic claim that "zero posts are deleted," Side A persuasively argued that on modern social media, reach and audience are intrinsic to speech. Side A demonstrated that a government-mandated private default paired with a "scare screen" opt-in effectively suppresses the voices of vulnerable minors who need public reach. Furthermore, Side A delivered superior rebuttals regarding the one-way ratchet of age-verification liability and the flaws in Side B's platform-internal measurement metrics, exposing Side B's mid-debate patches as insufficient to carry the burden of the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the pressure questions in Rebuttal 2, where they clearly explained the asymmetry in age-verification errors (minors lying upward to evade restrictions versus platforms over-classifying ambiguous users downward to avoid liability) and provided concrete external metrics to falsify harm migration, effectively neutralizing Side B's traps.
