### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and engaged directly, but PRO built the more resilient structural case: reciprocity (host communities/members bear costs or provide ongoing support) as a distinct, non-poverty, non-passion justification for a bounded allocation, paired with a lottery to keep money from deciding within that reserved slice. CON's strongest move—distinguishing "equal terms of competition" from "equal wallets"—was rhetorically sharp and exposed that PRO's mechanism still creates a first-claim privilege. But CON's own alternative ("loyalty can mean information, non-price perks") was repeatedly shown by PRO to be hollow: it offers no actual protected access when demand peaks, which is precisely the scenario the motion is about. PRO's answers to the pressure questions (A1/A2) were substantive and directly addressed the tension between rejecting the lottery's imperfections while defending it, whereas CON's answers, though clear, restated the "different defects" framing without fully resolving why an in-group first-claim isn't itself a form of protection worth valuing. The debate stayed tightly focused on the same clash without meaningful widening, and PRO's closing crystallized the core asymmetry (protected-but-bounded vs. no protection at all) slightly more effectively than CON's closing, which mostly restated "same sale for everyone" as an assertion rather than fully defeating PRO's reciprocity framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange and subsequent A1/A2 answers in Turn 7, where PRO distinguished reciprocity-as-justification from purity-of-passion-as-justification, most sharpened the clash—though CON's Turn 8 response (equal terms of competition vs. equal wallets) was the single most rhetorically effective counter-punch of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully justifying the reservation as a "bounded obligation" based on reciprocity, while effectively dismantling Side A's premise that dynamic pricing represents "equal access." Side A lands strong hits on the imperfections of Side B's lottery mechanism, but Side B neutralizes them by demonstrating that Side A's pure market alternative guarantees exclusion for all lower-income fans while abandoning community ties entirely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly capitalize on Side A's admission that loyalty means nothing when fans are priced out, proving that Side A's framework commodifies community ties.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO won by establishing that reciprocity justifies a bounded, meaningful reservation for residents and members, and that an open dynamic sale, while formally equal, simply substitutes purchasing-power inequality for the proposed status-based allocation. CON effectively exposed arbitrariness in geographic and membership boundaries but never squarely answered why money should dominate the entire inventory or what concrete access loyal members have when peak dynamic prices exclude them. PRO’s honest concession of costs and sharper comparative weighing in the closing—showing that CON offers community relationships no protected access whatsoever—made the decisive clash clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2, where it answered CON’s pressure questions by grounding the reservation in reciprocity rather than need or passion, and showed that CON’s “equal sale” still leaves low-income locals and members unprotected because equal eligibility is not equal purchasing power.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A built a vivid, consistent civic-standing narrative ("hosts bear costs, so hosts deserve first claim") and a clean sequencing framework, but never fully closed the logical gap B kept pressing: why bearing externalities (noise, congestion) entitles someone to the entertainment good itself rather than to compensation for the burden. B exploited two structural weaknesses that A never repaired: (1) the "residents or members" disjunction in the motion is internally inconsistent once A concedes membership can't substitute for residency, which B exposed cleanly in rebuttal and closing; and (2) A's answer to B's pressure question effectively conceded that a reserve should exist "in advance" even when locals already get substantial access through general sale, which B correctly flagged as undermining the necessity claim at the heart of A's case. B's alternative remedy (purchase limits, need-based pre-sale access, direct compensation for hosting costs) offered a real comparative option that addressed A's speed/capital mechanism without requiring geographic or membership priority, and A's closing did not engage this alternative or the concession point, instead re-asserting "first claim" rhetoric. B's closing crystallized the unresolved gap precisely ("repeating 'first claim' asserts entitlement; it does not justify it"), giving the cleaner reason to vote against the motion as worded.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's catch in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) that A's own answer to the pressure question conceded organizers "should reserve tickets anyway" even where locals already have substantial access — turning A's necessity-based justification against itself and going unanswered in A's closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly decoupling the harms of dynamic markets from the necessity of a geographic reserve. Side A demonstrates that anti-bot measures and affordability can be implemented without geographic exclusion, and effectively corners Side B on the "or members" wording of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 exposes that a geographic reserve does not inherently protect Side B's hypothetical teacher from wealthier, faster local buyers, and points out that Side B's attempt to rewrite "or members" concedes half the prompt.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON successfully showed that local/member priority arbitrarily excludes equally deserving outsiders while failing to guarantee access for the struggling locals PRO highlights, and offered a cleaner alternative—purchase limits and need-based pre-sale allocations—that protects residents without relocating barriers to a geographic boundary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s demonstration that the reserve grants inventory to a category without guaranteeing attendance for the struggling teacher, exposing the "or members" overbreadth, and showing that purchase limits and need-based pre-sale remove barriers rather than moving them to the town boundary.
