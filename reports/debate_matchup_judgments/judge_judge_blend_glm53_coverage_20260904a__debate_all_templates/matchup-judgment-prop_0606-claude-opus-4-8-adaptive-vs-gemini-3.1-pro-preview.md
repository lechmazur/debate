- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): B won by exposing A's internal contradictions (price anchoring), practical failures (31bis), and reintroduced delays in rebuttals, while A's responses remained optimistic without mechanisms.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 exposed A's anchoring surrender and 31bis rarity; B4 sharpened the contradiction and wrong-target point.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing internal contradictions in PRO's case. PRO's own claim that firms would price "just below the line" confirmed the threshold would become an upward price anchor, while PRO's reliance on WTO sanctions to prevent abuse reintroduced the slow diplomatic delays PRO had condemned as lethal. CON also showed that Article 31bis import mechanisms have proven nearly unusable in practice and that most essential-medicine deaths stem from off-patent infrastructure gaps rather than patents, making the automatic trigger a mis-targeted sledgehammer that threatens innovation without reliably saving lives. PRO's moral urgency could not overcome these structural and logical defects.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's identification that PRO simultaneously argued firms would strategically price "just below the line" [B1.4] and that monopolies already charge the absolute maximum [B5.2], exposing a fatal contradiction: either the threshold is ineffectual or it actively raises prices on currently affordable medicines.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins by exposing a fatal internal contradiction in PRO's core economic mechanism. PRO initially argued the threshold would incentivize firms to price "just below the line," which CON correctly reframed as a published target that would anchor currently affordable drugs upward. When PRO later pivoted to claiming monopolies already charge the maximum (making the threshold only a ceiling), CON cleanly trapped them: either the threshold does nothing, or it raises prices. CON also successfully grounded the manufacturing debate by noting the historical failure of TRIPS Article 31bis, forcing PRO to rely on optimistic speculation and WTO sanctions—the very slow diplomacy PRO condemned. PRO's moral urgency was clear, but CON's analytical rigor, consistent framing, and superior handling of pressure questions secured a decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing exchange on price anchoring. By highlighting PRO's shift from "firms will price just below the line" to "monopolies already charge the maximum," CON demonstrated that PRO's mechanism either inflates prices on competitive drugs or is functionally useless. This logical trap dismantled PRO's economic case and crystallized the debate around CON's comparative advantage.
