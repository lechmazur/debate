### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with real sophistication, but B built the more resilient throughline. B's core claim—that transient noise is structurally unprovable under the status quo and that certified logs are at least contestable, whereas vanished events are not—was never actually rebutted by A's proposed fix ("more inspectors, faster complaint routes"), which does nothing to solve the timing problem B raised from the opening and hammered again in closing. A's strongest move, that a certified log doesn't just prove an event but effectively becomes "the scale of reasonableness" and is asymmetrically rebuttable by wealthier parties, was clever and sustained across rounds, but B's answers (foundation requirements, burden staying on the proponent, ordinary lay explanations like absence or nearby construction rather than expert acoustics) blunted much of that force. A's insistence that any rebuttal requires "expert work" looked increasingly like assertion rather than demonstrated mechanism. B's opening was clipped for length, a real execution cost, but the substantive content delivered was largely intact and the later rounds compensated with clean A1/A2 answers and disciplined closing. On balance B gave the cleaner, more resolvable comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's "photograph proves presence; a decibel log proves the wrong itself" move in Rebuttal 1 was the sharpest single point of the debate, but B's closing reassertion that CON's only fix (faster inspections) still cannot capture noise that stops before anyone arrives was the point A never actually answered, and it decided the exchange.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained a live path that certified logs solve the transient-noise proof gap while staying contestable. CON argued logs = unreasonableness and require experts, but PRO answered with concrete foundation requirements, placement disclosure, limited collection, and burden staying on complainant. CON's alternative of more inspections repeats the same vanishing-event failure PRO identified, and its equity/privacy concerns were mitigated by PRO's safeguards rather than proving a need to restrict evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2 answering A's expert-burden and secret-dossier questions: foundation with photos/calibration/windows plus layered notice turning the use case from expert decoding to ordinary factual rebuttal, exposing CON's deference claim as assertion.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it successfully defends the practical necessity of the evidence for transient noise and provides concrete, non-expert mechanisms for rebuttal (e.g., proving absence or nearby construction) that defuse CON's "evidence arms race" fear. CON's rhetoric is exceptionally sharp ("the legal wrong rendered in units"), but its claim that challenging a log inherently requires expensive experts is overstated and effectively countered by PRO's examples of ordinary factual defenses. Furthermore, CON's alternative to certified logs ("more inspectors") fails to solve the core problem of vanishing noise, leaving PRO's "reviewable error vs. vanished events" asymmetry as the decisive weighing mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing rebuttal to CON's claim that sworn recollection is testable, pointing out that testing a witness's consistency is not the same as testing whether the transient sound actually occurred, effectively neutralizing CON's defense of the status quo.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON built the debate around a single, sustained dilemma: either certified monitoring evidence is properly founded (placement, calibration, chain of custody, completeness), in which case existing evidentiary rules already permit it to be weighed, or the proposal is quietly asking to lower those foundational requirements. PRO never cleanly escaped this trap. In Rebuttal 2, PRO's answer to the pressure question ("neither... admitted to be weighed, not obeyed") effectively conceded that no exclusionary rule needs to change, which CON exploited hard in Rebuttal 2 and the closing ("PRO never identified a rule barring such evidence"). PRO's positive contributions—certification standards, disclosure duties, quiet-nights-as-vindication—were rhetorically strong and added real texture, but they mostly described a new procedural regime (multi-sensor checks, neutral inspection, mandatory disclosure) rather than defending the narrower motion of expanding use of a single neighbor's certified device. CON also kept pressing the asymmetry point (complainant controls placement/excerpts, accused cannot reconstruct past conditions) and the informal-landlord-sanction problem, and PRO's proposed fix (disclosure/use conditions) was correctly identified by CON as an add-on regime rather than something inherent to "certified" evidence itself. Both sides handled the Pressure Questions format correctly with labeled A1/A2 answers, but CON's answers more directly reinforced its case while PRO's answer inadvertently strengthened CON's central dilemma. Overall CON's rebuttals were tighter and more corrosive to PRO's live route to victory, giving CON the clearer path to the decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "either unnecessary or a lowering of safeguards" dilemma (A2.1-A2.2), which PRO's own answer to the pressure question ("neither... admitted to be weighed") failed to resolve, allowing CON to press the concession through Rebuttal 2 and the closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) kept the decisive attribution gap alive throughout. Certified accuracy does not certify location, source, or unreasonableness, and privacy-safe level-only logs cannot resolve that, which undermines PRO's pattern solution. A also showed the expansion is either unnecessary - properly founded records are already weighable - or a lowering of safeguards, especially in informal landlord decisions without cross-examination. PRO's claim that A's A4.1 concession wins the motion failed because A clarified current rules already allow that weighing. PRO never identified a rule that categorically excludes a truly complete, neutrally placed record.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 A1/A2 - properly founded records already admissible, placement logs are the proponent's own account not neutral verification, and level-only data cannot prove source/unreasonableness, so "more use" is either redundant or lowers safeguards.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A built an inescapable logical trap regarding evidentiary standards: if the noise data is properly founded and authenticated, it is already admissible under current rules; if it is not, allowing it "more" inherently means lowering evidentiary safeguards, which risks unjust housing sanctions based on unattributed decibel spikes. Side B struggled to escape this dilemma, attempting to redefine "certified" to include a broad procedural regime (full disclosure, neutral inspections) that Side A correctly noted was external to the evidence itself. Side A's rhetoric was also sharper, perfectly distinguishing between a "certified instrument" and "certified proof."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they crystallized the dilemma that properly founded evidence is already admissible, meaning "more use" inherently requires lowering safeguards, effectively neutralizing Side B's core mechanism.
