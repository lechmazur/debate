### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): The decisive clash was CON's "proximity" mechanism: because legal supply remains open indefinitely while an ever-growing cohort of legally-barred adults lives, works, and socializes alongside legal buyers, proxy purchasing is uniquely easy and durable compared to a teenager's evasion problem under ordinary age laws. PRO's central rebuttal—that asking a spouse or friend for cigarettes creates meaningful "friction"—never squarely answered why that friction would hold for decades against a spouse or coworker with constant, casual access, especially once CON sharpened this in Rebuttal 2 and Closing with the "leak built into the design" framing. PRO's answers to the pressure questions (A1/A2) conceded diversion will happen but asserted, without much mechanism, that shrinking legal-buyer numbers outweigh growing barred-buyer numbers—this is asserted rather than demonstrated, and CON explicitly flagged this asymmetry (pool of buyers shrinks arithmetically slower than the pool of proxy-eligible relationships grows) without a real answer. CON also better weaponized the equality objection, converting it from an abstract complaint into a comparative burden ("proof of substantially greater prevention... PRO offers assumption, not proof"), which reframed the debate as a evidentiary weighing question that PRO's uncertain "our advantage is fewer initiations, not perfect abstinence" language could not satisfy. PRO's case was coherent and used the Q&A round reasonably, engaging directly with A1/A2 in proper format, which is a real strength, and its opening built a clear mechanism (permanent recruitment ban vs. temporary age gate). But across rebuttals, PRO increasingly conceded ground on diversion/proxy risk while asserting rather than proving that prevented initiation outweighs it, and never neutralized CON's "legitimacy cost" argument (spending ID-check credibility on carding 40-year-olds undermines the child-protection rationale that makes age laws work). CON's closing crystallized the clash cleanly and did not merely recap; PRO's closing also crystallized but rested on the same unresolved assumption. On balance, CON provided the sharper, more clearly won exchange on the decisive mechanism (durability of the barrier given proximity to legal buyers), giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to PRO's "shrinking pool" framing—arguing that the pool of legally-barred adults expands relentlessly and lives in close proximity to legal buyers, making proxy purchasing a durable social favor rather than a fading friction—directly undercut PRO's central prevention mechanism and was never adequately rebutted.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): B built the clearer path to victory by making the live clash decide on structure, not slogans. PRO’s prevention story—prospective barrier, friction against initiation, grandfathering, seller-side enforcement—was coherent and stayed disciplined. But CON repeatedly forced the harder comparison: a dual adult market that keeps legal retail open for decades beside an expanding excluded cohort is a built-in proxy and diversion machine, not a shrinking problem that merely trims benefits. A’s “purchasing inequality ≠ unequal standing” and “barred adults are not automatic demand” answers never fully neutralized that design critique, the ease of adult social diversion versus teen barriers, or the legitimacy hit of carding middle-aged adults. After the exchange, B’s case left the more decisive reason: certain permanent adult dual-track costs plus uncertain net prevention do not beat equal-adulthood tools that already cut smoking.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1/2 turn that the legal-buyer pool’s contraction is outrun by expanding excluded adult demand living next to open lawful supply—creating lifelong arbitrage rather than genuine phaseout—most damaged A’s “contracting supplier pool = net prevention” route.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): PRO established a clearer comparative advantage: a permanent barrier to commercial recruitment that prevents dependence without criminalizing users, while CON’s visceral equality and enforcement frames relied on assuming barred adults would automatically generate proportional illicit demand. PRO’s A4 reversal that a cohort containing more people who never start does not automatically sustain an arbitrage machine proved decisive, and its closing crystallized the clash effectively by showing prevention need not expire on a birthday.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4’s dismantling of CON’s “arbitrage machine” by showing that a cohort with more people who never start does not automatically generate the illicit demand CON assumed, shifting the burden back onto CON to prove displacement would overwhelm prevented initiation.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely coherent cases: B offered a plausible causal mechanism (aging peer-supply networks, a shrinking rather than replenishing legal pool, pragmatic grandfathering) with memorable framing ("winding down to zero" vs "refilling forever"). A's strategy of pressing the actual burden — does reduced legal access translate into reduced prevalence, and does that gain justify permanent unequal treatment of otherwise comparable adults — stayed sharp and consistent across every round. Crucially, A's closing exposed an unrebutted equivocation at the heart of B's central metaphor: "lawful sales wind down by definition; smoking does not," a distinction B never squarely closed despite repeating the metaphor rhetorically. A also kept pressure on the equity asymmetry (older nonsmoker free to start vs. younger dependent adult permanently barred), and B's answer ("statistical ghost" vs "addiction trap") asserted the tradeoff was worth it rather than resolving the underlying fairness objection. B's peer-network mechanism was the strongest point in the debate and A conceded it has some deterrent value, but A's persistent distinction between "fewer legal buyers" and "fewer smokers/addicts" was never fully answered, leaving a live logical gap in B's affirmative case at the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing line distinguishing "lawful sales winding down" from actual smoking prevalence directly undercut B's central "winding down to zero vs refilling forever" metaphor, and it went unanswered since it landed in the final speech.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A won by making the decisive comparison stick: conventional limits permanently refill youth peer supply, while a cutoff steadily widens the supplier gap and shrinks the legal base without overnight prohibition. B landed real pressure on adult equality, lifetime scope, and grandfathering inconsistency, but repeatedly conceded the teen mechanism and never showed age-only rules stop industry replacement well enough to defeat the phase-out trajectory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's peer-supplier/conveyor-belt reply: once no near-age cohort can buy legally, close school/friend resale withers rather than resetting yearly—materially damaging B's claim the barrier is unchanged.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clearer live route to victory: generational bans uniquely sever the peer-supply conveyor belt that sustains youth initiation, while conventional age limits permanently replenish it. Side B landed real pressure on proportionality and grandfathering inconsistencies, but never squarely neutralized A's attrition mechanism or showed that age restrictions could match the generational cutoff's trajectory toward ending recruitment. A framed the comparative choice as "leaks while winding down to zero versus leaks while refilling forever," crystallizing the clash more cleanly across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, which defused Side B's enforcement and equality objections by distinguishing retail recruitment from social attrition, exposed the double standard in applying resale fears, and reframed "temporary unevenness" as a necessary transition cost to stop manufacturing new victims.
