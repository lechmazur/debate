### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion on scaled cost-effectiveness, not cheapness. PRO’s upstream/surgical cost story was clear, but CON imposed the live burdens—scaling dilutes credible messengers, CVI partly borrows police leverage, and impulsivity weakens voluntary mediation while strengthening incapacitation—and PRO never fully discharged them. CON’s numerator weighing (cheap misses of high-risk actors are not cost-effective) made the ballot easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s impulsivity flip: PRO’s acute/non-rational retaliation story strengthens arrest/incapacitation over voluntary outreach (A2.1/A4.2).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing two unresolved burdens in PRO's case. First, CON showed that scaling CVI beyond small pilots dilutes the scarce credible-messenger capital on which the model depends, so PRO never proved the per-dollar shooting reduction survives citywide expansion. Second, CON turned PRO's own premise of impulsive, non-rational violence against PRO: emotional actors in acute feuds are poor candidates for voluntary mediation, making coercive incapacitation the more reliable prevention mechanism. PRO kept asserting an upstream/downstream cost gap but never demonstrated that scaled CVI preserves effectiveness or substitutes for enforcement, while CON crystallized that a cheap program missing the highest-risk clique is merely cheap, not cost-effective.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn of PRO's impulsive-violence premise: showing that emotional, retaliatory actors are least likely to pause for voluntary mediation, making incapacitation the more reliable prevention mechanism, and that PRO's own description of gun violence therefore undermined CVI's core mechanism rather than supporting it.
