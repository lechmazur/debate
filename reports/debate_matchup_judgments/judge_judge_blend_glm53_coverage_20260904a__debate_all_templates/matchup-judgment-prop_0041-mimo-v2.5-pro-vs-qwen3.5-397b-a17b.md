### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side A built and defended a coherent, principled case across all rounds while systematically dismantling Side B's core mechanism. The three decisive exchanges: (1) Side A correctly neutralized B's foundational aviation example by distinguishing time-based pricing from individualized same-moment pricing, forcing B into a weak "empirically contested" retreat. (2) The categorical-vs.-algorithmic distinction survived intact—A's transparency and group-verifiability line was never adequately answered; B's "precision lowers inclusion costs" response conceded the economic logic but ignored the transparency harm entirely. (3) B's own admission that consumers need browser extensions and incognito modes to detect personalized pricing [B2.8] directly confirmed A's information-asymmetry argument, and A's Q2 exploited this perfectly. The central subsidy mechanism—that extracting more from high-willingness buyers lowers prices for budget shoppers—was B's load-bearing claim, but B never demonstrated a concrete redistribution channel. Yield management applies cleanly to perishable-seat airlines; its extension to general retail pricing was asserted rather than shown. B's closing "access vs. exclusion" framing was rhetorically effective but rested entirely on the unproven subsidy premise. A's clipped speeches are a real format cost but the visible portions already secured the main arguments. B's rhetoric was punchy ("PRO's fairness is a luxury tax on the poor") but the underlying logic remained structurally dependent on the undemonstratable subsidy story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's neutralization of the aviation example in Rebuttal 1 [A2.2–A2.3] was the most decisive single exchange. A correctly identified that airline dynamic pricing is time-based and uniform at the moment of purchase—exactly the kind of pricing A explicitly excluded from the ban—turning B's strongest concrete example into inadvertent support for A's position. This stripped B of its most intuitive real-world illustration and forced B to rely on abstract yield-management theory for the rest of the debate.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): A won by making the decisive clash clearer: secret individualized pricing undermines comparison shopping and enables profit-maximizing extraction, while the ban preserves transparent category-based and market-based pricing. B had a strong access-versus-exclusion frame, but its subsidy claim remained too asserted and overgeneralized from yield management; it never fully answered why algorithms would reliably help vulnerable consumers rather than exploit them. A had execution costs from clipped turns and some bad reference labels, but still did more direct damage to B's core mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal distinguishing transparent discounts and time-based pricing from secret individualized price-setting, then turning B's browser-extension point into evidence of opacity, was the most decisive exchange.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side A wins by systematically dismantling Side B's "cross-subsidy" argument. A persuasively argues that profit-maximizing algorithms are designed for pure surplus extraction, not charitable redistribution, and successfully distinguishes individualized secret pricing from legitimate inventory-based yield management or transparent categorical discounts. Although Side A suffered execution costs with multiple clipped speeches, A's core rebuttals were delivered cleanly before the cutoff and decisively won the clash on market transparency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response in Rebuttal 2 highlighting that profit-maximizing algorithms do not contain a "redistribution clause" to pass extracted profits down to budget shoppers.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): CON wins on the structural enforcement catch that proved decisive. When pressed by CON's Q1 (loyalty member vs. first-time visitor), PRO answered by pivoting to a disclosure principle: "regulate for disclosure: if discount criteria aren't public, it's banned." CON correctly identified this as PRO quietly converting the ban into a transparency mandate—the same policy CON had already endorsed. PRO's closing tried to recover ("the ban targets inference, not agreement"), but never escaped the structural contradiction that their own best enforcement standard was indistinguishable from CON's preferred approach. The motion asks for a ban, and PRO's own live position had drifted to something softer by Rebuttal 2. CON also sustained the economic argument—that price-sensitive buyers currently receiving algorithmically lower prices would pay more under uniform pricing—which PRO dismissed as "a few lucky algorithmic winners" without engaging the systematic economic logic. PRO's consent distinction and moral framing (price tag as a lie, market integrity, insider-trading analogy) were genuinely compelling and kept the debate competitive throughout. PRO's insulin/desperation example was vivid but CON correctly noted emergency gouging is already separately illegal. The two format clips in CON's opening and closing are real execution costs but the core arguments survived intact. On balance, CON made the debate easier to decide by identifying the point where PRO's enforcement standard collapsed into CON's own position, which is a live route to victory that PRO never closed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 catch (B4.1–B4.2): after PRO answered CON's Q1 by saying "regulate for disclosure: if discount criteria aren't public, it's banned," CON immediately identified this as a transparency mandate—not a ban—and noted PRO had quietly adopted CON's own framework. This was sharp, accurate, and materially damaged PRO's live route to victory on the motion as written.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): B won by making the ban look overbroad and by keeping alive the access-cost argument: personalized pricing can lower prices for price-sensitive shoppers, while A never fully proved uniform pricing would be better under real retail competition. A had a strong fairness and trust case, especially on covert exploitation, but its later consent and disclosure carveout let B argue that A had shifted toward targeted regulation rather than the proposition's ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's decisive exchange was in Rebuttal 2, where it answered A's consent point by saying it justified disclosure rules, not a ban, and reframed the core comparison as hidden lower prices for budget shoppers versus visible but higher uniform prices.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully cornering Side A on the enforcement mechanism, forcing A to concede that transparency and consent would make the practice acceptable, which B correctly identified as regulation rather than the proposed ban. Side B also maintained a strong, unanswered economic defense of price discrimination benefiting price-sensitive consumers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 flawlessly capitalized on A's Rebuttal 2 concession ("regulate for disclosure"), exposing that A had abandoned a blanket ban in favor of B's own transparency framework.
