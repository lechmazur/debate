### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built and maintained a coherent case across all five speeches, with each round sharpening rather than widening the core comparative: survivability is the precondition for any platform's effect, and the asymmetric loss calculus (30% attrited swarm still fights; destroyed F-35 provides nothing) was never cleanly resolved by CON. PRO's "double standard" framing—crediting exquisite platforms for theoretical effects they can't deliver once destroyed while dismissing attritable systems for imperfection—landed effectively and CON had no direct answer. PRO also correctly labeled A1/A2 responses and addressed both pressure questions substantively, with the Ukraine rapid-iteration answer being particularly effective against CON's Q2 on EW vulnerability. CON's strongest contribution was the deterrence argument and the Chinese supply-chain dependency point, both genuinely sharp. However, CON never resolved the tension PRO exploited: deterrence requires adversaries to believe in platform survivability, so if exquisite platforms cannot survive A2/AD environments, deterrence is already eroding regardless of attritable maturity. CON's Rebuttal 2 being clipped is a real execution cost that left the Chinese supply chain counterargument and the deterrence point without their full development going into closing. CON's closing repeated prior claims without meaningfully sharpening the comparison. PRO's closing crystallized the existing clash more cleanly. CON's case is competitive and the deterrence argument deserved a fuller PRO response, but PRO's trajectory across the debate was more adaptive and decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 [A4.2–A4.3]: facing CON's sharpest pressure question about what specific advancement enables attritable systems in Chinese EW environments, PRO pivoted to Ukraine's documented rapid iteration (frequency-hopping, mesh networking, EW-hardened payloads fielded in months) and then reframed the comparison—not whether all drones survive jamming, but whether a distributed force where 30% of nodes survive produces more aggregate effect than a single exquisite platform once destroyed. This directly answered the pressure question, preserved PRO's core asymmetric-loss argument, and exposed CON's double standard in one move.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by reframing the decisive clash around survivability as a prerequisite for any military effect, exposing a double standard in how CON evaluated exquisite versus attritable systems, and showing that distributed, replaceable forces can absorb losses while exquisite platforms cannot. CON’s deterrence argument remained competitive but was weakened by PRO’s sharp contradiction frame and by CON’s clipped Rebuttal 2, which left critical pressure partly unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal (Turn 7), which identified the double standard of crediting exquisite platforms for theoretical effects conditional on survival while holding attritable systems to a standard of perfection, and crystallized the asymmetry that a 70% attrited swarm still produces aggregate effect while the loss of an irreplaceable exquisite platform ends the mission.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully framing survivability as a non-negotiable prerequisite for any high-end effect, which neutralizes Side B's deterrence and payload arguments. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): PRO builds the cleaner path to victory across the full exchange. Its strategic framing—A2/AD cost asymmetry makes exquisite platforms irreplaceable liabilities against saturation attacks—is grounded in real military context (Replicator, DF-21D, carrier cost cycles) and never fully neutralized. CON's best argument is cost-per-effect: cheap platforms that fail their missions produce zero effect at any price. This lands hardest on the SEAD mission dispute. But PRO's second rebuttal answer (A4.1)—attritable SEAD works through channel saturation and mission-by-mission attrition of IADS, not individual platform survival—is a plausible and specific military mechanism that CON can only respond to by asserting cheap platforms lack sensors and weapons to find and fix targets, which is asserted rather than argued. CON's contradiction charge (B2.2-B2.3: if carriers are saturation-vulnerable, escorts don't help; if survivable, the urgency collapses) is sharp and the most damaging moment in the debate, but PRO's response—attritable systems absorb fire to preserve carrier survivability rather than guarantee it—is minimally adequate. CON's reliance on existing layered escorts as already sufficient is never substantiated, while PRO's irreversibility argument (a lost $13B carrier cannot be replaced in a budget cycle; a lost $5M drone can) retains weight throughout. Both closings were clipped equally. On the decisive clash—whether the motion's "significant shift" is strategically justified even at reduced exquisite capability—PRO has more live arguments standing after the full exchange. CON's memorable framing ("losing cheaper") is effective rhetoric but rests on an unproven capability assumption about attritable systems' inability to accomplish any assigned mission.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1 answer to CON's SEAD pressure question is the most decisive rebuttal exchange. CON argued that $5M drones lack the stealth and ECM to survive SEAD missions. PRO responded that attritable SEAD operates through channel saturation—accepting individual losses to overwhelm limited engagement queues and degrade IADS long enough for follow-on assets—rather than individual platform survival. This directly answers CON's most sustained attack and offers a plausible operational mechanism. CON's counter (B4.2-B4.3: cheap platforms still need sensors and weapons to find and destroy targets) is a fair reply but amounts to an assertion rather than a demonstrated refutation of the saturation-suppression model.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO's substitution argument rests on a false equivalence between cost-per-unit and cost-per-effect. PRO successfully frames the A2/AD vulnerability of exquisite platforms, but never demonstrates that cheap attritable systems can actually accomplish the contested missions—especially SEAD and contested ISR—required to justify a significant procurement shift. CON's pressure questions and rebuttals show that drones lacking stealth, serious ECM, and adequate sensors cannot degrade integrated air defenses or persist in jammed environments, turning PRO's "mass" argument into waste. The motion's required reduction in exquisite investment therefore shrinks the only fleet capable of decisive effects, without a viable replacement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4) converting PRO's cost-per-unit logic into a cost-per-effect burden, demonstrating that attritable platforms unable to find, fix, or survive against contested targets achieve zero strategic effect regardless of price.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): CON wins by successfully framing the debate around cost-per-effect versus cost-per-unit, directly challenging PRO's assumption that attritable systems can perform high-threat missions like SEAD or saturation screening. While PRO effectively highlighted the strategic vulnerability of exquisite platforms to adversary mass, CON consistently demonstrated that cheap systems lack the physical capabilities (stealth, ECM, processing, aperture) to accomplish missions in contested environments, rendering their low cost irrelevant if the mission effect is zero. CON also better leveraged the motion's explicit trade-off, forcing PRO to defend a reduction threshold PRO could not substantiate. CON's rhetorical crystallization and sharper handling of the capability gap provided a cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing crystallization of cost-per-effect versus cost-per-unit, dismantling PRO's SEAD and screening arguments by showing that volume without capability yields zero effect, making the mandated trade-off strategically unsound.
