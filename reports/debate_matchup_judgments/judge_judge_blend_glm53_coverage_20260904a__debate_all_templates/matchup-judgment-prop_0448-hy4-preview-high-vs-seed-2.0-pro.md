PRO progressed the debate by narrowing the clash to the meaning of the motion itself, whereas CON's later speeches largely repeated their opening objections without resolving PRO's core reframes.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won because its partial-improvement and option-value framing survived: approved applicants avoid the sea journey, while unequal access and incomplete coverage do not by themselves justify withholding that route. Side A exposed serious accessibility, coordination, and backlog problems, but its claim that applicants would spend their money and then board deadlier boats remained speculative and was insufficiently weighed against those safely admitted. Side B’s unsupported exact mortality claims and failure to resolve allocation reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s answer that the policy need not abolish every dangerous leg to remove the deadliest one was decisive. Side A’s “costly queue before the boat” response was vivid, but it did not establish that this downstream harm would outweigh the crossings avoided by successful applicants.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO wins by establishing a cleaner comparative that survived the exchange. CON landed sharp, original objections—relocating peril, bureaucratic exclusion of the most vulnerable, and the unworkability of "unified"—but never squarely proved that the policy would produce net harm or more deaths than the status quo. PRO successfully framed CON as demanding perfection while offering only speculation, and crystallized the choice as partial, verifiable lifesaving against confirmed mass drowning. That framing remained live because CON's "extra deaths" mechanism relied on an attenuated, unquantified causal chain, whereas PRO's core harms were grounded and recurrent. A narrow but real edge to PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (B2.1–B2.3) showed the visa installs a queue before the boat rather than abolishing it, relocating peril and creating a new excluded class. This forced PRO to spend the rest of the debate defending partial progress against preventable death, defining the central clash.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clear and decisive comparative burden: proving the policy is imperfect is not enough to defeat it; Side B had to prove it was worse than the status quo. Side A successfully weighed a bureaucratic barrier against a lethal physical barrier, neutralizing Side B's strongest point about the most vulnerable being priced out. Side B's attempt to prove the policy causes extra deaths by forcing rejected applicants into worse boats was a logical stretch that failed to overcome Side A's core claim that removing the Mediterranean sea crossing inherently saves lives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first and second rebuttals, where they forcefully reframe Side B's critiques as merely proving the policy is imperfect rather than actively harmful, and effectively weigh the bureaucratic hurdles of a consulate against the lethal reality of the Mediterranean crossing.
