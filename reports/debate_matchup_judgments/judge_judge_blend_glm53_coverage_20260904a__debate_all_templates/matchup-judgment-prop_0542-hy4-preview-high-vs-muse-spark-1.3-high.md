### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): A won by showing that relative risk does not establish an adequate absolute threshold and that broader detention chiefly adds statistically adjacent defendants beyond those already reachable through dangerousness hearings. B powerfully weighed temporary detention against permanent violence, but repeatedly treated A's hypothetical twenty-percent rate as established, understated detention's duration and collateral harms, and never adequately bounded how many false positives were acceptable. A's nonexistent C-reference IDs and overreliance on the proxy-outcome objection reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's relative-risk response was decisive: even a substantially elevated risk can leave most flagged defendants as false positives, while B's answer relied on severity without supplying a limiting absolute threshold and later converted illustrative arithmetic into twenty assumed murders, rapes, or shootings.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON successfully exposed that broader use of risk tools would sweep in mostly false positives due to low base rates of violent reoffending, while the extreme cases are already detainable under existing dangerousness hearings. PROs moral weighing (temporary detention versus permanent harm) was eloquent but never squarely answered CONs arithmetic or the core distinction between group risk scores and individualized threat. CONs closing crystallization that for the eighty false positives detention is the certainty and violence is the speculation sharpened the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CONs Rebuttal 2 (B4), which directly answered PROs pressure questions and then landed the relative-risk illusion and identifies no one in particular critiques, showing that group odds cannot substitute for individualized proof in pretrial detention.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A built a more compelling comparative framework, effectively weighing the asymmetry of harms (temporary detention vs. permanent violent injury) to justify the increased detention rates. Side A also decisively answered Side B's pressure regarding who the "new" detainees are by pointing to geographic arbitrariness in the current system. Crucially, Side B committed a major execution error by hallucinating references to a non-existent "Side C" ([C1.2, C2.3], etc.), which severely undermined its grounding and epistemic discipline. Side A's rhetoric was sharper and its impact calculus more persuasive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's proxy-tool argument by strictly enforcing the motion's "validated for serious violent reoffending" condition, and clearly defined the "new" detainees as victims of geographic inconsistency rather than low-risk neighbors.
