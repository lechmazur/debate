### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a close, high-quality debate by keeping the comparison tied to the motion. Side A's capacity alternative still left frontline states handling the concentrated reception and processing burden, while its claim that recognized refugees could simply move lawfully elsewhere was insufficiently grounded. B also persuasively showed that a quota with financial buyouts remains a mandatory allocation system: compliant states take cases, refusers finance that burden, and frontline support becomes an entitlement rather than voluntary charity. A exposed genuine enforcement and secondary-movement problems, but did not prove that these defeat a quota binding most states.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to A's branch argument was decisive: allowing a state to discharge its allocated obligation through payment does not make the quota merely voluntary or identical to an ordinary capacity fund. The quota creates the debt, while deletion of the quota returns Greece to requesting discretionary assistance.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive comparative on the motion’s binary: CON’s capacity-only fix still leaves frontline states managing most arrivals, only better funded. CON landed the sharper enforcement and “dissolution” attacks—forcing PRO toward perform-or-pay and preference matching—but PRO recovered by showing that a priced obligation turns money into a debt owed to the overloaded rather than ad hoc charity, and that matching plus cost-shift still redistributes responsibility relative to pure Dublin concentration. After full exchange, PRO made the cleaner reason to prefer quotas-with-teeth over permanent geographic assignment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2 collapse of PRO’s “teeth” into a mandatory solidarity fund (perform-or-pay = CON’s capacity money), answered by PRO’s closing that only the quota turns those transfers into an entitlement rather than charity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing that PRO's quota mechanism, when pressed on enforcement and secondary movement, collapses into either CON's preferred capacity-and-fund alternative or the failed 2015 status quo. PRO never fully overcame the impossibility of physically relocating asylum seekers against member-state will, and their retreat to financial buy-outs conceded the central comparative ground. CON's throughput-versus-hosting distinction successfully reframed the crisis away from geographic distribution toward border capacity, leaving PRO's fairness argument insufficiently connected to the practical mechanism on offer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal showing that PRO's enforcement "teeth" produce a mandatory solidarity fund identical to CON's alternative, while PRO's preference-matching merely certifies journeys already happening—making the quota either redundant or unenforceable.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side A sharply exposed noncompliance, secondary movement, and political backlash, but repeatedly treated some leakage and some refusal as if quotas therefore produced no relocation at all. Side B gave the stronger comparison: most states can still comply, fines make refusal costly, and legal, financial, and processing responsibility shifts away from frontline states even without perfect applicant retention. Most importantly, Side A never solved Side B’s objection that voluntary flexibility defaults to no guaranteed contribution or placement. Side A’s two clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it openly accepted financial sanctions, distinguished a mandatory default with priced defection from voluntary contributions, and explained why secondary movement does not erase the transfer of case responsibility and costs. Side A’s claim that this simply became its own model was rhetorically strong but ignored compliance by states that do accept placements.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by collapsing PRO’s live mechanism into the same endpoint (northern money for southern capacity) while showing higher political and Schengen costs and no workable retention tool. PRO kept a coherent fairness/default-obligation frame and a real secondary-movement parity point, but after conceding financial sanctions and no retention lever, that frame never recovered a decisive delivery advantage over flexible solidarity. Two CON clips were execution costs, yet the comparative case still landed cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s enforcement/retention pressure (A2/A4): a “binding” quota cashing out as fines plus secondary movement to preferred states, i.e. flexible solidarity bought with litigation and backlash.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing that PRO's binding quotas collapse into the very financial transfers CON proposes, but only after extracting a steep price in political cohesion and European trust. Through sharp pressure questions, CON forced PRO to concede there is no Schengen-compatible retention mechanism and that enforcement against unwilling states yields fines rather than actual relocations. These concessions left PRO defending a symbolic default obligation that moves paperwork instead of people, while CON's flexible-solidarity alternative could deliver immediate caseworkers, faster processing, and returns that actually shrink frontline burdens. PRO's rhetorically potent default-zero framing could not overcome the demonstrated operational failures of the quota mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure questions and second rebuttal, which leveraged PRO's admissions that quotas lack a retention mechanism and that defiance ends in financial penalties to collapse the distinction between the two models and show quotas purchase the same euros twice: once in cash, once in lost cohesion.
