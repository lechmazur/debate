- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B built a sharper, more resilient case around a single decisive criterion—reversibility/accountability—and never let go of it. A also weakened its closing credibility with a reference-ID error (citing nonexistent "C2.10/C4.1"). Overall B progressed the debate more, answered pressure more directly, and left the cleaner, more memorable framing standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 answers plus the "medical-board analogy betrays them" and "true facts differently arranged" points), which converted A's own answers about reviewer selection and debate-presentation into concessions supporting B's accountability framework.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won the accountability and framing clashes. PRO's strongest card—irreversible harm to a cohort—was turned: A showed centralized scholarly error harms every cohort everywhere with no election to end it, while political error is temporary, plural, and visible.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A2.4/A4.3-A4.6 exchange — conceding facts vs arrangement, then using B's own answer that reviewers are self-governing associations to prove unaccountable error and turn B's medical-board analogy into political control with an extra layer.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by brilliantly turning Side B's strongest emotional point ("you cannot un-educate a child") into a comparative advantage. A argued that while no system refunds a lost decade, political error is local and reversible, whereas scholarly error is universal and permanent. A also effectively dismantled B's mechanism by showing that professional associations are closed guilds without democratic recourse, leaving B's case vulnerable to the charge of unaccountable, uniform error. Side B's arguments were competent but relied on generic appeals to expertise and failed to answer the structural critiques of their review mechanism.
