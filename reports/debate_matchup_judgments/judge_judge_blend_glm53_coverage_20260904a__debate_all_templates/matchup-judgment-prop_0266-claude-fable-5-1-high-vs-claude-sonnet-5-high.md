### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. B established that the rule merely compares the app total with the same restaurant’s independent channel and lets customers decide whether delivery is worth the premium. A effectively showed that pickup and delivery are different services and that salient comparisons can reduce sales even without deception, weakening B’s extraction rhetoric. But A never proved that legitimate costs must be audited or separated before an accurate alternative can be shown, and its liability and inevitable fee-increase claims remained speculative. B’s practical answer—restaurant-entered prices, a materiality threshold, and responsibility assigned to the submitting party—was sufficient despite the clipping penalties.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3 most decisively answered A’s central threshold objection: the rule compares two totals and need not determine whether the difference comes from markup, packaging, or dispatch. That shifted the relevant judgment back to the customer and undercut much of A’s workability case.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO's reframing of "independent" as independent of the app—pointing to the same restaurant's counter or direct channel rather than a rival business—neutralized CON's strongest opening objection and collapsed the compelled-cross-promotion argument. CON responded with sharp technical points on behavioral loss-aversion and the feedback-loop verification gap, but these remained contested while PRO's core transparency case stayed intact. The modesty of the rule and the moral framing that a premium requiring concealment is extraction proved more durable than CON's workability and fee-relocation objections, giving PRO the cleaner live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's reframing in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 that the "independent" option is the same restaurant (independent of the app), not a rival business, which forced CON off its strongest "compelled advertising" terrain and redefined the central clash.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins the defining scope fight. By clarifying in B4.1 that "independent" means independent of the app – the same restaurant's counter/phone/own site, with "nearest" handling chains – PRO collapses CON's core compelled-cross-promotion/slippery-slope argument and justifies the airline/APR analogy as disclosing its own inflated menu price. That plus materiality threshold answers workability and liability panic that existing unaudited menu prices already face. CON keeps economics alive with a strong Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: salience loss-framing can cause bail even if delivery is valued, and counter prices lack a transaction feedback loop. But PRO's framework leaves the cleaner vote: a premium that survives disclosure is kept, one that only survives in darkness is extraction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 redefining "independent" as independent of the app, not the restaurant, which nullifies CON's rival-till objection; answered by CON's A1 behavioral-economics counter that abandonment stems from last-screen salience, not just ignorance.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by maintaining the decisive distinction between transparent pricing of an app’s own service and compelled promotion of an independent rival channel. Side B established a plausible consumer-information harm, but its workable solution ultimately relied on the app’s own pickup price, abandoning the motion’s independent-option requirement. Side A also more convincingly exposed the verification problem for off-platform prices and showed that Side B’s analogies concerned seller-controlled prices rather than rival-channel comparisons.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal decisively identified Side B’s pivot: after being pressed on unverifiable independent prices, Side B substituted the app’s own pickup total as the anchor. Side A showed that this was either already available or merely its own-price transparency alternative, leaving the distinctively independent part of the proposition unsupported.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully demonstrated that PRO could not defend the motion as written. The proposition explicitly requires surfacing an "independent" lower-fee option, but under pressure CON showed that PRO gradually retreated to defending the app's own pickup toggle—a price the app already sets and often already displays—which is either the status quo or CON's own proposed alternative (all-in pricing), not the mandated rival-channel disclosure the motion demands. CON's "category error" framing proved durable: every precedent PRO offered (pharmacist generics, airline baggage, APR) involves a seller disclosing its own pricing, not compelled advertising for competitors. PRO conceded no law compels the exact behavior the motion requires and never solved the verification and liability problems for truly independent prices. By the closing, PRO had effectively abandoned the word "independent," leaving the proposition either unworkable or redundant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing, tracking PRO's retreat from the "independent" price anchor to the app's own pickup toggle, thereby showing PRO was either defending the status quo or an unverifiable mandate, not the written proposition.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the precedent and feasibility clash. PRO’s information-asymmetry story was intuitive but CON showed disclosure law targets a seller’s own price (airline own bag, pharmacy same counter), PRO conceded in B4.1 no rival-channel mandate exists, and that independent off-platform price is unverifiable. PRO then retreated to the app’s own pickup toggle, which CON correctly flagged as status quo and as CON’s own all-in-pricing alternative, leaving the proposition as written either redundant or unworkable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Rebuttal 2 A4.1-A4.3: distinguishing difference-in-seller vs difference-in-service to defuse baggage analogy and exposing PRO’s shift from “independent” to “app’s own pickup” as a concession that the only workable anchor is already shown.
