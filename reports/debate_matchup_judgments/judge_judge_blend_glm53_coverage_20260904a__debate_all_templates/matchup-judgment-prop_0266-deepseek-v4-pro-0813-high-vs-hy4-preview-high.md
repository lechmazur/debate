### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON built a tighter, more consistent case across rounds: the "product is different" framing (a delivered meal vs. a self-fetched one) undercut PRO's equivalence premise from the start, and CON's accuracy/feasibility attack (the app cannot verify a rival channel's true total without integration, and any estimate omitting known fees is non-compliant by PRO's own standard) was never fully resolved. The pressure-question exchange was pivotal: CON's Q1 exposed that the motion's text ("nearest... independent") plausibly reaches beyond the same merchant, forcing PRO to visibly narrow/redefine "independent" mid-debate to survive, which CON then correctly framed as a rewrite of the motion rather than a defense of it. PRO's own concession trap ("consumers already know") was clever, but CON's reply (people know pickup exists, not the specific lower-fee total) neutralized it cleanly. PRO's rebuttals were competent and the compelled-speech pushback was reasonable, but PRO never fully answered why an app that doesn't sell or control the rival channel must post its total, and PRO's opening and closing were both flagged as clipped for exceeding word limits, an execution cost that cost PRO its strongest final framing. Overall CON gave the cleaner, more resolved path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange (A3.1 vs B4.1): CON's question exposed that "nearest lower-fee independent" naturally could mean a different nearby restaurant, and PRO's answer narrowing "independent" to mean only the same merchant's own channel was then attacked by CON in Rebuttal 2 as effectively rewriting the motion rather than defending it — this exchange most clearly reframed who held the stronger textual and practical ground for the rest of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by effectively dismantling Side B's premise that the disclosure corrects an information asymmetry regarding hidden fees. Side A convincingly argues that delivery is a fundamentally different product (a service) than pickup, making the disclosure a forced advertisement for a substitute. Side A also successfully presses the logistical impossibility of knowing external fees without costly integration, which Side B weakly waves away by saying the rule wouldn't apply if the data isn't known.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 distinguishing a "pizza" from a "pizza delivered" perfectly refutes Side B's "same product" framing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO kept the debate on the same-merchant, same-order reading where "independent" means independent of the platform. That defuses CON's unbounded-ad-duty and delivers a live cure for the gap CON concedes in [B2.5]. Materiality and knowability answers in A4 handle accuracy/cost objections, while CON's core "different product" claim never overcomes that a buyer can still value convenience once informed. PRO made the decisive comparison clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.5/A4.1 - PRO's self-defeat turn on "consumers already know" vs "lack specific total" and the narrowing of "independent" to direct channel, collapsing CON's unbounded-set and integration-cost route.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but CON's final operational point—that the "nearest independent" pickup/direct price is, by the motion's own logic, external to what the app computes, forcing a choice between a toothless rule (silence when unverifiable) or compelled surveillance of competitors' pricing—was the sharpest unresolved tension in the debate. PRO's pressure-question answers (the invented floor-and-percentage "safe harbor") were a reasonable but partly ad hoc fix, and PRO's closing essentially conceded the rule would rarely trigger when independent prices aren't verifiable, which cuts against its own practical force. CON exploited this dichotomy cleanly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, giving the clearer final reason to vote CON. PRO's framing (choice architecture, "taxing discovery") was strong and rhetorically memorable, and its rebuttals were solid, but it never fully closed the verification/product-comparability gap CON kept pressing. CON's opening being clipped is a real execution cost, but the substantive rebuttal work in later rounds outweighs it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3) and Closing (A5.1-A5.3), arguing the app's own pickup total is not the "independent" price required by the motion, forcing either silence (if unverifiable) or surveillance of external competitor prices—an unresolved bind that PRO's closing (B5.2) only partially answered by conceding the trigger simply wouldn't fire in ambiguous cases.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully dismantling Side B's workability claims. Side A smartly distinguished between an app's own "mediated" pickup price and the "independent" pickup price required by the motion, proving that apps do not inherently possess the required data. This forced Side B's logic into a paradox where the rule either requires the external surveillance it denies or collapses into silence. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's defense of the "materially higher" trigger by exposing Side B's safe harbor as an invented standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 in Rebuttal 2, which definitively answered Side B's pressure question by pointing out that the pickup price an app already calculates is its own mediated price, not the independent walk-in price demanded by the motion.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO won the workability and stakes clash. A neutralized "materially higher" vagueness with a floor-and-percentage safe harbor using totals apps already compute [A4.1], and answered small-app burden by showing low-fee apps simply don't trigger [A4.2]. CON's strongest point that app pickup is not independent and direct prices need surveillance [B4.3] was blunted by PRO's conditional trigger - no verification, no duty [A5.2] - while CON's answers to pressure left uncertainty and clipped opening as execution costs. Moment-of-choice steering remained live for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3 / A2.3 exchange on whether the app "plainly sees" an independent pickup gap - CON's distinction between app-mediated pickup total and true independent walk-in price vs PRO's conditional-verification reply.
