### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that lower checkout prices merely conceal costs already borne through climate, health, and public services, while a production tax changes marginal incentives. Side B raised serious nutrition and implementation concerns, but overstated higher prices as guaranteed malnutrition and treated differentiated taxation and compensation as forbidden even though the motion does not exclude them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange exposed that Side B accepted targeted rules despite their price effects, undermining the claim that an immediate increase for poor households uniquely defeats taxation. Side B distinguished rules as more targeted, but never established that they avoid pass-through, leakage, or residual externalities.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the debate tied to what the motion actually guarantees: higher food prices for lower-income households. B's distinction between targeted rules (price rises tied to eliminating specific harms) and a blunt average tax survived A's responses, and B's answer to the pressure questions exposed that A's fairness case relied on off-ballot add-ons like guaranteed transfers and border adjustments. The 'certain malnutrition versus uncertain environmental gains' frame remained live through the closing, giving B the cleaner comparative path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal distinguishing targeted rules from an average tax that charges every kilo including sustainable protein, and showing A's fairness case depended on unguaranteed compensation and border adjustments not present in the motion.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by strictly enforcing the "even if" condition of the motion, forcing Side A to defend the tax without guaranteed compensatory transfers. Side B effectively contrasted the certain, immediate nutritional harm to low-income households against the uncertain, leakage-prone environmental gains, and successfully dismantled Side A's analogies to tobacco and fuel taxes. While Side A's rhetoric was punchy and the "invisible invoice" frame was strong, Side B's analytical precision on the motion's text and the inelastic demand for compact protein provided a cleaner, more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response in Rebuttal 2 to Side A's pressure question, where B conceded they would accept the tax if transfers were guaranteed, but immediately used that concession to prove the motion fails because it does not guarantee those transfers, effectively stripping Side A of its primary mitigation strategy.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by focusing on the motion’s demanding commitment to tax the full bundle of externalities despite regressive food-price effects. Side A established real livestock harms and answered the urban-incidence question, but repeatedly asserted rather than demonstrated that those diffuse benefits would exceed the immediate nutritional burden on poorer households. Side B more effectively distinguished local pollution, global emissions, and socialized dietary costs, then argued that targeted regulation, emissions pricing, and health policy address them without making staple food the collection mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive response exploited Side A’s concession that it would impose the tax even if rebates were late or missed informal workers. This weakened Side A’s equity defense and let Side B contrast a certain, immediate grocery burden with a heterogeneous and difficult-to-price bundle of harms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): CON successfully showed that "full" production-level pricing of diffuse health and regional environmental harms is epistemically unworkable, seized on PRO's concession that the tax should stand even if rebates fail, and maintained that staple nutrition should not be the collection mechanism for an uncloseable account. PRO's "zero is wrong" frame justified some tax, but could not defend the motion's demand for full internalization without fusing unlike bills and accepting certain food-price harm for uncertain gain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing crystallized that PRO's "hidden tax" fused three unlike ledgers—neighbor pollution, global methane, and individual diet-related disease—only one of which might be production-tractable, and that PRO's admission that the tax should proceed even without reliable rebates abandoned the claim of protecting lower-income households.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's conflation of local pollution, global climate, and individual diet-related illness. Side B effectively argues that diet-related illness is not a "farm spill" that can be accurately priced at the slaughterhouse, exposing the impossibility of taxing the "full" health externality as the motion demands. Side B also highlights that while Side A's "protection" via rebates is optional and uncertain, the immediate price shock to the urban poor is guaranteed. Side A's retreat to "zero is wrong" in the closing implicitly concedes Side B's point that the motion requires an honest "full" accounting, which Side B proved practically impossible to achieve at the production stage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dissects Side A's "socialized healthcare" argument by distinguishing between a localized farm spill and an individual health outcome, proving that a production tax is the wrong instrument for consumption-based health costs and exposing that Side A accepts guaranteed nutritional loss for uncertain rebates.
