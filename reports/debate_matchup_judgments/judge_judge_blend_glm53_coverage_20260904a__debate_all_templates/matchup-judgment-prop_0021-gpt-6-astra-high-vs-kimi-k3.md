### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A controlled the strategic framing of the debate, successfully characterizing Side B's stance as an unfalsifiable 'veto in slow motion.'   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A1 and Closing effectively synthesized Side B's Yellowstone concession to show that both sides ultimately rely on the same baseline criteria, leaving Side B with no unique ground to oppose the core proposition.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): CON kept the decisive clash on necessity/comparative justification. PRO's charge that CON's test is a hidden veto failed after A named a certifier (accountable agency with published thresholds) and a passing case (Yellowstone) in A4.1, directly answering B3.1. CON then damaged PRO's core asymmetries: both culls and coexistence need ongoing funding/inputs [A2.4, A4.5], predator survival ≠ recovery [A2.3], and targeted browsing control can start regeneration without imposing predators [A4.2][A5.3]. PRO's compensation-revisable vs degradation-irreversible frame thus leaves no reason to override when less-intrusive restoration is available.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.6 and A4.5 – turning veto accusation into assessable thresholds and showing both policies require durable institutions, collapsing B's "free predator vs perpetual cull rental" asymmetry.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B controlled the clash by effectively framing Side A's conditions as a disguised veto and forcing A to concede that reintroduction over objections is sometimes justified (e.g., Yellowstone). B's rhetorical framing of the "asymmetry" between revisable compensation and irreversible ecological loss was highly persuasive, and B successfully defended the long-term cost-effectiveness of self-sustaining predators over perpetual, publicly funded culling. Side A made strong points about the hidden costs of coexistence but struggled to clearly define when they would actually support the motion, leaving their position vulnerable to B's attacks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they provided concrete cancellation criteria in response to pressure questions and turned the spotlight back on Side A's inability to define a clear starting point, effectively exposing A's "assessable conditions" as a soft veto.
