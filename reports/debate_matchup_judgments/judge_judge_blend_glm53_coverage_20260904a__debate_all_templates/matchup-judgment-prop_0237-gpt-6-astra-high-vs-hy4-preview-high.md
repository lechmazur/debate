### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, competitive cases, but the decisive exchange came from A's pressure question asking whether restrictions automatically suspend when promised pathways fail to deliver. B's answer conceded that restrictions remain in force with only a non-refoulement floor as backstop—no guarantee of admission, safety, or timely protection. A exploited this concession precisely in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, framing it as proof that "PRO's bargain guarantees the restriction, not its replacement," which directly undercuts B's core causal story that the exchange delivers real protection to people like the hypothetical mother. B's closing reasserted the smuggling-collapse mechanism and non-refoulement floor but never adequately answered why "lawful status while waiting" equates to actual safety or access when the pathway itself is admittedly conditional. A also scored points earlier by noting that closing the presentation route could push migration underground rather than eliminate smuggling, a structural risk B never fully rebutted beyond reasserting its "product" framing. B's opening and results-based framing (ending the arrival-right, collapsing the smuggler's business model) were rhetorically strong and consistently maintained, but the unanswered asymmetry exposed in the pressure round tilts the debate toward A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (and Closing) exploiting B's Q2 answer—that restrictions do not automatically suspend when pathways fail, leaving only a non-refoulement floor—was the most decisive exchange, directly undermining B's claim that the exchange reliably protects vulnerable people like the "mother" hypothetical.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: the package guarantees transit-based exclusion more reliably than it guarantees usable protection. PRO’s opening magnet/smuggling story was vivid and coherent, but CON repeatedly forced the live test—whether designation equals accessible refuge, whether pathways substitute for an urgent applicant, and what happens when the “mandatory” alternative fails—and PRO’s A2 answer (restrictions do not suspend; non-refoulement is the floor) locked in CON’s comparative frame. PRO never showed why fixing release/delay and capacity required disqualifying by route rather than adjudicated protection, so CON kept the cleaner reason to reject the bargain as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2/Closing turn on PRO’s Q2 answer: restrictions stay up even when pathways cannot timely protect, so the deal locks in exclusion while the substitute remains conditional; non-refoulement alone does not supply admission, a safe place to wait, or an effective procedure.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the guarantee vs presumption clash. PRO conceded no automatic suspension when pathways fail [B4.3] and fell back to non-refoulement + review before removal. CON showed that does not supply admission, a safe place to live, or effective procedure [A4.4-A4.6], that exclusion still costs adjudication capacity [A4.1], and that eligibility ≠ automatic release, so PRO never proved transit disqualification is necessary. Smuggling displacement and fewer-claims ≠ fewer-deaths went largely unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 using PRO's own answers to A-Q1/Q2 to prove restriction is certain while the replacement is conditional, plus that lawful wait does not equal protection.
