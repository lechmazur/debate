### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but A's framing tracked the motion's actual question—who holds final authority—more cleanly, while B's "reciprocal safeguards" never specified what enforcement mechanism actually compels Ethiopia when it disagrees. A's pressure questions exposed this gap directly (Q2 on deadlock), and B's answer (interim rules pausing "additional storage" without stopping generation) was a plausible partial fix but still left unclear what happens if Ethiopia rejects even that pause—precisely the scenario the motion poses. B's counter-pressure (forcing A to admit "any outside compulsion is a veto") was rhetorically sharp and exposed A's position as opposing all binding constraint, not just Egyptian domination, but A's answer that "protection doesn't require compulsion" was consistent with its underlying philosophy (voluntary cooperation, transparency, physics-based limits on hoarding) rather than a true contradiction. A also better exploited the concrete asymmetry (Aswan's buffer vs. Ethiopian energy poverty) and used the turbine-release mechanism to blunt fears of permanent upstream hoarding. B's abstract "sole judge of its own case" framing was compelling but rested on an enforcement structure that stayed vague through both rebuttals and closing, leaving the practical bite of CON's position under-specified relative to the concrete stakes A kept surfacing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Q2/A2 exchange on deadlock scenarios: A's question forced B to clarify its enforcement mechanism (interim hydrology rules pausing storage without full shutdown), which was a real answer, but A's follow-up reassertion that "the only enforceable order that bites is stop filling, stop generating" was never squarely refuted by B's distinction, leaving the core enforcement question live in A's favor.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal catching Side B's conflation of 'slowing the fill' with 'stopping turbines,' noting that releasing water actually generates electricity, decisively neutralizing Side B's core emotional appeal.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by proving that enforceable reciprocal safeguards are not an Egyptian veto. B exposed that A rejects any compulsory constraint on Ethiopia's timeline, not merely Cairo's domination, and showed that interim hydrology-based rules can protect both continued generation and downstream needs without Egypt's assent. A's sovereignty frame collapsed into unilateral authority with no binding protection for downstream countries when cooperation fails.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, where B answered A's pressure questions by distinguishing independent drought rules from a veto, exposed A's rejection of all enforceability, and showed that slowing storage need not stop turbines—collapsing A's "switch off" frame.
