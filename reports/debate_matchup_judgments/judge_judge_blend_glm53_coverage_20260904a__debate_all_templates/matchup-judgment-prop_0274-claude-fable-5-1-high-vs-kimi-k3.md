- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A wins by decisively isolating the unique mechanism of geotags (algorithmic aggregation and precise coordinates) compared to general fame, and by winning the temporal clash.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal to B's "same feet, worse information" argument by explaining how a geotag is a coordinate and an algorithmic aggregator, not just a trail map, fundamentally dismantling B's claim that crowds would arrive in exactly the same way without the pin.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 A1/A2 - distinguishing editorial discretion from third-party speech limits and showing physical response (closure/barriers) arrives faster than takedowns while pin removal leaves hashtag/name search intact, exposing PRO's unfalsifiable "any nudge counts" retreat.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A successfully framed the debate around the asymmetry of prevention versus reaction, proving that physical infrastructure (Side B's preferred solution) is too slow and impractical for wild, fragile spots that go viral overnight.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they explained the aggregation mechanism of geotags and highlighted the asymmetry in the prediction problem—noting that a town can pull a pin or partner with a platform in hours, while building a physical reservation system takes a season.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won the weighing by effectively positioning the motion's tool as a low-cost, early-stage complement to capacity caps. Side B pressed hard on efficacy and the 'speech-editing' scope of reaching selfies, but Side A demonstrated that adding friction reduces algorithmic surges, and successfully weaponized Side B's alternatives (towing cars, closing exits, building fences) as vastly more disruptive to the very resident life the motion seeks to protect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure on 'gateless sites' in R2 and Closing, which forced Side B to defend heavy-handed physical restrictions (towing, fences, closed exits) as alternatives to simply removing a database coordinate.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): CON won the effectiveness and scope clash. PRO framed limits as a light complement to caps, but CON showed caps and other physical tools hit below capacity with certainty the day enforced while a pin-removal request depends on platforms and is rebuilt via comments/reverse-search. A forced and answered the dilemma: poppy fields were ordinary selfies, so motion is either toothless (commercial-only) or sweeping (reaches every selfie). B's concession in B4.1/4.2 that it does reach the selfie plus failure to name a single site saved by geotag limits alone, calling the question rigged, left the weighing claim "reversible vs irreversible" favoring CON's certain prevention.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's answers to pressure – gateless sites already have gateless enforcement (exit closures, fences, permit parking) and the line between controlling your own ads (Amsterdam) vs editing others' posts, paired with B's admission that the tool reaches ordinary selfies yet remains unenforceable and blinds visitors who need the pin for warnings.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively dismantling Side A's causal mechanism. B proved that physical capacity tools (fences, caps, closures) are what actually stop environmental damage with certainty, while geotag removal is unenforceable, easily bypassed via reverse image searches, and relies entirely on platform goodwill. Crucially, B's pressure questions forced A to admit the policy applies to ordinary users' selfies, exposing it as a sweeping speech restriction rather than a narrow commercial regulation. A's retreat to "it's a complement" failed because B effectively showed the complement adds no practical benefit to gateless sites while carrying heavy speech costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's distinction in Rebuttal 2 between a destination controlling its own advertising budget (Amsterdam) and asking a third-party platform to edit ordinary users' geotagged selfies. This cleanly neutralized PRO's best example and exposed the sweeping, unenforceable nature of CON's proposed mechanism.
