### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won a close debate because its rehabilitation case applied to the ordinary juvenile record, while B concentrated on a hard assault case that A's serious-offense exceptions and restricted safeguarding access could substantially address. B powerfully exposed the danger of victim nonappearance, but overstated that absence as the only available safety assessment despite retained court, police, prosecutor, and probation access. Most importantly, B conceded that its petition model leaves records publicly searchable and that some poor applicants will never obtain relief, preserving A's central argument that the wrong default converts temporary juvenile punishment into lasting exclusion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's sharpest exchange forced A to admit that a party-assault record could seal if notified objectors did not appear. A survived that pressure by distinguishing unrestricted public branding from confidential safeguarding checks and by returning the burden to the state actors who retain the full file.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the debate anchored to the text of the motion and exposing that PRO's safety, transparency, and broker remedies relied on mechanisms not actually in the proposition. CON's victim-burden narrative—showing that a teenage assault victim would have to prove ongoing risk and that her absence seals the record—was vivid, conceded in substance by PRO, and never neutralized. CON also landed the cleaner comparative framing on inertia: both models have idle non-actors, but PRO places the cost on strangers who cannot see the file, while CON places it on the person who holds the file. The broker-timing point and the "zombie record" argument further damaged PRO's digital-records claim. PRO's case remained plausible, but CON's rebuttals did more decisive damage to PRO's live routes than vice versa.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 ([A4.5]–[A4.6]), where CON seized PRO's concession that a party-assault record seals if the victim, after being served notice, simply does not appear. CON painted the hearing as forcing a sixteen-year-old girl to face her assailant and prove ongoing risk, calling the result "a rubber stamp with a victim's absence as the ink." PRO could only respond with procedural "absence of proof" framing that ignored the human and comparative cost, leaving the point live and damaging.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won the burden/inertia clash that both sides accepted as decisive. PRO's model requires victims/prosecutors to prove ongoing risk after notice, and PRO conceded in A4.3 that if they don't appear the record seals. CON turned that into a rubber-stamp with the victim carrying the cost, while its own petition model makes the person who knows the file bear inertia. PRO's fix — a court-supervised child-safety channel and supervised journalist access — was effectively flagged as outside the motion as written and contradictory (leagues can flag but landlords cannot). CON also neutralized the broker claim on timing: copies exist at 15-17, sealing at 18 does not delete them, and the certified-copy cure re-imposes the filing burden PRO decries.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B3 exposure of PRO's add-on background channel and the A4.3 concession that a party assault — not an automatic exception — seals if the victim after notice simply does not appear to prove future risk. That turned PRO's 'absence of proof' into a burden on the least-likely attendee.
