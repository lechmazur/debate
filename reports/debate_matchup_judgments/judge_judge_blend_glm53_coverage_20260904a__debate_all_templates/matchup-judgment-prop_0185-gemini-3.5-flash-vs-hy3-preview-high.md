### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): This was a genuinely close debate in which both sides argued competently. PRO's core structural argument—that electoral incentives systematically bias legislators toward deficit spending, transferring costs to future voters who cannot hold anyone accountable—was the most durable claim in the debate and was never squarely neutralized. CON's alternative (pure legislative discretion) never fully answered this incentive-alignment problem; CON's case was largely about design flaws in PRO's proposal rather than a positive defense of discretionary policy. PRO also benefited from framing the stabilizer exception as the answer to the main historical critique of rigid BBPs, which forced CON onto narrower ground. CON won several specific technical exchanges: the Germany rebuttal ([B2.3]) was the strongest single moment—CON correctly pointed out that Germany's debt brake already contains cyclical adjustments and escape clauses, directly undermining PRO's "incomplete design" explanation, and PRO's Rebuttal 2 never specifically addressed this counter. CON's argument that automatic stabilizers are insufficient for systemic shocks (2008, 2020) and that pro-cyclical revenue offsets during crises are economically self-defeating were also well-grounded. However, CON's closing introduced reference IDs ([C2.5], [C4.5], [C2.2], [C4.4]) that do not match the debate's established notation, a minor epistemic-discipline cost. More importantly, CON's positive path—trusting legislators who face the same incentive failures PRO diagnosed—was never made convincing. The debate resolves narrowly for PRO because the structural case for the proposition remained live after the exchange, while CON's alternative stood on weaker normative ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [B2.3] response to PRO's Germany claim was the most decisive single exchange. PRO argued Germany's debt brake failures were due to "incomplete design," implying the proposition's stabilizer exception would fix it. CON correctly identified that Germany's debt brake already features cyclical adjustments and emergency escape clauses—the very features PRO claimed were missing—yet still produced off-budget shadow funds and judicial chaos. PRO's Rebuttal 2 never specifically answered this point, leaving CON's empirical counter standing. This was the clearest case of one side landing a materially damaging blow that the other failed to address.
