### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by narrowing the motion to who bears default friction and by distinguishing bounded physical novelties from instantly repeatable, premium-currency gambling loops. Side B offered strong responses about authenticated purchases, fixed bankrolls, parental authority, and the lack of cash payouts, but its claim that defaults should be improved conflicted with its later insistence that adequate defaults already universally exist. A also showed that purchase authentication may govern the currency top-up without governing the randomized spending loop inside the game. B's proportionality case remained credible, but relied too heavily on an overbroad assertion about existing platform defaults and did not outweigh A's low-cost protection versus concentrated family harm comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal was decisive: it turned B's concession that minors are not financially load-bearing against the free-access objection, exposed that B itself endorsed better defaults, and sharply distinguished a cheap physical surprise product from rapid repeat purchases using stored value. B's later fixed-bankroll analogy recovered ground but did not fully answer the internal-game design problem.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully turned CON’s “minors aren’t load-bearing” point into proof that the developer objection in the motion costs nothing, and leveraged CON’s own call for “better defaults” to force the clash onto the default position. CON’s casino/cash-payout distinction and proportionality frame were sharp, but they never fully neutralized PRO’s core asymmetry: a platform password buys a currency top-up, while the motion’s protections address the frictionless randomized-pull experience inside the game. PRO’s “slot machine in the bedroom” framing held up as the cleaner live route to victory, whereas CON’s closing claim that every additional dollar requires re-authentication missed that the parent had already authorized a fixed budget the game converts into pulls. The debate narrowed effectively to PRO’s chosen ground, and B maintained the clearer comparative edge despite two clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s first rebuttal turning CON’s call for “better defaults at the platform settings level” into an endorsement of the motion, forcing the entire clash to rest on whether the resting position should sit at the platform menu or inside the game.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): PRO won the narrowing. CON conceded minors don't fund free access, nullifying the even-if clause, and conceded the need for better defaults and friction. PRO then showed the remaining gap: platform PIN guards a currency top-up, but 40 randomized pulls with near-miss animations and hidden odds happen after that PIN inside the game. CON's cash-out distinction is strong against a legal gambling label but doesn't answer the delivery-capacity line PRO drew for age-gating, and the "switch already closed" claim fails to address pull-level repetition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A5.3 – PRO's expose that a single authentication covers a bulk currency pack that converts into dozens of frictionless randomized pulls, so a console password is a lock on the front door with a slot machine in the bedroom; it directly undercuts CON's claim that existing defaults already solve the motion.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won by showing that platform controls alone protect mainly already-identified child accounts, while universal game defaults and layered safeguards also catch false ages and some use of adult accounts. B’s privacy fork exposed a real shift from A’s initial “one toggle” language to adult verification, but B overclaimed that game-level defaults add no protection and largely conceded the motion’s basic demand by advocating tighter default youth purchase controls at another layer. B also never fully answered design-level protections beyond checkout. Both clipping notes impose modest execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A’s layered-regulation answer to B’s platform-only alternative: checkout gates can block payment, but they neither govern manipulative design nor provide the redundant default barrier present in each still-locked game. B sharply exposed the verification cost, yet did not establish its claim of equal protection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A exposed a decisive tension in PRO's model: default protections that adults could opt out of via verification collapsed into the very universal ID regime PRO had dismissed as a strawman, while still failing the account-sharing child that motivated PRO's pressure questions. By showing the platform payment layer already addressed the financial harm more cleanly, CON established the motion imposed cost without additional protection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's fork showing PRO's default protections required either toothless toggles or universal ID verification, paired with the account-sharing child rebuttal that neutralized PRO's "uncaught child" pressure.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B exposed that "strict youth limit" requires age knowledge (ID for adults) or is just an opt-in toggle, and PRO then embraced universal per-purchase caps removable only by adult verification. That concedes the privacy/friction cost while still failing the shared-account child identically to the platform checkout model, which already has child-account purchase approval and a single regulable gate. A never answered that identical failure mode.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 fork + B4.1-B4.5: forcing PRO to choose between real youth enforcement = mass ID versus toggle = platform controls, then showing PRO's A4.3-A4.4 answer (defaults bind everyone, ID to remove) is the universal adult checkpoint it called a strawman in A2.3.
