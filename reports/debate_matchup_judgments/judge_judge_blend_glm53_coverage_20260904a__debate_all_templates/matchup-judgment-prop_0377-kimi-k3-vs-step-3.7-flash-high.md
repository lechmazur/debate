- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by establishing the clearest comparative mechanism: annual income ends or becomes taxable wealth, while inherited capital transmits compounded advantages across generations.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure Q2 plus closing crystallization: B never named the moment the heir is taxed and kept restating the founder's prior taxes.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): B established the motion’s central comparison: inherited wealth compounds advantages tied to birth, while most high income reflects current activity. A was sharper rhetorically and raised real avoidance concerns, but overstated them as total futility. Taxing first-generation estates can itself prevent new dynasties, and high exemptions substantially weaken A’s family-business objection. B’s unsupported precision and two clipped turns narrowed the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s fixed-pie framing was decisive: scarce housing, career access, risk tolerance, and startup capital showed how inherited wealth can displace or outcompete less wealthy peers even without literally taking a predetermined seat. A’s supply-side reply did not eliminate that relative opportunity advantage.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by expertly trapping Side B in the Pressure Questions and capitalizing on the answers. Side A forces Side B to admit that enforcing the tax requires entirely new policies (trust law rewrites) and that the revenue just funds standard welfare programs, which neutralizes Side B's comparative mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal dismantles Side B's answers to the pressure questions, showing that Side B abandoned the motion's mechanism for trust rewrites and failed to prove inheritance taxes are uniquely necessary over income taxes.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by making the decisive comparative and efficacy clash clearer and keeping it live: the tax is either too narrow/symbolic to reorganize opportunity or hits the wrong (unplanned/illiquid) estates while dynasties route around it, and A's own pressure answers reduced the remedy to ordinary spending programs any revenue could fund—killing the unique "bigger threat than high-earner income" claim. A had a coherent case on unearned compounding and loopholes plus a high-exemption defense, but repeated points, suffered clipping costs, and never restored a clean path from aggressive inheritance taxation itself to floor-lifting outcomes that income taxes couldn't match.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4–B4.5: forced A's pressure answers into a concession that the motion-as-written cannot reach dynasties (needs extra trust/basis reforms) and that the "channel" is fungible spending.
