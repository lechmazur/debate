### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B narrowly won because its core path stayed intact: kinetic ASAT tests are a discrete, observable, voluntary source of severe orbital debris, and a treaty directly raises legal and diplomatic costs for that act. Side A had sharper rhetoric and strong attacks on dual-use missile defense, enforcement, and overbreadth, but often overclaimed that the treaty changes “zero”; B’s target-based definition and explanation of unique orbital kill-chain/signaling value kept the ban from collapsing into mere theater. A’s point that treaties are imperfect and other debris sources remain did not defeat B’s comparative claim that banning destructive tests is still a meaningful, narrow risk-reduction step.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was pressing B to identify what the treaty uniquely changes beyond existing norms; however, B’s answer that treaty law supplies a clearer violation threshold, countermeasure basis, and institutional framework was enough to preserve a marginal pro-treaty advantage.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by effectively neutralizing every claimed benefit of the treaty, building a clear "zero marginal benefit" ledger. Side A turned Side B's defense of strategic signaling against them, arguing that actors testing for defiance will not be deterred by a treaty signature, and successfully leveraged Side B's concession that the main drivers of debris cascades are handled in "separate channels." Side B also suffered from repeated word count clipping, whereas Side A was exceptionally precise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, especially where A turned B's answer about "strategic signaling" into proof that a treaty wouldn't deter states aiming to signal defiance, while reinforcing the "zero benefit" ledger.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by neutralizing PRO's core routes: exposing the missile-defense loophole that leaves capability intact, showing tests are political signals treaties cannot deter (Russia example), and forcing the concession that main debris drivers fall outside the treaty. PRO's answers remained aspirational and failed to demonstrate unique added value over existing norms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 directly answered both pressure questions by showing engineering substitutes exist and no enforcement gain beyond the UN moratorium, then weighed zero new benefits against concrete costs.
