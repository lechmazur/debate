- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by decisively separating the act of remembering (using past words) from impersonation (generating new words). Side B's "ventriloquism" frame was philosophically sharp and rhetorically memorable, but it never squarely answered why a default prohibition is preferable to the cautious middle for private, labeled, non-authoritative remembrance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 reframe of B's rule as "prohibition dressed as permission" and the estranged-executor counterexample, which exposed an unsolved practical overbreadth in Side B's framework that persisted through the closing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively framing the proposition as a de facto ban on private grief technology, highlighting the practical reality that few people pre-register and estate control can be weaponized by estranged relatives. Side A's alternative framework of opt-outs, labeling, and use-constraints provided a more practical solution to the harms of deception and commercialization. Side B's philosophical distinction between remembering and performing was rhetorically poignant but could not overcome the heavy practical burdens and family-conflict risks inherent in its strict consent requirement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the "estranged executor" problem, sharply contrasting the fiduciary paperwork veto with the actual intimate's right to private, constrained remembrance, effectively neutralized Side B's defense of estate consent as a "flawed gatekeeper."
