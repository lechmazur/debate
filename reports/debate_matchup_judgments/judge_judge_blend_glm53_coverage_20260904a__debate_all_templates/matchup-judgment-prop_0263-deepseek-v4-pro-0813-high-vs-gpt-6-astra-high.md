### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON built a consistent cost-benefit frame from the opening onward: a mandate imposes financing burdens that fall hardest on marginal/niche originals, and PRO never resolved this. The decisive exchange came in Pressure Questions: CON asked what distinguishes a genuine niche project from a tax-driven write-off farm under PRO's rule. PRO's answer (an "objective" affordability test with no motive screen) effectively conceded that sincere niche work could fail the test just as easily as bad-faith productions, and CON's Rebuttal 2 explicitly exploited this concession ("PRO's answer... concedes the problem"). PRO's closing never returned to repair this hole, instead re-asserting the preventive-floor framing and the powerful but insufficient point that "money cannot recreate access." CON conceded that point too but correctly noted it establishes value, not that the guarantee is worth its costs — leaving the central trade-off unresolved in CON's favor. PRO's case also suffered an execution cost: Rebuttal 1 was flagged as clipped for exceeding the word limit, cutting off its final response. PRO's tax-gaming and consumer-bargain framing was rhetorically strong and the more memorable narrative, but CON's tighter, unanswered structural critique gave the cleaner path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to PRO's Pressure Questions answer (A4.4): pointing out that PRO's "objective" affordability test cannot distinguish tax-driven productions from sincere niche projects, which PRO never repaired in its closing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A wins because its prevention framing survived the full exchange more cleanly than B's diffuse alternatives. B conceded that money cannot recreate access to a vanished title, yet offered only reactive remedies—refunds, litigation over implied promises, tax-code fixes, and locked archives—that do not restore the lost work. A's specification of a concrete two-year floor answered B's pressure on vagueness, and A successfully tied the mandate to the reasonable expectations created by heavy "only on" marketing and public tax relief. B's rights-cost and lost-commissions objection remained theoretically live but never overcame the concrete, visceral harm of promoted originals disappearing for accounting reasons. A's clipped first rebuttal was a real execution cost, but A recovered enough to keep the central consumer-protection and public-bargain route intact, crystallizing it effectively in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal (Turn 4, [B2.1]–[B2.8]) was the most damaging exchange: exposing that A's "useful screen" actually filters for financing ability rather than tax abuse, rejecting A's "minor cost" claim by shifting the comparison to marginal revenue versus added rights commitments, and showing that removing a title erases future access but not viewing already delivered.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively exposing the trade-offs of Side B's mandate. While Side B effectively highlighted the consumer harm of early removals, Side A successfully argued that a blanket minimum availability period acts as a blunt instrument that chills marginal and niche productions without actually targeting tax abuse or deceptive advertising. Side A's rebuttal of Side B's "useful screen" claim was particularly damaging, and Side A's closing crystallized the core trade-off better than Side B. Side B also suffered an execution cost when its first rebuttal was clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's defense of the mandate as a "useful screen," pointing out that it merely tests a project's ability to finance longer rights rather than distinguishing between tax-driven write-offs and sincere niche projects.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON built a tighter, more damaging line of attack that survived the full exchange: (1) a "hostage paid earlier" cost-relocation argument showing the prospective fix doesn't eliminate leverage problems, just moves them upstream, and (2) most decisively, the harm-remedy mismatch — PRO's motivating examples are already-removed titles, yet PRO concedes at A1 that the rule "does not restore already-removed titles," leaving its strongest evidence unaddressed while still imposing forward costs. CON pressed this in Rebuttal 2 and again in closing ("either leaves its strongest examples unremedied or breaks its own cost-limiting premise"), and PRO's closing response ("prevention is prevention, not contradiction") is a reasonable but thin patch that doesn't fully dissolve the tension. PRO also conceded ground at B4.7 ("some marginal projects may consequently become less attractive... a tradeoff, not an automatic veto"), which CON weaponized effectively in the final speech as a decisive concession. PRO's core principle (exclusive control shouldn't mean exclusive erasure) remained rhetorically strong and CON even conceded there's "no market mechanism" protecting access, but CON successfully reframed that concession as insufficient to justify the specific remedy, given its acknowledged chilling effect on niche/risky originals and its inability to fix the harms it cites as justification. The pressure-question round sharpened this clash well, and CON's answers/rebuttals afterward exploited PRO's admissions more effectively than PRO exploited CON's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that PRO's prospective-only rule cannot reach the already-removed titles that motivate the whole case ([A2.5]-[A2.6]), later hardened in Rebuttal 2/Closing into "the proposal either leaves its strongest examples unremedied or breaks its own cost-limiting premise" — a tension PRO acknowledged but never fully resolved.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO wins because CON acknowledged that no market mechanism protects access to vanished exclusives yet failed to show that a prospective minimum-availability rule would be more harmful than the erasure it prevents. CON's argument that the remedy is disconnected from existing harms mischaracterizes a clearly prospective rule, and its claimed creative-diversity impact remained speculative. PRO cleanly answered pressure questions, defended the bounded obligation, and showed that exclusive public release should carry a finite responsibility to maintain access.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure questions exposing the tension between PRO's use of already-removed titles as the core harm and PRO's disclaimer of retroactive renegotiation, and PRO's direct A1/A2 answers crystallizing that prevention of future losses is a coherent and sufficient justification for the rule.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully turning Side B's (PRO) core impact against them. While PRO argued that a minimum availability window protects niche, slow-building shows from being erased before they find an audience, CON demonstrated that the fixed carrying costs (residuals, music licensing, server costs) required to keep such shows available for five years would actually cause risk-averse studios to reject them at the greenlight stage. CON also brilliantly exploited the tension in PRO's prospective rule, pointing out that it leaves the most famous examples of the harm (already-removed shows) untouched while imposing heavy, demand-independent liabilities on future projects. CON's "paying the hostage earlier" reframe was particularly devastating to PRO's claim that upfront rights clearing solves the cost problem. CON's rebuttals were sharper, economically grounded, and decisively won the comparative weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument that a prospective five-year minimum acts as a fixed liability that selects against the exact niche, slow-building originals PRO claims to protect, effectively turning PRO's own creativity impact into a CON advantage.
