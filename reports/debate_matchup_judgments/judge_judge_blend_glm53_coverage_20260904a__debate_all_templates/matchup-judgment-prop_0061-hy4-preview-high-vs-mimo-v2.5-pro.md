- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing that the decisive issue is sufficiency, not mere safety. Because both sides lacked a true precedent, the debate turned on which path could plausibly deliver care and demand, and PRO's argument that newcomers are the only lever adding both remained live after CON's best rebuttals.

B's A1 answer (worker-held portable status, ban-able firms, public matching) directly meets A's exploitation pressure, turning trainee abuse into an argument against precarious modesty. A's strongest hit is the Iceland category-error rebuttal, but that leaves A without any counter-precedent and with a clipped R1 that costs progression. Risk reversibility is strong for A, but PRO's human/fiscal necessity outweighs it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling Iceland as EEA free-movement in a 400k microstate - direct, quantitative, and exposing B's lack of comparable precedent - was the sharpest single exchange, even as B's portable-status fix was the most effective constructive rebuttal.

### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger comparative case by exposing a structural treadmill in PRO's immigration plan: PRO conceded that sustaining the dependency ratio requires recurring inflows of 200,000–400,000 annually over decades, and that the “bridge” recurs if fertility does not recover. CON turned this into a decisive frame—immigration enlarges tomorrow’s retirement queue rather than bending the cost curve, whereas automation is a one-time capital transition that raises output per worker. CON also caught PRO in an arithmetic contradiction (tripling current inflows approaches the million-a-year scale PRO disowned), and effectively rebutted the “managed decline” charge by showing right-sizing pairs fewer workers with higher productivity. PRO’s automation skepticism was blunted by CON’s specific, existing task-level technologies, and PRO’s closing slightly mischaracterized CON’s scaling argument. PRO’s peer-country examples were plausible, but the treadmill concession and lack of a clear terminal population target left PRO’s path to victory weaker after the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON’s Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 sequence on the treadmill: CON showed that PRO’s own intake numbers (tripling current flows) implied the radical scale PRO denied, and then demonstrated that because immigrants age and fertility remains near 1.2, stabilizing the dependency ratio requires repeating the intake every generation—making immigration a recurring demographic obligation rather than a one-time fix, while automation offers a permanent, per-worker productivity gain.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won the arithmetic/treadmill clash. B said tripling 200-300k is not a million-scale remodel, A showed 300k×3≈900k and then B conceded 200-400k for decades as a recurring bridge. A answered B's Q1 with specific task-level tech (exoskeletons, ceiling lifts, sensor monitoring, autonomous tractors) while B never explained why automation at record density hasn't worked beyond asserting it. Clipped openings/closings cost PRO completeness, leaving CON's cost-curve framing – cut the bill to fit the people vs enlarge people to fit the bill – as the live decider.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 / Rebuttal 2 exposure that B's own numbers concede the million-scale intake and a decades-long recurring treadmill, turning B's straw-man defense into a concession.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by decisively exposing the mathematical and structural flaws in Side B's immigration proposal. A trapped B on the numbers, showing that B's "modest" increase actually amounted to nearly a million migrants a year, and then hammered the "treadmill" nature of immigration, where today's imported care workers become tomorrow's retirees needing care. A also provided concrete, grounded examples of task-level automation (exoskeletons, ceiling lifts) to counter B's claim that automation is just an unproven forecast. B's speeches were repeatedly clipped for exceeding word limits, which disrupted their flow and left some arguments underdeveloped. A's rhetoric and structural weighing were superior.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they answered the pressure questions with concrete automation examples (exoskeletons, ceiling lifts) and turned B's admission of a 20-30 year immigration treadmill into a decisive structural disadvantage, framing automation as a one-time capital transition versus immigration as a recurring obligation that enlarges the future retiree queue.
