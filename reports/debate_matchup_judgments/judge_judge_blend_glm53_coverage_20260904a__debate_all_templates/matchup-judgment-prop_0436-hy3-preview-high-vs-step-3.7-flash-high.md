- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the motion focused on heavy default privileging that creates practical search costs and distorts restaurant visibility, not on banning ads outright. Side B had some unsupported specificity and a clipped rebuttal, but its comparative framing was clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answers to Side A's pressure questions were decisive: it explained how ads could retain value through targeted and labeled placements while defining user struggle as the real-world burden created by manipulative defaults, undercutting both Side A's revenue-collapse and no-harm claims.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 effectively exposed the economic flaw in Side B's case by pointing out that for-profit platforms will not simply absorb revenue losses from restricted ad placements, but will instead pass those costs onto the very independent restaurants Side B wants to protect through mandatory commission hikes.

A's consistent "optional vs. mandatory" framing demonstrates that stripping top sponsored placement would likely collapse ad demand, forcing platforms to replace voluntary ad spend with universal commission hikes that hurt all independents.
