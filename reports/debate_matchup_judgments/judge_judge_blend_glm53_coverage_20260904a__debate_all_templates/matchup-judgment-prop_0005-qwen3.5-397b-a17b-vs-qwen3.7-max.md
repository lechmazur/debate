### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): Both sides ran competent cases, but PRO wins on the accumulation of three advantages. First, the international comparison — that nearly every other developed nation prohibits DTC pharmaceutical advertising without producing the catastrophic awareness gap CON predicts — was introduced early and never answered. This is a live empirical counter to CON's core causal story: if Canada, Germany, and the UK function without these ads, the ban's predicted harms are not grounded in reality. Second, PRO's most effective move came in Rebuttal 2: "awareness is meaningless if the patient cannot pay the inflated price required to fund the commercial they just saw." This partial turn on CON's main economic argument was never cleanly neutralized; CON's macroeconomic cost argument assumes adherence but never addresses the access barrier created by marketing-inflated prices. Third, PRO's rhetorical framing — "medicine vs. marketplace," "trust the doctor, not the ad producer," "restoring the boundary between healing and selling" — consistently crystallized the core normative stakes in ways that made the ballot easier to cast. CON's strongest single argument was the awareness gap for asymptomatic and stigmatized conditions, and the Shingrix example was a legitimate specific counter to PRO's Q2. However, CON's claim that ads "heavily promote generics" was overstated and undercut by PRO's pointed Q2 challenge. CON's A1 answer on pharma grants (we allow grants but prohibit product ads) was coherent but raised a tension CON never fully resolved: if industry money can still fund awareness, the systemic bias PRO identified survives the ban, weakening CON's rebuttal without eliminating the funding concern. PRO's closings crystallized existing clashes cleanly; CON's closing was somewhat repetitive rather than sharpening the comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 response to CON's Q2 (emergency cost comparison): the pivot that "high drug prices caused by marketing markups are the actual barrier to access — when a medication costs three times more to fund a prime-time slot, patients skip it anyway" effectively turned CON's own economic argument. It forced the conclusion that the very advertising system CON credits with saving money is the mechanism pricing patients out of the adherence it promises. CON never cleanly answered this causal chain in the closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a consistent, well-reasoned case and forcing a fatal contradiction from Side B. In Rebuttal 2, B inexplicably argued the PRO side during A1, stating that banning ads "does not defund awareness; it stops companies from hijacking that awareness to push specific, high-margin drugs," effectively neutralizing B's own primary disadvantage. While B had an exceptional factual counter regarding mass-marketed vaccines (Shingrix, Arexvy) in A2, it could not outweigh conceding the debate's central mechanism. Furthermore, A's rebuttal that "emergency costs stem from inability to pay, not inability to see a commercial" successfully dismantled B's macroeconomic weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A2 citing Shingrix and Arexvy was a masterful use of concrete background knowledge to answer A's trap about vaccines, though A ultimately won the larger exchange due to B's A1 concession.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side A built the stronger case by anchoring on the awareness gap and directly answering pressure questions with concrete examples (e.g., Shingrix, Arexvy), neutralizing B's cost and purity arguments while exposing B's reliance on idealized public funding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal 2 (A4) cleanly answered B's pressure questions with specific vaccine examples before reframing the clash around measurable health outcomes.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side A built a tighter, more durable case across the full exchange. Three clashes proved decisive. First, the manipulation-versus-empowerment argument: A's point that ads push expensive patented drugs rather than generics or lifestyle modifications directly undercut B's "democratizing information" and "saves money" framings, and B never squarely answered it—responding only that newer drugs can sometimes be better, which concedes the market-distortion mechanism. Second, A's handling of B's Q1 contradiction (doctors too weak / patients too vulnerable) was clean and honest: it distinguished between general incompetence and structural vulnerability within a time-constrained, emotionally primed system, while B's "correlation does not prove coercion" dismissal of A's Q1 was thin and evasive. Third, A's A2 answer—calling pharma ads a "dystopian substitute for actual accessible care"—effectively reframed B's strongest equity argument without abandoning any ground. B's best moments were the Q1 pressure question (genuine internal tension) and the "ads populate exam rooms" framing, but these were answered adequately. B also relied on phrases like "silence kills" and "protect bodies not budgets" as rhetorical flourishes that did not resolve the underlying comparative. A's closing crystallized the existing clash rather than widening it; B's closing was largely recap. On format, both sides properly used A1:/A2: labels. Overall, A made the debate easier to decide by consistently narrowing toward the core question of who controls the clinical starting point, and B never offered a live route around A's market-distortion argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 A2 answer, reframing B's equity/access argument: "Relying on pharmaceutical commercials as a primary healthcare safety net for low-income patients is a dystopian substitute for actual accessible care." This single move neutralized B's most populist argument by accepting the real equity problem while denying that pharma ads were its legitimate solution—leaving B's access point with no live reply path.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a highly realistic, structurally sound mechanism (15-minute appointments, emotional priming, therapeutic trust) that convincingly explains why doctors cannot simply "just say no" to manipulated patients.
