### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): This was a tightly matched debate turning on whether reputational risk and operational risk can be cleanly separated. CON built a strong, well-grounded case that reputational aversion translates into real balance-sheet consequences (bank de-risking, merchant flight) and that forcing platforms to absorb those costs is coerced association with someone else's property. PRO's core distinction (operational risk vs. bare stigma) was clean and stayed tied to the motion's "solely" qualifier throughout, and PRO's pressure question forced CON into a decisive bite-the-bullet moment: CON explicitly conceded it would defend exclusion of a safely age-gated, non-loss-causing app purely on brand-identity grounds. PRO seized that concession in the closing to show CON was defending exactly the stigma-only exclusion the motion targets, which is the most damaging single exchange of the debate. CON's closing pivoted to a property-rights reframe ("who spends the asset") but never fully neutralized the concession's bite. CON also suffered a real execution cost: Rebuttal 2 was clipped mid-sentence before its final point landed, costing some closing punch. On balance, PRO answered the decisive pressure more directly, kept tighter fidelity to the motion's language, and ended with the cleaner, more damaging frame, giving it a modest but clear edge in a genuinely close contest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure question ("would you defend exclusion with no evidence of financial loss?") and CON's "Yes" answer, later weaponized in PRO's closing as proof CON was defending pure stigma-based exclusion, was the most decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON won by collapsing PRO's central operational/reputational distinction and establishing a persistent burden of cost and property rights that PRO never overcame. CON showed that reputational anxiety translates directly into counterparty and financial risk, making "solely reputational" exclusion practically inseparable from ordinary business judgment. CON's framing—"who spends the asset?" and "compliance keeps you out of court; it has never bought a stake in someone else's distribution network"—crystallized the debate into a clean, defensible choice for freedom of contract over compelled association. PRO's gateway theory and stigma framing were coherent but could not justify why creators' access should override processors' brand and balance-sheet interests, especially after CON extracted the concession that processors would simply absorb the losses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (Turn 4, A2.1), where they argued that what PRO calls stigma a CFO calls counterparty risk. This single move did the most damage by undermining PRO's attempt to separate operational and reputational risk into distinct categories, and it set up the asset/liability framing that dominated the rest of the debate.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A successfully isolated the core clash of the proposition by drawing a sharp, defensible line between curable operational risks and incurable reputational stigma. While Side B made a strong practical case that reputational risk translates to financial loss, Side A effectively demonstrated that accepting this framing would allow hostile third parties to veto any lawful business, rendering creator compliance meaningless. Side A also cleanly dismantled Side B's retail analogies by distinguishing payment and app infrastructure from expressive retail curation. Side B's clipped turn in Rebuttal 2 was a minor execution cost, but the decision primarily rests on Side A's cleaner conceptual framework, direct answers to pressure questions, and stronger defense of the proposition's normative boundary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where they dismantled the conflation of operational and reputational risk, showing that if financial consequence is the only metric, public hostility effectively becomes a valid reason for exclusion, giving hostile third parties a veto over lawful businesses.
