Clipping hurt both sides, but did not erase A's clearer route to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the regret-cancellation argument was decisive: accidental cancellation is generally reversible through easy re-subscription, whereas failure to cancel can produce recurring losses for months or years. Side B's claims about lost preferences and pricing narrowed that asymmetry but did not overturn it.

A also successfully weaponized B's "already illegal" argument to show that compliant firms would face zero new burden.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won narrowly by defending the natural functional meaning of “as easy”: the same channel and comparable effort, not literally identical screens. That answer preserved confirmation and retention options while neutralizing B’s small-business portal objection. B’s strongest closing claim—that same-channel cancellation permits a forty-minute hold—ignored A’s additional comparable-effort requirement. B did expose A’s weakly supported claims about the scale of harm, keeping the debate close, but its patchwork-law and market-tool responses did not answer the case for a general forward-looking standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s A4.1-A4.4 was decisive: it directly defined functional symmetry and explained that phone or invoice businesses could accept cancellation through their existing channels. This answered B’s implementation-cost pressure, while B’s subsequent hold-time objection attacked channel correspondence without addressing comparable effort.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully forcing Side A into a dilemma regarding the strictness of the rule, and by effectively minimizing the core harm. B's argument that indefinite charges are a memory problem, while deterred cancellers only lose one billing cycle, went largely unmitigated. Furthermore, B brilliantly capitalized on A's 'channel correspondence' pivot to demonstrate that A's softened rule would still permit the very phone-cancellation friction A's opening condemned.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 effectively dismantled A's 'channel correspondence' defense by pointing out it would legally bless phone holds for phone-enrolled businesses, and provided a strong logical mechanism for why deterred cancellers do not suffer indefinite charges.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by forcing a clean dilemma on the motion as written: literal “as easy as sign-up” bans confirmation screens and retention offers PRO later defended, while the softened “same channel / comparable effort” reading largely duplicates existing online-cancellation and unfairness tools and still legalizes the phone-hold gauntlet from PRO’s own opening. PRO never carried the “serious” welfare burden beyond annoyance-plus-bias, and market/legal workarounds plus channel-correspondence costs left the distinctive remedy underpowered. PRO argued dark patterns well but kept retreating on the operative test without neutralizing that clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A4/A5 lock: under PRO’s channel-correspondence fix, the phone-enrolled gym may still force phone cancellation with long holds—so the opening’s headline harm survives the headline remedy.
