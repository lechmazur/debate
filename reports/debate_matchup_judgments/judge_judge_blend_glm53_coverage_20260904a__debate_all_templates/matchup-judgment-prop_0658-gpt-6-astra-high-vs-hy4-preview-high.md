### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly decoupling strict oversight and rarity from the 'emergency' label. Side A systematically dismantling Side B's defenses, particularly by pointing out that 'more time' does not magically make failed targeted methods effective, and by using B's own architectural safeguard explanation to resolve the deletion audit dilemma. Side B's insistence that non-emergencies are inherently routine or unbounded was effectively neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 successfully turned B's architectural explanation into a concession that safe, auditable deployments are possible outside of emergencies, while simultaneously destroying B's reliance on targeted tools by noting that time does not make an inadequate method effective.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won the decisive clash: once both sides agreed justification and design limits are needed, PRO never adequately showed why imminence must be the exclusive gate rather than joint necessity/proportionality review. A forced B’s honest “Yes” that demonstrated necessity still loses without emergency, then flipped B’s own deletion/architecture concession and blocked the “routine expansion” caricature. PRO’s anonymity/general-warrant frame stayed sticky and rhetorically strong, but it applies to uses PRO allows and did not beat A’s cleaner weigh that frequency alone is not proportionality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 (esp. A4.3–A4.7): flips PRO’s transient-matcher concession, rejects “routine” conflation, and crystallizes that PRO excludes proportionate non-emergency uses solely for lack of imminence.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON kept a live route that emergency-only is overbroad. PRO's core harm - anonymity dies at the scan - is undercut because PRO itself allows that same scan in emergencies, conceding proportionality can exist. PRO answered A3 Q1 with a candid 'Yes' - even proven necessity for a serious exploitation network loses without imminence - which made the prohibition look rigid. CON turned PRO's strongest audit pressure: PRO admitted the isolated matcher and aggregate tallies work, then conceded they are 'not technologically unavailable outside emergencies,' resolving the paradox and showing bounded, non-retained, independently approved scans can be audited without creating a dossier. That left PRO without a principled reason why imminence is the only gateway.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's turn on deletion auditing - showing PRO's own emergency architecture proves verification without per-person logs, so the alleged unauditability does not distinguish emergencies from ordinary strict authorization - plus the point that if scan itself were intolerable, emergencies could not be allowed.
