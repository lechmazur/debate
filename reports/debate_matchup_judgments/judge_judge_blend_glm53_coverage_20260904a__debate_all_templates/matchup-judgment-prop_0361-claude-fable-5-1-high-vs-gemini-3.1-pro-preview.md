### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by explaining why exclusivity creates the urgency and public attention that both theatrical and later streaming revenue depend on. Side A identified a plausible momentum cost but never adequately established that rapid subscription availability produces durable retention rather than substitution and churn. Its examples were also weaker than advertised: Encanto retained a thirty-day window, Universal's model involved premium rental, and Glass Onion did not substantiate lasting subscriber effects. B's repeated clipping was a meaningful execution cost that kept the win competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure questions exposed the central evidentiary weakness: A selectively dismissed pandemic-era theatrical evidence while relying on Encanto from the same period, then characterized premium rental as subscription streaming. A answered directly but did not resolve the retention burden, allowing B to crystallize that A's principal examples still used theatrical windows.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): B won by locking the decisive comparison: a short window is nearly free (CON conceded early BO concentration) yet protects scarcity that manufactures opening, WOM, and later streaming value. B’s pressure questions exposed A’s 2021 inconsistency and forced A to rely on windowed or hybrid cases (Encanto’s 30 days, Glass Onion’s theatrical week, Universal’s hit/hold rental model). A’s momentum and access lines were live but never produced a clean day-and-date subscription counterexample that broke B’s causal story, so the vote stays with preserving a meaningful window.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s flip of the “dead zone” (window is cheap insurance for big openings; remove it and the dead zone starts day one) plus the Encanto/2021 consistency pressure that turned A’s best examples into support for windows.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won the central trade-off. CON conceded most money lands in weeks 1-3, so B turned the 45-day window into nearly-free insurance that protects the opening by making waiting irrational. B exposed inconsistency in CON's evidence: CON dismissed 2021 as pandemic-distorted to ignore Warner day-and-date failure, then used Encanto (Nov 2021, 30-day exclusive) and Universal 17-day paid rental and Glass Onion 1-week theatrical as proof windows fail — all examples that actually contain windows. CON never produced a major film rushed day-and-date to subscription that created durable hit + retained subs, while B cited the industry trial and reversal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Pressure Q1/Q2 and Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: showed CON's case is built on windowed movies and that late-week emptiness is the guarantee that makes weeks 1-3 big, collapsing CON's dead-zone and Encanto/Glass Onion momentum claims.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): B won by establishing the cleaner comparative: a meaningful window earns incremental transactional revenue while preserving later streaming value. A sharply attacked exhibitor splits, marketing costs, and delayed retention, but never adequately showed that the resulting subscriber gains would outweigh foregone box office revenue. B's claims about typical net profits were overstated, and its mistaken reference IDs cost it slightly, while A's clipped second rebuttal was also an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to A's alleged contradiction was decisive: zero direct revenue per stream can coexist with indirect retention value, and delaying streaming permits studios to collect theatrical revenue before receiving that library benefit. A's claim that the same household cannot support both streams did not fully defeat that additive model.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): A won by making the peak-demand ownership clash decisive: theatrical hands the studio half of a marketing-driven window while starving its own platform and retention timing, whereas streaming keeps the customer at the moment demand exists. B’s “capture both” and library-validation story never recovered from the zero-marginal-vs-retention contradiction or the repeated failure to supply a credible net theatrical figure for typical major titles. A’s sharper weighing and pressure use outweighed the format clip on A’s second rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.2 forcing B’s zero-marginal claim to collapse B’s own retention/lifecycle model, then crystallizing it in closing against B5.2/B5.6.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the central economic clash. B argued theatrical gives hard cash and streaming gives zero marginal revenue, but A showed that contradicts B's own "premium retention driver" lifecycle. A's timing argument - that a subscriber lost in month 2 never sees month 4's library, and ticket urgency cannibalizes retention - went unanswered, and B failed its pressure test to name a net theatrical return. B's piracy and double-culture claims were neutralized. Clipped A4 is a cost but did not erase the decisive contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 / Pressure Q1 exposing B's "zero marginal revenue" vs "retention driver" contradiction - B never resolved how a view worth $0 in month 1 becomes valuable in month 4 while still claiming both revenues.
