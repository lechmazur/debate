### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that A's documentation requirement either collapses into ordinary originality analysis once coherent recollection suffices, or uniquely forfeits genuine hybrid works when stricter records are demanded. B also persuasively distinguished human origination from control over every final detail through photography and process-art examples, which A never adequately answered. A's commons argument remained plausible but did not overcome B's case that existing doctrine already denies protection to one-prompt machine expression while protecting only demonstrable human contributions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, explained why a one-prompt claimant already loses under originality doctrine, and then showed that A's softened evidentiary standard cannot block assertions unavailable under B's approach. This turned the motion's distinctive effect into erroneous forfeiture rather than improved authorship screening.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by forcing Side B into a destructive dilemma regarding the definition of "documentation." When Side B conceded that "ordinary recollection" could satisfy the burden, Side A brilliantly capitalized by showing that Side B's rule either adds nothing to existing originality analysis or functions strictly as an unearned forfeiture trap for legitimate creators. Side A also introduced unanswered counter-examples (Pollock, resin pouring) to prove that "control over final expression" is historically inaccurate as a copyright standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of Side B's answer to Q1, pointing out that current doctrine already denies protection to a "one prompt and accept" user, leaving Side B's rule with no unique benefits and only unique harms.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON wins the central concession: PRO allows ordinary recollection, collapsing its documentation gate to ordinary originality, and its only named denied case (one-prompt) is already denied by ideas-not-protectable.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 pressure and Rebuttal 2 showing PRO's sole distinctive denial is already handled by existing originality doctrine, leaving the motion's only effect as a harmful forfeiture default.
