### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won because B conceded that supervision improves survival for covered consumption, while its coverage objection showed only that sites are incomplete, not that they fail to reduce deaths at the margin. A also framed the disorder comparison more plausibly as managed indoor use versus an existing open scene. B's opportunity-cost and Latin America challenges kept the debate competitive, but its demand for a visible city-wide mortality decline relied on an overly blunt metric and its fixed-queue harms remained insufficiently established.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest response was that rising province-wide deaths under a worsening poisoned supply do not disprove a counterfactual reduction from supervised use. Once B admitted that a person beside oxygen and naloxone fares better, the “seatbelt bolted to one chair” analogy limited the scale of the benefit rather than eliminating it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by relentlessly enforcing the motion's burden (city-wide impact) and dismantling Side B's mechanisms. Side A effectively separated the benefits of distributing naloxone from the necessity of a supervised consumption chair, rendering Side B's final defense moot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's reversal of the seatbelt analogy in Rebuttal 2—pointing out that a consumption room is a seatbelt bolted to one chair, whereas pocket naloxone travels with the user—was decisive.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON wins by exposing a persistent gap between the room and the city. They pressed PRO to defend city-wide outcomes, not just zero deaths inside a facility, and PRO never accepted that burden, retreating to unobservable counterfactuals. CON leveraged the Vancouver example and western Canadian closures to show that celebrated rooms coexisted with terrible province-wide mortality and neighborhood disorder. In Latin America, CON turned PRO's concession that death reduction is minimal into a decisive liability: the disorder half of the motion backfires when a fixed, state-sanctioned site creates a enforcement-soft customer base for armed groups. PRO's "factory that hands out seatbelts" reframe was rhetorically sharp but implicitly conceded that the supervised chair itself is dispensable, and CON crystallized that as a collapse into CON's own alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dismantling of the seatbelt analogy and the Latin America turn: after PRO conceded that averted overdose deaths in Latin America are "smaller" and that the "second promise does the heavy lifting," CON showed that the disorder promise becomes a fixed queue for traffickers, leaving half the motion surrendered by concession and the other half running backward.
