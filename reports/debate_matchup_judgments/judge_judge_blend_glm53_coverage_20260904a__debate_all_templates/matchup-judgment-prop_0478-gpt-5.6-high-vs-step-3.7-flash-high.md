- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but PRO (B) sustained a cleaner, more disciplined argument across rounds. CON (A) leaned heavily on a specific, unsupported "Prop 22, 12% pay cut" statistic introduced mid-debate, which is exactly the kind of brittle fake precision the format discourages; B directly and correctly flagged this as unsupported, damaging A's strongest empirical claim. A's mechanism for platform "capping" also assumed platforms can track a worker's cross-platform aggregate earnings, a weakness B exploited but A never resolved. Both handled Pressure Questions with proper A1/A2 formatting, but B's answers were more responsive to the underlying mechanism, while A's answers largely re-asserted prior claims. B's closing crystallized the normative stakes (who bears predictable labor risk) more sharply than A's recap-heavy closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response in Rebuttal 2 (B4.1-B4.5) directly dismantling CON's Prop 22 statistic as unsupported while reasserting that cross-platform aggregation defeats single-platform hour-capping was the most damaging exchange, since it undercut CON's core empirical anchor without CON offering a comparable correction.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the clash on risk-shifting and proportional design while neutralizing B's incentives and diversity attacks as non-essential to a well-built floor; B over-relied on gaming inevitability and unsupported Prop 22 exact stats that A rightly discarded as invented arithmetic of no force here, failing to justify why fragmentation should erase all labor-cost responsibility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 answers plus value weighing: phase-in/aggregation plus "benefits count as compensation" collapsed B's pass-through and cliff claims into attacks on non-required designs.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing that fragmenting work across platforms should not function as a loophole to externalize labor costs onto workers and the public. CONs Proposition 22 example and reclassification concerns kept the debate competitive but did not fully overcome PROs core comparative: protection against catastrophic risk has real value that cannot be reduced to immediate cash wages alone.
