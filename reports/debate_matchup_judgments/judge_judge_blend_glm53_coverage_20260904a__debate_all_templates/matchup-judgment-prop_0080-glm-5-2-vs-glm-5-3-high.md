### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won by establishing the cleaner comparison between negotiated, enforceable terms and rules institutions can change unilaterally. Side B compellingly exposed A's overclaim that football bargaining would automatically protect non-revenue athletes, but did not defeat A's multi-unit answer or show why fragmented collective bargaining is worse than the already fragmented status quo. B's flexibility defense also underanswered A's point that such flexibility largely belongs to schools, while bargaining agreements can expire and be renegotiated. A's clipped opening and speculative Title IX claims narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's sharpest attack was that sport-specific units cannot promise signatures or protections to walk-ons outside those units. Side A partially neutralized this by distinguishing separate bargaining units and showing that the motion does not require one nationwide unit, while also turning rigidity against B: negotiated terms can be renewed, whereas ad hoc rules can change without athlete consent.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON exposed a sustained tension in PRO's case: by conceding sport-by-sport bargaining units to manage scope, PRO stranded the non-revenue athletes their own rhetoric promised to protect. CON crystallized that the walk-on PRO invoked was not represented by the quarterback's bargaining unit, and that formal bargaining adds rigid, fixed revenue-sport costs that make non-revenue cuts more likely rather than less. PRO successfully narrowed the motion to blunt the fragmentation attack, but never fully answered why a football unit would bargain for swimmers or why bargaining does not functionally capture the athletic department budget for the benefit of a caste of revenue athletes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 [A2.1-A2.3] flipping PRO's sport-by-sport concession into a "permanent caste system" that excludes the very athletes PRO cited as beneficiaries, a frame PRO never fully escaped.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's scope - schools that directly share revenue - which neutralized CON's 350-school fragmentation attack and let PRO recast conferences as a de facto employer bloc. On the decisive walk-on clash, PRO's signature-before vs lawsuit-after framing held: CON's answer of Title IX claims and transfer rights conceded post-harm litigation, while PRO showed budget cuts flow from the decision to pay, not from bargaining, and that Title IX plus multi-unit bargaining gives the only ex ante seat. CON's caste-system point was real but left non-revenue athletes with flexibility that means absorbing rules others rewrite.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2/A4 response to "who bargains for whom" and Title IX - engineering via sport-by-sport units, employer-side obligation, and narrowing to revenue-sharing schools to defeat the 350-school objection - was the most damaging exchange.
