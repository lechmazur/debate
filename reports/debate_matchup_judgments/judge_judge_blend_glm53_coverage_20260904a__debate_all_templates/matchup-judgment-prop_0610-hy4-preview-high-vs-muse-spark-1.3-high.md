### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by defeating A's broad-or-nothing framing. B offered a plausible boundary around basic materials and material-like processed goods, then showed why extending the adjustment across most finished industrial products adds administrative difficulty and retaliatory exposure for diminishing climate benefit. A powerfully framed leakage as certain and retaliation as temporary, but never adequately established why retaliation would remain marginal or why a targeted charge could not preserve the ETS.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal answered the evasion challenge by distinguishing plate, pipe, castings, and clinker from complex machines, while contrasting traceable material benchmarks with economy-wide defaults. That directly weakened A's claim that every workable alternative is merely a broad adjustment renamed.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by accepting leakage as a real problem and then pinning the motion’s maximalism: broad coverage of most industrial goods even under major-partner retaliation. PRO’s home-market/certain-leakage framing was strong and memorable, but CON progressively showed that sprawling coverage creates an export-catalogue retaliation target that itself drives carve-outs and ETS dilution—the outcome PRO claims to prevent. The refined CON stop-line (plate/pipe/extrusion/casting/clinker vs finished assemblies) answered the “broad renamed” pressure without collapsing into surrender, making the “even if retaliate” burden the decisive, unresolved cost of voting PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.4 answering B’s pressure Qs: concrete material-content stop-line plus denying that narrow CBAM gives partners a veto over the ETS rather than over a self-defeating broad tax.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON granted the principle of border adjustments but successfully showed that a sprawling CBAM on most industrial goods creates unadministrable defaults, a massive target list for retaliation, and the political economy dynamics—carve-outs, ETS dilution, and bailouts—that would undermine the very climate ambition PRO seeks. PRO’s evasion argument remained live, but CON’s distinction between taxing embedded basic materials and taxing entire value chains provided a cleaner, enforceable alternative that better satisfied the motion’s "even if" retaliation condition. PRO’s "retaliation is temporary" framing never fully overcame CON’s specific mechanism by which export-sector pain forces policy collapse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s distinction in Rebuttal 2 between material-content goods (plate, pipe, extrusion, casting, clinker) and assembled machines, which directly answered PRO’s pressure question and blunted the "narrow is just broad renamed" attack by establishing a principled, narrower scope.
