### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing a persistent enforcement fork. If market access is genuinely withdrawn for disclosure failures, patients can lose an otherwise beneficial medicine; if withdrawal is reserved for concealment that already undermines the benefit-risk judgment, ordinary regulatory powers largely address that case. A also offered a more directly targeted trial-authorization regime that reaches abandoned trials the motion misses. B strongly established the harms of publication bias and reasonably defended graduated enforcement, but never fully explained the distinct necessity of the market-access lever over strong posting orders, turnover-based fines, and trial-conduct sanctions. B's repeated nonexistent C-reference IDs were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to B's concrete withdrawal scenario was decisive: B chose deliberate concealment of material safety or efficacy evidence, allowing A to argue that the authorization itself could already be suspended on benefit-risk grounds. This preserved A's claim that the proposed lever is either harmful in ordinary cases or redundant in grave ones.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B systematically dismantled Side A's mechanism by proving that conditioning market access structurally cannot reach abandoned trials, and that for approved drugs, Side A's withdrawal lever either collapses into existing safety laws or relies on the exact fines Side B proposed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 decisively turned Side A's concrete example of a hidden safety signal, pointing out that existing law already allows withdrawal if a drug's benefit-risk profile collapses, leaving the motion with no unique marginal benefit.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing the structural flaw in Side A's mechanism: conditioning market access only applies to drugs that actually reach the market, leaving the "dark corner" of abandoned trials untouched. Side B's alternative—conditioning the ethics license to conduct the trial—was a superior, comprehensive solution. Furthermore, Side B's "fork" argument forced Side A into a corner where market access withdrawal was either too harmful to patients or entirely redundant with existing regulatory powers, effectively neutralizing Side A's core leverage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side A's concrete example of withdrawal by showing that existing law already covers benefit-risk collapse, and introduced the "license to experiment" as a flawless alternative to capture abandoned trials.
