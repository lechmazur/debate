### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing the unresolved tension at the center of B’s case: the preference was portrayed as rarely consequential for admissions yet indispensable for maintaining donations. B’s late claim that different institutional outreach strategies explain its conflicting accounts of post-reform giving was unsupported and weakened the claim that preferences themselves are necessary. A also offered the cleaner fairness comparison and a direct administrative mechanism. B correctly noted that displaced applicants are not automatically disadvantaged, but never justified lineage as the appropriate tiebreaker or grounded its sweeping claims about scholarship losses. B’s clipped first rebuttal was an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange argued that a legacy boost cannot simultaneously be too minor to cause meaningful displacement and sufficiently valuable to donors to sustain a critical funding pipeline. B conceded that marginal non-legacies lose seats and answered the funding inconsistency only with a late, unsupported distinction between institutions.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by forcing and crystallizing a live structural contradiction: CON needed the legacy boost to be trivial enough to displace almost no one yet load-bearing enough to sustain alumni funding. A repeatedly made that dual burden unstable, turned the funding defense into a regressive transfer, and answered pressure cleanly. B offered a coherent access-funding story and useful holistic-review points, but suffered a clipped rebuttal, mixed giving narratives, and never fully neutralized the zero-sum/margin trap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 trap: legacy cannot be both minor (no real displacement/cost) and decisive (necessary to keep donor checks flowing).
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won the central contradiction clash: CON needed legacy to be both trivial enough to harm no one and vital enough to fund aid. B exposed this in R1 and in pressure Q1, and crystallized it in closing. CON's opening claim of giving erosion vs R1 claim of redirected giving boosting low-income support was never cleanly reconciled, and the clipped R1 added execution cost. PRO also answered CON's pressure cleanly while CON never answered who loses the zero-sum seat.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B5 line that legacy cannot be both too small to displace anyone and too important to remove without collapsing giving — framing it as a regressive transfer and asking what donors are actually buying — collapsed CON's funding + minor-factor defenses.
