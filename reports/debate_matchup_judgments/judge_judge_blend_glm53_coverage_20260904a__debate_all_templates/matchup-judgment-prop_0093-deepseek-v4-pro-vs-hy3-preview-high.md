### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO wins on the central throughput comparison that CON never fully neutralized. PRO's Rebuttal 2 math—ten displaced parking spots serving roughly 30 daily parkers versus an hourly bus carrying 180 people over a twelve-hour day—is the clearest quantitative comparison in the debate. CON challenged the assumptions but could not show the arithmetic reverses even under conservative conditions. PRO also held the stronger structural equity position: the motion targets curb allocation for the full urban population, where carless and transit-dependent households are the majority, and CON's counter-claim (roughly 60% of low-income households own cars) is presented with brittle confidence in a closed-book setting and applies only to a subset of a subset. CON's best move throughout was the "no guardrails" argument—the motion mandates no dynamic pricing, no frequency upgrades—and the SFpark dynamic-pricing objection was sharp and correctly landed. PRO's response (reallocation shrinks the driving pool regardless of pricing; cities retain authority to add permits or time limits; reallocation targets corridors where buses already exist) was adequate but not overwhelming, leaving CON with a live partial credit on implementation risk. However, this partial credit cannot close the throughput gap PRO established. PRO's repeated clipping across three speeches (Opening, Rebuttal 1, Closing) is a real execution cost that trimmed some developed arguments and contributed to occasional repetition in visible portions, but the visible core case remained coherent. CON was more disciplined in word count and made targeted rebuttals, but never assembled a comparative case strong enough to overcome PRO's fundamental arithmetic on curb efficiency and the broader equity distribution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 throughput math (A4.3): ten eliminated parking spots displacing roughly thirty daily parkers versus an hourly bus moving 180 passengers over a twelve-hour operating day. CON challenged the assumptions in B4.3 but could not demonstrate the calculation reverses under realistic adjustments, leaving this as the decisive unrebutted comparison of the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO wins by establishing a decisive throughput-and-equity frame that survives CON's counterarguments. CON successfully pressed the lack of mandated pricing and frequency guardrails, but PRO's second rebuttal showed that even modest bus ridership dramatically outperforms parking on a per-curb basis, undercutting the speculative low-ridership nightmare. CON's "no guardrails" critique lost comparative force because its own alternative demand-management policies are likewise outside the motion's text, and PRO's equity case remained live: CON never explained why the entire curb must be reserved for private car storage rather than using targeted permits to aid the car-dependent minority while reallocating the rest to higher-throughput public uses. The clipped opening and closing were minor execution costs that did not prevent PRO from presenting a complete comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (A4), which used clean arithmetic to answer B's low-ridership scenario and reversed the "guardrails" objection back onto CON by noting that targeted permits could aid vulnerable drivers without sacrificing the whole curb.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A wins by anchoring the debate to a clear, decisive metric: person-throughput per 20-foot curb segment. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A (PRO) built the cleaner and more sustained path to victory. Its core comparative — "for every car owner who loses a spot, hundreds of bus riders gain faster trips, delivery drivers skip double-parking delays, and cyclists gain safe passage" — was the debate's sharpest framing and went unrefuted as a proportional weighing argument. PRO correctly anchored the debate to dense cities (where curb space contention is actually live) and plausibly argued that the vast majority of low-wage urban residents there rely on transit rather than cars, which defanged CON's equity inversion across most rounds. PRO's off-peak flexibility answer to CON's night-shift pressure was responsive and practically grounded. CON landed some genuine hits — the "bait-and-switch" label for off-peak parking was clever, and the bike-lane-as-higher-income-privilege reframe was the most original move in the debate — but CON's enforcement-only alternative (cameras, dynamic meters, timed windows) never fully explained why decades of status-quo enforcement have failed to solve double-parking, and it relied too heavily on edge-case workers rather than engaging the median urban resident PRO kept foregrounding. Two format clips on CON's Opening and Rebuttal 1 also cut off arguments mid-sentence, materially weakening CON's case on businesses and off-street garages. PRO's discipline in directly answering both pressure questions with A1:/A2: labels and then zooming back to comparative weighing showed better execution discipline overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 response to CON's cruising claim [A2.2–A2.3] was the most decisive exchange. PRO correctly identified that cruising is caused by over-supplying cheap parking (not its removal), pointed out that double-parked delivery trucks are the larger congestion driver, and landed the memorable observation that the "tangle of frustrated drivers" CON predicted is already the status quo — making CON's doomsday scenario a description of the present rather than a consequence of reform. CON never cleanly recovered from this reversal.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by framing the proposition as a rebalancing of finite public space toward higher-utility uses, showing that protecting most parking harms more low-wage residents than it helps, and directly neutralizing CON's strongest equity objections with a flexible off-peak curb-use model. CON's reliance on edge cases, an unverified enforcement alternative, and clipped opening and first-rebuttal speeches left its business and congestion arguments underdeveloped and less persuasive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where the off-peak bus-lane parking proposal defused CON's night-shift worker argument, demonstrated that the motion requires reallocating most space rather than eliminating all car access, and sharpened the equity comparison by showing CON's case rested on atypical cases.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B wins by successfully turning Side A's equity framing and flexibility concession against them. While A argued efficiently about throughput and regressive parking subsidies, B grounded the clash in concrete, resilient demographics (night-shift workers, tradespeople, transit-poor families) that A repeatedly dismissed as "edge cases" without fully neutralizing. B's sharpest moment came in Rebuttal 2, where it exploited A's off-peak bus lane parking concession as a "bait-and-switch" that still strands irregular workers, effectively winning the equity weigh. B also undercut A's necessity claim by offering a plausible enforcement and timed-window alternative for double-parking, which A never squarely defeated. B incurred a minor execution cost for two clipped turns, but its clash management, comparative weighing, and rhetorical precision were consistently stronger across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 turn of Side A's off-peak flexibility concession: B argued that allowing parking in bus lanes only off-peak admits the need for spots but actively traps irregular-shift workers during peaks, reframing A's adaptation as a regressive bait-and-switch rather than a solution. This decisively flipped the equity clash.
