### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides competed well across the full exchange, but PRO held a narrow but real advantage on the decisive clash. CON's strongest move was the "gap has collapsed" argument: PRO conceded courts, forensics, preservation standards, and independent representation procedures, so CON claimed the positions were identical. PRO answered this pressure effectively in A4.1 by naming concrete decisions—refusing bone display, requiring reburial, insisting on coercion framing over "industrial heritage"—where substantial control and consultation genuinely diverge: in a consultation model the institution retains the override; in a substantial-control model descendant authorization is the default requiring affirmative justification to reverse. CON asserted these outcomes would follow from consultation anyway but did not argue it; that assertion-without-argument is a real gap. PRO's property-and-custody analogy held up: substantial but not absolute is how almost every recognized right functions, and CON's use of bounded-ness to deny the substance of the right did not succeed. The Confederate reframe was the most original single move in the debate—CON invoked descendant control as a warning, but PRO correctly identified that Lost Cause mythology arose from perpetrators' descendants dominating commemoration of people they enslaved, the precise opposite of victims' descendants controlling sites of their own victimization. CON's representation problem (who counts at Srebrenica?) was genuine and never fully resolved by PRO, and the point that representation selection IS narrative selection at contested sites had some force. But PRO's heir-analogy response—courts identify heirs without owning the estate; neutral procedures identify representatives without taking over governance—was the stronger analogy and CON never squarely dismantled it. On weighing: the default outcome matters most, and PRO preserved a live, coherent route to victory on that ground even after concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Confederate counter-example in A4.6 was the most decisive rebuttal exchange. CON used Lost Cause mythology as a warning against descendant control shaping narrative. PRO flipped it: that mythology flourished precisely because it was perpetrators' descendants who dominated commemoration of the people they had enslaved and oppressed. At the atrocity and forced-labor sites the motion covers, substantial control for the targeted victims' descendants is the antidote to that distortion, not its replication. CON never answered this distinction between perpetrators' and victims' descendant control, leaving the flip intact.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): The debate was genuinely competitive, with PRO landing a strong moral case and an unrefuted historical indictment of institutional stewardship. However, CON won the central definitional clash. Throughout the debate CON pressed the question of what "substantial" actually means in practice—whether it is consultation with real weight or binding authority—and PRO could not hold a stable position. In Rebuttal 2 A1, PRO explicitly conceded that substantial control includes real authority to "delay, modify, or refuse specific procedures" like DNA testing and excavation. CON immediately identified this as the decisive moment: once that power exists, identification of missing persons, prosecution evidence, and honest public records become contingent on community permission, which is a concrete harm that PRO never adequately defused. PRO's next-of-kin analogy was interesting but did not resolve the difference between individual medical authority and community-level binding authority over a public site serving plural purposes. CON's pressure questions were sharper and more surgically targeted than PRO's, forcing the A1 concession rather than merely requesting a comparative empirical example. PRO's strongest unrebutted point—that CON's "plural accountability" recombines the same institutions with the same design flaws—was real, but CON's response (diversify and constrain rather than transfer) was coherent even if somewhat thin. The closing round crystallized the decisive clash cleanly on both sides, but CON's final pivot to "participation without ownership" framed the resolution of the debate more precisely than PRO's appeal to historical failure, which CON had already acknowledged without conceding the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 exploitation of PRO's A1 concession was the most decisive exchange. PRO admitted substantial control includes real authority to "delay, modify, or refuse" excavation, DNA testing, and display. CON correctly argued this means identification of missing persons, prosecution evidence, and public records can be blocked by community permission—converting what PRO had framed as dignified inclusion into a binding veto over shared evidence. PRO never fully reconciled the next-of-kin analogy with community-level authority over a public atrocity site serving plural purposes beyond the descendant group's grief.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by cleanly separating 'mandatory participation' from 'binding control,' thereby neutralizing Side A's historical examples of institutional abuse while exposing the risks of giving one group veto power over public evidence and crime scenes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, effectively using Side A's answer to the pressure question to show that community veto power could block dissenting families from identifying their own dead or prosecutors from accessing evidence.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by showing that PRO's "substantial control" necessarily includes binding authority to delay or block excavation and evidence, which risks subordinating truth, justice, and minority dissent to a single stakeholder. CON's alternative of enforceable participation within plural, reviewable institutions better handled internal disagreement and institutional bias without creating a new monopoly over public evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal, which seized on PRO's admission that substantial control includes binding authority to delay or refuse excavation and DNA testing, showing this could block identification, prosecution, and truthful public explanation, and exposing the weakness of "structured deliberation" without independent review.
