### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent frameworks, but PRO's analogies (dialysis, insulin, pacemaker) more successfully neutralized CON's core "restoration vs. bypass" distinction by showing that accepted basic-care treatments routinely overcome rather than cure underlying pathology. CON's central move—that IVF's benefit "lands" in a third party rather than the patient's own body—was asserted repeatedly but never fully defended against PRO's circularity charge (that reproduction is arbitrarily excluded as a bodily function merely because its natural endpoint is another person, while contraception and prenatal care are allowed in). PRO's answers to the pressure questions (A1/A2) directly and sharply turned CON's "what is repaired?" challenge back on CON by pointing out it silently reinstated a cure requirement CON had earlier disclaimed. PRO also more effectively pressed the equity/rationing comparison (transparent clinical rationing vs. wealth-based exclusion), which CON answered only weakly ("wealth already rations many desired goods"). CON's case was clean and consistently argued but ultimately rested on an assumption—that pregnancy achieved via IVF isn't a bodily-function outcome—that PRO kept exposing as question-begging rather than demonstrated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 ("Nothing must be anatomically repaired for function to be medically restored... CON concedes cure is unnecessary, then quietly reinstates a cure requirement") was the most decisive exchange, directly answering CON's pointed pressure question while exposing an internal inconsistency in CON's framework that CON's final rebuttal did not fully repair.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won a close definitional clash by making B’s restore-vs-bypass line hard to hold. The insulin/dialysis/pacemaker analogies, plus the contraception asymmetry and non-circular rationing frame, did more damage to B’s live route than B’s third-party-benefit boundary did to A’s. B stayed coherent and disciplined, but largely restated the same end-point test and never fully neutralized wealth-as-gatekeeper or the “cure smuggled back in” pressure. A made the proposition easier to decide after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A2.1/A4.1 answer that medicine routinely restores function without curing underlying pathology, turning B’s “patient remains infertile” point into a revived cure requirement.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO won the core definitional clash. CON required basic care restore the patient's own body and claimed IVF creates a third-party beneficiary. PRO showed that standard fails for accepted bypass treatments (dialysis, insulin, pacemaker, prosthesis) and that contraception/prenatal care are healthcare despite involving a future child. Becoming pregnant and gestating is restoration of the patient's own reproductive function, even if tubes remain blocked. PRO also answered scarcity with bounded basic care and transparent rationing, while CON's opportunity-cost argument applied to all care and left wealth as the de facto gatekeeper.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that CON concedes cure is unnecessary but then demands anatomical repair after IVF success, likening it to a pacemaker leaving heart disease intact - exposing CON's restore/bypass distinction as a reinstated cure requirement.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won by consistently forcing the debate onto the scarcity/opportunity-cost terrain the motion actually requires, then exploiting a genuine structural tension in PRO's case: PRO wanted "basic" to mean both a guaranteed entitlement and merely a clinically sequenced queue item that can be postponed for higher priorities. CON's Rebuttal 2/Closing (A4.5, A5.1) named this dilemma cleanly and PRO never resolved it—its closing simply reasserted "clinical need decides the queue" without explaining how that squares with genuine guaranteed access. CON also repeatedly pressed that cycle caps still let wealth buy extra cycles after public limits are exhausted; PRO's own answer (B4.3) effectively conceded this ("tragedy CON would let the rich buy anyway") without neutralizing it, which undercuts PRO's central "medicine not money decides" framing. PRO's disease-based threshold and dialysis/chemo analogies were rhetorically strong and gave the debate real clash, but CON's distinction (life/function-preserving vs. uncertain, non-restorative substitution) blunted their force, and PRO never showed exclusion is net costlier or that IVF beats competing claims on scarce resources across the varied "countries" the motion covers. Both sides argued with good grounding and no fake precision, but CON's rebuttals did more damage to PRO's live routes than PRO's did to CON's, and CON's closing crystallized the decisive tension most clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5) pointing out that PRO's "sequencing" answer is self-defeating—if IVF can always be postponed for higher priorities, basic status guarantees nothing; if it's truly guaranteed, something else is displaced—combined with the unanswered "wealth still decides after caps" point that PRO's own B4.3 implicitly conceded.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash on what “basic” means under scarcity. A’s disease-status, two-tier, and substitution analogies were forceful, but never settled why IVF must outrank competing needs across countries or how sequencing leaves a real guarantee rather than an empty label. B kept pressing displacement, overinclusive thresholds, and that caps still reintroduce wealth; A answered partially but left the universal-priority burden unresolved. B’s clipped rebuttal was a real cost, yet the allocation case still carried the round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s reply that PRO’s “sequence IVF after childbirth/higher needs” hollows out basic status: either it displaces care or the guarantee becomes indefinite postponement.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash on scarcity and definition of basic. PRO's floor-with-sequencing conceded CON's point that IVF can be postponed whenever safe childbirth etc remains unfunded, making 'basic guarantee' incoherent, and never answered which service loses funding in 'countries' including low-resource settings. CON's restoration-vs-bypass distinction also blunted PRO's dialysis/insulin/joint analogy, while PRO's hidden-costs-savings claim was speculative and directly rebutted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 + Closing combo that (1) substitution preventing death/deterioration differs from uncertain creation and (2) basic cannot be both an enforceable right and an indefinitely sequenced queue, exposing PRO's contradiction.
