### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely original mechanisms — A's "release valve" (segmentation removes institutional pressure to fix the general pool) and driver-composition-skew arithmetic were the debate's most distinctive contributions, and A's pressure questions (especially on the race/gender consistency problem) were sharp. But B answered the pressure questions more directly and coherently, offered a principled (if contestable) risk-axis distinction for the race analogy, reframed the "labeling effect" as paternalism effectively, and closed by crisply isolating the decisive question of who bears the burden of proof — a frame A never fully dislodged. Execution also favored B: A's Rebuttal 1, Rebuttal 2, and Closing were all clipped for exceeding the word limit, with the closing cut off mid-argument, undercutting A's ability to crystallize its strongest points at the moment it mattered most. B had only one clipped turn. On balance, B made the clash easier to resolve and suffered less format damage, giving it a clear but competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to the race-analogy pressure question (A3.2) — distinguishing gender as "the variable that constitutes the harm" versus race as a "confounded proxy," backed by the medical-staff/train-car precedent — was the most consequential exchange, since it directly answered A's strongest slippery-slope pressure and reframed the debate's central principle question, even though A's rebuttal (that time/location/context are also proxies for gender-based risk) kept the point live rather than fully closing it.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO won the central comparison. CON's harms—labeling effect, release valve, and male-skewed general pool—rely on a fixed-pool assumption that PRO directly undercut: women already perceive risk [A2.1], and a women-only toggle can create net-new rider and driver participation rather than just fragment existing supply [A2.3-A2.4]. PRO's wheelchair-accessible and train-car analogies neutralized the protected-class objection, and its risk-axis criterion answered the race slippery slope [A4.4]. CON's driver-cohort math [B4.4] is its best point but still assumes no offsetting entry, and repeated clipping plus a speculative institutional story left its cost case less grounded than PRO's minutes-vs-agency weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's [A2.1]-[A2.4] rebuttal that the 2 AM woman loses nothing, the option responds to pre-existing fear, and safety is part of supply economics—exposing CON's fixed-pool premise and turning fragmentation into net-new capacity.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A built a highly coherent case around net-new supply and the direct relevance of gender to the specific risk of harassment, effectively neutralizing Side B's race analogy and release valve arguments. Side B introduced strong structural critiques regarding the two-tier system and general pool skew, but suffered severe execution costs by getting clipped mid-sentence in three separate speeches, including the final closing. Side A's cleaner execution, sharper rebuttals, and successful framing of concrete benefits versus speculative costs secured a solid victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the race analogy in Rebuttal 2, distinguishing gender as the direct axis of the specific harm rather than a confounded proxy, effectively dismantled Side B's slippery slope argument while sharply countering the general pool skew claim.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON (A) built a tighter, more damaging case around three linked mechanisms: (1) soft gender verification doesn't just fail neutrally, it can actively pre-sort the least-suspicious victims for a bad actor who games the field; (2) a marketed "women-only" tier implicitly relabels the majority of women's actual (mixed) rides as the unimproved, "risk-accepted" option, which PRO's business-class/shelter analogies did not successfully rebut once CON distinguished them (neither sells a safety guarantee the way this tier does); and (3) the option is most reliable exactly when risk is lowest (peak hours) and collapses exactly when PRO's own opening case located the harm (late-night travel restriction), a genuine internal contradiction CON exploited sharply in Rebuttal 2. PRO answered competently — conceding imperfection while arguing "friction at the margin" is enough, and that safety measures aren't zero-sum — but never fully closed the timing paradox or the mislabeling harm, and its closing, which promised the "decisive comparison," was cut off before delivering it. B also suffered three separate word-limit clippings (opening, rebuttal 1, closing) versus A's one, a real execution cost that blunted PRO's ability to land its strongest synthesis. On balance, CON's rebuttals did more damage to PRO's live routes than PRO's rebuttals did to CON's, producing a clear but not overwhelming win for CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turn (A4.3), pointing out that PRO's own opening rested on late-night mobility loss as the core harm, yet PRO's defense of matching relies on peak-hour density — meaning the tier works precisely where it's least needed and fails precisely where PRO's case lives. This internal contradiction was never fully resolved by PRO afterward.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won because it turned PRO's own case inside out: unverifiable gender label creates targeting risk rather than neutral imperfection, trust cost is marketing not budget, and peak-hour reliability collapses exactly on the late-night mobility loss PRO relied on [A1.5]. PRO conceded verification and off-peak limits but never answered the active-harm claim, while B kept universal safeguards as covering all trips. Clipped openings/closings also cost A execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 peak vs off-peak inversion combined with verification failure-mode as targeting — showed women-only tier is least available when PRO said women need it most and most dangerous when it fails.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core claims and controlling the central clash over trust. B brilliantly turns A's mobility argument against them by showing the option fails exactly during off-peak hours when late-night safety is most needed. B also neutralizes A's analogies (business class, shelters) and establishes that a soft-verified women-only tier misallocates trust rather than improving it, selling a safety guarantee it cannot honor. Side A's concessions on verification and off-peak availability fatally undermine their burden to prove the option improves trust "enough" to justify the complexity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantle Side A's business class and shelter analogies, and turn Side A's peak-hours defense against their own opening case about late-night mobility restrictions.
