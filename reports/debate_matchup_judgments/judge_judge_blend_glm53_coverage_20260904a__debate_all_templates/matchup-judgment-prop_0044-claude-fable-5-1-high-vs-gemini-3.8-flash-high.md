### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing that an enforceable minimum is better than concealed fragility and by placing risk on the seller best positioned to manage it. B also persuasively answered that firms unable to promise longevity should disclose that fact rather than receive a subsidy from consumer ignorance. Side A's two clipped rebuttals were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was its answer that a guarantee creates a floor, not a cap: firms may support products longer, while buyers can finally distinguish long commitments from short ones. This directly weakened Side A's central claim that legal liability necessarily produces uniform, shorter support.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on the word "guaranteed": liability over unpriceable third-party roadmaps produces a minimized legal floor that shortens support and manufactures the obsolescence A claims to fight. A's status-quo-is-zero and point-of-sale transparency points remained live but were outweighed once B showed the label itself is a worse, trusted form of asymmetry. Two clipped B turns were execution costs but did not erase the cleaner comparative path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.3–B2.4/B2.7–B2.8 dismantling the automaker-warranty analogy and showing the honest sensor maker loses the shelf to the platform that owns the API.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively dismantling the specific mechanisms of the proposition. Side A receives a slight execution penalty for two clipped turns, but the core arguments were fully delivered and substantively superior.
