### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON built and sustained a single decisive dilemma: binding authority in every accepted democratic form rests on expertise or electoral authorization, and sortition assemblies possess neither. CON used this to dismantle PRO's two load-bearing analogies — the jury analogy (juries apply existing law to facts; assemblies would author binding new policy) and the agency/central-bank delegation analogy (those bodies earn binding power via accumulated expertise and continuous accountability that a weekend panel lacks) — and PRO never rebuilt either analogy after CON's rebuttal. The pressure-question exchange was pivotal: CON's Q1 asked what stops a cohesive majority from repealing "on day 31," and PRO's answer ("Nothing stops a cohesive majority from repealing... democracy shouldn't") effectively conceded that the "binding" mechanism reduces to a procedural friction/public-vote requirement rather than genuine binding authority — confirming rather than refuting CON's framing that this is "advisory power with extra steps." PRO's counter that visibility differs from accountability (a story vs. a recorded vote) is a real and underexploited point, and PRO's "path of least resistance" reframing was clever, but it arrived too late and too narrowly to overcome the concession already made and the unresolved authorization/expertise dilemma. CON's closing crystallized this cleanly; PRO's closing restated its mechanism without closing the sovereignty gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that agency/central-bank delegation is legitimated by expertise and continuous accountability — which sortition assemblies structurally lack — turned PRO's central analogy into a liability, compounded by PRO's own pressure-question answer conceding that repeal is always available, which let CON reframe binding sortition as merely advisory power with extra steps.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully advanced and defended a concrete causal mechanism—default-shifting through conditional binding—while CON’s legitimacy objections, though philosophically sharp, could not fully overcome it. PRO showed that advisory status allows silent dilution after one bad headline, whereas binding status forces any reversal to be a public, recorded act. This distinction survived CON’s "advisory with extra steps" critique. CON’s core challenge that binding authority requires either expertise or electoral authorization was met by PRO’s delegation-and-pre-commitment framework: the legislature uses its standing mandate to bind its future self on a specific question, a recognized democratic tool. PRO’s rebuttals did more material damage to CON’s live routes than vice versa, especially on the advisory-sufficiency point and the repeal-default distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 distinction between advisory burial and binding repeal: that under advisory rules a minister can kill road pricing quietly after a headline, while binding rules require an affirmative public vote to reverse, shifting the burden from diffuse supporters to concentrated opponents. This exchange neutralized CON’s claim that advisory status already generates adequate democratic pressure.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO won the central dilemma. CON argued binding must be either sovereignty-loss or advisory-with-extra-steps because repeal remains. PRO answered that conditional binding still matters: it flips the default so inaction now favors the deliberated answer and forces repeal to be a public, recorded vote, like constitutions or central-bank statutes which are also repealable. CON's reply that burying an advisory report is already public failed to meet PRO's visibility-vs-accountability distinction, and its two-foundations test collapsed once PRO showed electoral chain runs through enabling statute and rights review. With minority protection and stall diagnosis conceded, PRO's targeted pre-commitment story was the cleaner voting reason.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1-A4.3 move that conditional != advisory: binding shifts inertia and forces affirmative public repeal, turning CON's B1.4/B2.3 dilemma from fatal to feature, which CON never recovered from in B4.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON (A) built a more conceptually consistent case around the distinction between descriptive resemblance and democratic authorization, and the jury-vs-lawmaking distinction (fact-finding under law with appeal vs. open-ended general rulemaking) was never fully dismantled by PRO. A's "temporary guests" framing and the dilemma it posed in Rebuttal 2/Closing ("binding with repeal" collapses into advisory pressure; true finality means unaccountable permanent power) landed cleanly, and A's closing correctly clarified that this is a dichotomy for PRO to escape, not a self-contradiction — defusing B's attempted "gotcha" in the final speech. PRO (B) had strong material — the representativeness critique of captured legislatures, the point that ballot-box accountability fails on multi-decade issues like pensions/climate, and a sharp pressure question exposing that A's "duty to answer" alternative has no proven electoral-punishment track record (A conceded this directly). Overall this was close and competitive, but A's tighter framework and cleaner final exchange gives it the edge.
