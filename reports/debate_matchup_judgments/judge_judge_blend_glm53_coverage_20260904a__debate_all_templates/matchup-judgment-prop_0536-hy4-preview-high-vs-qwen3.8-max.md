### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, competing frames—CON's chilling-effects/access argument versus PRO's invisible-exclusion/burden-of-proof argument—and both followed the Q&A format correctly. But PRO did more of the decisive work: it preempted and rebutted the "boundless proxy" strawman early, forced CON onto a narrower "targeted enforcement" fallback, then exposed that fallback's circularity (victims can't show a "clear disparity" without the very testing CON resists). PRO's answer to the chilling-effects worry—that firms driven to overt avoidance face easier-to-prove disparate treatment claims—was a clean, underdeveloped-by-CON turn that CON only answered by re-asserting (without new mechanism) that avoidance would be quiet and undetectable. PRO's proposed standard (materially predictive + no equally accurate less-discriminatory alternative) also tracked a recognizable legal logic, giving it more grounding than CON's alternative of "discovery after concrete signs," which still assumes plaintiffs can access aggregate patterns they realistically cannot see. PRO's closing crystallized the clash into a single clean burden-of-proof frame that is harder to answer than CON's repeated access-harm framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 point that outright avoidance of older applicants is disparate treatment (easier to prove than hidden algorithmic impact) directly undercut CON's central chilling-effects mechanism, and CON's reply—that avoidance would instead be quiet and untraceable—only reasserted the original claim rather than refuting the new point.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by exposing that CON’s targeted enforcement is structurally circular: victims cannot produce the concrete patterns CON demands because the models are opaque and trade-secret protected, making CON’s remedy a locked door that rarely opens. PRO’s transparency, age-disaggregated testing, and justification requirements narrowly target hidden proxies without banning genuine risk prediction, and PRO successfully answers CON’s breadth and chilling-effects objections by showing avoidance is disparate treatment and that legitimate predictors survive. CON’s access argument remains live but speculative against the concrete, present exclusion PRO establishes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s rebuttal showing that CON’s targeted enforcement is circular because victims cannot see black-box models to prove the patterns CON demands, and PRO’s clarification that mere correlation does not trigger liability—only unjustified proxy operation—directly defusing CON’s litigation-engine attack.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO built the live path: algorithmic exclusion is invisible because defendants hold code and trade-secret privilege, so CON's "show concrete signs first" is circular and leaves insurance/financial patchwork unreviewable. CON's boundless-proxy and avoidance warnings were neutralized by B's narrow framing – transparency, age-disaggregated testing, justification, less discriminatory alternative only where equally accurate – and avoidance as disparate treatment. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and handled the pressure-question format cleanly with proper A1/A2 labeling. PRO's strongest asset was the proof-of-harm framing: existing law assumes a visible human decision-maker, and an excluded older applicant cannot easily discover a hidden proxy inside a proprietary model, so protection should shift the burden upstream. CON never fully dismantled this — its answer that "disparity plus discovery" already opens the black box gestures at a mechanism without explaining how disparities in an opaque, non-aggregated system would even surface in the first place. However, CON's central weapon — the "redundant-or-tax" dichotomy — proved more durable under fire. When PRO answered the pressure question by conceding that firms need not adopt a materially worse alternative but must still prove necessity, calibration, and consider reweighting or human review, CON immediately turned this concession into confirmation of its own claim: if the factor is legitimate, the audit is a redundant certificate; if it survives only after costly justification, it is a tax on accuracy, not bias. PRO's later reframe ("burden moves upstream, preventing harm before it hardens") relocates the timing of the cost but does not defeat the substance of the tax critique, since the same necessity-proof and reweighting steps still apply. CON also successfully leveraged PRO's own admissions (more age-data collection, human-review appeals) against PRO's earlier warnings about human "culture fit" discretion, exposing an internal tension PRO did not fully resolve. The closings crystallized this clash well, but CON's framing was tighter and harder to escape.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.2 turning PRO's own pressure-question answer (B4.1, conceding firms must still prove necessity/calibration even for accurate factors) into a confirmation of the "redundant-or-tax" dichotomy was the most decisive single exchange, since PRO never cleanly dissolved that bind afterward.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won by exposing a persistent dilemma in PRO's advocacy: stronger protection framed as a "certificate" for accurate systems actually operates as a costly toll booth forcing firms to prove necessity, consider reweighting, and maintain human review. CON showed this is either redundant with existing disparate-impact law plus adverse-action explanations, or destructive to accurate risk pricing and market access. PRO's burden-of-proof argument was strong but never fully escaped CON's "redundant or destructive" weighing, and PRO's turn to human review undermined its own critique of subjective stereotyping.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's framing that PRO's audit is a "toll booth, not a certificate" and the ensuing "redundant or destructive" dilemma in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, which showed that accurate age-correlated models either survive unnecessarily or fail harmfully.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO won the burden-of-proof clash. CON argued existing disparity+discovery plus adverse-action explanations solves opacity and that audits tax accuracy and increase human bias. PRO answered both pressure questions directly: less discriminatory alternatives must be comparably effective, not merely less age-correlated, and data collection + recorded appeal is a constrained check, not open-ended culture-fit discretion. CON never resolved how an individual older applicant obtains cross-applicant statistics to trigger discovery before harm, leaving PRO's scale/opacity/proxy argument live. The redundancy-vs-tax dilemma was blunted by PRO's certificate model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - clarified comparably-effective standard for alternatives and reframed human review as recorded explainable appeal, directly neutralizing CON's core tax-on-accuracy and more-human-bias attacks.
