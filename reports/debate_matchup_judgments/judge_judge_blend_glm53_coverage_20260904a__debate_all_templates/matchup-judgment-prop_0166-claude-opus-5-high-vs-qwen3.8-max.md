### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's attempts to escape the opportunity cost. When A argued that mammoth enthusiasm would fund other conservation efforts via "binding co-benefits," B brilliantly reframed this as a "resurrection surcharge," pointing out that governments can just buy the co-benefits without funding the mammoth. B also successfully turned A's ecological timeline against them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (B4.2) dismantling A's "binding co-benefits" mechanism by calling it a "shopping list" with a "resurrection surcharge."
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by holding A to the motion's diversion premise and repeatedly forcing a cleaner comparison: any co-benefit A finally named (habitat, labs, monitoring, predator control) can be bought without a resurrection surcharge, while charisma money and ancient-DNA bottlenecks do not reliably transfer. A's gateway/capacity case stayed live but never traced a durable flow-down mechanism, and B flipped the Tasmania function claim by showing living devils already occupy the guild A wants rebuilt with hand-reared proxies. Prefer-survivors framing and the sticky-charisma/transferability attacks made the vote easier than A's capacity-and-repair story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 on A's co-benefit "mechanism" (land, labs, teams are ordinary conservation plus a surcharge) plus the living-devil function indictment.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON wins by showing that every operational benefit PRO names—land security, predator control, biosecurity labs—is fundable without de-extinction, while the ecological role PRO seeks in Tasmania is already occupied by the living Tasmanian devil. CON turns PRO's own functional-restoration goal against the project, demonstrating that de-extinction offers only a distant, caged proxy, whereas public money can instead prevent actual extinctions today. PRO never supplies a concrete, traceable mechanism for converting mammoth enthusiasm into gillnet enforcement or rat eradication, and repeatedly slips into budget-growth speculation that the motion's stipulated diversion forbids.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's demonstration that the Tasmanian devil already provides the marsupial predator and scavenger function PRO claims is missing, making de-extinction an unnecessary, decades-away proxy compared to directly treating the devil's facial tumour disease and preserving a breathing species.
