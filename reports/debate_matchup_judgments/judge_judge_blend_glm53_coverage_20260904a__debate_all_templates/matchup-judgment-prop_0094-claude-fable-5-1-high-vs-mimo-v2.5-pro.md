### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by effectively exposing a structural paradox in the proposition: capping fleets prevents the collection of the very "scale data" needed to write the rules that would lift the cap. Side A also capitalized on Side B's inability to define the "until" trigger and brilliantly caught a phrasing slip-up in Side B's closing to cement the argument that caps create permanent, lobbied scarcity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument that "the geofence is the cherry orchard"—pointing out that Side B's complaint about cherry-picked safety data is actually caused by the geofence Side B is defending.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON wins the central comparison. PRO never defines "until" – B presses for trigger, A answers "write benchmarks later," confirming indefinite hold. B's information paradox – caps forbid scale data needed to write scale rules, plus geofence creates cherry-picked data – directly damages PRO's learning claim. B also neutralizes PRO's pressure: curb rules can be generic, and medallion history shows temporary caps become permanent assets, a point PRO concedes in closing. PRO's safety cherry-pick charge is turned against itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2-B4 trigger/information exchange: PRO's "limited conditions" complaint is caused by geofence, capped fleet cannot teach curb conflict at 2,000 vehicles, and blank release condition makes cap a lock, not a runway. A4's reply admits trigger is still to-be-written.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger case by exposing the operational Catch-22 in the proposition: capping fleets prevents the collection of the very scale-data needed to write the rules that would lift the cap. Side A also brilliantly weaponized the "until" condition, demonstrating that without a defined trigger, a numerical cap inevitably becomes a permanent medallion system protected by incumbent lobbying. Side B's defenses, particularly that "until" is merely a "legislative directive" and that geofencing doesn't restrict weather, failed to address the core political economy and operational realities Side A highlighted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal in Turn 8, where it dismantled Side B's "geofence doesn't restrict weather" defense by pointing out that a geofence is literally a map boundary that excludes the complex intersections needed to test the vehicles, perfectly crystallizing the Catch-22 of the proposition.
