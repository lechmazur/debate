### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): A won a clear but competitive debate. B compellingly established misclassification and answered the dual-platform classification problem, but A created the more decisive comparison: reclassification may eliminate low-hours work or reroute it through evasive intermediaries rather than deliver protection. B largely treated displacement as proof of an exploitative model instead of showing how affected workers retain income or reach protected employment. A then crystallized that unresolved gap effectively, while B also incurred execution costs from two clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s treatment of the five-hour worker was decisive. B argued that removal merely exposes costs the platform had externalized; A correctly returned the debate to worker outcomes, stressing that lost income is a new and concrete harm unless B can establish a transition to protected work. B never supplied that bridge.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive practical clash clearer: default reclassification predictably produces shrinkage, exits, and intermediary reroutes rather than upgraded protection, so the proposition trades precarious-but-available work for compliant-but-deleted work. A's two clipped turns were a mild execution tax; B's answers, weighing, and closings more cleanly resolved the live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2/closing on Spain intermediaries plus the five-hour worker: not transitional protection but preemptive roster removal (zero income vs precarious income).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by decisively attacking the practical mechanisms of Side B's proposal. Side A effectively used real-world examples (Spain's rider law, AB5) to show that platforms evade reclassification through subcontracting and cooperatives. Side B's concession that "regulation needs to follow the work" was expertly framed by Side A as an admission that enforcement will lag for years, leaving workers unprotected in practice. Side A also won the clash over marginal workers (the 5-hour-a-week worker), demonstrating that reclassification leads to immediate job loss rather than protection, a practical burden Side B failed to resolve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's "bright-line" test for dual-platform workers by pointing out the impossibility of calculating scheduling and sick-pay obligations across two non-communicating algorithms, and turned Side B's "regulation must follow the work" concession into proof that enforcement loses the evasion chase.
