### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): The decisive exchange centered on whether formal reasoning can catch manipulation that rides on real, authentic sources with misleading statistics. PRO conceded outright that "no tool in the logic-probability curriculum directly certifies a premise as false" and pivoted to "plausibility detection" via base-rate sensitivity. CON's most damaging rebuttal exposed that this plausibility check itself requires external data (denominators, comparison classes, historical trends) that can only come from evaluating sources — i.e., exactly the media-literacy skill PRO had spent the debate dismissing as shallow. PRO's closing never answered this sourcing problem, merely reasserting that the demand for a denominator is "ideologically inert" without explaining where the denominator comes from. CON also landed a sharp secondary point (the "referent" argument) that provenance checks have verifiable right/wrong answers resistant to motivated reasoning, while "that number seems off" is elastic and partisan-friendly — a point PRO's closing did not squarely rebut. PRO scored real points earlier (splitting CON's examples into sampling/logic failures, the "deference" inversion on authoritarianism, symmetry of motivated-reasoning capture), keeping the debate competitive, but CON's final one-two punch (denominator sourcing + referent asymmetry) was the more decisive, unanswered blow. CON's case is docked somewhat for having its Rebuttal 2 and Closing clipped for length, an execution cost, but the core payload of both speeches landed before the cut, so the substantive damage to PRO's case stands.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 point that PRO's "plausibility detection" via base rates silently smuggles in source evaluation (since denominators/comparison classes must come from somewhere), paired with PRO's failure in Closing to explain where that data comes from without invoking the very media-literacy skill it dismissed.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that valid reasoning cannot verify fabricated premises and that A's proposed plausibility checks ultimately require external information and source evaluation. A strongly established the value of base rates and denominators, but repeatedly narrowed media literacy to provenance despite B's broader account of framing and contextual verification. B's transfer and motivated-reasoning objections also left A relying on a speculative claim that early training becomes an ideology-resistant reflex. B's two clipped turns reduce the margin but do not erase the substantive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal most decisively answered A's authentic-source example: determining whether a statistic is plausible requires denominators, trends, and comparison data obtained through source evaluation, while intuitive implausibility can itself be recruited by partisans. This undermined A's claim that formal reasoning independently supplies the superior safeguard.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): B won by converging the debate on a decisive comparative test: manipulation via legitimate sources using misleading statistics. B showed that in this scenario—which B persuasively framed as central to democratic resilience—media literacy’s provenance checks go silent while formal reasoning still generates a signal. A’s strongest responses (that logic must borrow source evaluation to supply base rates, and that "implausible" is subjective) were undermined by clipped late speeches that left the crystallization unfinished and key final comparisons unseen. B also landed the cleaner rebuttal on motivated reasoning: source-trust habits readily serve tribal loyalty, whereas base-rate demands are comparatively ideology-inert.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (B4.3-B4.4) showing that media literacy is equally blind to false premises when they come from legitimate sources, while formal reasoning still offers internal signals—sharply inverting CON's vault-door analogy and establishing the debate's decisive frame.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Both sides built sophisticated, well-grounded cases with vivid examples (base-rate/screening tests vs. bot-farm ecosystems, fire drills, self-correcting logic). The pivotal moment was B's rebuttal-1 strike that A's "friction survives arousal" mechanism is undercut by A's own admission that even PhD statisticians succumb to motivated reasoning on charged topics. A's answer in Rebuttal 2 walked this back to a softer "raises the cost" claim, which is defensible but reads as a retreat that B then hammered effectively in the closing ("PRO conceded the exact opposite of their central claim"). A's strongest counter—that provenance-based media literacy collapses when the "who" is itself forged—remained live and was never fully neutralized, but B's triangulation/ecosystem-awareness response and its reframe of "who benefits" as the answerable democratic question kept that thread contested rather than lost. B's closing also more directly exploited the tension between A's self-correcting-curriculum claim and A's own concession about motivated reasoning, even if that point slightly conflated two distinct mechanisms. On balance, B did marginally more damage in the decisive exchange and closed more crisply on the concession, while A's case, though strong and original, carried a small execution cost from a clipped rebuttal and left a key B critique (self-correction vs. motivated reasoning) unanswered in its closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal-1 point ([B2.1]) that PhD-level statistical training doesn't survive motivated reasoning, directly undercutting A's "friction that survives arousal" mechanism and forcing a retreat in A's next speech.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): B won by showing that democratic manipulation is often contextual, emotional, and ecosystem-level rather than a defect in abstract inference.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response that citizens cannot independently verify most public claims decisively undercut A's source-independent test. B paired this with the point that valid reasoning cannot reveal bot coordination or manufactured consensus, leaving media literacy with the more direct connection to the threats under discussion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully narrowing the debate to a comparative question CON could not answer: when source cues and provenance are forged—which is exactly what modern coordinated disinformation does—media literacy collapses, whereas formal reasoning still asks "is this claim true regardless of its source?" PRO sharply rebutted CON's ecosystem argument by noting that bot farms still have to persuade through distorted numbers and arguments, and then demolished CON's triangulation defense by observing that synthetic networks can fake cross-verification. CON's motivated-reasoning challenge was deftly reframed by PRO into the "expensive to fool" standard, and PRO's structural distinction between rigging examples versus rigging a self-correcting skill resolved the curriculum-bias clash in PRO's favor. CON's closing largely repeated earlier points without defusing these live routes to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's provenance-based defense: after CON offered lateral reading and triangulation as media literacy's answer to forged sources [A4.4], PRO demonstrated that synthetic bot networks and fake outlets citing each other destroy the independence triangulation requires [B5.2], leaving media literacy with nothing to check while formal reasoning's content-neutral question still stands.
