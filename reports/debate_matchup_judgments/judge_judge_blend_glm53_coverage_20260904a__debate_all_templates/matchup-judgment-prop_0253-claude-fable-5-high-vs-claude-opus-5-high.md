### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won narrowly by showing that verified, standardized portability changes the market equilibrium: once strong workers disclose, refusal itself becomes an adverse signal, so formal worker choice does not preserve a genuine fresh start. Side B defended the mobility and entrant benefits well, but its time limits, exclusions, and non-retaliation rule did not solve opaque inference and looked partly like safeguards added beyond the proposition. Side A also drew the key distinction between an existing siloed record and a newly networked, algorithm-readable one. Side B retained substantial competition benefits, so the result remains close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s response to the optionality defense was decisive: it argued that standardized verification makes non-disclosure informative, while silent algorithmic downranking is nearly impossible to police. Side B’s credential analogy and proposed safeguards mitigated record accuracy concerns but never fully neutralized that unraveling mechanism.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's claim that gig workers already enjoy high mobility. A's observation that multi-homing without reputation means starting at the bottom tier on three different apps effectively proved the harm of lock-in. While B had a strong argument about portability unraveling into a mandatory 'credit score' for labor, A successfully countered that verifiable context exonerates workers and that B's logic would require banning all professional resumes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Turn 7 (and Closing) reframing of Side B's multi-homing argument, pointing out that running three apps at the bottom tier is not leverage, but rather the same captivity three times over.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully reframing the "permanent dossier" as an existing reality that the motion merely democratizes, and by decisively defeating Side B's "multi-homing" argument. Side B conflated low onboarding friction with low reputation switching costs, and Side A's point that starting at zero on three apps is just triple captivity was a fatal blow to CON's competition story. While Side B's "unraveling" argument was strong, Side A's defense of standard-setting (trailing windows, context) was a plausible and sufficient policy response.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the multi-homing argument, pointing out that working on three apps without portable reputation means rebuilding from zero three times, effectively neutralizing Side B's claim that switching costs are already low.
