### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but the debate turned on whether a disease/enhancement (or monogenic/polygenic) line could be policed. B's pressure question forced the sharpest test of CON's regulatory framework, and A answered it with a genuinely workable principle—restrict edits to validated, high-penetrance monogenic pathogenic variants with no PGD alternative, explicitly excluding polygenic risk-score edits for schizophrenia/diabetes. That answer directly rebuts the slippery-slope worry with a concrete, plausible regulatory analogue (similar to how gene therapy is already limited to monogenic disorders). B's closing tried to reopen this by claiming the framework "licenses" polygenic edits, but that contradicts what CON actually said, weakening the final blow rather than delivering it. A also pressed effectively on offshore evasion (a ban that pushes rich patients abroad sacrifices domestic oversight without stopping the practice) and offered a concrete, if imperfect, liability/compensation mechanism, whereas B's "enforce, cooperate" answer to the offshore point stayed abstract. B's opening and PGD-alternative argument were strong and narrowed CON's case to rare edge cases, but A converted that narrowness into a humanitarian cost argument that was never fully neutralized. On balance, A did more precise, direct work resolving the decisive clash in the later rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's pressure question (A4.1), specifying that the licensing line is "high-penetrance monogenic, no PGD alternative" and explicitly excludes polygenic risk-score edits, directly answered the enforceability challenge; B's closing attempt to reopen it by claiming this "licenses" schizophrenia-risk editing mischaracterized that answer rather than refuting it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A wins by effectively proving that the regulatory line Side B relies upon (disease vs. enhancement) is inherently porous compared to the biological line of heritability. Side A successfully frames the denial of care to a tiny minority of edge-case patients as standard medical safety practice rather than an unprecedented cruelty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing sequence dismantling Side B's proposed regulatory framework, correctly pointing out that polygenic risks for serious conditions like schizophrenia will inevitably blur the line between therapy and enhancement.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won by showing that the structural consent problem across generations and the inevitability of market creep make CON’s regulated-exception framework unstable. CON could not defend a principled, enforceable line between therapy and enhancement once polygenic risk reduction for serious diseases enters the clinic, whereas a categorical ban at heritability offers a verifiable firewall without requiring impossible regulatory line-drawing. CON’s patient-access appeals were blunted by PRO’s demonstration that PGD, donor gametes, and somatic alternatives already address nearly all tragic cases.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s sustained attack on CON’s disease-enhancement boundary, demonstrating that polygenic conditions such as schizophrenia and heart disease would inevitably be marketed as preventive therapy and thereby collapse CON’s licensed exception, leaving only the bright-line heritability ban as a workable standard.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON (A) built the more resilient case by directly answering the two hardest pressure points: it gave a concrete, auditable license standard (correct only to the population-normal sequence, applied precisely where selection fails) and a specific institutional answer for who protects descendants (ethics boards, registries, follow-up), turning PRO's "no remedy under a ban" into a visible weakness. CON's non-identity move—"nobody is harmed into existence by being cured"—was never squarely rebutted; PRO's closing reasserted that "tracking is not consent" but did not explain what harm the corrected child or descendant actually suffers, leaving that question live and unanswered. CON also exploited a real tension in PRO's case: demanding multigenerational safety data while backing the one policy that forecloses collecting it, and PRO's rebuttal (organoids/animal models/somatic trials) only partially closes that gap for heritable-specific risk. PRO's consent/irreversibility framing and slippery-slope warning remained principled and were argued competently throughout, but after Rebuttal 1's "collapses on itself" challenge was answered by CON with a workable scope definition, PRO did not reopen or sharpen that attack, and its later speeches mostly restated the consent thesis rather than advancing new leverage against CON's concrete design.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to Q2 (A4.3), defining the "approved embryo" as the population-standard correction exactly where selection would fail, combined with the closing's "who is harmed, and how?" challenge — PRO never supplied a concrete victim or harm to counter the non-identity argument, leaving CON's strongest line unrefuted.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively reframing Side A's core 'consent' and 'authored lineage' arguments from a moral absolute into a metaphysical grievance. B's establishment of a strict scope limit (editing only to a population-standard sequence) effectively neutralized A's slippery slope, while forcing A to concede that their policy deliberately denies a genetic cure to families with no alternatives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, where B defined the 'approved embryo' as having a 'population-standard sequence,' neatly defeating A's slippery slope argument while exposing A's 'authored lineage' concept as an abstract concern weighed against a tangible cure.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins because it neutralized A's consent principle by showing all reproduction imposes unconsented genomes, reframed germline correction as restoring normal health rather than authoring lineages, and showed A's evidentiary standard is self-defeating under a ban. B's closing crystallization—who is harmed by replacing a fatal mutation with the normal sequence?—remained unanswered, while A never proved that "authored lineage" is a concrete harm outweighing the benefit of preventing fatal disease.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal (B4.1-B4.5), where B directly answered A's pressure questions with concrete governance mechanisms and then landed the decisive argument that "authored lineage" is rhetoric, not harm, because the child's interest is in not dying of cardiomyopathy rather than in the metaphysical provenance of their genome.
