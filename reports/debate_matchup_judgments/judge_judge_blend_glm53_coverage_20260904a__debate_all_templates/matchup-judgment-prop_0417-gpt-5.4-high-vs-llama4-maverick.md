### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Llama 4 Maverick (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B established a dominant conceptual frame from its opening—"exit strategy vs. prevention strategy," or upstream vs. downstream—and never lost it. That frame did real analytical work: earlier closing reaches every patron including those who drove in or lack network access, while transit only rescues the subset who are near a stop, willing to board, and did not arrive by car. Side A never found a clean answer to this coverage asymmetry. Its best response—"many people in cities with comprehensive networks can benefit"—was a quiet retreat from the broad motion, and B exploited it directly in both its questions and its Rebuttal 2. Side A's recurring move—"people will still drink heavily regardless of closing times"—was plainly addressed by B's "not perfect is not no effect" point, which Side A never recovered from. On disorder, B's distinction between peak intensity and total exposure window (a flatter spike over a longer night vs. a shorter night with managed exits) was a genuine advance that Side A's gradual-dispersal narrative didn't neutralize. B's closing crystallized all of this efficiently: "Many is not more effective than nearly everyone" and "transit mainly manages the leftovers" are sharp, grounded summaries of the comparative case as already built. The main cost to Side B is that both Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 were clipped for exceeding the word limit, which is a real execution penalty. However, the visible portions of those speeches were analytically stronger than Side A's complete speeches, and the core framework was unclipped in the opening, questions round, and closing. That gap keeps B clearly ahead despite the format costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to Q1 was the most decisive exchange: rather than claiming closing times change people into light drinkers, B reframed the mechanism as a simple margin—"if venues stop serving earlier, many patrons have less time for the last rounds that push them from impaired to very impaired." This directly defused PRO's "people will drink anyway" argument by showing a modest, plausible upstream effect still outperforms a downstream exit for a subset, and PRO never found a counter to it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Llama 4 Maverick (PRO)): Side A won by establishing a decisive, unanswered comparative framework: earlier closing times operate as an upstream prevention mechanism that reaches 100% of patrons, while transit acts as a downstream mitigation tool that only helps a willing, geographically fortunate subset. Side B never successfully overcame Side A's coverage argument, particularly the point about patrons who drove in, and struggled to defend why a longer, flatter disorder window is better than a shorter one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R2 A1 successfully neutralized Side B's best attack by explaining that earlier closing doesn't require patrons to magically become light drinkers; it only needs to cut off the final rounds that push patrons into peak impairment.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Llama 4 Maverick (PRO)): CON won by establishing a cleaner comparative frame: earlier closing reduces the risk pool upstream for virtually all patrons, while late-night transit mainly manages downstream consequences for a subset. PRO never overcame the coverage problem, conceding transit helps "many" but not everyone, and failed to rebut CON's distinction between peak disorder and total disorder exposure over time. CON's rebuttals directly answered pressure questions and sharpened the clash, whereas PRO's responses remained repetitive and conceded key scope limitations.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4) turning PRO's "significant portion" language into a concession that transit is narrower than early closing, and distinguishing total disorder exposure across a longer night from mere peak dispersal.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Llama 4 Maverick (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by establishing a clearer comparative framework: upstream prevention (earlier closing) versus downstream management (transit), and universal coverage versus subset access. B effectively turned A's gradual dispersal point into a liability by framing it as a prolonged disorder window, and directly answered pressure questions about behavior change by clarifying that marginal reductions in peak impairment still reduce the generation of highly intoxicated drivers. Side A relied on vague appeals to studies, repeated the rushed-exodus point without adequately answering the coverage gap or cumulative disorder arguments, and muddled its own framing in the closing by claiming an exit strategy works upstream. B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly and met the comparative burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 directly answers A's pressure questions, distinguishes peak density from cumulative disorder exposure, and clarifies that marginal reductions in drinking time still reduce the pool of highly intoxicated drivers. This neatly dismantles A's core mechanism, resolves the coverage clash, and secures the comparative win.
