### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by identifying genuine scarcity conflicts in which national membership changes who receives life-saving resources, even when universal allocation would favor needier foreigners. Side B repeatedly redescribed these cases as welfare delivery or capacity constraints without establishing that capacity fully explains passport-based priority. A also persuasively separated universal welfare aggregation from independent rights constraints that limit both sides. B was rhetorically sharper, but its zero-cost test removed the conflict rather than resolving which claim prevails.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest response was that costless aid presents no clash, while scarce vaccines and ICU beds do: prioritizing citizens over needier foreigners is precisely national loyalty defeating an impartial welfare ranking. Its further point that inviolability constrains both loyalty and welfare maximization undercut B’s attempt to treat every rights ceiling as proof of welfare’s supremacy.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): CON won the central clash. PRO equated the Nuremberg rights-ceiling with "universal welfare," but CON showed that ban on treating persons as tools limits both loyalty and welfare-maximization, so it does not rank welfare above loyalty. On scarcity, PRO called vaccine/ICU triage "accompaniment" and capacity, but CON forced the hard case: co-national gets the bed while a needier foreigner dies, which is a real conflict where membership overrides impartial tally. PRO's zero-cost test fails because silence without conflict doesn't show weakness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R1/R2 dismantling of family analogy and Nuremberg – side-constraint above both claims plus parent-saving-own-child vs two strangers as partiality beating welfare, reframing scarce vaccines as genuine override.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making a crucial and highly effective philosophical distinction between deontological side-constraints (basic human rights/inviolability) and positive duties to universal human welfare (welfare maximization). This allowed A to neutralize Side B's strongest point—the Nuremberg/atrocity ceiling—by correctly pointing out that rights constrain both national loyalty and utilitarian welfare maximization equally, meaning they do not prove loyalty is weaker than welfare. Side A then dominated the core clash over scarcity, convincingly arguing that prioritizing co-nationals for scarce resources (vaccines, ICU beds) is a genuine case of national loyalty overriding pure universal welfare. Side B struggled to answer this without redefining the examples as mere "capacity" or "accompaniment," which A successfully dismantled.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it sharply exposed Side B's conflation of basic human rights with "universal human welfare" and proved that special obligations of membership rightfully override impartial welfare aggregation in ordinary scarcity conflicts.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by separating practical sequence and institutional capacity from moral priority. Side A compellingly showed that states assign and execute duties, but never established why being the mechanism of aid gives co-national claims greater moral weight. Its insistence that domestic duties are inexhaustible effectively avoided weighing modest domestic gains against severe preventable harm abroad. Side B directly exposed that implication and supplied the clearer standard: equal status combined with comparative severity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s answer that securing core national capacity first reflects practical sequencing, not superior moral force, decisively weakened Side A’s central precondition argument. The follow-up that an open-ended national duty would permanently privilege marginal domestic welfare over outsiders’ lives made Side A’s position look like a refusal to compare harms.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side A kept the proposition's moral metric central—severity of harm and equal worth—while Side B recast strength as assignability and precondition. B landed sharp hits (intimacy test backfire, structure vs content) but never justified permanent preference for marginal domestic gains over acute foreign harm, so PRO's live route survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's turn on [A2.4] intimacy—showing if knowing the claimant matters, the equally unknown foreign child ranks below the co-national, undercutting PRO's own criterion for elevating universal welfare.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger structural case by distinguishing between "who counts" (equal moral worth) and "who is bound to act" (assignable perfect duties). Side A effectively dismantled Side B's "basics secured" threshold by pointing out that national duties are continuous and inexhaustible in reality, rendering Side B's surplus hypothetical moot. Side A also brilliantly turned Side B's intimacy argument against it, and Side B's subsequent concession that intimacy fails for both co-nationals and strangers undermined its own framework. Side A's rhetorical control and sharp rebuttals secured a clear victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled the "basics secured" threshold as a fictional post-scarcity hypothetical and clarified that structural reciprocity, not personal acquaintance, grounds the national claim, effectively neutralizing Side B's main offensive pushes.
