### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by defining a stronger right as a timely, evidence-based process rather than automatic approval. It preserved study delays, capacity and code refusals, and risk protections while showing that Side B never justified silence or indefinite postponement. Side B raised credible residual-risk and gray-capacity concerns, but largely assumed that deadlines make denial prohibitively costly and did not establish why documented refusals or contractual backstops are inadequate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s direct response to the pressure questions was decisive: unknown capacity permits a real, time-limited study, while verified problems permit denial. This undercut Side B’s repeated claim that a stronger request right necessarily forces approval, leaving Side B mainly defending unrestricted last-word authority.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by defining the clash as process versus inertia and showing that owners retain every substantive defense—verified-capacity refusal, time-limited study, and bonded risk backstops—while losing only the power to stall indefinitely. Side B’s warnings of coerced approvals in gray zones and unmanageable residual risk were blunted when A directly conceded that owners may commission real studies and remain the first addressee for post-tenancy claims, allocating risk through insurance and bonds rather than veto. B’s slippery-slope and attrition arguments never overcame A’s narrow, documented-refusal framework, and B’s closing mischaracterized A’s concessions. The stronger right emerged as a workable accountability mechanism rather than a confiscation of property.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal answers to Side B’s pressure questions (A4.1–A4.4), where A cleanly distinguished legitimate time-limited study from illegitimate inertia, honestly conceded the owner’s post-tenancy risk exposure, and reframed the residual-risk objection as a reason for backstops rather than permanent deferral—crystallizing that B was defending indefinite limbo, not genuine management.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully framing the proposition as a procedural right to a timely, justified answer rather than a substantive mandate to install. This neatly neutralized Side A's safety and capacity concerns, which B addressed through narrow refusal conditions. Side A's strongest counterargument—that the procedural clock creates substantive coercion in the "gray zone" of unknown capacity—was effectively parried by B's clarification that messy engineering results simply constitute a valid written refusal. Side B also handled the pressure questions more directly, honestly conceding the landlord's initial liability while explaining how financial backstops resolve it without granting a permanent veto.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they directly answered Side A's pressure questions by conceding that time-limited studies are legitimate delays and that the owner is the initial addressee of risk, but demonstrating how bonded and insured installations properly allocate that risk.
