### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A won by showing that distribution, replaceability, and magazine depth create a more credible denial strategy than relying primarily on scarce platforms concentrated at vulnerable bases. Side B’s jamming and hardened-backbone objection was substantial, but it repeatedly treated a significant funding shift as wholesale abandonment. A directly clarified that the backbone remains while attritable systems add autonomous, dispersed layers. B never adequately resolved A’s runway, base, and replenishment pressures. A’s clipped opening and some unsupported precision were execution costs, but not decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was its answer to the rifle analogy: the current force is a sophisticated rifle with too few magazines and years-long replacement times. This turned B’s metaphor by connecting survivability, production, and day-thirty deterrence, while B’s response largely asserted that exquisite platforms would end the war early without resolving ammunition depth or vulnerable basing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by proving that exquisite platforms are concentrated and vulnerable to a first strike against fixed bases, making their hardening irrelevant if they cannot launch, while distributed attritable systems can provide persistent denial with autonomous guidance. Side A (CON) never fully solved the cratered-runway problem and relied too heavily on asserting jamming would neutralize all cheap systems without adequately engaging PRO's specific claims about optical and inertial autonomy. PRO's flip of the rifle analogy and sharper pressure answers provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that a stealth fighter with perfect inertial navigation is still scrap inside a destroyed hangar, directly undermining CON's claim that exquisite platforms survive the opening salvo.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A won by effectively grounding the debate in the strategic reality of Pacific theater vulnerabilities (cratered runways, concentrated carrier groups) and production bottlenecks. While Side B mounted a strong technical defense regarding electronic warfare and inertial navigation drift rates, Side A successfully neutralized it by clarifying that the shift is a proportional share rather than a total liquidation, and by highlighting autonomous optical seekers. Crucially, Side A decisively won the rhetorical battle over the "rifle and bullets" analogy, flipping it to highlight the "beautiful rifle with two magazines and a three-year factory wait" problem. This crystallized the magazine depth and deterrence arguments, giving Side A the cleaner comparative path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's flip of the "rifle and bullets" analogy in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, turning Side B's strongest rhetorical frame into a devastating critique of exquisite platform production timelines and magazine depth.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that cheap mass remains dependent on survivable sensing, penetration, range, command, and logistics, so reducing the exquisite layer can make the added systems less useful rather than more resilient. Side A compellingly emphasized magazine depth and wartime endurance, but repeatedly relied on unsupported precision about losses, timelines, production ratios, and force sizes. It also never adequately established that its cheaper systems could reach and destroy decisive targets under peer defenses. B directly answered the day-nine challenge and offered the cleaner comparison: leverage and effects before loss matter more than raw platform count.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3-B4.6 was decisive: B answered what survives to fight on day nine, then used A's concession that the swarm needs exquisite fusion and targeting to expose the central dependency. B also sharply answered A's proposed survivor-based force sizing by noting that planners cannot know in advance which platforms survive.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won because it exposed a fatal, unresolved tension in Side B's case: distributed attritable swarms depend on exquisite enablers for targeting, C2, sensing, and logistics, yet the motion explicitly endorses shifting investment even if it reduces those exact enablers. Side B admitted this dependency in [B4.1] but never reconciled it with the required tradeoff, effectively arguing for a smaller, more brittle host to support a larger parasite. Side A's leverage-before-loss framework, its demonstration that cheap systems cannot replicate deep penetration or theater-level battlespace awareness, and its basing and logistics rebuttals proved that reducing exquisite investment collapses both deterrence and wartime effectiveness. Side B was rhetorically formidable and landed memorable framing, but its reliance on brittle unsupported precision and its inability to show how attritable systems function without the very networks the motion allows cutting left its path to victory incomplete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal showing that "three hundred postage stamps don't sum to a theater picture" and that attritable systems parasitically depend on the exquisite fusion layer that the motion explicitly permits reducing. This turned Side B's mass-based awareness claim into a single point of failure and became the decisive structural flaw in Side B's case.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively proving that attritable systems are technically dependent on the exquisite platforms the motion asks to cut. Side B's "parasite/host" framing brilliantly exploited the motion's condition ("even if it reduces investment"), showing that shrinking the exquisite fusion, C2, and stealth layer renders the cheap swarm blind and vulnerable to peer electronic warfare. Side A's "shooter vs. shot" and "day nine" framings were intuitive and forced Side B to work hard, but Side B's technical rebuttals regarding sensor fusion, range, and IADS penetration ultimately neutralized them. Side B also won the pressure exchange by forcing Side A into a concession that undermined its own adherence to the motion's text.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 dismantling Side A's concession that the swarm still needs exquisite enablers to fuse the picture. By pointing out that the motion explicitly requires reducing investment in those very enablers, Side B trapped Side A in a fatal contradiction: you cannot shrink the fusion layer and expect the dependent swarm to survive.
