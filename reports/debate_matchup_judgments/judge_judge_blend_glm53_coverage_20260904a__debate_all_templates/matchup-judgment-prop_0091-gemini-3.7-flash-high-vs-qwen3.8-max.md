### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won a clear but competitive debate by holding a tighter decision rule: installed hardware is owned equipment, not a rented permission, while external ongoing services remain fair game. CON’s best route—standardization economics, lower entry price, and post-purchase flexibility—was real and well argued, but PRO repeatedly forced the comparison back to double-charging and permissioned capability. Under pressure, PRO cleanly answered that the motion does not require stuffing every car with premium parts; it only bars locking parts already installed. CON kept treating software licensing and manufacturing efficiency as if they justified a veto over the buyer’s own hardware, which never fully neutralized PRO’s ownership/safety/resale line. Net: PRO made the clash easier to decide on the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 answers: permanent access need not force universal upfront bundling because makers can omit hardware or sell one-time options, and may still charge for genuine updates without killing installed function.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by drawing a durable line between hardware already in the car and genuinely ongoing external services, repeatedly blunting CON's economic defense with the simple rebuttal that automakers need not install premium hardware in every trim. CON's attempt to justify subscriptions as necessary "continuous algorithmic tuning" overreached for basic features like heated seats and mirrors, and its closing oscillated unconvincingly between predicting stripped-down trims and universal upfront price hikes. PRO's ownership frame—crystallized as a "tollbooth" on resale and a "discount versus toll" on pricing—remained the cleaner, more intact path to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's opening rebuttal [A2], especially the "seat heaters are not Netflix" line and the "proves too much" response to CON's claim that all installed hardware requires continuous software support, decisively collapsed CON's attempt to conflate basic hardware function with ongoing cloud-like services.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion narrow - permanent access to hardware already installed, not free cloud services - and won the decisive clash. CON's lower-base-price and ongoing-tuning story was undercut by simple heaters/tow hardware that work locally and by PRO's point that the proposition does not require installing premium parts in every car. CON never justified re-renting physical equipment or billing-risk, leaving PRO's ownership, double-charging, and resale tollbooth route intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 A1/A2 answers: no mandate to install everywhere, and basic function vs premium updates, which directly neutralizes CON's manufacturing-efficiency and liability-cost claims.
