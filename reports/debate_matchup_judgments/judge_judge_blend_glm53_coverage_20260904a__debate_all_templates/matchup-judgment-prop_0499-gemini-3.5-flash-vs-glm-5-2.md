CON compounded this with two sharp rebuttal moves that PRO couldn't recover from: (1) demonstrating that tethered ROVs use fiber-optic cables, not radio signals, directly undermining PRO's foundational technical premise; and (2) dismantling the "command hub" analogy by correctly noting that Mars rovers are commanded from Earth's surface, not the ISS—an unanswered specific rebuttal. CON co-opted the "this is a funding gap, not a platform gap" framing and used it more effectively. The motion asks about crewed missions at space-scale funding; CON consistently argued that crewed missions are the wrong tool for that specific objective, and PRO never provided a clean answer to the geometry problem. CON's closing was clipped but its core arguments were already fully developed. PRO's closing recycled earlier points without sharpening the choice.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): CON built the cleaner path to victory around two live physical arguments: (1) fiber-optic zero-latency removes the decisive reason human presence is required in space, and (2) a scientist sealed inside a high-pressure sphere operates through the same joystick-and-manipulator interface as an ROV pilot on the surface, so the spatial location of the operator does not change the science. PRO's best counter was the field-geologist analogy and the double-standard charge against crewed spaceflight. CON answered the spacewalk comparison credibly: astronauts on EVA physically grasp objects with gloved hands; a deep-sea scientist cannot exit the hull, so the analogy does not transfer. PRO's "peripheral sensory integration" claim [A4.2] remained somewhat speculative and was plausibly met by CON's observation that a surface team with multi-angle HD cameras, live sensor suites, and global expert collaboration has superior—not inferior—observational bandwidth to someone squinting through a small viewport. PRO's "new investment, not reallocation" move [A4.6] blunted the starving-robotics concern but did not answer CON's deeper efficiency point: within any ocean-science expansion, the same dollars deploy far more robotic coverage than a handful of crewed dives. The motion's own justification ("given how much of Earth's ocean floor remains unmapped") also cut against PRO, since bathymetric mapping is a sonar-and-AUV task; PRO reframed it as evidence of systemic underfunding, which was reasonable but left the "crewed specifically" burden only partially met. Both sides used some nonexistent reference IDs, slightly discounted but roughly balanced. CON's latency-physics argument remains the clearest structural distinction and PRO never neutralized it, giving CON a clear but competitive edge across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.2–B2.3 exchange was decisive: it established that the deep-sea scientist uses the same joystick-and-manipulator interface as a surface ROV operator while being confined inside a hull that cannot be exited, and simultaneously that zero-latency fiber-optic tethers eliminate the one physical justification—light-speed latency—that requires human presence in space. PRO's attempt to answer via the spacewalk analogy was turned back in B4.1 with the observation that spacewalking astronauts physically grasp objects with gloved hands, which a submersible occupant literally cannot do. This exchange remained live through the closing and was never squarely answered.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side A won by making the crewed-versus-robotic distinction decisive. Side B proved the ocean is important and underexplored, but did not adequately justify space-level funding for crewed deep-sea missions specifically. Side A repeatedly showed that mapping and much deep-sea sampling are better served by sonar, AUVs, ROVs, surface expert teams, longer endurance, lower risk, and lower cost. Side B's human-presence argument was rhetorically strong but less grounded because humans in submersibles still work through viewports and mechanical arms rather than direct field contact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to embodied judgment was most decisive: a scientist inside a pressure hull does not directly touch or freely observe the seafloor, while ROV teams can use similar manipulators with better cameras, collaboration, endurance, and safety.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by decisively breaking PRO's core space analogy and dismantling the mechanism for why humans must be physically present. PRO's case hinged on the claim that "embodied judgment" and adaptive discovery require a scientist at the seafloor, mirroring NASA's rationale for crewed spaceflight. CON systematically neutralized this by highlighting the physical reality of deep-sea subs: scientists remain trapped in a titanium hull at 1,000 atmospheres, manipulating the environment via joysticks and hydraulic arms—the exact same interface used by surface ROV pilots. CON correctly noted that zero-latency fiber-optic tethers eliminate the light-speed delay that necessitates astronauts in space, while surface control rooms offer superior safety, endurance, multi-angle sensors, and collaborative expertise. PRO repeatedly handwaved the joystick constraint with romanticized claims about "peripheral awareness" and "sediment texture," but never squared how a scientist inside a pressure sphere gains tactile or sensory advantages over a surface team. CON's opportunity cost argument (space-level funding for a few crewed subs starves scalable robotic fleets) further cemented the win. PRO's closing attempt to claim astronauts also only use mechanical arms was a weak save that failed to restore the broken analogy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1) directly answering PRO's pressure question on spacewalks. CON exposed that astronauts physically manipulate objects with gloved hands, whereas deep-sea scientists cannot leave their hulls and must use joysticks identical to surface ROV pilots. This single comparison collapsed PRO's "embodied judgment" warrant and broke the space analogy that PRO's entire case relied upon.
