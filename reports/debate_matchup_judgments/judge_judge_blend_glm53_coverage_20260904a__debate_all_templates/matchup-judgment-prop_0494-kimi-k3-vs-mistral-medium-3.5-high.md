### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by identifying unresolved incentive and power problems in both reforms. It showed that small payments risk piecework incentives while meaningful compensation creates substantial costs, and that signed publication exposes junior reviewers to retaliation. Side B never supplied a convincing mechanism showing payment would overcome that chilling effect or explaining who bears meaningful review costs. A's clipped second rebuttal was an execution cost, but its decisive reasoning was already visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange exposed the tension in B's claim that anonymous review entrenches powerful scholars while paid, signed criticism would become a career asset. B did not explain why the same powerful actors who allegedly abuse secrecy would reliably reward identifiable critics, leaving A's retaliation argument intact.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B built the cleaner path by consistently judging the motion on peer review's job (candid expert judgment) and showing both pillars either fail to improve it or actively degrade it. A never resolved the live contradiction that senior retaliation both requires removing anonymity and somehow turns public candor into a career asset, nor who absorbs real costs without crushing unfunded work; closings and pressure answers stayed recap-heavy. B's comparative framing ("flaws with an invoice") and pressure handling made the vote easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's capture of the A4.3 contradiction (anonymity entrenches powerful yet signed+paid review makes candor a career asset) plus the computing flagship that keeps reviewers anonymous.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won the two central clashes.
