### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by showing that foreign investment transfers scaling expertise, networks, and validation that authoritarian state funding cannot perfectly replace. B compellingly stressed visibility and physical chokepoints, but repeatedly treated screening as wholesale disengagement and never adequately connected outbound stakes in foreign firms to domestic market scale or laboratory funding. A therefore established a clearer marginal security benefit for targeted screening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange challenged B’s proposed trade of financing for visibility: if investors cannot prevent military diversion, an audit trail is a weak benefit compared with accelerating the underlying capability. B’s intelligence and entanglement response was sharp, but did not overcome that asymmetry.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by locking a decisive asymmetry PRO never neutralized: cash is replaceable for state-bank-backed rivals while market scale, commercial visibility, and recycled R&D profits are not replaceable for the screening country. Screening therefore raises militarization and blindness without defeating real export-control chokepoints. PRO’s fusion/smart-money and policy-schizophrenia frames were forceful but lost the comparative weighing once CON cleanly answered the fungibility trap and veto strawman.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A4.4 asymmetry resolution (cash fungible for them, scale/deal-flow not for us) directly dismantled PRO’s B4.4–B4.5 contradiction trap and carried into the closing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side A built a more coherent and damaging case, effectively exposing the contradictions in Side B's arguments regarding capital fungibility and the limits of Western corporate leverage in authoritarian states. Side A's framing of "policy schizophrenia" and the absurdity of using venture capital as an "expensive spy program" were highly persuasive and directly neutralized Side B's core defensive claims. Side B's attempt to split the difference on fungibility felt like a retreat, and its reliance on corporate audits to counter state military mandates was less convincing against Side A's reality checks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's "audit trail" defense by pointing out that national intelligence agencies exist for visibility, and sharply highlighted the contradiction in Side B's claims about capital being easily replaceable for the rival yet restricting it starving the domestic innovation base.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by defending a narrow screening regime rather than the blanket retreat Side B repeatedly attacked. A showed that investment can carry validation, networks, and tacit assistance beyond replaceable cash, then weighed even partial delay against irreversible security transfers. B preserved some value for commercial intelligence, but never established that investor visibility reaches military diversion or outweighs the security externality. Its claims of total global redlining and permanently induced rival self-sufficiency were comparatively speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer that export controls deny particular artifacts while outbound screening denies financing and support for workarounds most effectively defeated B’s redundancy argument. Its prospective risk standard and safe-harbor response also undercut B’s claim that screening necessarily creates impossible retroactive liability.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner path to victory: irreversible capability transfer plus classic externality outweigh reversible fund returns and compliance costs, and export-control complementarity survived CON's redundancy attack. CON's best live routes—intelligence windows and fungibility/self-sufficiency—were repeatedly damaged by secrecy, profit-motive, and "rivals still chase Western backing" pressure; CON never fully neutralized that weighing. CON's chilling-effect and early-stage pivot points remained live but secondary to PRO's decisive stakes frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 A1/A2: capital funds workarounds export controls cannot stop, and screening uses knowable-at-investment criteria with safe harbors rather than crystal-ball liability—collapsing CON redundancy and broad-redline claims.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A secured a solid victory by systematically dismantling Side B's core arguments regarding intelligence gathering and capital fungibility. A persuasively argued that minority investors in authoritarian regimes lack both the access (due to secrecy laws) and the incentive (due to profit motives) to act as reliable early-warning systems. Additionally, A effectively distinguished between mere financial capital and the ecosystem validation, talent attraction, and workaround funding that Western investment provides, proving that screening imposes a meaningful strategic tax on rivals. Side B's case relied on an overly optimistic view of VC visibility in closed societies and struggled to overcome A's sharp, well-grounded rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the "VC as intelligence asset" argument, highlighting that "a profit-seeking guest shown only what hosts choose is not visibility, it is vanity," and explaining that capital funds the very workarounds that defeat export controls.
