### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): A wins because it made the decisive burden clearer: expanding coercive policing powers requires more than asserted pilot success and hypothetical safeguards. B had a strong urgency frame and effectively pressed A for an immediate alternative, which A largely dodged, but B's case relied heavily on brittle exact claims and an untested bundle of oversight mechanisms. A's repeated point that targeted stop-and-search has historically produced racial disparities, and that B could not prove its safeguards would contain those harms at scale, left the stronger live route to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's most decisive exchange was turning B's Scotland example: even if reporting reduced disproportionality, disparity remained, and B still had no real precedent for the full reporting-plus-disciplinary-board package it wanted Europe to expand.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a clear, heavily weighed tradeoff: saving lives immediately versus accepting a reduced, but non-zero, rate of unjust stops. Side B effectively proved that A's safeguards are untested as a complete bundle and that racial disparities will persist, but B failed to offer an immediate alternative to save lives or overcome A's moral weighing that preventing deaths matters more than eliminating all unjustified stops.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, which introduces the Scotland example to prove partial safeguard efficacy and heavily presses Side B's lack of an immediate alternative intervention to save lives.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON won by showing that PRO's bundled safeguards—real-time reporting plus independent community oversight with disciplinary power—have never been implemented together at scale, making the policy an untested experiment rather than an evidence-backed expansion. PRO's reliance on Scotland's partial reporting-only system was exposed as extrapolation, and CON's critique of biased intelligence inputs and 911 calls undermined the feasibility of "targeted" enforcement. While PRO framed the moral urgency effectively, they could not meet the burden of proving this expansion would overcome the historical pattern of racial disparities in stop-and-search powers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (Turn 8), which dismantled PRO's Scotland evidence by showing it lacked disciplinary oversight and left substantial disparities intact, while exposing that 911 calls and police intelligence themselves encode the racial bias the safeguards claim to solve.
