### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A built a consistent throughline from opening to closing: broad tariffs are taxes requiring congressional consent, emergency declarations let one person set scope and rate, and the veto/filibuster dynamic makes "reform" toothless once a tariff is already in force. Crucially, A's fallback position (Congress may pre-authorize narrow, time-limited, mandatory-vote responses) was stated in the very first speech (A1.6), not invented late, which undercut B's closing "gotcha" that A had abandoned the motion. A's pressure questions exposed a real structural weakness in B's design (inertia favors the tariff staying alive), forcing B to redesign its proposal mid-debate into an automatic-sunset/fast-reauthorization model. That adaptation was clever and substantively useful for B, but it also functioned as a concession that unbounded presidential discretion (the status quo A attacks) is indeed unworkable without exactly the kind of binding, Congress-controlled trigger A was defending. B's strongest closing move—claiming A had quietly abandoned strict elimination—was rhetorically sharp but overstated given A's consistent position, and it substituted definitional point-scoring for direct engagement with A's consent/enforcement framing. On balance, A gave the cleaner, more sustained comparative case and handled pressure more directly, while B's case, though strong and adaptive, ended by relying on a contestable reframing rather than fully neutralizing A's core distinction between unilateral declaration and ex ante congressional authorization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's veto/filibuster pressure question (A3.2) and B's forced pivot to an automatic-sunset/fast-reauthorization fix in rebuttal 2 (B4.2) was the decisive exchange: it showed A's structural critique had real force, while B's fix, though inventive, tacitly conceded that current open-ended emergency tariff authority is indefensible without something functionally close to what A proposed.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): This was a tightly matched debate, but CON's rebuttal work did more damage to PRO's live route than vice versa. PRO's case rested on a distinction between "targeted safeguards" and "sweeping tariff regimes," but CON's Rebuttal 1 (B2.1-B2.2) exposed that this line collapses exactly where it matters, and CON's Pressure Questions and Rebuttal 2 (B4.4) turned this into a symmetry argument: PRO's own "scope-to-conduct" test is just as contestable as CON's security-nexus standard, so the real choice is about speed and consequences, not about which side has a cleaner rule. CON also never let the speed critique drop—pointing out that "expedited congressional procedures" still mean committees, markups, and veto points, a point PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1) did not squarely rebut. PRO's strongest and most consistent point—that letting the president apply standards first with only after-the-fact disapproval reverses Article I—remained live and was pressed effectively in closing, but CON's answer (pre-collection delay, privileged disapproval, judicial review before the main economic effect) was a serious procedural response that PRO's closing addressed only by gesturing at "announcement effects" without explaining why a floor vote would be faster or less disruptive. Both closings crystallized the clash well, but CON's framing that a reformed reserve authority beats outright elimination proved slightly more resilient under the pressure exchanged.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 (B2.1-B2.2), which exposed the definitional collapse in PRO's targeted/sweeping distinction, and its callback in Rebuttal 2 (B4.4) turning PRO's own pressure questions into a symmetry argument against PRO's alternative test.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing response to Side B's "delay before collection" argument was decisive, pointing out that retaliatory threats and supply chain shifts begin upon announcement, meaning the economic burden of a broad tax is imposed before Congress ever votes.
