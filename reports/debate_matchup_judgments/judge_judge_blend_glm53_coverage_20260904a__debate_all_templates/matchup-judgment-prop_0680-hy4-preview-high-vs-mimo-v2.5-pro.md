- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): B wins by maintaining a tighter logical grip on the proposition's text and the specific mechanism of harm.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (A2) cleanly separates structural bias in hospital data from the self-fulfilling 'ratchet' of historical arrest data, neutralizing A's strongest pressure question.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won a close, high-quality clash by defending the motion as written: systems whose dominant patrol signal is historical arrest/incident data should be banned because that input is endogenous and self-ratcheting. CON’s best move—the ban-vs-mandate and removal-test dilemma—was sharp, but it mostly described lawful enrichment that PRO already treated as outside the ban; CON never cleanly showed why cities should keep systems that still run primarily on that foundation under audit theater. PRO’s endogenous/feedback distinction, functional “primarily” test, and continuous-harm weighing stayed live and decisive. CON’s clipped first rebuttal and thinner alternative mechanism cost some ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 plus B4 answers: if enrichment dethrones historical enforcement data, it is already a different tool; if incident data remains the foundation/driver, audits leave the ratchet running—so the motion’s ban, not a mandate that licenses that foundation, is the right line.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO controlled the definition of "primarily" with a functional removal test that answered CON's vagueness and scope attacks. CON conceded the foundation was enforcement data and that enrichment could remove it, then argued for a mandate that PRO showed is either superfluous or licenses the banned ratchet, while CON's alternative remains district-commander discretion. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly winning the comparative metric. Side B opens by arguing that human bias infects historical data, but later defends unaudited human intuition (supervisors reviewing maps) as superior to algorithms. Side A ruthlessly exploits this contradiction, proving that an auditable, regulated algorithm with corrective triggers is much safer than reverting to the very human discretion that created the biased data. Side B's multiple clipped speeches also hindered their execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing successfully turned Side B's defense of human crime maps into a fatal contradiction, arguing that if human review of incident data is legitimate, an audited model is just that same judgment made testable and bounded.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by forcing a decisive comparative frame: PRO effectively legitimized human review of shootings/911 incident maps, then could not cleanly show why an audited model using the same serious-incident signals, with stop-data excluded and feedback frozen, must be categorically banned. CON repeatedly converted PRO’s feedback and civil-rights critique into a regulation-vs-discretion choice, arguing a ban removes the measurable brake while leaving unauditable historical deployment intact. PRO’s core loop/“thought experiment” attack was powerful and rhetorically sharper, but repeated clipping and a weaker answer on the human-map concession left the ban looking overbroad rather than necessary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A5: PRO’s concession that a commander’s monthly serious-incident map is legitimate undercuts categorical prohibition of a regulated model using the same victim/sensor inputs once stop-driven feedback is excluded and audits have mandatory triggers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON won the core trade: PRO's feedback loop assumes arrest-driven inputs that CON explicitly excludes, replacing them with victim/sensor serious-incident signals and frozen feedback with audit triggers. PRO conceded human review of shootings/911 maps is legitimate but failed to justify why the same data made auditable should be banned, and its key rebuttals and closings were clipped. The ban thus removes accountability without removing history-based deployment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2 directly answering A3: explaining how excluding patrol-generated stops, weighting by call rates, and pairing audits with mandatory re-weight/pause breaks the loop and turns audit from record of harm into safeguard.
