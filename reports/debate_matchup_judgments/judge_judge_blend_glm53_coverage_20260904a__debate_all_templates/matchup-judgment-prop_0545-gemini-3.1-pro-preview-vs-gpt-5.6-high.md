### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A built the cleaner comparative path: opt-in systematically misclassifies silence as refusal while presumed consent makes donation eligible under notice/opt-out/family reporting without treating silence as irrevocable permission. This directly yields a measurable (even modest) increase for most patients via more candidates entering a functioning recovery pathway; B's harms require assuming away ordinary safeguards or forcing inflammatory framing that is not inherent to the default. B's ethical asymmetry and soft-opt-out adversarial claims remain live but never neutralize the mechanism or prove necessary rather than contingent rises in violations/distress. A better answered pressure and consistently weighed two imperfect real defaults rather than perfect opt-in vs faulty presumed consent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 answers: soft default converts uncertainty into consideration without inventing family authorship or coercion, while silence is only rebuttable eligibility after accessible notice (not forced consent), exposing B's "inevitable" extraction as an implementation assumption rather than structural necessity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won by reframing the debate as a choice between two imperfect defaults rather than a comparison of perfect opt-in against flawed presumed consent. Side A landed a powerful ethical asymmetry—active bodily violation versus passive omission—but blunted its own impact by conceding that soft opt-out systems still consult families, which narrowed the window for its claimed inevitable violations and made the worst-case scenario seem more contingent than structural. Side B maintained a direct, plausible mechanism for a marginal rate increase, answered pressure questions cleanly, and showed that Side A's empirical backlash stories depended on poor implementation rather than the default itself. Side A kept the debate competitive with memorable rhetoric and a strong moral intuition, but Side B's consistent distinction between rebuttable eligibility and irrevocable consent, together with its exposure of Side A's false dilemma, provided the clearer path to affirming the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer to Side A's Q2 in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3–B4.5), distinguishing rebuttable legal eligibility from irrevocable consent and exposing the false dilemma between perfect registration and coercive extraction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO established a direct default-effect mechanism for a modest measurable increase and answered the two core CON charges: that any imperfection equals ethical violation, and that soft opt-out must be adversarial. CON relied on inevitable failure and backlash from Brazil/Chile and Spain coordinator claims but did not show those failures are typical for most patients or that distress must rise under compassionate implementation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 reframing of silence, soft opt-out question shift from "decide" to "evidence of objection," and infrastructure-as-complement, which directly undercut CON's equivalence and inevitability claims.
