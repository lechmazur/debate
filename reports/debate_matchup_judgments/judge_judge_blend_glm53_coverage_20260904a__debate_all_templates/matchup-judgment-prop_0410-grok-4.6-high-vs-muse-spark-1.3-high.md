- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by grounding outcomes in stability, caregiving quality, and like-for-like comparisons, then connecting those factors directly to concrete legal protections. Its argument repeatedly bundled prior breakup, donor conception, biological relatedness, and parental sex while asserting gaps without adequate grounding. It also treated equal legal protection as declaring mothers and fathers interchangeable, which did not answer A’s child-security rationale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest reply was that stable-to-stable comparison does not remove the defining feature of same-sex parenting; it separates that feature from disruption and disadvantage. A also neutralized B's practical harms by conceding that policy can secure ties to existing caregivers without legally declaring mothers and fathers interchangeable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's breakdown of B's drug trial analogy in Rebuttal 1, arguing that family form is not a pill given to identical children but the very pathway into the home, decisively framed B's controls as evading the actual average.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): A won the decisive methodological clash by showing that controlling for stability and income isolates the actual effect of parental sex, whereas B’s “on average” conflated family structure with prior disruption. A also landed the sharper policy point: denying rights harms real children in foster care without increasing mother-father placements, while B’s distinction between “loss” and “design” remained unsupported and selectively applied.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s rebuttal that controlling for confounds stops blaming two mothers for a prior straight divorce, which neutralized B’s core claim that “stable-to-stable” comparisons change the question.
