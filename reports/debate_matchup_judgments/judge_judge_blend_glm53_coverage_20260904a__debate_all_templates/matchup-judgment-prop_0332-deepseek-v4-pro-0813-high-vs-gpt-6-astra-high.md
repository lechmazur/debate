### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and stayed on the motion, but CON (A) built the more coherent path. A's opening distinction between "targeting minors" and "audience likely to include minors" forced PRO to defend a fuzzier standard, and A's alternative package (mandatory age-assured distribution, creator liability for arranging/concealing evasion, operator/platform duties) gave a concrete less-restrictive comparator that PRO had to beat, not just gesture past. PRO's rebuttal 1 landed a real hit exposing tension between A's claim that age gates are "porous" and A's reliance on age-assured accounts, but A defused this with the seatbelt analogy (risk-reduction vs. guaranteed outcome) and clarified its standard in rebuttal 2. Under pressure, PRO's answer that "absent or mixed" audience data alone should trigger prohibition let A land the sharpest closing blow: this turns mere uncertainty into grounds for banning, and the same repost/spillover risk PRO cites to reject a safe harbor applies symmetrically to the "genuinely adult" promotions PRO claims to preserve — a contradiction PRO's closing never answered, instead mischaracterizing A's model as reactive "waiting for remediation" when A had already specified pre-publication adult-only distribution. That unaddressed asymmetry, plus A having the last, most surgical word, tips a competitive debate to CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing/rebuttal-2 point that PRO's "absent or mixed data" standard turns uncertainty itself into grounds for prohibition, combined with the observation that the same repost/spillover risk PRO uses to deny a safe harbor applies equally to the adult-audience promotions PRO claims to leave untouched — a contradiction PRO never squarely resolved.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully exposing a fatal contradiction in Side A's reliance on imperfect safeguards. Side A argued that age-gates fail because videos are clipped and reposted, but Side B accurately pointed out that Side A's permitted "genuinely adult" content would face the exact same third-party copying. Side B effectively demonstrated that Side A was demanding impossible guarantees rather than regulating controllable conduct.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was the decisive turn. By forcing Side A to admit that even "mixed" data or foreseeable reposting would trigger the ban, Side B proved that the proposition acts as an overbroad penalty for uncontrollable internet dynamics rather than a targeted harm-reduction measure.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by making the decisive clash cleaner: the motion’s audience-composition trigger is broader and less workable than enforceable conduct/distribution duties. PRO had the stronger intuitive harm story (parasocial endorsement, residual risk on the paid promoter) and landed early pressure on imperfect gates, but never stably defined “substantial/likely” without collapsing uncertainty into a ban, and rejected whole-video safe harbors for the same foreseeable-repost problem that also hits the “adult-only” promotions PRO promised to spare. CON’s alternative package plus that contradiction left the clearer reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2/closing trap: PRO’s hard no to a reasonable adult-only safe harbor on the sponsored video, justified by foreseeable coded reposts, collides with PRO’s claim that genuinely adult promotions remain untouched—exposing overreach rather than necessity.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides ran a coherent, well-executed case, but PRO built the more resilient line. PRO anchored on a clear normative distinction—recruitment (trusted-endorsement persuasion) versus access (registration/verification)—and sustained it across every round, while proposing a concrete, if imperfect, operational test (conduct-based safe harbor plus content/context recruitment markers) in response to pressure. CON's strongest weapon was the "unworkable standard/de facto ban" objection, and its pressure questions forced PRO into a real concession: the safe harbor effectively excludes ordinary open-feed influencer promotion. But PRO absorbed that concession cleanly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing by reframing it as the intended, proportionate cost ("some adult advertising reach will be lost... narrower than CON's silence"), which tracks the motion's literal scope (prohibiting promotion to audiences likely to include minors, i.e., most influencer audiences). CON never fully answered why access verification alone neutralizes the persuasion/normalization harm PRO kept isolating; its closing repeated "regulate the transaction, not the audience estimate" without dissolving PRO's point that transaction-stage fixes leave trust-based recruitment untouched. CON's underground-displacement argument was plausible but speculative and was met with a reasonable (if not airtight) rebuttal that concealment incentives already exist under disclosure regimes. Overall PRO answered pressure more directly and kept the normative throughline sharper, giving it the cleaner path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over PRO's safe-harbor test (B4.1) and CON's exploitation of it (A4.4, A5.1) as proof of a de facto ban was the pivotal clash; PRO's closing reframing of that restriction as the intended protection rather than a failure mode was the more persuasive resolution of that exchange.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clear distinction between the harm of 'recruitment' (normalization via trust) and 'entry' (access to accounts), which neutralized Side B's reliance on age-verification checks at the casino. Side B leaned heavily on the impossibility of verifying every viewer (e.g., shared devices), but Side A effectively neutralized this by establishing a prospective, conduct-based safe harbor that did not demand perfection. Side A then expertly capitalized on Side B's concession in Rebuttal 2 to seal the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Closing exposed a fatal contradiction in Side B's fallback position. When Side B argued that "if the platform cannot separate minors, the platform should not carry that placement," Side A rightly pointed out that this effectively concedes the core premise: influencers should not be allowed to sell high-risk gambling placements to mixed-age feeds.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the motion’s decisive clash live: whether promoters may sell high-risk gambling through foreseeably mixed-age trust relationships, and by showing access checks address entry not recruitment. CON’s de facto-ban/workability attack was real and well pressed, especially against ordinary open feeds, but PRO’s safe-harbor and objective-recruitment answers, plus CON’s own platform-fallback concession, left PRO with the cleaner burden assignment and narrower commercial limit rather than a speech ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 answers (A1/A2) plus closing use of CON’s “if platform cannot separate, platform should not carry” line—turning CON’s workability objection into agreement that mixed-age high-risk placement should be restricted while still assigning the sponsorship choice to creators.
