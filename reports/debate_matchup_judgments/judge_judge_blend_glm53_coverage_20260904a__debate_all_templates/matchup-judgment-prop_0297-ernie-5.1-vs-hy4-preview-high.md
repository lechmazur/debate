### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): The debate turned on the Germany analogy and the honor-vs-evidence distinction. A built its opening case partly on Germany's Nazi-era sites as proof that "contextual preservation" of honorific objects works, but B's pressure question (Q2) exposed that Germany actually destroyed honorific statues and only preserved crime-scene sites, forcing A into a direct concession ("I cannot, because Germany destroyed those") and a strained pivot claiming relocated monuments become "crime scenes of memory." B correctly identified this as smuggling a redefinition rather than answering the disanalogy. B also successfully reframed Bristol from A's "public learning" success story into a cautionary tale of appeasement and ongoing cost, and consistently pressed the sharper conceptual point that museum preservation still dignifies the honorific object (reversibility as "life support" for the tribute) while archives/photos already preserve the evidentiary function—directly undercutting both halves of the motion's justification clause. A's case remained coherent and well-argued throughout, with good use of contested-space framing and reversibility-as-virtue, but its flagship example collapsed under direct questioning and its recovery attempt was less convincing than B's clean, escalating rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Question Q2 forcing A to concede Germany destroyed (not relocated) honorific Nazi statues, followed by B's rebuttal exposing A's "crime scenes of memory" pivot as a redefinition rather than a real answer.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won decisively by establishing a strict distinction between evidentiary artifacts (or crime scenes) and honorific monuments. By pressing this in the Pressure Questions, Side A forced Side B to concede that Germany destroyed its honorific statues, collapsing Side B's core historical analogy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second pressure question trapped Side B into admitting Germany destroyed its honorific statues, which Side A then brilliantly used in Rebuttal 2 to show Side B had conceded the core clash regarding celebratory bronze.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: contested monuments are honorific awards, not neutral evidence. PRO never escaped that; after pressure, PRO conceded Germany destroyed celebratory statues and that its model is “not preserving celebratory bronze in a gallery,” then stretched monuments into “crime scenes of memory.” CON’s empty-plinth/archives reply beat the amnesia charge, and Colston was used as a live cost of indoor harm rather than proof of learning. PRO had a coherent irreversibility/teaching theory, but it lost the comparison on the actual objects at issue.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s honorific-vs-artifact distinction plus the Germany/Nazi-statue pressure, capped when A turned B’s B4.3 concession against the relocation model.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases, but PRO (A) sustained the more decisive comparative frame: irreversibility. A's central asymmetry—"the flawed placard can be corrected next year; the erased statue cannot be recalled"—was never fully neutralized by CON. CON (B) landed real damage with the UDC example and the "corrigible in theory vs. practice" critique, and its pressure question about Memento Park collapsing "museum" into any peripheral dump into triviality was sharp. But A's answers, while not airtight, kept the throughline consistent (democratic oversight continues after the square is reclaimed; context+access, not location, is the standard), and A's closing tied the asymmetry argument directly back to the motion's own language about erasure being worse than preservation. B's strongest counter—that destruction delivers "moral clarity" and a "decisive break"—remained more asserted than demonstrated, and its answer to the martyr-myth pressure question ("the space itself is evidence") was comparatively thin against A's concrete point about physical evidence enabling future accountability. The debate was close and both sides answered pressure questions with proper A1/A2 formatting, but A's framework proved slightly more resilient under sustained clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 point that "corrigible in theory and corrigible in practice are different things," backed by the UDC museum-wing example, was the most damaging single exchange—but A's counter in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, reasserting that even slow correction is possible only if the object survives while destruction forecloses correction permanently, was the exchange that most shaped the final decision.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won a very close debate by effectively attacking Side B's mechanism of 'contextualization.' Side A demonstrated that heroic bronze statues inherently resist critical framing and continue to act as shrines, successfully framing the resulting 'corrigible' museum experience as a permanent, unwinnable curatorial war.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response in Rebuttal 1 distinguishing between a lynching photograph (which inherently horrifies) and a heroic bronze statue (which inherently draws pilgrimage and veneration), neutralizing Side B's claim that context easily turns monuments into neutral evidence.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by holding the cleaner comparative: relocation already ends civic honor while keeping a corrigible record; destruction clears a square and creates an irreversible void that mythmakers can fill. CON landed real pressure on unreliable museum framing, UDC-style capture, and residual pilgrimage, but never fully showed that imperfect curation is worse for the public than losing the object. PRO answered both pressure questions cleanly, kept the square-plus-evidence vs gesture-plus-void weighing live, and crystallized the fixable-placard vs unrecallable-statue asymmetry without late evidence dumps. CON’s best route—context often fails, so the proposition’s benefit claim fails—was strong but incomplete against irreversibility and the martyr-myth burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s repeated asymmetry weighing (esp. B2/B4/B5): imperfect museum context is rewriteable because the object survives; melted bronze cannot be recalled and weakens future rebuttal of lost-cause myths. CON’s lynching-photo distinction was the sharpest counterpunch but did not neutralize that comparison.
