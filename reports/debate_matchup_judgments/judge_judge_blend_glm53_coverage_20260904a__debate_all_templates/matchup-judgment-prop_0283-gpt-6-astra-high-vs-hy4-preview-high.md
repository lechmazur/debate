### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON built the more durable case by anchoring on the historical fact that Mexico has already run a decade of militarized, intelligence-led, kingpin-focused enforcement — directly undercutting PRO's framing of "avoidance." PRO tried to escape this by redefining "effective" confrontation as sustained hold-and-govern rather than raid-and-withdraw, but CON's pressure question ("what specific military action have you not already tried?") exposed that this redefinition lacked concrete new content, and PRO's answer confirmed it by offering only vaguer emphasis on "sustained" and "not treating arrest as completion" rather than a distinct mechanism. CON then weaponized PRO's own concessions — "escalation does not make officials honest" and "military support need not manufacture honesty" — to argue that PRO's proposal either applies only where the state is already capable (redundant with what CON already conceded as ordinary enforcement) or fails precisely where cartel power is entrenched. PRO's closing narrowed the case to "clear and hold wherever feasible," which CON effectively reframed as proof the harder approach shrinks away from the hardest cases. CON's rhetoric (harder war vs. harder state; "arson with a sharper slogan") was sharper and more memorable, and CON maintained a more consistent throughline across rounds while PRO's case increasingly relied on definitional distinctions that never got concretely cashed out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question ("What specific military action do you now propose that has not already been tried—beyond 'try harder and succeed'?") combined with PRO's own admission that "escalation does not make officials honest," which CON then used in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to show PRO's safeguards concede the case.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash by collapsing Pro’s “avoidance” premise into an already-tried militarized decade and then using Pro’s own prerequisites (hold capacity, honesty, witnesses, durable policing) to show that escalation now either shrinks into ordinary enforcement Pro already concedes or repeats clear-and-withdraw fragmentation. A argued the complementary-force point sharply and forced B to accept military backup when police are overmatched, but never escaped the circular “it wasn’t effective confrontation” defense or the concession that force does not manufacture institutional honesty where cartel control is worst.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.2–B4.4 / B5.1–B5.2: Pro’s pacing-to-capacity and “force doesn’t make officials honest” concessions turn the harder approach into either ordinary enforcement in already-capable places or another hollow-state raid elsewhere.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won by showing that Mexico’s prior decade of militarized enforcement already tested the harder approach and produced fragmentation, abuses, and persistent cartel control because institutional prerequisites were missing. CON effectively turned PRO’s concessions—that force cannot make officials honest and that operations should concentrate where civilian institutions can consolidate gains—into a decisive dilemma: the harder approach either applies where the state is already strong (making it unnecessary) or repeats clear-and-withdraw failures where cartels dominate. PRO’s distinction between past raids and sustained holding operations was plausible but never fully overcame the historical pattern or justified how escalation would succeed without the very capacities PRO admitted were lacking.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal (Turn 8), where B answered A’s pressure questions, leveraged PRO’s admission that escalation does not create institutional honesty, and showed that PRO’s own safeguards shrink the motion’s harder approach to ordinary enforcement in already-secure areas or doomed offensives in hollow-state zones.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and used real, plausible background knowledge (Culiacán, Ovidio Guzmán's 2019 release and 2023 recapture, National Guard) without fake precision. A built a strong opening narrative that non-confrontation produced entrenchment and mass violence, and pressed effectively on B's lack of a limiting principle for what "harder" excludes. But B's core structural critique—that A's remedy assumes the very "hold" capacity whose absence explains cartel resilience—was never neutralized, and A's own answer to B's pressure question ("If even that hold is impossible... strike anyway, then hold") handed B exactly the concession it needed. B exploited this decisively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, showing A's escalation proposal risks retaliation and fragmentation without securing the protective benefit it promises, and cleanly called out A's later mischaracterization of B's standard as demanding a "perfect hold." Since the motion requires showing a harder approach is justified, not just that restraint failed, B's unanswered capacity/fragmentation critique carries more weight at the end of the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 response to A's A1 answer ("an impossible hold does not become possible because it comes later in a sentence"), which converted A's own concession about striking without holding capacity into the decisive proof that the proposed "harder approach" risks civilian exposure without delivering the protection it promises.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won by keeping the burden on durable protection under shared constraints and forcing PRO's sequence into a damaging concession: strike even when holding capacity is unavailable. That undercut PRO's claim that expanded command-node offensives make protection stick, while CON preserved a coherent limiting principle (force only when protection can support it) and showed that PRO's "harder" package still assumes the capacities it claims are missing. PRO built a clear failure narrative around Culiacán and the shopkeeper, but never closed the gap between aggression and reliable post-strike security.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5/B4.6 catching A's "if hold is impossible, strike anyway" answer and showing the promised hold cannot do the work assigned to it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A won by proving the status quo has demonstrably failed on its own terms—producing entrenched cartel sovereignty, sustained high violence, and corrupted local institutions—while showing that the federal capacity B demanded already exists. A successfully turned B’s shopkeeper hypothetical and exposed that B lacked a limiting principle: B conceded arrests, interdictions, and even command-node recaptures were compatible with its view, making its alternative indistinguishable from the failed policy. B’s fragmentation warnings were plausible but remained speculative, whereas A offered a concrete sequencing story and met the comparative burden that contesting criminal control is better than ceding it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing dismantled B’s capacity objection by showing federal forces already exist, that “build first” is impossible under cartel curfews, and that B could not name a command-node offensive it actually opposed—revealing B’s alternative as the failed status quo rebranded.
