### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A won by turning B's concessions about solitary behavior, individual housing, enrichment, and stunning into a clear conflict between industrial density and species-appropriate welfare. B offered a plausible regulatory alternative, but its relocation and research claims were sharply answered, while its closing relied on a late wild-capture argument. A's economic incompatibility claim was not fully proven, keeping the debate competitive, and A also incurred clipping costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was the individual-enclosure dilemma: if every octopus requires its own enriched den, the model loses the density that makes industrial farming attractive; if density is restored, the conceded welfare problem returns. B's premium-market response showed theoretical possibility but did not fully answer A's concern that cheap, cruel production would remain commercially favored.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO established that industrial octopus farming is structurally incompatible with octopus welfare because their solitary, territorial, enrichment-dependent biology cannot be reconciled with the density required for industrial profit. CON conceded cognition but relied on speculative market optimism and untested scaling of low-density husbandry, failing to show how a welfare-compliant model could be economically viable at industrial scale. PRO's pressure questions exposed this gap, and PRO's closing crystallized the dilemma: CON's defensible farm is not industrial, and its industrial farm is not defensible. CON's closing improperly introduced a new wild-capture argument that had not been part of the live exchange, leaving PRO's structural cruelty argument as the decisive, unanswered route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's dismantling of CON's pig-regulation analogy by distinguishing social, domesticated pigs from solitary, cannibalistic octopuses, showing that the welfare fix for pigs (more space together) is biologically meaningless for octopuses and that CON's own species-appropriate welfare standard (individual enclosures and enrichment) necessarily destroys the industrial density that makes farming economically viable.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO built a much stronger and more cohesive case, effectively trapping CON in a dilemma between "industrial" (which CON conceded requires cruel density) and "humane" (which PRO proved is uneconomic and not truly industrial). PRO's dismantling of CON's pig and free-range egg analogies was decisive, and the closing argument about the reality of a "commercial failure" in aquaculture was rhetorically and logically devastating.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1, where they dismantled the pig analogy by highlighting the solitary vs. social nature of the animals, and flipped the prohibition paradox by pointing to the global nature of the proposed ban.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that complex cognition supports stringent welfare standards but does not establish that all commercial confinement is inherently cruel. A conceded that individual humane housing is biologically possible, then relied mainly on speculative cost pressure. B answered that concern with a licensing gate: require adequate housing, enrichment, and validated stunning, and permit no operation that cannot afford them. A also never convincingly resolved why banning farms improves on wild capture, while its proposed demand shift remained hypothetical. B occasionally overstated the claim that cognition alone was A's argument, but its proportionality and counterfactual analysis still carried the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest response was that commercial cost does not justify a categorical ban: regulators can mandate the humane standard, allowing no farm if compliance is unaffordable and humane farming if it is feasible. This directly undercut A's move from biological complexity to claimed economic impossibility.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON won by showing that PRO retreated from the motion’s explicit ground—complex cognition justifying a ban—to the narrower claim that humane commercial farming is currently unprofitable. CON’s licensing-gate alternative and the persistent wild-caught counterfactual demonstrated that a global ban would protect hypothetical farmed octopuses while abandoning real ones to unregulated deck slaughter, making abolition disproportionate and inconsistent with the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing that forced PRO to concede individual housing and anaesthesia are biologically possible in labs, collapsing PRO’s case from “cognition makes confinement indefensible” to “producers won’t pay for it,” which is an economic rather than cognitive argument.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a highly convincing case by leveraging the exact text of the motion and introducing the "licensing gate" policy mechanism. CON successfully forced PRO to concede that humane housing is biologically possible (as seen in labs), shifting PRO's argument to economic tractability. CON then neutralized this by arguing that strict regulations would simply price out non-compliant farms, achieving PRO's welfare goals without the downsides of a global ban. CON also maintained strong pressure regarding the unregulated wild-catch alternative, which PRO failed to adequately resolve. Side B (PRO) argued well but relied on a somewhat rigid definition of "industrial" and struggled to overcome CON's logical traps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it introduced the "licensing gate" concept to answer PRO's pressure questions, effectively neutralizing PRO's economic tractability argument while adhering to the regulatory framework.
