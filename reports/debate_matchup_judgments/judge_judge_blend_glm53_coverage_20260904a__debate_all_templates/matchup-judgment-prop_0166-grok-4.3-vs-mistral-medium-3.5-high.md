### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side B won by keeping the debate centered on the motion's actual tradeoff: governments should fund de-extinction even when it diverts money and attention from current endangered species. Side A repeatedly tried to deny or minimize diversion through private funding and spillovers, but that evaded the harder burden and relied on overconfident, weakly grounded claims about already-delivered cross-benefits. Side B gave the cleaner comparison: proven prevention for living species has immediate, measurable returns, while mammoth and thylacine revival adds uncertainty, infrastructure cost, regulatory burden, and speculative ecosystem benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's most decisive rebuttal was that any useful genetic or reproductive technology could be funded directly for endangered species, without routing scarce public resources through the riskier and slower task of reconstructing extinct animals.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly dismantling Side B's core "force multiplier" argument. Side A successfully points out that if the goal is to develop gene-editing tools for living endangered species, direct funding is vastly more efficient and less risky than routing that research through the highly uncertain middleman of an extinct species.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 dismantles both Side B's technological spillover argument (noting that tools should be applied directly rather than routed through extinct species) and the Yellowstone wolf analogy (distinguishing a briefly absent extant species from an extinct one entering a permanently altered Arctic).
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side B wins by successfully isolating the motion's core constraint: finite government resources and regulatory capacity. While Side A built an ambitious case around technological spillovers and ecosystem restoration, it relied on suspiciously precise, likely fabricated claims about de-extinction pipelines already delivering disease-resistant honeycreepers and improved ferret breeding "years ahead" of direct programs. Side B sharply dismantled this by arguing that the same tools could be applied directly to endangered species faster and with less risk, making de-extinction an inefficient detour rather than a multiplier. Side B also effectively neutralized the Yellowstone analogy by distinguishing extant reintroduction from reconstructing extinct roles in fundamentally altered ecosystems. Side B's closing cleanly crystallized the clash around irreversible present losses versus hypothetical future gains, providing a clearer, more epistemically disciplined path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 exchange, which dismantled Side A's Yellowstone analogy and spillover claims by introducing the direct-vs-indirect routing comparison. By showing that genetic rescue tools advance faster when applied straight to living species rather than routed through high-risk extinct species reconstruction, Side B neutralized PRO's central multiplier argument and preserved the opportunity cost clash.
