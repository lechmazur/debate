### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B won by isolating and weaponizing two concessions from Side A: that the mandate exposes code to attackers and imposes real preparation costs on taxpayers. In Rebuttal 2, A explicitly stated "I accept that risk" regarding accelerated attacker discovery and admitted that "preparation costs are real and fall on taxpayers." B immediately converted these into a clear story in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, framing A's benefits as merely theoretical leverage while the harms to local infrastructure were concrete and immediate. Although A maintained a principled public-control frame, the concession that the policy arms attackers and taxes underfunded municipalities proved fatal because B never let it go. B's pressure questions were sharper, its rebuttals did more comparative weighing, and its closing crystallized the clash around A's own admissions, making the decision cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8), where B seized on A's twin concessions—that the mandate arms attackers and imposes real unfunded costs—and framed them as decisive against A's speculative long-term leverage, setting up the knockout Closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO kept a live path on lifecycle control: distinguishing software from credentials/secrets, showing narrow exception can cover genuine critical systems, and weighing upfront publication costs against lock-in premiums. CON's strongest security and vendor-pool attacks were answered but repeated without adaptation, and its concessions framing ignored A's total-cost comparison. A handled pressure questions directly with A1/A2 while exposing CON's volunteer-only defense framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 – accepting that source helps attackers but explaining withholding doesn't fix bugs, that publication is software not credentials, and that openness enables paid competing maintainers without requiring an extra auditor ecosystem, which directly neutralized B's frictionless-roadmap and unfunded-tax core.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A wins by effectively shifting the debate to the total lifecycle cost of software and the practical reality of vendor lock-in. While Side B raised plausible concerns about upfront preparation costs and cybersecurity, Side A successfully neutralized the security argument by pointing out that closed source relies on security through obscurity and that open licensing allows agencies to hire competing defenders rather than being locked into a single incumbent. Side A's pressure questions exposed Side B's reliance on a "volunteer" strawman, and Side A's closing cleanly crystallized the core tradeoff between upfront costs and long-term public ownership.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing to the security and cost concessions, clarifying that open source doesn't require an "ecosystem" of new auditors but simply removes the legal barrier to hiring alternative maintainers, effectively dismantling Side B's "frictionless roadmap" and "unfunded mandate" claims.
