### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully grounding its case in the prompt's explicit requirement for "privacy-preserving" checks, neutralizing Side A's attacks on current non-private implementations. Side B also decisively won the weighing on circumvention by pointing out that a lack of parental controls does not automatically equal a child's ability to seamlessly use VPNs or offshore sites.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing argument that Side A's case relies on a false equivalence between a child with a negligent caregiver and a child guaranteed to successfully circumvent all provider-side checks.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): A won by holding the proposition as written: privacy-preserving eligibility checks can be justified, adult intrusion is not a veto, and household tools leave a real coverage gap. B’s best pressure—ideal crypto vs actual ID/face/card mandates, plus displacement—forced useful concessions, but partly swapped the motion for “judge today’s non-privacy-preserving schemes.” A answered pressure cleanly (ideal credential still resisted only if privacy fails; displacement can’t erase prevented casual access) and kept the cleaner decisive comparison: provider-side boundary vs protection that depends on attentive caregivers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 / closing on coverage: a home that never enables controls is not automatically a minor who will defeat every provider check, so B’s alternative leaves the boundary absent while A at least interrupts easy entry.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO wins the decisive definitional clash. The proposition explicitly requires privacy-preserving checks, so CON's attack on ID-upload/facial-scan mandates is off-motion. PRO gives a plausible unlinkable, general-purpose eligibility credential (issuer doesn't learn destination, site doesn't learn identity) and conditions compulsion on audit/readiness, directly neutralizing the blackmail/metadata claim. On displacement, PRO weighs both casual-use deterrence vs determined evasion; CON only counts evaders. On alternatives, PRO exposes the coverage gap: household controls protect only when enabled, while provider checks protect precisely when they are not. CON never shows comparable coverage and concedes PRO is not defending deployed systems.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 (A4.5-A4.6): reusable general-purpose credential need not be adult-content-specific or contact issuer per visit, defusing B's centralized-token metadata attack, and showing missing parental controls does not equal guaranteed circumvention – reframing coverage gap as PRO advantage.
