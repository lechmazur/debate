### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by keeping the decisive clash on scale and knowledge: small instrumented tests with no detectable climate signal cannot produce the monsoon/lock-in harms PRO invokes, while a blank pause until a binding treaty is a de facto ban that weakens detection baselines. PRO’s security and forum-shopping points landed but never overcame CON’s workable alternative of stepwise licensing, registries, and existing stop points; CON also answered pressure more cleanly and crystallized the choice as observable limits versus politicized ignorance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2 answers on forum-shopping and scale-up: mutually reinforcing constraints plus stepwise permits beat PRO’s claim that only a finished treaty can police rogues.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by establishing a clean, workable distinction: experiments too small to generate a detectable climate signal cannot plausibly disrupt monsoons or justify a global ban, while experiments large enough to matter are already stoppable by national licensing, environmental review, and transboundary-harm duties. PRO’s demand for a binding international framework before any outdoor research proved self-defeating—CON showed that frameworks like the ozone regime succeeded only after states had measurable baselines, which a moratorium would prevent. PRO’s rogue-actor and forum-shopping concerns were blunted when CON demonstrated that a moratorium does not improve detection of secret tests or bind non-parties, whereas public registries, stepwise authorization, and independent monitoring create the transparency that actually enables accountability. PRO’s geopolitical “suspicion” argument remained speculative and never overcame CON’s physical threshold argument or the practical sequencing problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s dismantling of PRO’s “binding framework first” argument by showing that enforceable treaties require the baseline knowledge produced by research, and that a moratorium removes the very transparency tools (public registries, monitoring, published results) needed to distinguish legitimate research from rogue deployment.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (A) built the more resilient path: if a field test has no detectable climate signal it cannot plausibly cause the monsoon/famine harms PRO fears, and if it is large enough to matter existing licensing, airspace control and transboundary-harm duties provide stop points. - **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): B (PRO) won by keeping the decisive clash on authorization and consent over a shared atmosphere, not microphysics.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R1/R2 turn that kg-scale physical safety still leaves decisive political/legal harm (normalizing field testing and shifting from consent to protest)
