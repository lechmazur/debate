### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): PRO built and sustained the cleaner path to victory. Its core principle—that isolation and burnout are social and structural problems requiring social and structural solutions, not individualized coping tools—was established early, maintained across rounds, and never squarely neutralized by CON. PRO's "lonely transaction vs. actual belonging" framing and the observation that apps privatize stress while leaving the always-on engine untouched were rhetorically effective and logically sound. CON raised legitimate operational concerns (workload compression, opt-out by isolated employees, financial inaccessibility of time off for hourly workers) that PRO only partially answered. PRO's response to workload compression—that the spend is a redirection of existing wellness app budgets and that culture change itself reduces ambient pressure—was adequate. PRO's response to the opt-out concern (flexible, low-pressure formats vs. mandatory offsites) was partial but functional. CON's strongest underaddressed blow was that truly isolated workers systematically avoid voluntary gatherings, and PRO's flexible-format reframe didn't fully close this gap. However, CON's own alternative (apps creating real social bonds to address isolation) rests on a weaker causal chain: the motion targets isolation specifically, and peer message boards and guided audio resets cannot plausibly substitute for the relational bonds PRO describes as the actual cure. PRO's closing crystallized the decisive clash cleanly; CON's shorter closing was largely repetitive. Two clipped PRO speeches are an execution cost but the displayed portions still carried the argument. CON's grounding was equally careful but the comparative weighing never overcame PRO's stronger causal story on the core motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's pressure question about opt-in gatherings systematically missing the most isolated employees, followed by PRO's Rebuttal 2 response distinguishing flexible hybrid formats from mandatory offsites, then reinforced by PRO turning the equity argument back on CON (engagement data collapsing among hourly staff, screen-fatigued workers, and caregivers). CON maintained the pressure in Rebuttal 2 with the financial impossibility point for hourly workers, which PRO never fully closed—but the motion's framing ("employers should spend more") implies employer-borne cost, partially defusing it.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): A won narrowly by making the cleaner causal distinction: burnout and isolation are structural and relational problems, while apps mostly manage symptoms privately. B had strong practical attacks about workload compression, opt-out gatherings, and equitable access, but its app alternative leaned on idealized claims about engagement, peer circles, and data compelling leadership without fully answering why apps would reduce the always-on culture. A's clipping and somewhat thin answers on implementation kept the margin close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was against B's data and continuous-support framing: dashboards and coping tools may identify or manage stress, but they do not themselves lighten workloads, restore boundaries, or create the ordinary human bonds that reduce isolation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing two unresolved structural problems in PRO's case: predictable time off compresses workloads without added capacity, and in-person gatherings systematically exclude the most isolated workers—caregivers, night-shift staff, and the socially anxious—who opt out or cannot attend. CON's rebuttals turned PRO's own equity examples against it, showing apps scale across shifts and geographies while gatherings privilege the already-connected. PRO's clipped turns and reliance on aspirational "culture change" framing failed to neutralize these operational realities, leaving CON's continuous-access, data-informed alternative as the cleaner path to sustained relief.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Side B's pressure questions in Rebuttal 2, arguing that a warehouse worker's 15-minute break is exactly when an audio wellness tool fits, while predictable time off is often financially impossible for hourly staff and in-person events are logistically out of reach—flipping PRO's equity attack into a decisive advantage for apps.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built and sustained the debate's decisive frame: the motion asks about *reducing* burnout and isolation, not managing them, and apps—however sophisticated—equip workers to tolerate broken conditions rather than remove them. "Teaching cognitive reframing between meetings does not reduce burnout; it normalizes it" was the pivot point of the debate, and Side B never adequately answered it. Side B's strongest material—the backlog anxiety objection, the 364-other-days challenge for remote workers, and the exclusion critique of in-person events—was real and put genuine pressure on Side A. But Side A responded directly: funding predictable time off means funding coverage protocols and workflow pauses, not just handing out calendar blocks; episodic gatherings create anchoring trust that makes continuous digital work feel collaborative, not isolating. Side B's closing then conceded the frame by calling daily workplace pressure "the weather"—an analogy that actually strengthens Side A's roof-repair metaphor. Critically, Side B had two speeches clipped for exceeding the word limit, which is a real execution cost: arguments ended mid-sentence in both Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2, leaving the judge unable to weigh the full response to live pressure. Side A maintained clean A1:/A2: labeling and stayed within format throughout. The combination of a consistently stronger structural argument and Side B's repeated format failures produces a solid rather than close win for Side A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Q1 in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive exchange. Side B's pressure question challenged whether predictable time off could survive without enforceable coverage. Side A answered directly: the whole point of *spending* on predictable time off is buying the handoff protocols and temporary coverage that prevent pile-up, turning the objection into a further argument for structural investment over app subscriptions. The follow-up line—"Tolerance is not reduction; it's deferred collapse"—crystallized why CON's model fails the motion's own goal.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by making the core comparison clearer: predictable protected rest and in-person trust attack the structural causes of burnout and isolation, while apps mostly help employees cope with conditions left unchanged. B had good pressure on backlog, inclusion, and daily reach, but often proved apps are useful complements rather than better spending priorities. A answered backlog with funded coverage and workflow planning, and framed app-based support as symptom management. B also incurred execution costs from clipped rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer that backlog anxiety is a reason to fund predictable coverage and workflow pauses, not resilience apps, was the decisive exchange because it turned B's strongest attack into evidence for structural spending.
