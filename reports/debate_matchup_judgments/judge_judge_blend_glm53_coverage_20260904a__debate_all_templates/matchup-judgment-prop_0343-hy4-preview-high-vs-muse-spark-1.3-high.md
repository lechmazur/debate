### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully turning Side A's pragmatic arguments against them. When Side A proposed take-home justifications to verify AI-assisted drafts, Side B correctly pointed out that the AI could just draft the justification too. Furthermore, Side B neatly neutralized Side A's attack on the scalability of in-class writing by pointing out that Side A's own "in-class validation" relies on the exact same staffing bandwidth.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Turn 8, where Side B points out that "staffing is symmetrical"—if Side A can run randomized in-class validation at scale, Side B can use those same resources for actual unassisted composition, bypassing the need for A's take-home justifications (which B notes can also be AI-generated).
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive clash on what a degree certifies: construction of reasoning versus later defense of possibly machine-framed architecture. PRO’s disclosure-plus-ownership package and anti-ban enforcement story were strong, but CON repeatedly showed that “unpacking,” two-sentence justifications, and even oral snippets can be tool-generated too, so accountability becomes post-hoc performance. CON also answered scale pressure with existing sections/exams and drew a clearer grammar-vs-generative process line than PRO could police. PRO’s “grade visible justification, not origin myths” frame was memorable, yet never fully neutralized the ritual-accountability charge on most coursework.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5–A4.6 turning B4.2’s “successful defense = ownership”: in large courses the same tool can draft the unpacking, so PRO grades performed understanding rather than built argument (“map vs walk the ground”).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully reframing authorship as "responsibility and defense" rather than "solitary genesis," and by decisively winning the scalability and enforcement clashes. Side A exposed that Side B's process-heavy alternative (in-class writing, oral defenses) either fails to scale to large lectures without gutting assignment depth or relies on an unenforceable distinction between "grammar tools" and "framing." Side A's rebuttals were sharper, particularly the flip that Side B demands an impossible "origin story" of the thesis while Side A demands auditable, visible justification anchored to specific course materials.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it flips Side B's "triage is not authorship" argument by contrasting Side B's impossible demand to prove who first thought of the thesis with Side A's auditable standard of present understanding, while simultaneously dismantling Side B's "grammar vs. framing" compromise as having no enforceable edge.
