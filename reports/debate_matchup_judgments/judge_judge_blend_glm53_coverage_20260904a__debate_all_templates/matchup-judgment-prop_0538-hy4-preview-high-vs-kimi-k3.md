- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won narrowly by preserving the motion’s more natural distinction: countries can use current commodity earnings during a transition and still prioritize regional, value-added production over continued raw-export dependence. Side A successfully framed this as "sequencing" rather than prioritizing manufacturing *over* exports. Side A also delivered a fatal blow by pointing out the circularity of Side B's plan to fund manufacturing by "defunding the import bill."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4.1, which exposed the temporal circularity of Side B's plan to fund new refineries with savings from fuel imports that cannot be stopped until the refineries exist.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the motion’s operative rankings the decisive clash and forcing PRO to abandon them. PRO’s value-capture story (cocoa/chocolate, crude/petrol, jobs per ton) was vivid and economically plausible, but under pressure PRO conceded that raw exports keep flowing and that regional buyers need not be ranked over rich markets—reducing the case to “process more,” which CON already accepted under sequencing. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by clarifying that prioritization does not require abruptly banning raw exports, then showing that Side A's own policy of directing commodity rents toward power, skills, and industry substantially concedes the proposition. B also answered the foreign-exchange challenge with existing African import demand and turned A's tariff-escalation point against continued dependence. A kept a legitimate concern about hard currency and sequencing alive, but repeatedly treated deprioritization as cessation and never answered why existing regional demand could not be progressively captured. B's clipped opening and some overbroad historical claims narrowed the margin, while A's nonexistent closing reference IDs added an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both questions, argued that raw exports can continue during an industrial policy shift, and used A's tariff-wall concession to show why dependence on rich buyers can obstruct upgrading. A's response largely repeated the claim that prioritization would shrink exports rather than proving that necessary link.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by maintaining a relentless, economically grounded focus on the foreign exchange constraint. Side A successfully proved that deprioritizing raw exports cuts the very funding needed to buy capital goods for factories, and brilliantly dismantled Side B's alternative by showing that Africa's import bill is itself paid for with raw-export dollars.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.4) identifying the circularity in B's plan to capture the import bill, noting that the bill is funded by the exact raw export dollars B seeks to deprioritize.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): A (CON) won the decisive live clash on foreign-exchange sequencing: factories need imported capital goods bought with hard currency that still comes overwhelmingly from raw sales to rich markets. B never fully broke the circularity—Africa’s “own import bill” is paid with those same dollars, so deprioritizing the source shrinks the bill rather than repatriating it—while tariff walls and fragmented low-income neighbors cannot substitute at scale. B’s “prioritize bans nothing” softened the motion and the late attempt to collapse A’s “tax the rung / build the ladder” into the proposition was correctly rejected; reinvestment while still prioritizing raw-export earnings is not the same as prioritizing manufacturing and intra-African trade over continued dependence. B landed sharper early turns (value capture, volatility, extraction geography, Korea/Botswana folds) and better rhetorical framing, but A’s engine-vs-destination comparison remained unanswered enough to decide the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4/A5.4 circularity hit: the import bill B treats as the replacement market is itself funded by the raw-export dollars the motion would deprioritize; shrink those sales and the funding mechanism vanishes rather than relocates.
