### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by isolating the ban’s distinctive feature: shutdown at a contested threshold rather than shutdown as the final backstop of an escalating compliance regime. A directly answered both pressure questions, showed that political weakening applies to bans as well as fees, and explained why regulators may enforce a rising fee more credibly than a supply-disrupting prohibition. Side B established methane’s urgency and the value of a firm legal prohibition, but never demonstrated that immediate threshold-based shutdown secures reductions unavailable through A’s ladder. Its closing claim that the proposition itself accepts the supply cost was circular and left the central tradeoff unjustified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s last-resort-versus-default-trigger distinction was decisive. It answered B’s demand for a hard compliance endpoint while preserving A’s argument that fees, deadlines, permit revocation, and eventual shutdown can reduce emissions without treating supply loss as the policy’s primary mechanism.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively collapsing Side B's unique mechanisms. Side A successfully proves that Side B's defense of a ban relies on the exact flexible tools Side A proposed (penalties, deadlines, and fees to fund rebates), leaving the ban with only the unique downside of a hard upfront supply sacrifice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's first rebuttal effectively ends the mechanical debate by pointing out that B retreated to "presumptive standards, repair deadlines, escalating penalties," which was exactly A's proposed alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that the ban either collapses into CON's escalating-fee-and-deadline regime or inherits the same measurement and enforcement problems, leaving its only distinctive feature a designed supply sacrifice that purchases no additional methane reduction. PRO repeatedly borrowed CON's instruments—fees on remaining production, trade policy, repair deadlines—to answer hard questions about funding and substitution, while never resolving the tension between a true prohibition and the penalty-based enforcement PRO admitted was necessary. PRO's closing resorted to circular reasoning ("the motion explicitly accepts that cost"), and CON's political-economy argument—that thresholds get loosened under the same pressure that caps fees, while an imperfect fee still prices every ton—went largely unanswered. After the full exchange, the live route to victory was CON's: the shared toolkit achieves the cuts, and the ban's unique cost is unjustified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 caught that PRO's "presumptive standards, repair deadlines, escalating penalties" was structurally identical to CON's flexible fee regime, posing a lethal dilemma: either the ban collapses into that regime, or it relies on contested thresholds and immediate shutdowns that inherit every measurement problem PRO admitted. This exchange defined the rest of the debate.
