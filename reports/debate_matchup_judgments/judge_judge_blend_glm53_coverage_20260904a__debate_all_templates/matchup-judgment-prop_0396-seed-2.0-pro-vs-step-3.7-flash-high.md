- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins on two arguments PRO never fully neutralized. First, the underlying financial liability from medical debt is real before any credit report records it: a borrower carrying an unpaid $20,000 hospital bill already has reduced cash flow and genuinely elevated default risk, regardless of how the bill arose. PRO's causation claim—that the credit penalty alone manufactures the default correlation—is asserted but never demonstrated; CON's "the debt is real" framing is more economically intuitive and survived all rebuttals. Second, CON's Rebuttal 2 introduced a strong asymmetry: lenders who see medical debt can already discount or ignore it for borrowers with strong track records, but hiding it entirely forces every lender to price all applicants as potential unknown risks, raising universal baseline rates. PRO's response—that removing the broken signal lowers, not raises, costs—required accepting the unproven premise that medical debt is a purely manufactured signal rather than a genuine cash constraint, which CON contested throughout. CON also successfully identified PRO's repeated reliance on billing-error examples to justify a blanket ban on all medical debt reporting, including undisputed valid debt—a logical gap PRO acknowledged but never fully closed. PRO's voluntary-versus-involuntary distinction and causation argument have real force and kept the debate competitive, but neither was sufficient to displace CON's grounded claim that financial capacity matters more than intent in credit risk assessment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [A4.3–A4.4]: "Lenders can already discount it, weight it lightly, or ignore it entirely for borrowers with strong track records.
