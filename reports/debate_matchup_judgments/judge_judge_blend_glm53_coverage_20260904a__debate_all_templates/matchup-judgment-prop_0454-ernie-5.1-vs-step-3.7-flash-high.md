### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A wins on the central clash—political permanence—because CON's core rebuttal mischaracterized PRO's own design. PRO specified in A4.1 that the reauthorization vote fires during LOW unemployment (economic booms), when pressure to maintain the program is minimal. CON's repeated "paper tiger" riposte argued the clause fails because "when unemployment is HIGH no legislature will vote to cut aid"—directly misreading the mechanism. That execution error left CON's most important argument hollow. PRO also landed the sharper pressure questions: Q2 caught a genuine tension in CON's inflation story (transfers activate during demand slumps, not recoveries), and CON's A2 lagging-indicator counter, while valid, never fully displaced PRO's phase-out mechanism. The concrete 2009 legislative-lag example (four months, 1.3 million workers into long-term unemployment) remained live throughout and was never neutralized; CON asserted targeted aid has higher multipliers but provided no comparably grounded causal story. CON's opening conflation of ad-hoc 2020 stimulus with the proposed rule-based stabilizer was identified early by PRO and never credibly distinguished away, weakening CON's debt-spiral narrative. CON's shorter closing repeated the paper-tiger claim without engaging PRO's actual reauthorization design, ceding the final framing. PRO's margin is clear but competitive because CON's lagging-indicator and fiscal-dominance points are real concerns that PRO only partially answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 (A4.1), which introduced a reauthorization trigger keyed explicitly to LOW unemployment periods, neutralizing CON's political-permanence argument in principle. CON's response in B4.2 ignored this design element entirely and restated the wrong version of the claim ("when unemployment is high no legislature will vote to cut aid"), leaving PRO's safeguard standing and CON's central line of attack materially damaged for the rest of the debate.
