### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) built a cleaner, more internally consistent case across all rounds and exploited the decisive contradiction in CON's position. The pivotal exchange is the "neutral baseline" trap: CON argued that teaching achievements acts as a neutral foundation while mandating atrocity coverage constitutes ideological coercion, but this requires special pleading that CON never resolved. PRO's Q1 landed this asymmetry sharply, and CON's A1 in Rebuttal 2 essentially restated the contradiction rather than escaping it ("a neutral baseline for collective identity" vs. "the state codifying contested historical judgments"). PRO also correctly identified from round 1 that CON was arguing against a strawman—the resolution says "not just achievements," not "instead of achievements"—and CON's finite-instructional-time counter, while reasonable, never squared with the resolution's actual text. PRO's "you cannot teach the cure while censoring the disease" framing was the most persuasive weighing argument: CON repeatedly claimed the mandate crowds out foundational civics but could never coherently explain how students learn the purpose of civil rights legislation or constitutional reform without learning the abuses those mechanisms were designed to correct. CON had legitimate concerns about state power and definitional disputes over what counts as an atrocity, and PRO's answer to Q1 (same process as defining achievements) was somewhat dismissive—but CON never developed this into a decisive blow because CON never showed that defining "achievements" poses the same ideological risk. CON's opening was also clipped, imposing an execution cost. PRO answered its pressure questions with more substance (concrete A1/A2 responses), maintained sharper rhetorical focus, and progressed the arguments further across rounds rather than repeating them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question Q1 and the subsequent Rebuttal 2 exchange on the "neutral baseline" claim. PRO asked why a state curriculum that includes atrocities is censorship while one that omits them is considered neutral. CON's A1 in Rebuttal 2 tried to distinguish the two by calling the achievements-focused curriculum a "neutral baseline for collective identity"—but this restated the asymmetry rather than defending it, and PRO had already crystallized in its closing that a curriculum celebrating majority achievements while erasing state-sponsored abuse of marginalized communities is itself propaganda, not neutrality. This exchange was the most decisive because it cut to the logical core of CON's case and was never repaired.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO wins on the decisive live clash: CON's only concrete alternative is the decentralized status quo, and PRO established early—and CON never refuted—that those very decentralized boards are already banning consensus-level history. CON's core contradiction argument (partisan governments cannot be trusted with a new mandate) cuts both ways, and PRO exploited this skillfully: the current system is already partisan and lacks a legal floor, so the mandate creates enforceable accountability rather than adding new vulnerability. PRO's anchoring of the mandate to formally recognized content—congressional apologies for internment, federal acknowledgments of slavery—provided a plausible mechanism CON never displaced. CON's closing attempted to flip this by saying "if it's already recognized but still banned, a mandate won't help," but that actually weakens CON's own position: recognized atrocities with legal mandate create more political and legal leverage than recognized atrocities with none. On cynicism, CON never substantiated the claim that honest history produces civic withdrawal, while PRO's counter-framing—that discovering lies erodes trust more than learning hard truths—was more intuitively grounded and went unanswered. The German continuity point was CON's best stand-alone argument, but PRO narrowed it adequately by separating documentation quality from regime continuity. Both sides answered pressure questions with the required A1:/A2: structure. Both had clipped final speeches, so no asymmetric penalty applies. PRO's case remained coherent and cumulative; CON's case remained largely circular, defending a system it had already conceded was failing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's pressure question on cynicism [A4.2–A4.3]: PRO turned CON's own concession (governments already ban consensus history) into a refutation of CON's alternative, noting that culture wars are raging precisely because there is no mandated baseline, and pressed that CON never offered a concrete mechanism to stop the partisan erasure it admitted was already occurring. CON never recovered from this comparative framing in the remaining rounds.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO won by showing the status quo of sanitized curricula causes concrete democratic harm and marginalization, while CON’s institutional objections remained speculative and lacked a credible alternative to ongoing partisan erasure. CON’s strongest line—the politicization contradiction—landed hard, but PRO plausibly answered it by anchoring mandated content to formally recognized, consensus-level abuses, whereas CON’s defense of decentralized “organic” integration amounted to the same failing status quo. PRO also turned CON’s cynicism claim more effectively, arguing that honest history fosters civic accountability while sanitized narratives breed distrust when students later discover the omissions. CON’s fears of cohesion collapse were largely armchair theory unsupported by concrete examples, whereas PRO’s causal story about democratic accountability had stronger grounding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s response in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 that sanitized history—not honest accounting of abuses—is what actually breeds cynicism and civic withdrawal, neutering CON’s central psychological objection and turning it into a live reason to vote PRO.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully exposing a structural contradiction in Side B's mechanism: if state governments are currently too partisan to teach honest history, a top-down government mandate will not bypass those same political actors. Side A consistently pressured this implementation flaw, arguing that mandates become statutory weapons for culture wars rather than objective baselines. While Side B (PRO) effectively pressed Side A on the inadequacy of the status quo, Side A's defense of decentralized control and sharper weighing on political reality and civic cohesion provided a cleaner, more logically coherent path to victory. Side A's closing crystallized this decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing dismantled Side B's solvency by highlighting that politicians, not historians, write and execute mandates. This directly answered Side B's pressure questions while turning Side B's own premise (that current boards are politicized) against their proposed solution, creating an unresolved burden that decided the debate.
