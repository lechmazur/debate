- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by establishing a cleaner risk calculus and a more persuasive comparative framework. The decisive moment came in PRO's closing, where they pointed out that if a sensor network degrades or fails offline, agencies simply revert to CON's preferred baseline of manual inspection; but if the sensors function, they catch internal fatigue that visual checks physically cannot. This asymmetric risk framing defanged CON's worst-case scenarios. PRO also handled pressure questions more directly, using the answers to reinforce the core safety weighing and climate-adaptation necessity. CON's case was technically detailed and rhetorically sharp, but relied heavily on speculative bureaucratic zero-sum budgeting and worst-case degradation narratives that PRO adequately contextualized. PRO's consistent focus on life-safety, the fundamental blind spots of intermittent inspections, and the manageable nature of tech maintenance provided the clearer, more resilient path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing sequence, where they directly answered CON's pressure questions on scope and expertise atrophy, then crystallized the debate with the fail-back-to-baseline risk calculus that effectively neutralized CON's degradation and cybersecurity warnings while sharpening the comparative safety advantage.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): CON won by exposing a fatal contradiction in PRO's case: PRO's own flagship example, the I-35W bridge collapse, occurred 40 years after construction, yet PRO conceded embedded sensors die within 15 years. CON also effectively weighed the financial toxicity of an “all” mandate, showing how permanent IT/cyber costs for temporary, low-yield telemetry would cannibalize funds for legacy repairs. PRO’s pivot to “early-defect detection” and “baseline data” was coherent but failed to outweigh the massive misallocation of resources CON outlined, and PRO’s capital-vs-maintenance budget distinction felt like an accounting technicality rather than a substantive answer to finite public funds. CON’s rebuttals were sharper, directly answered pressure, and maintained a clearer, more realistic path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Turn 8 (A4.1) turning PRO’s I-35W example against them by noting the bridge collapsed at 40 years—decades after PRO’s conceded 15-year sensor lifespan would have expired. This move crystallized the lifespan mismatch clash and severely undermined PRO’s core safety justification.
