  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: the off-board collection and targeted-discount alternatives directly separated A’s desired benefits from fare abolition, while forcing A to defend why those gains warranted the much larger funding change.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A (CON) wins a close debate by making the decisive comparative case: permanent abolition of a major user-revenue stream is a structural fiscal and political shift whose claimed dividends—speed, equity, stability—are either modest, achievable without killing fares, or not clearly superior to less radical tools. B (PRO) was rhetorically stronger and better owned the motion’s “modest ridership” concession from the start (tollbooth framing, all-door boarding, progressive lockboxes), and answered pressure cleanly. But A’s contactless/off-board alternative undercuts the operational justification, targeted discounts undercut pure equity claims while preserving dedicated revenue, and the recurring scale/net-replacement pressure left B defending aspiration (sculpted broad taxes + lockboxes) against a concrete permanent gap. Clipped A turns were a real execution cost, yet the live clash still resolves against the proposition’s cost–benefit balance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second-rebuttal pile: speed is already obtainable with contactless/all-door while keeping fares; if equity needs heavy progressive carving of the “broad” base, targeted discounts deliver it without dismantling user funding—collapsing B’s three remaining pillars against the permanent replacement cost.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON wins on weighing: even granting the motion's modest ridership concession, it keeps a live fiscal hole (hundreds of millions to ~$1B annually even after 10-15% collection savings) and shows PRO's main non-ridership gains — all-door speed and equity — are achievable via contactless/off-board boarding and targeted discounts without permanently abolishing a dedicated, user-based revenue stream. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won by exposing the unsupported link between modest ridership growth and savings sufficient to replace major-city fare revenue. A never established that new riders predominantly displaced car trips, generalized too readily from smaller systems, and did not resolve the tradeoff with service investment. B also answered the pressure questions directly and offered a clearer alternative of targeted discounts plus better service. A's repeated clipped turns added an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it converted A's demand for a threshold into a clear test based on reduced vehicle-miles and audited savings, then showed that A had neither identified the share of riders diverted from cars nor demonstrated savings exceeding lost fares and added operating costs.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive comparison stick: certain, permanent broad-based costs and lost fare revenue versus unproven modest, diffuse benefits. CON owned PRO’s “modest gains” concession, forced the car-diversion/VMT test, and kept pressure on incidence—any levy broad enough to replace major-city fares hits non-riders and renters, so LVT/progressive labels do not escape the motion’s broad-based framing. PRO’s public-good and collection-net points were live but never quantified past assertion; repeated externalities language and several clipped turns left the benefit side thinner than CON’s certain-cost frame. PRO’s alternative (targeted discounts cost admin too) was fair, but did not neutralize lock-in or the shallow-slope problem. Overall path to victory favored CON after pressure answers and closings crystallized risk assignment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 (A4.1–A4.3): LVT is either not broad or capitalizes onto non-riders, plus the concrete car-diversion threshold PRO could not meet after admitting no per-rider figure.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won the core trade-off clash. PRO needed to justify certain, broad-based taxation for admittedly modest gains via cross-budget savings and greater equity. A showed broad-based means non-riders pay and rent capitalization undercuts the progressive land-value claim, that Kansas City does not scale to major-city fare bases, and that modest gains x diffuse per-trip savings = still modest. A answered both pressure questions with a VMT-based threshold; B conceded no per-rider figure [B4.3] and never answered what share of new trips comes from cars [A3.2], leaving its benefit case unquantified. Lock-in plus targeted discounts/services remained a live, cheaper alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3/A4.1: A set a car-diversion + audited-savings-exceeds-lost-fares test, exposed B's lack of quantification, and turned B's progressive broad-based claim into rent-incidence harm to non-riders that B never rebutted.
