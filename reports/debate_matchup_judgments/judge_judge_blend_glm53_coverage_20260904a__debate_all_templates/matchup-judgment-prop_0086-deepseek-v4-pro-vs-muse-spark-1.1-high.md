### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a tighter case around the motion's own stipulated condition—"even when staffing is tight"—arguing that under real short-staffing the counterfactual to AI companionship is not human presence but empty hours of isolation, so AI fills a void rather than displacing human care. This reframing survived scrutiny well: when PRO's pressure question forced CON to reconcile "filling voids" with "replacing most hours," CON answered directly (A1/A2 format followed cleanly) that both are true and not contradictory, and repeatedly pressed PRO on how its "volunteers and family" solution actually functions at 3 a.m. in flu season—the precise scenario the motion stipulates. PRO's central rebuttal (that AI use removes pressure to fix staffing, and that authentic valuing requires a valuing subject) was philosophically interesting but never squarely resolved this practical gap, and PRO's own answer to CON's Q1 ("don't leave hours empty... arrange volunteers") implicitly relies on resources the tight-staffing premise says aren't there. PRO also suffered a real execution cost: four of its five speeches were cut off mid-argument, including the closing itself, which ends mid-sentence ("PRO wins [clipped]"), undercutting its ability to crystallize the case at the point it mattered most. CON's speeches were complete, sharper, and more consistently tied to the operative comparison ("presence that arrives beats authenticity that never does"), giving it the cleaner, more decidable path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3–A4.5) turning PRO's own Pressure Question answer against it—showing that PRO's "use volunteers/family" solution collapses precisely in the tight-staffing, night-shift, flu-season scenario the motion stipulates, and that PRO's admission it would never correct a joyful elder while still building policy to deny her that joy is paternalism rather than dignity.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): A won the practical comparison. It repeatedly showed that under actual tight staffing, many supposedly replaced companionship hours are presently empty, while B relied on volunteers, family, and future reform without establishing that these substitutes would be available when needed. A also turned B’s concession that it would not correct a joyful elder into a strong welfare-and-autonomy argument. A risked conflating supplementing absent companionship with replacing existing human companionship, but B did not exploit that wording cleanly enough. B’s repeated clipping also weakened execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 was decisive: A directly answered both questions, reconciled filling empty hours with using AI for most routine companionship, and exposed the weakness of B’s proposed volunteer-and-family alternative under the staffing conditions specified by the motion.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): B won by cleaner comparative force under the motion's "even when staffing is tight" clause: empty hours are the real baseline, AI fills them without stealing scarce human touch, and lived clinical benefits beat ontological authenticity that often never arrives. A pressed the dignity and moral necessity case well but kept relying on volunteers/redesign that fail exactly in the hard case B kept forcing. Multiple A clips added execution cost; B's path stayed clearer and more decisive after exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 on empty hours vs volunteers (and A4.2 paternalism flip): exposes that A denies lived joy for a theory that fails precisely when staffing is tight.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B built a clearer, more internally consistent case: it distinguished "filling empty hours" (permitted) from "deleting the scarce human hours that remain" (banned), and it landed a hard-to-answer accountability point — an AI that misses a wince or untouched tray leaves a log saying "companionship delivered" with no one accountable, "laundering neglect into a product." Side A's most interesting move, the "lethal contradiction" (any AI effective enough to matter becomes "most" contact and is thus banned), is clever but rests on a strained reading of "replace" as raw time-share rather than substitution, and B's closing directly rebutted this by reasserting that filling silence isn't deletion of human hours. Side A never cleanly answered the "companionship delivered" accountability charge, instead offering a pulse-oximeter analogy that doesn't resolve who is responsible for a missed cue when no human is even present to receive an alert. Compounding this, four of Side A's five speeches were clipped for exceeding the word limit, including the opening, both rebuttals, and the closing — a real execution cost that repeatedly cut off arguments mid-thought and blunted rhetorical impact. Side B faced no such issue, executed the Pressure Questions/Answers format cleanly, and closed by crystallizing the clash rather than trailing off. On balance, B provided the more decisive, better-supported, and more completely delivered case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.2's "the log just shows 'companionship delivered' — no one has failed" answer to Q2 on accountability was the most damaging unanswered exchange; A's pulse-oximeter analogy in A4.4 did not resolve who is accountable when no human is present to act on an AI's alert.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing Side A's central equivocation: adding AI during otherwise empty hours is supplementation, whereas the motion opposes displacing most existing human companionship. Side A repeatedly treated AI becoming the majority of total interaction as necessarily replacing human contact, so its empty-room scenario never answered B's policy of preserving scarce human moments while using AI around them. B also won the accountability comparison and executed every round cleanly, while Side A was repeatedly clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both questions, distinguished supplementing empty hours from deleting remaining human contact, and showed why clinical alerts cannot replace an accountable human advocate. Side A's reply continued relying on the same disputed interpretation of “most” and did not resolve who is accountable when the AI itself misses a cue.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built the cleaner path by treating the motion as written (protect remaining human core, allow AI fill of silence) and repeatedly dismantling B's empty-room rewrite as a sleight that treats scarce human minutes as non-companionship then equates any effective AI with banned "most." B landed real practical pressure and the "supplement becomes majority" trap, but repeated clipping left incomplete turns, and its answers never neutralized the advocacy/accountability distinction or the laundering-neglect risk. A stayed progressive across rounds and made the vote easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 plus [A4.3]: remaining human contact is still majority of human time and must stay protected; empty-room cases are already allowed as supplements, so CON deletes the human core.
