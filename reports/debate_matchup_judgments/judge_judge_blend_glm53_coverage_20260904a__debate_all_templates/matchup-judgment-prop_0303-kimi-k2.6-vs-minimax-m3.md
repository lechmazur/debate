### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): PRO (Side B) ran the stronger debate on three decisive grounds. First, PRO correctly framed the motion from the outset — "overstated," not "fake" — and exploited CON's straw man throughout. CON's opening attacked a position PRO never took, and this cost CON credibility in every subsequent exchange. Second, the "front door vs. house" distinction was the sharpest organizational move in the debate. PRO successfully separated the front gate (need-blind admissions, rising first-gen numbers, which CON defended well) from downstream pathway mechanisms — legacy preferences, donor lists, unpaid internships, alumni referral chains — that CON largely conceded as real while dismissing them as "shrinking." CON never showed these mechanisms were truly shrinking or outweighed by technical filters; that was asserted, not established. Third, PRO's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive single move: the structural change test. If successful people genuinely recognized their advantages, they would end legacy admissions at their own alma maters, abolish unpaid pipelines, and stop hiring through class-stratified networks. None has happened at scale. A formulaic commencement confession while the board selects successors through the same funnel is not recognition; it is the status shield with a mic. CON's response — that "revolution" should not be the test — was partially fair but never supplied an alternative concrete test, leaving PRO's distinction between confession and action standing. CON's best live argument — that first-gen students thrive inside elite institutions, proving the pathway is meritocratic where it matters — had genuine force, but PRO correctly noted that a slightly wider front door does not change whether the inner rooms remain class-sorted. CON never squarely answered where in the pipeline class stops operating, making the A1 non-answer an execution cost. PRO wins a clear but competitive debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3): the structural change test. PRO conceded that verbal acknowledgment of privilege exists but showed that genuine recognition would manifest as structural action — ending legacy admissions, refusing unpaid pipelines, dismantling class-stratified hiring. None has occurred at scale. CON's rebuttal that the test demands "revolution" was only partially effective; CON never offered a concrete alternative measure of genuine recognition, leaving PRO's distinction between the confession and the rooms intact as the most damage-doing exchange in the debate.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): A won by keeping the motion narrower than “merit is fake” and repeatedly showing why examples of strivers do not disprove class reproduction. B’s best move was distinguishing upstream inequality from the elite pathway itself, but A answered by naming downstream mechanisms inside the pathway: legacy, donor preference, unpaid internships, alumni referrals, and cultural-fit filters. B asserted that these are shrinking and outweighed by objective technical gates, but that was less grounded and did not fully answer A’s broader sequence-of-pathways argument. A’s clipped closing was an execution cost, but the live comparative case still favored PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 was decisive: after B pressured A to separate upstream disadvantage from pathway reproduction, A directly identified institutional downstream mechanisms and reframed B as defending only the front gate rather than the full pathway.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): PRO wins by successfully defining elite pathways as a multi-stage sequence rather than a single admissions gate, which neutralizes CON's attempt to isolate upstream inequality from institutional reproduction. PRO's turn on CON's ritual acknowledgment point is decisive: framing compulsory privilege-checking as a status shield that substitutes for structural change cleanly wins the more than they admit half of the motion. CON competently defended technical screens and first-gen throughput, but relied on asserted trends like shrinking legacy slots and paid pipelines crowding out unpaid ones without sufficiently answering PRO's downstream mechanisms. PRO's closing clipping is a noted execution cost, but the substantive clash was already resolved through sharper burden management, clearer weighing, and more damaging rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 directly answers CON's pressure questions by isolating downstream mechanisms (legacy, donor lists, unpaid internships, alumni referrals, cultural-fit interviews) as intrinsic to the pathway itself. PRO then sets a concrete structural test for genuine admission of privilege, cleanly turning CON's compulsory confession argument into proof of the status shield. This exchange crystallized the house versus front-door clash and secured PRO's path to victory.
