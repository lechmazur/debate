- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully neutralizing Side A's core impact (supply security). Side A drops this upstream-dependence argument, leaving Side B's weighing of "certain cost vs contingent, flawed benefit" decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they weaponize Side A's own example (gallium) to show that an input cutoff would stall tariff-protected domestic factories anyway.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal 2 on the "leaky bucket" subsidy and present-tense dumping as the certain harm.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by consistently controlling the weighing frame and exposing critical gaps in CON's security case. PRO's marginal-effect framing successfully establishes that even when paired with subsidies, tariffs impose a certain, compounding deployment drag that subsidies must inefficiently offset. More decisively, PRO's point that tariffs on finished goods do not protect against upstream input cutoffs (gallium, polysilicon, wafers) neutered CON's supply-security argument, and CON never adequately answered it. PRO's closing weighing—that an instrument whose entire defense relies on other policies compensating for it has already lost the comparison—crystallized the debate cleanly. CON offered strong industrial policy mechanics (the "leaky bucket" subsidy-drain analogy and transshipment concerns), but ultimately relied on a contingent embargo/dumping scenario while conceding the certain near-term deployment cost, making PRO's path to victory clearer and more complete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 response to CON's security framing, specifically highlighting that domestic factories themselves rely on imported upstream inputs, so tariffs on finished goods cannot insure against the very supply shocks CON fears. This directly collapsed CON's resilience case and forced CON onto the back foot for the remainder of the debate.

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by cleanly resolving Side B's core contradiction trap and maintaining a live offensive argument that Side B failed to adequately address. - **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by cleanly resolving the debate's central logical clash. When PRO pressed that price couldn't be simultaneously lethal to factories and trivial to buyers, CON's "different denominators" explanation (thin module margins vs. total installed system cost) was economically sound and directly neutralized PRO's main trap.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 resolution of the price contradiction ("same dollars, opposite significance... factory absorbs it whole, buyer absorbs a fraction") directly dismantled PRO's core logical pressure and reframed the tariff as a targeted, efficient instrument rather than a blunt deployment brake.
