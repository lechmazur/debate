- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO won a close, high-quality exchange by making the decisive “more than” comparison easier to resolve under closed-book rules. CON’s best work—separating storage from compulsion, and opportunities from injuries prevented—kept the debate honest and competitive, and PRO’s two clipped turns cost polish. But CON never showed that remaining financial/access/legal burdens rival irreversible youth harm once costs and enforcement were mitigated, and expressly declined to claim defensive lock failures dominate on magnitude. In a no-stats setting, PRO’s mechanism-plus-asymmetry path carried the proposition more cleanly than CON’s residual burden-of-proof posture.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 vs B4.4: PRO’s asymmetric causal-chain weighing (continuous youth-access reduction versus rare decisive lock-delay defense) against CON’s demand for symmetric contingency counting on both sides.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) won by making the net comparison clearer and answering the core burden question. B correctly distinguished lock efficacy from law efficacy and demanded consistent contingency accounting, but never showed the remaining burdens are weighty.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B4.4 / A4.1 clash on whether 'hours of reduced access' equals injuries prevented versus whether defensive harm requires a narrow alignment of contingencies; A answered with asymmetric probability but B's point that reduced exposure is opportunity not outcome was the debate's most decisive weighing moment.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B wins by systematically dismantling Side A's primary weighing mechanism. Side B sharply exposed the double standard in this logic: a locked box provides an *opportunity* for prevention every night, but it does not prevent a shooting every night; prevention also requires a chain of contingencies (the child would have accessed the gun and caused harm). Furthermore, Side B maintained a much tighter analytical focus on the actual proposition, consistently forcing Side A to defend the marginal impact of a *legal mandate* rather than just the abstract benefits of secure storage. Side A's clipped turns also incurred a minor execution cost, but Side B's superior clash resolution and logical precision secured the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it exposes Side A's double standard in weighing causal chains, noting that reduced exposure is an opportunity for prevention, not an injury prevented, and that a locked box does not prevent a shooting every night just because it is locked.
