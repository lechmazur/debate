### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that aggregate spending caps can provide budget certainty without patient-level outcome tracking or mid-year cutoffs. Side A’s strongest objection—that manufacturers would reject unlimited liability—depended on treating every negotiated cap as arbitrary and commercially ruinous. B plausibly answered that caps can use tiered rebates or subscription structures while preserving substantial guaranteed revenue. A’s alternative relied heavily on future competition and downstream savings without adequately proving they would make broad, lifelong coverage affordable. B did overstate that any nonzero unit price must break budgets, so the win remained competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal was decisive: it used the proposition’s “or” to bypass A’s administrative critique of outcome tracking, then clarified that a spending cap limits payer expenditure through rebates rather than automatically stopping treatment. This materially weakened A’s central rationing argument.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): B won a competitive debate by making fiscal sustainability the decisive criterion and keeping it live: lifelong use for a huge eligible population undercuts CON’s statin/Hep C/diabetes analogies, and unhedged coverage was shown as the real driver of prior-auth rationing. A landed a sharp “only when” impasse critique and forced a targeted-coverage concession, but never fully answered compounding budget exhaustion or next-month cash-flow versus distant savings. B’s cleaner comparative path made broad access without risk-sharing look less credible than conditionality as the workable gateway.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s finite-cure vs lifelong-compounding demolition of A’s Hepatitis C/competition alternative (pressure Q1 into Rebuttal 2/closing), which neutralized CON’s main substitute for caps/outcomes.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A wins by neutralizing CON's admin nightmare via the or-cap option, and by decisively distinguishing finite curative Hep C from compounding lifelong obesity volume, which CON never answers beyond generic competition claims. A also flips the rationing clash, explaining caps as rebates enabling broad formularies. CON's hostage argument is real but blunted by A's incentive account that a capped multi-billion market beats exclusion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.4 directly answering B's pressure: manufacturers will accept tiered rebates over exclusion, and the Hep C analogy fails because cured patients exit the pool while obesity treatment compounds indefinitely - collapsing B's central market-will-fix-it claim.
