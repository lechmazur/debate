### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by distinguishing system-level speed from rushing individual hearings: more judges, clerks, interpreters, and remote access can shorten queues while preserving preparation and continuances. Side B vividly identified remote-hearing risks, but repeatedly assumed that added capacity must produce packed calendars and immediate removals after technical failures without establishing that inevitability. Side A also offered the fuller comparison by weighing those risks against lost evidence, logistical nonappearance, and backlog-driven error.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the throughput argument was decisive: increasing capacity can reduce case age without reducing time or care per case. Side B's claim that speed necessarily consumes all slack never squarely defeated that distinction.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully exposing a structural contradiction in Side A's case. Side A argued that added staffing would provide 'slack' for tech checks, continuances, and deeper research. Side B effectively countered that the motion's mandate to 'speed up processing' means this capacity will be spent on throughput, not idle slack, thereby reducing time for case preparation. Side B's realistic framing of how procedural defaults operate (e.g., dropped calls becoming in absentia orders despite 'visibility') overcame Side A's more idealized procedural mechanisms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, particularly the observation that 'The proposition does not purchase unused capacity. It purchases faster remote calendars,' which cleanly neutralized Side A's primary defense against rushed errors.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won because it landed sharper institutional critique: the proposition itself commands speed, so PRO cannot treat added staff as neutral slack, and without an objective cap on cases or a right to in-person hearings, throughput pressure will consume new capacity. CON also drove home that a visible dropped connection is not a legal stay, while in absentia orders and rapid deportations make technical failures just as wrongful as missed buses. PRO’s evidence-rot and void-vs-glitch frames were strong, but CON’s terminal impact of irreversible wrongful removal and its cleaner answers to Pressure Questions left the sharper live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A4 rebuttal showing that extra staff cannot rewrite the default rule that a broken video link becomes an in absentia removal order, and that the proposal contains no institutional guardrail to prevent speed metrics from swallowing the claimed slack, directly gutting PRO’s "cured glitch" and "unused capacity" defenses.
