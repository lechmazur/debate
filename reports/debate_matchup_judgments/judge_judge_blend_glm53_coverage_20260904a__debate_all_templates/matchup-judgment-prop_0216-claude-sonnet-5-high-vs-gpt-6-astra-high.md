### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by effectively neutralizing Side B's two main offensive arguments (leakage and state capacity) while maintaining a clear, unanswered moral framework. A shielded against the leakage argument by repeatedly emphasizing the local, non-fungible benefits of preventing peat fires and watershed damage, which B never addressed. A also successfully co-opted B's alternative of enforcing existing maps by arguing that closing exceptions within those maps is exactly the kind of aggressive regulation that utilizes existing capacity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing successfully turned B's "enforce existing maps" alternative into a concession, proving that usable state capacity exists to close damaging exceptions.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive comparison on third-party irreversible harms versus forgone expansion income, and by forcing CON’s capacity critique into inconsistency: the same imperfect institutions CON trusts to “enforce existing maps” can also close damaging exceptions and condition new corporate permits. CON landed the sharper institutional attack—enforcement surface area, smallholder incidence, and unproven net benefit under leakage—and made risk-of-error vivid, but never showed that leakage reliably cancels local watershed/fire gains or that imperfect prevention is worse than legally permitting the damage. On the motion as written, PRO’s surviving path (accept incomplete compensation; prevent durable harm governments can still constrain) was cleaner than CON’s demand that PRO prove near-perfect targeting before any tighter rule is justified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s A3.1/A4.1–A4.5 reply: capacity objection cannot veto tighter forest/peat limits while sparing “enforce existing rules,” plus the permitting-focused mechanism for concentrating scarce enforcement.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON won by decisively exploiting the state-capacity gap in PRO's enforcement mechanisms. While PRO advocated for aggressive regulation, CON convincingly showed that expanding the regulatory surface area in weak-capacity states inevitably harms smallholders over conglomerates. CON's distinction between enforcing already-mapped moratoriums (narrow, visible) versus adjudicating new permit boundaries (wide, contestable) was a fatal blow to PRO's case. Furthermore, CON successfully shifted the burden of proof on leakage, framing the debate as a gamble of certain smallholder costs against uncertain, uncosted global benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled PRO's "permitting system" answer from the Pressure Questions round, showing that adjudicating contested permit histories requires the exact state capacity PRO failed to establish, while contrasting it with the narrow, satellite-verifiable enforcement of existing moratoriums.
