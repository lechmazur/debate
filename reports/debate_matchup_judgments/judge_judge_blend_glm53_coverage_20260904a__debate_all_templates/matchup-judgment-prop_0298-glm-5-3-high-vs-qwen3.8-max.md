### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by showing that state capture already governs the hardest records and that private markets cannot unseal, compel disclosure of, or reliably preserve them. Side A offered a sharp warning about state control, but repeatedly assumed the proposition required a single centralized portal or monopoly, despite Side B’s compatible federated model. Side B also weighed public accountability and free access more convincingly against market paywalls and profit-based selection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive response was that leaving records mostly to markets does not remove state control over sealed files; it merely leaves that control intact without creating a public access duty. The federated-public model then undercut Side A’s single-point-of-capture framing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO (B) won a clear but competitive decision. The decisive clash was who can open and preserve the hardest records named by the motion—sealed adoption/removal files, agency ledgers, unprofitable local holdings. CON’s capture critique and “don’t hand keys to the perpetrator” frame were sharp and forced PRO to defend oversight and federation, but CON never neutralized the core gap: markets cannot subpoena, unseal, or compel indexing of state-held files, and commercial selection leaves politically inconvenient collections undigitized. Under pressure, CON pivoted to already-open bulk records and outside litigation/movements, which concedes the motion’s hardest cases rather than answering them. PRO’s public-duty claim better matched the written comparison (government digitize/fund access vs leaving recovery mostly to private markets) and made suppression contestable rather than dormant. CON’s mid-debate “motion rewrite” attack scored real points on single-portal risk and funding strings, keeping the race competitive, but did not outweigh PRO’s cleaner route on the records that define the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.5: keys already sit with the state; markets cannot compel sealed files and often abandon unprofitable ones—forcing CON to concede hard cases and retreat to already-open records.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO kept the decisive clash on sealed/unprofitable records: only state action can unseal adoption/removal files and fund preservation of non-commercial ledgers.
