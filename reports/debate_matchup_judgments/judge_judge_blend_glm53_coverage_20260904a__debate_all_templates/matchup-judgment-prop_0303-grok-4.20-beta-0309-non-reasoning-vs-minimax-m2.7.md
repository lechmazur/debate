### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): PRO (Side A) wins for three interlocking reasons. First, PRO consistently argued the right claim — not that meritocracy is fake, but that it is overstated because class reproduction is more systematic than successful people acknowledge. CON repeatedly argued against a stronger target, which wasted argumentative energy and ultimately did not neutralize the actual proposition. Second, CON's most sophisticated move — distinguishing pre-institutional sorting from institutional selection — actually backfired. CON conceded in Rebuttal 2 that wealthy families "buy better coaching, fund more applications, and normalize elite aspirations," and PRO correctly turned this in the closing: those upstream advantages ARE the elite pathway, not a separate family matter outside the institutional system. The proposition covers the full pipeline, and CON's own admissions confirmed the pipeline tilts heavily by class. Third, CON never answered why the thirtyfold gap persists across generations even as specific features (legacy admissions) are supposedly being removed; PRO's response — that multiple reinforcing layers exist rather than isolated removable holes — remained live through the closing. CON's falsifiability challenge had real bite and PRO's offered threshold was somewhat circular, but CON also never provided its own threshold when pressed, making that exchange roughly a wash. CON's two clipped speeches (B3, B5) added minor execution costs on top of the substantive deficits. CON argued with intellectual rigor and the pre-institutional/institutional distinction is genuinely worth credit, but it did not escape the scope of the proposition, and the cascade of CON's own concessions kept reinforcing PRO's core story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was PRO's Rebuttal 2 turning CON's pre-institutional concession back against CON. When CON argued in B4.1 that the thirtyfold gap reflects family-level preparation occurring before any admissions committee acts, PRO (A4.3) correctly pointed out that this admission IS the class-reproduction mechanism the proposition identifies — the whole pipeline from coaching to cultural normalization to application readiness constitutes the "elite educational and professional pathway," not just what happens inside the admissions room. CON never recovered from this because the pre-institutional/institutional distinction, while intellectually real, does not escape the proposition's scope, and CON's own concession had confirmed the upstream tilt.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO)): PRO (B) won by showing that elite pathways encompass the entire pipeline, including upstream family investment in preparation and application culture, making the skew of elite attainment toward the wealthy evidence of class reproduction rather than neutral meritocratic filtering. CONs attempts to isolate institutional selection from pre-institutional sorting felt technically narrow and did not squarely address the motions focus on pathways, while CONs unfalsifiability challenge, though sharp, was adequately answered by PROs threshold of several-fold overrepresentation. PROs case that the merit narrative understates inherited scaffolding remained the cleaner live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PROs reframing of the thirtyfold attendance gap as measuring institutional filtering rather than mere self-selection, by arguing that application-rate differences themselves reflect prior class advantages in information and guidance. This neutralized CONs strongest methodological objection and linked the entire pipeline to class reproduction.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): PRO wins by maintaining a tighter link to the proposition’s descriptive claim. PRO successfully argued that “pathways” include upstream pre-filtering and cultural scaffolding, directly answering CON’s attempt to separate family preparation from institutional selection. CON’s repeated concessions that meritocracy is “imperfect, biased, and incomplete” and explicitly “overstated” undermined its stance on the motion. PRO also handled the falsifiability pressure more effectively by shifting the burden back to the lived outcomes data, while CON’s pivot to alternative systems and outlier success stories failed to neutralize PRO’s core mechanism. PRO’s closing crystallized the clash cleanly, whereas CON’s closing conceded the proposition’s key term.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 absorption of the upstream sorting argument. By reframing application-rate disparities as evidence of class-transmitted resources that shape the entire pathway, PRO turned CON’s methodological challenge into confirmation of the proposition, effectively collapsing CON’s institutional vs. pre-institutional distinction.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (CON)): Side A built and sustained a clean through-line across the whole debate: class produces traits → system rewards traits → system rewards class → this is structural reproduction. The decisive moment is A's exploitation of B's own concession at [B2.5] — that "the distribution of who starts ahead reflects real differences in parental investment" — which A correctly argues admits the core claim while trying to name it something else. B never escapes this trap: calling the same process "selection" rather than "laundering" is a relabeling, not a rebuttal. B's strongest counter — that children from identical brackets diverge, proving agency operates independently — addresses intra-bracket variation but doesn't touch the systemic class-to-outcome correlation that is the actual motion. B's "what's the alternative?" framing was its most original move, but A correctly neutralized it: the proposition doesn't demand an alternative system, only honest acknowledgment that elite pathways reproduce class privilege more than successful people admit. A's pressure questions were sharper and more on-point, and A answered B's pressure questions cleanly (the circularity charge was met head-on: "There is no neutral space called genuine merit"). B's Asian-American counterexample remained live but was adequately contained: A noted that mobility required sufficient material stability to sustain multi-year educational investment, and B's response ("stability helpful but not a prerequisite") was asserted rather than argued. Multiple clipped speeches slightly penalized both sides but hurt B marginally more since its opening and first rebuttal both ended mid-argument. A's closing crystallized the clash cleanly and memorably with the starting-line image; B's closing mostly repeated prior points without new weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal using B's own [B2.5] concession against it: B admitted that starting differences reflect "real differences in parental investment," and A turned this into the decisive link — if unequal investment creates unequal starting traits and the system rewards those traits, the system rewards class investment, not raw merit. B's attempt to answer (children from identical brackets still diverge) addressed a different question and never closed the wound.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): PRO successfully framed merit as traits produced by class-advantaged environments, making it structurally circular to treat such traits as purely individual merit. CON’s concession that starting differences reflect real parental investment was turned into evidence that the system rewards class investment. While CON raised plausible counterexamples like Asian-American mobility and within-income divergence, PRO blunted the former by showing it required economic stability, and the latter was not directly answered but did not overcome the structural framing. The proposition’s modest wording—reproduction is understated by successful people—was correctly upheld without needing to propose alternatives, whereas CON often argued against a stronger version of the motion. Clear competitive edge to PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s exploitation of CON’s admission that "the distribution of who starts ahead reflects real differences in parental investment" [A2.5], arguing that rewarding resulting traits means rewarding class investment rather than raw merit [B4.5][B5.1].
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully framing the debate around the origin of "merit" and demonstrating that Side B's defense of trait-based selection actually concedes the proposition's core claim. A consistently argued that conscientiousness, vocabulary, and cultural habits are themselves products of class-resourced environments, turning B's mechanism into proof of structural reproduction rather than a refutation of it. A handled pressure cleanly with direct A1/A2 answers, leveraged B's concession about parental investment, and correctly clarified that the proposition is descriptive, not prescriptive, neutralizing B's late pivot to "what's the alternative?" Side B's case relied heavily on asserting the independence of culture and agency but never broke A's causal chain, and the late introduction of genetic predispositions and burden-shifting weakened its grounding and focus. A's tighter clash management, sharper weighing, and more disciplined execution secure the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 directly answers Side B's pressure questions by exposing the circularity in rewarding "developed traits" when those traits are funded by unequal parental investment. By turning B's own concession about starting differences into proof of structural laundering, A collapses B's selection-vs-reproduction distinction and decisively advances the proposition.
