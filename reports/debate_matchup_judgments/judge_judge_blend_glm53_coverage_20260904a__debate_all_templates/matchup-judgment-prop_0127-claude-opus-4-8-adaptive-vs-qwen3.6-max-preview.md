### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully trapping Side A in a dilemma regarding the cost of food rescue. Side B argued convincingly that if rescue is cheap and profitable, a mandate is redundant; if it is costly, a mandate forces logistical failures and spoilage because it does not fund the necessary infrastructure (trucks, cold storage). Side A had strong defensive responses regarding shopper behavior and the unpredictability of dynamic markdowns, but failed to adequately answer Side B's pressure on physical capacity limits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal cleanly exposed a contradiction in Side A's opening, pointing out that Side A cannot simultaneously claim rescue pays for itself through savings while also arguing stores refuse to do it without state coercion.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A won by exposing and sustaining the contradiction in B's cost/profitability and capacity claims across rebuttals and pressure answers; B never resolved it while A answered questions directly and crystallized the fork.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 ([A2.1]) locking B into mutually exclusive cheap-vs-costly positions from B's own opening.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won because its structural framing—that the mandate establishes a flexible hierarchy (donation or discount) that blocks landfilling as a default—weathered CON’s sharpest attacks. CON successfully exposed tensions in PRO’s cost story and landed a memorable contradiction turn, but PRO recovered by showing the problem is patchy coordination and free-riding rather than absolute profitability. CON’s closing was clipped for exceeding the word limit, leaving its final crystallization incomplete, while PRO’s closing cleanly restated the live clash. CON’s alternative of funding trucks remained underdeveloped, and its finite-demand objection, while sharp, assumed a demand inelasticity that PRO reasonably contested with perishability and price-elasticity arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s contradiction turn in Rebuttal 1 ([A2.1]) forcing PRO to defend whether rescue was simultaneously cheap enough to be trivial and costly enough to need coercion, which shaped the economic clash for the rest of the debate.
