- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A won by cleanly differentiating between regulating harmful techniques (like live odds and 'bet now' prompts) and regulating product categories. Side A's 'speed limit' analogy in the closing decisively refuted Side B's claim that technique-based rules are just the motion in disguise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side B's 'uniform rules' attack by pointing out that Side A had explicitly proposed banning live odds for all advertisers, exposing Side B's argument as a strawman.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON successfully established that technique-based regulation—banning live odds, urgent prompts, and in-play inducements across all gambling products—targets the precise harm PRO identified without creating an arbitrary category hierarchy. PRO never showed that differential treatment of sports betting as a class adds protection beyond what neutral rules against harmful conduct already provide, and CON’s speed-limit analogy decisively separated unequal impact from unequal law, leaving PRO’s closing synthesis unconvincing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 correction that it had expressly proposed banning live odds and player prompts as techniques—not product categories—exposed a material mischaracterization by PRO, and the closing speed-limit analogy cemented why differential impact does not equal differential law.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won the comparative burden. PRO showed a distinctive fit (youth co-viewing + emotional activation + immediate convertibility) but never showed it is greater overall harm than a casino-app ad in the same broadcast enabling rapid repeated play. CON's technique-based alternative (ban live odds, bet-now prompts, inducements, watershed/frequency for all) meets PRO's concrete harms without product hierarchy and avoids porosity and leniency gaps. PRO's closing that unequal impact = motion fails against CON's speed-limit analogy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4/A5-B5 exchange: CON's speed-limit distinction between differential impact and differential law, showing neutral technique bans achieve PRO's goals while PRO's category rule adds no protection.
