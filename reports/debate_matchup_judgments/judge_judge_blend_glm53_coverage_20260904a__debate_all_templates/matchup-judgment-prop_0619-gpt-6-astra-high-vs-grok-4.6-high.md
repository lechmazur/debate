### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a very close, high-level debate by systematically dismantling Side B's attempt to cleanly separate a "harassment kit" from a "legitimate demand." While Side B powerfully highlighted the gap in Side A's alternative regarding clear-cut harms, Side A successfully proved that Side B's inclusion of "throwaway brigades" as a vector to suppress would capture legitimate anonymous organizing. Side A sharply demonstrated that Side B's proposed legal "shield" still required platforms to defend their classifications in court, leaving the incentive to overblock intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which exposed the flaw in Side B's "express shield" by showing that preserving a dual-use network still forces a platform to defend a disputed classification, making quiet suppression the safer legal bet.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): PRO won because it established a cleaner, more defensible boundary for the duty and showed CON's alternative leaves a harmful enforcement gap. PRO successfully reframed the duty around identifiable vectors—home addresses, fake-emergency scripts, and throwaway brigades—rather than political opinion, and directly answered CON's strongest objection with a concrete "strip the kit, keep the demand" rule coupled with an express shield for lawful organizing. This broke the alleged asymmetry that would incentivize overblocking. CON's chilling-effect concerns were plausible and well-pressed, especially on the difficulty of distinguishing brigades from legitimate anonymous coordination, but PRO progressively narrowed the dispute by showing that the mixed-campaign scenario actually illustrates the duty's edge rather than its unworkability. CON conceded that platforms should remove clear swatting tools, which PRO leveraged to show that without a legal duty, harmful infrastructure remains optional to address. By the closing, PRO had the clearer live route to victory: the platform controls the amplification and the list, no other actor is obliged to act before a court order, and the duty can be structured to hit the tool while protecting the speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's answer to CON's mixed-campaign objection in Rebuttal 2 and Closing—arguing that the duty strips the "swatting kit" (doxxing, scripts, brigades) while expressly shielding the remaining lawful organizing, thereby making cutting the list the legally safer path rather than burying the workers. This reframe blunted CON's central chilling-effect argument and turned the mixed campaign into evidence that the duty has a workable edge.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A built a more concrete and mechanically sound case by clearly distinguishing between the specific vectors of a harassment campaign (e.g., home addresses and swatting scripts) and legitimate organizing. Side A effectively neutralized Side B's chilling effect arguments by proposing an express shield for lawful speech once harmful vectors are removed, and forcefully pointed out in the closing that Side B's preferred alternative leaves the platform legally free to keep distributing the locating tool while targets absorb the harm. Side B's arguments about mixed campaigns and liability asymmetries were strong but ultimately failed to overcome Side A's sharp, targeted distinctions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1, which sharply dismantled Side B's false equivalence between a boycott and a swatting campaign by distinguishing a storefront on a flyer from a home address paired with a script for a fake emergency.
