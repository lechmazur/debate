### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A (PRO) built the stronger through-line across all rounds. Its core claim—that the ban is what converts husbandry reform from an optional cost into an economic necessity—was introduced early and survived to the closing intact. CON's central counter (mandate reforms directly instead of banning the tool) was answered cleanly in A4.1: a bright-line prohibition removes the crutch and restructures farmer incentives in a way that fragmented direct-mandate enforcement cannot replicate. CON never fully neutralized this incentive-structure argument. PRO's secondary argument that the evolutionary mechanism is indifferent to labeling conventions (growth promotion vs. disease prevention vs. veterinary prophylaxis) also held up; CON's "surgical approach" alternative presupposes a meaningful distinction that PRO effectively challenged. On the regressive-cost clash, PRO's counter—that resistance itself falls hardest on those who cannot afford second-line drugs—was genuine and never fully dismissed, only called "rhetorically clever." CON's closing "80% of the goal with 0% of the food security damage" framing was asserted without grounding already in the debate, making it a late-stage unsupported claim. Format execution further favored PRO: CON was clipped in its Opening, Rebuttal 1, and Rebuttal 2, creating real gaps in its case, while PRO properly structured its A1/A2 answers to pressure questions and maintained coherent argument progression throughout. CON had a genuinely competitive alternative (targeted regulation) and a real food-security concern, but could not deliver the payoff argument that targeted regulation would produce equivalent resistance reduction, which left its case resting on an asserted but undemonstrated premise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1 response to CON's Q1 was the most decisive exchange. CON asked why not mandate husbandry reforms directly rather than imposing a blanket ban. PRO answered that a prophylactic ban creates an enforceable bright line that makes better husbandry the cheapest path rather than a regulatory afterthought, while direct mandates are gameable and fragmented. This directly neutralized CON's strongest alternative-policy argument and shifted the burden back to CON to explain why direct reform mandates would succeed where prior targeted regulations have not—a burden CON never met.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by systematically dismantling Side B's alternative. When B argues we should directly mandate husbandry reforms rather than banning the antibiotics that compensate for bad conditions, A persuasively demonstrates that preserving the "crutch" ensures reforms will be delayed or gamed via veterinary loopholes. A's framing of the economic transition costs as temporary versus the permanent loss of medical efficacy also decisively answered B's strong impacts on global food security.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's argument that keeping prophylaxis legal under "veterinary oversight" simply relabels the exact same subtherapeutic dosing without changing the evolutionary pressure on bacteria.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully collapsing CON’s distinction between growth promotion and prophylaxis, demonstrating that both function as evolutionary incubators and economic crutches that disincentivize necessary husbandry reform. B’s framing of the ban as a structural catalyst rather than a blunt instrument directly answered CON’s alternative policy, while B’s weighing of transitional food costs against irreversible medical collapse proved more decisive. CON raised valid concerns about transition cliffs and proportionality but could not overcome B’s cleaner causal mechanism on resistance dynamics or the incentive argument for why targeted mandates would remain gameable without a bright-line ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 directly answering A’s pressure questions, particularly the argument that mandating husbandry reforms is fragmented and gameable whereas a prophylactic ban creates an enforceable bright line that restructures economic incentives, making better husbandry the cheapest path rather than a regulatory afterthought. This crystallized the central policy clash and neutralized CON’s main alternative.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): CON wins by a clear but competitive margin. Three things tip the balance. First, CON caught a material factual error in PRO's Rebuttal 1: foot-and-mouth disease is viral, making antibiotics entirely irrelevant to PRO's catastrophic-herd-loss scenario. This was not a minor slip—it was PRO's central counterexample to the food security objection, and its collapse damaged PRO's epistemic credibility throughout the rest of the exchange. Second, CON's naming of WHO, EMA, and FDA as bodies already classifying antibiotics by crossover risk directly neutralized PRO's core attack—that targeted stewardship is an unspecified slogan with no institutional machinery. PRO never answered this concretely; claiming these bodies are too slow is weaker than showing they don't exist. Third, CON's closing precision paradox landed cleanly: PRO itself acknowledged tetracyclines and macrolides as the primary crossover threat, which undercuts the claim that eliminating all prophylactic use—including low-risk compounds—is "surgically precise." PRO has real strengths: the horizontal gene transfer argument is genuinely original and CON's response only partially addresses co-resistance via plasmid transfer; the "ban the practice, not the compound" framing is durable; and the long-term stakes of resistance are the stronger ultimate harm. PRO's Rebuttal 2 clipping is a real execution cost, cutting the Denmark stabilization data and the final weighing before they could land fully. On balance, CON's regulatory-infrastructure concreteness, its clean catch of the foot-and-mouth error, and its closing precision argument together outweigh PRO's HGT point and narrative strength.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 [B2.1] was the most decisive exchange. Catching that foot-and-mouth disease is viral—making PRO's flagship herd-collapse scenario simply irrelevant to antibiotic policy—was a direct, factually grounded strike that materially damaged PRO's live route on food security, and it was never recovered. CON also immediately turned PRO's own pollution analogy back: we regulate and target, we don't ban all emissions, so the analogy supports stewardship, not a blanket ban.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A won by successfully defending the need for a blanket ban over targeted stewardship. Side A's explanation of horizontal gene transfer effectively neutralized Side B's claim that 'zero-crossover-risk' antibiotics exist, and Side A persuasively framed prophylactic use as a 'chemical crutch' that indefinitely delays necessary improvements in farm conditions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's use of horizontal gene transfer and plasmid co-resistance in Rebuttal 2 to dismantle Side B's reliance on 'zero-crossover-risk' compounds.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON wins by grounding its alternative in existing regulatory frameworks (WHO/EMA/FDA classification systems), effectively neutralizing PRO's central attack that targeted stewardship is just a vague slogan for inaction. CON also decisively won the precision clash, demonstrating that a blanket ban unnecessarily sacrifices low-income nutrition and agricultural stability to restrict compounds with minimal human crossover risk. PRO's case was materially weakened by a significant factual error (citing the viral 2001 foot-and-mouth crisis to argue about bacterial antibiotic failure) and a rhetorically strained claim that a blanket prohibition is surgically precise. CON's direct PQ answers, cleaner causal weighing, and avoidance of major execution errors secure the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's immediate correction of PRO's foot-and-mouth analogy (noting it is viral and irrelevant to antibiotics) paired with the concrete citation of WHO/EMA drug classification tiers to answer PRO's pressure questions. This sequence dismantled PRO's credibility on disease mechanics and proved CON's alternative was already operational, not abstract.
