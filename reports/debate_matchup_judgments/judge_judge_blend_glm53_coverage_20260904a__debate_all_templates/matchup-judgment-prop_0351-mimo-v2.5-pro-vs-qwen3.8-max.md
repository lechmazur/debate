### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Both sides built plausible causal stories about proximity-based mentorship versus structured digital learning, but the motion's decisive burden was the "admission gap" — whether harm is understated relative to what employers/workers say. B kept returning the debate to this precise wording, arguing that RTO mandates, hybrid policies, buddy programs, and junior requests for office time are loud, costly, public admissions, not concealment. A's counter — that this negotiation is about logistics rather than the specific unmeasured magnitude of harm — is a clever distinction but increasingly feels like redefining "admission" narrowly to save the case, especially since real-world RTO justifications commonly invoke junior mentorship explicitly. A's mechanism arguments (informal osmosis, asymmetric harm to juniors) were vivid and rhetorically strong, but B's rebuttal that the pre-remote office was itself an unreliable, exclusionary "lottery" blunted the claim that remote work created a clean loss rather than a tradeoff. A's case was also weakened procedurally: both of its rebuttal turns were clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning key closing arguments in those rounds were never fully delivered, a real execution cost per the judging framework. B's speeches stayed within limits, engaged the pressure questions cleanly with proper A1/A2 structure, and consistently sharpened the comparison rather than repeating points. On balance, B gave the cleaner, more motion-faithful path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over return-to-office mandates: A used them (A2.5–A2.6, A4.6) as proof that "the digital alternatives are insufficient" and thus evidence of hidden harm; B reframed them (B2.5, B4.1–B4.3) as loud, costly public admissions that directly negate the motion's "usually admit" framing. B's reframe more precisely tracked the actual wording of the proposition and was never fully neutralized by A's logistics-versus-magnitude distinction.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won a solid victory by relentlessly exposing a strategic contradiction in Side B's case. Side B attempted to use the existence of massive Return-to-Office (RTO) mandates as proof of the harm, but Side A correctly pointed out that if employers are spending vast political capital to fight WFH, they are explicitly admitting the harm, which directly contradicts the 'more than they admit' burden of the prompt.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument that Return-to-Office mandates are the loudest possible admission of the problem, flipping Side B's best evidence of harm into proof that the harm is not concealed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON wins because the motion requires showing not merely that remote work hurts junior development, but that the harm is worse than employers and workers usually admit. CON successfully turns PRO's best evidence—return-to-office mandates and public policy fights—into proof of open admission and negotiation, not concealment. PRO's "structural blind spot" argument is philosophically interesting but cannot overcome the fact that companies and workers are loudly debating exactly this developmental tradeoff. PRO establishes a plausible mechanism for informal learning loss but fails to prove the admission gap, while CON crystallizes that the costs are named, contested, and baked into visible policies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turning of PRO's RTO mandate evidence in Rebuttal 2 and Closing. PRO argued that costly RTO mandates prove managers see hidden developmental harm; CON rebutted that these mandates are the loudest possible public admission of the harm, proving the opposite of concealment. This exchange decided the debate.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built a coherent case, but PRO better engaged the motion's specific "under-admitted" clause. CON's central weapon — that this risk is the "most rehearsed argument in remote work" — is rhetorically sharp but never squarely defeats PRO's distinction between naming a risk and admitting its true scale (A2.3-A2.4: mentoring programs may be "cheap administrative fixes" rather than genuine acknowledgment). PRO's lottery/expected-value analogy (A4.2) also effectively neutralized CON's "already-unreliable baseline" attack, showing that shrinking an uneven system's opportunities still worsens average outcomes — a point CON's deliberate-practice counter (asserted without real grounding) didn't fully rebut. CON's survivorship-bias and access-vs-apprenticeship points were strong and original, and PRO's answer there was more concession-and-reframe than knockout, but PRO's overall throughline (delayed, hard-to-measure damage masked by output metrics and flexibility gratitude) held up better under cross-examination. B's second rebuttal was also clipped before completion, a real execution cost that likely lost part of its final synthesis.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's lottery analogy in Rebuttal 2 (A4.2: "cutting the number of tickets can still make the average outcome worse") directly answered CON's claim that an already-unreliable mentoring baseline meant further erosion couldn't be a major loss, and CON never fully recovered from it beyond reasserting that deliberate practice outperforms incidental exposure without real support.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by masterfully weaponizing the exact wording of the proposition. By pointing out that Side B's core harm (loss of ambient mentorship) is the single most rehearsed concern in the remote-work debate, Side A made it nearly impossible for Side B to prove this damage is "worse than usually admitted." Side B tried to argue that talk is not admission, but Side A successfully reframed employer behavior as rational, open triage rather than suppression.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 sequence systematically dismantled Side B's burden by exposing that Side B was arguing the conventional wisdom, effectively proving the harm is highly admitted.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO maintained a coherent mechanism—ambient apprenticeship and delayed, compounding losses—while distinguishing discussion of remote-work risks from genuine organizational accounting. CON’s critique of romanticized offices and the "dominant narrative" was strong, but PRO recovered by showing that shrinking the "surface area of chance" reduces expected development, and that employer behavior (office cuts, output focus) reveals minimization, not honest admission. CON’s clipped Rebuttal 2 and underdeveloped deliberate-practice alternative left key burdens unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s distinction between naming a risk and admitting its magnitude—using employer behavior (office cuts, output metrics) to show the concern is minimized rather than truly owned—decisively blunted CON’s claim that the damage was already the dominant narrative.
