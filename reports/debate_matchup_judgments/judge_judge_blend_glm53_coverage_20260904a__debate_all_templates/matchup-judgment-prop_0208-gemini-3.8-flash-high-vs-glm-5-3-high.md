### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a more disciplined and harder-to-dislodge case. Its core mechanism—that raw completion rates track student population (working, parenting, transferring students) rather than program quality—was never squarely refuted; PRO's answer to the pressure question on this point (B4.1) quietly retreated to "six- and eight-year tracking" and transfer-adjustment, which CON correctly flagged (A4.1) as conceding that the motion's actual trigger (bare completion rates) is indefensible. CON's "best case vs. worst case" critique (A2.1-A2.2, reinforced in A4.5) landed and was never cleanly answered—PRO kept comparing successful vocational completers to failed degree students without acknowledging equivalent attrition risk in shorter programs. CON's cost-arithmetic point (building flagship labs/faculty costs more per seat than sustaining existing humanities sections, so on PRO's own "finite resources" logic, funding community college seats dominates) was a decisive, original angle that PRO's rebuttal (B4.2) answered only by reasserting institutional capability, not economics. PRO's opening was strong and its debt-trap framing rhetorically compelling, but its closing mostly restated the risk frame without resolving CON's arithmetic or selection-effect points, while CON's closing directly inverted PRO's "who bears the risk" framing by showing the risk stems from underfunding students, not curriculum length. CON also used slightly more grounded, cautious language, while PRO leaned on unverified precise-sounding stats ("zero to sixty percent," "fewer than half") that read as brittle specificity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A2.1-A2.4 (exposing that PRO compares vocational finishers to degree dropouts, and that "stackability" requires the very bachelor's infrastructure the motion would defund), followed by PRO's retreat in B4.1 to refined completion metrics not found in the motion, which CON then punished decisively in A4.1.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the motion's actual wording and tradeoffs live: shift vs add, completion rates as a demographic trap rather than a program diagnosis, expensive flagship duplication versus cheaper community-college capacity, and PRO's repeated best-case pathway finishers versus degree dropouts comparison. A landed a real risk asymmetry on debt-without-credential and forced a partial concession that shorter stop-outs cost less, but never neutralized B's arithmetic, metric, or stackability-bridge critiques. Across rounds B narrowed the dispute more cleanly and left fewer unrebutted burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's finishers-vs-dropouts and stackability-bridge attack (B2.1–B2.4), later crystallized in B4/B5: PRO kept comparing technical completers to bachelor's leavers while its own shift would burn the upper-division programs stackability needs.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a cleaner comparative impact regarding student risk and financial ruin. While Side B offered exceptional rhetoric and a strong textual critique of the motion, Side A successfully defended the mechanism for identifying failing programs using longitudinal tracking and won the crucial clash on dropout outcomes. Side B's concession in Rebuttal 2 that vocational dropouts accumulate less debt fatally undermined their argument that both pathways hit the "same wall," allowing Side A to decisively frame the status quo as a catastrophic downside risk versus a manageable setback. Side A also effectively neutralized the community college duplication argument by correctly noting that university resource reallocation does not inherently fund community college waitlists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's "same wall" argument by highlighting the massive practical difference between manageable debt from a 12-month program and catastrophic, non-dischargeable debt from a three-year degree, effectively neutralizing Side B's core equity claim.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): B built a tighter, more responsive case across the exchange. B's opening reframed the equity issue effectively, and Rebuttal 1 directly neutralized A's central "tracking" charge by reframing non-completion itself as the worst form of tracking. B's pressure questions were sharply targeted at the weakest point in A's position (an "invest more" standard with no exit condition, and a best-case-vs-worst-case comparison flaw), and B's answers to A's own questions (A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2) were crisp, directly responsive, and introduced the decisive "compare completers to completers" logic. A, by contrast, answered B's exit-condition question with only vague language ("restructuring... when independent review reveals failure") that never specified any actual threshold for cutting a program, effectively conceding the "invest more forever" critique B had raised. A's closing recapped prior claims (tracking, mobility, ladder metaphor) without directly repairing this exposed weakness. B's closing correctly seized on that gap as the hinge of the debate, giving the clearer final reason to vote for its side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions Q1 (demanding a concrete exit condition for "chronically" failing programs) followed by A's vague, non-committal answer in Rebuttal 2, which B then exploited devastatingly in Closing ("restructure, modernize, repair—but never, under any circumstances, stop").
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by making the live decision rule clearer: chronically low-completion programs already exact real student harms, so the status quo needed an exit condition and lost when it could not give one. A's abandoned-degree vs finished-credential comparison beat B's best-case bachelor's framing, and A handled pressure more cleanly than B. B's tracking and mission arguments stayed competitive but became more repetitive and less answer-driven after the Q round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's exit-condition pressure and later A1/A2 answers: no permanent-repair rule, plus honest completer-vs-dropout pricing of outcomes.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by decisively controlling the central comparison: the actual outcome of a student in a chronically low-completion program (debt without a degree) versus a completed vocational credential. Side A's pressure question regarding an "exit condition" for failing programs exposed a critical flaw in Side B's case, as Side B essentially admitted they would never liquidate a failing program. Side A's rebuttals were sharp, directly answered pressure, and maintained a clear, student-centric weighing mechanism that Side B could not overcome with institutional or best-case-scenario arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically the A2 answer dismantling the earnings comparison by pointing out that Side B was pricing the bachelor's degree at its best-case outcome (completion) while ignoring the reality of the dropout, effectively neutralizing the "tracking into lower ceilings" argument.
