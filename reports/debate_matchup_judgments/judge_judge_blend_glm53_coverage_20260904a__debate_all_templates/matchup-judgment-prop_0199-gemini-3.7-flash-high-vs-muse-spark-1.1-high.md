### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): B won the decisive framing battle early: A's opening treated thresholds as a post-hoc "autopsy" on distributed weights, but B correctly pointed out the proposition's thresholds gate release itself, not punish it afterward. A never fully recovered from this correction, repeating the irreversibility point across rounds without engaging the actual pre-release-testing mechanism B specified (elicitable capability including foreseeable fine-tuning uplift). B also landed a sharp "proof too much" trap in Rebuttal 2 — if A's standard demands proof no downstream fine-tune could ever unlock catastrophe, that same standard indicts closed models, which rely on patching precisely because they weren't provably safe either. A's answer (closed models get active monitoring, open models don't) is a fair distinguishing move but arrived late and didn't fully dissolve the tension. B also grounded the concentration argument more concretely (hospitals, Global South universities needing local, inspectable models) and sustained that thread through pressure questions and rebuttals, while A's answer (enterprise licensing, enclaves, narrow models) felt more asserted than argued and didn't resolve the "permanent rented sovereignty" concern. Both sides handled the Q&A format correctly with proper A1/A2 answers, but B's answers did more real work reframing the clash, while A's answers mostly restated prior claims. A's irreversibility thesis remained a live and serious concern throughout, keeping this competitive, but B's superior handling of the framing correction, the proof-too-much trap, and concrete stakes gives it the clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 correction that thresholds operate pre-release (not post-distribution), compounded by the Rebuttal 2 "proof too much" trap showing A's safety standard would also disqualify closed models — a exchange A never fully neutralized.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that CON's demanded safety standard—proving no catastrophic capability under any future adversarial fine-tuning—is functionally impossible and would ban closed frontier models too, making it a disguised ban-by-default. PRO's framework of pre-release thresholds accounting for elicitable risk, combined with export-control-style safeguards above the line, remained coherent and proportionate. CON never resolved how hospitals or Global South institutions achieve data sovereignty and local adaptation without legal weight access, and their "active defense" distinction did not explain why the same impossible standard should not apply to closed APIs that are also leaked and jailbroken.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, where A exposed that CON's pre-release proof standard is unmeetable for any frontier model, open or closed, and paired that with the unanswered sovereignty critique showing that auditable APIs cannot deliver local, independent defense.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully reframing the irreversibility clash and exposing the impossible nature of Side B's safety standard. While Side B strongly emphasized the one-way door of open weights, Side A effectively countered that closed weights also inevitably leak, making the arming of defenders via legal open access below the threshold the more robust strategy. Side A's closing crystallized this perfectly. Side B's insistence on a standard that requires proving a model lacks catastrophic capacity under any adversarial fine-tuning trapped them into advocating for a de facto ban on frontier AI, which Side A ruthlessly exploited. Side B also struggled to fully answer the sovereignty and concentration harms for Global South institutions without conceding they would be locked out of frontier capabilities.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they directly answered the pressure questions by clarifying that thresholds apply to foreseeable uplift, and then used Side B's own irreversibility argument against them by showing that in a world of inevitable leaks, Side B's regime leaves defenders blind while Side A's regime arms them with inspection and adaptation tools.
