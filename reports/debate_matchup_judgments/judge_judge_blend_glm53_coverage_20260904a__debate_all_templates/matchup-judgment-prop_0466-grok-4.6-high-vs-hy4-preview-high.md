### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the debate focused on the motion's mandatory 30% floor rather than the general value of marine protection. A showed that targeted spawning closures could preserve PRO's biological benefits without requiring a much larger lockout, while PRO never adequately established why 30% was necessary or how ecologically meaningful siting could consistently spare heavily used fishing grounds. A also answered the enforcement challenge and weighed immediate displacement against PRO's uncertain transition support.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4 most decisively exposed PRO's dilemma: if crucial habitat patches are small, targeted closures suffice and the 30% floor is surplus; if meaningful protection must fill 30%, it necessarily reaches working waters. PRO repeatedly asserted that a vast EEZ resolves this tension but did not explain how geography makes productive habitat separate from fishing grounds.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by establishing and sustaining a lethal dilemma: a rigid 30% EEZ floor is either theater (useless deep water) or confiscation (closure of productive inshore grounds). PRO never escaped this fork. PRO’s “patch” defense—protect nursery habitat while leaving prime grounds open—was neatly demolished by CON’s quantitative turnaround: if ecologically essential patches are small enough to spare daily fishing grounds, the 30% mandate is surplus; if 30% must be real habitat, it necessarily swallows working inshore water. CON also kept the debate anchored to the motion’s hard case—communities that depend on fishing income now—while PRO’s “transition help” and “co-management” sat outside the resolution’s text. PRO’s biological case (reserves as insurance, spillover, seed-guarding) was plausible but could not explain why the specific 30% figure was scientifically necessary rather than a political acreage quota, or why weak states would succeed at policing a vast forbidden zone when CON’s alternative of small, fisher-chosen spawning closures and enforced catch limits needs less jurisdictional overhead. Across the exchange, CON narrowed the clash rather than widening it, crystallized cleanly in the closing, and left PRO’s core frame damaged.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 response to PRO’s “patches” argument: CON showed that PRO could not simultaneously claim critical habitat is spatially modest enough to leave prime fishing grounds untouched and still justify a 30% national floor—either the percentage is ecological surplus or it confiscates working inshore water. This exchange decided the central clash.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by decisively trapping Side B (PRO) in a spatial dilemma: a 30% no-take floor in an EEZ must either be placed in unproductive deep water ("theater") or overlap with the inshore grounds that dependent communities actually fish ("confiscation"). Side B's reliance on the sheer size of the EEZ failed to account for the reality that small-scale dependent fishers operate in the exact inshore habitats that need protection. Side A's rebuttals were sharper, directly answering pressure questions and effectively turning PRO's own analogies against them, making the comparative case much cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled PRO's "patches" defense by showing that if habitat patches are small enough to spare the working coast, the 30% floor is arbitrary and surplus, but if it must cover 30% of real habitat, it inevitably swallows the working water.
