### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won a competitive debate by showing that carbon prices and equal dividends do not themselves solve infrastructure, coordination, and first-of-a-kind technology barriers. B made strong efficiency, transparency, and capture arguments, but its concession that grids and demonstration projects require separate public tools left it relying on an inadequately defended assumption that other budgets would fund them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5 most decisively leveraged B's admission that price alone does not finance transmission, green steel, or hydrogen, arguing that B's mechanism would secure cheap marginal reductions while stalling on the hardest sectors.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully dismantling Side A's core premise that a dividend guarantees the political durability of a carbon price. By deploying the Canada example and highlighting the inelastic demand of the 'Maine oil-heater', Side B proved that a dividend cannot overcome voter anger if alternatives do not exist. Side B also secured a crucial concession that price alone cannot build first-of-a-kind infrastructure, effectively demonstrating that Side A's plan stalls decarbonization where it matters most.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal weaponized the Canada example to neutralize Side A's Gilets Jaunes argument, proving that visible rebates do not buy consent when citizens lack clean alternatives.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON built the stronger path by reframing the political-economy clash: industrial subsidies create supply that permits a moderate carbon price, buying durability through lower energy bills rather than through compensation for punitive costs. This answered PRO's central claim that dividends are necessary to keep high prices alive, leveraging real-world examples (gilets jaunes, Canada) to show visible rebates do not prevent revolt when alternatives are absent. CON also capitalized on PRO's honest concession that first-of-a-kind plants have never been financed by price alone, validating the supply-side narrative, and landed a persistent equity critique that identical per-capita checks paper over geographic and structural cost differences. PRO's case remained analytically clean on efficiency and capture, but was progressively narrowed to a thinner "political survival" claim that struggled against CON's fiscal-opportunity-cost argument that carbon revenue is the only large climate-linked pot available to build domestic capacity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal reframing that industrial revenue use keeps carbon prices moderate because clean alternatives lower needed price levels, directly neutralizing PRO's argument that dividends are required to buy consent for high prices, and supported by the argument that voters revolt against high pump prices even with rebates when no bus or heat-pump workforce exists.
