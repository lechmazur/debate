### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) wins a clear but competitive debate for three main reasons. First, PRO's opening was substantially stronger: it identified a concrete mechanism (the boom-bust cycle), attached plausible harms (rent inflation, GP backlogs, stalled researcher visas), and showed precisely how the policy corrects them. CON's opening raised valid structural objections but was clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost. Second, PRO's pressure question Q2 exposed a decisive gap in CON's case: CON never credibly filled the immediate-relief vacuum. CON's counter-proposal—temporary rental caps and surge GP funding—was not developed in the opening, felt like a late addition, and was arguably self-contradicting for a side opposed to caps. PRO correctly called this out and CON never recovered. Third, PRO's framing of the choice (managed sustainability vs. chaotic overload) was cleaner and more consistently applied across rounds. CON's strongest argument—that hard housing caps force merit to yield to bricks-and-mortar arithmetic—was genuinely damaging and never fully resolved by PRO; PRO kept saying institutions still select on merit within caps but never squarely answered that the TOTAL sponsorable pool is capped at housing stock, not talent. Still, this one live CON argument is not enough to overcome PRO's superior opening, clearer alternatives comparison, and CON's execution deficit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's A1 in Rebuttal 2: that a top lab with 50 exceptional researchers but only 10 new dorm beds cannot sponsor the other 40 regardless of merit. This correctly identified that the hard cap subordinates merit to housing stock in a way PRO never squarely answered. However, PRO's Q2 pressure and subsequent closing argument—that CON's alternatives (temporary rental caps, planning reform) are either contradictory or years away from delivering relief—proved more decisive across the full exchange because it left CON without a credible interim solution, the debate's central practical test.
