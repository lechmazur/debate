### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by showing that limits can close legitimate, concentrated programs while transferring control over sensitive partnerships to the domestic state. Side A's clipped second rebuttal was an execution cost, but its core comparison remained clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange exposed the tension between Side B's ideal of many non-decisive paymasters and a policy that restricts foreign funding while exempting domestic state dominance. Side B defended that asymmetry through democratic accountability, but Side A persuasively answered that the domestic sovereign has greater coercive power over universities.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON demonstrated that PRO's mechanism would concentrate university funding under domestic state oversight while claiming to protect independence. CON's structural alternative of severed-renewal funding and faculty governance answered anticipatory compliance more precisely than statutory caps, and the licensing-authority turn showed the cure was worse than the disease.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 exposed that PRO's proposed 'transparent academic-funding registrar' was still a state licensing body for foreign partnerships, and that PRO's admission that domestic funding needs no cap [A4.3] meant the motion simply swapped foreign leverage for exclusive domestic dependence—the 'changed masters' frame that decided the debate.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON won the central weighing: concentration caps invert - they kill small area-studies centres where one foreign ministry is the only patron - and a registrar ruling on "sensitivity" hands a domestic veto to the actor with real coercive power. CON's endowment/non-renewable + faculty control alternative directly answered the renewal lever without requiring a cap, while PRO left disclosure (which already exists) doing most work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.3-B2.6 inverted-cap and domestic-dominance symmetry, amplified by B4.4-B4.6 exposing PRO's registrar as a licensing authority that makes universities more dependent on their own state.
