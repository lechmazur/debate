### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A built the cleaner route to victory and maintained it across the full exchange. Three decisive advantages: First, the double-standard argument—conventional agriculture was never required to prove long-term sustainability before receiving comparable subsidies—was established in the opening and never squarely answered by CON. CON's response was that externalities are handled through regulation, but Side A's Q1 exposed that concretely: if regulation were sufficient, soil erosion, nitrate contamination, and pollinator decline would not still be worsening. CON's A1 conceded regulation is "chronically underfunded and unevenly enforced," which materially undermined their own position. Second, the circularity trap—you cannot demand data from a system you refuse to fund at comparable scale—was set in the opening and crystallized in the closing with the "ankle weights" analogy for contaminated pilot-scale evidence. CON's phased-approach alternative was introduced late, underdeveloped, and did not answer why pilot data gathered under a structurally disadvantaged subsidy architecture would satisfy CON's own evidentiary standard. Third, Side A correctly caught and explained the misattribution in CON's Q2, turning the contradiction back onto CON. Side B maintained live concerns about fiscal recklessness and opportunity cost, and the ethanol analogy had initial force, but Side A's process-vs-commodity distinction blunted it and CON never recovered. Two clipped speeches for CON are a further execution cost. Side A's closing crystallized the clash without introducing new evidence, while Side B's closing recycled earlier points with less sharpness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's reversal of the circularity charge was the most decisive exchange. CON accused PRO of circular reasoning—funding regenerative agriculture to get data presupposes it can compete. Side A turned this back cleanly: CON demands evidence from a system farmers can't afford to adopt under the current subsidy architecture, then cites lack of evidence as justification for withholding support. CON never escaped this trap, and their eventual concession in A1 that regulation is underfunded effectively confirmed that the status quo is not the neutral baseline they relied upon throughout.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side B won by making the central clash clearer: the status quo already receives large subsidies despite known environmental harms, and withholding comparable support creates the very evidence gap CON invokes. Side A had a real fiscal-responsibility argument and a plausible phased-support alternative, but leaned too hard on contested claims that regulation already handles externalities and repeated the ethanol analogy without fully answering B's parity and lock-in arguments. Side B also benefited from catching Side A's misattributed pressure question and from stronger comparative framing in the final speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to the phased-pilot alternative was the decisive exchange: B argued that pilots conducted under a subsidy structure still penalizing regenerative adoption would produce distorted evidence, while parity would both correct the imbalance and generate the relevant scale data.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A secured a clear win by effectively turning Side B's yield-dip argument into proof of structural lock-in, demonstrating that demanding data before subsidies creates an impossible catch-22. A cleanly answered the fiscal drain concern by noting parity funding can be withdrawn if practices fail, and sharply exposed B's contradictory stance on regulation after B conceded it is underfunded. B's clipped speeches, misattributed pressure question, and reliance on a fraying status quo defense weakened its case, while A's ankle-weights analogy and epistemic trap framing crystallized the clash decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the yield-dip and circularity charges, arguing that transition losses are exactly why farmers cannot switch without parity support, making CON's demand for prior data a structural barrier that permanently protects a degrading system. This turned CON's core risk argument into a reason to vote PRO.
