- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and executed the pressure-question format cleanly with properly labeled A1/A2 answers. B's core architecture—separating "memory" (curricula, museums, plaques) from "honor" (the name itself)—proved durable: A conceded repeatedly that memory survives fine without the name, which let B isolate the entire dispute onto whether a retained name can be neutralized into a "scar" through context. A's "continuity-with-truth" model was intellectually consistent and produced a genuinely interesting counter (the Stalin-road answer, biting the bullet with courage), but B's closing exploited exactly that admission, landing the "performative contradiction" line—you cannot claim to repudiate a man while still bestowing his name on public infrastructure—which A's final dichotomy (indelible vs. not indelible) reframed but never fully dissolved. B's pressure questions were sharper and did more damage (forcing A to defend keeping Stalin's name), while A's pressure questions, though clever, were answered adequately by B's structural-embedding response. On the "primarily" vagueness clash, B's point that the motion's own wording already narrows to extreme, non-mixed cases blunted A's slippery-slope worry. Overall B kept tighter control of the central comparison and closed with the more memorable, less rebuttable line.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Question forcing A to either rename Stalin's road or concede the standard, followed by B's closing "performative contradiction" attack on A's answer, was the decisive exchange: it exposed the sharpest tension in CON's model and was never fully resolved.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): PRO won because its honor-versus-memory distinction held under pressure: once mandatory teaching is available either way, a retained name uniquely preserves state endorsement that plaques cannot convert. PRO better resolved the live burden on what retention still adds and why a corrective verdict is not morally identical to the old exclusionary one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal that mandatory context cannot turn a name on diplomas/letterhead from endorsement into repudiation (B4.3–B4.6), locked in by the Stalin closing as performative contradiction.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): PRO won by maintaining a clear, structurally grounded distinction between honoring and remembering. While CON offered a sophisticated "continuity-with-truth" model and the memorable refusal to rename even Stalin, PRO successfully showed that a retained name structurally endorses regardless of context, and that all educational benefits CON claimed are fully available after renaming. CON's "repudiated scar" argument collapsed under PRO's pressure that you cannot simultaneously name an institution after someone and claim to repudiate them, making PRO's path to victory the cleaner and more decisive one after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (B4), which answered CON's pressure questions by demonstrating that a name's honoring function is structurally embedded in maps, diplomas, and letterheads, making it impossible for plaques or curricula to convert state endorsement into repudiation—sharply undermining CON's core "scar" defense before PRO crystallized it in the close.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases with real clash, but PRO's core frame—that naming is active, ongoing civic celebration rather than inert "sediment"—held up better under pressure. PRO repeatedly and effectively weaponized CON's own concession that "plaques go unread" and "nobody learns history from street signs," turning CON's proposed remedy (contextual signage) into a self-defeating half-measure: if signage doesn't teach, CON's alternative offers neither honor-withdrawal nor real education, just cosmetic continuity. CON's strongest weapon—the "who decides 'primarily'" objection—was blunted by PRO's tu quoque (CON's own contextualization judgments require the same political adjudication), which CON never cleanly answered; CON mostly reasserted the objection rather than refuting the parity argument. CON's Butler Avenue counter-example was sharp but backfired by undermining CON's own claim that keeping names preserves teachable prompts. In the closing, PRO's "a school is not a display case" rebuttal to CON's museum-label analogy landed cleanly and went unanswered in CON's final speech, which pivoted back to the political-process argument instead. CON had the last word structurally but did not use it to resolve PRO's strongest late-round point, leaving PRO's normative case (withdraw honor, keep history in classrooms/archives) as the cleaner, more decisive path through the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's repeated use of CON's own concession ("the plaque goes unread," "nobody learns history from street signs") to show CON's contextual-signage remedy is inert while the name itself remains a form of ongoing state honor—capped by the closing "a school is not a display case" line that CON never directly rebutted.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the decisive clash—whether state naming is present-tense honor—clear and answering CON’s best pressure without sliding off the motion. “Primarily honor,” unread-plaque concessions, and Spain-as-selective-withdrawal beat CON’s slippery-slope and sediment frames, which never fully neutralized yearly reissuance of honor on schools, streets, and holidays. CON made real comparative damage on who decides and factional maps, but PRO’s honor/memory split remained the cleaner path to the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 A1: turned CON’s “plaque goes unread” / sediment-vs-prompt tension into proof that keeping the name leaves active honor standing while the supposed remedy fails.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully established that public naming is active present-tense honor, not inert history, and that contextual signage cannot neutralize a commendation. CON's contextualization alternative was undermined by its own concessions that plaques go unread, and its objection about political subjectivity was turned back when PRO showed that bounded criteria are workable and no more political than CON's own remedy. PRO's closing crystallization—"Can a disclaimer unsay a commendation?"—proved the cleanest frame for deciding the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing leveraged CON's concession that "the plaque goes unread" to show that contextual signage leaves state honor intact while renaming alone achieves withdrawal of honor, effectively collapsing CON's primary alternative remedy.
