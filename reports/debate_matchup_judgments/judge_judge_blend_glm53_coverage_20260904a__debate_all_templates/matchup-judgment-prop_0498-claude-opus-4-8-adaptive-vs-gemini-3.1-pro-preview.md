- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): B wins because it exposed and crystallized an unresolved contradiction in A's core 30% commission claim (real extortion vs. internal transfer), showed behavioral remedies already achieved A's desktop example, and left A's separation remedy unable to address the narrowed harms while preserving integration benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1-B4.4] on the commission inconsistency and the dual-horn trap.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a central, unresolved tension in PRO's case: either the 30% commission is a real economic weapon, in which case structural separation leaves it intact because an independent store still charges it, or it is a meaningless internal transfer, in which case there is no extortion justifying breakup. PRO never escaped this dilemma across two rebuttals and closing. CON also deftly turned PRO's desktop example against them to show that vertical integration plus behavioral openness can work, and showed that the line between OS core and competing apps is hopelessly gray. PRO's closing reasserted an incentive flip but did not squarely resolve the contradiction, leaving CON's comparative path to victory live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's horns dilemma in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, demonstrating that PRO could not consistently treat the 30% fee as both a destructive weapon against rivals and a harmless internal accounting transfer, and that either horn rendered structural separation unnecessary or ineffective.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): CON wins by successfully trapping PRO on a decisive dilemma regarding the 30% commission and internal accounting. When PRO dismissed an internal fee as a meaningless pocket-to-pocket transfer, CON correctly noted this undermined the extortion harm; if the fee is a real competitive weapon, structural separation does not fix it since a spun-off store still controls the chokepoint and charges commissions. CON also brilliantly turned PRO's desktop analogy by noting Windows and macOS achieved openness through behavioral tools and sideloading, not breakups. PRO's incentive-flip argument was clear but could not overcome CON's comparative weighing that separation merely relocates the monopoly, requires the same difficult regulatory line-drawing, and destroys genuine integration benefits. CON's closing was clipped, which is a minor execution cost, but the substantive victory was already secured through superior clash management and a cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1]-[B4.4] masterfully crystallized the commission dilemma, forcing PRO to choose between admitting the fee is a harmless internal transfer or conceding that structural separation leaves the pricing harm completely intact. This framing neutralized PRO's core economic harm and dictated the final rounds.
