### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by proving that the word "mainly" requires a primary strategy to reduce the root cause of nightlife harm—intoxication and dense-venue violence—rather than merely managing its aftermath. CON offered plausible comparative evidence (Newcastle, Nordic cities) showing trading hours drive assault rates, while PRO conceded that transit and street teams are "not meant to outperform a lockout at stopping bar fights." When pressed to name a city that made nightlife safer through transit alone without touching hours, PRO evaded the burden, claiming that was "not the motion." CON also showed earlier closing uses existing transit, whereas PRO requires expensive new 3 a.m. infrastructure. PRO's strandedness narrative was coherent but could not overcome the decisive concession that their primary tool does not touch the central harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question asking PRO to name a single city that improved nightlife safety by extending transit and street teams while leaving trading hours untouched, and PRO's reply that it would "not pretend that is the motion"—precisely the motion at hand. CON crystallized this in closing to show PRO could not shoulder the burden of "mainly."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): PRO redefined safety to sidewalk harms and conceded transit is "not meant to outperform a lockout at stopping bar fights" [A2.4], leaving "mainly" uncarried. CON answered displacement directly - earlier closing cuts dose, house parties lack corridor density, and drink-driving doesn't balloon - and flipped strandedness: 12:30 closings fit the existing network while 4am requires a second network at overtime. When pressed to name a city safer via transit alone, PRO declined, while CON kept both the input reduction and the exit lighting/transit, giving the cleaner path under the word "mainly."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's R1/R2 turn on [A2.4] - using PRO's own concession that buses don't cut central assaults, plus the inversion that early closing solves stranding by using real buses instead of building ghost buses - was most decisive.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by decisively controlling the central clash over the word "mainly" in the proposition. CON successfully argued that late-night transit only manages the aftermath of drinking and cannot be the primary safety strategy, a point PRO fatally conceded when admitting transit isn't meant to stop bar fights. Furthermore, CON effectively dismantled PRO's "stranded" argument by pointing out that earlier closing times align with existing, cost-effective transit networks, whereas PRO's plan requires expensive, underutilized overtime service. PRO's evasion during the pressure questions—refusing to name a city where transit alone reduced harm—sealed CON's victory by proving it could not meet the core burden of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it capitalized on Side B's failure to answer the pressure question about transit-only success, effectively proving that Side B could not meet the "mainly" burden of the motion while simultaneously defending the cost-efficiency of existing transit networks.
