### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully separating the elimination of a visible product from the elimination of environmental harm.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 exposed the circularity of Side A's 'safety valve' argument, clarifying that a container being 'most wasteful' does not automatically make its replacement environmentally superior or practically affordable.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A won a close debate by defending the motion as written: targeted phase-outs of the most wasteful items can justify some inconvenience. B’s net-gain, recurring-cost, and circularity critiques were sharp and kept the comparison live, but B never showed the less-restrictive alternative shrinks volumes and slightly over-raised the burden beyond the motion’s “some costs / most wasteful” tradeoff.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2: concedes continued use after charges is not automatic failure, then lands that B offered no volume-reduction evidence and that “lighter single-use” still leaves the permanent stream—turning B’s remedy into renting harm rather than exiting it.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live path: most wasteful = low recyclability, huge volume, workable pooled reuse/deposit models, and phase-out solves coordination that voluntary pricing does not. That concession plus the unanswered "lighter vs less" distinction gave PRO the cleaner comparative weigh.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 pressure on volume reduction forced A's explicit concession "I have not offered a measured estimate," which B then leveraged to argue charges rent harm while phase-out exits it, neutralizing CON's preferred alternative.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won by establishing a structural dilemma—phaseouts are either too trivial to solve the stated harms or too broad and run into massive life-cycle replacement costs. Side B also effectively weaponized the Pressure Questions to force a concession on the lack of empirical pre-ban measurements, and successfully contrasted the rigidity of bans with the adaptability of fees.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 brilliantly constructed and enforced the 'trivial vs. dangerous' dilemma, capitalizing on Side A's 'outer bag' example to prove the middle ground was empty.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by locking the clash on a live dilemma—meaningful enough to matter vs safe enough without hard measurement—and forcing A’s concession that no pre-ban worked comparison could be named. That hollowed PRO’s conditionals and made the fee-as-flexible-alternative path cleaner than A’s defended middle ground, despite A’s solid externality framing and honest epistemic restraint.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R2/closing use of A’s A1 concession (no named pre-ban measured format) plus the outer-bag example to prove the trivial-vs-dangerous fork.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO stayed on the proposition as written - a targeted phaseout where practical lower-burden alternatives exist, with mitigation - and correctly noted CON's strawman that alternatives were assumed costless and that fees need the same accounting CON says bans cannot do. CON's powerful Q1 exposed lack of a recalled case study, but PRO's honest refusal to invent data is good discipline, not proof no assessment is possible. CON's trivial-vs-dangerous dilemma fails for single-use-to-single-use switches (e.g., removing a redundant outer bag or replacing expanded polystyrene with paper) that don't require reuse logistics, and CON's fee-dominance claim concedes transition costs remain if the fee works. Full recurring public costs vs managed one-time transition gives PRO cleaner weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B2.4 - B2 directly corrects the "assumes alternatives cost nothing" distortion and turns CON's accounting objection back on fees, showing both instruments require lifecycle assessment, which undercut CON's core asymmetry between self-correcting fees and sticky bans.
