### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by preserving stronger routes on durability, unmanaged public costs, and service concentration. A compellingly established affordability and low barriers, but too often assumed that indoor zones caused vacancy and that trucks would generate rather than follow demand. B directly answered that decline could result from external shocks and showed why permanent venues remain necessary anchors. A’s clipped closing and claim that this answer was never given also hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered A’s central pressure: the need for revival does not prove the indoor model caused decline. B then turned A’s restroom partnership proposal into evidence that mobile vending still depends on permanent infrastructure, weakening A’s claim that permits fully internalize the costs.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins the revival burden. CON defends indoor concentration as providing infrastructure and tax base but never answers PRO's pressure: if that model worked, why does nightlife need reviving? PRO reframes vacancy as the starting point – trucks generate new low-cost demand, foot traffic spillover, and eyes on street via permit conditions (proximity restrooms, sanitation fund, insurance, grease contracts) that internalize costs CON called unfixable. CON's cannibalization/parasitism claim assumes thriving anchors to skim, which contradicts the motion's premise, and concedes dependence while PRO shows dependence is paid partnership that makes future leases viable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's R2 A1/A2 – turning CON's restroom/security attack into a permit-design solution and flipping parasitism: trucks don't park where margins are healthy to steal, they activate dead blocks where anchors are already gone, so the truck arrives first and the lease follows.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side B won by decisively leveraging the "revive" condition in the prompt. B effectively argued that relying mainly on indoor hospitality zones is doubling down on a failing model, whereas street food lowers barriers to entry and activates dead zones. Side A's defense that macroeconomic shocks caused the decline failed to explain why indoor zones are the better revival tool. Side B's pressure questions exposed this flaw, and B's rebuttals cleanly dismantled A's cannibalization argument by distinguishing between dead zones (where there is nothing to skim) and thriving ones. Side A's strongest point on infrastructure and parasitism was well-handled by B's permit design and symbiosis arguments. Side B's closing was clipped, which is a minor execution cost, but the substantive advantage and core clash were already securely won in the earlier rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they answered the pressure questions by exposing Side A's assumption of a functioning indoor ecosystem, pointing out that in the dead districts needing revival, there are no healthy margins for food trucks to skim, and that food trucks generate the initial foot traffic that makes permanent anchors viable again.
