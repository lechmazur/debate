  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated status-quo comparison was decisive: B's harms mostly already exist under prohibition, while B never gave a convincing mechanism showing prohibition reduces black-market harms or life-years losses better than regulation.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (no reasoning) (PRO)): PRO won by maintaining the comparative frame throughout: the choice is between regulation and the actual status quo of black-market brutality and dialysis deaths, not between regulation and perfection. CON landed sharp theoretical critiques about structural coercion, demand expansion, and donor morbidity, but never provided concrete evidence that a regulated market would increase total black-market harms or that donor life-years lost would outweigh recipient gains. PRO offered Iran as the only real-world existence proof in the debate, effectively showed that society already accepts permanent organ loss in living donation, and forced CON to defend a prohibition that is demonstrably failing now. CON’s alternative amounted to preserving that body count.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s repeated insistence that CON was comparing regulation to an imaginary clean slate rather than to prohibition’s documented body count, which reframed the entire debate around the status quo and neutralized CON’s perfectionist critiques about exploitation and follow-up.

CON's accurate correction of PRO's plasma and liver analogies, combined with a compelling critique of how long-term follow-up care is practically unworkable for impoverished donors, shifted the debate from "net gain" to "health transfer." CON also provided a plausible mechanism for black-market bifurcation and demand expansion that PRO never fully neutralized. PRO's clipped closing and unforced biological errors were minor execution costs, but CON's tighter grasp of the motion's specific metrics and superior framing secured a clear edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 effectively collapsed PRO's analogies by distinguishing renewable resources (plasma, labor, liver tissue) from irreversible organ loss, and then leveraged that biological reality to undermine PRO's follow-up care promises. This sequence forced PRO onto the defensive and fundamentally altered the life-years weighing.
