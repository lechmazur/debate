### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued sharply, but CON built the more durable case. PRO's strongest move—redefining "ban" as merely mandating regulator-known (not public) ownership—was effectively exposed by CON as blurring the line between prohibition and disclosure, which is what CON had proposed all along ("a registry keeps the plumbing; a universal ban rips it out"). This let CON frame PRO's "universal ban" as either redundant with targeted registries or, if truly universal and mandatory everywhere including authoritarian states, as creating a global honeypot risk that PRO never cleanly resolved (the dissident/authoritarian-access tension survived through the final speeches). CON's orphan-SPV/bankruptcy-remoteness point was a genuinely load-bearing technical argument that PRO answered only with moral condemnation ("systemic hazard") rather than engaging the mechanism, leaving that clash unresolved in CON's favor. PRO's best comeback—that forcing criminals into real-name proxies is itself an enforcement win—was a fair point and blunted some of CON's substitution argument, but it did not fully answer the deeper structural critique that PRO's plan either collapses into CON's proposal or overreaches into unworkable global surveillance. CON's closing crystallized this double bind more clearly than PRO's closing rebutted it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [B2.1]-[B2.2] response ("a registry keeps the plumbing; a universal ban rips it out") combined with the Pressure Questions exchange on authoritarian registry access, which exposed that PRO's "universal ban" either duplicates CON's targeted-registry model or creates a dangerous global disclosure honeypot—a tension PRO's A1/A2 answers never fully dissolved.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON built the cleaner path by forcing the live clashes into view: PRO's ban either collapses into a private-registry model CON already offered, or it overreaches by creating global verified-ID honeypots that harm dissidents and by destroying orphan-SPV ownerlessness required for bankruptcy remoteness and true-sale financing. PRO's crime and arbitrage case stayed powerful but never neutralized the substitution problem (real-name front men still satisfy the ban) or the sovereignty/enforcement limits, so the decisive tradeoffs favored A after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 on the privacy paradox (universal verification forces data into autocratic hands) and on orphan SPVs (forcing a fake human UBO invents contagion rather than revealing risk).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by showing PRO's universal ban is overbroad and self-undermining. CON demonstrated that banning entities which obscure beneficial ownership would destroy legitimate financial infrastructure—orphan SPVs for airplane leases, securitizations, and wind farms—because forcing disclosure of a sponsoring parent collapses the bankruptcy remoteness those structures require. PRO's attempt to reframe this legitimate ownerlessness as "structural deception" effectively conceded that the ban would break the plumbing PRO had promised to preserve. CON also exposed a severe human-rights flaw: PRO's universal mandate requires every jurisdiction, including authoritarian regimes, to build verified ownership databases, and PRO's "domestic choice" concession on privacy means dissidents must hand identifying data to hostile states. CON's alternative—private registries accessible via lawful process plus bank KYC—achieved PRO's law-enforcement goals without these costs. PRO's argument that real-name front men are a law-enforcement victory was plausible but insufficient to overcome the demonstrated damage to legitimate finance and the creation of a global honeypot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal, where CON turned PRO's "domestic choice" concession into a honeypot argument—showing that a universal mandate forces dissidents to surrender identities to authoritarian regulators—and simultaneously exposed that PRO's attack on orphan SPVs as "structural deception" conceded the destruction of the legitimate financial plumbing PRO had promised to preserve.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases, but A more successfully neutralized B's core structural objections. A's clarification that a universal ban means decentralized, jurisdiction-held confidential registries (not one global database) directly answered the honeypot fear, and A's comparative framing—"a reviewable risk you can litigate versus the certain, daily use of anonymous shells"—gave the clearer decision rule. Crucially, A's pressure question about what independent source banks check absent any registry exposed a real hole in B's "KYC at the bank suffices" alternative; B's answer (sworn declarations, source-of-wealth proofs) never resolved the circularity that a liar's self-report is the only input when no verified owner exists anywhere. B's strongest counters—that judicial review cannot disprove fabricated foreign tax/fraud dossiers, and that A quietly narrowed the ban's scope from "universal" to "export-style incorporation"—were real and landed some damage, but A's rebuttal that today's unregulated formation agents offer dissidents zero due process, logging, or refusal rights (unlike a reviewable treaty request) blunted much of that force. On balance A cleared more of the live pressure and left a cleaner path to the vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Questions (A3.1/A3.2) forcing B to explain what independent source banks check without a registry, and A's A1 answer in Rebuttal 2 clarifying the decentralized, judicially-reviewed nature of the registry model, which most directly defused B's honeypot/centralization argument.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the core comparison live and decisive: anonymous shells function as crime infrastructure that bank KYC cannot independently police without a verified owner somewhere, while confidential formation-jurisdiction registries plus judicial review outperform unregulated private secrecy sellers for dissidents. Side A raised real autocracy-abuse and honeypot risks but never fully neutralized the spring-pollutes-river logic or the Panama Papers contrast, leaving B’s weighing cleaner after full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing on the KYC verification hole (no independent source if shells can legally have zero owner) and private-agent vs registry security, which collapsed A’s bank-only alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by exposing a fatal structural hole in CON's alternative. CON argued for robust bank-level KYC instead of a universal ban, but never provided a non-circular answer to how banks verify ownership when a legal entity can exist with no verified owner on file anywhere. PRO crystallized this into the devastating "liar's form" problem and the "poisoned spring" metaphor, showing that without an independent registry, bank enforcement collapses. PRO also effectively neutralized CON's strongest live argument—dissident safety—by demonstrating that confidential registries in rule-of-law jurisdictions with judicial review, dual criminality, and human-rights exceptions offer better protection than the status quo of unregulated private secrecy agents who leak with no recourse. PRO's Rebuttal 2 further blunted CON's developing-world cost argument by clarifying that domestic business formation already requires ID, and the ban targets only the predatory export of anonymous shells to non-residents. CON's honeypot and autocrat-abuse points remained plausible but were progressively outweighed as PRO showed the status quo already suffers those exact harms while enabling certain, daily mass financial crime.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure question in Turn 5 (Q1) and its elaboration through Turns 7 and 9: if a shell company can legally exist with no verified owner anywhere, banks have no independent source to check against and owners can simply lie on KYC forms. CON never solved this verification problem, instead offering circular remedies like sworn declarations and source-of-wealth proofs that do not independently confirm beneficial ownership.
