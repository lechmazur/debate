### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. It showed that knowing a ranking mechanism exists can meaningfully change how users interpret outcomes without revealing exploitable weights, and that audits are complementary rather than a substitute for user notice. Side A’s banner critique was sharp, but its marginal-gaming claim remained speculative and its demand for individualized information imposed more than the motion requires.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange separated meaning from exploitability: disclosure can explain that visibility is algorithmically filtered while hidden weights still prevent effective gaming. The credit-score analogy and the point that a non-ranking app would carry a different label directly answered Side A’s central uselessness and uniform-label objections.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully defending the psychological and market value of a simple disclosure while dismantling Side A's alternatives. Side A's "three audiences" defense in the closing was brilliant, but Side B systematically neutralized Side A's gaming argument and devastatingly flipped Side A's checkpoint analogy to prove why users, not just regulators, deserve the information.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing takedown of the checkpoint analogy, pointing out that dating app users are not threats but the very people being screened.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping the motion narrow (a bare existence notice) and forcing B into an unstable triad: the sentence is too trivial for users, strong enough to industrialize gaming, and important enough for regulators. B’s best pressure—shrinking consolation, identical banners, SEO farming—landed hard, but A answered the pressure questions cleanly, used B’s own “hidden weights / not mere existence” concession against the gaming claim, and left the cleaner residual path: the ranked person is the one party B wants kept dark. B never fully escaped that fairness comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 (A1/A2): redefines consolation as ending false certainty, turns inability to name a non-ranking competitor into the market case, then freezes B’s regulator-vs-user double standard.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won because the modest autonomy claim survived: knowing that an app actively ranks visibility is materially different from merely knowing it offers matching, even if that notice does not itself reveal criteria or prove bias. Side A sharply exposed B's overstated audit and complaint benefits, but its strongest harms either depended on a more detailed disclosure than the motion requires or remained speculative against the low burden of notice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B most effectively neutralized the scammer argument: if the disclosure only confirms that ranking occurs, it reveals no exploitable criteria, while Side A itself claimed sophisticated users already know ranking happens. That removed a major claimed victim cost without defeating the notice's autonomy value.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by systematically demonstrating that Side B's "modest" bare-notice proposal could not achieve any of Side B's claimed benefits. Side A's "cookie banner" and "nutrition facts" analogies perfectly exposed the gap between the sweeping harms Side B identified and the impotent remedy Side B defended.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal dismantling the nutrition label analogy ("Nutrition labels don't say 'this food contains ingredients.' They list the ingredients...") was devastating and anchored the rest of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash on the motion as written: bare always-on notice lacks the discriminating signal, reasons, and lever that make real notice rights work, so PRO’s accountability/autonomy payoff lives in the “house” they disavowed while defending only the “door.” A’s fork pressure and self-refutation hits were sharp, but B answered pressure directly, reversed the nutrition/sponsored analogies, and crystallised that the mandated sentence is either already inferred from “matching” or too thin to cure self-blame or enable complaints.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 nutrition/sponsored-content reverse plus B4.1–B4.2 adverse-action contrast: bare dating notice has none of the features that make those labels informative.
