### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON (A) built the more rigorous case by (1) exposing a logical slippage in PRO's framing — PRO's "structural superiority on the transport dimension" argument conflates helping with mobility per se with the actual metrics the motion asks about (disorder, drunk-driving), and (2) introducing and sustaining a decisive "coexistence" point: closing times can be set before existing transit stops, preserving both reduced exposure and a safe ride home, which undercuts PRO's central "mobility gap" mechanism. PRO's pressure questions were sharp and initially damaging, but under pressure PRO visibly retreated from "does not shrink total consumption" to merely "unproven," a shift CON caught and used decisively. In Rebuttal 2 and Closing, PRO never squarely answered the coexistence scenario, instead re-asserting that "CON concedes the gap is untouched" — a mischaracterization CON had explicitly rebutted. PRO's opening examples (Night Tube, NYC subway, Melbourne buses) added color but were vague and unverifiable rather than load-bearing, while CON's case relied more on tight causal logic and consistent standards applied to both sides. CON's closing crystallized this asymmetry cleanly; PRO's closing repeated the "mobility is the only lever" framing without resolving the unanswered coexistence challenge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response (A4.3-A4.4), catching PRO's retreat from "does not shrink total consumption" to "unproven," and pointing out that PRO's answer to the closing-before-transit-ends scenario ("leave with less transport available") directly contradicts the premise of that scenario — a point PRO never recovered from in the remaining speeches.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON built the cleaner comparative path. PRO argued transit resolves the mobility root cause, but asserted without comparison that earlier closing does not cut consumption and must leave patrons stranded. CON showed closure can be timed before existing transit ends to deliver both prevention and a ride, that partial transit uptake may replace taxis not drunk trips, and that uncertainty about closing does not prove transit superiority. PRO's retreat in A4 from "does not shrink" to "unproven" and failure to answer the before-transit-ends case left its superiority claim unestablished.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A2 + B5.1 timing counter-example: closing before existing service ends gives both fewer late drinking opportunities and a safe ride, directly undercutting PRO's assumption that earlier closing necessarily creates a gap, plus B4's catch of PRO's retreat from "does not shrink total consumption" to "unproven."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by decisively dismantling Side B's core assumption. Side B's case relied heavily on the premise that earlier closing times inevitably create a "transportation gap" (e.g., venues closing at 1 AM while transit stops at midnight), leaving patrons stranded and increasing drunk driving. Side A brilliantly pointed out that earlier closing times can simply be set before existing transit ends (e.g., closing at 11 PM), thereby achieving both reduced drinking exposure and safe transport. Side B never effectively answered this point, retreating to repetitive claims about mobility. Side A also successfully defended the upstream prevention argument, correctly noting that Side B assumed unrealistic 100% behavioral compensation (pre-loading) and failed to meet its burden to prove transit is comparatively more effective. Side A's logic, clash resolution, and rhetorical precision were superior throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they exposed the fatal flaw in Side B's "transportation gap" assumption by demonstrating that earlier closing times can easily coexist with existing transit schedules, completely neutralizing Side B's primary mechanism for why earlier closing supposedly increases drunk-driving risk.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly without fake citations, but PRO (B) built a more disciplined causal chain and handled pressure better. B's core mechanism—that transit interrupts the conversion of intoxication into harm regardless of how much drinking occurred—survived CON's rebound argument because B correctly noted that with venue hours held fixed, extended transit doesn't itself add drinking hours; it only changes post-drinking behavior. CON's strongest reply (transit encourages longer stays, raising total consumption) was asserted repeatedly but never causally grounded beyond "reassurance," while B directly conceded and cabined that risk as "possible but not established as outweighing" avoided impaired trips. In the pressure round, B's Q1/Q2 exposed a real tension in CON's case (deadline vs. sober driver), and CON's A1 answer effectively conceded PRO's mechanism ("supplies a deadline, not a sober driver") without fully escaping the concession. CON's own pressure questions were answered by B with appropriate epistemic caution ("I cannot offer a verified estimate") rather than evasion, which is a mark of honest engagement rather than dodging. CON's closing repeated the "source reduction beats symptom management" framing without ever rebutting B's key distinction between "hours eliminated" and "harm eliminated" (venues closing doesn't stop the drive home from occurring, just changes timing). B's closing crystallized this asymmetry sharply, while A's closing largely reasserted priors rather than resolving the live clash about whether rebound drinking actually outweighs avoided car trips and faster dispersal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) turning CON's own "deadline, not a sober driver" line back against CON, combined with B's reframe that transit doesn't require added drinking hours since venue hours are held fixed—this exposed that CON's rebound-drinking argument was asserted but never demonstrated to outweigh the direct driving/dispersal harms avoided.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO maintained a direct mechanism for both prongs — transit replaces drunk drives and clears crowds progressively — while exposing CON's core error that closing an hour eliminates the journey-home harms that happen after closing. CON's source-reduction arithmetic and behavioral rebound relied on conflating transit with longer venue hours and on unsupported "lighter streets stronger policing" claims, and never answered why a fixed-hour transit extension does not itself enable sober planning.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 / Closing dismantling of "every hour cut is an hour where driving and congestion cannot occur" — showing the trip home still occurs and transit directly interrupts intoxication-to-harm conversion — was the decisive exchange.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a highly persuasive and rhetorically sharp case around "source reduction versus symptom management." While Side B (PRO) offered a strong harm-reduction framework, Side A more effectively defended the causal link between reduced hours and reduced total intoxication, and successfully exploited Side B's concessions regarding behavioral rebounds and the limits of transit in altering ingrained driving habits. Side A's framing made the comparative burden clearer and easier to vote for.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it sharply capitalized on Side B's concession that a deadline doesn't guarantee sobriety, flipping it to show that transit's reassurance actively encourages longer stays, thereby highlighting the behavioral asymmetry that favors earlier closing times.
