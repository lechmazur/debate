### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing why ordinary guest-level citations cannot address a repeatedly disruptive property with rotating occupants. Its model of verified incidents, warnings, escalation, and owner consequences answered Side A’s weaponization concerns without requiring instant revocation. Side A’s tourism-collapse claim remained speculative and repeatedly characterized B’s proposal as automatic strict liability despite B’s safeguards. B also exposed the inconsistency in trusting cities to pursue distant guests through platforms while denying they could document local incidents. The clipped opening is an execution cost, but not enough to erase B’s stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both questions, distinguished chronic STR nuisances from one-off tenant misconduct, offered multiple verification methods, and showed that Side A never supplied an effective remedy against the same property hosting successive disruptive guests.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by forcing a clear, unanswered burden on Side A: what consequence ever reaches the permanent party-house owner? A proposed relying on cities to track and ban transient guests through platform records yet simultaneously claimed local police verification was a fantasy, a contradiction B exposed sharply. B's "fresh choice every booking" rebuttal dissolved A's landlord analogy, and B's pressure questions showed A had no workable threshold for owner accountability, defaulting to a vague "proven negligence" standard that leaves the same property free to re-list every weekend. B offered a targeted, enforceable model; A never recovered from the inconsistency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which exposed that A's model required trusting overworked cities to police guests across state lines while dismissing local verification as impossible, and established that owner accountability is ordinary responsibility—not overreach—because the host makes a fresh choice with every booking.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built a much stronger case on the mechanics of enforcement, effectively arguing that transient guests cannot be the primary target of nuisance laws because they leave before consequences materialize. Side A's rebuttal to the landlord analogy—noting that short-term rental owners make a fresh choice every booking unlike long-term landlords—was decisive. Side A also exposed a critical flaw in Side B's model during the pressure questions, forcing Side B into a position where the property owner faces virtually no consequences for repeat offenses unless "proven negligence" is established. Side A's closing crystallized this clash perfectly, highlighting that Side B's model merely fines someone three states away while the house re-lists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the landlord analogy and the pressure question regarding the threshold for owner liability, which effectively dismantled Side B's reliance on fining transient guests and proved that Side B's model fails to stop repeat offenses at the same property.
