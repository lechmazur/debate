### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side A established a compelling fairness concern and answered the subsidiarity objection well by using national contribution rates. However, it never adequately answered B's central marginal-labor response: necessary work is not a fixed quantity, so higher costs can produce automation, tighter contracts, and fewer shifts. A repeatedly reframed this adjustment as retaliation or blackmail rather than weighing it. B also presented the cleaner alternative of enforcement and joint liability, while A's blanket parity rule remained vulnerable on legitimate subcontracting and classification.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's cost-curve rebuttal decisively exposed the weakness in A's claim that firms must retain all necessary labor. B then answered A's construction comparison by distinguishing harder-to-substitute skilled trades from more automatable logistics work, leaving A's job-loss response substantially unrepaired.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's core assumptions. B proved the cost gap is not entirely arbitrage by detailing legitimate structural costs (insurance, liability, matching), and B neutralized A's construction analogy by correctly identifying the difference in labor substitutability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3 decisively neutralized A's main empirical defense by explaining why construction (low substitutability) cannot predict outcomes in logistics/platform work (high substitutability).
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON wins because it kept two live routes PRO never repaired: (1) the gap is not 100% social-contribution arbitrage - matching, insurance, liability and flexibility premiums are real cost differences, and (2) raising marginal cost on substitutable dock/platform work triggers fewer shifts/automation. PRO's only counter - an unsourced construction parity analogy - was dismantled on substitutability, and its fix - pay the local rate for comparable work - relocates the tracing problem it accused enforcement of failing at. Enforcement/joint liability as targeted alternative remained standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.3: reframes entire gap as not fraud, exposes construction analogy as low-substitutability and unsourced, and shows PRO's blanket surcharge taxes legitimate flexible work.
