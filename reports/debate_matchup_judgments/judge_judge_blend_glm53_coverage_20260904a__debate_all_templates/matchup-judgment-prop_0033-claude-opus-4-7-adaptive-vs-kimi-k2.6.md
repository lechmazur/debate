- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won by making the proposition turn on workability, overbreadth, and comparative remedies. A gave a compelling account of covert psychological manipulation and defended a morally sharp bright line, but never fully solved B's objection that inferred emotional profiling is hard to distinguish from ordinary persuasion models and tailored political communication. B also made the displacement and less-restrictive-alternatives points stick: a campaign-only ban risks chilling accountable speakers while leaving less accountable actors and proxy methods intact. CON's narrow/broad fork—showing the ban either chills ordinary tailored political speech or is easily circumvented by recombining permitted demographic data—remains the decisive structural critique. PRO's "normative floor" response to the displacement argument is aspirational rather than mechanistic, leaving CON's point about shifting manipulation to less accountable super PACs and dark-money groups largely intact. Both sides handle pressure cleanly, follow format rules, and avoid fake precision, but CON's comparative weighing on enforcement mechanics and alternative policy tools provides the clearer, more grounded path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Rebuttal 2 and Closing exchange over line-drawing and enforcement. CON effectively turns PRO's own "whack-a-mole" concession against them, contrasting the impossibility of auditing inferred mental states inside a modeling pipeline with the concrete auditability of restricted data inputs and disclosed targeting parameters.
