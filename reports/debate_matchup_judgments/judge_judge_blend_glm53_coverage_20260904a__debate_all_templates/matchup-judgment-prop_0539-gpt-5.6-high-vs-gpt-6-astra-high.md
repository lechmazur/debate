### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by drawing a sharp, decisive distinction between penalizing a noncompliant provider and declaring the underlying surrogacy arrangement illegal.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, answering Q2 by pointing out that domestic legislatures cannot force foreign courts or insurers to honor savings clauses for a contract that has been labeled "illegal."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A defended the proposition by distinguishing conditional commercial permission from unconditional participant protection, showing that the illegality label targets the transaction while preserving rights through statute. Side B’s attempt to collapse the distinction into mere provider regulation was blunted when A demonstrated that B also blocks unprotected transactions from proceeding, effectively accepting the operative condition while only disputing the label. A’s emergency-care example and the prospective-versus-continuing-protection frame gave the cleaner comparative warrant, and B’s cross-border uncertainty argument remained too speculative and symmetric to outweigh A’s concrete gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.2–A2.3 and A4.1–A4.2, where A showed that post-hoc damages are no substitute for secured emergency funds and that separating commercial permission from continuing protection is legally coherent and practically decisive.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won the core definitional clash.
