### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built genuinely strong cases—PRO's "availability/maintainability is the real bottleneck" frame against CON's "validation and accountability prevent active harm" frame—and neither fully dismantled the other's core mechanism. But on execution, PRO was cleaner: it properly answered CON's Pressure Questions with clear A1/A2 labels, and its own pressure questions (demanding evidence that IP pipelines deliver repair-dependent hardware, not just drugs/vaccines) exposed a gap CON never squarely closed. CON's Rebuttal 2 labeled "A1/A2" but actually continued litigating its own prior questions rather than directly answering PRO's Q1 (accountability evidence) and Q2 (naming a maintainable hardware class)—a real format and substance lapse that PRO explicitly flagged and CON never repaired. CON also suffered two clipped speeches (opening and closing), losing the tail end of its strongest asymmetry argument ("inert device vs. actively dangerous device") just as it was being crystallized. PRO's case progressed cleanly across rounds, kept pressure alive, and closed within limits with a coherent final frame. CON's safety/validation arguments were substantively strong and never fully neutralized, keeping this competitive, but the execution costs and unanswered pressure tip it to PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question Q2 in Turn 5 ("name one class of repair-dependent hardware IP pipelines made affordable and locally maintainable at scale")—which CON never directly answered despite restructuring its Rebuttal 2 around A1/A2 labels—was the most decisive exchange, since PRO explicitly called out the evasion in [A4.4] and CON never recovered the point.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A won by keeping the comparison focused on access and maintainability in low-income settings. B powerfully established that openness alone cannot supply validation, calibrated testing, or accountability, but never answered A’s required pressure questions about whether proprietary warranties function in these settings or which repair-dependent hardware class the traditional pipeline has made affordable and maintainable at scale. B instead relied partly on drugs and vaccines, then shifted from lives saved to harms avoided. Its repeated clipping and failure to begin Rebuttal 2 by answering A’s questions were meaningful execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s calibrated-equipment challenge sharply exposed the weakness in A’s claim that public specifications enable local verification. However, A’s response that proprietary devices face the same missing infrastructure—and B’s failure to demonstrate superior hardware delivery and maintenance—was ultimately more decisive on the motion.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by demonstrating that IP-protected hardware fails to save lives in low-income settings when it cannot be maintained. Side B suffered a severe execution cost by completely evading Side A's Pressure Questions, failing to name a single class of IP hardware that remains maintainable in these contexts. Side B's closing concession that a bricked IP device "saves no one" cedes the core metric of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R1 distinction between drugs (which scale well via IP pipelines) and hardware (which require local repair ecosystems), effectively neutralizing Side B's primary empirical defense of the status quo.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B built a sharper, more consistent mechanism throughout the debate: reframing "deployment" to require calibration, liability, and safety, and distinguishing visible failure (broken branded devices, a known problem) from invisible failure (a miscalibrated open-source copy that "quietly under-delivers"). This mechanism was never squarely neutralized by A. B's pressure questions ("who funds traceable standards and technician salaries," "which life-saving device has been cleared at scale via open-source alone") extracted a damaging concession from A ("No single life-saving device has achieved that perfect profile through open-source alone... I will not pretend otherwise"), which A then tried to reframe as a "trajectory" argument rather than repair the underlying comparative claim. A's own best example (OpenFlexure) was also conceded by A itself to work because of institutional partnerships funding calibration — which B immediately turned into proof that industrial capacity, not IP-freedom, is doing the life-saving work. A's case also suffered execution costs: two rebuttal turns were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off argument development at critical junctures. B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly and reused the "whisper vs. break" framing memorably, while A's closing largely reasserted earlier claims without resolving the invisible-failure risk or the concession's damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q1 asking for a concrete life-saving device deployed via open-source alone) forcing A's explicit concession in Rebuttal 2 ("No single life-saving device has achieved that perfect profile through open-source alone"), which B then leveraged decisively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to argue that PRO's own best example proves industrial/regulatory capacity—not IP-freedom—is the operative variable.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by making safe, sustained deployment the decisive metric and showing that open designs do not themselves supply calibration, quality systems, servicing, or traceable accountability. A persuasively argued that open source can coexist with regulated manufacturers and that proprietary devices often remain broken, exposing B's overstated claim that openness necessarily eliminates revenue or liability. However, A conceded the absence of a scaled, maintained high-acuity example and never fully established that lower licensing and repair barriers outweigh validation risks. B also benefited from sharper crystallization and A's two clipped rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was pressing A for a cleared, calibrated, maintained life-saving device at scale, then using A's concession to distinguish emergency prototypes and the lower-acuity microscope example from reliable clinical deployment.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around the hidden risks of uncalibrated medical devices. While Side A effectively proved that traditional IP pipelines fail to provide maintenance and access in low-income settings, Side B established that broken equipment (visible failure) is less dangerous than uncalibrated open-source equipment (invisible failure). Side B also neutralized Side A's examples by pointing out that the microscope is low-acuity and COVID ventilators were desperation measures, forcing Side A to concede a lack of scaled, life-saving open-source successes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's distinction between the visible failure of a microscope (blurry image) and the invisible, lethal failure of an uncalibrated oxygen concentrator dismantled Side A's primary example of open-source safety validation.
