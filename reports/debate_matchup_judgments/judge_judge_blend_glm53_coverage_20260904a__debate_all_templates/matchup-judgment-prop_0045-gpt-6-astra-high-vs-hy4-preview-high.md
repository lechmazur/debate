### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides argued at a high level, but the decisive exchange centered on whether A's "record stated preferences, not personality types" alternative was functionally identical to DISC/MBTI (B's claim) or meaningfully different (A's claim). A's distinction — that a direct question like "do you prefer written or verbal feedback" is a face-valid, task-specific statement requiring no inferential leap, whereas MBTI/DISC infer a general trait from indirect items and then apply that inference across contexts — survives B's "it's just DISC without shorthand" rebuttal, which never explains why coding a direct answer carries the same validity burden as coding an inferred trait. A's closing dichotomy ("if the profile guides attention, its weak validity matters; if it doesn't, the test is superfluous") crystallizes this cleanly and is never squarely answered by B, who instead re-asserts the regulation-not-abolition frame without resolving the unequal-scrutiny concern (that a weak trait label triggers different follow-up questions for different candidates). B's reframing (predictive validity is the wrong metric for coordination use; supplementary hiring use needs no independent validity) was clever and forced real concessions from A, and B's pressure questions were sharp, but B's own answers under pressure leaned on analogy and relabeling more than on defeating A's core distinction. That leaves A with the cleaner, more resolved path to the motion by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.4 distinction between "recording a stated preference" and "assigning an interpretive personality type" directly rebuts B's "DISC without shorthand" claim [B4.3], and A's closing dichotomy in [A5.4] forces a choice B never cleanly answers.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO won by locking the decisive dilemma: if labels still direct attention or expectations, low predictive validity still matters; if they do not, the useful work is already done by direct questions and observed conduct, so the tests add unearned distortion. CON’s “regulate, don’t abolish” reframe and taxonomy-rebuild point were sharp and consistent, but PRO repeatedly showed residual skew from “focusing” evidence and cleanly separated recorded preferences from personality-type interpretation, making the stop-use comparison easier to decide under the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 / Closing: residual “distorted attention” plus the structure-with-inference vs structure-without-it comparison, answering CON’s non-decisional safeguard.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A wins because it showed that even supplementary use of low-validity personality labels distorts attention in hiring and team-building, and that employers can preserve all genuine benefits—structured conversation, comparability, and audit trails—through direct questions about concrete work preferences without the unsupported inferential overlay of MBTI/DISC types. Side B’s “regulation not abolition” frame and “filing system” analogy were sharp, but never overcame the motion’s central logic: if the labels lack predictive validity, their authority in employment contexts is unearned, and Side A demonstrated they are replaceable by structurally equivalent but epistemically cleaner practices.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 (A4), which cleanly separated recording stated preferences from assigning explanatory personality types, neutralized Side B’s claim that abolishing the tests would sacrifice audit trails and shared taxonomy, and established that the useful work is done by direct structured questions rather than the tests themselves.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO built a tighter, more self-reinforcing case. It secured an early, largely unrebutted concession on hiring (CON's B1.7 bans type-based vetoes and permanent role assignments, which A repeatedly and correctly frames as conceding the hiring half of the motion), then narrowed the fight to team-building. There, PRO's "just ask" counterexample and its pressure question (Q1, Turn 5) forced CON into a costly admission in B4.1 — CON could not cite any comparison showing MBTI/DISC outperforms a label-free discussion, conceding this is "possible usefulness, not established superiority." PRO exploited that admission decisively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, turning CON's own "earn its place" standard against it. CON's distinction between predictive validity for individual performance and effectiveness as a team intervention was a smart and original reframe, and its epistemic honesty in Rebuttal 2 was commendable, but that same honesty undercut its substantive case: by its own admission, the sole surviving defense (team-building) had not "earned its place" either. CON's closing reasserted the predictor/intervention distinction but never repaired the evidentiary gap A had exposed. Both sides argued cleanly with no fake citations and strong structural discipline, but PRO ended with the cleaner, more complete route to the resolution as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Rebuttal 2 exchange around A's Pressure Question 1 (Turn 5) and CON's answer in B4.1 ("I cannot cite a controlled comparison... this limits my claim to possible usefulness, not established superiority") was decisive — it forced CON to concede the missing evidence for its only remaining defense (team-building), which PRO then leveraged in both Rebuttal 2 and Closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by narrowing the live dispute to MBTI/DISC specifically, locking in CON’s hiring concessions, and forcing CON’s own “earn its place” standard against empty developmental upside. CON’s predictor-vs-intervention distinction was real and well framed, but never showed residual value for the named tools beyond a plain preference talk, while PRO kept the stereotype risk and label redundancy live. After pressure answers, PRO closed the cleaner vote: stop failed instruments; keep the norm without the horoscope.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 on the outline example: the working agreement comes from asking about preferences, not type labels; CON then admitted no controlled comparison showing MBTI/DISC beats label-free discussion under its own earn-place rule.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by narrowing the debate to instruments whose type-label unreliability and lack of incremental validity make them unfit for both hiring and team-building. Side B’s distinction between selection and development was conceptually interesting, but Side A neutralized it by showing that the only valuable output in team-building (working agreements, surfaced preferences) is obtainable without the test, while the type label is the sole non-duplicable ingredient and carries stereotyping risk. Crucially, Side B conceded the hiring half and admitted in Rebuttal 2 that no controlled comparison shows MBTI/DISC outperforming a label-free discussion. Under Side B’s own “earn its place” standard, that admission is fatal: the tools remain as optional conversation starters that add no demonstrated increment. Side A crystallized this cleanly across the later speeches, while Side B’s closing merely repeated the prediction-versus-intervention distinction without supplying the missing developmental evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 and closing, which used Side B’s own “earn its place” criterion against it: because Side B admitted no controlled evidence shows MBTI/DISC beats a plain label-free conversation, and because the type label is the only part not duplicable by simply asking about work preferences, the instruments fail even the bounded use Side B proposed.
