### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash whether expectation adds anything beyond rights, services, and natural incentives. A had a plausible pro case and a good distinction between integration and assimilation, but relied heavily on the asserted link that expectation creates support infrastructure. B repeatedly attacked that link, argued services can do the protective work without stigma, and crystallized the harms of vague expectation framing more cleanly. A's Germany and Canada answers kept the debate competitive, but B's final comparison was clearer and A's clipped closing cost some execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was answering A's expectation-support claim: B argued that the protective goods A named come from funded classes, interpreters, shelters, and enforceable rights, while expectation rhetoric is what enables loyalty tests, headscarf bans, and accent policing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully isolating the 'expectation' framing as a harmful surcharge while attributing all of Side A's cited benefits to baseline rights and state-funded infrastructure. Side B's final comparative weighing decisively neutralized Side A's 'internal brake' argument by showing that universal liberal principles do not require an immigrant-specific expectation to function.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which brilliantly turns Side A's 'internal brake' argument by pointing out that liberal principles already bind and protect everyone, rendering the proposition's immigrant-specific expectation redundant and isolating it as a purely harmful 'surcharge.'
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): CON successfully undermined PRO's load-bearing claim that expectation generates the political will to fund support, showing instead that high-expectation rhetoric in the UK, France, and Netherlands accompanied funding cuts, while support scales when framed as a public good. CON also exposed that PRO's "core norms" either collapse into existing liberal rights that already bind everyone—making the expectation framing redundant—or are vulnerable to majoritarian abuse via ordinary legislation, with no neutral standard inside PRO's framework to prevent it. PRO's closing was clipped and failed to demonstrate that expectation causally produces benefits rather than merely correlating with them in selective cases, leaving CON's "keep support, drop the surcharge" frame as the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's historical comparison showing expectation rhetoric rises as integration funding falls, and its turning of PRO's Canada example into evidence that rights-based access scales without adoptive-expectation discourse.
