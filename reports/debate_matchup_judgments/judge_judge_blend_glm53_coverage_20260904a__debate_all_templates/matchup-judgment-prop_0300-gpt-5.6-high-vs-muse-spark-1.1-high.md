- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but the decisive late exchange favored CON. PRO's answer to A1 (B4.1) conceded that an apology is "not a contractual promise of unlimited compensation" and that recognition matters even if compensation "follows imperfectly" — CON seized on this in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, framing it as proof that PRO's model risks exactly the hollow, unfunded symbolic gesture PRO itself condemned ("words powerful enough to trigger claims but too careless to guarantee justice"). PRO's closing did not directly answer this charge, instead re-asserting the moral urgency argument, leaving a live route open for CON. CON also offered a more concrete rebuttal to the "roadmap to never" charge by specifying a bounded, timetabled sequencing mechanism (fact-finding, claimant participation, legislated framework, then apology), which PRO answered only by reasserting "that is indefinite delay" without engaging the specifics. PRO's opening and early rebuttals were rhetorically strong and morally forceful (the corporation/state distinction, the Australia example, the "truth vs. denial" framing), but CON's distributive-justice point (litigation-ready claimants vs. undocumented victims) and hollow-promise critique went materially unanswered by the end. CON's case suffered execution costs from two clipped turns, which cost it some rhetorical polish and left some conclusions truncated, but the core substantive arguments were delivered before the cuts. On balance, CON built the clearer, more resolved path to victory by the end of the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3) turning PRO's own answer to Q1 — that an apology remains honest even if compensation is later denied — into evidence that PRO's model produces exactly the hollow, unenforceable recognition PRO criticized in CON's "responsible sequencing," a point PRO's closing did not squarely answer.
