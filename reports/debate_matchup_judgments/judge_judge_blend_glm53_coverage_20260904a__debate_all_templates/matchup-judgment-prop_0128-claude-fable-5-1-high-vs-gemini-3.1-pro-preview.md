### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by distinguishing considering a degree from making it an automatic requirement. That preserves the credential’s signaling value while allowing experience, certifications, and demonstrated skills to compete. B raised credible concerns about screening costs and inexperienced applicants, but repeatedly asserted without sufficient support that a degree is the only scalable, objective proof of reliability. A’s clipped first rebuttal and incorrect C-reference labels were execution costs, but did not erase the stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 decisively answered B’s newcomer challenge: experience screens already coexist with degree screens, graduates retain their degree as a positive signal, and removing the categorical bar only expands consideration. B’s claim that alternative filters would become harsher did not establish why a four-year diploma must remain mandatory.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): B won by staying tighter to the motion as written: if a degree is not needed for performance, a mandatory auto-reject is harder to justify than a preference. A’s soft-skills/floodgate/nepotism case was competitive and forced real work on newcomers and volume hiring, but it increasingly defended convenience and residual screening rather than necessity. B’s preference-versus-requirement distinction, work-history counter to the soft-skills proxy, and unanswered “why specifically a diploma” pressure made the choice clearer by the end. A remained credible on mass-hiring logistics, so the win is clear but not dominant; B’s clipped rebuttal was a mild execution cost that did not erase the comparative edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2): degree can remain a plus while ending auto-reject, plus the “proves too much” soft-skills turn and unanswered diploma-specificity burden.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by sharply distinguishing between a degree as a "preference" (which PRO allows) and a "requirement" (which the motion targets). Side B effectively neutralized Side A's soft-skills and floodgate arguments by pointing out that work history is a better proxy and that HR convenience does not justify auto-rejecting qualified candidates when the motion stipulates the degree is unnecessary for performance. Side A's case ultimately collapsed into defending the filter's convenience rather than the credential's necessity, a concession Side B exploited masterfully in the closing. Side B's Rebuttal 1 was clipped, a minor execution cost, but their overall argumentative progression and rhetorical framing were dominant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side A's "newcomer" and "soft skills" arguments by pointing out that work history proves perseverance better than a degree, and that removing the hard requirement doesn't erase the degree's value as a "plus" for graduates, thereby exposing Side A's defense as purely about HR convenience.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won by defending the proposition's actual distinction: employers may assess competence and still recognize degrees without making degree completion an exclusionary prerequisite. B offered sharp criticism of alternative filters, but repeatedly treated ending a requirement as ignoring or devaluing degrees, which the motion does not entail. B also never adequately established that referrals and nepotism must replace structured assessments. A's equity, applicant-cost, and future-incentive weighing therefore remained stronger, though unsupported precision and overclaims about cheap validated tests narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to B's sunk-cost argument was decisive: tuition already paid by current graduates does not answer how unnecessary requirements pressure future applicants into costly degrees. This exposed B's failure to justify maintaining the prerequisite prospectively.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by making the motion’s own “genuinely necessary” test unworkable for PRO and then converting PRO’s concessions into a cleaner comparative: every filter is a proxy, certifications fail the same test PRO applies to degrees, and dropping the degree line for the admin/sales cases PRO relies on leaves referrals and unvalidated screens. PRO’s equity story stayed powerful but was never fully rescued from CON’s inversion that removal redistributes sideways to the networked while devaluing the public ladder. PRO’s R2 answers and closing restated ideals more than they resolved who actually gets sorted when the line falls. A’s clipped R2 was an execution cost, but the clash still favored CON’s live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s capture of B4.1/A4.6–A5.4: PRO’s CompTIA move (trait necessary, parchment not) identically saves the degree, so the motion arbitrarily fells only one proxy.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger, more logically rigorous case. A successfully attacked the "genuinely necessary" standard, proving that all hiring filters are proxies, and defended the degree as a highly effective, hard-to-fake proxy for conscientiousness. A's most decisive move was reframing the equity debate: dropping the degree removes the "public ladder" (a credential anyone can earn without connections) and leaves "private ladders" (referrals, networks), which disproportionately benefit the privileged. Side B struggled to defend the exact text of the motion, shifting to "historically unneeded," and offered weak alternatives (e.g., Salesforce quizzes) that A easily dismantled as unvalidated proxies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of PRO's CompTIA vs. Degree distinction. When PRO argued CompTIA skills are necessary while degree coursework isn't, A pointed out that the trait the degree certifies (conscientiousness/finishing hard things) is also necessary, meaning both credentials stand or fall by PRO's own logic. A also sharply countered PRO's "60% auto-rejected" claim by clarifying that the degree protects the working-class graduate from losing out to the connected non-graduate for marginal middle-class roles.
