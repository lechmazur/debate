### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that open demand incentives primarily expand the incumbent’s order book rather than create domestic capacity. Norway, Hungary, Sweden, and China formed a coherent comparison, while A never supplied the requested example of neutral demand policy producing a domestic cell maker. A persuasively established that demand is necessary and producer support can waste money, but B’s sunsetted output-credit model answered that risk without conceding demand should be favored.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Hungary response was decisive: A’s example of demand attracting European investment involved foreign producers receiving supply-side incentives, while the indigenous Swedish champion failed. This sharply undermined A’s claimed path from demand to durable domestic industry.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by making the live decision rule clearer: pure demand adopts clean tech by buying incumbents’ capacity (Norway; Europe’s import path), while capacity that exists grew under producer support, so “favor demand over large producer subsidies” fails the strategic goal on the table. A’s case was strong on stranded plants, fiscal drains, and rented dependence (Hungary/CATL), and A answered pressure with externality pricing rather than a passport whitelist. But B repeatedly forced the comparison A never escaped—A’s best Europe examples were bankrupt (Northvolt) or producer-lured foreign plants, and “smart demand” on cycle life/range/carbon was turned into either winner-picking that still favors today’s leader or local-content subsidies by another name. B’s two clipped turns are a real execution cost and keep the margin from being larger; still, after full exchange B left the cleaner path to vote CON: demand alone does not summon owned or even in-jurisdiction cell makers, and the version of demand that does effectively pays producers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s capture of A’s Hungary example (B2.1–B2.3) and the later squeeze that A’s ideal “CATL builds clean cells in Europe” outcome is exactly the producer-paid plant A called dependence, while pure-demand Norway got nothing—plus the unanswered empty-set challenge that no open neutral demand program produced a domestic cell maker.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by demonstrating that every existing battery industry was built with producer support, while PRO could not name a single open, chemistry-neutral demand program that created a domestic cell maker. The unanswered B3.2 challenge, combined with CON's co-option of Hungary and Norway as counter-examples, left PRO with elegant theory but no empirical path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (B2) turned PRO's European examples against them by showing Hungarian plants were lured by producer grants, and the standing B3.2 challenge—demanding one open demand program that produced a domestic cell maker—remained unanswered through closing.
