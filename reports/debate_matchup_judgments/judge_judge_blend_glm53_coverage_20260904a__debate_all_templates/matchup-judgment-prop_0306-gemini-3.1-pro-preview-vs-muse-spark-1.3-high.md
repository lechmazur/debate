### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a more resilient case by repeatedly turning PRO's own concessions and proposals against it. When PRO conceded the "ark" function works but proposed off-grid breeding centers instead of public zoos, CON immediately exploited this in rebuttal and pressure questions, forcing PRO to explain how such centers would be funded without visitor revenue — PRO's answer ("grants, NGOs, philanthropy") was answered convincingly by CON's point that grant funding is unreliable and lacks the sustained public constituency zoos provide. CON's Q2 about habitat that is actively burning or war-torn also exposed a real gap: PRO's proposed "guarded in-situ sanctuaries" don't solve the immediate rescue problem, and CON hammered this unanswered tension into the closing ("you cannot run a guarded sanctuary inside a war zone"). PRO's case, while well-argued on education/accessibility grounds (documentaries vs. costly zoo visits) and on ethical framing (captivity as harm requiring justification), never fully closed the funding-reliability and urgency loopholes CON kept pressing. PRO's Rebuttal 2 was also flagged as clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost that cut off its closing argument mid-thought. Both sides argued with grounded, plausible examples without resorting to fake precision, but CON's answers to pressure were tighter, its framing ("necessity remains because nothing else keeps life and love alive together") more memorable, and its rebuttals did more direct damage to PRO's proposed alternatives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn of PRO's "off-grid breeding centers" concession into a self-defeating admission (A2.1, reinforced in A3.1 and A4.3) was the most decisive exchange — it exposed that PRO's alternative to zoos still depends on the same funding/political support problem PRO says zoos solve, and PRO's answer in Rebuttal 2 never fully neutralized this pressure before running out of room.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON (Side A) built the stronger path to victory by showing that the proposition's conditional clause—"when immersive digital tools and habitat protection can do the job"—was not satisfied. CON demonstrated that public exhibition creates a steady, reliable funding stream and political will that grants and screens cannot replicate, that habitat protection fails when ecosystems collapse from war, disease, or fire, and that PRO's alternative model of off-grid breeding centers lacked a convincing funding mechanism. PRO's repeated "inefficient middleman" charge was squarely answered by CON's "reliable river" reframe, which PRO never adequately addressed. PRO's Turn 7 being clipped for length and its closing largely recapping earlier points without sharpening the comparative tradeoff further weakened its position.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal that PRO's funding model assumes zoo revenue exists without zoos—a "small slice of a reliable river beats a promised flood that never comes when attention shifts"—decisively blunted PRO's economic alternative and exposed the unmet burden in PRO's replacement model.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's proposed alternatives. While Side A argued for off-grid breeding centers and in-situ sanctuaries, Side B effectively exposed the practical and financial flaws in these ideas, pointing out that grants are fickle and guarded sanctuaries are impossible in active war zones. Side B also provided a much stronger defense of the economic and empathetic necessity of public exhibition, contrasting the "reliable river" of zoo funding with the "promised flood" of distant donations. Side A's reliance on digital tools and idealized habitat protection felt abstract and underfunded compared to Side B's grounded, realistic defense of modern zoos.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered the pressure questions by reframing the "inefficiency" charge as a misunderstanding of how conservation funding works, and decisively dismantled Side A's unrealistic proposal for "guarded in-situ sanctuaries" in war zones.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but B more effectively adapted across rounds. B's key strategic move—conceding that specialized breeding centers may be necessary while sharply distinguishing them from ticket-funded display zoos—forced A into defending a "you must exhibit thousands of non-releasable animals to fund the ark" position that reads uncomfortably like captivity-for-revenue, and B exploited this in Rebuttal 2 and Closing ("concession decides the debate"). A's central counter—that public zoos are financially inseparable from the arks they fund—was asserted repeatedly ("you cannot amputate the public zoo") but never mechanistically rebutted B's seed-bank/NGO funding analogy. B also landed a clean, largely unanswered hit on the education point: that watching stereotypic pacing teaches domination rather than respect, whereas A's "visceral proximity" claim was reasserted but not defended against that specific charge in later rounds. A retained a genuinely live, underexploited point on zoo research (vaccine testing, reproductive baselines) that B dropped after Rebuttal 1, which kept A's case from collapsing entirely, but this alone wasn't enough to offset B's sharper framing of the core necessity test embedded in the motion and B's more effective use of the Pressure Questions exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 turn (B4.3-B4.4), where B reframed A's "ark needs zoo funding" defense as a concession that display zoos confine thousands of non-releasable animals purely for revenue, directly weaponizing A's own argument against the motion's ethical-necessity framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing the ethical contradiction at the heart of modern zoos: thousands of non-endangered, non-releasable animals are held in lifelong captivity primarily to generate entertainment revenue that subsidizes a small fraction of genuine conservation work. While CON effectively argued that zoos convert casual spending into conservation funding, they could not independently justify the display zoo as a conservation or education institution without conceding its core business model depends on captivity. PRO showed that immersive digital tools and habitat protection can fulfill education and conservation with less suffering, and that specialized breeding centers could persist without the costly display infrastructure. The remaining ethical cost of the modern zoo exceeds its claimed necessity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (B4), which reframed the display zoo as holding "sentient hostages" to subsidize conservation and argued that genuine arks are far cheaper without public exhibits, directly neutralizing CON's claim that public zoos are financially inseparable from conservation breeding.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by effectively separating the ethical burden of the prompt from Side B's pragmatic funding arguments. A's decisive move was forcing B to admit that public zoos keep thousands of non-endangered animals in captivity primarily to generate ticket revenue to subsidize the few endangered "ark" species. A brilliantly framed this as "captivity for revenue" and "holding sentient hostages," which fatally undermined B's ethical justification. A also provided a clean, plausible alternative for funding closed breeding centers (comparing them to seed banks funded by NGOs/governments), neutralizing B's strongest practical attack.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where A answers B's pressure questions by comparing closed breeding facilities to seed banks that don't require public display to be funded, and sharply turns B's funding defense into an ethical indictment by pointing out that B's model requires lifelong confinement of non-endangered animals purely to skim entertainment budgets.
