### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won by showing that containment, treatment, restitution, public acknowledgment of harm, and procedural safeguards can exist without criminal conviction. That left Side B relying mainly on the symbolic value of guilt, while insufficiently answering A's claims about diminished culpability, stigma, criminal records, and biased capacity assessments. B's warning about opaque welfare detention was strong, but A directly accepted and incorporated counsel, proof, appeal, review, and release limits into secure care.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer that procedural rights come from procedure rather than the penal label decisively weakened B's claim that criminal conviction was needed to make coercive intervention lawful and bounded. B then characterized the remaining difference as semantic, allowing A to clarify that blame and lasting criminal stigma were precisely the substantive harms at issue.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO established that criminal conviction before fourteen adds nothing indispensable that well-resourced civil secure care cannot supply—containment, treatment, restitution, and even public acknowledgment of harm—while the penal label introduces disproportionate communal blame and a lifelong record for children whose impulse control and future orientation remain developmentally unstable. CON’s rebuttable presumption never overcame PRO’s critique that case-by-case capacity findings invite race- and class-inflected bias, and when CON conceded that civil detention could borrow criminal procedural safeguards, their insistence on the criminal "label" collapsed into a demand for condemnation rather than accountability. PRO’s closing crystallized this by showing that restraint without guilt is the precise demand of proportionality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s sustained demonstration across B2, B4 and B5 that civil secure care can replicate containment, public harm-findings, and procedural rights, leaving CON unable to explain why the unique stigma of criminal guilt (rather than bounded, non-penal accountability) is indispensable for under-fourteens.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained the decisive distinction between restraint and penal blame. CON's claim that low ages are needed for rights and victim recognition was neutralized by B's showing that secure care can be given counsel, proof, appeal, and clear release criteria, and can publicly name the harm and order repair without a criminal record. CON's rebuttable-presumption safeguard remained vulnerable to race- and class-inflected maturity findings, while the fourteen floor offered a fairer, administrable bright line that still allows coercive, accountable intervention.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 A2 and Closing: rights come from procedure not the penal label, and a conviction is not therapy-delivery but community blame—restraint without guilt separates necessary containment from disproportionate condemnation.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by showing that a low age need not entail full adult-style culpability: an incapacity presumption, act-specific proof, and proportionate youth measures offer a credible middle course. A persuasively defended bright lines and stigma reduction, but often treated any criminal adjudication as branding a child a finished offender and never fully established why its protective process would outperform a safeguarded youth court rather than merely replicate it under another name.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was the argument that A’s alternative still permits coercive confinement while requiring many of the same safeguards already supplied by youth court. A answered well by proposing fixed limits and judicial protections, although B weakened its follow-up by continuing to describe that system as necessarily open-ended despite those explicit safeguards.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON exposed the motion's false dilemma: jurisdictions with low minimum ages already use youth courts with doli incapax and child-focused disposals, not adult retribution. CON's most decisive move was turning PRO's welfare alternative against itself by showing that removing criminal jurisdiction would likely subject children to open-ended, clinician-driven confinement without proof beyond reasonable doubt or fixed release dates, replacing accountable courts with less accountable predictive welfare discretion. PRO's abstract assurance that safeguards could simply be "built in" never overcame this concrete institutional critique, and PRO's stigma argument was insufficient to outweigh the comparative threat to liberty and procedural rights.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 demonstration that PRO's welfare alternative would likely result in open-ended confinement renewed on predictive "risk" with fewer procedural protections than a youth court—turning the "humane" welfare frame into a more coercive and less accountable system.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON wins by redefining the clash: low ages do not equal fully punishable adult treatment but youth courts with doli incapax, proof beyond reasonable doubt, and proportionate limits. B's bright-line and stigma arguments were answered by A showing welfare alternatives risk open-ended risk-based confinement and displace victim acknowledgment. PRO's promise to build safeguards in B4 was not shown to be superior to safeguards youth courts already provide, leaving CON's accountability route live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.3-A4.3 - welfare therapeutic confinement can be renewed on clinician risk without verdict or fixed maximum, while youth court ties coercion to proven act and capacity finding, making the 14-line less protective.
