### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): CON built the stronger cumulative path to victory through three compounding moves. First, CON systematically turned PRO's own examples back: South Australia still runs gas peakers for around 20% of supply during low renewable output, and Texas's 2021 blackouts were partly caused by the renewable failures PRO cited as a success. Germany's coal restarts—dismissed as a "geopolitical blip"—exposed a structural vulnerability CON had predicted, and France's maintenance outage was explained plausibly as a COVID backlog rather than a systemic flaw, with France still showing lower per-capita emissions than Germany even during the worst period. Second, CON effectively seized on PRO's significant concession in Rebuttal 2 that "in the near term, some backup remains necessary during the most extreme weather events." PRO framed this as an edge case manageable by geography and emerging storage, but CON correctly identified it as the core decarbonization problem: eliminating fossil use during multi-week lulls is not a footnote—it is the goal. PRO's answer relied on technologies (long-duration storage, hydrogen, compressed air) that PRO conceded are not yet deployed at scale, whereas nuclear already delivers proven dispatchable zero-carbon power. Third, PRO's closing—its best rhetorical moment with the "gap-filler vs. central pillar" reframe—was clipped before completion, reducing its impact, and CON's clean, focused closing answered the reframe directly: if filling that fossil gap is the entire point of decarbonization policy, nuclear filling that gap makes it central by definition. PRO's cost and deployment-speed arguments remained real and were never fully neutralized, and the SMR challenge landed (NuScale cancellation was a fair point CON partially addressed with the China example). But the intermittency problem for large, non-interconnected grids remained a live, unanswered burden for PRO across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's Rebuttal 2 capitalizing on PRO's near-term fossil concession. PRO admitted "some backup remains necessary during the most extreme weather events," intending it as a narrow qualifier. CON reframed it as an outright concession that full decarbonization without nuclear is impossible today—the very gap that defines the mission. This turned PRO's honest acknowledgment into a structural admission that nuclear currently is necessary, precisely what the motion denies, and CON maintained this pressure effectively through the closing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): A wins because its rebuttals and direct answers to pressure questions exposed B's France example as currently failing and kept the focus on speed and deployment realities, while B introduced late widening claims and could not neutralize the central-strategy vs gap-filler distinction crystallized in A's closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2's handling of B's France example and lifespan comparison directly damaged B's core reliability and longevity route while answering both pressure questions up front.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by effectively exploiting Side B's concession that renewable grids require near-term fossil backup for extreme weather lulls. A directly tied this admission back to the motion, arguing that if renewables cannot yet deliver full decarbonization without fossils, nuclear remains necessary as a central dispatchable pillar. A also handled pressure questions cleanly, contextualized the France outages persuasively, and delivered a tight, unclipped closing that crystallized the clash. B's cost and speed arguments were strong, and the gap-filler versus pillar reframe was rhetorically clever, but it did not fully neutralize A's substantive point about the decarbonization gap, and B's clipped closing incurred an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which seized on Side B's admission of necessary fossil backup to argue that nuclear is still essential for actual decarbonization, directly flipping B's concession into a winning link to the motion.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): CON wins on three decisive grounds. First, the 4-hour storage gap was never adequately closed by PRO. PRO's A4.1 answer cited lifecycle cost comparisons for "100+ hours of long-duration storage" that are clearly forward-looking projections rather than present realities, and the proposition is framed in the present tense. CON's core rebuttal—that the full-system storage solution PRO's reliability case requires does not yet exist at scale—remained live through the closing. Second, PRO's single most important factual claim in rebuttal 2, that EU-wide wind output "never drops below 12% of total installed capacity" even in the worst winter cold snaps, is brittle fake precision that deserves epistemic discount; it was unsupported and suspiciously exact. Third, CON's temporal framing in the closing ("the motion does not ask whether nuclear will someday become unnecessary—it asks whether it is unnecessary today") was accurate and sharp, correctly narrowing the proposition rather than evading it. PRO had live strengths: the speed-and-cost-overrun argument is real, the opportunity-cost framing (every year of delayed emissions matters) was never fully neutralized, and CON's own acknowledgment that no large economy has fully decarbonized via nuclear undermined the reliability-proof claim. But PRO's rebuttal ultimately rested on falling cost trajectories and contested projections, while CON correctly kept the debate anchored to current deployable reality. The clipped ending of CON's second rebuttal is a small execution cost but did not cost CON the key arguments already made. "Reliance on projections is not a decarbonization strategy. Nuclear is." crystallizes the winning comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's attack in Rebuttal 1 on PRO's 4-hour storage figure was the most decisive exchange. PRO had cited a 20–40% cost advantage "even with four hours of storage," and CON immediately exposed that four hours is irrelevant to the seasonal, multi-week reliability gap in northern climates. PRO never produced a system-level cost comparison using genuine long-duration seasonal storage, and that gap remained open through the closing, undermining the entire economic pillar of PRO's case.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): A built the stronger overall case by consistently centering speed, total system cost, and climate urgency while directly answering pressure questions on storage costs and weather correlation; B's reliability arguments were undercut by admissions that no large nuclear-heavy grid has decarbonized and by the clipped rebuttal 2 that exceeded limits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's direct answers on full-lifecycle costs with 100+ hour storage plus EU interconnection data neutralized B's winter reliability pressure more effectively than B's rebuttals addressed A's delay/speed advantage.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON wins by successfully anchoring the debate to present-day reliability constraints versus future projections. While PRO argued forcefully on cost and deployment speed, CON consistently exposed the gap between long-duration storage models and commercial deployment, particularly for seasonal and winter reliability. CON's "northern winter evening" frame and sharp rebuttal on the inadequacy of 4-hour storage materially damaged PRO's claim that nuclear is currently unnecessary. CON also deftly neutralized PRO's trap question about fully decarbonized nuclear grids by highlighting that neither path has achieved it yet, but nuclear provides firm zero-carbon capacity today without relying on fossil backup to mask intermittency. CON's closing cleanly crystallized this distinction, giving a clearer, more grounded reason to vote against the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 effectively dismantled PRO's storage claims by distinguishing between short-duration battery limits and the undeployed reality of grid-scale seasonal storage, while turning PRO's "name a decarbonized nuclear economy" trap into a symmetry argument that highlighted nuclear's present-day firm capacity advantage over weather-dependent generation.
