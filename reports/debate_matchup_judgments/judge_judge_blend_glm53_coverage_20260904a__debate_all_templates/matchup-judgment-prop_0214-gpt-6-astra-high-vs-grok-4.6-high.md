- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely strong, well-metaphored cases (A's "ratchet"/"sureties"/"claims office for the dead" framing versus B's "locked door"/"chapter is not closed" framing), and the debate stayed tightly on the actual clash: whether a bounded, evidence-gated compensation-and-archive scheme is a real stopping rule or an open-ended ratchet. A's early catch that "meaningful access" as a trigger isn't a genuine deadline was the sharpest moment of the debate and forced B to specify concrete mechanisms (independent tribunal, balance-of-probabilities standard, material-obstacle-only extensions, one divided award per survivor). B's answers to the pressure questions were direct, labeled correctly, and substantively narrowed the scope of descendant claims in a way A never fully dismantled. That unresolved concession, combined with B's more concrete handling of the "multiplying claimants" and privacy objections (redacted extracts, one award divided among heirs), gives B the cleaner path to victory, though A's stopping-rule and governability critique kept real pressure on B throughout, making this a competitive rather than lopsided decision.

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by systematically bounding Side B's slippery slope arguments, proving their mechanism had a concrete stopping rule and did not multiply liability, while successfully pressing the moral hazard of allowing the state to benefit from its own record delays.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): A won by showing that treating forced sterilization as a closed chapter lets governments escape liability through bureaucratic delay and death. - **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with discipline and no fabricated sourcing, but CON built the tighter logical throughline. CON's key move—catching PRO's shift from "irreversibility means never closed" to "adequacy means reaching living harm"—was a genuine, damaging catch that PRO never fully repaired; CON kept returning to it ("that concession defeats the claim... no payment could ever satisfy that test") and used it to force PRO to accept assessment could find claims speculative, which CON then used to show PRO's own framework admits a completable endpoint. PRO's rebuttals were vivid and emotionally forceful ("the program chose lineage," "intended absence") but repeated the same framing ("the bloodline the state tried to erase") without ever supplying a demonstrable standard for when the descendant-facing duty is discharged, which is exactly what CON's pressure questions (A3.1) demanded and PRO's answer (A1 in B4.1) left vague. This makes CON's path to victory clearer and more resolved at the end of the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 catching PRO's shift to "adequacy reaches living harm" and turning it into "that concession defeats the claim that legal closure is illegitimate" was the pivotal exchange; PRO never recovered a bright-line standard for descendant-obligation discharge afterward.

B's finality argument is powerful but leans toward defending the status quo the proposition critiques and never fully justifies why lineage-targeting creates no special continuing duty.
