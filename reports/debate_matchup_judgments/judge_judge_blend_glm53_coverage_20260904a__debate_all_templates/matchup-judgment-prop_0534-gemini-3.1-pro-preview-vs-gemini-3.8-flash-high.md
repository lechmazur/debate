### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: irreversibility, fresh untied addresses, and peer-to-peer/non-compliant settlement make “legal until tied to specific conduct” structurally too late. PRO’s liberty and FTX points landed, but its enforcement path repeatedly collapsed into hoping criminals cash out at compliant ramps after conceding private keys cannot be forced open. CON answered pressure directly and made that comparison easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A2.3/A4.3 turn: public-ledger tracing is not recoverability, and PRO’s model hinges on criminals obligingly touching centralized infrastructure after keys are unbreachable.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by showing that the proposition's standard—legal unless directly tied to specific criminal conduct—is structurally unworkable for irreversible, pseudonymous, borderless transactions. They demonstrated that post-hoc enforcement guarantees funds are gone before specific conduct can be proven, that fresh addresses evade even preventative sanctions, and that shadow economies can operate entirely outside compliant fiat ramps. Side B (PRO) offered compelling civil liberties and consumer protection framings, but never resolved the core enforcement gap: how to stop irreversible harm or sanction fresh untied addresses under a default-legality regime. CON's pressure questions exposed these weaknesses, and their rebuttals cleanly distinguished prudential financial custody rules from banning code.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.5), where they used PRO's concession that private keys cannot be forced open to collapse the fiat-ramp enforcement model, and showed that ransomware and sanctions evasion rely on instantly generated fresh addresses that make PRO's "specific criminal conduct" standard structurally helpless.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B wins by winning the decisive enforcement clash. After A argued ramps and public-ledger tracing are sufficient, B showed visibility is not enforceability: irreversible self-custody transfers mean once funds leave regulated custody, post-hoc tracing only observes the getaway, and fresh untied addresses defeat the proposal's "tied to specific conduct" standard. A's own Second Rebuttal conceded keys cannot be forced open and enforcement depends on criminals voluntarily touching centralized infrastructure. B's answers on code vs commercial regulation and on peer-to-peer black-market settlement were more direct, leaving A's liberty case intact but insufficient to overcome the national-security / sanctions timing problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2/B4 attack that "visibility is not enforceability" and leveraging A's A4.1 concession that private keys cannot be mathematically forced open, showing PRO's model collapses when the shadow economy never uses compliant ramps.
