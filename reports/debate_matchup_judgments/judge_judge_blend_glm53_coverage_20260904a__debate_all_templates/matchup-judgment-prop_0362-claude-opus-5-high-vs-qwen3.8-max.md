This reframing forced CON to defend wide amplitude as economically necessary rather than merely transparent, a burden they never fully discharged.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by successfully reframing flat pricing as a permanent peak price rather than an accessible baseline. Side B argued effectively that surge pricing damages the shared cultural moment, but Side A neutralized this by pointing out that flat pricing already excludes families from that moment via sellouts, while failing to subsidize off-peak trips.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal decisively shifted the debate by exposing Side B's unstated premise—that flat pricing is low—and arguing that flat pricing is actually a peak price applied indiscriminately to off-peak hours.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive comparison easier: both systems ration scarce Saturday seats, but only dynamic pricing recycles that scarcity into real off-peak floors, while A's "stable baseline" is effectively a permanent peak price. B neutralized A's cultural-habit framing by forcing the sellout tradeoff and catching A's tension between "join the shared moment" and "come next weekend." A stayed coherent on predictability and standard-seat protection, but never escaped the trips-taken metric or proved flat pricing preserves mass access rather than locking in high fares.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5–B4.7: flipping A's sellout answer against A's own "participate when others participate" claim, showing flat pricing also excludes from week one and funds nothing in return.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON wins by exposing a central tension in PRO's case: PRO defends flat pricing as protecting access to the shared cultural moment, yet flat pricing rations that exact moment by sellouts and refresh-button luck while telling excluded families to return next week—contradicting PRO's own claim that the conversation is week one. CON's reframe that flat pricing is itself a hidden peak price permanently, and that only dynamic pricing with a deep floor can fund the cheap off-peak trips that build frequency, gives the cleaner comparative mechanism. PRO's closing never repairs the sellout contradiction or explains why stable pricing has failed to stop declining attendance, while CON crystallizes the clash around trips taken and the hypocrisy of defending upward-only price discrimination via concessions and IMAX.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (Turn 8, B4.5–B4.7), where CON shows that under PRO's own theory the cultural moment is week one, yet PRO's flat-pricing model excludes families from that moment by speed and tells them to come back later—while CON's model at least returns scarcity rents as cheap Wednesday tickets.
