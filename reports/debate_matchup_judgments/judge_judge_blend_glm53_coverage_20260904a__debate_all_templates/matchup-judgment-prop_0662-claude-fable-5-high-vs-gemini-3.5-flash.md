### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): A won by making the debate comparative: the status quo demonstrably leaves platform workers uncovered and creates fraud-prone emergency improvisation, while standardized records and contributions at least improve funding, eligibility, and verification. B’s strongest point was the involuntariness problem, but A answered it well by noting that ordinary unemployment insurance already relies partly on claimant certification and that platform records can be better evidence than self-attestation. B repeated the “human intent” objection and flexibility-cost claims without proving they outweigh the uncovered-worker problem or engaging A’s legal and administrative analogues.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the multi-app loophole by arguing aggregation closes it, then undercut B’s verification standard by showing traditional UI does not actually verify intent as cleanly as B claimed.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): A wins by showing verification and funding are feasible using existing UI analogs (partial benefits, transaction levies like NY Black Car Fund), directly answering pressure questions on intent and classification while exposing B's final alternative as the failed status quo. B's core mismatch claims were neutralized and never offered a workable path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 directly dismantled B's verification and classification objections by mapping to current UI practices and statutes, collapsing B's live routes.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by showing that CON's core objection—platforms cannot verify involuntary loss of earnings—applies equally to the existing UI system, which runs on claimant certification and employer separation forms rather than deep intent verification. PRO further neutralized the multi-app problem with aggregation logic and provided a concrete, classification-neutral funding model (NY Black Car Fund), while CON's alternative of general assistance and portable savings accounts was introduced too late and effectively dismantled in closing. PRO's framing of "solvable engineering versus guaranteed disaster" accurately captured the asymmetry after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to the verification puzzle: demonstrating that traditional UI does not verify ongoing intent either, and that standardized platform records (deactivations, demand data, cross-app aggregation) provide at least as much evidence as the employer separation forms CON treated as uniquely objective. This rebuttal blunted CON's central structural-mismatch argument and held through the remainder of the debate.
