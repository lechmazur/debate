### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won because it kept the debate tied to the motion: public digitization and funding versus reliance on paywalled private markets and inaccessible physical archives. Side A had a powerful conflict-of-interest frame, but it repeatedly inflated the proposition into a state monopoly or single federal portal, which was not clearly entailed. B's answers on access, preservation, and legal accountability gave a clearer reason why imperfect public mandates beat the status quo. A's community-archive alternative was appealing but underexplained and often depended on public funding itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was the response that the state already holds many of these records, so the real choice is whether to compel public access or leave descendants with private paywalls and geographic barriers. That undercut A's "seizing control" frame and made A's pluralism defense look less responsive to the motion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON successfully identified a structural paradox in the motion: empowering the perpetrator-state to curate digital evidence of its own crimes creates dangerous interpretive control beyond mere physical custody. PRO's reframing—that the state already holds records—was blunted by CON's sharp distinction between physical storage and algorithmic or metadata curation (A2.1). CON's historical analysis showing that marginalized communities have been systematically denied the very democratic leverage PRO relies on (A4.5-A4.6) proved decisive in the accountability clash. While PRO landed real blows on market concentration and geographic barriers, CON's closing crystallization left the cleaner live route. PRO's repeated word-limit clipping added execution costs that compounded their failure to fully answer CON's power-analysis.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's twin blows distinguishing physical custody from interpretive control (A2.1) and demonstrating that democratic accountability has historically failed the exact communities at issue (A4.5-A4.6), which together neutralized PRO's central reframe and theory of leverage.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a clearer comparative on accountability and leverage. While Side B effectively framed the state as a conflicted perpetrator and raised strong concerns about interpretive control and the historical limits of democratic accountability for marginalized groups, Side A successfully neutralized the "gatekeeper" frame by noting the state already holds physical custody. A’s consistent weighing—that statutory frameworks and democratic processes, however imperfect, offer descendant communities legal and political leverage that corporate terms of service and fiduciary duties fundamentally deny—proved more decisive. Side A’s pressure questions also exposed a practical gap in Side B’s community-archive alternative regarding sustainable funding and geographic access, which Side B never fully resolved. Both sides were rhetorically sharp and well-grounded, but Side A’s comparative asymmetry argument and tighter response to live pressure provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered Side B’s pressure on community governance and statutory durability by contrasting corporate fiduciary duties with democratic/legal leverage. The framing that a community disagreeing with a corporate paywall can only "cancel their subscription and stare at a locked door" sharply crystallized the accountability asymmetry and materially damaged CON’s pluralism defense.
