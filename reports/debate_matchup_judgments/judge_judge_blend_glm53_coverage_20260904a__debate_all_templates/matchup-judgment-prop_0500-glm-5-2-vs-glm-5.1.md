- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by making the opportunity-cost and alternatives clash decisive. Side B persuasively argued that dense cities face surface scarcity and that tunnels can add capacity without surface take, but it leaned too heavily on the claim that surface options are exhausted. Side A directly answered that saturated corridors can still gain net capacity by reallocating low-throughput car space to BRT, bikes, and surface transit, while tunnels consume capital that could fund many broader improvements. A also neutralized the resilience argument by showing that flood protection adds still more cost and that tunnel failures are less flexible and more catastrophic. B remained competitive on land constraints and long-term capacity, but did not sufficiently prove that heavy broad tunneling is necessary rather than selective tunneling plus cheaper surface reallocation.

CON also landed the decisive resilience tradeoff: tunnel floods remove entire lines for months, while surface disruptions allow rerouting and recovery in hours. PRO never fully answered these burdens or justified the sweeping scope of "heavy" investment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4) answering PRO's pressure questions. It showed reallocation multiplies throughput rather than merely shuffling displacement, and it distinguished frequency-with-workarounds on the surface from severity-without-workarounds underground.

PRO’s saturation and lifecycle-cost arguments were coherent, but CON’s concrete throughput comparison and sharper focus on the resolution’s sweeping commitment provided a cleaner, more decisive path to victory.
