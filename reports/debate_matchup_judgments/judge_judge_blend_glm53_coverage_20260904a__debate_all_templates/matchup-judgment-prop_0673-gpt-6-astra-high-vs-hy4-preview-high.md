### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a more concrete, cumulative causal story: that 60-day authorization does not merely "move up" an existing eventual benefit but creates a materially new, near-certain payoff for migrants who would never otherwise complete or even begin a weak claim (the "eventual = never" / "template claim" framing). CON used PRO's own concessions ("could influence some applicants," "not defending zero inducement," "still needing help with food") as leverage across rounds, and tied the fraud prong directly to the assistance prong (induced entrants add full costs while existing-applicant savings are only partial). PRO's rebuttals were competent and procedurally clean (proper A1/A2 answers, a genuine accounting framework, and the sharp point that "reduced reliance" doesn't require full independence), and PRO correctly flagged that CON's "years of pending-case employment" scenario partly bundles pre-existing status-quo benefits with the 60-day change. But PRO never fully neutralized CON's "marginal migrant" expected-value logic (that delay/uncertainty discounting explains why shortening the wait could pull in new filers who wouldn't otherwise apply), repeatedly falling back on unquantified assurances that migration costs and rejection risk "limit" inducement. That left CON's dual-prong attack more intact by the close, giving CON the clearer path to victory, though PRO's disciplined framework-based answers kept the margin from being large.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over B2.1/B4.2 ("this bundles years of pending-case employment with a sixty-day timing change") versus A2.1/A4.1 ("the marginal migrant would not otherwise file, so eventual often means never") was the decisive clash: PRO scored a real technical point isolating the counterfactual, but CON's reframing around the marginal applicant's expected-value calculation better survived scrutiny and was never squarely rebutted with equal force.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won because it defended both prongs of the proposition with a tighter causal story and sharper rebuttal. On assistance, Side A established that earlier lawful earnings directly reduce shelter and service costs, while Side B’s offsetting “induced entrant” argument depended on an unproven surge whose scale was never established. On fraud, Side A’s persistent distinction between non-meritorious and deliberately fabricated claims exposed that Side B’s inducement story showed more filings but not necessarily a material rise in deception—a gap Side B never closed. Side B’s “new prize” reframe was original and rhetorically striking, but it rested on speculative scale assumptions that Side A successfully neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s repeated distinction between fraudulent and merely non-meritorious claims, which revealed that Side B’s inducement argument did not establish a material increase in deliberate deception, decisively blunting the fraud prong of CON’s case.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained two live routes: earlier lawful earnings directly reduce shelter/support needs among existing applicants, and fraud requires deliberate deception not just more filings or weak claims. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but CON (Side A) built the more durable path to victory on the decisive second prong of the motion — "without materially increasing fraudulent claims." CON's core move, sharpened through pressure questions, was to force PRO into a dilemma: if informal work/family/credit already bridge the waiting period (as PRO claimed to blunt the fraud-incentive argument), then that same fact undercuts PRO's dependency-reduction mechanism; if those stopgaps are weak, then earlier lawful income is significant enough to matter for fabricators too, not just genuine claimants. PRO's "either way" response in Rebuttal 2 tried to resolve this but CON's final rebuttal showed the two effects (dependency reduction and fraud incentive) are compatible rather than mutually exclusive, leaving the dilemma essentially live. CON also caught a meaningful drift in PRO's argument: redefining "material" fraud increase as one that would "alter the net verdict" rather than as a freestanding threshold, which CON correctly flagged in closing as quietly changing the proposition's own two-part test. PRO's strongest material — the "job ban vs. permission" framing and the claim that unchanged screening/penalties limit the fraud pull — was clear and intuitively appealing, but never fully closed the marginal-incentive gap CON kept pressing (identical risk, earlier reward). Both sides handled Pressure Questions well and answered with proper A1/A2 structure, but CON's answers, while hedged, kept the burden correctly on PRO to establish immateriality, and PRO's closing still relied on asserting the six-month wait "was never a real filter" without fully neutralizing the marginal comparison. The debate is close and both sides avoided fake precision, but CON's sharper exploitation of PRO's internal tension and more careful policing of the proposition's wording gives it a clear, competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's Rebuttal 1 point [A2.7-A2.8] turning PRO's own "informal work already bridges the wait" claim into a dilemma against PRO's assistance-reduction mechanism, followed by PRO's attempted "either way" resolution in Rebuttal 2 [B4.3-B4.4], and CON's final rebuttal [A4.5] showing the two effects (dependency reduction and fraud incentive) can coexist rather than cancel out — leaving PRO's resolution incomplete.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a direct, plausible mechanism for reducing dependency and successfully undermining Side B’s fraud concern: the 60-day shift is a minor timing change on an already high-risk gamble, while B never grounded the claimed materiality of any fraud increase.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s rebuttal that the six-month wait was never a real fraud filter—so shortening it to 60 days merely shifts timing without creating a new class of false claims—directly deflated Side B’s central argument and framed the fraud prong as speculative.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON concedes some assistance reduction but keeps the second condition live. CON's marginal comparison — same credibility review and penalty, earlier lawful payoff — shows why a 2-month vs 6-month financing constraint can make fabricated filing feasible for some, and PRO never resolves the inconsistency flagged at [A2.8][A4.5]: early income cannot be transformative for dependency yet negligible for incentives. Gain on prong one does not discharge prong two.
