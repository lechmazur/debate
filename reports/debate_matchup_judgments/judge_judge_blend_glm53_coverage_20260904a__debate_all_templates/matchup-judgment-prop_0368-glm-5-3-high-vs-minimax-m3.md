### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by proving that the harms of default urgency cues are asymmetric. Side B established that cautious shoppers lose real money due to manipulation, while decisive shoppers lose only an anxiety cue they don't need for fast booking. Side A critically conceded this in Rebuttal 2 by admitting that "what's lost when the fake clock goes is nothing real," which collapsed their argument that the motion unfairly burdens decisive shoppers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly exploited Side A's logic by pointing out that "nobody needs a lying clock to book quickly," permanently neutralizing Side A's claim that decisive shoppers are harmed by the motion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO successfully established that removing manufactured urgency by default restores a neutral baseline rather than imposing paternalism, and that the burden of proof belongs on platforms holding the information. CON's enforcement objection and symmetry argument were plausible but never overcame PRO's decisive point that decisive shoppers lose no real functionality when fake cues disappear, whereas cautious shoppers currently pay an attention-tax on every screen. PRO's rebuttals were cleaner, more directly engaged the live clash, and crystallized the choice most effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (A4) dismantling CON's symmetry and enforcement objections by showing that calm is the neutral state, urgency is an active intervention, and anti-fraud rules do not collapse merely because violators invent cover stories.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A wins by successfully neutralizing Side B's two main lines of attack: enforceability and asymmetry. On enforceability, Side B argued that platforms would simply use "cover stories" to justify fake timers, making the standard unworkable. Side A decisively countered this by shifting the burden of proof to the information-holding platform, using a highly effective analogy to anti-fraud and advertising standards (we don't abandon rules just because fraudsters lie). On asymmetry, Side B argued that removing default urgency imposes an "opt-in tax" on decisive users. Side A cleanly dismantled this by pointing out that decisive users do not actually need *fake* or resetting timers to book quickly; removing the prop removes anxiety, not transaction speed. Side A's rhetorical framing ("storefront vs. slot machine," "calm is the neutral state") was sharp, memorable, and directly advanced their comparative case, making them the clear victor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 response to the enforcement critique, effectively arguing that the inability of users to verify timers simply means the burden of proof must fall on the platform, just as it does in fraud and advertising compliance.

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A wins by effectively collapsing Side B's distinction between fact and flourish. Side A persuasively argues that a countdown timer is simply the most efficient, salient way to convey a time-sensitive fact to a busy user, and that feeling accurately rushed is an honest reflection of reality rather than harmful manipulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively answers Side B's distinction by pointing out that a sprinting traveler can absorb a countdown instantly but would have to read a timestamp and do mental math while running, proving that salience serves a legitimate functional purpose.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B collapsed Side A's fact-versus-manipulation distinction by showing countdowns convey truthful urgency, exposed the unadministrable "mainly designed" standard, and weighed concrete harms to time-pressed users against Side A's abstract claims. Side A's clipped Rebuttal 2 left key pressure unanswered, and Side B's closing turn of the salience concession sealed the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 fork ([B2.2]) that forced the choice between banning truthful notices or reducing the motion to font policing, which Side A never fully escaped.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won the central clash by reframing the emotional pressure of countdown timers as an accurate reflection of reality when deadlines are genuine. Side B's "sprinting traveler" example effectively demonstrated the utility of salient time cues, and Side A failed to decisively counter the point that feeling accurately rushed is not a harm. Side A also suffered an execution cost from a clipped turn and struggled to defend the workability of the "mainly designed" intent test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they crystallized the argument that "when a deadline is real, urgency isn't injected; it's reported," effectively neutralizing Side A's claim that the emotional pressure of a countdown is inherently manipulative.
