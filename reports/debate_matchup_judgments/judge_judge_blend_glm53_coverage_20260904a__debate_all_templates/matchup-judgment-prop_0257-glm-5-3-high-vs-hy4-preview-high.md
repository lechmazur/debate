On balance, A's narrower, more logically airtight scope argument and cleaner exploitation of the pressure-question exchange gave it the more decisive path to victory.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued well, but A's central structural move—that PRO's own fallback mechanisms (identity-bound backups, sunset-able transfer, escrow-dependent continuity) collapse into "a regulated license with a property label"—was never squarely rebutted. B's closing conceded resale can be "sunset where impractical" [B5.3], which effectively hands A the resale/duplication issue that A had flagged since the opening as the crux (zero-degradation copies making mandated resale a standing discount on future sales). B's strongest remaining claim was the "veto vs. final sale" framing, but A's closing analogized ownership to repossession/seizure-for-cause, showing that real property ownership already tolerates process-based loss, which blunts B's claim that a cause-and-compensation license is categorically different from ownership. B never fully answered why compensation-after-loss is inferior to guaranteed possession when A explicitly built in escrow, notice, and hearings. The pressure questions round was even, but A's answers in Rebuttal 2 (framing refund as backstop, escrow as bounded and terminal) integrated better with the overall case than B's answers, which introduced new enforcement machinery (fingerprints, watermarks, funded ledgers) that A then used against B as proof that "ownership" had quietly become a relabeled license. This is the pivotal exchange. A's closing crystallized the debate more cleanly by isolating the one live disagreement (perpetual pristine resale) and noting it was structurally under-defended by B, who had already conceded sunset limits on it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.4 ("the key-transfer proposal... collapses... into a permanent resale queue") combined with A4.4–A4.6 exposing that PRO's fallback mechanisms mirror CON's regulated-license architecture was the most decisive sequence, since B's closing implicitly conceded the resale point could be "sunset where impractical," undercutting the strongest distinguishing feature of their case.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by maintaining a crucial, unanswered offensive line regarding the economic damage of a "pristine resale queue" and successfully absorbing Side B's mechanisms. Side A effectively proved that Side B's digital ownership model either collapses into uncontrolled distribution or relies on the exact same licensed, state-enforced architecture Side A proposed, minus the destructive resale right.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal powerfully synthesized Side B's own answers to the Pressure Questions, demonstrating that Side B had inadvertently constructed a "licensed-copy regime" complete with watermarks and state-administered records, while leaving the economic threat of pristine resale unaddressed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by showing that PRO's ownership model either required implausible eternal infrastructure or reduced to CON's own regulated license with a property label attached. CON's rebuttal crystallized the debate around a single unanswered harm—pristine digital resale undermining creator markets—while persuasively arguing that durable access with guardrails delivers everything consumers actually need.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4) exposing that PRO's fallback baseline was identical to CON's regulated license, leaving only a destructive resale right as the motion's real payload.
