### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): B built a tighter, more scalable case (more kids served per dollar, cost/schedule barriers, injury/burnout costs) and consistently forced the resolution's actual tradeoff into view ("who gets the field"). Crucially, B's reductio — that if elite programs privatize without subsidy anyway, public money is merely buying a handful of scholarships rather than expanding access — was never squarely answered by A, who kept re-asserting the "economic engine" and "privatization" claims without engaging the scale disparity or the "extraction vs. funding" critique of tournament revenue. In the pressure round, B's questions ("what proof expands access rather than buying elite capture," "how does a fee waiver overcome hotels/schedules/shifts") exposed the weakest joints in A's case, and A's answers ("the proof is in the infrastructure") were largely circular restatements rather than direct engagement. B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly around who still has a place after cuts, while A's closing mostly recapped its opening claims without resolving the live tension the questions had surfaced.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 point that "if those programs simply privatize without subsidy, that proves they are already a private market" directly undercut A's central bridge/equity argument, and A never adequately answered this reductio in either subsequent rebuttal.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO kept the proposition as prioritize, not abolish, and won two central clashes: (1) field-time capture – rec gets leftover hours while travel gets prime time, so tournament revenue does not expand access when waitlists grow; (2) equity – a 20-person subsidy that still requires hotels and Saturday shifts is a lottery for organized families, not a pathway for working families. CON's ladder and privatization story collapsed into asserting the engine funds rec without answering that capture cost, and contradicted itself by promising localized training after defending out-of-town tournaments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 / Rebuttal 2 combo turning CON's economic engine into "leftover hours" and fee waivers into "smaller gate with municipal sign" – directly neutralized CON's democratization and sustainability route.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins by decisively dismantling Side B's core equity and economic arguments. Side A's point that fee waivers do not overcome the structural time and travel barriers of elite sports ("A fee waiver does not buy a parent's Saturday") effectively neutralizes Side B's claim that public subsidies democratize elite travel. Furthermore, Side A's rhetorical framing of the "ladder" and resource capture was much sharper, exposing the flaws in Side B's assumption that tournament revenues automatically translate to expanded recreational access without causing local waitlists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the pressure questions, specifically pointing out that hidden costs like weekend shifts and hotel travel inherently exclude working families regardless of roster subsidies, and that year-round specialization causes the very teen drop-off Side B warns against.
