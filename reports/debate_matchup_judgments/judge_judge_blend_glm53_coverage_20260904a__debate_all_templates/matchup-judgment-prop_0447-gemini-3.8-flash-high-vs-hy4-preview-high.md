### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that targeted reclassification and upstream chain liability address abuse while the proposition, as written, charges lawful subcontracting too. A also mischaracterized chain liability as merely chasing insolvent intermediaries and did not answer this distinction in closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions directly, distinguished targeted reclassification from a universal levy, and exposed the contradiction between A’s claimed exemption for compliant suppliers and the motion’s requirement for a higher contribution share.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B exposed that the levy taxes lawful flexibility while leaving worker status unchanged, with incidence falling on hours/automation. CON's chain liability answers A's judgment-proof problem more precisely, and A's claim that compliant firms already satisfy the levy makes the proposal either redundant or punitive. That clash survived rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2-B4 redundancy trap: if compliant subcontractors already pay, the 'higher share' collapses into CON's chain liability; if it still charges them, it double-taxes lawful flexibility while giving only portable credit not employer duties.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully exposing the logical flaws in Side B's policy mechanism. Through sharp pressure questions and rebuttals, A forces B into a dichotomy where the proposed levy is either redundant (merely replicating CON's preferred chain liability for compliant firms) or punitive (double-taxing legitimate flexibility). A also effectively distinguishes its targeted enforcement approach from B's blunt tax, maintaining a cleaner, more logically consistent path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantles Side B's claim that "legitimate" subcontractors already satisfy the requirement, proving that this either makes the motion identical to CON's chain liability or results in double-taxation.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B won a competitive debate by establishing two unresolved costs: the motion’s broad trigger can capture legitimate specialist subcontracting, and lead firms can shift much of the surcharge through procurement pressure. Side A offered a plausible social-insurance mechanism and exposed limits to reclassification, but effectively added a control-based carve-out not contained in the proposition and conflated statutory liability with economic incidence. Its concession that piece rates could fall gave B the clearest comparative harm. B’s categorical legal claims and repeated invalid reference IDs reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the specialist-chain question with a concrete industrial example and exploited Side A’s admission of possible piece-rate pressure. Side A argued that benefits would offset lower cash earnings, but did not establish that this trade would leave precarious workers better off.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO maintained the live route that contributions should follow control/benefit and can be credited to national schemes via existing SSN/hour tracking, while narrowing the trigger to labour under lead-firm control. CON's core hits – indiscriminate scope and pass-through to piece-rates – were blunted: the avionics chain example conflates product supply with labour platforms, and the admitted 'pressure' was answered with statutory incidence on the lead firm plus offsetting entitlements that workers lack today. The Platform Work Directive alternative leaves the zero-benefit status quo intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure Q1 forcing a labour-platform example and its A1 answer that the surcharge is the missing employer share remitted to the competent national institution neutralized CON's legal/administrative and overbreadth attacks.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger case on economic incidence, convincingly arguing that a surcharge on subcontracted labor would be passed down to vulnerable workers as lower piece rates and reduced hours. Side A also effectively exposed Side B's attempt to read unwritten carve-outs into the motion and highlighted the legal friction of crediting third-party surcharges across 27 distinct national welfare systems without an employment relationship. Side B's concession that piece rates might face pressure, coupled with a weak defense of tax incidence, fatally undermined its claim to protect workers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it seized on Side B's admission that the surcharge would exert "residual pressure on piece rates," framing it as "enforced wage compression" that trades immediate take-home pay for distant social credits, effectively neutralizing Side B's core protective claim.
