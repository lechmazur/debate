### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the debate centered on demand formation rather than final access to procedures. B’s clinic-door and parental-consent firewall was a strong argument, but A persuasively answered that marketing can harm and normalize procedures before any medical gate is reached, and that the motion concerns paid promotional content served to minors, not adult medical choice. B’s overbreadth and displacement arguments were competitive, but often overstated age-gating as censorship and did not fully defeat A’s narrower framing of warnings and age-gating for sponsored elective-procedure marketing. A had some grounding weaknesses and a clipped closing, but still left the cleaner route to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s “clinic door is the last line, not the first” response was the decisive exchange: it directly undercut B’s main firewall argument and reframed the clash around influencer-driven demand rather than whether minors can secretly complete surgery.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won because it successfully exposed the motion's central tradeoff: algorithmic age-gating would suppress legitimate, regulated healthcare content for adults while pushing minors toward unregulated organic content, all to solve a "harm" that CON reframed as mere parental nagging for procedures already legally barred without explicit consent and medical oversight. CON's legal firewall argument remained largely intact after PRO's attempts to pivot to medspa "tweakments," and PRO's closing was clipped, leaving its final crystallization incomplete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2, which directly answered PRO's pressure questions, showed that automated age-gates cannot distinguish cosmetic from restorative dermatological content, and used the "nagging" reframe to deflate PRO's harm story while reinforcing that hiding regulated sponsorships creates a vacuum for dangerous unregulated content.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B wins by successfully minimizing Side A's core harm and winning the feasibility/overbreadth tradeoff. When Side A conceded in Rebuttal 2 that the primary mechanism is priming teens to "nag, save, and pressure" parents rather than secretly accessing surgery, Side B seized this to reframe the harm as routine family friction that does not justify sweeping algorithmic censorship. Side B also consistently won the technical clash: platforms cannot reliably distinguish sponsored medical dermatology from elective aesthetics, meaning age-gating would inevitably suppress legitimate adult healthcare information while pushing minors toward unregulated, undisclosed organic content. Side A's "checkout counter" framing and medspa pivot were rhetorically sharp, but Side B's legal grounding (prescription devices still require consent) and cleaner weighing of the intervention's collateral damage provided the more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing expertly weaponized Side A's concession that the motion addresses teen "nagging" rather than secret access. By contrasting this minimized harm against the proven risks of algorithmic overbreadth and content displacement, Side B crystallized a decisive cost-benefit comparison that Side A could not overcome.
