### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with discipline and no fabricated evidence, but PRO's core claim—that the university as a continuing institution still holds non-fungible advantages (land, prestige, network access) built on historical extraction, and that boards already treat old capital as "live" when funding chairs or quads—was never squarely answered by CON, which kept returning to a narrower "money is fungible" rebuttal that addressed dollar-tracing but not the land/prestige continuity claim. PRO also scored a sharp tu quoque in Rebuttal 2/PQ answers, pointing out that CON demands tracing and democratic legitimacy only when repair would return access, not when boards fund unnamed priorities or honor ancient restrictions—a point CON reasserted rather than defeated. Meanwhile CON's own remedy (expanded need-based aid plus progressive taxation) functions as a form of "long-term scholarship" funded beyond mere acknowledgment, which partially concedes the motion's core thrust and leaves CON contesting design details (need vs. targeted community criteria) more than fully negating the resolution. CON's targeting/administrability objections (geography vs. lineage boundary problems) were real and not fully resolved by PRO, keeping the debate close, but on balance PRO's throughline (compounding advantage vs. static acknowledgment) survived the exchange more intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1–B4.3), answering the "name the specific fund" pressure question by arguing boards already spend unrestricted yield on unnamed priorities and only demand tracing when repair would return access to excluded groups — a point CON never fully dislodged beyond repeating the fungibility claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO successfully defended institutional continuity and showed that CON's traceability standard was applied asymmetrically—demanded only when repair was at stake, while boards freely spend unrestricted funds and honor legacy benefits without tagged provenance. CON's alternative was well-argued but could not fully overcome the intuition that a continuing beneficiary should be a continuing debtor, and its repeated reframing of "long-term" as "perpetual" slightly weakened its precision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The traceability exchange in A4.1 and B4.1, where PRO rejected the false precision of a "tagged slavery coin" and argued that boards already spend unrestricted yield on unnamed priorities, so demanding traceability only for repair is special pleading, while CON countered that relabeling modern income does not make it extracted repayment. PRO's "mixing is not innocence, it is permission they already use" frame survived more intact and did more damage to CON's live route.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides executed the format well and sustained a genuine clash on whether university endowments still hold a measurable "tainted" advantage. CON's strongest move was demanding a quantifiable baseline for disgorgement and exposing that once PRO made ancestry optional and shifted to need-based, geographically targeted aid, the remedy risked collapsing into ordinary duty already performed via need-based aid and acknowledgment. PRO's strongest move was framing this as unjust enrichment/equitable tracing (doubt resolved against the wrongdoer in commingled funds), a plausible general-knowledge mechanism that answered CON's quantification pressure without needing exact tracing, and it kept "source and destination" (tainted income, harmed communities) analytically distinct from generic aid even after conceding ancestry was optional. CON's rebuttal to that doctrinal move (A4.1-A4.2) mostly reasserted "still no baseline" without directly engaging why equitable resolution-against-wrongdoer reasoning shouldn't apply, leaving that line of PRO's case less damaged than CON's own crowd-out/administrability critique was damaged by PRO's geography-as-harm-proxy answer. The debate stayed close throughout, but PRO's closing more successfully crystallized the decisive frame ("acknowledgment leaves the balance sheet untouched") while CON's closing repeated its baseline demand without fully resolving the equitable-tracing counter, giving PRO a modest edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over quantification: A's Pressure Question demanding a measurable baseline for "disgorgement" (A3.1) was met by B's equitable-tracing answer (B4.1, resolving doubt against the wrongdoer in commingled funds) — a real mechanism CON never squarely dismantled in Rebuttal 2, leaving PRO's unjust-enrichment framework the more intact live route.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A sustained a coherent framework of beneficiary responsibility and unjust enrichment, arguing effectively that acknowledgment alone leaves the balance sheet untouched while targeted long-term investment actually repairs access. Side B raised sharp epistemic objections about tracing and administrability, but A deflected these without abandoning its core claim: using conservative floors, mixed-fund principles, and geography-plus-need targeting, A showed the remedy was bounded and feasible. Crucially, A exposed that B's alternative collapsed into baseline present-tense duties any decent institution owes, failing to explain why compounding wealth from documented wrongs should remain in the endowment. A's closing crystallized this distinction cleanly, giving the stronger live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it answered the quantification challenge with the "equity resolves doubt against the wrongdoer" principle and collapsed Side B's "ordinary duty" dilemma by distinguishing the tainted source of endowment income from the need-and-geography-based destination of scholarships.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live unjust-enrichment route: commingling is not cleansing, so present endowment is still inflated.
