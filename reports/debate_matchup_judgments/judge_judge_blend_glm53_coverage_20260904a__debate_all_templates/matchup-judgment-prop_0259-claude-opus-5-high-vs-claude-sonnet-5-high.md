### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully defended the status quo as a coherent adaptive system that already delivers standard time in winter, neutralizing PRO's dark-morning safety arguments. CON showed PRO's permanent standard time imposes a chronic summer cost—wasting daylight at 4:30 a.m. while darkening active evening hours—and that this recurring, months-long misalignment outweighs the transient transition harms of switching. CON also effectively turned PRO's own "light where people are" criterion against it and exposed the coordination problems with PRO's decentralized-scheduling alternative. PRO was unable to show that transition harms are avoidable rather than merely repeated, and never overcame the asymmetry that the status quo already provides the winter benefits PRO claimed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turning of PRO's "light where people are" framework in Rebuttal 2 ([B4.7]), showing that permanent standard time dumps daylight onto sleeping people at 4:30 a.m. while removing it from crowded evening streets, decisively reframed the summer trade-off and undermined PRO's circadian rationale.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the central weighing. PRO's strongest material – dark winter mornings, bus-stop safety, the 1970s DST failure – proves switching > permanent summer, but switching already runs winter on standard time, so that attack does not prove permanent standard > switching. CON makes that reframe explicit in [A1.1] and sustains it. On the live summer question, CON's criterion "light where people are" wins: 4:30am light falls on sleepers while loss falls on after-school and evening-commute hours when streets are fullest. PRO never solves the coordination problem in [B4.1] vs [A4.4] – decentralized shifting is either DST by another name with the same sleep cost, or it fails to coordinate. Transition health costs are acknowledged but shown to be transient and partly anticipatable versus a chronic 7-month daily loss.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: [A1.1] / [A2.8] – CON shows PRO's dark-morning case is satisfied by status quo switching and that the US after rejecting permanent DST returned to switching, not permanent standard, turning PRO's asymmetry claim into evidence for CON.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B dismantled Side A's case by pointing out that the status quo already uses standard time in winter, rendering A's dark-morning arguments irrelevant to the clash over the switch. B then effectively shifted the debate to summer, where A's permanent standard time causes earlier sunsets, a point A never adequately answered. B's thermostat analogy and coordination critique were decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's dismantling of A's "decentralized shifting" proposal, pointing out that it either fails due to coordination problems or just recreates DST with more friction.
