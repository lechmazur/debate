### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by making the mandate a truth-in-pricing baseline rather than an expensive universal benefit, while preserving charges for genuinely additional baggage. Its distinction between the airline's small marginal carry-on cost and the passenger's punitive gate fee directly weakened B's claim that inclusion must substantially raise every fare. B established a plausible backpack-only traveler, but repeatedly assumed large fare increases without adequately grounding them. B also conceded that carry-on charges push travelers toward overstuffed personal items, which reinforced rather than defeated A's operational case. Two clipped turns and a logically confused closing further hurt B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A1/A2 response was decisive: disclosure cannot repair misleading search rankings, whereas a common included baseline makes headline fares comparable; meanwhile, a cost can be trivial to provide yet highly profitable to charge for. B's later admission that fees create personal-item cramming handed A a clean answer to the bin-scarcity objection.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A built the cleaner path by consistently showing the status-quo fare is bait that harms average and low-income flyers via drip fees and chaos, while the baseline only forces a tiny honest correction rather than a real cost floor. B's access and scarcity claims were answered and partially self-undermined; clipped B speeches also cost progression and weighing. A made the decisive tradeoff easier to resolve for the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answers on disclosure-vs-trap and the non-contradiction of "trivial to provide yet punitive as ransom," collapsing B's core regressivity and cost framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A wins because it framed the current model as deceptive drip pricing that turns the advertised fare into a lottery ticket, while showing that a baseline merely reveals true prices rather than materially raising them. A's answer to B's pressure question about the cost contradiction—distinguishing trivial airline provision from punitive gate fees—defused CON's strongest objection and turned the regressivity argument. Moreover, CON conceded in Rebuttal 2 that charging for cabin bags fails to manage bin scarcity and instead causes the overstuffed-personal-item chaos PRO described, undermining CON's operational counter-argument. B's two clipped rebuttals and its ungrounded assertion that backpack-only travelers outnumber surprised roller-bag flyers left its access argument speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B3.2's attempt to catch PRO in a cost contradiction was sharply answered in A4.3, where PRO distinguished trivial marginal provision from $65 gate rent, collapsing CON's claim that the mandate would destroy low fares and reframing it as a truth-in-pricing correction.
