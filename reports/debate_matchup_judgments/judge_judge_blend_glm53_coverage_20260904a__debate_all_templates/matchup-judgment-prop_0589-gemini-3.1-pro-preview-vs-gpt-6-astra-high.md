### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A built and sustained the decisive framing: cost-effectiveness must be measured in shootings prevented per dollar, not in headcount or "footprint" per dollar. Throughout the debate, A repeatedly forced B to defend the leap from "more, cheaper civilian workers" to "more prevented shootings," and B never closed that gap—relying instead on unsupported ratios ("five to ten mediators"), vague liability figures, and repeated mechanism narratives (mediation, hospital outreach) without quantifying comparative effectiveness. A's sharpest and largely unanswered point was the asymmetric-accounting charge: B credited CVI's retaliation-interruption (hospital outreach after a shooting) as genuine prevention while dismissing police interruption of subsequent attacks as merely "reactive." B's closing repeated the squad-car/boiling-pot metaphor but never directly rebutted this asymmetry. B's pressure questions were sharp (especially on reactive arrests only occurring after a shooting), and A's answer—reframing prevention around retaliation and next attacks—was a reasonable, non-evasive save that mirrored B's own hospital-outreach logic, further exposing B's inconsistency. A's questions to B (on translating "footprint" into real prevention, and on scaling without cost/access degradation) were also well-targeted and never fully answered with anything beyond assertion ("massively untapped resource"). B's rhetoric was vivid and often more memorable, but rhetorical flourish substituted for the missing quantitative or logical bridge A kept demanding. On balance, A maintained tighter argumentative discipline, better matched the motion's actual comparative burden, and left B's core mechanism-cost story analytically unproven.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's recurring "asymmetric accounting" critique (crystallized in A4.5/A5.4-5.5)—pointing out that PRO counts interrupted retaliation as genuine prevention for CVI's hospital outreach while dismissing analogous police interruption as merely reactive—went unanswered in B's closing and was the single most damaging, unresolved point in the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the decisive metric dollars-per-shooting-prevented and forcing A to defend actual prevention, not headcount. Across rounds B dismantled A's core leaps: cheaper staffing does not equal more shootings stopped; voluntary mediation has reach limits police capacity can address; scaling credibility is not automatic; and A counted interrupted retaliation for hospital CVI while discounting the same for patrol/investigation. A was vivid and consistent on cost structure and access, but repeatedly treated team size and "root cause" storytelling as proof of superior returns. B's consistent-accounting frame made the proposition easier to decide against PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2/closing attack on asymmetric accounting: A credits hospital outreach and mediation for stopping next/retaliatory shootings, then treats police interruption/arrest capacity as merely reactive or displacement, while converting civilian headcount into guaranteed prevention.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO built an intuitive case around proactive mediation and lower per-person costs, but CON successfully reframed the decisive metric as shootings prevented per dollar rather than workers hired per dollar. CON's sharpest work exposed that PRO never bridged the gap between "more outreach contacts" and "more shootings prevented," while inconsistently counting interrupted retaliation and deterrence as real prevention for CVI but not for policing. By the end of the exchange, PRO's "structural math" collapsed into an unsupported assumption that staffing inputs equal shooting reductions, and CON's demand for consistent accounting—treating police deterrence, repeat-offender arrest, and patrol interruption as preventive in the same way PRO treated hospital outreach and mediation—remained unanswered. CON therefore built the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 distinction that "staffing is an input, not a shooting reduction," which dismantled PRO's core claim that hiring five to ten mediators for the price of one officer mathematically yields more prevention, and the accompanying charge of asymmetric accounting in counting deterrence benefits.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides ran a competent, well-matched debate, but CON built a slightly more damaging and sustained line of attack. CON's pressure question on uncooperative aggressors forced PRO to concede that CVI cannot stop a determined shooter and must "seek emergency police assistance," a concession CON then leveraged repeatedly into an "expensive middleman" framing that PRO never fully neutralized (PRO's complementary-role reply was adequate but not decisive). CON's scalability critique—that credible-messenger capacity is a scarce, non-fungible resource—was pressed consistently and PRO's answer (phased expansion, shared supervision) asserted away the bottleneck without explaining how professionalized teams replicate the specific street credibility CON identified as the mechanism of effectiveness. PRO's best moves were reframing "coercive capacity" versus "cost-effectiveness" and correctly noting CON's "mathematically more cost-effective" language was rhetorical rather than quantified—a fair and effective epistemic check that slightly dents CON's overconfidence. But CON's closing kept the concession-driven middleman and scarcity arguments live and unanswered, giving CON the cleaner path to the decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Q2 pressure question forcing PRO to admit CVI "cannot forcibly stop someone who refuses all cooperation" and must call police, which CON then converted into the "expensive middleman" argument in Rebuttal 2 and Closing—this exchange did the most to reshape the comparative burden in CON's favor.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the live question locked on the proposition’s actual test—shootings prevented per next dollar—rather than whether police authority is real. A’s precision/pre-arrest access frame and realistic (not ideal) comparison survived; B’s strong credible-messenger bottleneck and gun-removal points were sharp but repeatedly slipped into proving police necessity/capability instead of superior marginal cost-effectiveness, and B never converted seizures or hot-spot presence into a clean shootings-per-dollar win after A’s pressure on replacement, coverage costs, and non-detectable conflicts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s sustained turn that coercive “definitive” intervention and police backup prove a role for existing enforcement, not that another officer beats targeted CVI on preventable shootings per dollar (A2.1–A2.7, crystallized A5.1–A5.2).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A maintained a sharper focus on the comparative cost-effectiveness standard, exposing that Side B’s confiscation and deterrence arguments rested on unsupported assumptions about shootings prevented per dollar and weapon replacement, while offering a coherent account of how phased, precision civilian intervention reaches conflicts additional patrol cannot. Side B’s scalability critique was plausible but speculative and insufficiently grounded to overcome Side A’s sustained comparative framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s pressure question and rebuttal sequence dismantling Side B’s claim that confiscation is “mathematically more cost-effective,” revealing the unwarranted leap from gun seizures to proven shooting reductions per marginal dollar.
