### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically shrinking Side B's case and delivering a devastating final closing. Side A successfully argued that government funding brings bureaucracy that kills informality, and effectively framed "zoning" as deregulation (a rule change, not a budget issue). In the final exchange, Side A brilliantly dismantled Side B's claim about fixed costs by pointing out that existing community buildings already pay for heat and lights, meaning the true barrier is liability rules, not a lack of funding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Closing response to Side B's final bottleneck test, pointing out that schools and churches already pay their utility bills and only liability rules keep the doors locked at night.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the dual burden (decline “enough” + active funding and zoning) decisive and then shrinking PRO’s case through concessions: zoning half became repeal, already-funded parks/libraries absorbed much of the “shortage,” and thin-area demand was reduced to a handful of regulars who need an unlocked existing hall more than a revival appropriation. PRO kept a live public-goods route on fixed costs in profit-thin places, but never showed why liability unlocks and ordinary keys fail once containers already exist, nor why grants can reliably keep informality without the strings CON flagged. Clipped R2 cost A some polish; the comparative frame still favored CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s redefinition strike (classic paid third places vs PRO’s free/cross-class pivot) plus the later “nine regulars need a key” turn after pressure, which collapsed funding into marginal access to spaces already heated/insured.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively shifting the clash from theoretical public goods to the practical realities of government intervention. B's sharp distinction between "rules" (liability, permits) and "budgets" (funding) dismantled A's claim that active funding is the necessary bottleneck. B's memorable rhetoric ("nine people need a key, not a capital program", "funding friendship") and strong closing crystallized the debate, showing that existing spaces are locked by regulation, not a lack of appropriations. A's theoretical case was solid, but A struggled to defend the practical implementation of government grants without stifling the very informality that makes third places work, waving away audit requirements as a mere "procurement choice."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing exchange, where B sharply countered A's "fixed costs" argument by pointing out that existing community spaces (schools, churches) already pay for heat and lights, and are only locked due to liability fears (a rule, not a budget).
