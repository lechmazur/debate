### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won the marginal comparison. National enrollment and narrow regional tools could address exclusion without a general continental identity layer, while interoperability itself would make cross-border tracking cheaper and easier. Side A offered creative safeguards, but audit logs act after abuse and its suspension lever depended on AU enforcement it never convincingly established. Side A also failed to show why its largely national service-delivery examples required continent-wide interoperability. The clipped rebuttal and Side B's erroneous reference labels were execution costs, but they did not reverse the substantive result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer that data may remain national while the person becomes continent-wide identifiable decisively undercut Side A's decentralization defense. Its related point that logs document rather than prevent a persecutor's query also weakened Side A's main privacy safeguard.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B wins the central clash on interoperability vs centralization. A correctly notes no single vault is proposed, but B shows a shared general credential with cross-border query is itself the surveillance upgrade even if files stay national. B also neutralizes the necessity claim: half-billion exclusion is a failure of birth registration and enrollment, fixable by mobile drives, offline verification and narrow regional compacts, not a continental passkey. A's key safeguard – conditional recognition/suspension and audit logs – is undermined by B's enforcement critique and A's own admission that no regime enforces perfectly, compounded by A's clipped R2 execution cost. The irreversible, non-reissuable biometric tracking margin outweighs contested delivery gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.3 – "data stays national is true of files and false of person" – turns A's bank-card analogy and framework-not-vault defense into a race-to-the-bottom general-purpose key argument, the most decisive reversal.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by successfully arguing that the surveillance risk of a continent-wide framework is structural and irreversible, stemming from the interoperability of a general biometric credential rather than just a central database. Side A's alternative of scaled national enrollment combined with narrow, purpose-limited regional protocols effectively undercut Side B's binary choice. Furthermore, Side B's proposed safeguards (audit logs and network suspension) failed to overcome Side A's strong point that the AU lacks the supranational enforcement to stop repressive regimes from exploiting the interoperable tracking layer, making the "race to the bottom" in mutual recognition a decisive vulnerability for the PRO case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "bank card" analogy, explaining that unlike purpose-limited payment networks, a mutually recognized general biometric ID creates a universal cross-border passkey that forces trust in the most repressive member state's registry, thereby systematizing the surveillance risk rather than mitigating it.
