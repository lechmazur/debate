### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): PRO built a coherent, rhetorically consistent case: political boards demonstrably shift content for electoral gain, while scholarly standards at least provide a methodological floor. CON's opening effectively challenged scholarly neutrality, but the decisive pivot came in Rebuttal 2. PRO's answer to CON's Q2 (claiming the Reconstruction correction validates scholarly self-correction) was factually inverted by CON [B4.3]: the correction came from journalists, activists, and communities outside the academy, not from within the scholarly review system. If PRO's model had governed textbooks in 1920, those external voices would have had no institutional standing. PRO's closing response—"scholarship is designed to assimilate such shifts"—was an assertion that evaded the structural point: PRO's model explicitly insulates scholarly review from outside pressure, which is what the Reconstruction correction required. CON also sustained the accountability asymmetry throughout (peer review tells us how scholars hold each other accountable, not how the public holds scholars accountable), and PRO never provided a satisfying mechanism for public challenge beyond "transparency and methodology." These are live, unanswered routes to victory for CON. PRO's margins come from superior comparative framing—the intuition that political boards in practice are demonstrably worse was never directly refuted—and from CON's format penalties (clipped Rebuttal 1 and Closing). But the Reconstruction inversion was the most decisive single exchange of the debate: PRO mischaracterized it, CON corrected it, and PRO's closing ignored the structural implication. CON wins on the live substantive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3 in Rebuttal 2: PRO tried to claim the Reconstruction consensus correction validated scholarly self-correction (A4.2). CON reversed this directly—the correction came from external journalists, activists, and communities who challenged academic authority, not from within the scholarly review system. CON then drew the structural conclusion: under PRO's model, those outsiders would have been dismissed as unqualified critics with no institutional standing. This turned PRO's own example against them and exposed the core structural flaw in the proposition without inventing new evidence.

That left B's evidence-floor-over-political-comfort framing live and decisive.
