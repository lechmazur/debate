B's case stayed at a higher level of abstraction (equal worth, moral lottery, tool-vs-objective) and never squarely answered why a claim that cannot move without the very institutions/loyalty it demotes should count as "stronger." B's Q&A answers were format-compliant but substantively conceded ground A immediately capitalized on. A's opening being clipped is a real execution cost, but the surviving content and A's superior rebuttal work across rounds still gives A the more persuasive, better-resolved case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's parent/medicine analogy in Rebuttal 2 and Closing (A4.1, A5.1-A5.2), which reframed "bounded by justice" as a limit on permissions rather than a demotion of rank, directly neutralizing B's central pressure question and B's closing reliance on the same analogy.

PRO's central pressure—that national loyalty is "bounded by justice" and therefore weaker—was decisively turned by CON's parent analogy: a parent's duty to her child is bounded (she may not steal a stranger's medicine) yet still outranks an unbounded stranger's claim when allocating resources. This distinction held through the closing and undermined PRO's attempt to derive hierarchy from boundedness. While PRO opened with strong cosmopolitan intuitions, its rebuttals never fully recovered from CON's conceptual turnaround, and its closing merely restated the tool-vs-objective distinction without answering why an unbounded concern that cannot move agents without national loyalty is morally "stronger." The clipped opening is a real cost for A, but B did not exploit it, and A's later speeches built a cleaner comparative path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing defense of the parent analogy (A4.1 / A5.1), distinguishing the boundary of a duty (where justice forbids stealing) from its rank (prioritizing one's own child), which neutralized PRO's attempt to prove national loyalty's subordination from CON's earlier concession.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully reframing the debate from an abstract hierarchy of values to the operational reality of moral duties on agents. - **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): B built a cleaner, more consistent framework (severity/personhood/capacity as the drivers of moral strength) and used the pressure round decisively: A's concession that outsiders retain full negative rights and that loyalty yields in genuine conflicts [A2.6] was exploited relentlessly, forcing A into a retreat from "foundational architecture" to "permissible fiduciary role." A did land real hits (the union/shared-fate analogy, the taxation-as-prioritization point, and a good pressure question about domestic spending), keeping this competitive, but B's case progressed more coherently across rounds and closed the debate on the more damaging framing.
