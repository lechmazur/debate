### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won on feasibility, expected value, and sequencing. It showed that professional recognition faces both negotiation and implementation vetoes, while tariff bargains have reciprocal concessions and automatic border implementation. Side B never established that economic size translates into realizable gains, and its working examples involved partners that had already liberalized goods trade. Side B also suffered from repeated clipping and unsupported overstatement about immediate gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B argued that entrenched tariff lobbies made both options equally difficult. Side A decisively answered that tariff negotiations offer bargaining currency, phase-ins, and neutral customs implementation, whereas licensing boards gain nothing from foreign entry and retain power after signature. It then turned Side B’s Trans-Tasman example into evidence for tariffs-first sequencing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by providing a stronger structural mechanism for why tariffs are easier to negotiate than MRAs, resolving Side A's accusation of a contradiction. Side B also effectively turned Side A's primary historical examples to prove that tariff cuts necessarily precede MRAs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which introduced the concept of political "currency" to explain why tariff lobbies can be negotiated with while licensing boards cannot, and brilliantly pointed out that Side A's Trans-Tasman example proved Side B's preferred sequencing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive expected-value clash: finite capacity, post-signature friction, and political currency make tariff cuts collectible while MRAs routinely stall at licensing boards. A pressed a real lobby/ease contradiction and the services-share stake, but never defeated B's unbundling answer, buy-off distinction, or sequence turn that A's best examples (Trans-Tasman, SA digital) followed goods liberalization. Repeated clipping also cost A on completeness. B made the choice easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 / Closing: tariff politics has exchange currency and self-executes at the border, while boards have nothing to trade and control implementation; Trans-Tasman arrived after tariffs were already gone, so PRO's showcase is the CON sequence.
