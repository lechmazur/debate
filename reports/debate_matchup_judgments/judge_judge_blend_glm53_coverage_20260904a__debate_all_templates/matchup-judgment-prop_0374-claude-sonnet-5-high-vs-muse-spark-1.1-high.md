### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): A won by showing that diffuse political chilling damages the classroom's general capacity for inquiry, while disputed content is usually more visible and contestable. B's distinction between recoverable omission and sticky misinformation was credible, but it never adequately answered the loss of discussion across many subjects. Its final alleged contradiction was a false dilemma: A could consistently distinguish difficult statewide textbook changes from the local controversies that commonly trigger broader chilling. B also relied on weakly grounded claims about family flight and misreferenced nonexistent C4 tags.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer that omitted subjects often cannot be challenged because students do not know what is missing most effectively reversed B's claim that omissions are neutral and recoverable. The line that guidance can restore rules but cannot immediately restore professional courage sharpened the durability comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A builds a highly consistent and persuasive case that self-censorship fundamentally breaks the educational environment, whereas controversial content is a visible, localized, and correctable error. Side B stakes its late-game strategy on a mischaracterization of Side A, claiming Side A contradicted itself by conceding that controversial content is a decade-long textbook adoption. In reality, Side A explicitly argued the opposite—that the textbook narrative collapses precisely because real political fights are over localized, easily removable materials. Because Side B's final rebuttals hinge entirely on this hallucinated trap, Side A wins the structural clash cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4 dismantling of the "decade-long textbook" narrative, coupled with A5's sharp takedown of Side B's claim that omissions are visible because "older siblings" notice them.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by maintaining a clearer, more consistent causal story about what education quality requires and why self-censorship uniquely undermines it. While CON landed a sharp philosophical blow with the active-miseducation vs passive-omission distinction and a clever "pick-one" trap in Rebuttal 2, PRO successfully framed education quality around the lived practice of open inquiry rather than the static correctness of any single belief. PRO's mechanism—vague laws causing rational, profession-wide retreat to safety—was plausible, well-developed across speeches, and rhetorically anchored by memorable lines about replacing books but not courage. CON's statewide-textbook claim remained more abstract and less grounded than PRO's survey-backed portrait of teachers avoiding central topics, and CON's closing slight misattribution of reference IDs was a minor execution cost. The debate was competitive and philosophically rich, but PRO left the more live route to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B2-B4 argument that self-censorship destroys the correction mechanism itself by training students and teachers that hard questions are not for school, making it systematically harder to reverse than discrete content removal.
