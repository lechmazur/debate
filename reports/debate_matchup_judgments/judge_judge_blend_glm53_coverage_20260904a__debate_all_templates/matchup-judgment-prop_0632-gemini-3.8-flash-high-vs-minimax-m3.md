### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by showing that coverage mandates create certain, large present costs while Side B never established that manufacturer rebates or downstream savings would offset them. Side B made a compelling prevention and insurer-externalization case, but repeatedly treated future catastrophes as inevitable and value-based contracting as automatically producing meaningful concessions despite the concentrated market. Side A’s claim that Side B contradicted itself on surrogate endpoints was overstated, yet its broader scale, pricing-power, and timing objections remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer to the hypertension analogy was decisive: cheap generics supported by mature outcomes evidence are materially different from expensive branded drugs prescribed across a vast eligible population. That distinction undercut Side B’s attempt to make skepticism about these contracts equivalent to rejecting ordinary preventive medicine.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: scale/volume cannot be wished away by stepped-care rhetoric; a clinically inelastic GLP-1 duopoly is not disciplined by conditional access slogans; surrogate rebate triggers cannot simultaneously justify huge lifelong spending and count as rigorous outcomes risk-sharing; and certain present premium costs beat speculative deferred offsets under ordinary discounting. PRO’s disease-framing, free-rider story, and pressure analogy to HTN/statins were strong and memorable, but never neutralized CON’s stacked structural failures, and PRO’s own month-four 5% weight-loss/A1c rebate example confirmed rather than escaped the surrogate critique. Closing crystallized the mutual inconsistency cleanly for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2/closing flip after B’s A2: B’s concrete 5% weight-loss / A1c rebate triggers confirmed A’s live charge that PRO’s “validated endpoints” are the same surrogates already attacked as inadequate for justifying monopoly-scale lifelong spend.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): CON won the core fiscal-control clash. PRO needed mandate + value-based pricing to create leverage, but B showed a must-cover rule expands a duopoly's captive market rather than disciplining it, and that rebate triggers at 5% weight loss etc. are the same surrogates B flagged as inadequate for justifying thousands per patient lifelong. B also neutralized the hypertension analogy by distinguishing mature generics from branded GLP-1s and reframed "ending free-riding" as relocating externalization to all premium payers now versus speculative future savings discounted away. A repeated catastrophic-cost claims without resolving the leverage paradox.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 on duopoly inelasticity and surrogate-endpoint contradiction — showing conditional market access cannot force net-price cuts when coverage is mandated, and that 5% weight-loss rebates are paperwork around the same list price — directly dismantled PRO's value-based pricing mechanism.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A won the central fiscal clash. It consistently distinguished per-patient performance from aggregate budget exposure and explained why delayed rebates, enrollee churn, broad eligibility, and mandatory class coverage could still produce immediate premium pressure. Side B compellingly established disease burden and status-quo access failures, but largely assumed that well-designed contracts and narrower clinical gatekeeping would solve the disputed mechanics. Those assumptions were not sufficiently defended, and Side B's clipped rebuttal added an execution cost. Side A did overstate some unsupported figures, especially discontinuation and premium increases, but its core causal argument remained stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it argued that Side B must choose between broad, costly uptake and restrictive authorization, while delayed outcomes warranties do not solve next-year cash-flow exposure. Side B's answer that liability is bounded per successful patient never resolved the aggregate-cost objection.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON built the clearer live route: a statutory class mandate for mass-prevalence, lifelong monopoly biologics detonates near-term premiums, and outcomes contracts cannot neutralize churn, attribution, discontinuation losses, or lost walk-away leverage against a duopoly. PRO’s status-quo-failure and disease-framing landed, but never solved captive-payer bargaining, immediate cash-flow exposure, or the breadth of FDA-threshold eligibility without quietly reintroducing rationing. B’s clipped Rebuttal 2 further weakened closure on A’s pressure. Vote A on actuarial mechanism defeat.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.3 / A4.3–A4.5: multi-year CV warranties as unscalable fantasy across churned plans, plus “clinically indicated” still ≈ tens of millions under FDA BMI/comorbidity labels, so delayed rebates cannot stop next-year premium blast.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): CON wins the decisive economic clash. PRO’s route depended on outcomes-based contracts bounding cost and clinical filtering limiting uptake. B exposed both as unworkable: no infrastructure to track churned patients across plans for multi-year warranties [B2.1], short-term weight loss triggers payment despite indefinite use [B1.3], and high first-year discontinuation leaves spend unrecoverable [B2.5/B4.5]. A’s attempt to redefine clinically indicated narrower than FDA BMI 30/27+comorbidity [A4.2] contradicts its access goal and leaves the immediate premium spike [B3.2] unanswered, while its second rebuttal was clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.5 — multi-year warranty illusory plus adherence failure: CON showed contracts can't track churn, attribute distant CV events, or claw back spend on patients who stop within months, neutralizing PRO's price-control mechanism.
