### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by distinguishing long-term freedom from zero oversight. Side A repeatedly treated competition at entry and periodic review as disproving block funding, although both are compatible with the proposition. B then offered the stronger weighing: in heavy-tailed fundamental science, tolerating some visible waste is preferable to systematically producing false negatives, safe projects, and premature judgments. A raised serious concerns about institutional capture and corrective feedback, but overstated block grants as forbidding all mid-course judgment and never fully answered proposal distortion under short cycles.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively answered Side A's pressure by noting that the motion accepts less accountability, not none, and by separating retrospective review of a long body of work from repeated enforcement of promised short-term aims. This exposed Side A's central attack as aimed at a more extreme model than B defended.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A won by effectively embracing the proposition's tradeoff and weaponizing Side B's own mechanisms against them. When B praised short-cycle grants for enabling 'continuous reallocation' away from failing projects, A decisively flipped this by arguing that in fundamental science, early failure is indistinguishable from gestation, meaning B's 'reallocation' is actually institutionalized misdiagnosis. A also successfully defended their examples by pointing out the motion specifies 'less' accountability, not zero.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2, where A flips B's "continuous reallocation" into the exact pathology of the short-cycle system by explaining that year-three legibility kills gestating breakthroughs.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) built the cleaner path by centering heavy-tailed discovery, the indistinguishability of mid-cycle gestation vs failure, and visible salary waste vs invisible lost breakthroughs; CON pressed the parasite/inheritance/freeze points effectively and forced useful distinctions on review, but never fully neutralized the false-negative cost or showed why continuous short-cycle reallocation is net better for fundamental science under the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's [B4.5]-[B4.7] on year-3 signals being undetectable and trading zombie salary for lost-tail variance, which reframed continuous reallocation as the real pathology.
