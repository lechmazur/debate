### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the more analytically disciplined case. Its core definition—populism's distinctive feature is the exclusive claim that one faction constitutes "the people," treating courts, media, and opposition as illegitimate rather than merely wrong—survived CON's tautology charge. PRO correctly pointed out that defining a phenomenon by its characteristic ideology is not the same as defining it by its outcome; a movement can hold the anti-pluralist logic and still be contained by strong institutions. CON's circularity critique was rhetorically effective but analytically weaker than it needed to be, because PRO successfully defended a mechanism-based, not outcome-based, definition. PRO's most decisive framing—"a democracy surviving a stress test does not prove the stress was medicine"—directly answered CON's mature-democracy base-rate challenge without conceding the core motion. PRO also partially closed the empirical gap with US "fake news/deep state" and UK Brexit judge-attack examples showing that even stable democracies exhibit the institutional delegitimation pattern under populist pressure. CON's strongest live route was the "usually" burden: the long list of mature-democracy populist movements (Syriza, Podemos, Five Star, Nordic parties, Tea Party) that produced disruption without backsliding. PRO addressed this structurally (containment ≠ medicine; survival ≠ health) but never established a clear majority count showing warning-sign cases outnumber corrective cases across all democracies. That gap kept CON competitive throughout. However, PRO's absorption of CON's historical examples (Progressives, FDR) through the pluralism distinction was largely successful, and PRO's mechanism gave the debate a cleaner analytical spine. Multiple clipped PRO turns are a real execution cost, but enough of each argument came through to assess the substance. The margin is real but modest because CON's base-rate empirical challenge was genuine and partially unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's tautology pressure question was the most decisive exchange. CON pressed that PRO's definition reduces to "anti-pluralist movements are usually anti-pluralist." PRO responded that its definition specifies a mechanism—converting accountability into delegitimation of neutral referees—not merely an outcome, and added US and UK examples to show the same logic operates in mature democracies even when institutions survive. This successfully defended the definitional line and partially closed the empirical gap CON had opened, shifting the burden back to CON to show that most movements claiming to speak for "the people" do not deploy the delegitimating logic PRO identified.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by establishing that Side B's narrow anti-pluralist definition excludes healthy populist corrections via post-hoc relabeling, successfully pressing the base rate question on mature democracies, and showing PRO examples are mostly fragile-system outliers while CON examples demonstrate corrective pressure without automatic backsliding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which begins with direct A1/A2 answers to pressure questions on tautology and base rate before crystallizing the definitional clash.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully defending a functional definition of populism and turning Side A's broad categorization against them. A's strategy relied on folding nearly all mass reform movements (Progressives, FDR, labor) into "populism," which B effectively exposed as emptying the term of its distinctive anti-pluralist feature. B's analogies ("smoke warns you because the fire is burning," "surviving a stress test does not prove the stress was medicine") cleanly separated the warning-sign mechanism from the underlying decay, neutralizing A's causation argument. Crucially, A conceded in Rebuttal 2 that the exclusive "only we represent the people" claim is genuinely dangerous, which undermined their own broad definition and handed B the decisive distinction. B maintained tighter weighing on the "usually" standard and closed with a clearer, more damaging comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 answer to the base-rate pressure question: acknowledging that mature democracies often survive populist surges but arguing that guardrail containment does not make the movement healthy, while pointing to US/UK institutional stress to show the same delegitimizing logic operates across consolidated systems.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO wins on three interlocking arguments CON never fully neutralized. First, the cross-continental pattern (Orbán, Erdoğan, Chávez, Bolsonaro, Modi, PiS, Marcos) is too consistent across starting conditions to be explained away as selection bias alone. Second, the institutional credit argument — when populism is "corrective," the constructive work is done by institutions taming the movement, not by the movement itself — cleanly handles CON's denominator objection: movements that moderate do so because they are contained, which is resilience, not correction. Third, PRO's inception-framing test (a movement's foundational rhetoric toward institutions, observable before outcomes materialize) partially answers CON's circularity charge without being outcome-dependent. CON's strongest moves — the unfalsifiability charge and the selection-bias/denominator problem — were genuine pressure points, but PRO adapted to both in Rebuttal 2. CON's best counter-example (Five Star) was thin; PRO's closing correctly noted it fragmented under constraints it did not build. CON also suffered real execution costs from being clipped in both the Opening and Rebuttal 1, leaving key burdens incompletely developed. PRO's "immune system vs. pathogen" framing kept the key stakes clear across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 (B3.1) — demanding a case where a populist movement that actually gained power strengthened courts, press, or legislative oversight — was the most decisive exchange. CON's answer (Five Star) was the weakest moment in the debate: a movement that never consolidated institutional control and ultimately fragmented. This concretely exposed the gap in CON's corrective-use case and validated PRO's core empirical claim about the direction of causation once populists hold power.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO maintained a coherent definition of populism as anti-institutional framing and showed consistent erosion patterns when in power (Orbán, Erdoğan, etc.), while CON's redefinition and Five Star example did not demonstrate net strengthening of democratic checks, leaving PRO's "usually" claim stronger after exchanges.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answered pressure questions on pre-outcome classification and credit attribution, then weighed the track-record asymmetry to neutralize CON's moderator/coalition defense.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B wins by maintaining a tighter, more defensible definition of populism centered on anti-institutional framing, which directly links to the motion's claim about decay. B effectively neutralizes A's historical examples by distinguishing reform movements that work through institutions from populist movements that treat institutions as enemies. B's pathogen versus immune system framing successfully turns A's moderation argument: institutional constraints stopping backsliding proves populism was a threat, not a corrective. A's Five Star example answered pressure but lacked the clear institutional-strengthening track record needed to overturn B's cross-continental pattern. B's closing sharply capitalized on A's concession that the real danger is illiberal concentration, arguing that populism's inherent logic points exactly there.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 dismantled A's historical examples by introducing the working-through versus working-against institutions distinction, rendering A's Progressive and labor examples non-populist by the operative definition. B's answer to A's pressure question on classification (foundational framing at inception) was clean and prevented A's unfalsifiability charge from sticking.
