### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's economic mechanisms. A successfully argued that institutional equity is more resilient than bank debt during downturns, and that B's cap would destroy the retirement equity and collateral of current farmers. When B attempted to solve this by proposing grandfathering and public guarantees, A cleanly leveraged those concessions to show B's policy fails its own goals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively weaponized Side B's concessions about grandfathering and public guarantees, proving the cap would still force entrants into debt while banning their best landlord options.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash clearer: institutional equity is more patient through downturns than the debt it would be replaced by, tenancy from non-farming owners expands (not blocks) young-farmer access, and harms of short leases/monoculture are better fixed by tenure and practice rules than by a class-wide ownership ceiling. PRO’s rootedness and power narrative stayed live but was undercut by its own concessions that caps need grandfathering plus public guarantees/land banks to avoid crashing retirement collateral—revealing the proposition adds fragility rather than resilience. CON’s consistent “conduct not capital” frame and answers to pressure resolved the choice more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s price/succession contradiction (A2.1–A2.5 and carried into A4/A5): you cannot treat inflated prices as the entry barrier then crash them via banned buyers without destroying the seller equity and rental landlord pool the next generation needs.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by demonstrating that the proposed cap would undermine the very resilience and rural succession it claims to protect. B effectively exposed a tension in A's case—simultaneously complaining that land is too expensive and promising to crash its value—and showed that young farmers need affordable rents (enabled by patient institutional landlords), not debt-fueled purchases. B's reframing of institutional equity as counter-cyclical and more resilient than mortgage debt, combined with the concrete alternative of conduct regulation and secure leases, provided a cleaner path to victory. A's enforcement argument and powerful closing rhetoric were compelling, but never fully overcame the capital-structure and entry-mechanism problems B raised.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's sustained rebuttal that capping ownership destroys the retirement collateral and rental market that actually enable young farmers to enter, while swapping resilient patient equity for fragile, pro-cyclical debt.
