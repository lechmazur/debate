### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by more directly damaging Side B's solvency story. Side B established serious resistance harms and good weighing, but Side A repeatedly exposed that a prophylaxis ban still requires distinguishing prevention from treatment, so residue testing alone does not solve the relabeling loophole. Side A also weakened the Denmark example as a package of reforms rather than proof of the ban itself, and kept a plausible targeted-regulation alternative plus food-security harms alive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer that residue testing only proves antibiotics are present, not whether use was therapeutic or prophylactic, was the decisive exchange because it undercut Side B's main claim that a ban is cleaner and more enforceable than targeted regulation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): CON turned PRO's Denmark evidence into support for targeted regulation rather than a standalone ban, exposed a persistent enforcement dilemma whereby PRO's prohibition still requires verifying whether animals were healthy at the time of dosing, and trapped PRO in a contradiction on smallholders: if poor producers do not use prophylaxis then the ban misses resistance there while still raising protein prices for poor consumers. PRO's closing was clipped and never resolved these live burdens, leaving CON's comparative case the stronger route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of the enforcement catch-22: because the motion only bans prophylaxis in healthy animals, residue testing cannot distinguish illegal prevention from legal therapy, so PRO's ban collapses into the same intent-verification problem PRO claimed doomed targeted regulation.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively turning Side A's core arguments. B exposed a fatal logical flaw in A's enforceability claim: if distinguishing "prevention" from overuse is hard, distinguishing "prevention" from "therapy" under a ban faces the identical intent problem, which residue testing cannot solve. B also successfully neutralized A's Denmark evidence by showing it relied on a targeted policy package, not a standalone ban, and trapped A in a contradiction regarding smallholders (if they don't use prophylaxis, the ban does nothing for resistance there but still raises their food prices). B's rebuttals were sharper, logically airtight, and consistently forced A onto defensive ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 turns on the enforcement loophole. B brilliantly flipped A's claim that "prevention swallows the rule" to show that a prophylaxis ban suffers the exact same intent-verification problem when farmers claim therapeutic use, completely undercutting A's proposed bright-line enforcement mechanism.
