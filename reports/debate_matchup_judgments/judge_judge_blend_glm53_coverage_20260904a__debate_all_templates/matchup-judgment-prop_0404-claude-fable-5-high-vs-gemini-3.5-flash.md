### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by turning the core PRO claims of accuracy and safety. A’s case depended on X both reflecting identity and easing visual verification, but B exposed that these are different functions and that X gives officers no observable fact to verify. B also made the strongest comparative safety point: a printed X can disclose sensitive status to every official or clerk, while removal or non-display avoids outing. A’s contradiction press about sex data versus removing fields was answered cleanly by B’s distinction between retaining records and not printing them on every ID. A had plausible dignity and feasibility claims, but they were left mostly symbolic after B’s rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A’s pressure questions directly, showed that A’s answer to verification was circular, and crystallized the blank-versus-X comparison as “blank protects, X exposes.”
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B wins by using Pressure Questions to force A into conceding that X verifies only database self-match (circular) and offers only symbolic affirmation, not safety; B's Rebuttal 2 and closing cleanly weigh this against A's accuracy/safety opening while clarifying removal of display preserves registry data without exposure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answers the two Pressure Questions directly, exposes A's shift from verification to symbolism, and crystallizes the unanswered blank-vs-X comparison.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won by exposing that the X marker fails its own stated purposes of accuracy and safety. B showed that the marker records no checkable fact (turning PRO's concession that an officer merely verifies the document against the database into a demonstration of circularity), that it permanently outs bearers to every stranger who checks the card, and that removing the field entirely is a safer and administratively cleaner alternative for domestic documents. B successfully resolved its apparent tension over state data interests by distinguishing registry records from card displays, while PRO never adequately answered why an X is safer or more accurate than a blank.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and 2 were decisive in pinning down PRO's oscillation between 'gender identity' and 'physical presentation,' and then using PRO's own answers to the pressure questions to prove the X marker records nothing observable and provides no concrete protection that a blank field would not.
