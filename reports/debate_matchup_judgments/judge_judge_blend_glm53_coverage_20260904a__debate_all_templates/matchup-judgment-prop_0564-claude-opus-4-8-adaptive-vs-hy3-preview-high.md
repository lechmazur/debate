### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the central tradeoff clearer: fare-free transit may remove a price barrier, but it risks weakening the service low-income riders actually depend on, while targeted free passes and decriminalized fare enforcement can capture much of the equity benefit without eliminating fare revenue. Side B had a real access and anti-policing case, but never fully answered A's accounting point that fare collection savings are much smaller than fare revenue, and its shift to progressive taxes did not fully resolve the broad-based funding/regressivity challenge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was exposing the fare-collection argument: saving the cost of collecting fares does not replace the much larger revenue stream fares generate. That undercut B's claim that eliminating fares could simultaneously fund better service.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's funding and equity mechanisms. A's '90 cents on the dollar' math neutralized B's claim that eliminating fare collection could fund service improvements, and A correctly pointed out that relying solely on taxes reduces revenue diversification. B's closing ignored these structural deficits, leaving A with a clear, unopposed path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's first rebuttal exposing the accounting flaw in B's plan—pointing out that eliminating fare collection saves a dime but loses the whole dollar, making it impossible to fund service improvements from those savings.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing the fatal fiscal trade-off in PRO's plan: eliminating fares saves minimal collection costs but vaporizes the full revenue dollar, forcing transit onto politically volatile general funds and undermining the service quality that low-income riders actually need. CON showed the same equity and policing gains could be captured more cheaply through targeted subsidies and decriminalization without sacrificing diversified revenue. PRO never recovered from the accounting and stability challenges, and their late shift to hypothetical progressive income levies contradicted the motion's explicit "broad-based taxation" frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dime-vs-dollar rebuttal in Turn 4, which demonstrated that PRO's "reallocation" was actually a massive net revenue loss, and that PRO's only response was to assume replacement taxes would appear without political friction or deadweight cost.
