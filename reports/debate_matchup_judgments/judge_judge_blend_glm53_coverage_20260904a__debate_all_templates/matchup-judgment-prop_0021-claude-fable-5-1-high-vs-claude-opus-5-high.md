### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B narrowly won because its deliberate-release examples, especially Yellowstone and Swiss lynx, prevented Side A from establishing that community hostility necessarily makes reintroduction fail. B also retained the ecological comparison: predators provide continuous population and behavioral control that A's improved culling regime did not fully replicate. A offered excellent political-feasibility analysis, a concrete consent rule, and a strong verification alternative, but overstated occasional illegal killing and top-ups as proof that established populations cannot restore ecosystems.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer to A's first pressure question was decisive: it supplied deliberate releases that established despite organized opposition, directly attacking A's central claim that consent is a prerequisite for survival. A limited those analogies effectively but did not eliminate them.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that forced reintroductions fail ecologically due to unverifiable sabotage. A brilliantly contrasted the impossibility of monitoring dead predators with the high verifiability of culling targets, and turned B's own concessions into a devastating critique of an endless "pipeline" of replacement animals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to Side B's question about fake culling targets: "You can fake a paper cull, but you cannot hide ten thousand red deer on an open hillside... A dead lynx is a hole in the ground nobody counts."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash on feasibility. PRO's need for regeneration was strong, but CON's crate-vs-paws distinction, verification asymmetry (can't hide 10k deer but can hide a dead lynx), and lynx/roe vs red-deer mismatch undercut that predators would work when imposed. PRO's own examples conceded decades of illegal killing and need for top-ups, and its answer on consent left CON's defined, time-limited, valley-level veto intact. Direct answers to B's pressure questions sealed the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.4 / A4.3-A4.4: natural recolonisation is blameless and can be funded without crates, while outcome-based deer/sapling counts make culling verifiable and coerced release slower and hidden.
