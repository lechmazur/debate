### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won the central comparison: an optional car provides immediate protection without logically preventing broader reform, while Side A never established that banning the option would force universal safety improvements. Side A offered a plausible institutional-substitution concern, but its claimed loss of equal access was weakened by B's point that women retain access to mixed cars. B's clipped opening was an execution cost, but its later rebuttals clearly resolved the motion's permissive and partial-solution framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was turning Side A's concession that women outside the reserved car face the same immediate physical risk into a decisive optionality argument: banning the car helps those women no more while removing protection from women able and willing to use it. B also exposed that Side A's substitution mechanism could apply equally to visible universal measures.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won by systematically reducing Side B's case to a theoretical institutional harm, then weighing it against concrete physical safety. Side A used the pressure questions perfectly to force two major concessions: that the displacement of other reforms was an unproven incentive rather than an observed fact, and that women outside the reserved car faced the exact same physical risk in Side B's world.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, where they flipped Side B's 'cheap visible answer' argument by pointing out that all of Side B's preferred alternatives (like patrol vests or cameras) could equally be used by agencies as superficial press-conference defenses.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won by keeping the debate anchored to the motion’s narrow permission question and its explicit "even if partial" framing. PRO’s core case—real women groped in body-to-body crushes get immediate relief—remained live after full exchange, while CON’s central objection (institutional substitution) collapsed when CON conceded no actual system had abandoned staffing, lighting, or prosecution after adopting the policy [B4.1]. PRO leveraged that concession ruthlessly in closing, showing CON offers identical physical risk for the woman who misses the car while denying the option to others. PRO also turned CON’s own remedy list against CON by identifying a women’s car as exactly the "design change that reduces anonymous crush" [A2.7]. CON’s reframing attempts and theoretical incentive story could not overcome the lack of grounding or the motion’s tolerance for partiality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s pressure question demanding one named transit system that abandoned universal reforms after adopting a women’s car [A3.1], which forced CON to concede "no specific cancellation is named" [B4.1], thereby reducing CON’s key institutional-drift argument from a live harm to an unsubstantiated hypothesis.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by showing plausible mechanisms through which the supposed extra option transfers risk: offenders remain, vulnerable nonusers become more isolated and blameable, and scarce enforcement concentrates in the marked car. Side B established immediate benefits for users, but repeatedly asserted that voluntary sorting could not worsen mixed cars without adequately answering those mechanisms. Its reliance on parallel safeguards and oversight was also weakened because the motion does not guarantee them and the systems are already defined by institutional failure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the optionality challenge with concrete risk-transfer mechanisms, then turned Side B’s own concentrated-enforcement claim and list of existing oversight institutions against it. Side B’s closing nevertheless claimed no mechanism had been supplied, leaving the central pressure unresolved.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won decisively by demonstrating a clear mechanism for how women-only cars actively harm the women left in mixed cars, which Side A repeatedly failed to neutralize and bizarrely claimed did not exist in its closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively weaponized Side A's own claims, showing that "concentrating" limited enforcement meant subtracting it from mixed cars, and that catching a man in the wrong car does not remove an actual groper from the system.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON (Side B) won by demonstrating that women-only cars reassign rather than reduce harm: finite enforcement and social attention shift away from mixed cars, leaving remaining women with the same predators but fewer witnesses and a new implicit blame structure. PRO’s claim that the measure is purely voluntary and additive never squarely answered the arithmetic of risk transfer or the institutional incentive to treat the car as a cheap substitute for systemic reform.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s dismantling of PRO’s seatbelt analogy and the turn around “name one harasser removed,” showing PRO’s only example was a trespasser ejected from the wrong carriage—not a groper caught for assault—proving the policy sorts harm rather than reducing it.
