### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but B built and sustained a more rigorous burden-of-proof framing: agreement between parents and clinicians establishes assent, not that the intervention is clinically warranted. B's Rebuttal 1 exposed a key asymmetry in A's case (treating puberty's certainty as equivalent to treatment's uncertain net benefit), and B correctly flagged that A's "persistent dysphoria" restriction was an unlicensed gloss on the motion's actual text—a point A never squarely answered. Most decisively, A's closing repeated the "reversible, monitored decision vs. guaranteed irreversible one" framing without addressing B's prior rebuttal (B4.8) that hormone therapy, which the motion explicitly covers, is itself irreversible—collapsing A's central asymmetry. B's closing then landed this unanswered point last, leaving the debate's core clash resolved in B's favor. A scored real points with the pressure question forcing B to concede that waiting also forecloses future authority, but B's reframing (difficulty of choice ≠ proof treatment is better) blunted that concession's force. Both sides handled Pressure Questions properly with clean A1/A2 structure, but B's overall rebuttal engagement was sharper and more consistently unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 point (B4.8) that "the safer mistake does not necessarily remain reversible" because hormone-induced changes are permanent, which A's closing failed to rebut, directly undercutting A's central reversible-vs-irreversible asymmetry framing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by consistently enforcing the distinction between the certainty of a physical process (natal puberty) and the uncertainty of a clinical outcome (treatment), and by preventing Side B from using the reversibility of puberty blockers to shield the irreversibility of hormone therapy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly handled a tough pressure question, conceding that waiting does not preserve complete bodily authority, but maintaining that this cost does not automatically justify the separate, permanent risks of hormone therapy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing that deferral is not a neutral preservation of future autonomy, since natal puberty imposes its own irreversible bodily changes before adulthood. Side B’s concession that waiting “does not preserve complete authority” defanged the core autonomy argument, leaving B relying on an evidence standard that A showed was both unattainable under a ban and asymmetrical—B demanded proof of net benefit for treatment while offering no comparable proof that waiting was safe. A’s rebuttals consistently returned to this structural asymmetry: the motion permits a monitored, individually gated pathway, whereas the status quo guarantees one irreversible trajectory. B’s skepticism about long-term outcomes and hormone permanence was substantively sound but ultimately could not overcome the problem that the default it defended was itself actively and irreversibly harmful.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s first rebuttal establishing that “deferral is not abstention” and that uncertainty applies to both branches, paired with Side B’s honest but damaging concession in its second rebuttal that waiting closes bodily options and does not preserve complete authority.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and answered pressure questions in proper A1/A2 format, but B built the tighter throughline. B's regret-asymmetry argument (waiting preserves the option to transition later, while early treatment forecloses fertility/sexual function permanently) was never fully neutralized: A's counter that voice/breast changes are "also irreversible" doesn't rebut that transition remains available after waiting, whereas fertility does not remain available after early treatment. B also used the pressure round effectively, forcing A to concede a categorical pre-pubertal exclusion, then leveraging that concession to argue A's own framework already accepts bright lines, undermining A's "individualized assessment only" purity. A's strongest counter-punch — that B would deny even a demonstrably mature 17-year-old with full parental/clinical support, exposing B's rule as unable to recognize a justified exception — is a real and damaging point, and B's answer ("age lines are imperfect but less dangerous") is more assertion than resolution. That keeps the debate close, but B's core asymmetry argument remained the more decisive, less-answered piece of clash by the closings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's regret-asymmetry response in Rebuttal 2 (B4.1-B4.2), distinguishing "waiting still allows later transition" from "sterilization cannot be undone," combined with B's use of A's pre-pubertal concession to expose inconsistency in A's individualized-access framework.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully breaking down Side A's "regret asymmetry." By proving that compulsory waiting also results in irreversible physical changes, B forced A to rely on a naturalistic distinction (that unwanted puberty is a "normal outcome" rather than a clinical one). B also cleanly dismantled A's claim that adolescents cannot understand fertility without adult experience, noting that many adults make fertility choices without having been parents.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A2 in Rebuttal 2 decisively neutralized A's argument about the inability to consent to infertility, pointing out that understanding a consequence does not require having already lived the life it affects.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO showed that CON’s categorical ban could not justify refusing treatment even to a mature 17-year-old with demonstrated understanding, reducing CON’s position to a formalistic reliance on a birthday rather than substantive patient assessment. PRO also undermined CON’s central asymmetry by demonstrating that unwanted natural puberty inflicts real, irreversible harm, so waiting is not a neutral preservation of options. While CON’s proxy-consent concerns were philosophically serious, they were not enough to overcome PRO’s more adaptable, individually responsive framework.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s pressure questions and follow-up rebuttals on the mature 17-year-old and the irreversibility of unwanted puberty, which exposed that CON’s age-18 line was legalistic rather than developmental and that classifying natural puberty as merely “normal” ignores genuine patient harm.
