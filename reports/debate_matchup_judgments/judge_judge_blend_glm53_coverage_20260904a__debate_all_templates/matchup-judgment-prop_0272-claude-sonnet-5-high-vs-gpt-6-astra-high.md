### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A effectively weaponized the 'before expanding' wording of the motion, proving that categorical sequencing forces vulnerable students to wait for slow physical infrastructure pipelines to clear before they can access ready-now digital enrichment. Side B made a strong case that physical needs will be permanently deferred if not prioritized, but A successfully showed that B's strict freeze harms students without actually speeding up those physical pipelines.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1) directly answered B's Q1 by proving that the speed and cost asymmetry between physical and digital programs is a structural reality (certifications, liability, pipelines) rather than an unfairly stacked comparison.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO’s category-based sequencing is overbroad and produces gratuitous harm. CON showed that making digital programs wait does not accelerate physical, arts, or play provision—because the bottlenecks for physical programs (specialized staffing, adaptive equipment, liability coverage, facilities) are structurally different from launching a coding club on existing library terminals. Since the categories often do not compete for the same fungible dollars, freezing digital expansion sacrifices real students (disabled, rural, transportation-limited) without helping the physical-access child any faster. PRO’s “hardest-to-replace” frame and equity argument were coherent, but they never demonstrated that a categorical rule outperforms need-based triage, and their concession that “some digital expansion will wait” allowed CON to hammer the concrete cost of delay across multiple rounds. CON’s closing crystallized the clash cleanly: triage by need protects every child; triage by category sacrifices some for no one’s gain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal (A4), which defended the structural asymmetry between launching digital clubs and launching compliant physical/arts programs, and showed that PRO’s categorical freeze stalls ready-now digital offerings that serve entirely different needs than the playground funding PRO wants to prioritize.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): CON won the sequencing clash. PRO argued physical play is irreplaceable and needs equity protection, but conceded "some digital expansion will wait" and gave only a vague "meaningful increase" mechanism. CON used that concession to show a category-wide freeze harms disabled/rural kids with ready-now digital options while not making playgrounds appear faster, and clarified its need-based triage still prioritizes a child lacking safe play because coding doesn't meet that need. PRO's best point about finite budgets exposed CON inconsistency but didn't restore a reason to sequence by category rather than by need.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 turning B2.6 concession into structural asymmetry attack [A2.1-2.5] and Rebuttal 2 A2 clarifying its rule matches remedy to unmet need, so physical-play kids stay prioritized without freezing unrelated digital clubs.
