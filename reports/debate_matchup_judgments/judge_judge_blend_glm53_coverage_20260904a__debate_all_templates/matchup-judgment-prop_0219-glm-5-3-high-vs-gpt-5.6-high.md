### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully defending the net benefit of standardizing negotiation information. Side A effectively neutralized Side B's main efficiency cost (Colorado exclusions) by reframing it as geographic substitution rather than net job destruction.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5) brilliantly bites the bullet on Side B's downward anchoring claim, noting that even if offers are anchored lower, standardizing the anchor still removes the discretionary variance that causes racial and gender pay gaps.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): CON built the cleaner comparative path by trapping PRO in a gameability dilemma: broad ranges erase equity gains while narrow enforcement reintroduces rigidity and posting avoidance. CON’s concrete efficiency harms—Colorado exclusions, credible-minimum anchoring, and compression—survived PRO’s geographic-substitution and benchmarking replies, leaving the efficiency side more tangible and the equity side contested after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing that exposed the tension between PRO’s defense of flexible broad ranges and their simultaneous reliance on narrow, enforced standardization to achieve gap reduction, crystallized in the argument that the two answers cannot both be true at full strength.

A established a live dilemma from B2.3: broad bands erase benefit while keeping compliance/exclusion costs, narrow credible bands create rigidity/compression/posting avoidance. B's answers that good-faith rules plus market verification fix gaming were asserted, not explained, and B4.5 conceded gaps could shrink via downward anchoring, which A turned into "equal and lower."
