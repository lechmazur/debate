### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing the breadth and vagueness of granting estates control over artistic style. Side A repeatedly narrowed the motion to deceptive commercial marketing or explicit invocation of a named artist, but that is not the full proposition and largely overlaps with false endorsement. B then showed that the remaining application is a veto over truthful stylistic tribute, creating chilling effects and favoring wealthy rights holders. A retained a plausible case about irreversible reputational harm, but its two clipped rebuttals, questionable opening examples, and failure to resolve B's duplication-versus-prior-veto dilemma left B with the cleaner decision path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's final dilemma was decisive: deceptive claims can be targeted through passing-off rules, while truthful labeling would make permission a direct toll on stylistic influence. This turned Side A's narrowing concession against it and showed that its defense either duplicated existing remedies or failed to defend the motion's broader wording.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won by exposing a fatal dilemma: the motion either targets deceptive AI fakes, which existing false-endorsement and passing-off laws already address, or it targets honest, labeled stylistic tributes, which improperly chills influence and free expression. A never escaped this fork despite narrowing the rule to commercial marketing, a concession B exploited to show the duty rides on invocation of a name rather than deception. B also successfully neutralized A’s power-dynamics and prior-restraint concerns by showing that bad-faith actors will ignore permission requirements anyway, while honest creators bear the chilling cost. A’s case was further weakened by two word-limit-clipped rebuttals, a real execution cost. B’s closing crystallization—“Punish the lie, permit the echo”—provided the cleanest comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sustained dilemma that the motion either duplicates existing false-endorsement law (for deceptive fakes) or becomes prior restraint on honest tribute (for labeled stylistic echoes), sharpened across Rebuttal 2 and Closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won the definitional clash. PRO narrowed the rule to explicit commercial invocation of a specific dead artist, but CON's dilemma showed that prong is already covered by false endorsement (fake 'lost' tracks) while truthful labeling ('AI in style of...') would turn influence into property, chilling honest tributes. CON also showed permission doesn't stop bad-faith labs but burdens compliant indie creators and consolidates estate/label power. PRO's two rebuttals were clipped for length, weakening its answers to that ex-ante knowability and enforcement cost pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing dilemma: if marketing lies it's already punishable, if marketing is truthful the only harm is stylistic echo = influence not theft, so the motion either duplicates existing law or becomes prior restraint on honest tribute – PRO never resolved this after narrowing its trigger.
