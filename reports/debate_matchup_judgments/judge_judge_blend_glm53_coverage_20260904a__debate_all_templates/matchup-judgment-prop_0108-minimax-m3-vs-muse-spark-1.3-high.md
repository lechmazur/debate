### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases, but CON (Side A) landed the more consequential blow: it forced PRO to concede that coalition-style moderation is "structurally unavailable" in a presidential system, then used that concession to argue PR's headline mechanism cannot function as advertised in Congress. PRO's answer (separating RCV's majority-threshold logic from PR's coalition logic) was reasonable but muddied the two reforms rather than cleanly defending each. CON also pressed a live and under-addressed point in the final rebuttal/closing — that RCV/PR mechanisms mainly bite in competitive districts while the actual engine of polarization (safe, gerrymandered, low-turnout primary seats) remains largely untouched; PRO's multi-member-district answer address this in principle but was met with a real (if contested) localism/dilution counter that was never fully resolved. PRO's Duverger-trap framing and "who must you persuade" reframe were rhetorically strong and mechanistically plausible, and its rebuttal of the parliamentary-collapse analogy (no executive to fall) was effective, but it never fully closed the "mechanism without coalition government" gap CON opened, nor decisively answered the safe-seat critique in the final exchange. That leaves CON with the cleaner, more resilient path to the decision by the end of the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 move ("if Congress cannot form or select the executive, what exactly does PR accomplish?") turning PRO's own admission about the absence of parliamentary collapse into a concession that PR's core moderation mechanism is unavailable — a point PRO's later answer (separating RCV's majority-threshold logic from PR's coalition logic) only partially neutralized.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins decisively because Side A hinges its closing on a fabricated concession that Side B explicitly denied.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 cleanly rejecting Side A's invented concession about coalition moderation being "structurally unavailable" and turning the fragmentation harm into a point about transparent negotiation versus secret hostage-taking.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side B wins by effectively exposing the structural limits of the proposition's reforms in the US context. B's argument that RCV does nothing to fix polarization in safe 70-30 seats (where primary extremism actually lives) is a decisive blow against the RCV half of the motion. Furthermore, B's critique that proportional representation in a presidential system multiplies legislative veto points without the disciplining mechanism of parliamentary coalition-building is sharper than A's defense. While Side A's rhetoric and framing are exceptional, Side B's rigorous dismantling of the institutional mechanics gives it the edge.
