### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-organized cases and honored the PQ format with proper A1/A2 answers. The pivotal clash was over what actually causes decade-long transmission delays. B offered a tighter causal chain (interconnection queues are backlogged because transmission capacity doesn't exist, and that capacity can't be built while permits are tied up in litigation), and pressed this specifically against A's "supply chain/utility monopoly" counter-narrative. A never fully dismantled this chain, instead reasserting alternative causes without directly refuting why review-driven delays wouldn't shrink under streamlining. A's strongest material — sacrifice zones, tribal land, and the equity argument that litigation is the primary shield for marginalized communities — was partially blunted by B's response that current review already forces siting into marginalized areas and that the motion only removes "some" pathways, not all protections, though this answer stayed somewhat asserted rather than deeply substantiated. A's backlash-paradox argument was live throughout but B's response (that paralysis under status quo is already causing comparable political dysfunction, and that preemption is the necessary trade-off) gave a workable weighing frame tied to the urgency thesis. Overall B did more of the comparative weighing work explicitly demanded by the motion's language ("even when it reduces some review"), while A's case, though principled and clearly argued, relied more on parallel assertions that were never fully closed off against B's rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's [B2.1]-[B2.2] causal chain on interconnection queues/transmission capacity, and A's inability in [A4.2] to fully refute it beyond re-listing alternate causes, was the most decisive exchange shaping the outcome.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by establishing a clearer causal hierarchy: decade-long permitting delays block the transmission buildout necessary for decarbonization, and the local ecosystems CON seeks to protect will be destroyed by systemic climate collapse long before siting errors matter. CON's democratic-legitimacy and bad-siting objections remained live, but they never squarely answered PRO's timeline pressure or showed how exhaustive local review could function within climate constraints without simply conceding the decade-long paralysis PRO identified. PRO also cleanly turned CON's wildfire and backlash arguments by showing unchecked warming creates the tinderbox conditions and that local vetoes already paralyze the grid.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal argument that existing local zoning bans already paralyze grid buildout, so streamlining and federal preemption are necessary to prevent hyper-local holdouts from holding continental decarbonization hostage—directly turning CON's backlash pressure into a reason to affirm.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won the crucial clash over gridlock bottlenecks by effectively arguing that projects cannot enter supply chains or interconnection queues until they clear permitting, neutralizing Side B's claim that supply chains were the true independent bottleneck. Side A also delivered a more decisive rhetorical crystallization in the closing, exposing Side B's reliance on an illusion of "frictionless" infrastructure planning. While Side B's warnings about political backlash and bad siting were strong, Side A's structural arguments about the incompatibility of local consensus with continent-spanning infrastructure were more compelling and directly justified the proposition's tradeoffs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 point that "You cannot order a transformer for a project that a local lawsuit has stalled indefinitely," which effectively dismantled Side B's argument that supply chain shortages and interconnection queues were the real bottlenecks independent of permitting delays.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but PRO's throughline—that procedural delay is not environmentally neutral and that interstate transmission is structurally incompatible with unlimited local veto points—was sustained more effectively across rounds. CON's strongest original contribution (that wealthy enclaves use zoning/political capital rather than courts, so stripping litigation disarms only the marginalized, and that removing citizen-suit threat causes agency rubber-stamping) was compelling but never fully pressed home; PRO directly disputed the premise in A2 rather than merely dodging. Crucially, in the Pressure Questions exchange CON's answers to PRO's sharpest question (about single-jurisdiction litigation attrition and fossil-plant continuity during review) were evasive—A4.1 assumed only "legitimate" suits would be filed, ignoring the attrition-tactic problem PRO had emphasized, and A4.2 addressed only administrative backlog, not litigation-driven delay, which was the actual crux of the question. PRO's answers to CON's questions, by contrast, offered a specific alternative mechanism (consolidated review with binding mitigation standards) and directly contested CON's equity narrative. This made PRO's case marginally more resilient under pressure, and its closing crystallized the clash more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1/Q2 pressure questions about single-jurisdiction litigation attrition and continued fossil pollution during review, followed by CON's A1/A2 answers that sidestepped the attrition-tactic and litigation-delay mechanics rather than directly resolving them, was the most decisive exchange, exposing a real gap in CON's practical alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO won because its core argument—that interregional transmission requires crossing dozens of jurisdictions, making unconstrained local litigation a structural veto that guarantees fossil fuel permanence—remained live after the full exchange. CON responded with strong environmental justice and smart-siting claims, but never mechanistically resolved how to prevent multi-jurisdictional procedural paralysis without streamlining, relying instead on asserted alternatives like "funding staff" and "forcing compliance from the start." PRO effectively showed that delay is not environmentally neutral and that procedural perfectionism locks in the very fossil harms CON claims to oppose.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal that wealthy enclaves do not need litigation to block projects while marginalized communities depend on it as their only equalizer, directly blunting PRO's environmental justice flip.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built a much stronger and more realistic case regarding the structural realities of multi-jurisdictional infrastructure and the environmental cost of delay. Side A effectively neutralized Side B's environmental justice arguments by highlighting the localized toll of prolonged fossil fuel pollution, and decisively defeated Side B's weak "hire more staff" solution to the holdout problem. Side B's reliance on "smart siting" and "just plan compliant routes" failed to address the weaponization of litigation pathways that Side A successfully targeted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "hire more staff" answer in the closing, highlighting that administrative expansion cannot fix a structural framework where any single localized holdout possesses a perpetual legal veto.
