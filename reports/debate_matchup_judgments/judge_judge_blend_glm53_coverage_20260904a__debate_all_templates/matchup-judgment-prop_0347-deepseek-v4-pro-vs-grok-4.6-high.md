### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won a competitive debate by showing that systemic cleanup is compatible with, rather than a substitute for, protection against losing access while cleanup is pending. B raised a serious strategic-refusal problem, but repeatedly treated the motion's objective condition as automatic self-declaration. A supplied plausible pattern-based limits and preserved accountability through completion data and verified complaints. B's proposed independent review either entails delay or requires an immediate universal freeze that largely concedes A's case against compulsory use of the contaminated score.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest response was that if platforms cannot distinguish bias from legitimate criticism, they cannot safely make the disputed score a condition of access. This exposed the timing gap in B's independent-review alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): A wins because it exposed the motion’s core mechanism as an unworkable, self-executing exemption. Without an independent, non-self-serving threshold to separate genuine bias from strategic refusal, the right collapses into a general veto that shields bad actors and turns unrated status into a market penalty for honest workers. PRO never operationalized the trigger convincingly, and CON’s alternative—immediate platform-wide suspension of tainted ratings plus independent review—captures the urgency PRO demands without accepting the motion’s perverse incentives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.4–A2.5 and A4.1–A4.2: CON’s trust-infrastructure/unrated-pool argument combined with the platform-wide suspension alternative, which answers PRO’s “wait” problem while showing individual opt-out is the inferior remedy.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): CON won the operational clash. PRO kept the moral urgency but never closed the threshold gap for "strongly shaped" – B asked for a non-self-serving test and PRO offered patterns platforms "already see" without adjudication. B's answer to pressure – immediate platform-wide suspension/quarantine via independent review rather than individual self-declaration – preserves PRO's core (no forced submission to a bias machine) while avoiding the unrated-pool warning label and strategic exit that PRO left live even with completion data.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2 – B reframes PRO's forced-choice pressure by conceding workers should not lose access, but argues the dirty signal should be removed for everyone via independent review, breaking the submit-or-lose-jobs dichotomy and exposing individual refusal as the worst option.
