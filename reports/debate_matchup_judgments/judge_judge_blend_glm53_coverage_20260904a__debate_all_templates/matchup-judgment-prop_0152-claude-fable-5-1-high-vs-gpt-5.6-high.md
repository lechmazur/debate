### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won by systematically dismantling the resolution's core mechanism, proving that "realistic alternatives" are neither necessary (a priest must wear vestments regardless of other job options) nor sufficient (an employer shouldn't force a janitor to wear religious symbols just because other jobs exist) to justify dress codes. PRO argued well but failed to overcome the critique that their model commodifies conscience based on local job markets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal exposed a fatal flaw in PRO's "self-tightening market" defense by pointing out that a religious employer in a large city could force arbitrary religious dress on a janitor simply because alternative jobs exist, perfectly illustrating why exit does not equal consent.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO successfully showed that CON conceded the core "some room" principle and then demonstrated that CON's alternative functional boundary was either unadministrable without courts parsing religious roles or perversely bound the priest with no exit while freeing the mobile worker. PRO's voluntariness-and-exit framework handled the hardest cases by weighing realistic costs and preserved intuitive pluralism. CON's "exit is not consent" argument remained philosophically forceful but never overcame the practical line-drawing deficits exposed under pressure, leaving PRO with the cleaner live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal exposed that CON's functional test required secular courts to make theological distinctions and was gameable by employers, whereas PRO's test—whether the person could realistically walk away—actually tracked conscience. CON never recovered from this structural critique.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won the boundary debate. PRO's condition was shown neither necessary (priest/rite vestments remain legitimate even with no nearby alternative) nor sufficient (janitor/nurse forced to conceal hijab just because 3 other jobs exist still regulates conscience, not function). PRO conceded "some room" exists, then its self-tightening reply was directly refuted and its R2 was clipped. CON's presence-vs-profession and representative-vs-ordinary distinction gave a clearer, exit-independent reason to allow what the motion wants to allow while blocking what it should block.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.2 plus B4.5 - vestments as actor-costume justified by representative function regardless of exit, so exit is neither necessary nor sufficient, and extending dress rules to all staff does not shrink the labor market, collapsing PRO's self-tightening mechanism.
