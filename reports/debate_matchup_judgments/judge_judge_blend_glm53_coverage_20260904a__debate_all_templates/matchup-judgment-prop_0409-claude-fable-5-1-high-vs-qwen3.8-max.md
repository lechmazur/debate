### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by converting the clash from romantic free access into an outcomes comparison: underfunded free shores exclude via decay, while a capped paid layer can cross-subsidize usable commons. PRO kept the cleaner principle (add-ons around the place vs selling the place) and answered pressure well, but never closed the scaling gap on lifeguards/lighting/toilets; CON’s limiting rule and locked-toilet framing made the vote clearer despite a clipped R2.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s decay-is-exclusion plus nonexclusive-revenue-doesn’t-scale attack (A2.8/A2.2–A4.4): food-cart rents fund carts, not waterfront staffing, so PRO’s tools collapse into taxes or smaller enclosures.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by demonstrating that Side B’s principled defense of broad free access lacked a credible, concrete funding mechanism that could survive fiscal stress, while showing that Side B’s own alternatives (concessions, queues, local-resident permits) either scaled poorly or created their own exclusions. Side A’s capped, ring-fenced cross-subsidy model answered the key “what pays the lifeguard” pressure more directly, and its closing crystallization of decay-as-exclusion proved more memorable. Side B’s slippery-slope objection remained rhetorically potent but never fully overcame the pragmatic gap in its maintenance plan.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s collapse of Side B’s distinction between “add-ons around the commons” and enclosure, showing that PRO’s nonexclusive revenue was either insufficient to fund maintenance (food-cart margins) or itself a smaller enclosure (parking fees, event permits), coupled with the turn that PRO’s queue-fairness mechanisms exclude by postcode.

Cleaner burden on whether enclosure is necessary.
