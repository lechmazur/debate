- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by cleanly differentiating the legal mechanics of personhood (adversarial standing) from Side B's alternative (co-management and standard-setting).   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 cleanly addressed B's pressure questions by contrasting the outcomes: "Co-management without personhood is a seat at the table where someone else holds the pen. Personhood gives the pen to a court."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that PRO's model either handed a self-selected guardian veto power over public decisions or, if it required plural consent and careful legislative design, collapsed into CON's preferred alternatives of co-management and ordinary bargaining.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which crystallized that because PRO admitted a request only triggered a process and the river could still lose, personhood added chiefly litigation cost and delay to the ordinary judicial review and political negotiation CON already supported.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live route: existing standards fail when regulators are captured, and personhood adds independent standing with fiduciary oversight, not an automatic veto.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's circle attack in A5/B4: CON's answer that communities can challenge unlawful permits is circular when the law itself tolerates destruction, exposing why co-management without rights-holder standing fails.
