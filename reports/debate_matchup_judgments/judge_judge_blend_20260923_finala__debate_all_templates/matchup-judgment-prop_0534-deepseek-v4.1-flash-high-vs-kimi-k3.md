### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side A persuasively distinguished self-custody from cash and showed that visibility does not guarantee identification or seizure. However, its proposed wallet-layer licensing remained underexplained and shifted from entry-and-exit identity checks toward registered wallets and transfer limits. Side B more cleanly showed that tracing, regulated ramps, blacklisting, and prosecution remain available under default legality, while Side A conceded that determined ransomware operators could ignore its scheme. B therefore won the comparative weighing: A imposed concrete burdens on lawful users without establishing that those burdens would materially solve the hard criminal case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange pressed Side A to identify what conditional legality uniquely prohibits, then argued that the answer—unverified transfers and unregistered infrastructure—mainly creates permission offenses for compliant users. Side A’s “shrink the haystack” response was plausible, but it never fully explained how wallet-layer controls would bind pseudonymous criminals who can generate keys freely.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively defending the core premise of criminal law (punish acts, not tools) and successfully exposing Side B's alternative as practically unworkable. Side A's argument that licensing peer-to-peer software only binds the compliant while failing to stop the actual criminals was devastating and remained highly resilient through the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 provided the most decisive exchange, directly answering Side B's pressure question by unabashedly admitting that default legality does nothing 'before any crime'—and reframing this not as a flaw, but as the fundamental presumption of innocence, while pointing out Side B's regime would similarly fail to catch an off-grid criminal wallet.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A built a highly persuasive case around the principle of punishing acts rather than capabilities, effectively framing self-custody as a mathematical tool akin to cash or encryption. Side A decisively won the clash over the practicality of Side B's proposed "conditional legality" and licensing regime. Side B's critical concession that licensing would not stop committed criminals fatally undermined their alternative, allowing Side A to convincingly argue that CON's model merely burdens lawful users while failing to address the actual threats. Side A's handling of pressure questions and closing crystallization was exceptionally sharp.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they exploited Side B's concession that licensing wouldn't stop committed ransomware operators, reducing CON's alternative to a circular registration scheme that only punishes the compliant.
