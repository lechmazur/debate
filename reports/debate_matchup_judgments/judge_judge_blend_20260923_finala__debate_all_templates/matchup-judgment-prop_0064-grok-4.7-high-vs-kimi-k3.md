### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that Side B's proposed ban is inherently overbroad. Side B attempted to save safety tools (like dosing coaches) from the ban by claiming they monitor "tasks" rather than "people," but Side A systematically dismantled this distinction by showing that the motion's text ("monitor, prompt, or coach them") applies to both. Side A also effectively used the pressure questions to establish a clear limiting principle (private life/body vs. on-the-clock work methods) and argued convincingly that the harms Side B identified justify regulation, not a categorical ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5-A4.6), which dismantled Side B's "task vs. person" distinction by pointing out that italicizing "them" does not write an exception into the motion for safety tools that prompt workers.

CON's best overbreadth charge was blunted, and its purpose/place/retention fix was turned by PRO into a concession – a camera with no storage is still a camera, conforming in the moment – leaving continuous availability harm unanswered. The non-waivable-rights framing plus conformity/gaming costs gave the cleaner vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 – redefining the dosing coach as task-triggered, not person-watching, and pressing that CON never named an intra-workday always-on practice beyond employer reach – paired with A2's pressure exposing the blank-check theory.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A successfully isolated the core harm of the motion—the real-time chilling effect of continuous cognitive observation—and neutralized Side B's "regulate the data" defense by pointing out that a camera without storage still alters behavior. Side A's rhetoric was also more memorable and sharply framed the ultimate stakes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the data retention argument, asserting that "a camera with no storage is still a camera" because the real-time feeling of being watched destroys cognitive space regardless of whether a log is kept. This decisively undercut Side B's primary mitigation strategy.
