### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): A (CON) built a more adaptive, comparative case: the pig analogy exposed that cognition alone can't be the trigger, forcing B to retreat to "cognition plus structural inability" — a concession A exploited hard. A's aquaria pressure question was the pivotal moment: B had to concede individual octopuses can be kept decently, conceding "only scale is disputed," which undercuts the motion's claim that confinement is inherently indefensible rather than an engineering/regulatory problem. A also pressed a live, unanswered comparative harm point (wild trap/trawl suffering vs. farmed slaughter) and an enforcement point (certification/import leverage beats an unenforceable global ban, which just offshores demand) that B never squarely rebutted, especially in closing. B's case remained coherent and well-grounded in ordinary cephalopod-cognition knowledge, and its burden-shifting move ("no working humane commercial model exists") was a reasonable rhetorical counter, but it repeatedly asserted market-expansion and irreducible cruelty without directly neutralizing A's comparative wild-capture and enforcement arguments. A's closing crystallized the clash more cleanly than B's, which mostly reasserted the burden-of-proof frame without answering the leverage/enforcement rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's aquaria pressure question (Q1) and B's necessary concession in A1 of Rebuttal 2 — admitting individual octopuses can be kept decently while trying (only partially successfully) to cordon that off from industrial scale — was the decisive exchange that reframed the debate from "impossible cruelty" to "unproven but improvable regulation," favoring A's framework.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully proving that the burden rests on Side B to demonstrate a working humane model for a new, unnecessary luxury industry. Side B's admission that commercial-scale humane farming is currently only a 'promise' allowed Side A to convincingly frame the debate as a reckless gamble with animal welfare driven by commercial economics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 and A5.1 successfully dismantled B's 'aquaria' trap by distinguishing between individual displays and the inescapable economic pressures of commercial mass breeding, turning B's admission of a hypothetical model into a fatal flaw.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the motion’s burden live: complex solitary cognition plus optional luxury food plus no working commercial humane model makes industrial farming a wager animals pay. CON’s reform path, aquaria analogy, and wild-capture comparison had real force, but stayed either hypothetical at scale or partly orthogonal; PRO’s replies on scale vs. individual housing and market expansion largely held. The debate stayed competitive because CON scored consistency and enforceability pressure, yet PRO made the decisive choice clearer after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s A1/A2 and follow-on: aquaria do not prove a meat supply chain, and farming adds scalable deprivation rather than merely swapping wild deaths—then leveraged CON’s admission that no finished commercial model exists.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) kept the debate anchored to the actual burden of the motion — a categorical, global, permanent ban requires showing no regulated system could ever meet a welfare floor — and consistently forced PRO to either concede that point or retreat into arguments about current bad practice. CON's moratorium move ("no validated stun, no license; no demonstrated enrichment, no license") absorbed PRO's strongest pressure question (no humane slaughter exists at scale) without surrendering the regulation-vs-ban framing, and CON's offshore/non-signatory relocation argument was raised early, repeated, and never squarely answered beyond the assertion that "bans still starve capital." PRO (B) delivered vivid, well-grounded biological arguments and landed real pressure with its two questions, but its answer to "where is the line" ("commercial production itself") was largely conclusory rather than demonstrated, and its rebuttal to the relocation/black-market critique stayed at the level of analogy (whaling, fur) rather than engaging the specific mechanism CON raised. Both sides avoided fake citations and argued with plausible, ordinary-knowledge mechanisms, but CON's tighter engagement with the actual wording of the motion and cleaner handling of the decisive concession gives it a clear, if competitive, edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange on the validated-slaughter-method gap: PRO's Q1 forced CON to admit no current method is licensable, but CON's A1 reframed this as a moratorium rather than a permanent global ban, and PRO's closing attack on that distinction, while sharp, never fully closed the gap CON opened between "not yet proven" and "impossible forever."
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by demonstrating a structural contradiction between octopus biology (requiring isolation to prevent cannibalism but suffering cognitively in solitary confinement) and the demands of industrial farming. Side B's pivot to advocating for a "moratorium" effectively conceded A's point about current conditions, while A successfully proved that commercial logic prevents B's highly regulated, individualized vision from ever scaling.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal, where they dismantle B's regulatory vision by arguing that commercial standardization will inevitably strip away B's theoretical enrichment, reducing it back to "a pipe in a bare box," and directly countering the "offshore" argument by noting bans starve the necessary legal capital.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion's high bar in view: PRO had to show industrial confinement is permanently indefensible, not merely hard or currently cruel. CON's burden shift held—cognition supports strict welfare rules, not automatic prohibition—and PRO's strongest pressure (no validated stun; isolation vs. enrichment double-bind) was absorbed as a licensing moratorium and method bans rather than a forever global ban. CON also won the comparative: ban displacement vs. conditional market access. PRO was vivid and biologically sharp but never fully closed the gap from bad factory models to categorical impossibility everywhere.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A1/A4.1 flip: refuse conscious slaughter today without converting present technique gaps into a permanent global ban.
