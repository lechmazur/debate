### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a competitive debate by narrowing the policy to halting additional long-lived terminals after already-approved capacity roughly doubles exports. That blunted A’s near-term scarcity case and shifted the burden to explaining why post-2030 LNG would still displace coal rather than renewables. B also showed that demand is partly created by infrastructure and contracts, while A repeatedly assumed that every withheld or resold cargo would otherwise replace something dirtier. A landed strong responses on dispatch economics, future scarcity, and cheap domestic gas undermining renewables, with the domestic-price objection never fully resolved. But B’s temporal weighing, lock-in analysis, and unrefuted Qatar comparison provided the cleaner overall path to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s most decisive exchange was the approved-capacity and lock-in response: near-term coal displacement could continue under capacity already authorized, while A still had to justify additional terminals operating into the 2040s. A answered with future demand growth, but its claim that resold gas would necessarily displace a dirtier fuel made its counterfactual difficult to falsify.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO won by defining a moderate cap (no new terminals beyond already-approved capacity that doubles shipments), forcing the clash onto 2040s lock-in rather than this decade’s coal, and showing CON’s displacement story became unfalsifiable (every resold molecule always beats something dirtier). CON’s rerouting and “engineered scarcity” lines were sharp early but never fully answered Qatar’s cleaner footprint or why infinite post-doubling buildout is required once this decade is conceded. PRO’s later weighing and closing crystallized the choice more cleanly despite clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s R1 contradiction (cheap gas is renewables’ enemy at home but coal’s enemy abroad) plus the pressure/closing extension that CON’s 2045 counterfactual can never lose.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO defined restriction as capping at already-approved capacity that roughly doubles exports, narrowing CON's scarcity and coal-displacement claims to post-2030 marginal terminals. CON never rebutted that Qatar leaks less per delivered unit [B2.5/B4.2] nor answered where scarcity arises in a doubling [B3.1], and its counterfactual that any resale always displaces dirtier became unfalsifiable [B5.2/A4.3]. B's lock-in/balance-sheet argument and luxury-good bidding war from 2022 better explained global methane.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): A won by showing that reduced U.S. supply can be replaced through a mix of coal and leakier foreign gas, while associated Permian gas may be flared and cheaper domestic gas weakens electrification incentives. B never established that its partial demand reduction outweighs those dirtier substitutions, and its domestic decarbonization story remained strained. A's two clipped rebuttals were execution costs but did not erase the substantive advantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A decisively answered B's claim that coal and foreign-gas substitution were contradictory: both can occur across the market, and both can be dirtier than U.S. LNG. The closing line that partial reduction helps only if the partial is clean exposed the central flaw in B's weighing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive round by keeping the decisive "compared to what" test live and showing that every plausible gap-filler—coal lock-in, leakier foreign gas, or Permian flaring—is dirtier than a U.S. cargo. PRO's best move, the partial-reduction/consistency frame, was sharp but insufficient once CON established that the partial itself is the dirty outcome, not clean residual savings. The domestic channel hurt PRO after a real flip from "higher prices slow electrification" to "keep gas cheap," and the oil-driven Permian point was never squarely answered. Clipped B rebuttals were a real cost but did not erase the cleaner comparative path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's domestic-price pressure and follow-through: catching PRO's higher-prices vs keep-gas-cheap tension, then locking it as cheap gas leaves the furnace/heat-pump ratio unchanged while flaring and coal fill the gaps.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the substitution and domestic-mechanism clashes. It showed restricted US LNG would be replaced by leakier foreign gas, 40-year coal, and increased Permian flaring because associated gas drilling follows oil price and flaring rules exempt stranded gas. Side B (PRO) conceded short-run coal burn and never resolved its price flip – that higher gas prices both hurt and help heat pumps – and its claim that planned restriction simply prevents terminal buildout was undercut by inelastic Asian demand growth. Partial reduction is not enough when the partial is dirtier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1 that enforcement and an export outlet are complements – restriction creates the no-takeaway flaring exemption and destroys the price incentive to capture methane – directly neutralized B's enforcement attack and turned the methane argument.
