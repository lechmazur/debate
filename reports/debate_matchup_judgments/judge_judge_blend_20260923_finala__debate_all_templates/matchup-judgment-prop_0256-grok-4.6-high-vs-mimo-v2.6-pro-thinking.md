### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): PRO opened with a compelling moral frame ("stop paying the ocean twice," equity for renters and the elderly) and pressed a real weakness in CON's early case with sharp pressure questions about the stopping rule and receiving capacity. But CON's answers in Rebuttal 2 supplied a concrete stopping rule (end reconstruction of repetitive-loss parcels, buy out and disperse across many functioning towns) and, crucially, turned PRO's own capacity objection back on them: if existing inland cities already ration housing, a brand-new city with zero infrastructure and no employers is worse positioned, not better. PRO's answers to CON's pressure questions then conceded that the refuge city would be phased, staffed by transferred existing public payroll, and dependent on firms "following occupied demand" — effectively collapsing PRO's distinctive proposal into CON's "third path" of targeted buyouts plus growth in existing places. CON's closing explicitly named this concession and used it to show the "rather than" framing in the motion no longer had a defensible referent, while PRO's closing mostly restated the plan without repairing the contradiction. CON's Rebuttal 1 was clipped for length, a real execution cost, but the substantive content lost was minimal and CON's subsequent speeches more than compensated by sharpening the decisive comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.2), pointing out that if existing inland cities already "ration homes and strain water" as PRO claims, a brand-new city built from raw land with no firms or tax base is even worse off — combined with PRO's own Rebuttal 2 answers admitting the refuge city would be phased and staffed by transferred existing payroll, which let CON's closing argue PRO had conceded to CON's own policy.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully dismantling the economic feasibility of building new cities from scratch, effectively comparing the difficulty of creating new labor markets on raw land against dispersing households into existing economies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which turned Side B's housing scarcity point by arguing that dispersing 50 households across 20 towns is easier than building a new city of 20,000 without an existing economy.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the "rather than" comparison. PRO establishes the waste of ritual rebuild, but CON gives a stopping rule - buy out repetitively lost parcels only, elevate/adapt the rest, disperse households via checks to many existing inland towns - and presses the funding and labor-market burden of a greenfield city. PRO's phase-and-transfer-payroll answers in B4 concede the city is incremental growth around existing payroll waiting for firms, collapsing distinctiveness while retaining debt-service risk of graded streets and water lines. PRO never solves its own scarcity objection: if existing towns ration homes, a raw-land city is less, not more, ready.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's turn that B's phased, payroll-transferring refuge city is just CON's "ordinary planning" with a ribbon, plus the counter that scattering 50 households to 20 towns dominates concentrating 20k on raw land where occupancy shortfalls must be carried as debt.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON (A) built the more disciplined case by exploiting a genuine tension in PRO's proposal: once PRO conceded (in A2 of Rebuttal 2) that elevation, buyouts, and nearby retreat remain funded for households that refuse the inland city, CON's pincer landed hard — either PRO has quietly abandoned the "rather than" substitution the motion requires, collapsing into CON's own policy, or the inland city still takes capital first and the refuser gets nothing. PRO never cleanly resolved this dilemma in Rebuttal 2 or Closing, instead reasserting that only "the most disaster-exposed" rebuilds end, which sits uneasily with CON's opening point that existing coastal policy already includes selective retreat, elevation, and buyouts — narrowing the real daylight between the two positions in CON's favor. CON also pressed effectively on labor-market realism (ports need embedded workers, not commuters or greenfield back-office jobs), and PRO's answer (warehousing, hospitals, construction-phase jobs) never fully answered why fishers and longshoremen would actually relocate for those anchors. PRO's opening and second rebuttal were both clipped for exceeding word limits, a real execution cost that cut off developing arguments (e.g., the rebuttal collapsing CON's equity claim). While PRO's "choice not eviction" framing and threshold argument were strong and its Q2 pressure question was sharp, CON's tighter, unresolved dilemma about the meaning of "rather than" was the more decisive thread running through the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 dilemma (A4.4-A4.6): forcing PRO's Q2 answer (still funding elevation/nearby moves for refusers) into a contradiction with the "rather than" framing of the motion — either PRO abandons strict substitution or leaves refusers with nothing — went unresolved through Closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling the economic feasibility of Side B's inland cities, proving that greenfield job anchors do not map to the skills of displaced coastal workers. Side A also provided a credible, localized alternative (nearby elevation and denser housing) that nullified Side B's claim that residents would have nowhere to go.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 argument that Side B's job solutions amount to "conversion, not choice," exposing the mismatch between coastal livelihoods and inland greenfield employment.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON wins a narrow comparative. PRO's central distinction - defend ports, not doomed residential blocks - is strong, but CON answers the decisive "where do people go?" with same-labor-market elevation and adjacent higher ground, and hits PRO's live feasibility gap: warehouses, a greenfield hospital, and construction [B4.1] do not replace waterfront livelihoods. PRO's answer to Q2 that elevation still stays funded softens the starvation charge but blurs "rather than" and leaves its one-time city without a durable jobs case. Two clipped PRO turns also trim its path. CON provides the cleaner functional threshold - stop when next dollar no longer reduces risk - without needing a new metro.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5/A5.3 - CON's turn on B's job anchors: back-office, warehouses, construction ending at pour, and training fishers into relocation roles is conversion not choice, leaving the inland city as empty streets that still needs occupants.
