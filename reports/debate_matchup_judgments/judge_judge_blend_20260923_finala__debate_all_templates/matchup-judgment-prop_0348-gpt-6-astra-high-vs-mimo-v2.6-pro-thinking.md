### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A successfully carved out a coherent middle ground: an integrated, voluntary AI doorway reduces access friction and connects patients to care without displacing human triage or making software compulsory. While B’s silent-failure safety argument was potent, A showed that imperfect monitoring also exists in human channels and that delayed access is itself a safety harm worth weighing. A’s cleaner handling of the pressure questions and more textually grounded interpretation of the motion give it the edge despite B’s rhetorical force.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 escape from B’s “dangerous or pointless” dilemma, demonstrating that optional integration with appointment routing is a meaningful policy change without compulsory displacement, and that imperfect oversight is a reason for governance rather than blanket discouragement.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live path: optional integrated front door reduces friction (history collection + direct appointment) without requiring compulsory gatekeeping, and bounded approval plus governance answers safety. CON's dilemma (dangerous if primary, pointless if optional) and silent-failure accountability critique was strong but relied on absolute invisibility, was clipped in both rebuttals, and never showed discouragement beats governing the tool alongside nurse-led access.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 handling of accountability and silent failure — reframing permission vs replacement, noting telephone triage shares info-poor limits, and offering concrete active follow-up/linked encounters as detection, conceding incompleteness while preserving institutional duty.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A successfully trapped Side B in a dilemma regarding the definition and function of a "front door." If the AI tool genuinely directs patient volume, it creates silent, unaccountable failures; if it is merely an optional convenience, it adds little beyond the status quo and fails to justify the motion's specific framing. Side B struggled to escape this trap, arguing for an "integrated optional route," but Side A effectively demonstrated that this either cannibalizes human triage or reduces the policy to a trivial scheduling link. Furthermore, Side A ruthlessly capitalized on Side B's pressure question concession that "an unreachable patient may remain unobserved," turning it into a fatal blow against the idea that formal institutional responsibility can govern an invisible failure mode. Side A's comparative weighing of visible, attributable human errors versus invisible AI errors was decisive and cleanly executed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's exploitation of Side B's concession in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing that "an unreachable patient may remain unobserved." Side A used this to shatter Side B's claim that institutional oversight and duty of care could mitigate the risks of AI under-triage, proving that a harm never detected cannot be governed.
