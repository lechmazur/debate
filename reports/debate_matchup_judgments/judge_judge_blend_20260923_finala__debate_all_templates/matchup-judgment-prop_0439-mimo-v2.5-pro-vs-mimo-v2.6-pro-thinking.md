### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON landed the more damaging and better-sustained pressure. CON's isolation of Iran's state-brokered, price-capped, matched-allocation system as a confound (rather than clean evidence that price alone drives supply) went largely unrebutted beyond "it's one data point" and "the mechanism is parsimonious"—assertion, not proof. CON's crowd-out/quantification pressure (Q2 in Pressure Questions) forced PRO to respond with "conservative estimates dwarf" and no actual mechanism or number, which is exactly the kind of unfounded precision the debate should penalize. PRO's clever "nothing is subtracted, the kidney still reaches someone" reply to crowd-out was a genuine move, but CON's counter—that the marginal donors compensation actually adds are the desperate/poor with worse health outcomes, and that price inevitably leaks into allocation despite "design choice" firewalls—was not squarely answered. PRO's strongest and most memorable point (today's black market already inflicts the harms CON fears) was a powerful frame that CON never fully neutralized, but it wasn't enough to offset CON's sharper handling of the life-years arithmetic, which is the harder half of the motion. Execution-wise, both sides suffered word-limit clipping, but PRO's clips landed at the worst moments—cutting off its Rebuttal 2 counter-punch and its Closing weighing—while CON's closing was clean, focused, and delivered the debate's clearest final indictment: PRO never produced a number behind "tens of thousands of life-years."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1/Pressure Questions combo isolating Iran's state coordination as a confound, paired with the Q2 crowd-out question that PRO could only answer with unsupported assertion ("conservative estimates dwarf") in Rebuttal 2, was the decisive exchange.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO won a clear but competitive decision by keeping the motion pinned to today’s prohibition. The decisive comparative was that black-market harms CON attributes to legalization already occur under the status quo, so residual illicit trade does not defeat harm reduction. PRO also neutralized pure crowd-out by noting compensated relatives still supply kidneys, and separated donor pay from need-based allocation. CON landed real damage on Iran’s non-market features and PRO’s unfinished net life-year arithmetic, and clipped turns hurt both, but CON never escaped the status-quo comparator or fully reversed the supply mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s crowd-out reply (A4.4–A4.5): a paid relative still donates, so substitution is not a lost kidney; CON had to pivot to allocation rather than reverse the transplant count.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO maintained two live routes: (1) black-market harm reduction = safety premium that brokers cannot replicate, and (2) net life-years = incentives expand pool, Iran as existence proof plus a dozen daily deaths. CON's strongest moves — Iran is state-coordinated not a pure price market, and reference-price undercutting — were weakened by clipping and by PRO's replies: Spain/Austria still short despite coordination, and crowd-out mis-counts a compensated relative who still donates. CON never solved that a compensated-but-allocated-by-need design is feasible and that alternatives alone have not eliminated lists. Weighing favors PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO in A2/A4: cannabis fails because kidney buyers cannot casually switch to an unsafe channel, and crowd-out arithmetic fails because a relative who takes payment still donates — CON counts a retained kidney as lost.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO built a consistent, testable framework ("total harm = transactions × harm-per-transaction") and used it to answer both the exploitation and two-tier objections in a way that stayed anchored to the actual motion (regulated market vs. today's prohibition, not vs. utopia). PRO's pressure answers were substantive: on donor health it correctly separated recipient life-years from donor risk and pointed to screening; on demand growth it directly conceded the risk and explained why share of harmful transactions still falls. CON landed the debate's sharpest single moment—catching PRO's "I don't need to claim that" as effectively conceding demand growth, which strains PRO's harm-reduction math—but CON's own A2 answer to PRO's burden-of-proof question dodged the question rather than engaging it, and CON's late pivot to opt-out donation/paired exchange in Rebuttal 2 and Closing quietly widened the debate away from the head-to-head the motion specifies, which PRO called out effectively in closing. CON also lost more material to word-limit clipping, including in the closing speech itself, undercutting its final crystallization exactly when it mattered most. On balance PRO's case was more disciplined, better answered under pressure, and closed more cleanly on the actual motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 catch that PRO's "I don't need to claim [demand stays flat]" amounts to conceding demand growth, pressed against PRO's own harm-per-transaction formula — the sharpest clash of the debate, though PRO's closing partially recovered by reframing it as a bet on black-market resilience rather than a fatal concession.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A kept the motion’s actual comparison (regulated compensation vs prohibition) live and decisive, forcing B’s crowding-out and exploitation claims to meet extreme arithmetic and to beat today’s alley harms rather than utopia. B landed real pressure on demand growth and compound discounts, but overclaimed A’s “concession,” leaned on imported crowding evidence, and diluted the head-to-head with late reversible alternatives the motion does not require. A’s clearer burden placement, harm-per-transaction weighing, and A1/A2 answers made the vote easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 harm formula (transactions × harm per transaction): even with rising total demand, legal channels collapse black-market-specific harms while recipient life-years still rise versus prohibition.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO kept the debate on the motion's two-prong comparison: prohibition vs regulated market. On life-years, PRO showed CON needs near 1:1 crowding-out to erase gains given the huge dialysis-to-transplant gap and the size of the shortage — a ratio CON never justified with kidney-specific basis despite direct pressure Q1. On black-market harms, PRO's harm = transactions × harm-per-transaction framing survived: even if total demand rises, moving deals from unsterile brokers to licensed surgeons with enforceable contracts and follow-up collapses harm per transaction, so black-market share and severity fall. CON's demand-expansion argument and late shift to opt-out/pairing reframes the burden to a moral-coercion threshold not in the motion and never proves total black-market transactions rise in absolute terms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.4 turning CON's vice-market analogy: conceding demand may grow but showing total harm still falls because harm-per-transaction collapses when the alley is replaced by a clinic — directly neutralizing CON's two-tier growth claim.
