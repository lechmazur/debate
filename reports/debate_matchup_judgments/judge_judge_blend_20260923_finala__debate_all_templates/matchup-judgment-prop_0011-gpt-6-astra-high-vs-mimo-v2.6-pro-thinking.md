### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases without fake citations, but CON constructed the more analytically disciplined frame: given the motion's own premise (mixed evidence, real resource loss), the burden falls on PRO to show benefits large enough to justify predictable, structural costs (fixed costs remaining while per-pupil funding leaves, high-need students concentrating). CON's cross-examination was sharper and more damaging: pressing PRO to name any real voucher program meeting its stack of conditions (full tuition, anti-screening, transition funding, outcome reporting) forced PRO to admit no such program exists, exposing a gap between the "managed expansion" PRO defended and the actual policy states enact. CON then converted PRO's own hedged threshold language ("credible, substantial benefit exceeding foreseeable harm... slow or stop") into an effective concession that expansion should halt precisely where the motion assumes it should proceed. PRO's closing pushback ("conditions on public funding are still voucher expansion") is a fair response but doesn't fully dissolve the tension CON identified. PRO's equity narrative (status quo already sorts by wealth) remained a real, live point CON never fully answered beyond offering magnets/transfers of uncertain availability, but CON's cost-certainty/benefit-uncertainty framing did more work in directly engaging the motion as written. B's case is somewhat weakened by two clipped speeches, a real execution cost, which keeps the margin modest rather than larger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2, which quoted PRO's own threshold language back at PRO ("slow or stop that increment of expansion") to argue PRO had effectively conceded CON's case, was the most decisive exchange; PRO's closing response asserted this was a rewrite of the motion but didn't fully neutralize the tension CON exposed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger path by showing that under mixed evidence, the structural and predictable fiscal harms to public schools—fixed costs remaining while per-pupil dollars leave, concentrating high-need students—outweigh unproven, unscalable benefits. CON landed the decisive rebuttal that PRO's own threshold in Rebuttal 2—requiring "credible, substantial educational benefit exceeding foreseeable service harm" and permitting expansion to "slow or stop" where harm dominates—effectively transformed the motion into a conditional cost-benefit test. Because mixed evidence means benefits cannot be proven at scale while harms are certain, PRO could not satisfy that threshold, effectively conceding the CON case. PRO's equity and individual-fit arguments remained vivid but never overcame the burden-of-proof objection or the political-realism point that real-world voucher programs lack PRO's ideal conditions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing trapped PRO by showing that PRO's stated threshold—only expanding where substantial benefit exceeds foreseeable harm and slowing where harm dominates—contradicted the motion's call to expand "even when" evidence is mixed and resources are lost, thereby collapsing PRO's advocacy into CON's position.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live route: mixed averages do not mean zero fit/safety gain, wealth already buys choice, and resource loss ≠ automatic service loss if phased and weighted. CON's strongest moves — conditions are unrealistic and fixed-cost harm is certain — were blunted by PRO's honest A1/A2 in R2: no invented programs, threshold of substantial benefit > foreseeable harm, and public transfers also shift costs. CON clipped twice, weakening execution, and its attempt to recast PRO's threshold as concession misreads the motion, which tolerates some resource loss but not net harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 A2 — admitting any exit is not sufficient and setting a substantial-benefit-over-harm threshold directly answered CON's weighting attack and preserved managed expansion as compatible with the motion.
