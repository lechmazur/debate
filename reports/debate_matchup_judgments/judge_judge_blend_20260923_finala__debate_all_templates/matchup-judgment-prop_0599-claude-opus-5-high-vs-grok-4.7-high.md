- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that most service gains could come from national issuance, document recognition, and fallbacks without creating the additional continental surveillance capacity. A offered a credible federated, safeguard-heavy framework and effectively exposed that B’s issuer checks resemble interoperability, but never fully resolved how excluded refugees obtain trusted attestations or how post-admission abuse is reversed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A’s questions directly, conceded that digital credentials provide speed, then argued that A’s framework had narrowed toward B’s mutual-recognition model while retaining the irreversible query risk. The contrast between a small service delta and a large risk delta gave B the cleaner voting rationale.

Side B showed that every safeguard Side A added to prevent exclusion directly undercut the service gain the ID was supposed to create, locking Side A into a paradox: a credential that never blocks adds nothing, while one that can block repeats the very harms Side A decried. Under pressure, Side A’s “continent-wide digital ID framework” shrank to federated standards, conditional mutual recognition, and revocable endpoints—functionally very close to Side B’s alternative of civil registration and mutual recognition of national credentials—while Side B demonstrated that hard-case inclusion still fell to human fallbacks and that regional models like ECOWAS already handled cross-border trust without new continental surveillance plumbing.
