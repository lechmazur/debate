### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A wins by effectively framing the theatrical window as an irreversible option that enhances, rather than merely delays, streaming value. Side B argues well for flexibility and the perishability of marketing, but Side A successfully counters that a theatrical run generates its own paid marketing while preventing day-one piracy and sustaining the necessary theater infrastructure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 beautifully crystallizes the risk asymmetry, pointing out that rushing a release permanently destroys box office and feeds day-one piracy, while a window merely defers streaming revenue the studio will still collect.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): A won by forcing the decisive comparison under uncertainty: a six-week window mostly defers streaming upside while still collecting it later as a built event, whereas early streaming permanently kills theatrical upside, hands pirates a day-one copy, and free-rides on (then erodes) the theater network spectacles still need. B’s flexibility/forecasting case was coherent and correctly blocked A’s attempt to collapse “major” into pure spectacles, but never produced a workable pre-release criterion that made “case-by-case” more than a soft guess, nor fully neutralized the asymmetry-of-error and irreversibility weighing. A’s pressure questions and A1/A2 answers narrowed the dispute cleanly; B’s counters on perishable platform value were real but did not overturn the presumption for major titles. Clipped A turns cost some polish, yet the live route to victory still favored A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 asymmetry: mistaken delay only costs weeks of still-collectible value; mistaken early streaming permanently loses box office, venue, and clean copies—plus CON never answered Q1 with a hard pre-release criterion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing a decisive asymmetry under uncertainty: preserving a theatrical window keeps the streaming option open while rushing to streaming permanently destroys theatrical revenue, exclusivity marketing, and anti-piracy protection. CON's flexibility case was intellectually coherent but could not supply a practical pre-release criterion for identifying which major films were weak theatrical fits, and PRO effectively used the Barbie example to show that ex-ante forecasting fails precisely on the films where the decision matters most. CON's counter that streaming value is also perishable was plausible but never fully overcame the structural difference between deferred and foreclosed value, leaving PRO's irreversibility framing as the live route to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's R2 and closing crystallized that "the window IS the option" — it lets studios observe real ticket demand before pivoting, whereas early streaming cashes the film's most valuable weeks for a temporary subscriber spike and forecloses theatrical permanently. This reframing absorbed CON's "portfolio management" language and turned it against them by showing that a guaranteed window preserves optionality while rushed release destroys it.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully trapping Side A on the definition of a 'meaningful' window, arguing that A's concession of 'roughly six weeks' collapses into the very 'quick' streaming model the motion opposes, and makes A's policy vulnerable to its own cannibalization arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 decisively capitalized on Side A's answer to Pressure Question 1, demonstrating that if four weeks trains audiences to wait, 'roughly six weeks' is also 'imminent' enough to trigger the exact same harm.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A kept the live burden on total value and sequencing: theatrical premium first, then streaming, and B never showed earlier platform access produces more durable incremental subscriber revenue than the tickets it risks cannibalizing, including from marginal buyers. B’s sharpest work was turning A’s ~six-week/flexibility language into a motion-collapse, but that mostly reframed “how many weeks / who times the release” rather than proving quick-for-growth superior on the proposition as written; theater collective-action and announcement effects also stayed more live for A. B was the cleaner tactical debater; A built the stronger path to the ballot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R2/closing turn of A’s “roughly six weeks” + film-specific judgment into CON’s short flexible window (strong tactic; did not fully erase A’s sequencing burden).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by collapsing the distinction between the two sides. It showed that Side B's (PRO) own description of a "meaningful" window—roughly six weeks, flexed to each film's demand—was functionally the same "quick" post-pandemic streaming trend the motion rejected. CON demonstrated that PRO's warnings about cannibalization and demand exhaustion therefore landed on PRO's own policy, leaving PRO with no coherent "rather than" to defend. CON also cleanly answered pressure questions and crystallized that both sides agreed on following demand curves, so the remaining question was speed, which PRO could not consistently oppose.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (A4.6-A4.9), where it showed that PRO's cannibalization argument undermined PRO's own roughly six-week window—since six weeks is also "imminent" once announced from day one—and therefore the motion's "rather than" distinction between meaningful exclusivity and quick streaming disappeared.
