### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won the comparative by showing that legalization need not eliminate every illegal transaction: diverting screenable donors and ordinary recipients into a safer channel plausibly reduces the black-market customer pool while adding transplants. Side B identified real risks from coercion, crowding-out, and residual trafficking, but mostly asserted that these effects could outweigh the gains without establishing why they likely would. A’s clipped second rebuttal was an execution cost, but its key answers were already delivered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s response to the residual-market objection was decisive: the existence of impatient buyers and rejected sellers does not show that black-market harm remains unchanged when many patients gain a supervised alternative. Side B’s public-price recruitment theory created pressure but never convincingly outweighed this substitution effect.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B built the clearer path to both parts of the motion. It showed that a regulated channel would pull screenable donors and desperate patients out of the shadows, shrinking the illegal remainder while adding transplants for patients who never had a family donor. Side A raised coherent theoretical burdens—crowding out, residual black-market coercion, and donor risk—but never demonstrated that those losses would exceed the gains from newly legal supply, nor that the illegal market’s total harm would rise rather than shrink. Side B’s framing that prohibition makes the entire trade a black market, whereas regulation converts the majority into supervised exchanges, was the decisive comparative lens. Side B’s clipped Rebuttal 2 created a real execution cost but did not hide the answers to the pressure questions or obscure the core ledger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s answers in Turn 7 (A1 and A2) to Side A’s pressure questions: it argued that shortening legal waits with compensated, screened donors would cause most buyers to leave foreign clinics, thinning the illegal remainder, while the pool of healthy strangers willing to sell under regulation would dwarf the set of matching relatives—making many grafts net additions rather than substitutions.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the cleaner comparison: under prohibition 100% of trade is the dangerous remainder; a screened, paid channel gives most buyers a safer substitute they prefer over next-week foreign surgery. CON's recruitment/advertising and extraction story asserts a larger but more hidden harm without showing it outweighs that capture, and its life-years subtraction never shows donor losses or crowding-out exceed years off dialysis for patients with no relative donor. Despite the clipped R2, PRO answered pressure more directly.
