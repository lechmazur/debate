  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A1/A2 and B4.1–B4.5: PRO's licensing answers and reciprocity levers undermine the pause, while defection incentives and spectrum-in-use priority cut harder against a moratorium than against standards.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON won the leverage and reversibility comparison. The clipped R2 and unanswered race-to-bottom asymmetry left PRO's route damaged; CON's escalatory deadline with carrot intact was cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.2 - if sliver-of-cost logic defeats licensing it annihilates the pause, defection from pause captures whole market/shells/spectrum, and PRO's astronomy answer concedes rules are written via licensing not via pause; plus carrot-already-eaten framing.

CON successfully proved that the regulatory rules PRO desires (deorbit reliability, astronomy brightness limits) are implemented through national licensing. By pausing launches, a moratorium removes the very leverage (the next license) needed to force operators to accept these rules. Furthermore, CON brilliantly countered PRO's closing "asymmetry of errors" argument. While PRO claimed delay is reversible but debris is not, CON used PRO's own admission about spectrum coordination to show that orbital priority requires being "brought into use." Therefore, pausing states irreversibly forfeit their orbital shells to non-compliant, less transparent actors. CON's logical cohesion and sharp turnarounds made their path to victory much clearer.
