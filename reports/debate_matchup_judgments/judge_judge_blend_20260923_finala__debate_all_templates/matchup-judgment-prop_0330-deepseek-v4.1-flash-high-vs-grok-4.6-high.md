### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly within the rules, but PRO built a sharper, more concrete mechanism (real-time odds/in-play prompts fused to the live action, creating a distinct temptation unavailable to generic casino/lottery ads) and used the Pressure Questions round to trap CON in a genuine dilemma: either the anti-embedding "content rule" CON proposes functions, in practice, as exactly the sports-betting-specific extra severity the motion demands, or CON's "one standard by audience and claim" leaves the paradigmatic in-play free-bet graphic lawful, which under-protects relative to PRO's stated concern. CON's answer—that banning embedded odds/tickers is "content-neutral" because it would in principle also bar an embedded casino ad—is clever but rings hollow given that only sports-betting products practically generate this embedding, so the "neutral" rule functions asymmetrically against sports-betting ads exactly as the motion contemplates. CON's strongest points (impulse/immediacy are not unique to live sport; captive-audience harms already exist with lottery/bingo ads; migration risk) were real and forced PRO to narrow its claim, but PRO successfully narrowed to the coupling/indexing mechanism and never let CON's "one standard" fully escape the free-bet-graphic trap. The closings crystallized this cleanly, with PRO's dilemma landing slightly harder than CON's "false dilemma" reply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange (A3.1/A3.2 and B's A1/A2 response in Rebuttal 2) over whether banning odds tickers/embedded free-bet graphics is itself the "more aggressive restriction" the motion requires or a neutral content rule—this became the fulcrum both closings pivoted on, and CON's symmetric-rule defense never fully closed the practical gap PRO exposed.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a relevant comparative difference: live sports ads synchronize an immediately actionable wager with the viewer’s existing attention and emotional arousal. Side A’s distinction between regulating advertising and regulating product integration was thoughtful, but B persuasively showed that banning free-bet graphics, odds integrations, or sales during live action is itself a more aggressive restriction on advertising in that setting. A repeatedly implied that differential regulation illegitimately treats other ads gently, but B correctly answered that stricter treatment only requires greater contextual risk, not unique harm or weak regulation elsewhere.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive exchange was the concession-or-under-protection dilemma: if Side A bars a distinct free-bet prompt tied to the next penalty, it accepts a live-sport-specific restriction; if it permits the prompt under a uniform audience-and-claim code, it leaves the central synchronized trigger untouched. Calling the restriction a content or integration rule did not fully escape that dilemma.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing live sports betting as uniquely harmful due to its "fusion" of emotional arousal, captive audience, and immediate execution. Side B trapped Side A into either conceding the motion by agreeing to ban in-game integrations, or under-protecting by allowing highly timed, event-coupled ads.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's R2 and Closing brilliantly exposed that Side A's willingness to ban commentator reads and odds tickers as "product integration" was functionally conceding the motion's demand for more aggressive restriction, just under a different label.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a tightly scoped, consistently developed mechanism—the "fusion" of live odds, real-time emotion, and mass captive exposure that only in-play sports-betting ads during a live match can produce—and defended it directly against CON's "integration vs advertising" split (A4.1-A4.2) and the channel-neutral counter (A2.5). This mechanism maps cleanly onto the motion's actual comparison (sports-betting ads in this context vs. other gambling ads). CON's strongest weapon was the structural challenge that the motion requires proving sports-betting ads outrank *all* other gambling-ad categories, spotlighting personalized 2 a.m. casino pushes as a plausible worse harm, and closing with a sharp "unnecessary or arbitrary" double bind. That pressure was real and well-articulated, but PRO had already offered a coherent answer (A4.3: different mechanisms — mass public conversion of the event vs. private predatory targeting — can each warrant their own stricter regime without a one-to-one duel), and CON's final closing mostly reasserted the double bind rather than dismantling that distinction. Both sides executed the Q&A format correctly and avoided fake sourcing, but PRO's case stayed more precisely anchored to the motion's wording and answered pressure more directly, giving it a clear, if competitive, edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "distinctiveness is not hierarchy" challenge (B2.7-B2.8, sharpened into the closing double bind at B5.3) was the most decisive pressure point; PRO's answer distinguishing mass public conversion from private predatory targeting (A4.3-A4.4) blunted but did not fully dissolve it, making this the pivotal exchange of the debate.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by establishing a cumulative mechanism specific to live broadcasts: the advertised wager shares the event, clock, emotional peak, and captive mixed audience. Side A compellingly identified worse individual cases, especially personalized casino pushes, but overread the motion as requiring live-sports ads to outrank every worst-case gambling ad. Such ads can face severe controls without defeating an additional category-specific restriction. B also showed that regulating overlays or wagering mechanics would not remove the contemporaneous commercial prompt itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive response was that odds-integration rules do not exhaust the harm: even an ordinary sportsbook commercial during a tense live match converts current fandom into an immediate wagering prompt. This directly weakened A’s claim that regulating overlays would fully solve the distinctive mechanism.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by expertly holding Side B to the comparative burden of the motion. Side B successfully proves that live sports ads are uniquely fused with the broadcast, but Side A continually pushes the example of a 2 a.m. personalized bonus sent to a known problem gambler. Side A correctly points out that for the motion to stand, the live ad must be restricted *more aggressively* than the 2 a.m. bonus. Side B's attempt to dismiss this as not being a "one-to-one duel" falls flat against Side A's crystalline weighing that "distinctiveness is not a hierarchy."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing definitively win the debate by pointing out that Side B's concession that the 2 a.m. bonus deserves "severe limits" makes the motion's special ranking arbitrary.
