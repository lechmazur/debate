### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO built the more decisive economic mechanism: even granting pass-through, the *relative* cost differential between recyclable and non-recyclable packaging still drives redesign, whereas the status quo imposes zero such differential. PRO also turned CON's regressivity argument back on itself by noting the current system already buries costs in rent and utility bills with no refund path, while a deposit at least returns money. CON's strongest counters—pass-through neutralizes relief, and a "modest fee + design standards" captures most of the benefit without "much more" cost shift—were real and repeatedly pressed, but CON never squarely rebutted the relative-cost mechanism (it mostly reasserted that pass-through happens, which PRO had already absorbed into its argument) and never explained how design standards get funded/enforced without producer money, leaving PRO's "residual public funder" critique largely live. CON's leverage/governance concern was also met by PRO's formula-based, statutory-service counter, which CON's final rebuttal did not directly dismantle. That said, Side A's case suffered a real execution cost: four of its five turns were clipped for exceeding word limits, including the close of both rebuttals and the closing itself, denying the judge full visibility into its strongest concluding lines. That repeated formatting failure tempers what would otherwise be a more commanding win, but the substantive clash still favors PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1 response to the pass-through pressure question—distinguishing total revenue pass-through from the surviving *relative* cost differential that drives design choices—was the pivotal exchange; CON's B4.1/B4.3 reply reasserted that consumers still pay but never neutralized the differential-incentive logic itself.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that pass-through does not eliminate either the relative incentive to redesign packaging or the benefit of funding cleanup when redesign does not occur. Side A kept regressivity and scale concerns live, but its modest-fee-plus-regulation alternative left taxpayers paying the residual costs of legally sold packaging. B also answered the governance objection with rule-bound fees and statutory service standards. Repeated clipping reduced B's margin but did not erase its clearer comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's A1 response in Rebuttal 2 was decisive: even if aggregate costs reach shoppers, recyclability-based cost differences still reward redesigning firms, while firms that retain wasteful packaging fund cleanup. That directly weakened Side A's central claim that pass-through makes producer responsibility ineffective.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping the live clash on residual subsidy and design incentives. CON landed a real pass-through and “much more vs modest fee” pressure, but PRO’s relative-cost answer and residual-funder comparison better preserved the motion as written. Clipping cost PRO some polish, yet CON’s case stayed more like a narrower alternative policy than a full defeat of producer-pays at scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1–B4.2: pass-through does not erase the packaging-type differential on the producer’s P&L, so the design lever survives CON’s central objection.
