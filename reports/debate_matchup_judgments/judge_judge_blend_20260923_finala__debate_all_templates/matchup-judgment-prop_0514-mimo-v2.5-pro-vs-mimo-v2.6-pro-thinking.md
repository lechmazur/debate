- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B won by consistently separating a usable-export contractual floor from the proposition's broader preference for portable, open architectures. B also answered A's vendor-failure pressure with concrete contractual alternatives. A's clipped later turns added an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it conceded standardized exports while showing that this does not entail open-stack procurement, then turned A's fire-escape metaphor by noting that resilience still imposes real construction and maintenance costs. Its response that multi-vendor architectures relocate rather than eliminate vendor fragility also directly weakened A's core resilience claim.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B won by drawing a sharp distinction between a contractual floor for data export (which B conceded) and an architectural mandate for switching portability (which B successfully argued burdens under-resourced schools and clinics with an unmanageable integration tax). Side A failed to explain how small institutions could manage this burden, and A's examples of the NHS and Estonia backfired when B pointed out they are highly centralized systems possessing the exact IT integration capacity B argued small districts lack.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2, which cleanly separated A's demands into one B already conceded and one A couldn't defend for small clinics, while effectively turning A's Estonia/NHS examples into proof of B's integration-capacity argument.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by showing that open standards and coherent integration are compatible, while Side B repeatedly assumed rather than established a necessary usability penalty. A also provided the stronger comparative mechanism: portability lets rivals price migration and disciplines renewals, whereas B's boundary mandates leave incumbent leverage untouched. B's capacity objection was meaningful, but A answered that limited internal capacity makes an externally purchasable exit more important, not less. Clipping cost both sides, but A's central path remained clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to the capacity objection was decisive: a district need not perform migration itself if open formats let a rival scope and price it, while proprietary lock-in prevents even a credible quote. B replied that transition management still requires capacity, but never explained why eliminating portability improves that problem or what otherwise constrains renewal pricing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won a highly competitive debate by successfully turning Side B's strongest argument (lack of buyer capacity) and winning the final weighing on reversibility. Side A convincingly argued that fragile, low-capacity districts are precisely the ones that need "purchasable exits" via standards, otherwise they are completely captive at renewal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side B's capacity argument by framing it as "captivity dressed as realism," arguing that low-capacity districts need open standards the most so third parties can execute migrations for them.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO won the central tradeoff. CON framed open standards as fragmentation vs reliable integrated workflow and as certain daily cost vs contingent future benefit. PRO neutralized both: open standards does not forbid a single-vendor coherent product, it only requires exportable data and published interfaces; and lock-in cost is not contingent—it arrives at every renewal. PRO's A1/A2 on pressure—rival can price a migration so threat is purchasable, which helps low-capacity buyers most—directly answered CON's capacity/credibility challenge that CON never solved for price discipline. CON never explained what disciplines renewal pricing without export rights, leaving PRO's reversible-vs-irreversible asymmetry as the live route to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's answer to B's Q1—threat doesn't require district to self-migrate, plus complete exports let a rival bid fixed-scope work—flipped the capacity objection into an argument for portability as protection for the weakest buyers.
