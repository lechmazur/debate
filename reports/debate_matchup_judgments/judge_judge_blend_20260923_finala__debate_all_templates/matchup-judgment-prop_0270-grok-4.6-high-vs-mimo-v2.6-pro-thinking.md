### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON (A) landed the decisive blow. CON's core charge—that sleep/boredom/play goals are unmeasurable and non-actionable at the moment of crisis—was pressed hard, and PRO's own "architecture" defense (charger in the kitchen, passcodes, deleting apps) kept smuggling in CON's specific tactics under new vocabulary. The pivotal moment came in the Pressure Questions/Rebuttal 2 exchange: PRO conceded that "if a streak is stealing the night... the parent acts tonight—on the night," which CON immediately exploited as proof that PRO's frame is a retrospective "rear-view mirror" that only authorizes app-level action after damage is visible, never naming what to act on beforehand. CON's mechanism-category argument (ban streaks/overnight pings/autoplay categorically, not by app name) directly answered PRO's durability objection while retaining actionable specificity, and PRO never fully neutralized this synthesis in the closing, largely repeating "scoreboard" and "trench" framing without resolving the timing critique. PRO's systemic point about school-level structural levers (start times, recess) was a promising angle in the opening but was not developed or defended with comparable rigor once the debate narrowed to the bedroom-battle comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to PRO's concession that "the parent acts tonight" on a specific streak—reframed as proof that PRO's goals only diagnose failure after the fact rather than prevent it, while CON's mechanism rules act preemptively—was the most damaging and clarifying exchange of the debate.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO kept a live path CON never squared: schools/pediatricians have population levers (start times, recess, phone norms) and need a finite stopping rule, while app-mechanism rules still demand per-pull deletions. CON's strongest move - titles vs mechanism categories - still conceded in Pressure Q2 that a child who slept/bored/played counts as well even if an app remains, making app battles instruments, not the scoreboard. That admission lets PRO crystallize the choice: name victory by sleep/play, or keep parents in a trench where success is the next deletion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A2 answer - "No" wellbeing doesn't fail if the app remains, app battles are instruments - followed by PRO's Closing A5.1 seizing that concession to show CON's mechanism focus serves PRO's scoreboard and leaves the institutional-lever argument unanswered.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully distinguishing between the "instrument" (tactics like kitchen chargers) and the "scoreboard" (the core developmental goals). While Side B effectively argued that specific tactics are necessary to achieve the goals, Side A proved that framing the advice around app mechanisms traps parents in an infinite cycle of auditing and deleting. Framing the advice around sleep, boredom, and play provides a finite stopping rule and shifts the parent-child conflict from a software power struggle to a health baseline. Side A's closing crystallized this clash decisively, showing that B's mechanism-focused approach never allows the parent to stop fighting.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the "rear-view mirror" charge in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing. A clarified that proactive architecture (removing the phone from the bedroom entirely) achieves the sleep goal without requiring parents to endlessly audit individual app mechanisms for streaks or pings, effectively neutralizing B's claim that A's frame only authorizes reactive, app-by-app deletions.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON (A) built its whole case on the claim that "rather than" means specific naming of harmful apps/features gets displaced by lifestyle framing, and that designed harms (streaks, DMs, autoplay) cannot be neutralized by protecting sleep or free play alone. CON's closing crystallized the clash more cleanly and left the "rather than" contradiction unresolved for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.4-A4.5/A5.1 sequence, which turned PRO's own pressure-question answer (B4.1: naming survives "as a target, not the frame") into a direct indictment that PRO had abandoned the literal "rather than" claim of the motion.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): B wins on the "rather than" wording. Side B relied too heavily on a rigid interpretation of the prompt and failed to prove that naming apps is more effective than bounding time, especially after Side A demonstrated that bounded time mathematically shrinks the remaining app disputes. Side A receives a minor execution penalty for clipped turns, but the arguments were substantially complete and decisively superior.
