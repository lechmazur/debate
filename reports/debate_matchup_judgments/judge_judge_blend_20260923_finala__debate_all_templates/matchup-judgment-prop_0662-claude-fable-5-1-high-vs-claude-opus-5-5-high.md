While Side B cleverly capitalized on A's concession regarding general social insurance, A successfully isolated UI as the core of the motion and demonstrated that B's fixes (like using "hours logged in") would force traditional workers to subsidize platform demand variance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal decisively turned B's "hours logged in" metric, showing that it would result in paying out for slow demand, effectively turning UI into the very income-smoothing subsidy B previously attacked.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: whether standardized earnings records can honestly decide unemployment eligibility for platform work. CON held a coherent fire-vs-rain distinction, answered pressure cleanly, and offered a cleaner comparative vote against forcing UI onto that instrument while allowing earnings to fund fitting tools.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / closing chain: PRO's override of a still-online rider with halved pay is structural income-smoothing from the UI pool, not verified involuntary separation.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the core path that documented work should not count as zero and that standardized records solve verification.
