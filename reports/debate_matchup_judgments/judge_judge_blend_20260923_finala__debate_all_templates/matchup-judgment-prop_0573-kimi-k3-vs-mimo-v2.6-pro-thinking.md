### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Both sides fought a genuinely substantive fight over whether standardization solves the core asymmetry or just repackages it, with sharp exchanges on gaming/Goodhart's law, formula-exposure/collusion, cost burdens, and the "capacity to act" versus paycheck analogy. B built a cleaner throughline: opacity is the proven, unconditional harm, while CON's harms (gaming, collusion, cost) are conditional and answered by design features (plural metrics, confidentiality, tiering). B's pressure question on the paycheck analogy and the "contractual-only audit access" concession forced A into a defensive posture that A only partially escaped. A's rebuttals were intellectually sharp (especially turning "majors already know the formula" into a "capacity to act" point, and distinguishing pay stubs from shared-pool formulas), but A's opening, both rebuttals, and closing were all clipped before reaching their conclusions, repeatedly cutting off A's final synthesis and weakening the completeness and persuasive landing of its case. That repeated execution failure, combined with B's cleaner closing crystallization ("cure for information imbalance is more information, not less"), tips a close debate to B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal 2 turning the financial-statement analogy against A's "no real verification without source access" claim, paired with A's unanswered concession that audit rights "remain contractual" for independents — this exchange, highlighted again in B's closing, was the most decisive clash because it exposed that A's preferred status quo also lacks source-level verification while offering independents even less leverage than the standardized regime would.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by showing that standardized disclosure creates comparability, bargaining value, and a benchmark for accountability even without automatically granting source-system access. Side A’s strongest objection treated anything short of a full audit as nearly worthless, but never adequately answered B’s point that its own preferred collective audits and comparison tools depend on consistent data. The gaming, collusion, and compliance harms remained plausible but comparatively speculative and mitigable. Side A’s repeated clipping was an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s closing turned Side A’s proposed alternatives against it: rights societies, representative audits, and cross-platform comparisons all require common definitions to operate efficiently. That directly weakened the claim that standardization adds costs without producing verification or practical value.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO won the central verification and asymmetry clash. CON argued standardized self-reports remain unverifiable and create gaming/collusion and cost harms, but PRO answered with the financial-statement analogy — self-reported plus standard plus penalties is how verification starts — and turned CON's own alternative of rights societies and collective audits into a reason TO standardize, because those collective mechanisms are data-hungry. CON's clipped openings/rebuttals and its failure to give a path for independents without leverage, plus a partial answer to the paycheck analogy, left its live harms as conditional and patched by tiering/confidentiality/plural metrics, while PRO's present harm of opaque royalty statements remained unneutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1-A4.2 / A5.2 double turn: financial statements are also self-reported yet auditable because a standard makes divergence falsifiable, and collective audits and offer-comparison — CON's alternative — presuppose standardized data to scale.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely live cases, but CON (A) sustained a clearer throughline: the decisive money is split downstream in label/distributor books the motion never reaches, standardized platform disclosure is either redundant (where dashboards already exist) or misleading (across incommensurable business models), and mandatory uniform reporting imposes fixed costs that entrench incumbents while creating a de facto public price signal that invites tacit coordination. PRO (B) landed a strong counter in Rebuttal 2/Closing — that the platform's wire-to-intermediary figure is an independent check that makes downstream claims falsifiable rather than a "second copy" — but CON's "already owed under her contract" response, while not airtight, was never squarely refuted, and B's closing narrowed almost entirely to the falsifiability point, dropping the unanswered compliance-cost and coordination threads CON had pressed. Execution also favored CON: B's opening and second rebuttal were both clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real cost that cut short exactly the rounds where B needed to consolidate its strongest specific answers, while A's speeches were clean and properly formatted throughout, including well-targeted pressure questions and correctly labeled A1/A2 answers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer (A1/A2) that the platform's wire amount to the label is an independently verifiable figure enabling dispute, regulatory evidence, and deal comparison was the strongest single exchange — but A's follow-up reframing it as a "redundant second copy already owed under her contract," combined with the broader unanswered compliance-cost/coordination critique, kept CON's case intact through the close.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that platform-side data provides an independent input against which downstream accounting can be checked. Side A compellingly argued that disclosure cannot reveal every label deduction and raised compliance and coordination costs, but incompleteness did not establish uselessness, while those costs remained comparatively speculative. Side B also identified concrete uses such as intermediary audits and comparing gross platform payments with net receipts. Its two clipped turns were execution costs that narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s A1/A2 response was decisive: it conceded that some deductions occur downstream but explained why platform-controlled stream, pool, and payment figures still make intermediary accounts falsifiable and enable audits. Side A’s claim that these were merely duplicate records did not overcome the value of an independent source.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): PRO maintained a live path: platform owns numerator, denominator, and wire to intermediaries; standardized format (not single per-stream number) makes downstream audit rights falsifiable at low marginal cost. CON's core "does not possess decisive data" conceded capability in R2 and retreated to "not decisive," while its standardization-as-manufactured-number and tacit-coordination harms remained speculative and were directly answered by PRO's grammar/sentences distinction and concrete leverage examples (audit with incoming figure, regulator evidence, take-rate comparison). Incompleteness became an argument for, not against, opening the only ledger we can open.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 — naming the exact platform-held figures (streams by track/territory/window, pool totals, per-stream rate, wire to label) and listing three actionable uses, which neutralized B's Q1/Q2 about downstream decisiveness and "no concrete action" and turned CON's privity claim into a verification argument.
