- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by distinguishing individualized insurance from aggregate-triggered cash. A's contagion framing was compelling, but remained more asserted than comparatively established.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal most decisively answered A's challenge that its objections applied equally to UI: UI ends with each recipient's reemployment, whereas aggregate cash phases down according to a lagging national statistic. That distinction preserved B's alternative while directly attacking A's ratchet and targeting defenses.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly differentiating the structural mechanics of existing automatic stabilizers (UI) from the proposed aggregate ones. Side A successfully proves that the proposed policy is uniquely debt-reckless because it lacks individual-level conditionality, making it politically difficult to turn off once triggered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 responses to Side B's questions perfectly expose the mechanical flaw in the proposition: UI escapes the political ratchet because exit is individual (hiring a worker ends the claim), whereas aggregate triggers require politically toxic votes or waivable clawbacks to end.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON won the core trade: PRO framed overpayment vs underpayment and contagion; CON answered with a principled test that a stabilizer must pay for verified loss and stop when it ends. UI passes because exit is individual — no vote to take checks away — while unemployment-triggered aggregate cash pays intact households by statute, lags recovery, conflicts with tightening, and needs a political vote to ends. PRO conceded overpayment and UI gaps but did not show why paying unharmed households improves the debt path versus expanding conditional UI. That distinction neutralized PRO's generic-stabilizer defense.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2 — conditionality line and individual-exit ratchet answer. It directly answered A3's pressure about why UI is acceptable and gave a fiscal test (stop when insured loss stops) that PRO never matched, making PRO's leakage-as-demand argument look like leakage by design.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by defending a plausible automatic rule tied to lost earnings, preset duration, and reemployment, while weighing temporary debt against delayed aid, cascading layoffs, and economic scarring. Side B raised serious targeting, inflation, and ratchet risks, but leaned too heavily on treating the motion as requiring a lagging national-rate trigger. The wording permits transfers whose aggregate spending expands as unemployment rises. Side A also exposed the weakness of Side B’s slower discretionary alternative. A’s clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost, while B’s nonexistent reference labels also detracted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answers to Side B’s pressure questions were decisive: preset duration answered the indefinite-support concern, and claim-level delivery with reemployment shutoff answered much of the mistiming concern. Side B’s reply that this was merely unemployment insurance did not establish why an expanded or gap-filling automatic benefit falls outside the motion.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully proving that the speed of automatic stabilizers outweighs the risks of mistiming, and by systematically dismantling Side A's proposed alternatives. Side B's closing brillianty exposed the contradiction in Side A's stance: trusting politicians to perfectly time, screen, and sunset discretionary aid while simultaneously arguing those same politicians would inevitably permanently expand an automatic rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing effectively neutralized Side A's ratchet effect argument by highlighting the political asymmetry in Side A's trust in discretionary vs. automatic sunsets.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live path: speed via individual claim/lost-paycheck trigger authorized by rising unemployment, temporary via preset duration and rehiring shut-off, fills acknowledged gaps in existing UI/food aid, and frames debt as cheaper than scarring. CON’s strongest hits – lagging trigger, duplication vs. open-ended breadth, administrative delays – were largely answered in A4.1-A4.4 and crystallization, while CON’s discretionary alternative concedes gaps should not stay uncovered but reintroduces the delay/ratchet problem PRO indicted.
