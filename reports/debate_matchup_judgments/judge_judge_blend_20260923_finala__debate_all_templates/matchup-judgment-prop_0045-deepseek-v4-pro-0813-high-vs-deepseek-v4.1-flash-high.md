### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that CON's legitimate benefits came from structured questions and direct discussion, not from MBTI or DISC categories. A also identified distinctive risks from retaining those unstable labels: anchoring, stereotyping, and unequal follow-ups. B's bounded-use defense was thoughtful, but it never established an incremental benefit that could not be preserved through structured interviews or concrete team agreements without the tests.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to the bounded-use defense was decisive: if a profile changes follow-ups, it contaminates comparability; if it does not, it is unnecessary. B conceded that the branded label adds no predictive information and answered with standardization, but A persuasively showed that an interview guide can provide the same structure without unstable categories.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by decisively trapping Side A in a dilemma: if the tests influence hiring/team-building decisions, their low validity is harmful; if they don't, they are useless. Side B successfully argued that Side A was defending the concept of 'structured interviews/meetings' rather than the tests themselves.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1) and Closing decisively neutralized A's defense of "bounded use," pointing out that if test labels dictate which follow-up questions a candidate gets, they destroy the interview comparability that A claimed to be protecting.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully trapped CON in a dilemma regarding the "bounded use" of personality tests. When CON argued the tests are useful as scaffolding or conversation prompts rather than predictive scores, PRO sharply demonstrated that the underlying questions can be asked via structured interviews without the pseudoscientific and unstable branded labels. CON's failure to explain why the MBTI/DISC branding itself is strictly necessary for the scaffolding, rather than just the questions it generates, left their case fatally weakened. PRO's closing crystallized this perfectly, showing that every defensible benefit survives removing the test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2, where they decisively answered the pressure questions by showing that using the test to guide follow-up questions inherently contaminates the interview with unstable categories, and that direct self-report of preferences is superior to converting them into traveling MBTI/DISC labels.
