### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B won a clear but competitive debate. Its stockpiling-versus-continuous-shipping comparison, immediate restart benefits, and historical fossil substitution established a credible path to reducing both insecurity and emissions. A compellingly exposed severe nuclear outage risk and B's overstatement that any residual gas fails the motion, but did not sufficiently show that renewables, storage, and hydro could supply firm power at the necessary scale or that nuclear expansion as a complement would worsen outcomes. A's repeated clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to the uranium-import objection was most decisive: dense reactor fuel can be stockpiled for years, unlike LNG requiring continuous vulnerable deliveries. A correctly shifted attention to plant outages, but showed episodic concentrated risk rather than proving it outweighed the persistent import dependence nuclear would reduce.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won the core clash on grid reliability by proving that nuclear's failure mode (unannounced, years-long outages due to courts or earthquakes) is far harder to manage than renewable gaps (forecastable weather events). Side B had excellent rhetoric regarding 'warehousing vs shipping lanes,' but Side A successfully neutralized this by pointing out that warehoused fuel is useless if a judge or earthquake shuts the plant down.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's distinction in Rebuttal 2 that legal risks for renewables merely delay future small projects, whereas legal injunctions against nuclear plants take massive amounts of existing generation offline overnight.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively attacking the "expand" half of the motion and winning the comparative clash on grid vulnerabilities. Side A relied heavily on restarts and used factually weak examples for expansion (Shimane and Ohma), which Side B effectively dismantled by noting they broke ground pre-Fukushima and remain unfinished, proving the "decades" timeline. Side B also won the crucial "gap" clash by distinguishing between forecastable, hours-long renewable gaps (manageable with peakers and storage) and unannounced, years-long nuclear fleet outages caused by structural legal and seismic risks specific to Japan and Korea. Side A's attempt to fiat away these structural risks ("this motion repeals [politics]") was a significant argumentative weakness. Side B receives a slight execution penalty for being clipped in three turns, but the clipped text was minimal and did not obscure key burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to Side A's pressure question about specific new reactors. Side A named Shimane 3 and Ohma as expansion proof, and Side B devastatingly pointed out they broke ground before Fukushima and still generate nothing, effectively destroying Side A's expansion timeline and reinforcing the structural delays inherent in these specific nations.
