### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Both sides executed the format cleanly, including proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure, and the debate stayed tightly on the stipulated motion (transparent, accountable hunting) rather than drifting into general distaste arguments. B built a coherent and well-phrased counterfactual case (ban doesn't eliminate demand, only legitimacy and local buy-in), but A's central dilemma—if a state has the administrative capacity to certify a hunt as "transparent" (verifying age, sex, quota, revenue flow), it necessarily has the capacity to instead collect levies, trust payments, or PES funds without killing anything—was never fully dismantled. B's best answer (a single high-value permit is easier to collect than building tourism infrastructure) was a real counter but did not fully neutralize the dilemma, and B's closing never engaged A's concrete Kenya counterexample (a real state sustaining conservancies without a hunting revenue stream), leaving a live gap. A also sustained a second live thread throughout—those who administer "transparent" trophy programs are the same actors capable of corruption and providing legal cover/paperwork templates that traffickers exploit—which B addressed mostly by definitional fiat ("that's not what the motion covers") rather than by showing the condition is achievable in practice. B's rhetoric and community-conservancy framing were more polished and memorable, but A's structural paradox was the sharper piece of argumentative engineering and went unresolved at the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated dilemma—that any governance capable of certifying "transparent" hunting revenue is equally capable of funding non-lethal alternatives (levies, PES, trust funds)—forced B into conceding "can" while only weakly distinguishing ease of collection; B's answer narrowed the gap but never closed it, especially once A introduced the Kenya counterexample in Rebuttal 2, which went unaddressed in B's closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's transparency filter live: where revenue is traceably shared, a single high-value permit can fund rangers and compensation in remote areas where photographic levies on few tourists yield almost nothing. CON's best push - that same governance could route non-lethal finance and that legal trade launders demand - was answered by the revenue-base vs ability-to-collect distinction and by quota/age regulation. The replacement case remained "can" not "does," while PRO's regulated-use vs unregulated-poaching counterfactual survived, giving PRO the cleaner path to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1/A5.2 response to B's governance Q1 - ability to collect does not equal existence of a revenue base; a levy on almost no tourists yields almost nothing, while a capped hunter quota yields a fundable fee - directly undercut CON's core symmetry argument and powered the closing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully turning Side A's "transparency" condition into a logical trap regarding state capacity. Side B argues convincingly that the high state capacity required to transparently audit and enforce trophy hunting quotas is the exact same capacity needed to manage non-lethal conservation finance. Side B also effectively highlights the ecological harm of targeting prime specimens and the enforcement risks of legal trade, providing a more comprehensive and logically airtight case against the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the state capacity and revenue replacement clash, specifically the argument that a state too weak to collect a bed-night fee is also too weak to verify lion age and sex, effectively neutralizing Side A's defense of the motion's transparency condition.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued sharply and adapted well across rounds, but B built the more resilient case. B grounded its position in concrete, plausible examples (Namibia conservancies, the Botswana 2014 ban) that A never directly rebutted, instead answering only in generic terms about "available alternatives." B also neutralized A's central "legal market enables laundering" mechanism by pointing out that fraud and padded quotas fall outside the motion's own "transparently used" clause—turning A's corruption stories into arguments against the wrong (unregulated) case rather than the one on the table. A's strongest weapon was the pressure-question exposing that the motion contains no necessity test, forcing B to concede "yes, it still permits the hunt, and that's the point." But B recovered by correctly noting the motion is a legality permission, not a mandate, and that A's necessity requirement was importing a rule the proposition never contained—a fair defense of arguing the motion as written. A's ecological rebuttal (disrupted social groups, perverse incentives to breed shootable males) was met with a specific, unanswered counter-mechanism from B (age-structure protection, since a male must survive years to become valuable). On balance, B's answers were more directly responsive, better grounded, and left fewer of its own claims exposed, giving it a clear but competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to A's laundering pressure question (A1), showing that forged permits and padded quotas fall outside the motion's "transparently used" clause by definition, which directly defused A's core corruption-based objection to the clean case the motion actually defends.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept the debate on the motion as written: revocable legality only where revenue is traceably used for conservation and livelihoods. It offered a concrete mechanism — Namibia-type conservancies, marginal lands where photo tourism doesn't reach, scouts/water/tolerance funded now — and handled corruption by noting fraud exits the clause. CON's strongest moves were the pipeline-cover and incentive-to-manage-for-males critiques, but it conceded countable revenue and relied on a necessity test not in the proposition and promissory bridge finance, failing to show concrete harm in the clean verified case that outweighs immediate loss to locals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.2 answer to B3.1: fraud = excluded from "transparently used" and grounds for revocation; without a legal tag there is no document for villagers/monitors to mismatch — turning CON's laundering pipeline argument into a reason to keep auditable permission.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by tightly controlling the definition of the motion's condition ("transparently used") and effectively demonstrating that Side B's corruption and necessity objections either fall outside the motion's permitted cases or misunderstand the permissive nature of "remaining legal." Side A's defense of local choice and the immediate reality of conservation funding on marginal lands outweighed Side B's reliance on hypothetical "bridge finance" and unproven alternatives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where they decisively answered the pressure questions by defining fraud as inherently non-transparent and clarifying that legal permission does not mandate hunting, effectively neutralizing Side B's two main lines of attack.
