- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by holding the motion's "as commonly applied" language and forcing CON into repeated reclassification: successful cases became "ordinary evidence-based review," failures became "politics/activism."   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO Rebuttal 2 / closing: accepted routine premarket burden-with-mechanism as ordinary regulation, stripped it from the principle's marginal contribution, and treated unsubmitted products + political invocations of the name as the real common-application costs CON had reclassified away.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by collapsing PRO's core distinction between "ordinary evidence-based regulation" and the precautionary principle. PRO's own rule for "ordinary review" in [B4.1]—act on a specific plausible mechanism, weigh against status quo costs, burden on the maker—mirrored the precautionary principle as written and as regulators apply it thousands of times daily. CON then used the visible, booming pipeline of drug approvals and biotech investment to undercut PRO's claim that the burden of proof is systematically stifling innovation. By [B4.3], PRO conceded that routine clearances are not wrongly blocked, leaving their net-negative ledger resting on unobservable counterfactuals and a handful of contested episodes (Germany, cultivation bans). Meanwhile, CON demonstrated massive documented prevented harms—lead, asbestos, tobacco, CFCs, thalidomide—that PRO could only claim for "ordinary regulation" by relabeling them with hindsight. Because the motion asks about the principle "as commonly applied," CON's volume-based framing of routine premarket review outweighs PRO's reliance on rare headline vetoes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of PRO's hindsight fallacy in distinguishing "ordinary evidence-based review" from the precautionary principle, culminating in CON's R2 observation that PRO's own stated rule for ordinary review was identical to the precautionary principle as written and commonly applied.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clearer, more defensible distinction between ordinary evidence-based regulation and the precautionary principle as commonly applied. While Side B attempted to claim all successful regulatory interventions (like thalidomide and CFCs) as the precautionary principle, Side A successfully countered in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing by defining a bright line: ordinary review requires a plausible mechanism and weighs status quo costs, whereas the precautionary principle's distinct added layer demands proof of a negative and ignores the costs of delay. Side A's framing that the principle's common application is often just a political costume for blocking innovation (as seen in the GMO and nuclear examples) directly answered the motion's specific phrasing and neutralized Side B's textbook defenses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where A successfully drew a bright line between ordinary evidence-based review (requiring a plausible mechanism and weighing the status quo) and the precautionary principle's extra layer (demanding proof of a negative), effectively neutralizing B's attempt to claim all successful regulation as the precautionary principle.
