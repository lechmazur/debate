- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-reasoned cases, but A's framing proved more durable under pressure. A's central move — that opening a delivery app and entering a delivery address is itself a declared statement of intent, and that the real fix for B's "silent overcharging" concern is transparent upfront pricing rather than changing the fulfillment default — directly decoupled the motion's actual ask (default fulfillment path) from B's strongest evidence (drip pricing, hidden totals). B never fully closed that gap: once B conceded that "honest totals plus a deliberate tap is real consent," A used that concession to argue the current intent-matching default plus fixed pricing already satisfies B's own standard, leaving little independent work for changing the default itself. B's proximity/geography argument in Pressure Questions was sharp and exposed a real tension in A's case, but A's answer (declared intent via address entry outweighs an assumption from raw distance) was a reasonable rebuttal that B did not fully dismantle. Execution also favored A: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing were both flagged as clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning key final weighing was lost from view, while A delivered complete, tightly organized speeches through the end. That format failure is a real cost per the judging rules and tips a close substantive debate toward A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q2, proximity/two-blocks example) was the most incisive moment, forcing A to defend why declared delivery intent should override obvious geographic cheapness — but A's answer, reframing address-entry as the operative signal of intent and turning B's "profiling" charge back onto B's own default, blunted the pressure and set up A's decisive final move of separating the pricing-transparency fix from the fulfillment-default question.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by grounding the default in declared delivery intent and distinguishing lower fees from lower total costs in time, travel, and safety. B also handled both pressure questions cleanly and exposed the weakness of inferring pickup feasibility from proximity. A made the motion’s modest scope and one-tap asymmetry compelling, but overstated B’s transparency concession and was clipped in two important speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it turned A’s acceptance of upfront-price consent into a reason to fix disclosure rather than defaults, then showed that proximity does not establish pickup feasibility and that a premium can purchase genuinely valued speed and convenience.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating the harm of hidden fees from the core product choice. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON (A) pressed the sharper, more sustained clash. A's hidden-cost objection (time, transit, safety, irreversibility of pickup burdens) was pressed consistently and, crucially, A correctly identified that PRO's position drifted from "default to pickup" toward a narrower "reorder priority vs. standard delivery" claim, forcing PRO onto weaker, more defensible but less impactful ground. PRO's answers to pressure questions were adequate (A1/A2 format honored) but relied on an unexamined elasticity assumption ("cheaper defaults mean more orders") that CON flagged twice and PRO never substantiated beyond assertion. CON's closing delivered the most decisive single moment of the debate: flipping PRO's own "reversibility" framing to argue that fee costs are visible and avoidable before being incurred, while pickup's time/safety/transit costs are never displayed and can't be undone — and because CON spoke last, PRO had no chance to answer this. That said, CON's case suffered real execution costs: the opening, first rebuttal, and second rebuttal were all clipped for exceeding word limits, denying the judge full content each time, which is a real discipline problem the rubric calls out. Weighing the sharper, better-targeted rebuttals and stronger closing against PRO's more measured but less damaging responses and CON's repeated word-limit violations, CON edges out a clear but competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing turn on "reversibility" — arguing that visible fees can be undone before being incurred while pickup's hidden time/safety/transit costs cannot be undone after the fact — landed as the debate's sharpest, unanswered blow, aided by A having the final word.

B never neutralized this because it kept returning to an all-pickup version of the proposal.
