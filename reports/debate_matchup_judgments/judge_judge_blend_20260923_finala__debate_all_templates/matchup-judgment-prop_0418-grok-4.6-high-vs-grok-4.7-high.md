### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly without fake sourcing, but B built and sustained the more decisive structural critique: the exact conditions that make women-only rides feel necessary (night, unfamiliar areas, thin liquidity) are the conditions in which the eligible-driver pool is thinnest, so the product's safety promise collapses precisely when it matters most. A's opening was vivid and well-framed (opt-in, mobility tax, single-sex transit analogy), but its core defense against the liquidity paradox—"idle drivers aren't stolen supply" and "no match forms, no bill"—never squarely answered B's point that verification, impersonation risk, and dual-inventory dispatch are standing architectural costs that exist independent of whether a match forms, and that occupying an idle woman driver is still an opportunity cost against future mixed (including other women's) requests. A's closing partially conceded this ("inefficiency the proposition already priced") without showing the benefit still clears the bar at the margin that matters. B's pressure questions and answers stayed on-target and its closing line reframing the product as "a lunch pairing with a standing bill" crystallized the clash far more sharply than A's closing appeal to "the door she chose." Both used the Q&A format correctly, but B's questions did more damage and were engaged with more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 point that a driver "still has one car" and dispatch must choose, directly undercutting A's claim that drivers can serve both mixed and women-only pools cost-free — later crystallized in B's closing framing of the product as delivering safety at lunch (when it's least needed) while carrying a standing bill at night (when it's most needed).
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by establishing the better baseline and proportionality test. For riders who refuse a male-driver match, liquidity in the mixed network does not itself produce a usable ride; an optional women-only match can therefore restore mobility and trust. Side A persuasively showed that female drivers are not costless surplus and that verification and thinner matching impose real costs, but B ultimately conceded the matching inefficiency and weighed it against completed trips and reduced feared pairings. A never adequately showed that an imperfect proxy must be useless or that thinner nighttime supply defeats gains wherever matches do occur.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive response was that women being capable of harm does not negate a meaningful reduction in the pairing riders most fear, and that perfection is not the burden. Coupled with the point that visible fallback caps the downside while successful matches create trips otherwise refused, this weakened Side A’s central claim that imperfect availability makes the option unjustified.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live path: baseline for refusers is no trip, so any completed women-only trip is net gain; idle-only matching makes empty hours cheap and pairs cost with gain. CON's tax-on-default and empty-lane-fails-when-needed pressures were answered, while CON never solved that fallback to mixed is optional and that women-on-women risk isn't the pairing causing cancellations. Safety/trust vs minutes weighing favors PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 reframing that liquidity only helps riders who will board nearest car, and that idle-only matching with visible wait caps loss and leaves unmatched riders with existing choices, neutralizing B's matching tax and midnight emptiness charge.
