### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive decision. A powerfully established the danger of covert union suppression, proxy discrimination, and self-fulfilling scores. However, B better answered the categorical motion by showing legitimate individualized retention and early-intervention uses, while arguing that disclosure, auditing, and adverse-use restrictions preserve a record that a ban would eliminate. A's need-based response did not resolve why scarce retention resources cannot legitimately target those most likely to leave, and its objection that disclosure rules do not currently exist was weak when the proposed ban is itself a new rule. A's repeated clipping was also a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's scarcity challenge was decisive: A proposed allocating help by present burden, but that did not substitute for identifying likely departures or productivity declines. B then paired that practical benefit with a narrower disclosure-and-audit regime, undercutting the necessity of a total ban.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's "visibility" and "retention" arguments. B effectively demonstrated that AI models give employers an unaudited alibi, capitalizing on A's concession that disclosure rules do not currently exist. Furthermore, B completely turned A's retention examples, first by arguing that flight-risk triage abandons the most desperate workers in favor of those with options, and second by pointing out that using a unionization score to selectively fix an organizer's complaints is itself illegal interference.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's argument that AI flight-risk models allocate hospital relief based on employer value rather than worker need, leaving exhausted but trapped nurses at the back of the line.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won the live clash. The unionization prong stayed decisive after B forced A to concede individual targeting and then never produced a clean legitimate use of an individual organize-likelihood score; B turned A's "remediate her complaints first" answer into selective reward/interference. On visibility, B repeatedly showed A's inspectability path depends on disclosure and third-party audit rules A admits do not exist, while B's bright-line tool ban is enforceable via contracts and vendors. A's retention/auditable-trail frame was serious and kept the debate competitive, but it lost the weighing once enforcement under real conditions was pressed. Clipping of some B turns was a mild execution cost and did not erase the comparative edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 plus Rebuttal 2/Closing on union scores: after A called organize-likelihood an individual target, B demanded one non-interference use; A's remediation answer was reframed as the classic selective-fix playbook, sealing that prong.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that the ban removes an auditable tool without stopping managers from making the same forward-looking judgments informally. B also successfully separated harmful uses from prediction itself and emphasized that A's strongest case concerned union-related adverse action, not every productivity or retention forecast covered by the proposition. A made a compelling case about anchoring, opacity, and preemptive harm, especially for unionization scores, but never established why prohibition was superior to disclosure, auditing, burden-shifting, and use restrictions. B's repeated clipping was an execution cost, but the decisive arguments remained visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to A's early-injury framing was decisive: mentorship and assignment judgments still occur after a ban, but informally and without a record. B then argued that regulated disclosure could expose and contest the forecast before harm, turning A's timing argument against the ban.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by relentlessly forcing the comparative: banning AI does not stop preemptive speculation, it merely returns it to unaccountable human intuition. Side A successfully co-opted Side B's strongest point about "early injury" by showing that early human bias is invisible without a documented score.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly accepted Side B's premise that harm happens early (in mentorship), using it to prove that a ban leaves this early discrimination entirely invisible, while regulated AI would provide a visible, challengeable record.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive comparison easier: a full ban does not stop early human prejudgment about who is “leaving anyway” or “going places,” it only makes that treatment invisible, while a use-regulation path can force disclosure, logs, driver explanations, and burden-shifting before mentorship and assignments are lost. PRO’s best live route—preemptive labeling as treatment, especially union-likelihood laundering—was real and sharper on one prong, but never fully carried all three, and left CON’s clean-records overbreadth and contestability arguments largely standing. Multiple CON clippings were an execution cost, yet the remaining clash still resolved for regulation over ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing reverse of PRO’s “injury timing” test: if harm lands early in silent assignments/mentorship, the ban leaves that manager forecast intact and only deletes the auditable record a worker could contest in time.
