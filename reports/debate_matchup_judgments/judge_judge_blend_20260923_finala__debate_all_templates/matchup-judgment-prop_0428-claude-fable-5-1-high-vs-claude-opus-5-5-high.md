### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing the practical flaws in Side A's proposed mitigations. While Side A offered creative solutions like conditional licenses, Side B effectively argued that low-cost venues cannot afford real safeguards and that probationary periods force residents to endure months of disruption. Side B also maintained a highly persuasive narrative about who bears the true risks of these venues.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 dismantled the probationary license by noting that an operator's self-kept log is 'a diary written by the defendant' and that a six-month trial still means six months of lost sleep.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO (A) built the stronger path to victory by showing that CON's (B) alternative effectively conceded the motion's core principle and by introducing a workable conditional-licence framework that answered B's enforcement objections. B's case narrowed the motion to worst-case late-night venues and never fully addressed the structural bias toward organized objectors or the accessibility gap in B's "commercial corridor" alternative. B's mixed-use answer—admitting venues open over upstairs residents' worries because the ground floor was designated commercial—functionally accepted the proposition while relabeling it, which A crystallized effectively in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's introduction of the default-lapse probationary licence in Rebuttal 2, which shifted the enforcement burden from the resident to the operator and undermined B's claim that revoking a licence is politically impossible.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by tightly anchoring to the text of the motion and exposing Side B's evasion. Side B tried to defend the motion using trivial examples (knitting circles) that do not generate noise or parking worries, and later smuggled in the word "alone" to shift the burden. Side A effectively dismantled Side B's enforcement mechanisms (probationary licenses, self-kept logs) by highlighting the real-world burden placed on residents, and delivered a highly memorable comparative framing ("the operator wagers money, the neighbour wagers sleep"). Side A also successfully navigated Side B's closing trap by clearly distinguishing between invalid worries and overruled worries.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1, which pointed out that Side B's "knitting circle" example fails because nobody objects to it, meaning the motion only applies when venues actually generate the predicted noise and parking harms, forcing Side B onto the defensive regarding late-night crowds.
