### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and stayed on the actual motion, but PRO (B) more successfully forced the decisive tradeoff into the open and answered it more directly. The pressure-question exchange was pivotal: B's A1 admitted the government keeps veto over the *public* warning under CON's "joint release" model, which let PRO show that CON's supposed compromise still lets a government suppress a verified, growing threat indefinitely—exposing that CON's safeguard has "no endpoint independent of the government withholding clearance" (B5.3). CON never rebutted this cleanly; its closing repeated that neighbors already get evidence and can act, but never explained why a government should retain an unbounded veto over public confirmation of a *verified* threat, which is the strongest form of PRO's asymmetry argument (irreversible cost of suppressed accurate warning vs. reversible cost of corrected false alarm). CON's concealment-incentive argument was substantive early, but PRO's rebuttal that "increased difficulty of sustaining concealment" cuts against it, plus the concession that shared evidence already risks the same border/market reactions CON fears, undercut CON's strongest structural claim (that confidential sharing is safe while public messaging is not). CON's closing mostly restated prior points rather than resolving this specific tension. PRO's closing crystallized the clash more sharply and left the "unaccountable veto over completed findings" as the live, undamaged claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1/B5.3's answer to Q1 — conceding the government keeps control of the public warning but showing this still permits suppression of a *verified* growing threat with "no endpoint independent of the government withholding clearance" — was the most decisive exchange, since CON never adequately closed that gap.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by demonstrating that the motion's unique mechanism—constraining national control over early messaging—risks perverse incentives that shrink the evidence base rather than expand it, and by maintaining that an unaccountable public megaphone freezes irreversible market and border reactions that private evidence sharing avoids. A's conflict-of-interest case remained live but could not fully neutralize B's showing that officials would learn to delay, narrow, or avoid creating outbreak signals if incomplete notes could become uncleared international announcements.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 answer that officials need not destroy clinical charts to thwart verification; they can simply route fewer samples outward, escalate disputed clusters more slowly, and tighten internal circles before writing an outbreak signal—directly neutralizing A's claim that patient-care records ensure evidence survives for investigators.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, competitive cases, but CON's chilling-effect mechanism was more consistently developed and better survived pressure. CON's opening reframed the burden ("does transfer of authority produce faster warning, or does it deter the initial signal") and sustained that frame through rebuttals, pressure questions, and the final word. The decisive moment was A4.4's rebuttal to PRO's claim that "access refused, picture incomplete" falsifies "controlled": CON correctly noted that a documented refusal establishes noncooperation, not the outbreak's actual extent, exposing an overclaim at the heart of PRO's clock mechanism. PRO's answers (a clock that starts on "corroborating signal") ended up converging toward CON's own proposed threshold, without ever explaining why legal access rights would move a determined concealer who can still obstruct entry, samples, and records. PRO's opening and closing were rhetorically vivid and the "ceremonial access" framing was strong, but CON's asymmetry argument (the entitlement's clearest cost lands on cooperative governments, while its clearest need is against resistant ones where it is least effective) was never fully neutralized, and CON had the last word to crystallize that gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4's rebuttal that "refusal establishes noncooperation; it does not establish the outbreak's extent" cleanly punctured PRO's B4.1 claim that a documented refusal itself constitutes a verified finding that falsifies "controlled," exposing the central weak link in PRO's disclosure-beats-contradiction mechanism.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A built a more resilient causal story. It showed that independent verification power changes host incentives so that early disclosure beats later contradiction, turned obstruction into a legible finding rather than a dead end, and demonstrated that CON’s corroboration threshold functionally waits for the outbreak to escape. While CON’s chilling-effect objection was sharp, it never overcame PRO’s argument that concealment incentives already exist and that a host sitting on the only file has no reason to share it under either regime.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s answer to CON’s pressure questions in Rebuttal 2—treating refusal itself as a verified finding that falsifies “controlled,” and using a fixed clock plus corroborating signals to force the host’s hand—successfully neutralized CON’s claim that stronger access is useless against resistant governments.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO won the incentive clash that both sides accepted as decisive. CON argued stronger access chills early ambiguous reports and falls hardest on cooperative governments. PRO answered directly: the chill already exists when the host alone owns the headline, and a clock fixed in advance plus triggers from travelers, receiving hospitals, and willing clinicians plus treating "access refused, picture incomplete" as a public verified finding flips the payoff to disclosure beats contradiction. CON's alternative — warn on corroborated risk without new entry rights — never closed the gap PRO identified: it starts after external corroboration and cannot reach site/sample/file, leaving the host's edit intact while exponential spread continues.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.2 — reframing refusal as a verifiable public finding that falsifies "controlled" and showing cooperative governments are less exposed because they shape the first statement, which directly mitigates B's asymmetry argument in B2.2/B3.1.
