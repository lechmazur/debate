### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won because Side A ultimately conceded that default framing can increase donation in marginal cases, then imposed an unsupported requirement of a guaranteed system-wide effect. A also treated effects mediated through coordinators and family conversations as separate from the legal default, even though changing institutional behavior is precisely the proposed causal mechanism. B more successfully compared the systems: family consultation exists under both, while opt-out and veto safeguards protect known objections and presumed consent reduces inertia among willing but unregistered donors. A’s autonomy asymmetry was forceful but did not fully answer that opt-in also leaves families making bedside decisions without confirmed personal authorization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s B4 response was decisive: it identified default framing as the remaining causal channel, rejected the “multiplier on zero” argument because functioning infrastructure is not zero, and explained why even a modest consent gain can be measurable. Side A then conceded that framing can nudge cases but dismissed the effect by raising the motion’s burden beyond what was written.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained a live route on all three prongs: default framing changes the family conversation from permission-seeking to objection-seeking, capturing unregistered supporters and yielding measurable transplants even in functioning systems. CON's infrastructure confound ("multiplier on zero") and "same conversation" claim were blunted by B2.1/B4.1. On ethics, PRO's safeguards (easy opt-out + family veto means no known refusal overridden) neutralized CON's strongest unequal-access/action-vs-inaction push, while CON never answered why burying a willing donor's organs is not the graver autonomy failure. Distress clash favored PRO's burden-of-authorization vs burden-of-objection framing. CON needed to break one link decisively; it raised equity but did not topple the rate or distress links.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.1 - consultation is not consent; default script changes answers, so "same decision point" does not mean same outcome, turning CON's A1.2/A3.1 causal challenge into evidence for PRO.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B effectively dismantled the conjunctive burdens of the motion. B forced PRO to concede that the causal mechanism for increased transplant rates relies heavily on institutional infrastructure rather than the statutory shift itself, undermining the "measurable increase" claim. Furthermore, B's distinction between the ethical failure of inaction (opt-in) versus unauthorized physical intervention (presumed consent) was sharp, repeatedly pressed, and never fully neutralized by PRO's utilitarian framing. B controlled the clash and crystallized the debate cleanly in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8), where B trapped PRO on the causal mechanism ("PRO has now retreated from 'the switch causes gains' to 'the switch operates through institutions'") and sharply distinguished the ethical asymmetry ("Taking action on an unconfirmed will is categorically worse than failing to act on an unconfirmed wish").
