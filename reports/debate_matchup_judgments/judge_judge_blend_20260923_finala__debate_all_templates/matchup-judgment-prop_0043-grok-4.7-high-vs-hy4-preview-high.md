### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuine, competing mechanisms and pressed real weaknesses in each other's cases, but PRO's closing sequence was decisive. CON's own pediatrician-chart example, introduced to show media literacy catching what logic misses, was turned back on CON in PRO's closing: if provenance checks return "clean" on a genuine but truncated chart, only formal statistical reasoning catches the distortion, meaning the media-literacy-distinctive tools (provenance, source-check) do no work in exactly the scenario CON chose to illustrate. CON's rebuttal to this ("necessary, not sufficient... one integrated curriculum") retreats to redefining "media-literacy curriculum" to include years of formal training plus add-ons, which PRO had already flagged (B2.6, B4.3) as effectively conceding that the formal content is the load-bearing element and the "media literacy" label is doing no independent work. CON never squarely answers that meta-critique in the closing. CON's strongest surviving point—that formal reasoning cannot detect deepfakes or bot-driven amplification—remains a real, unrebutted gap in PRO's universal claim, which keeps the margin modest rather than large. But on the central exchange that both sides treated as decisive (the trusted-source chart), PRO landed the cleaner, more damaging rebuttal and closed on it more effectively than CON recovered from it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing reversal of CON's own trusted-pediatrician/truncated-chart example (B5.1–B5.4): showing that provenance and source-checking return a "clean" result on a genuine chart, so only formal statistical reasoning catches the truncated axis—directly undercutting CON's central illustrative example and exposing that CON's "integrated curriculum" fallback concedes the formal content is what actually does the work.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by exploiting the proposition’s universal comparison while also showing substantive gaps in formal reasoning alone. An integrated media-literacy curriculum could teach the same statistical tools while adding provenance, authentication, amplification, and resharing checks. Side A’s authentic-but-misleading chart powerfully established that formal analysis is necessary, but not that it outperforms a curriculum containing that analysis plus additional safeguards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s closing answer to the truncated-chart example was decisive: the example proved the value of formal reasoning, but the integrated curriculum would catch the same axis problem while also addressing synthetic media, missing context, and manufactured amplification that logic alone cannot detect.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) exposed the motion's overreach as a universal boast ("more than any") by showing that an integrated media-literacy curriculum can teach the same formal tools while adding indispensable non-inferential checks—provenance, bot detection, synthetic-media verification—that pure formal reasoning cannot perform. CON also turned PRO's "trust" pressure effectively: loyalty can rationalize away a base-rate objection, but it cannot reinterpret a failed lookup or synthetic-artifact detection. PRO's framework remained elegant but could not prove that formal training alone outperforms every possible integrated curriculum, nor fully answer how early primary instruction escapes slogan-level misuse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.3), where CON distinguished external verification (which returns results loyalty cannot rationalize) from internal inference (which trust can override), and blocked PRO's attempt to subsume integrated curricula into the motion by showing provenance and amplification checks are genuinely additive.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases, but CON (A) landed the more damaging structural blow: since "any serious media-literacy curriculum" can itself incorporate formal logic/probability as a component while adding production, funding, timing, and amplification analysis, the proposition's "more than any curriculum" claim becomes very hard to sustain. A pressed this dependency/inclusion point relentlessly and PRO's answer—that inclusion as a "late, thin subunit" is inherently weaker—remained largely assertion rather than demonstrated. A also neutralized PRO's signature claim that formal reasoning is uniquely immune to tribalism, showing that "rigged" is just as easily used to end inquiry as "biased" is, and that garbage premises (forged quotes, deepfakes, dark-money ads) defeat even perfect probabilistic reasoning. PRO's rebuttals were clever (the "biased ends inquiry, rigged starts audit" line, the wave-inference reframing of deepfakes) but did not fully close these gaps, and B never squarely resolved the inclusion problem raised repeatedly by A. A also had the benefit of the final speech and used it to sharpen rather than merely restate the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated "dependency gives the comparison away" move (Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2), showing that if a media-literacy curriculum can include the same formal-reasoning content plus funding/production analysis, formal reasoning alone cannot beat "any" such curriculum—paired with A's "loyalty decides, not vocabulary" answer that dismantled B's claimed audit-trail immunity to tribalism.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won by exploiting the proposition’s demanding comparison against any media-literacy curriculum. B showed that a serious curriculum can apply logic and statistics to real claims while also examining funding, selection, timing, and amplification. A’s response that such inclusion must be thin, late, or subordinate was largely stipulated, and A never established why abstract early instruction transfers better than repeated practice on polls, clips, and ads. A offered a memorable case for durable reasoning habits, but not for their superiority to the fuller program B described.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s decisive response was that logical priority does not establish comparative impact: formal reasoning is one component of an audit, while media literacy can include that component and still address how premises and apparent consensus are manufactured. This directly undercut A’s foundation argument and the motion’s universal comparison.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON wins because PRO could not sustain the steep comparative burden that early formal teaching alone would do more than any serious media-literacy curriculum. CON showed that serious media literacy already incorporates logic and statistics while adding essential analysis of funding, amplification, and production systems that pure formal reasoning cannot reach. CON’s pressure questions exposed a unresolved tension in PRO’s case between calling formal reasoning a non-rival foundation and claiming it outperforms the full structure built upon it, and CON’s rebuttals demonstrated that without detecting operations, forged material, and dark-money timing, demanding a denominator collapses into either blind assent or the blanket distrust PRO itself condemns. PRO’s engine-versus-paint frame was memorable, but CON successfully collapsed the false choice: democratic resilience requires calibrated trust in the messy claim as it arrives, not just abstract inference habits hoped to transfer from primary-school dice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal (A4), where it directly answered PRO’s pressure questions by showing that production and circulation decide which premises citizens receive, that treating included logic as automatically thin is an unsupported stipulation, and that refusing assent without spotting influence operations becomes the blanket distrust PRO itself rejects.
