### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-executed cases, but PRO's central reframe proved more durable under pressure: the "premium" CON calls a victim's bargaining chip is recast as the price of enforcing silence against third parties who never consented to the bargain, and PRO sustains this through pressure questions and both rebuttals ("if silence is voluntary, it needs no lawsuit to sustain it"). CON's strongest hits—that settlement consideration can't be cleanly severed, and that the motion is overbroad against neutral confidentiality terms—are real, but PRO offers a workable narrowing (NDA vs. general release, "involving" targets secrecy about the conduct itself) that blunts the overbreadth charge, and PRO's answer to the discovery-cost argument (liability and reputation still drive settlement) is not squarely dismantled by CON, who mostly reasserts the package-deal point rather than showing why employers wouldn't still settle substantially. PRO's closing crystallizes the clash more memorably and directly answers the live tension about timing on CON's "danger shown" carve-out (protection arrives only after someone is already harmed). CON keeps this close with a genuinely strong victim-welfare framing, but PRO's rebuttal chain and final weighing give the cleaner path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 reframing of the $60k/$5k gap as "the price of a damages threat against a later warning," directly answering CON's package-deal objection and reasserting that liability/reputation independently drive settlement, was the most decisive exchange—it survived CON's follow-up and anchored PRO's closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully reframing Side A's strongest pragmatic point. While Side A argued effectively that voiding NDAs removes a financial "premium" victims use as leverage, Side B successfully categorized that premium as an illegitimate sale of third-party safety. Side B also provided cleaner, more direct answers during the Pressure Questions round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 exchange where it answers Side A's question about the financial premium, clarifying that the state shouldn't back a contract that essentially prices "other people's ignorance."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger path by reframing the settlement premium as a payment for institutional impunity rather than victim compensation, cleanly answering Side A’s overbreadth worry by distinguishing secrecy about misconduct from general releases or settlement amounts, and crystallizing the debate around whether courts should be used to keep future workers blind. Side A’s economic argument and narrow alternative lost comparative force once the gap was shown to purchase third-party ignorance, and Side A never fully recovered from Side B’s pressure question exposing that third-party safety is not a legitimate chip for one victim to sell.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over the $60,000-versus-$5,000 settlement gap, where Side B reframed the premium as the price of court-backed impunity and exposed that third parties who never signed the agreement were the true commodity, undercutting Side A’s claim that unenforceability simply makes victims poorer.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and engaged the format rules faithfully (clean Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 compliance). B's strongest contribution was the economic "pricing the check" mechanism and the proposed narrower carve-out (void only gags on reporting or ongoing threats), which pressured A to justify why a blanket rule is needed rather than a tailored one. A's best answer, delivered in Pressure Questions and reinforced in Rebuttal 2, was that "one-off" status cannot be verified ex ante—repeat predation looks identical to a first offense until a second victim surfaces—so a bright-line rule is the only workable safeguard given information asymmetry favoring the wrongdoer. This directly neutralized B's carve-out alternative. Conversely, when A pressed B on what exactly is "lost" besides the value of hiding abuse (Q1), B's answer mostly restated that "certainty of silence" has value to her without escaping the framing that this certainty's content is precisely silence about illegal conduct—leaving A's "hush money, not remedy" reframe largely standing. A's closing weighing (structural, foreseeable harm to future workers vs. a speculative, individualized bargaining loss) was slightly sharper and better tied to the specific policy carve-outs already recognized in contract law for concealing illegal conduct or ongoing danger. B's case remained strong and never collapsed, but A more decisively answered the central pressure point and ended with the cleaner comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's Pressure Question on "one-off" cases (A4.2: repeat predation looks identical to a one-off until a second victim comes forward) most effectively defused B's strongest alternative proposal (narrow carve-outs instead of a categorical ban), while B's parallel answer on what is "lost" besides hiding-abuse value (B4.1) was comparatively circular and left A's hush-money framing largely intact.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that a blanket ban forces the current victim to absorb the cost of a lower settlement in exchange for a highly uncertain public benefit. Side A's relentless focus on the economic reality of the settlement premium dismantled Side B's argument that only the employer loses out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 that relabeling the settlement premium a "bribe" does not put the money back in the victim's hands, perfectly setting up the weighing that the motion spends a certain remedy for an uncertain warning.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): B won by demonstrating that enforceable NDAs in abuse cases are court-sanctioned gags that externalize harm to future workers. B successfully reframed the lost settlement premium as hush money, separated the NDA from genuine privacy protections for workers, and showed that one-off abuse cannot be verified at settlement, undermining A's carve-out alternative. A's economic arguments remained live but were outweighed by B's clearer structural-harm framing and sharper answers to pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 distinction that voiding the victim's gag does not license employer disclosure—protected by separate privacy laws and protective orders—directly defused CON's "mutual shield" argument and clarified the NDA was employer insurance, not worker privacy.
