### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON built a tighter, more disciplined case around the motion's literal text and used concrete counterexamples (the compliant manufacturer, the self-pricing freelance designer) to expose that a blanket structural surcharge would tax legitimate contracting, not just evasion. The decisive exchange came when PRO, answering Pressure Questions, redefined its own proposal so the duty "bites only where the app or the chain is the discount" and compliant users "owe nothing more" (A4.5). CON immediately and correctly identified this as a quiet rewrite of the motion (B4.3-B4.4), arguing that once the trigger is narrowed to genuine shortfalls, PRO's own toolkit (joint liability, presumption, escrow) already achieves the same result without a new structural levy—collapsing PRO's case for necessity. PRO's closing never squarely answered this "no new law needed" challenge, instead reasserting the merits of targeting fragmentation without reconciling it with the motion's plain wording. PRO also suffered real execution costs: two speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off potentially important development of its mechanism (e.g., "use the records [clipped]" and "collects at the point of [clipped]"). CON's Pressure Questions and answers followed the required A1/A2 format cleanly and did real argumentative work, while CON's closing crystallized the clash ("punish evasion, not the structure of work") more cleanly than PRO's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3-B4.4), catching PRO's pressure-question answer as an implicit narrowing of the motion to a shortfall-trigger, then arguing this concession makes the new EU levy unnecessary since CON's joint-liability/escrow alternative already reaches the same debtor—this exchange reframed the entire remaining debate and PRO never fully recovered from it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B successfully framed the motion as a structural tax on contracting rather than a targeted anti-evasion measure. When Side A attempted to defend their case by carving out compliant subcontracting chains, Side B astutely demonstrated that Side A had retreated to Side B's exact alternative (joint liability for shortfalls), effectively conceding the core clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal (B4.3-B4.4) masterfully capitalized on Side A's concession that compliant users "owe nothing more," proving that Side A had abandoned the proposition's structural mandate in favor of Side B's shortfall alternative.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON's overbreadth challenge survived the full exchange. The manufacturer buying compliant machined parts and the self-pricing freelance designer [B2.3] showed the motion as written taxes structure even where no contribution is missed. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): The decisive clash was whether the motion, as written, actually tracks "control" and unpaid social contributions, or whether it is a blanket surcharge on organizational form. PRO tried to narrow the motion with an implicit control/"core operations" test to answer CON's honest-specialist objection, but CON caught this in Rebuttal 1 ("PRO's rebuttal does not defend the motion. It replaces it") and pressed it further in Pressure Questions and Rebuttal 2, showing PRO then abandoned its own filter by insisting warehouse agencies and last-mile subcontracts must be charged regardless — an internal inconsistency CON exploited cleanly. CON's "second euro" framing forced PRO into conceding, in its own answers, that the charge applies both where nothing is paid and where everything is paid, which CON turned into the closing line that clean chains "pay a residual premium so dirty ones might be dearer." PRO's comparative defense of ex ante pricing over status presumptions/joint liability was reasonable but never resolved CON's sharper point that the mechanism collects money without enrolling the excluded worker in any scheme, leaving PRO's closing claim that contributions "buy" coverage unsupported by the text. CON's Rebuttal 2 was clipped for length, a real execution cost, but the substantive content had already landed before the cutoff. Overall CON kept tighter textual fidelity to the motion and exploited PRO's shifting goalposts more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 opening move — "PRO's rebuttal does not defend the motion. It replaces it" — which exposed that PRO's control/integration filter has no textual basis, and which CON sustained through the Pressure Questions and Rebuttal 2 to catch PRO contradicting its own carve-out when charging warehouse agencies.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully proving the motion acts as a blunt tax on an organizational form rather than a targeted penalty on control or abuse. By forcing Side A to concede that fully compliant chains would also pay the surcharge, Side B effectively framed the policy as double-charging honest actors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 expertly capitalized on Side A's Pressure Question answers, cementing the 'second euro' argument and pointing out that a surcharge paid by the company does not mechanically enroll an excluded platform worker into a pension scheme.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B wins on textual fidelity and weighing. PRO first redefines the motion to 'scalable fragmentation' to dodge the honest-specialist cases, then in A4 concedes warehouse agencies and last-mile chains are taxed because 'form is the risk.' CON's A1 in B4 shows 'higher, triggered by use' is an added obligation, not a reallocation, collecting a second euro on clean chains without guaranteeing the excluded rider gains pension rights. PRO never bridges that gap between payment and membership, while CON's presumption-plus-joint-liability path tracks control ex ante and collects only the missing euro.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.4: exposing that PRO replaced the motion with a control filter not in the text, that the charge follows the invoice not the fragmenter, and that making clean chains insure dirty ones is not pricing control.
