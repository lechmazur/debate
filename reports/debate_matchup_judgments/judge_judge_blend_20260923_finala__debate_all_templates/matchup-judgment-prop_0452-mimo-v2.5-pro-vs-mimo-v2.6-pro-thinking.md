### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won the central comparison: occasional coal failures do not establish that retiring functioning capacity before firm replacement is available would improve manufacturing reliability. Side B compellingly identified pollution, water, and trade costs, but never adequately showed that clean generation and storage can presently replace existing coal at the necessary scale without creating the energy gap A emphasized. B also overstated A's sequencing position as indefinite lock-in. A's repeated clipping was an execution cost, but its comparative path remained clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer that reliability is comparative rather than absolute was decisive. It conceded coal's supply problems while distinguishing fixable logistical failures from the structural capacity shortfall created by retirement before firm replacements are online.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly separating the economics of building new coal from the grid mechanics of retiring existing assets. Side B's argument that an imperfect coal fleet is better than a structural energy gap—because grid-scale storage cannot yet replace 75% of India's power—went largely unrefuted and served as the decisive comparative wager.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal successfully reframed coal's supply hiccups as solvable logistical issues, weighing them against the much worse structural failure of shutting firm capacity without adequate storage.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON wins the decisive firm-power clash. PRO's case that existing coal must be kept to enable manufacturing requires coal to be a cheap, reliable backbone. CON showed it is neither - still paying fuel, rail, retrofits, low PLF, throttling in crunches and droughts, competing for water, and imposing CBAM/supply-chain penalties even on domestic producers. PRO's reply that this is logistical not structural and that useful life is 25-40 years left "sequencing" open-ended and left the double-cost argument unanswered, with two rebuttals clipped. That leaves no live route that delaying retirement buys manufacturing growth.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R1/R2 sunk-cost reversal plus unreliable-backbone attack - exposing that existing plants have real marginal costs and fail during coal/rail/water crunches, turning PRO's reliability premise into a tax on manufacturing.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won through the clearer sequencing and risk comparison: retaining selected existing coal capacity preserves dispatchable optionality while clean power expands, whereas retiring it before firm alternatives are available risks blackouts, costly backup, and lost industrial investment. Side B raised credible health, transition, and path-dependency harms, but often treated delayed retirement as decades of new coal-centered planning rather than directly defeating A's narrower policy of retiring the worst plants first and retaining newer assets as temporary backstop. Side B's repeated clipped turns were also an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was the asymmetric-risk rebuttal. Side B admitted it could not specify when firm clean power would arrive, while its answer about accelerating investment did not fully explain how factories would receive reliable power during a shortfall. Side B's path-dependency response was plausible but relied on assumptions about long-term coal contracts and infrastructure that A's model did not require.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully establishing that continuous manufacturing requires firm baseload power, which renewables cannot yet reliably provide at scale. Side A concedes they cannot guarantee a timeline for firm clean power, allowing Side B to weaponize an asymmetric risk framing: an early coal retirement leading to power shortfalls causes irreversible industrial stagnation, whereas keeping coal as a backstop is reversible. Side A's execution is severely marred by exceeding the word count and clipping in four of their five speeches, blunting their final impacts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 neutralizing Side A's health impacts by arguing that joblessness and informal labor are a far worse health catastrophe for workers than managed coal emissions.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the motion's sequencing frame alive: delivered firm MWh vs auction prices, and error asymmetry - idle coal later is reversible, lost industrial takeoff is not. CON (A) landed real counters on path dependency, CBAM, and health, but conceded in A4.1/B5.1 no firm date for multi-day clean firm power, which B turned into the decisive comparative. B also answered A's pressure directly with fleet differentiation (retire oldest first, keep supercritical) and pollution control plus joblessness-as-health. A answered B's Qs but with "fast vs fast enough" signaling claim that lacked concrete traction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Rebuttal 1 / Closing extending the asymmetric-risk weighing - framing CON's case as forecast-dependent and then leveraging CON's "I do not claim firm clean power arrives on a certain date" as fatal to CON's no-tradeoff claim.
