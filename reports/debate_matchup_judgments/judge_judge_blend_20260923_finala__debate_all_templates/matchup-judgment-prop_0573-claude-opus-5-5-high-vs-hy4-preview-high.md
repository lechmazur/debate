### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that standardized disclosure need not standardize prices, contracts, or fraud-detection rules. Side A raised serious intermediary, fraud, and compliance concerns, but its downstream-only remedy did not fully answer inconsistent upstream reporting or explain why giving creators access to data already held by buyers would harm them more than continued information asymmetry. Side A's three clipped turns also imposed an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's distinction between standardizing reporting fields and dictating business models was decisive. Labeling a deal as flat-fee while reporting viewing data does not convert it into per-view compensation, substantially weakening Side A's central model-diversity objection.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's case by showing that the lack of transparency occurs at the intermediary level, not the platform level.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly turned Side B's "aggregate invalid-traffic deductions" into a tuning manual for bot farms, showing how A/B testing tracks would allow fraudsters to grade their evasion tactics.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive clash on the right target: platforms already feed granular usage to licensors, so independents lose visibility downstream and a platform-wide standardization mandate misses the bottleneck while creating real side effects. PRO’s meter metaphor was clear and PRO answered pressure questions cleanly, but PRO conceded majors already get rich feeds, that independents fail via small distributors, and “yes” on mandatory viewing counts for flat-fee titles—opening CON’s strongest turns on renegotiation leverage, fraud farms reading per-track filter outcomes, and narrower relay/audit fixes. Three clipped CON speeches cost polish, yet CON still crystallized the cleaner comparative path: harmed creators get little new, unharmed creators get hurt.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 on the documentary concession and fraud tuning: PRO’s “yes” on per-title counts plus “aggregate invalid-traffic deductions” let CON show leverage and experiment-scoring without needing heuristics.
