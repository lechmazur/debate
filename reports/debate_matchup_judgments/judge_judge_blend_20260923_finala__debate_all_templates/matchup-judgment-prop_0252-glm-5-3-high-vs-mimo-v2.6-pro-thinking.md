### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively dismantling Side B's two main pillars: the assumption that banned unpaid roles convert to paid ones, and the workability of the "genuinely educational" exemption. A forced B into defending "discontinued" roles, which A successfully framed as helping zero applicants, and A brilliantly proved that B's standard (banning "routine productive work") precludes actual on-the-job learning.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5/A4.6) pointing out that B's standard banning "routine productive work" would ban actual on-the-job learning, neutralizing B's "surgical strike" claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by exposing that PRO's policy mechanism either relied on an unproven conversion of unpaid slots into paid ones or, under PRO's own fallback, simply eliminated opportunities altogether. CON crystallized this into the decisive "zero helped" arithmetic: when placements vanish rather than convert, no applicant is better off, while the ambitious but poor student who scraped through loses her foothold. CON also offered a coherent limiting principle—consent and honest terms—against PRO's bureaucratic standard, and showed that PRO's complaint-driven enforcement created the very chilling effect CON predicted. PRO's moral case about class filtering remained resonant, but could not overcome CON's demonstration that heavy restriction risks leveling-down harm without assured upside.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of PRO's dilemma in the second rebuttal and closing, showing that PRO's fallback—"paid or discontinued"—means zero workers are helped when firms simply drop the role, collapsing PRO's equity claim and turning the burden back onto PRO as punitive rather than remedial.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): PRO maintained two live routes: equity filter laundering wealth into merit, and displacement/power. CON's strongest hit - slots vanish not convert - was answered by A's dilemma that productive work must then be paid and marginal work costs nothing to lose. CON never supplied a workable line between education and exploitation beyond thin consent/honest terms, which A exposed in Pressure and Closing as permitting the coffee-run status quo. A offered an administrable educational test that directly implements the proposition's exemption, making PRO's path clearer to a third-party reader.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1/A5 pressure that B's consent-and-honesty standard cannot distinguish a genuine training placement from a consensual coffee-run, leaving CON defending the status quo rather than a competing standard.
