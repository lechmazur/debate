- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by making the motion's residual work decide the round. After accepting standard-over-priority and preference-following, CON forced PRO to name a first-time signal beyond past choice; PRO offered none and retreated to an active "Delivery or pickup?" screen plus tip/priority cleanup. That left PRO defending either status-quo ground CON already granted or transparency CON had offered from the opening, while the hard pickup default stayed undefended and its failure cost was admitted as one cold meal. PRO's silent-vs-loud stickiness critique and motion wording ("or lower-fee") were the best counter-pressure and kept the margin modest, but they did not restore a clean affirmative path once the contested steering was hollowed out.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4/A2 sequence after the signal question: no non-preference signal for stranger pickup, so PRO's leftover proposal is active choice/transparency under the motion's label.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by defending a broader, textually grounded reading of the motion: it covers not only pickup but lower-fee defaults generally, such as removing priority highlights, inflated tip presets, and pre-filled delivery modes. Side A showed that these silent, recurring costs exploit the same user inattention Side B relied on, making Side B’s transparency alternative insufficient. Side B’s error-asymmetry and “pickup for strangers” framing were blunted by Side A’s targeting distinction and by the unresolved tension that a user inattentive enough to miss a toggle will also miss fee disclosures. Side A’s closing crystallization of “loud versus silent errors” and “who should the default serve” provided the cleaner comparative lens and the more live route to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s distinction between loud, self-correcting pickup errors and silent, compounding fee extraction, and its successful flipping of Side B’s cost question to show that invisible recurring overcharges are costlier and less remediable than a rare, visible, refundable mistaken pickup.
