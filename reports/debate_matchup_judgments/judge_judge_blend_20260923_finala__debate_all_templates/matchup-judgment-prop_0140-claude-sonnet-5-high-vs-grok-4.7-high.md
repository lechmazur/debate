### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by isolating the missing causal link in Side A's case. Side A showed that a bounded zone could make some drinking more visible and easier to supervise, but never established that legalization would meaningfully replace pregaming, flasks, or parking-lot drinking rather than add another low-friction drinking period. Side B also persuasively separated general nightlife-management tools from the specific permission to carry alcohol in public. Side A answered the enforcement objection well, but did not resolve cumulative service across vendors or increased total consumption.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's decisive response was that open-container legalization does not reach drinking that occurs before patrons arrive, so Side A's claimed relocation may instead be addition. Side A repeatedly asserted that the zone competes with hidden spaces but never explained why those drinkers would substitute rather than do both.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a persistent burden PRO never answered: legalizing open containers does not replace hidden pregame and alley drinking, it adds a new layer of sanctioned street consumption on top of it. PRO's framework assumed relocation, but CON showed persuasively that drinking in homes and cars happens before the zone, while the zone itself removes tabs, last calls, and single-server accountability. This "addition not relocation" reframe survived the full exchange, and PRO's closing merely repeated that tools cannot stop all hidden drinking rather than showing why adding visible-but-diffused drinking improves the overall risk profile. CON also successfully pressed the enforcement burden of cup-policing a crowded street and showed that management tools are separable from legalization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "addition not relocation" turn, first developed in Rebuttal 1 and sharpened in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which showed that pregame drinking persists while open-container zones layer a new ungoverned drinking period on top—changing the comparison from visible-versus-hidden to one-unregulated-period versus two.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully defending the core comparative frame of the debate: managed street drinking versus unregulated hidden drinking. While Side B made strong rhetorical moves arguing that open containers "add" to rather than "replace" hidden drinking (focusing heavily on pregaming), Side A effectively neutralized this in the later rounds. Side A's sharpest insight was that Side B's preferred tools (lighting, patrols, curfews) cannot govern drinking that has already escaped the licensed premises into alleys and cars. Side A's answers to the pressure questions regarding enforcement (checking cups at the point of sale and via spot checks at the zone's edge) were highly practical and dismantled Side B's assumption that a citywide ban is easier to enforce. Side A's closing crystallized the clash perfectly, leaving Side B's "addition" argument unresolved against the reality of the "overflow" that already exists under a ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's "addition vs. relocation" frame by clarifying that the managed zone competes with the unregulated alley, not the licensed bar, and that CON's management tools cannot reach drinking that has already left the premises.
