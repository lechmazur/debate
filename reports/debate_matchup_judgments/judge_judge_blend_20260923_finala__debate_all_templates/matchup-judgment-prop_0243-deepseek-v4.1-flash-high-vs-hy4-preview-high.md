- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing that ride-hail structurally adds immediate, concentrated low-occupancy vehicle miles and induced demand in dense cores, while persuasively casting Side B’s offsets as diffuse, conditional, and unweighed. Side B’s citywide-ledger rebuttal was plausible but never squarely proved its subtractions exceeded Side A’s additions, and its reply that induced trips are local or off-peak failed to neutralize Side A’s chokepoint argument. Side A’s cleaner comparative framing and more tangible congestion mechanism provided the decisive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 1 dismantling of car-shedding and taxi-displacement offsets, showing that platform expansion and deadhead legs swamped claimed efficiency gains and forcing Side B to rely on unweighed speculative savings.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully shifting the burden of proof to Side B on the "overall" net ledger and systematically dismantling Side B's core mechanisms. Side A's contrast between the "visible" addition of app deadheading and the "invisible" subtraction of parking searches, blind taxi cruising, and owned-car VMT was highly persuasive. Crucially, in the closing, Side A completely neutralized Side B's most memorable soundbite ("a private car parks; the app car keeps moving") by pointing out that the parking was never free and often involved circling saturated blocks. Side B's failure to isolate app congestion from background trends (freight, population) left their "worse overall" claim unsubstantiated against Side A's citywide ledger framework.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing rebuttal to Side B's "private car parks" soundbite, explaining that pre-parking search involves circling the same blocks, effectively turning Side B's strongest rhetorical device against them.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by making the decisive clash easier to judge: deadhead and occupancy loss are additive even under CON’s best substitution story, transit-to-app shifts concentrate low-occupancy trips where roads fail nonlinearly, and peak-core delay sets overall city congestion more than off-stage ownership/parking offsets. B’s burden and visibility-bias framing was sharp and kept the race competitive, but stayed mostly “unproven / incomplete audit” without neutralizing A’s mechanism-plus-weighing path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R1/R2 deadhead point: even one-for-one private-car replacement is not a wash once empty approach/reposition miles are counted, so CON’s ledger never starts even.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins by establishing a coherent, mechanism-driven causal story—deadhead miles, modal shift from high-occupancy transit to low-occupancy vehicles, and nonlinear congestion effects in dense peak cores—while successfully reframing "overall" congestion as a system outcome driven by its critical bottleneck rather than a naive spatial average. CON's framework demanding perfect net audit was reasonable but remained largely defensive; its offsets (avoided second cars, eliminated parking search, first/last-mile benefits) were repeatedly shown to be either unmeasured, parked most of the week, off-peak, or still adding low-occupancy vehicle trips to saturated streets. A's pressure answers and closing crystallized that CON's unquantified hypotheticals could not outweigh PRO's demonstrated preponderant mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal answers to CON's pressure questions, where A demonstrated that app growth concentrated where transit/walking were already convenient (revealed substitution), that taxi fleets were capped while platforms multiplied vehicles, and that CON's own first/last-mile example still added a low-occupancy car to saturated streets—sharply undermining CON's offset narrative.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by establishing a stronger causal mechanism and a more effective weighing framework. Side B's argument about the nonlinear breakdown of traffic at the "tipping point" in dense cores successfully explains why adding low-occupancy, deadheading vehicles disproportionately worsens overall city congestion, effectively countering Side A's attempt to define "overall" as a simple citywide average. Furthermore, Side B decisively weighs the active, peak-hour congestion caused by ride-hail against Side A's passive, hypothetical offsets (like avoided second cars that sit parked most of the week). Side A relies too heavily on shifting the burden of proof and asserting that invisible offsets exist, without successfully proving they outweigh the visible, active additions to saturated road networks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of CON's "invisible offsets" by distinguishing between active peak-hour congestion (deadheading, idling, curb blocking) and passive offsets (parked second cars), combined with the nonlinearity argument showing how core breakdown defines overall city congestion.
