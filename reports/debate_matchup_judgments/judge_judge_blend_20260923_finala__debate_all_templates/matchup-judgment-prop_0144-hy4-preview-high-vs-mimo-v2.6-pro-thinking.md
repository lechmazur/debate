### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, escalating cases, but B's pressure-question sequence (the grief-bot analogy) forced A to clarify its "capacity" standard, and A's clarification shifted the test from a category-based rule ("engagement runs through amorous/sexual reciprocity") to a mechanics-based rule (memory of disclosures, escalation, withholding). B caught this drift explicitly in Rebuttal 2, showing it undercuts the case for banning an entire feature category by default rather than regulating the separable manipulative mechanics, and pressed the further reductio that any successful romance feature will out-engage "ordinary utility tools" and thus can never pass A's own exit test. A's closing reasserted its general framing (choice vs. pre-selection) but never directly repaired this internal inconsistency, leaving a live, unresolved route to victory for B. B also effectively turned A's "opt-in satisfies the rule" concession into a "signage, not protection" critique in the final exchange, which A's closing answered only partially. A's textual reading of "auto-suggest by default" was arguably the more natural one, and its onboarding-injury framing was clear and vivid throughout, but it did not close the specific coherence gap B exposed. B's Rebuttal 2 was clipped for length, which is a real execution cost, but the substantive content that survived still landed the decisive blow before the cutoff.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 catching that A's falsifiability answer (A4.1-A4.2) silently swapped a category-based reciprocity test for a mechanics-based test, then using A's own "ordinary utility tools" language to argue no compliant romantic feature could ever pass—an inconsistency A's closing never directly repaired.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): B won by establishing that default promotion creates an upstream exposure and consent problem even if manipulative mechanics should also be regulated. A compellingly attacked the imperfect remedy and B's shifting test, but overstated a one-tap opt-in as censorship and never showed that discoverability benefits outweigh reducing unsolicited exposure to dependency-forming intimacy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer that direct regulation and safer onboarding address separate stages was decisive: opt-in need not eliminate every later abuse to reduce the distinct harm of platforms proactively steering first-time users toward simulated intimacy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully established that romantic and sexual AI role-play features carry a distinctive capacity for dependency because the system becomes the object of attachment, making default auto-suggestion to new users unjustified. CON’s strongest line of attack—that manipulative mechanics, not the category, are the real problem—functioned more as a counterplan than a defense of the default, and PRO crystallized this effectively in closing by noting CON never actually defended auto-suggesting simulated intimacy to first-time users. PRO answered CON’s pressure on falsifiability with a coherent structural test and persuasively distinguished grief-support tools from romantic role-play. CON’s attempt to equate “auto-suggest” with any surface-level mention overread the motion’s “by default” language, and its Rebuttal 2 word-limit clip added an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 response to CON’s Pressure Questions, where PRO specified the exit criteria for its test (design audit showing no simulated amorous reciprocity, no memory used to escalate intimacy, no withholding affection) and demonstrated that the line was structural rather than based on content distaste, neutralizing CON’s “unfalsifiable standard” and “overreach” charges.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more consistent case around consent quality and the distinction between disclosure (which A explicitly conceded was fine) and an auto-initiated persona relationship (which A argued is the actual harm the motion targets). A capitalized effectively on B's own concession that "the attachment engine is the risk, not the onboarding tile," turning it into a foothold that the tile is precisely the on-ramp into that engine. B's strongest moves—timing (disclosure is cheapest before attachment forms) and the vocabulary-gap/unvetted-clone displacement argument—were plausible but never fully closed the loop: since A's proposal keeps the feature discoverable via menu/search/neutral note, the claim that hiding the default necessarily exports users to worse, unregulated products remained asserted rather than demonstrated. B's core defense that the tile is "just a label" also sat awkwardly against its own description of companions as memory-bearing, escalating, emotionally coercive systems—undercutting the claim that a dismissible card meaningfully equalizes the encounter. Both sides answered pressure questions in proper A1/A2 format, but A's answers more directly reinforced its persona/information distinction, while B's answers largely restated its tile-as-disclosure framing without resolving why the tile must instantiate a companion rather than plain text. A did suffer real execution costs from two clipped speeches, but the substantive content in both cases was already delivered before the cut, so the damage was limited to rhetorical polish rather than lost arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's move in Rebuttal 1 ([A2.3]) turning B's own concession—"the attachment engine...is the risk, not the onboarding tile"—into proof that the tile is the on-ramp to that engine was the most decisive exchange; B never fully neutralized it, instead retreating to timing and labeling arguments that didn't address why a persona-based default (rather than plain disclosure) is necessary.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won the central comparison: platforms can clearly disclose and safeguard intimate features without default recommendations that predictably increase uptake into the risky attachment engine. Side A's vocabulary-barrier argument was creative, but its claim that users would migrate to unvetted clones was speculative, and it never established why warnings could not appear after deliberate opt-in. B's two clipped turns modestly reduced its margin but did not obscure its case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively separated neutral, explicit disclosure from auto-suggestion and showed that menus, category listings, warnings, and safeguards all remain available under PRO. This undercut A's repeated framing that the motion necessarily hides the feature.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO won by isolating the decisive distinction between passive disclosure (category listings, neutral notes) and active introduction (auto-suggesting a romantic or sexual companion persona). CON repeatedly defended disclosure—calling the tile a "map," "warning label," and "shelf label"—but never squarely justified why a platform must actively guess a new user's desire for intimacy rather than simply informing them the feature exists. PRO capitalized on CON's concession that the attachment engine is the real risk, showing that auto-suggest is the unrequested on-ramp delivering users straight into that engine without any signal of interest. CON's displacement argument (unvetted clones) remained speculative and was blunted by PRO's reminder that search, menus, and category browsing all remain available. The closing crystallized the clash cleanly: the metric is consent quality, not discoverability, and CON never defended the actual introduction of an intimate persona to strangers at signup.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's distinction between disclosure and introduction in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which turned CON's supermarket analogy against them: shelves disclose inventory, but auto-suggest is handing a stranger a specific intimate relationship they never ordered.
