### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Both sides built well-constructed, closely matched cases with vivid, plausible mechanisms and no invented sourcing. CON's throughline—durable incapacitation versus temporary "dosage"—stayed sharp and was reinforced by two hard-to-answer concrete points: patrol cannot reach domestic violence inside a home, and hot-spot deterrence requires sustaining a high volume of discretionary stops under tight accountability, which is far more fragile than the single clean arrest CON's mechanism needs. PRO's strongest thrust—that incapacitation is inert until an arrest happens, and most shootings go unsolved—was a genuine structural threat, but CON's answer (repeat offenders are eventually caught for something, and the chill problem is asymmetric because hot spots need volume while sentences need one event) blunted it enough to hold the comparison. PRO's attempt to fold domestic violence "addresses" into hot spots was correctly called out as definition-stretching, and its closing move ("hot spots supply the arrests sentences require") was clever but conceded rather than won the comparative question. The pressure-question exchange was the sharpest moment of the debate and CON's follow-up answers landed more cleanly than PRO's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to PRO's chill-symmetry argument—distinguishing the volume of stops hot-spot dosage requires from the single clean arrest a sentence enhancement needs—most directly neutralized PRO's strongest pressure point (the "shield and sword" accountability paradox) and reset the comparative frame in CON's favor.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A won narrowly by identifying the arrest-and-conviction bottleneck that added sentence years cannot solve and by explaining how accountable hot-spot policing can prevent violence before conviction. B powerfully established durability, but repeatedly treated marginally longer sentences as though they were incarceration itself, assumed repeat offenders would inevitably be caught again, and never answered A’s age-out objection to added years.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest response distinguished the repeated discretionary contacts needed to sustain hot-spot policing from one clean arrest producing years of incapacitation. A ultimately limited its force by showing that visible disruption need not mean indiscriminate stops and that B’s sentencing mechanism still depends on the scarce arrest it assumes.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by making durability and mechanism asymmetry the decisive comparison. A’s bottleneck argument (sentences need convictions; many shootings go unsolved) landed and forced real answers, but B neutralized it by limiting the claim to proven repeaters who already cycle through arrests and by showing that extra years erase a string of later crimes once that touch happens. B’s live route—temporary rented quiet versus 24/7 removal that reaches homes, displaced blocks, and intimidated witnesses—survived more cleanly than A’s front-loaded certainty, which still ends when dosage ends and depends on the high-volume discretionary contacts A’s own accountability package risks chilling. A debated strongly and kept the case comparative; B made the choice easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 chill-asymmetry turn: hot spots need continuous high-discretion volume that accountability freezes, while longer sentences need one clean individualized case then durable removal.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent causal stories, but PRO (B) won the central clash over displacement/replacement. CON's strongest weapon was "removing the recruiter removes the network," but PRO turned this against CON in Rebuttal 2 with the "the corner selects for lethality, not against it" argument—claiming that killing the deterrence-via-incapacitation logic backfires because profitable corners promote the most violent successor. CON never squarely answered this in its final rebuttal or closing, instead repeating that a successor "faces the same enhanced sentence," which doesn't rebut the escalation mechanism. PRO also answered CON's sharpest pressure question (that accountability rules would suppress deterrent stops) with a concrete, plausible mechanism—documented suspicion, cameras, data-driven deployment, and audits—that preserved the deterrence story while conceding only "low-yield" stops. CON's Rebuttal 2 was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, costing it its final full articulation of the durability argument, a real execution penalty per the judging frame. PRO's closing ("violence is a job opening, not just a bad man") crystallized its case memorably and left the network-selection point unanswered, giving it the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to the recruiter/replacement argument—"the network promotes the most violent successor... break the place and you break the promotion ladder"—turned CON's own incapacitation logic against it and was never adequately rebutted afterward.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by comparing immediate, geographically concentrated prevention against the merely marginal years added to already-long sentences. B made durability and displacement credible concerns, but repeatedly treated sentence enhancements as though they created the entire incapacitation period and never adequately answered A’s aging-out, delay, replacement, and community-cooperation arguments. A also directly answered both pressure questions with concrete accountability rules and rotating deployment, while B’s second rebuttal was clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange turned B’s recruiter argument against it: if offenders congregate and recruit at profitable violent locations, removing one person leaves the underlying market and replacement mechanism intact, whereas accountable place-based policing can disrupt multiple prospective offenders at once. B’s claim that successors would be less lethal remained asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping the live comparison on immediacy, incomplete catch rates, and place ecology: B’s “congregate” premise was flipped into a reason corners keep producing replacements, so delayed incapacitation of the few convicted does less total prevention than securing the stage tonight. B’s durability/portability line stayed alive and clean, but the clipped R2 and weaker answers on “protect the block tonight” and seamless replacement left A’s path easier to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R1 turn of B’s congregate claim: remove one man and the corner recruits by weekend; secure the ecology and the network loses its stage (A2.1–A2.3), later capped by the “selects for lethality” reply (A4.5).
