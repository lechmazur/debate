### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON built the more resilient structure. Its sharpest move was the dilemma pressed at A2.3: if loss aversion truly hijacks judgment, a self-armed toggle set in a calm moment is exactly what collapses at the moment of peak pressure; if the user is reflective enough to hold the toggle in place against temptation, the paternalism case weakens. PRO's answer (friction/delay, analogized to Do Not Disturb) never fully escapes this — CON's comeback that "friction that binds is my instrument, not theirs" turned PRO's own defense into support for caps/cooling-off over toggles. CON also sustained two under-answered lines: the regressive effect on small creators who rely on visible momentum, and the line-drawing problem for "personalized upsells" (a named goal pitched to a lapsed donor is both targeted and monetized, and PRO's closing reassurance didn't cleanly exclude it). PRO did land real points — especially clarifying that the mute is self-only, preserving third-party visibility for moderators/friends — but CON never squarely absorbed this, repeating the "hides the trail" claim in later rounds without engaging the refinement, which is a genuine gap on CON's side. On balance, though, CON's cumulative rebuttal package (contradiction, displacement, administrability, regressivity) proved harder for PRO to fully close than PRO's package was for CON, even accounting for CON's two clipped speeches as a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over pre-commitment: CON's contradiction charge (A2.3) forced PRO into a "friction/delay" defense (B4.1) that CON then converted into the debate's clearest weighing line — "friction that binds at the hot moment is my instrument, not theirs" (A4.3) — showing CON's caps/cooling-off succeed exactly where PRO's toggle concedes it can be reopened.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the motion narrow — user-controlled, opt-in hides and disables — which directly neutralized CON's opacity and regressivity claims. CON's best push (hijacked user won't use toggle, caps are better) was answered by B2.3/B4.1: toggle is private to viewer while still visible to moderators, and friction/delay makes pre-commitment work unlike CON's caricature. CON conceded displacement helps PRO [B2.8] and suffered repeated clipping costs, leaving no live harm that outweighs giving users a switch for weaponized intimacy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.3-B2.8 plus B4.1-A1: turning opacity charge into user-side valve and recasting displacement as intended degradation, with friction-delay answering the hijack contradiction.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B wins by exposing the central paradox in Side A's case: a user whose judgment is "hijacked" by parasocial pressure is the least likely to effectively use a self-help UI toggle. When Side A attempts to solve this with "friction" and "delay" to re-enable the toggle, Side B sharply points out that this is just a clumsier version of the hard financial caps and cooling-off periods Side B already advocated for. Side B's distinction between "theatre" (a UI toggle) and "protection" (a financial cap) decisively resolves the core clash. Although Side B suffered minor clipping at the very end of several turns, the core arguments were fully visible and highly effective, making the execution cost negligible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's pre-commitment defense, showing that a UI toggle with a re-enable delay is just an inferior, easily bypassed version of Side B's proposed financial cooling-off periods and hard caps.
