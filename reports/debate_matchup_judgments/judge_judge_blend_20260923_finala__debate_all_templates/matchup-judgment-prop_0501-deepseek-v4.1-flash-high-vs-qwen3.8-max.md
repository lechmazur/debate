### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): The pivotal exchange was the Pressure Questions round. CON forced PRO to specify weights, triggers, and treatment of supply-driven price rises, and PRO's answers (no fixed weights, no automatic rate rise, exemption for supply-driven increases, credit tools first) effectively conceded that the operational mandate is a leverage/financial-stability framework rather than a genuine asset-price target. CON then drove this home in Rebuttal 2 and Closing: "if it means avoiding unsustainable credit spirals, that's leverage, not price; if it means a real second target, you still can't specify weights or triggers." PRO's response—that this is "constrained judgment" backed by mandatory publication and accountability—never resolved the core definitional problem: once supply-driven price increases are exempted and rate action is contingent rather than automatic, the distinctive claim of the motion (targeting asset prices, especially housing, as such) collapses into the financial-stability regime CON already endorsed. PRO's closing reasserted the accountability framing well but did not rebut the specific concession CON extracted. CON's case was slightly more disciplined throughout: it consistently distinguished leverage indicators from price targets and used the political-asymmetry point (falling prices as "instability," rising prices as "unfairness") to show real-world cost of an explicit price mandate, a point PRO never fully neutralized. Both sides argued well and stayed on topic, but CON's use of the cross-examination to expose a structural ambiguity in PRO's proposal, combined with a clean closing that named the concession, gives it the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Questions (Q1/Q2) forcing PRO to specify weights/triggers and the supply-vs-credit distinction, followed by PRO's answers in Rebuttal 2 conceding no fixed weights, no automatic rate action, and exemption for supply-driven prices — which CON then leveraged in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to argue the "target" is really just a leverage-focused financial-stability mandate in disguise.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash by forcing PRO to operationalize the housing target as no fixed weights, credit tools first, no automatic rate rise, and exemptions for supply-driven rises—then showing that either collapses the motion into a leverage/financial-stability regime (losing its distinct claim) or leaves a politicized second objective with no coherent metric. PRO’s early-action timing case stayed live but was undercut once its own answers conceded CON’s toolkit hierarchy; PRO never recovered a non-duplicative reason why “asset-price stability” must be the mandate rather than accountable leverage tools.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2 (A4.4–A4.7): using PRO’s A1/A2 answers to show the “target” is either financial-stability under another name or an undefined second CPI-style objective that invites distributional politics.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON successfully trapped PRO in a dual bind: the proposed housing target is either too vague to be operational or it collapses into a leverage-focused financial-stability framework. When pressed for exact weights, triggers, and automatic responses, PRO conceded no fixed numbers, credit tools first, and supply-driven exemptions. CON weaponized this to show the motion adds no distinct value beyond a financial-stability mandate and would introduce politicized conflicts over undefined price goals. PRO's case that CPI alone misses housing booms remained live but was insufficient to overcome the demonstration that their remedy was either unworkable or redundant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing crystallized that PRO's answers to pressure questions described a financial-stability escalation ladder rather than an asset-price target, leaving PRO unable to explain why the motion's distinctive additional mandate was necessary or definable.
