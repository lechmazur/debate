### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by separating contestability from mandatory human authorship. It showed that reasons, agency accountability, correction, and human review where needed do not require a human to make every initial high-volume determination. Side B powerfully framed individualized harm and listening, but never adequately resolved A’s dilemma: either a signature suffices and invites rubber-stamping, or the enforceable test is whether reasons engage the facts, in which case the human-species requirement adds little beyond explanation and review while creating delay. B’s repeated clipping was also a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was the response to B’s sham-signature standard: if courts establish genuine judgment by checking whether reasons engage the claimant’s facts, that same safeguard can review an automated determination without mandating a human “last click.” This directly weakened B’s claimed distinction between human accountability and ordinary contestability.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A wins by effectively narrowing the debate to the mechanical consequences of the right. A successfully shows that B's standard for enforcing human judgment (testing if reasons engage with facts) collapses into an explanation requirement, not a mandate on the decision-maker's species. A also convincingly flips B's "human before the harm" argument by showing that mandating human intervention on every initial decision creates a massive queue, meaning "the human-actor right is the wait." Side B argued with strong rhetorical appeal but suffered execution costs from repeatedly hitting the word cap and failing to defend the scalability of their mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 decisively leveraged B's answer to Q1, pointing out that if the legal test for a "real human" is just whether the written reasons engage the facts, then "species dropped out" and B is merely arguing for better explanations.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively isolating the mechanism of the proposition: a human decision-maker for the initial decision. Side A effectively demonstrates that this creates a massive bottleneck, causing the very delays and harms Side B decries. Furthermore, Side A traps Side B on the definition of a "real" human decision-maker; if the test is simply whether the reasons engage the facts, then the biological human requirement drops out, reducing the proposition to a right to adequate explanation, which Side A already accepts. Side B's emotional appeals were strong, but they could not overcome Side A's tight logical dismantling of the motion's practical and theoretical implications.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal in Turn 8 and Closing, where they point out that if Side B's test for a "real human" is just whether the reasons engage the facts, then the "species" requirement drops out, and Side B is merely litigating a right to adequate explanation rather than a human decision-maker.
