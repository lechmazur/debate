### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON built and sustained a genuine fork that PRO never cleanly escaped: either the sleep/boredom/play frame stays pure and leaves awake, in-bounds harms (grooming in chat, self-harm feeds during permitted hours, spending loops) completely uncaught, or PRO imports feature/design-level rules to catch them — in which case the "core" is doing no real protective work and the motion's own toolkit (app/feature judgment) is quietly reinstated. The Pressure Questions round crystallized this (B3.2's 4pm self-harm-feed case), and PRO's answer in Rebuttal 2 had to invent a freestanding principle ("do not leave a child alone with a system that escalates distress") that, as CON immediately noted, has no derivation from sleep, boredom, or play. PRO's closing reasserted that these cuts "serve" the three goals but never showed the logical link for cases outside idle/play time, leaving the fork unresolved in CON's favor. PRO's case (sleep/curfew durability, displacing app-chasing) remained plausible and rhetorically clean, but it did not answer the load-bearing objection that the "core" label is doing less work than claimed. Both sides suffered clipped speeches from length overages, slightly more often for CON, but this did not offset the substantive gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question (B3.2) about the 4pm self-harm feed, combined with B4.1's follow-up that PRO's answer ("do not leave a child alone with a system that escalates distress") is a design/supervision principle "with no reference to sleep, boredom, or play" — exposing that PRO's core frame needed a smuggled fourth principle to catch the hardest cases.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by preserving the motion’s exact distinction: making durable developmental goals the core does not require silence about safety, and cross-platform rules about chat, spending, or harmful feeds are not battles fought one app at a time. Side A’s awake-harms fork created strong pressure, but ultimately conflated general feature-pattern guidance with the rotating title-by-title approach the proposition rejects. Side B’s safety-floor model therefore survived, though its attempts to derive every harm from play or idleness were somewhat strained. Side A’s repeated clipping also imposed a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s answer to Side A’s fork was decisive: a short, durable safety floor can coexist with sleep, boredom, and free play as the core frame without restoring an app-by-app hit list. Side A never fully answered why rules about recurring mechanics such as open chat or paid randomness count as fighting one app at a time.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): B won the durability-vs-content clash. PRO's own test that "a pattern that invades rest or play earns a rule" [A2.7] lets a 4pm self-harm feed, grooming chat, and spending loop pass. To patch it PRO added "no unsupervised adult contact, no spending loops, no system that escalates distress" [A2.4][A4.4] — feature-level rules that are exactly the app-level judgment the motion says to stop leading with. B's fork — strip the bolt-ons and awake harms are unaddressed, keep them and the three goals are a preamble, not the core — was never resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/C2.1 fork exposing that PRO's rest/play test passes core harms and that PRO's operative safety advice smuggles back design-level guidance, turning PRO's durability claim into durable absence of guidance.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Both sides built serious, well-structured cases, but CON's rebuttal sequence did more decisive work. PRO's central weapon was that app-by-app lists "expire" while sleep/play frames don't; CON neutralized this by reframing its own proposal as function-based (no unsolicited contact, no purchase loops, no nighttime push, no infinite autoplay) rather than app-name chasing, showing these classes survive rebrands just as well as PRO's frame does, while also covering harms (grooming, financial exploitation) that displacement-based framing structurally cannot reach. PRO's answer to CON's sharpest pressure question (Q1: is a well-slept, well-played child still exposed to stranger contact a "success"?) effectively conceded that such harms sit outside the contested "core," which CON then used decisively in closing to argue the frame measures the wrong thing and that "core" language, by the motion's own "rather than" phrasing, means these safety concerns get deprioritized under real-world time constraints — a dynamic PRO's own rebuttal (advice under a short visit) inadvertently confirmed. PRO's counters (seatbelt-analogy reversal, institutional ownership of recess/bedtime, the disclosure/trust argument) were solid but didn't fully dislodge CON's core distinction between displacement harms and categorically different content/contact harms, nor fully rebut CON's functional-class fix to the "list expires" problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's answer to PRO's Pressure Q1 (A4-parallel B4.1-B4.2), which reframed CON's proposal from app-specific to function-specific rules, directly defusing PRO's central "the list always expires" argument while simultaneously exposing that PRO's frame cannot cover harms like stranger contact or spending loops — a concession PRO made explicit in A4.1 and that CON then weaponized in its closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that sleep and play protect against displacement but cannot serve as the core response to contact, spending, and design harms that occur even when a child sleeps and plays. A also replaced app-by-app policing with durable function-level rules and identified concrete school-account and household levers, undercutting B's scalability and control objections. B's claim that no device tactic must be dropped conflicted with its own insistence that scarce guidance prioritize the three proposed goals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered B's future-feature challenge with standing functional categories and answered the control challenge with school-managed accounts, allow-lists, messaging restrictions, purchase controls, and downtime protocols. This directly weakened B's claim that only its framing survives redesigns.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON won the core coverage clash. PRO’s durable-goals frame is attractive for displacement, but conceded that stranger contact and spending loops fall outside those three core goals. B’s pressure Q1 exposed that a child can sleep nine hours and still be in harm, which PRO answered only as baseline safety plus disclosure, not prevention. B then answered the lever-ownership challenge with school-controlled account/OS controls that survive a rebrand, neutralizing PRO’s “vendors own the toggle” claim. The “rather than” substitution made PRO’s attempt to keep both frame and feature battles look decorative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1-A2 plus B5 closing: showing a new feature can run all afternoon on a well-slept child, and that function-classes (no unsolicited adult contact, no purchase loop, no night push) are enforceable at the account layer schools issue, while PRO’s reply relied on offline identity and trusted-adult disclosure.
