### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won a strong, competitive debate by isolating the motion's actual burden: not whether accessibility needs enforcement, but whether cash fines add value beyond binding remediation. Side A persuasively defended consequences and proportional enforcement, yet its central claim that fines supply the trigger was defeated because fines themselves require the same regulator, audit, notice, and verification as orders. Side B also maintained plausible comparative harms involving checkbox compliance and service withdrawal, while Side A's deterrence claim largely assumed that monetary sanctions uniquely create urgency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's decisive response was that fines do not self-execute: the regulator that identifies repeated failure and issues a fine could instead issue a deadline-backed remediation order. This separated the enforcement trigger from the monetary sanction and undercut Side A's repeated description of fines as the system's necessary motor.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating the enforcement mechanism (the regulator) from the penalty (the fine). A proved that Side B's benefits actually stemmed from the regulatory apparatus (audits, notices, orders) rather than the cash fine itself, while highlighting the unique harms of fines (checkbox compliance, overlays, freezing innovation) which B left largely unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's response to B4's claim that fines act as a "motor" or trigger. A pointed out that fines do not self-execute and require the exact same regulatory intake and audit steps as a remediation order, collapsing B's primary advantage.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that fines add distinct pathologies—checkbox overlays, service-removal incentives, and unthreadable scale problems—without demonstrating unique compulsion beyond what contempt-backed remediation orders already provide. PRO never squarely answered why a regulator with audit powers and orders deters less than one with invoices, and CON's analytical separation of the enforcement trigger from the cash sanction proved the more durable framework through the later speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Questions and subsequent rebuttal exposing that PRO's recurring escalating fines are essentially a remediation order with a meter bolted on, and that the cash component introduces perverse incentives (automated scanner theater, chilling effect on launching new services) that the order alone avoids.
