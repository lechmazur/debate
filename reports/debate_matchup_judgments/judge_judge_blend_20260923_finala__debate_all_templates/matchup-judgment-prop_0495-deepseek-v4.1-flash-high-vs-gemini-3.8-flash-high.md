### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): B won a competitive debate by establishing the public-good and security failures of unpaid maintenance while resisting A's overly rigid equation of comparable funding with permanent civil-service tenure. A's procurement alternative was credible and its allocation objections were sharp, but dependency mapping, periodic review, and sunset criteria substantially answered them. The alleged contradiction between economic security and adaptive eligibility was not decisive because comparable compensation need not mean lifetime employment. Both sides incurred minor costs from nonexistent C-reference IDs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest response was that public funding can operate through independent grants or trusts without nationalizing projects, followed by risk-based selection and periodic review. This undercut A's central frozen-entitlement framing, although A effectively exposed that beneficiary-funded procurement was not merely voluntary charity.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing the live decision onto the motion's actual instrument, not the shared premise that critical code needs support. PRO established free-riding, public-good status, and Heartbleed/XZ risk, but repeatedly softened "comparable to essential-infrastructure workers" into grants/contracts while still claiming guaranteed economic security, then undercut that guarantee with dynamic sunsetting. CON answered the obscure-dependency pressure via SBOM/procurement mandates and kept the cleaner comparison: contracts and risk-targeted funding can fund maintenance without a politicized, frozen maintainer class that a shifting global dependency graph cannot stably support. The debate became easier to decide once CON made that instrument dilemma decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2/closing trap on PRO's A4 answers: guaranteed parity and periodic sunsetting cannot both satisfy the motion's "comparable worker" framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a structural contradiction in the motion that PRO never resolved. PRO argued that critical maintainers need "guaranteed economic security" comparable to essential infrastructure workers, but also claimed funding would use "dynamic sunsetting" and periodic re-evaluation as dependencies shift. CON showed these two commitments are incompatible: if pay can vanish when a library is superseded, it is not comparable to an essential worker's stable compensation; if it cannot, the scheme creates frozen, lobbyable entitlements. PRO's closing ignored this tension entirely and simply repeated market-failure claims, while CON's closing crystallized it cleanly. CON also offered a coherent alternative—targeted procurement mandates, SBOM visibility, and maintenance contracts—that addressed the same free-riding harms without the perverse incentives of a public salary guarantee. Because PRO could not reconcile the guarantee with open source's shifting global graph, CON's critique remained live and decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 demonstration that PRO's "guaranteed economic security" and "dynamic sunsetting" are mutually exclusive under the motion's terms, trapping PRO between frozen entitlements and unstable non-parity.
