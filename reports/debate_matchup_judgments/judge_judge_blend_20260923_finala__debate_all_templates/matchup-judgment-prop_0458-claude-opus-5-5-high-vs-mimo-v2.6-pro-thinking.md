### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the policy comparison more precise: restricting future terminals does not abruptly cancel current cargoes, while partial rather than complete foreign replacement still avoids some drilling, leakage, and long-term gas dependence. Side A persuasively explained that coal and foreign gas can substitute at different margins, defeating B's claimed logical contradiction, but it never established that these effects would outweigh reduced gas demand. B also retained the clearer domestic pathway through avoided export-driven wells. Side A's extra clipping and mistaken reference further weakened execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing distinction between current shortages and future terminal approvals was decisive. It undercut Side A's strongest coal-switching example and reframed partial replacement as sufficient for avoided methane, even though Side A had correctly answered that coal and foreign gas substitution can coexist across different time horizons.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won by pairing a tight policy mechanism (capping only *new* terminals) with a perfectly executed trap regarding substitution timelines. Side A forced Side B to split its substitution impacts into a short-term price shock (coal) and medium-term replacement (dirtier gas), which Side A then dismantled in the closing by pointing out their policy creates no short-term shock and tilts medium-term planners toward renewables.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Closing brilliantly weaponized Side B's Pressure Question answer, demonstrating that the short-term coal spike Side B relied on would not occur under a policy that only caps future terminals.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won because it successfully narrowed the debate to the long-term lock-in effects of new terminals, protected existing contracts to blunt B’s supply-shock narratives, and exposed a central tension in B’s substitution story: withheld supply cannot simultaneously be fully replaced by dirtier gas and price poor buyers into coal. A’s “cleanest molecule is the one never extracted” frame gave voters a concrete standard, and its closing crystallized that partial replacement over thirty years means gas never drilled and never leaked. B’s comparative-leakage argument remained plausible but less decisive because it relied on an undemonstrated “looser jurisdiction” counterfactual and never fully answered A’s pressure that new wells drilled for export terminals add directly to American methane inventories.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Pressure Question 1 and the follow-up in Rebuttal 2 ([A3.1]–[A4.6]), where A showed that B’s twin claims of full foreign replacement and coal displacement for poor buyers were mutually undermining, and then used B’s concession that replacement is partial to argue that the unreleased share is gas permanently kept in the ground.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won the global comparison. It plausibly showed that withheld US supply divides between replacement by less accountable producers and unmet demand that raises prices and encourages coal use. Side B never adequately answered this; its claim that an additional cargo raises the global LNG price was economically backwards. Side B did establish a credible domestic upstream-methane benefit from preventing new infrastructure, but its broader domestic-transition claims were weaker and source regulation remained a viable alternative. A's two clipped turns were execution costs, but its central case remained clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A decisively answered B's alleged contradiction by explaining that substitution and scarcity are simultaneous shares of the same market response, then exposed B's erroneous claim that added LNG supply raises the global clearing price.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won by maintaining a stronger, more realistic causal mechanism for how global markets respond to supply restrictions. PRO made a critical error in arguing that adding global supply increases clearing prices, which CON effectively exploited. CON also successfully narrowed PRO's domestic decarbonization impacts to upstream leaks, and then convincingly argued that shifting those leaks to less regulated foreign basins defeats the motion's purpose.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's explanation in Rebuttal 2 and Closing that a withheld cargo naturally splits between partial substitution (dirtier gas) and unmet demand (coal) perfectly neutralized PRO's attempt to frame this as a contradiction.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive clash over global emissions. PRO showed that restricting exports would curb new US drilling, but never proved that the withheld supply would simply disappear from the global market. CON successfully argued that every withheld cargo splits two ways: some demand is replaced by foreign suppliers with weaker oversight, and the remainder is rations the poorest buyers back onto coal. This dilemma undercut PRO's core claim to lower global methane. PRO's response—calling the split "rhetoric" rather than engaging the market mechanics—was too thin. On domestic decarbonization, PRO offered a coherent upstream-capital story, but CON showed that the same upstream leaks are better targeted by source regulation on every molecule, while an export cap leaves domestic combustion untouched. CON's sharper pressure questions and cleaner weighing made the path to victory clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing framed the decisive dilemma: a withheld American cargo is partially replaced by dirtier foreign gas and partially leaves poor buyers burning coal, so PRO cannot claim lower global emissions either way.
