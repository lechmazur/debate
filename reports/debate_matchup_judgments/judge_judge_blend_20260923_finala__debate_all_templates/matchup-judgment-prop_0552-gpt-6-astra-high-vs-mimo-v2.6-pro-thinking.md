### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): The debate turned on a single structural tension in PRO's case: PRO's own "certified regional processing" exception is itself a cross-border shipment, which CON exploited relentlessly ("PRO cannot distrust the label when I propose it and trust it when it reappears inside its own policy"). PRO's answer—that its regional processing is "bounded" with "joint oversight" versus CON's licensing—was asserted more than justified, since it never explained why boundedness solves the enforceability problem that originally motivated the ban but doesn't equally apply to CON's conditional-trade model. CON also landed a strong causal point that bans remove the lawful channel and the leverage (license revocation, bonds, inspection hooks) that makes compliance worth protecting, while PRO's rebuttal that "both systems need enforcement" underplayed the qualitative difference between banning a whole income-tier and revoking individual bad actors' licenses. CON's closing crystallized this into a memorable frame: PRO's live distinction had narrowed to destination income level alone, which reads as paternalism rather than harm-based policy. PRO never fully rebutted this with a developed account of why regulatory capacity or bargaining power correlates with income (it gestured at this in the opening but didn't develop it under pressure). PRO's case remained coherent and principled (cost internalization, upstream incentives, phased implementation) and its pressure questions were sharp, but CON's rebuttals did more damage to PRO's core mechanism than PRO's did to CON's. CON's clipped opening is a real execution cost, but it did not lose a decisive line of argument and CON recovered fully in subsequent rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 move (B2.1–B2.2) catching PRO in the certification contradiction—"either certification is credible enough to verify hazardous handling abroad... or it is not"—was the most decisive exchange; PRO's later attempt to distinguish "bounded" regional processing from CON's licensed export model never fully closed that gap, and CON's closing exploited the unresolved tension directly.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B won by successfully exposing the contradiction in Side A allowing 'certified regional processing' while banning exports based on income level. B neutralized A's concerns about cleanup with a compelling financial bond mechanism and persuasively weighed funded, regulated residual risk against the unfunded black-market risk created by a blanket ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's R2 A1, which elegantly defeated A's claim that licensing and bans face equal evasion risks by explaining that deterrence only works when a profitable, legal alternative is kept open to compliant firms.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON won the core enforcement/consistency clash. PRO's accountability principle never solved B's contradiction: if certified regional processing is verifiable, then a standards-based export model is verifiable too; if not, PRO's own exception fails. B's A1/A2 - license value as deterrent and pre-posted bond for residue - directly answered PRO's pressure on evasion and cleanup, while PRO's answer to B's Q2 on regional vs conditional export relied on vague "bounded" safeguards and conceded storage costs and lost trade, leaving the income filter as protectionism rather than risk management.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2.1-B2.4 / B4.1-B4.4: certified-regional contradiction plus license-vs-ban deterrence logic and financial bond for insolvent subcontractor, which turned PRO's fallback into CON's model minus the income ban.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON built the sharper, more disciplined case by isolating the real fault line: is the problem geography or verifiability? CON pressed this from the opening (specialist facility hypothetical) and forced PRO, in Pressure Questions, to concede directly that excluding a fully compliant distant facility adds "no additional safety benefit." That concession is the pivot of the debate: it shows PRO's blanket geographic ban is not calibrated to the harm it claims to prevent, and PRO never fully recovered, retreating to an administrability argument about "the trade that exists" rather than defending the rule as written. CON also caught and named PRO's evasion when PRO answered a storage-vs-compliant-treatment question by comparing storage to open burning instead (B4.6), and corrected PRO's attempt to claim CON had conceded that "most shipments" would fail verification (B4.1). PRO's opening and rebuttals had strong rhetorical framing (polluter-pays, "distance is not neutral"), but three of PRO's five speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits, an execution cost that repeatedly cut off PRO's own argument mid-thought. CON stayed within limits, answered pressure cleanly with A1/A2 formatting, and closed by crystallizing the decisive concession rather than merely repeating rhetoric. On balance, CON provided the clearer, more defensible path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Questions answer "In that strict hypothetical, no [additional safety benefit]" (A4.1), and CON's follow-up in Rebuttal 2 (B4.4-B4.6) turning that concession into the decisive frame while also catching PRO's evasive storage/open-burning comparison, was the turning point of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a structural contradiction in Side A's case: the regional exception relies on the exact same verification mechanisms that Side A claimed were impossible globally. Side B forced a direct concession that banning a fully compliant distant facility adds no safety benefit, cleanly winning the comparative weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively leveraged Side A's concession to Q1, showing that Side A's geographic veto removes safe treatment capacity without adding protection, while cleanly correcting Side A's misunderstanding of how domestic financial guarantees work.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON wins the enforceability clash. PRO concedes that banning a fully audited, residue-tracked facility adds no safety benefit [A4.1], shifting the dispute to whether verification is possible at distance. CON answers with a concrete model  security posted with home regulator, independent auditors funded by producer fees, no authorization without access, and halt/draw on security if records fail  which directly answers PRO's "theatre" claim and mirrors the burden PRO already accepts for regional certification. PRO never explains why that verification is credible regionally but impossible distantly, beyond asserting proximity. With both sides rejecting most mixed loads, the remaining unique prohibition lacks justification and forces storage over verified treatment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 and extension that geographic proximity does not create legal cooperation, and that security held at home with conditional authorization and material-balance audits directly neutralizes PRO's claim that distant verification is impossible theatre.
