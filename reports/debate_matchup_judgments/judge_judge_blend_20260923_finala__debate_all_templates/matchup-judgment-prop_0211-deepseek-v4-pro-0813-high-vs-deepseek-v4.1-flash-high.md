### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A won by showing that population-scale uptake creates aggregate budget risk that ordinary unit-price negotiation does not fully control. B persuasively exposed the danger of making access contingent on a deal, but its attack on outcome attribution did not defeat the alternative spending-cap route, and its rationing objection depended too heavily on a worst-case cap design. A also showed that both models require manufacturer agreement, while its model secures stronger fiscal predictability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to the prevention-attribution objection was decisive: because the motion is disjunctive, difficulty measuring long-term outcomes does not defeat spending caps. A then answered B’s calendar-cutoff framing by distinguishing a contractual expenditure ceiling from an automatic denial of the next patient.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won by making the decisive clash clearer: the proposition’s “only when” functions as a patient-facing gate if manufacturers refuse, and PRO repeatedly conceded that residual access then collapses to narrow high-need coverage. CON also kept forcing the motion-as-written problem—bare spending caps permit cutoff, while PRO’s rollover/exceptions rescue is an amendment—and showed ordinary unit-price negotiation already supplies an enforceable pre-listing limit without a disease-specific risk-shift. PRO’s fiscal-durability case was strong and coherent on population-scale budget shock, but never fully neutralized the veto/arbitrariness pressure or the prevention multi-causality critique.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 / closing crystallization of A’s concession (no deal → only narrow coverage) as the exact gate the motion creates, paired with the bare-text cap objection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that the proposition’s conditionality creates a manufacturer gate that undermines the promised broad access. PRO never escaped the implication that manufacturer refusal would leave only narrow coverage, and PRO’s defense of spending caps required idealizing the text with features (rollover, clinical exceptions) not actually in the motion. While PRO’s fiscal durability framing was plausible, CON successfully established that ordinary negotiated price, rebates, and volume thresholds can control budget shock without singling out obesity drugs for a special risk-shifting precondition that effectively gives manufacturers a veto over broad access.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal (Turn 8), where B sharpened the “judge the text as written” point, showed PRO was silently amending the motion to rescue spending caps, and demonstrated that PRO could not answer its own Pressure Question on enforceable limits without borrowing CON’s actual tool of an upfront negotiated unit price.
