### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): The decisive clash was whether deployment-level accountability (CON's regulatory standard) can substitute for the loss of human judgment at the moment of killing (PRO's accountability standard). PRO's cleanest contribution was the "pre-statable vs. unscripted judgment" line drawn in response to pressure questions: interceptor engagements can be fully specified in advance (physics-based hostility, no distinction/surrender judgment needed), while human-target engagements cannot, so the ban's line is principled rather than an escape hatch. CON never fully closed the gap PRO pressed hardest in closing: proportionality is a per-attack legal test, while CON's "reasonable duties/testing" standard operates at the level of a test set or procurement record, not the individual strike. CON's concession that "if all reasonable duties were met, there may be no criminal wrongdoer" let PRO reframe that admission as proof that autonomous deployment makes unaccountable killing the structural norm rather than a rare, reviewable exception as with human error — and CON's reply (asserting this doesn't become "standing condition... simply because targeting is automated") was asserted rather than demonstrated. PRO's closing crystallized this weighing effectively. B does lose some credit for two clipped speeches (opening and rebuttal 2), which is a real execution cost, but the visible content was sufficient to win the substantive exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's A1/A2 pressure questions (B4.1-B4.2), drawing the "pre-statable vs. unscripted judgment" line, combined with the closing highlight of CON's concession in A4.3 that reasonable-duty compliance can leave "no wrongdoer" — PRO used this to show the accountability gap becomes structural under autonomy rather than a rare, reviewable exception.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the live burden on a categorical ban versus strict, context-specific regulation and by more consistently answering the defensive/interceptor clash without definitional special pleading. PRO’s accountability and IHL-judgment case remained forceful and rhetorically sticky, but CON neutralized the “no wrongdoer” route enough—via deployer duties, human-error symmetry, and the admission that attack-level lawful use (not autonomy alone) does the real work—while PRO’s clipped turns and interceptor exception left residual overbreadth unanswered. Net path to victory: ban what cannot be used lawfully; do not ban autonomy as such.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 (B4.5–B4.8): reframed accountability as reviewable deployment duties and used the interceptor/debris point to show PRO’s exception already licenses software attack-level judgments.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO won because CON never overcame the core argument that international humanitarian law requires human judgment about specific targets at specific moments. PRO showed that deployment-level accountability—certifying a class of systems and reviewing procurement—cannot substitute for answerability over the particular strike, and that under autonomy the absence of a wrongdoer becomes the structural standing condition of every killing rather than a rare, bounded tragedy. CON’s regulatory alternative was coherent but begged the question by assuming software could lawfully assess proportionality without meaningful human control, and conceded that certified systems could kill without a criminal wrongdoer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over per-attack versus deployment-level accountability, where PRO demonstrated that proportionality and distinction require judgment about the particular target and moment, while certification only evaluates against a test set—making procurement review a poor substitute for human responsibility over the specific killing.
