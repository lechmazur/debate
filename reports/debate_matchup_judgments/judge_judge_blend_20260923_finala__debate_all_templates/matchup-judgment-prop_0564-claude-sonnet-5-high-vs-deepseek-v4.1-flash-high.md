### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by making opportunity cost and permanence decisive. Side B established real equity, administrative, and boarding benefits, but never adequately compared fare elimination with using the same limited tax capacity for better service plus targeted waivers. Its repeated claim that cities could fund both relied more on political possibility than a demonstrated comparative advantage. Side A also answered the waiver-access pressure directly and explained why retaining partial agency-controlled revenue matters.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange exposed the weakness in Side B’s “fund both” response: broad taxation does not erase scarcity, so replacing fare revenue still competes with frequency and reliability. Side B’s answer that tax capacity is a political choice did not resolve that tradeoff, allowing Side A to characterize permanent fare abolition as an inflexible allocation rather than a costless equity gain.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO defended the motion on its own terms more effectively. Even conceding modest ridership, PRO established persistent equity, dignity, and boarding-efficiency gains that do not depend on headline ridership. CON’s strongest line was opportunity cost—waivers plus frequency beats free-but-infrequent service—but PRO successfully pressured the waiver alternative as retaining bureaucracy, stigma, and dwell-time delays, while noting fares cover only a minority of operating costs. CON never fully recovered from PRO’s documentation-barrier pressure on waivers, and the unanswered road-funding analogy weakened CON’s claim that broad-based taxation is uniquely unstable. CON’s permanence-as-trap argument had force, but PRO absorbed it by framing permanence as institutional credibility for a public right, which fit the motion’s wording better than CON’s flexibility frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 1 (B2.3) and Second Rebuttal (B4.1-B4.4) dismantling CON’s waiver alternative as preserving stigma, paperwork, and collection machinery. This forced CON into a defensive posture for the rest of the debate, and CON’s auto-enrollment response, while clever, never fully neutralized the dignity-and-friction gap between targeted waivers and universal free access.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON wins on opportunity-cost and reversibility. PRO never shows cities can fund both free rides for all and more frequency; its 'political decision' reply concedes the finite-pool worry. CON's auto-enrollment answer blunts the stigma point, while its control/permanence argument - fares as insulated, adjustable revenue and permanence as a trap - survives PRO's principle claim. The decisive comparison becomes free-40 vs more-frequent paid/low-fare, not free-40 vs $2.75-40.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2/A4 framing that the real trade is free infrequent service versus better-funded frequent service, plus exposing PRO's 'fund both' as assertion without evidence of tax capacity.
