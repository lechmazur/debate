### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by making the live metric clearer: accessible homes that actually get built and occupied beat perfect universal-design units that stall on the margin. PRO’s permanence/retrofit and visitability case was strong, but it depended on “modest” costs while also treating canceled projects as acceptable, and it overclaimed that CON’s concession of low-cost adaptability collapsed the whole CON case. CON kept a coherent middle path—visitability plus adaptability plus a meaningful accessible share—answered the aging-tenant pressure without surrendering supply, and left PRO without a clean limiting principle beyond blueprint idealism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2/closing double bind: if the features are truly cheap, targeted baseline already captures them; if not, PRO’s supply losses are real and decisive.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by collapsing CON's supply-doom argument using CON's own concessions. CON admitted that low-cost adaptability—wider framing, blocking, visitability—is desirable and inexpensive, then struggled to explain why mandating exactly those modest geometric baselines would paralyze construction. PRO sharpened this into a clean "permanence versus obsolescence" frame: housing stock lasts decades, retrofitting finished structures is often structurally impossible, and CON's targeted model forces displacement when residents age or become injured. CON's closing "either way we win" trick ignored that if the features are genuinely cheap, a targeted share still leaves most units inaccessible, undermining visitability and aging-in-place. PRO's rhetoric ("homes with expiration dates") made the comparative stake memorable, while CON's reference to constrained budgets, though intuitively appealing, was blunted by their inability to draw a clear cost line between their own endorsed baseline and PRO's mandate. A minor execution ding for PRO's invalid reference IDs in Rebuttal 2, and a slight format slip by B interposing prose between A1 and A2, were insufficient to change the outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal and closing, which weaponized CON's concessions on low-cost adaptability to undermine the claim that modest geometric standards would freeze housing supply, then pivoted to the "expiration date" frame showing CON's targeted units still force displacement.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing the central tradeoff: PRO's cheap geometric baselines are already conceded by CON's visitability + blocking baseline, so PRO's "stronger" standards must be expensive enough to threaten marginal projects. PRO's claim that CON surrendered collapses because CON distinguished cheap adaptability from gold-plating.
