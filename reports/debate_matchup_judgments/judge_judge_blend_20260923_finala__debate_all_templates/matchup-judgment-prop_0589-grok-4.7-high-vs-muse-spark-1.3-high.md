### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's primary limitation on police. Side B relied heavily on the claim that police cannot act without cooperative witnesses to the most recent shooting. Side A effectively countered this by pointing out that the concentrated network of shooters already carries illegal guns, open warrants, and parole conditions, allowing police to incapacitate them without needing new witness testimony.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized B's strongest mechanism by explaining that police do not need witnesses from the last shooting to remove a shooter, as they can use open warrants and parole conditions to incapacitate known actors.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won because it landed the sharper rebuttals on the decisive clash over immediacy and police power. A showed that officers do not need witness cooperation on the last shooting to act tonight; they can use existing warrants, probation and parole conditions, and illegal-gun seizures to remove persistent shooters before retaliation. PRO never recovered from this, repeating the discredited witness-dependency claim through the closing. A also kept pressure on the motion’s explicit scalability requirement: pilot successes or selected-site drops do not automatically scale, credible messengers face dilution and supervision problems, and cost-effectiveness requires a true effect size PRO could not show survives fair comparison at scale. PRO’s attempt to reframe the choice as keeping police and adding civilians, rather than the marginal tradeoff the motion states, was exposed and left its comparative routes weakened.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s response to PRO’s recurring claim that police require a cooperative witness and solved crime to incapacitate shooters before the next retaliation. CON demonstrated that officers already have alternative legal avenues—open warrants, parole conditions, illegal gun possession, and prior intelligence—to stop and remove likely shooters without relying on the victim or community cooperation on the last shooting. PRO repeated the original claim in later speeches but never engaged this counter, materially deflating its “police arrive too late” route to victory.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively dismantling Side B's core assumption that police are powerless to prevent retaliatory shootings without witness cooperation. A effectively argued that officers can use outstanding warrants, probation conditions, and illegal gun possession to incapacitate persistent shooters, whereas civilian interrupters rely entirely on voluntary persuasion, which fails when the shooter refuses. A also maintained a stricter, more accurate interpretation of the marginal cost-effectiveness comparison, proving that a cheaper intervention is not cost-effective if its effect size collapses when persuasion fails.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where A directly answered B's pressure question by explaining that police do not need witnesses to the last shooting to arrest a network member tonight, because these individuals already carry illegal guns and have open warrants or supervision conditions. This completely neutralized B's main offensive argument.
