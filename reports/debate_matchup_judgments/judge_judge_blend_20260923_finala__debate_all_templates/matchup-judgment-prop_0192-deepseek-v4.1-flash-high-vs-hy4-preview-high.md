### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but CON more effectively steered the clash onto the terrain that actually decides the motion: the legal mechanics of "personhood." CON's rescue-orphan and identical-enclosure thought experiments forced PRO to carve "narrow exceptions," which CON then used to show the right is really a custody/regulation scheme wearing a personhood label. CON's guardianship critique landed hardest: a non-human person cannot instruct counsel, discharge a guardian, or exercise the autonomy PRO's rhetoric promised, so "the animal holds the pen herself" collapses into "a guardian holds it instead of an owner" — CON's closing directly dismantled PRO's central closing image, which is the most decisive single exchange in the debate. PRO's "flip the baseline" and title/custody distinction were genuine, non-trivial responses, and the line-drawing exchange was closely fought (PRO's "protect the clear cases first" is a reasonable reply to CON's slippery-slope charge), but CON's alternative — direct statutory bans plus custody law — offered a concrete mechanism achieving the same protective goals without the definitional strain, undercutting the necessity of personhood itself. PRO never fully closed the gap on why personhood is more robust than statute, leaving CON's framing as the last word and the cleaner throughline across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's closing rebuttal directly refuting PRO's "the animal holds the pen herself / personhood is the lock on the door" image by pointing out a non-human person cannot instruct counsel, discharge a guardian, or exercise any of the autonomy claimed—exposing that guardianship merely relocates human control rather than eliminating it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing the live clash onto necessity and coherence: PRO’s own rescue/custody exceptions plus a criterion that also reaches pigs, parrots, and octopuses showed personhood does little work beyond what direct bans and custody rules already supply, while remaining a status the animal cannot exercise. PRO’s ownership-vs-wardship and baseline-flip replies were sharp but never escaped that guardianship still relocates human control, or that statutes can strip sale/breeding/separation without the three-species crown. CON made the vote easier by showing the motion overpromises and underdelivers relative to its own concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s identical-enclosure/motive attack and later statutory-sticks alternative: once PRO admitted narrow care exceptions and no non-arbitrary exclusion of other species, the “right” reduced to custody rules and bans CON could deliver without incoherent personhood.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by showing that PRO’s personhood proposal either collapsed into the very regulation it disdained or rested on an arbitrary charisma-based line. CON’s identical-enclosure thought experiment and “crown it cannot wear” framing demonstrated that guardianship still leaves a human holding the pen, while direct statutory bans could deliver the same anti-commodification protections without the conceptual inflation. PRO’s concession that there was no non-arbitrary way to cabin the “subjective life” test to apes, whales, and elephants weakened the principled basis for the motion, and the rescue-exception defense confirmed that the real work was being done by custody rules, not by personhood status.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 dismantled PRO’s claim that personhood secures unique protections by showing that the elephant’s subjective experience is identical in a sanctuary and a zoo if the physical conditions match; the only difference is human motive or legal title, which direct bans on sale, breeding, and separation can regulate without declaring the animal a person. The “crown it cannot wear” crystallization was the debate’s most damaging exchange.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON's opening dilemma (categorical right vs. welfare balancing) forced PRO into defining its standard as "necessary, least restrictive, serving the individual's interest" — which CON immediately and correctly characterized as "welfare balancing in robes." PRO's attempted escape (the default/burden shifts even if the mechanism looks similar) was a reasonable move but never resolved CON's sharpest unanswered points: who serves as guardian for a wild pod or migrating herd, and who adjudicates the "recognized interest" vs. "profit" line when courts and experts already bless captivity as conservation/education under current law. CON's closing crystallized this cleanly — personhood adds "a litigation layer, not a liberty baseline" — while PRO's closing reasserted the importance of defaults without directly rebutting that specific framing. CON also offered a coherent alternative (statutory presumption-flipping with species-specific rules) that neutralized PRO's "only personhood ends ownership" claim. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but CON's questions exposed deeper structural gaps that PRO's answers narrowed but did not close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that PRO's own necessity/least-restrictive standard "concedes" the dilemma and is "a welfare balancing test in robes, not a right against captivity" — a rebuttal PRO never fully escaped through the rest of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON built the clearer path by forcing PRO’s “liberty baseline” into an admission it is necessity/least-restrictive balancing, then showing that standard neither ends ownership nor supplies workable rules for wild pods, guardians, or “exploitation,” while statutes can flip the default without the label’s incoherence. PRO kept the moral and default-shift case live but never resolved who holds the status or how experts stop self-certifying, so CON’s comparative workability won the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal that PRO’s conceded necessity/least-restrictive test is welfare balancing in robes, dissolving the claimed categorical right against captivity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON showed that PRO's personhood standard collapses into the welfare balancing it claimed to replace, and that determinate statutory protections can flip the same default without imposing unworkable guardianship on diverse wild populations across borders. PRO's burden-shift argument kept the debate competitive, but never fully escaped the dilemma that necessity exceptions swallow the right, nor explained why personhood is more durable or administrable than species-specific legislation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "welfare balancing in robes" reframe, which demonstrated that PRO's necessity-and-least-restrictive standard was functionally indistinguishable from rigorous welfare law while adding unmanageable jurisdictional and metaphysical overhead.
