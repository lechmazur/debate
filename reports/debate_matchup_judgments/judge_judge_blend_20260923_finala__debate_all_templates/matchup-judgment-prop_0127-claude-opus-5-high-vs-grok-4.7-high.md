### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that donation or markdown remains less attractive than a full-price sale, so reducing the loss on surplus does not make over-ordering profitable. B also used the alternative discount limb to answer charity-capacity limits and plausibly framed assortment reduction as reducing the upstream waste A prioritized. A raised meaningful concerns about cannibalization and manipulated edibility standards, but did not establish that these effects would outweigh the food diverted from disposal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response that a smaller loss is not a reward most directly damaged A's central over-ordering mechanism. Pairing that with the discount option also prevented limited charity capacity from becoming a decisive objection.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing a fatal structural contradiction in Side B's case. B argued both that the mandate would cause supermarkets to over-order (growing the carbon footprint) and that it would cause them to aggressively shrink their assortments (reducing the footprint but hurting consumers). Side A cleanly caught this in A4 and A5, neutralizing B's core offensive mechanisms. Side A also effectively defended the discount limb as a viable, non-cannibalizing exit for the residual tail.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's point that the mandate cannot be simultaneously guilty of inflating surplus and shrinking the fresh assortment that creates it, which perfectly turned B's upstream emissions argument into a benefit.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): PRO retained the core residual-tail argument: salvage is still a loss so over-ordering is not rewarded, and the discount limb solves charity capacity. CON's memo-or-forced-cut dilemma was sharp but PRO's reply that a markdown steals no sale from the dumpster and that a memo codifies good practice left PRO with the cleaner live route to a smaller bin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's two-manager equilibrium in B4.5 - punishing the transparent logger while rewarding early-pull manufacture of spoilage - vs A's A4.3/A4.4 answer that early pull starts the duty and dropping waste-heavy lines cuts upstream cost.
