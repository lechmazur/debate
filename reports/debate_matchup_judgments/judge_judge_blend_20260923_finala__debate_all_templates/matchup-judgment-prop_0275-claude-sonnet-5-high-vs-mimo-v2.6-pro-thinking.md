### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a concrete status-quo harm—travelers cannot compare material fees or suitability rules before commitment—and showing that earlier disclosure directly reduces it. Side A offered creative objections, but its liability-laundering case wrongly treated visibility as legal ratification, while its pricing-distortion mechanism depended on unsupported assumptions about ranking and host behavior. A better all-in-price rule could complement the motion, but did not negate the benefits of upfront fee and rule disclosure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was its answer that disclosure neither legalizes discriminatory terms nor prevents challenges, while an all-in total addresses only price and leaves unsuitable house rules hidden. This exposed Side A’s preferred alternative as incomplete and its timestamp theory as overstated.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live, concrete harm — $89 listings becoming $340 after checkout and rules like age/no-refund bans discovered after commitment — while CON's two main objections collapsed. The laundering claim fails because illegal rules stay illegal and lawful harsh rules are plainly better avoided than discovered at the door; the folding claim had its causality reversed and PRO showed itemized early totals already solve it without mandating folding. PRO answered both pressure questions directly and showed total-price alone misses the house-rules half of the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2.2/B4.1 on laundering: early visibility doesn't ratify illegality and timestamp proves platform knowledge, shifting burden back to CON to explain why ignorance protects consumers better than disclosure for the lawful harsh terms it pivoted to.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B built a much stronger and more cohesive case, successfully reframing PRO's disclosure mandate as a liability shield for platforms rather than a consumer protection. B's economic argument about search-ranking incentives driving hosts to fold fixed fees into nightly rates was highly persuasive and largely unanswered. Most decisively, B exposed a fatal contradiction in A's case during the closing: A argued that late disclosure prevents meaningful consent due to sunk-cost psychology, but then claimed that early disclosure of harsh rules constitutes a "real choice." B capitalized on this to show that A's mandate merely creates "buyer beware with better paperwork," securing a clear victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing argument exposing Side A's contradiction on "meaningful consent"—pointing out that if the booking funnel prevents real choice at checkout due to friction and sunk costs, it equally prevents real choice at the listing page, making the early timestamp a legal shield for the platform rather than true empowerment for the traveler.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won by identifying that A initially attacked an imagined search-results mandate rather than disclosure immediately before checkout or a booking request. B then established a clear comparative: even an imperfect display gives travelers a chance to reject unusable or deceptively priced rentals before committing. A raised legitimate implementation and small-platform cost concerns, but overstated ambiguity and never showed why those costs outweighed the conceded benefit. A also mischaracterized B's permission to highlight rules as permission to withhold them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal was decisive: it corrected A's timing misread, used A's concession about voluntary total-price tools to support universal requirements, and showed that price toggles do not solve hidden occupancy, pet, or other usability rules.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO won the feasibility clash. B showed the mandate triggers after dates/guests are entered, dissolving CON's provisional-numbers and 50-card clutter attack, and established that even a 30-field dump before booking beats concealment after booking. CON conceded the core benefit for honest hosts but relied on market-will-fix and vague compliance-cost claims that B answered by noting data is already held and voluntary toggles are revocable and don't cover house rules. The house-rules usability harm went largely unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.2 timing correction: PRO reframed disclosure as post-dates, pre-checkout/request, collapsing CON's search-results over-disclosure scenario and turning CON's own market-evolution concession into an argument for permanence and universal coverage.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by strictly adhering to the text of the motion and effectively dismantling Side B's strawman arguments about search-result clutter. Side A's rebuttals, particularly in answering the pressure questions, were exceptionally sharp, framing the choice as between imperfect disclosure and total concealment. Side B relied too heavily on pedantic drafting objections and compliance cost asymmetries that failed to outweigh the core consumer protection benefits clearly articulated by Side A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Side B's pressure question about the 30-rule dump (A1 in Rebuttal 2), where Side A brilliantly argued that relocating the information to the decision point is the entire victory, as a cluttered disclosure is a UI problem while a hidden one is an ambush.
