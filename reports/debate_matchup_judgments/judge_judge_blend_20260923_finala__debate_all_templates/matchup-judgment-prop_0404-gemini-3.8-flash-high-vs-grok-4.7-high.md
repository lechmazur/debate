### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): The decisive clash was whether the civil gender marker is an objective biological baseline or an administratively revisable legal category. CON's entire case depended on the former, but PRO's opening and Rebuttal 1 pointed out that states already amend M-to-F and F-to-M through legal process rather than chromosome testing, which undercuts CON's "objective verification" framing. CON's response—asserting that binary transitions require clinical diagnosis and hormonal therapy while X requires nothing—was assertive but never squarely refuted PRO's core analogy, and it introduced somewhat brittle claims about universal medical requirements for gender-marker change that PRO's "checkbox was never a medical examination" line handled well. On the institutional-dilemma front (prisons, shelters), PRO's answer that staff already use non-invasive, statute-based placement rules rather than treating any marker as an anatomical certificate was a reasonable, if somewhat general, response; CON's rebuttal that this "concedes zero operational direction" landed some damage but proved equally true of revisable M/F markers, so it didn't cleanly favor CON. Both sides executed the Q&A format properly with labeled A1/A2 answers, but PRO's answers more directly dismantled CON's foundational premise, while CON's closing mostly reasserted earlier claims without resolving PRO's precedent-based rebuttal. This makes PRO's path to victory clearer and less contested by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 point that M/F markers are already administratively revisable without a biological assay [B2.1], repeated and sharpened through the Pressure Questions exchange, is the most damaging blow: it directly undercuts CON's central premise that the document field is an objective biological baseline, and CON never fully neutralized it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by exposing an unresolved asymmetry in CON's case and dismantling the claim that a third marker uniquely creates institutional chaos. CON argued that official documents require objective, biological baselines and that an X marker would force sex-segregated facilities into invasive inspections or abandon protections. PRO successfully showed that states already amend binary markers administratively without chromosomal tests, demonstrating the field records legal gender rather than biological anatomy. When CON was pressed to identify the "observable test" that makes binary transitions objective while a third category is pure subjectivity, CON could only cite medical transition steps that PRO had already shown are not universal and are administered legally, not biologically. PRO's sharpest turn—"Binary amendments already let checkbox and body diverge. Policy closes that gap; X does not open it"—deflated CON's institutional safety argument by showing that frontline facilities already handle revised markers without defaulting to strip searches. CON's late speeches also mischaracterized PRO's explicit answer that an X would use the same legal gatekeeping (clinician letters, durability requirements) as existing transitions, falsely claiming PRO "conceded" the marker was a blank check. Because PRO's rebuttals directly neutralized CON's routes to victory while CON failed to answer the core asymmetry problem, PRO built the stronger path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B4 answer to CON's pressure questions, particularly the argument that binary amendments already allow the marker and body to diverge, so X does not uniquely create institutional problems—"Policy closes that gap; X does not open it"—undercut CON's central safety and objectivity claims.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully dismantling Side B's foundational premise that identity documents function as strict biological assays. A convincingly argued that the gender marker is a legal administrative record, pointing out that binary transitions already occur without chromosome tests at the DMV. When B pressed on the impact on sex-segregated spaces, A effectively neutralized the threat by explaining that facilities already rely on comprehensive placement rules and non-invasive inquiries for transgender individuals, meaning an "X" marker does not suddenly mandate invasive strip searches. A's direct and grounded answers to the pressure questions solidified their advantage, while B's reliance on an idealized, purely biological standard for identification crumbled under A's administrative reality checks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically the A1 and A2 answers to the pressure questions, where A decisively explained that frontline staff use existing, non-invasive placement rules for "X" markers just as they do for administratively changed "M" or "F" markers, completely deflating B's claim that a third marker necessitates invasive anatomical exams.
