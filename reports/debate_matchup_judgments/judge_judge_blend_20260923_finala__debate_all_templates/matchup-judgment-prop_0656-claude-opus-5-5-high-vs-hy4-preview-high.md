### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A decisively won the mechanistic clash by proving that Side B's proposed safeguards (velocity checks, per-wallet caps) are incompatible with absolute anonymity and easily circumvented by creating infinite anonymous wallets. Side A effectively demonstrated that removing the physical friction of cash while retaining its anonymity creates unbounded digital crime, outweighing Side B's privacy concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal exposing that anonymous wallets cannot enforce per-user caps, and that criminals receive (rather than traceably load) funds, dismantling Side B's technical defenses.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a structural tension PRO could not resolve: truly anonymous, unlinkable digital transfers below a threshold cannot simultaneously support the anti-structuring and anti-fraud safeguards PRO invoked. CON showed that digital anonymity strips the natural physical limits of cash, enabling remote, scalable extortion and laundering that cash makes costly. PROs admission that no one can name an anonymous recipient from sub-threshold payments became a live concession CON leveraged across rebuttals and closing. While PROs architectural-privacy framing had normative force, CONs alternative of siloed identity, warrant gates, and short retention offered a credible middle ground that preserved investigative recourse without endorsing total surveillance, and CON did sharper comparative weighing to show the motion guaranteed harms that their model merely risked. Multiple clipped speeches from B are an execution cost, but the visible content remained robust enough to carry the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The deciding exchange was CONs structuring dilemma (B2.1-B2.3 / B3.1-B3.2), which forced PRO to choose between unlinkable anonymity and detectable pattern analysis. PRO responded with local device caps and traceable conversion points, but CONs follow-up demonstrated that victims provide the identified funding, criminals never need exit, and free anonymous wallet creation lets abuse scale infinitely. This collapsed PROs technical defense and proved that digital anonymity below a threshold enables systemic abuse that cash physics naturally constrains.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's practical mechanisms for preventing crime under digital anonymity. Side B's pressure questions and rebuttals exposed a fatal contradiction in Side A's case: Side A's reliance on velocity checks and local device caps to prevent structuring either requires linkability (abandoning the motion's anonymity requirement) or relies on the criminal's own device to enforce limits. Furthermore, Side B effectively highlighted that digital anonymity removes the physical constraints of cash, enabling scalable, remote extortion without the need for the criminal to ever "exit" the system. While Side A presented a strong philosophical case for architectural privacy over administrative silos, Side B proved that Side A's implementation of the motion guarantees severe, untraceable harm at scale, making Side B's pseudonymous, warrant-gated alternative the more viable path. Side B suffered minor execution costs due to speech clippings at the end of several turns, but the substantive clash was won clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's structuring defense in Rebuttal 2, pointing out that "identified loads" belong to the victims, not the criminal, and that a "rolling cap" on the criminal's device is enforced by the criminal themselves.
