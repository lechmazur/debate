### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A won a clear but competitive debate. It showed that standardized fields can disclose work-level viewing, payment models, and upstream allocations without imposing one payout formula or revealing every confidential deal. This substantially weakened B's lock-in and heterogeneity objections. B persuasively argued that self-reported files cannot replace audits, but A did not need to eliminate audits: regular comparable disclosure can make audits, contract checks, and bargaining more effective. B never established that the remaining compliance costs outweighed that benefit, and repeated clipping also imposed an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 most effectively answered B's confidentiality and incompatible-model dilemma: the platform can report the amount allocated upstream for a work while leaving downstream label splits and rival rate cards undisclosed, and flat-fee services can share standardized audience fields without pretending their payouts are per-stream.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's counter-proposal (audit rights) and resolving the practical dilemmas B posed. A's explanation that the file provides the necessary "upstream sum" for an artist to check their label's math neutralized B's strongest argument about industry intermediaries. Side B struggled with word economy, clipping in three speeches, and ultimately talked past A's mechanism by insisting A was conceding to audits, when A was clearly differentiating between routine upfront verification and expensive back-end legal discovery.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4) directly resolving the "confidential rates vs unmatched figures" catch-22 by explaining work-level upstream reporting, followed by A5's crystallization: "A split checked against folklore is not a check."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by clearly distinguishing between standardizing reporting fields and standardizing business models, effectively neutralizing the lock-in critique. Side A also provides a compelling answer to the middleman problem by showing how upstream transparency prevents label fraud. Side B makes strong points about audits and verification but suffers execution costs from multiple clipped turns and fails to fully overcome Side A's epistemic point that audits require a standardized object to audit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it cleanly answers the pressure questions by explaining that the file reports the upstream sum paid for the work, allowing the artist to check the label's split against a known figure rather than folklore, effectively defusing the middleman trap.
