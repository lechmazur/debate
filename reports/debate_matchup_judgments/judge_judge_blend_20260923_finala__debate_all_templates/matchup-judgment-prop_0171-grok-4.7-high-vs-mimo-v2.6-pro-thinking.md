- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): The pivotal exchange was B's Rebuttal 1 concession that challenge data "must not be read as field protection" and cannot certify elderly, immunocompromised, or waning-immunity outcomes. B's Rebuttal 2 answered the pressure questions cleanly with proper A1:/A2: formatting and offered a workable institutional mechanism (independent designation, pre-registered use restrictions), which is a real strength, but A's closing dichotomy — "if the gates are strict, 'more broadly' is empty; if loosened, the ethical bargain collapses" — landed as the last word and was never directly rebutted, since B's closing preceded it and relied on an under-defended claim that a published default matters even if application frequency doesn't change. A's case suffered two clipped speeches (opening and Rebuttal 2), a real execution cost, but the substantive content of both was largely delivered before the cut, limiting the damage. On balance, A built the clearer, more damaging rebuttal chain and closed with the sharper unresolved tension.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 response to B's concession that challenge results "must not be read as field protection" — turning PRO's own limiting admission into proof that the value of broader permission is narrower than the certain harm it requires — was the most decisive single exchange, and it structured the rest of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won by maintaining extreme discipline regarding the exact purpose of challenge studies (acting as an early discriminator to kill bad candidates before field trials) and by trapping Side B on a factual error. - **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A built a stronger, more cohesive case for why controlled infection studies are necessary and clearly defined "more broadly" as establishing a default regulatory pathway rather than loosening safety gates. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON (A) built a coherent bright-line distinction between deliberately causing disease and merely permitting research amid naturally circulating pathogens, and pressed a genuine dilemma that PRO never fully resolved: criteria strict enough to satisfy the safety conditions PRO proposed (characterized strain, dependable rescue, no transmission risk) already describe cases handled under current bespoke permission, so "broader" permission changes nothing for the safe cases, while the emerging, poorly-characterized pathogens that motivate the proposition necessarily fail those same criteria. PRO's answer (B4.1-B4.2) — that the obstacle is merely institutional inertia, not substance — gestured at a standardization defense but never developed it into a clear account of what new territory broader permission actually unlocks without loosening ethical criteria, and this gap was reasserted sharply in A4.5-A4.9 without an adequate PRO reply in the closing. PRO's rhetorically sharp closing move (turning CON's honest "no" answer to Q2 into "a prohibition wearing an objection's clothes") is a clever framing but doesn't refute CON's substantive negation of the resolution, since arguing the objections are dispositive is a legitimate way to answer "should regulators permit... even though objections are serious." PRO also suffered two clipped speeches (opening and rebuttal 2), a real execution cost per the judging rules, while CON's speeches were all fully delivered and consistently exploited the unresolved dilemma. Overall CON gave the cleaner, more resolved path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over the regulatory "gate": CON's dilemma in [A2.4]/[A4.5-A4.9] that strict safety criteria unlock nothing new (already handled case-by-case) while loose criteria compromise the very objections the motion concedes, versus PRO's [B4.1]/[B4.2] claim that broader permission is really about standardizing an ad hoc pathway — a response that was asserted but never fully closed the gap CON identified.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a clear comparative framework and successfully forcing Side B to defend an absolutist position.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Turn 7, where Side A uses concrete examples of pathogens (shigella, norovirus, RSV) to break Side B's dilemma that strict safety criteria would exclude all the cases the proposition is meant to address.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A built a highly persuasive comparative case, effectively arguing that the alternative to controlled human infection studies is not zero harm, but rather uncontrolled, unrescued infection in large field trials. Side A's pressure questions successfully forced Side B into an absolute prohibition stance regarding the motion's specific condition (inadequate animal models). Side A brilliantly exploited this in the closing, highlighting the real-world costs of a rigid deontological constraint. Side B's philosophical distinction between causing and foreseeing harm was coherent but practically weak against Side A's regulatory framework.
