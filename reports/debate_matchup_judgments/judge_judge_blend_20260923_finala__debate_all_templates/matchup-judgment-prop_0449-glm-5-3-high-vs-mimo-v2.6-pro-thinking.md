### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing judgment of the motion as written: one identical 2040 date plus mandatory penalties, with assistance and differentiation only permitted, not required. PRO’s best points (residual state-backed coal, free-riding under pure voluntarism) never neutralized the incidence/debt-trap and stick-over-carrot asymmetry. CON’s ozone concession trap and clearer comparative weighing made the clash easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R1 (A2.1–A2.2): PRO’s ozone concession that sanctions worked with assistance and differentiated timelines convicts the undifferentiating, assistance-free motion PRO actually had to defend.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON wins by tethering PRO to the actual text of the motion. PRO defended a regime that supposedly permits differentiation and assistance, but CON showed that the motion mandates a single 2040 date and penalties while only permitting support, creating a catch-22 where capital-poor countries lose the finance they need to transition. PRO never fully answered the incidence problem—penalties on coal pass through to electricity and industrial costs in developing economies—or the "debt trap" logic that missing the deadline costs a country the capital required to meet it. While PRO landed effective points about the limits of voluntarism, CON's textual fidelity, the "enforcement plan that hopes a transition shows up" frame, and the sustained pressure on economic incidence provided the cleaner reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dismantling of PRO's claim that "penalties fall on coal, not the poor" by demonstrating the economic incidence passes through to households, workers, and industrial prices, paired with the "debt trap with a climate alibi" argument that finance penalties strip capital from the very countries that need it to build alternatives.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by strictly holding Side B to the text of the motion. Side B spent much of the debate defending a version of the policy that included differentiated timelines and transition assistance, which Side A correctly pointed out were absent from the proposition. Side A's framing of the motion as "an enforcement plan that hopes a transition shows up" perfectly crystallized the asymmetry of mandating penalties while only permitting assistance, making it the clearer and more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's claim that "sixteen years is already the differentiation" and exposed that Side B was defending a hypothetical, better-designed treaty rather than the punitive one actually on the table.
