### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a close, high-quality debate by showing that abolishing the veto would not remove great-power immunity, which rests on material power, but could sacrifice cooperation on peacekeeping, sanctions, and diplomacy. Side B compellingly exposed the veto's moral inconsistency and paralysis, yet did not adequately establish that Council mandates would remain enforceable after major-power withdrawal or widespread noncompliance. Side A's two clipped turns modestly reduced its margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest answer separated power over the target from power over third states: a resolution may be unable to stop a nuclear power while still forcing other countries to choose blocs. This directly answered Side B's toothless-versus-powerful dilemma and made the costs of abolition concrete.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A won a close, high-clash debate by clearer comparative weighing: the veto’s only marginal effect is shielding great-power cases, working functions do not require it, and B’s “keep the table” metric treats immunity as membership. A’s pressure questions forced B’s toothless-vs-powerful tension into the open; A then crystallized that Chapter VII cannot be legitimate against small aggressors and illegitimate against large ones without becoming a theory of rank. B’s consent/circuit-breaker case remained coherent and realist, but it never fully escaped special pleading or showed why preserving chairs that go empty precisely when they matter outweighs restoring the Council’s core purpose under the motion’s explicit withdrawal risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 synthesis (A4.5–A4.6) pairing B’s two pressures: if Council acts are toothless the walkout threat is empty; if they bite the accused should not hold the switch—then closing’s identical-instrument/“theory of rank” frame.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): CON's live path survived: abolition does not make nuclear powers obey (PRO conceded votes didn't stop Iraq/Ukraine), but it converts voluntary outside coalitions into binding duties that split the world, while risking the only forum where rivals still bargain. PRO's fire-department case was strong on paralysis, but its answers that peacekeeping/NK sanctions don't need P5 and that defiance just means isolation undercut its own claim that Council decisions matter, and CON's distinction — toothless against the target, powerful against bystanders — resolved the alleged contradiction and turned the withdrawal threat real.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.4 resolving B's toothless vs world-splitting dilemma with the “gun that only hits the neutral middle” — Council can't stop a P5's tanks but can legally force third parties to pick sides, so abolition buys condemnation without compliance while destroying consent-based authority.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): B won a close, high-quality debate by showing that abolition need not enable war against nuclear powers to create substantial gains: it can permit aid, accountability, and coordinated legal pressure against their protected clients. A persuasively established the costs of withdrawal, especially lost sanctions compliance, funding, and logistics, but often treated enforcement as impossible without the departing patron even when many other states and institutions could still act. B therefore preserved a clearer net benefit, despite the execution cost of clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Syria response was decisive: it broke A’s claim that the Council already acts whenever a great power is not itself the aggressor, then distinguished Russia’s military deterrence from Assad’s much broader veto-created immunity. A answered strongly with examples of cooperation obtained through Russian consent, but did not fully neutralize B’s point that abolition would let the rest of the international system act beyond areas Russia physically controls.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON built the cleaner decisive path: great-power consent is the price of real enforcement capacity, so abolition even under withdrawal risk trades workable action for paper Chapter VII. PRO landed a strong Syria/client-state attack and forced useful concessions on lesser reforms, but never fully neutralized the “who still enforces after the trucks leave” burden after conceding deterrence and mocking GA resolutions. League “keep the loot” and sanctions-binding-only-while-inside comparisons kept tipping the scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.6–B2.8: PRO’s own nuclear-deterrence and “microphone” concessions are turned into the claim that abolition only buys an unenforceable binding stamp, later hardened on Syria in B4.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the core enforcement comparison. PRO's strong client-protection critique (Syria: aid mandate, ICC referral, CW attribution) was met by CON's decisive concession trap: B admitted nuclear deterrence, not procedure, protects Russia [B2.6], making a Chapter VII over a P5 objection the same unenforceable paper as the GA "microphone with power cut" [B2.9]. CON also exploited B1.5 vs B2.2 tension on withdrawals and showed leaky compliance with P5 inside is not equal to no legal obligation after exit - Russian banks bound on North Korea only because Russia voted for them and remains bound. That narrowed the vote to real enforcement in many cases vs symbolic enforcement against clients who still enjoy Russian jets on the ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.6-A2.8 / A4.1-A4.4: turning PRO's nuclear-deterrence concession into proof abolition yields paper against P5, plus the Syria ledger (chemical stockpile removed in 2013-14 and aid crossings existed only because Russia voted for them) to show one crossing inside vs zero with Russian jets still guaranteeing Assad's physical immunity outside.
