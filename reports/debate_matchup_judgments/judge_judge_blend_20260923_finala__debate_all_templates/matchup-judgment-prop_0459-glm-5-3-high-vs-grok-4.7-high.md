### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): B won narrowly because A's proposed committed volume and cost-gap top-up largely conceded that public subsidy and guaranteed demand are needed to create alternative capacity. A compellingly exposed risks of capture, permanent support, and slower deployment, but often attacked a permanent, market-wide nationality wall broader than B's limited bridge for critical capacity. B therefore retained the cleaner mechanism linking policy to bankable diversification.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing use of A's own top-up proposal was decisive: it showed that A accepted the core industrial-policy mechanism while disputing its geographic conditions. A's bankability-versus-sunset response was sharp, but did not establish that a finite contract cannot finance capacity and later expire.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it successfully undermined CON's central alternative. CON argued for diversified global supply via allied offtake and stocks, but PRO showed that an offtake allowing buyers to defect to the incumbent's cheaper prices is not a bankable commitment, leaving CON with no credible mechanism to build the alternative capacity it promised. While CON landed a sharp theoretical blow on PRO by exposing a tension between bankability and temporariness, PRO had the stronger empirical track record and kept the core clash focused on whether CON's counter-strategy could actually exist. PRO's historical example of losing solar manufacturing under open-market conditions and its closing crystallization that CON essentially conceded the need for committed public money gave PRO the cleaner comparative victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's collapse of CON's allied-offtake alternative into either unbankable wishful thinking or disguised industrial policy (B2.1, B4.1, B5.2), which forced CON to defend a mechanism that could not survive open-price competition against China's state-built scale.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO won the central mechanism clash. CON wanted diversification without national fencing via "allied offtake buying cheapest qualified cell" but PRO's pressure Q1 exposed that no second plant is bankable if buyers can defect to incumbent state-built scale. CON's answer - a committed volume + declining top-up for producers outside the choke - conceded the need for industrial policy, allowing PRO to reframe the debate as bounded premium vs unbounded choke. The turbine-at-premium still decarbonizes vs cancelled turbine decarbonizes nothing asymmetry was never squarely answered, giving PRO the cleaner path to the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B4.1-B5.2 chain: turning [A1.2] "several suppliers" into requiring the very industrial policy CON opposes, then using CON's own [A4.1] top-up proposal as a concession in closing.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by defending targeted localization of genuinely critical links rather than the autarkic duplication Side A repeatedly attributed to the motion. B showed that stockpiles, contracts, and friend-shoring cannot create missing factories without some form of subsidy, financing, or guaranteed demand, while competitive procurement, legal imports, triggers, and sunsets directly addressed patronage concerns. A compellingly weighed deployment costs and fiscal opportunity costs, but overstated the linear link between price premiums and slower decarbonization and never fully explained why coordinated second-source investment was categorically different from the industrial policy it opposed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it used the word “critical” to answer the alleged retreat in scope, then exposed that Side A's preferred second-source and friend-shoring strategy still required financing and bankable demand. Side A's response relocated the investment abroad but did not eliminate B's underlying industrial-policy mechanism or guarantee control during a supply shock.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins because it successfully framed the motion as an inefficient blanket mandate that PRO gradually narrowed into a far more limited policy, and because A’s political-economy critique—that sheltered domestic firms require elaborate safeguards precisely because they lack competitive discipline—remains live after the exchange. A also crystallized the decisive comparison: both sides want second sources, but the motion forces each country to overpay for domestic duplication rather than achieving resilient diversification through coordinated global investment. B’s clipped first rebuttal and inability to fully reverse the narrowing compounded the damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first rebuttal (A2), which identified the learning-curve contradiction in protected markets, branded PRO’s shift to narrow links as a quiet retreat, and showed that real-world export controls have been partial and reroutable rather than the total embargoes PRO’s model assumes.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live route after full exchange: zero-shipment dominates a smooth price premium, and friend-shoring assumes plants that themselves require subsidies and bankable orders. CON's cost-per-tonne and patronage risks are real but PRO answered from the opening that "critical" means choke points (processing, cells, magnets) not autarky, with competitive procurement, cost triggers, sunsets, and legal imports to force learning. CON's second rebuttal concedes a second source must be financed somewhere, which validates industrial policy as a tool, and its rare-embargo claim was weakened by PRO's duty vs stop distinction and the point that partial controls still block without a second plant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A2 shift from price-stop to tariff-stop plus competitive procurement / triggers / sunsets as discipline mechanism that directly answered A3.2's sheltered-incumbent pressure.
