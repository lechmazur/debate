### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by showing that Side A effectively demanded certainty rather than a safety threshold. B offered a plausible regulated evidence ladder, explained why residual risk exists in all irreversible medicine, and weighed that risk against the serious and sometimes unavoidable harm of inherited disease. A identified genuine uncertainty and consent concerns, but repeatedly asserted that relevant harms can only be known in later human generations without adequately grounding that claim or explaining why no pre-birth evidence could ever suffice. A also overstated the motion as creating an international entitlement or industry rather than conditional permission under rules.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions directly, argued that A’s proposed threshold was functionally an unfalsifiable veto, and showed that difficult boundary cases do not erase clear cases such as Tay-Sachs. B’s final response that prohibition also imposes irreversible consequences on children sharpened the comparative weighing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively weaponizing the wording of the resolution to trap Side B, arguing that if B is right about safety thresholds being impossible to meet, the motion's condition is never satisfied and thus harmless. Side A also successfully turned B's 'irreversibility' standard by pointing out that a ban guarantees the irreversible inheritance of fatal diseases.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly flipped Side B's standard, demonstrating that a ban does not avoid irreversible multi-generational outcomes, but rather chooses them with 100% certainty in the form of uncorrected disease alleles.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO won the safety-threshold clash. By framing permission as conditional, B forced CON to either admit the threshold could be met (then a ban blocks a proven cure for families where PGD fails) or insist it can never be met (then the motion triggers no edits and CON's lineage-catastrophe disappears). A's "falsified too late" and scale arguments were plausible but demanded multigenerational human proof no prior advance has met, while B's ladder — animal lineages, genome screening, licensed first cases with registries — plus the UK mitochondrial precedent and the uncorrected allele replicating forever, gave a cleaner, more reversible failure mode. B answered Q2 on line-drawing and exposed the unfalsifiable standard from Q1.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2/B4/B5 dilemma: if safety is impossible the motion is harmless, if possible the ban is a permanent veto — turning A's strongest point ("trigger never fires" A4.2) into a concession that neutralizes CON's harm case.
