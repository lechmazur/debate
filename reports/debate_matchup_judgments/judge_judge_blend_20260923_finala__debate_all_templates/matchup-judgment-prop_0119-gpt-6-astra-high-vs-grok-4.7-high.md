### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): The decisive clash was whether A's proposal actually delivers what it promises: a wage that reliably replaces tip income. B's sharpest and most sustained line of attack was the "pass-through problem" — a service charge is house revenue, not a wage, and nothing in the motion pegs the new wage to the tips it displaces or to hours actually offered. A's answer to this (repeated across rebuttals) was largely definitional: "a restaurant that doesn't fund the wage hasn't implemented the proposal," which restates the aspiration rather than showing a mechanism that forces competitive, matched pay. A conceded directly (A4.1, A4.5) that the motion guarantees neither prior earnings nor scheduling, which let B close on "an obligation smaller than the claim it deletes is not the stronger protection" — a clean, damaging line B repeated effectively into the closing. A's best moment was flipping B's blunt answer to Q2 ("None on that check") to show tipping doesn't reliably reward good service on an individual level either; this was rhetorically sharp and genuinely damaged B's "sanction" framing. But B's "portfolio" defense (aggregate room-level tips still function as a real, same-night, worker-owned claim) survived that hit reasonably well, and B's anchoring/demand argument about menu pricing was never fully answered beyond "wider adoption fixes it," which doesn't address B's "outside option" rebuttal (eating at home, rival non-adopters during transition). B's rebuttal 1 was clipped by the word-limit format note, a real execution cost, but B's later speeches (Pressure Questions, Rebuttal 2, Closing) recovered and sharpened the same points without needing the cut material, limiting the damage. On balance, B did more to expose an unresolved structural gap in A's case and better crystallized the final choice for the reader, giving it a narrow but real edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over the pass-through problem — B's Pressure Q1 ("what rule makes the service charge fund a wage matching what strong servers already earn... instead of a rate the house posts and can offset by cutting shifts?") and A's answer (A4.1, conceding no guarantee of matching earnings or protected scheduling) — was the most decisive, since A never closed that gap and B repeatedly returned to it through Rebuttal 2 and Closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully proving that the proposition replaces a worker-owned revenue stream (tips) with house-controlled revenue (service charges) without guaranteeing matching wages. Side A capitalized effectively on Side B's concessions regarding lost upside and shift vulnerability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R2 and Closing brilliantly neutralized Side B's attack on stiffing, explaining that a single zero-tip table is offset by the rest of the room's tips, which forms a safer "portfolio" than a fully employer-controlled wage.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully attacking the practical mechanisms of the proposition, specifically the "pass-through" vulnerability of service charges and the reality of menu-price anchoring. While Side B argued normatively that employers should bear payroll responsibility and relied on the proposition's requirement for higher base pay, Side A effectively pointed out that this merely restates the wish without preventing owners from capturing the service charge as house revenue or cutting shifts to offset costs. Side B's eventual concession that the new model cannot guarantee previous earnings for strong servers allowed Side A to decisively weigh the immediate, worker-owned "portfolio" of tips against a potentially lower, fully employer-controlled wage. Despite a minor clipping issue in Rebuttal 1 and a risky "none on that check" answer during pressure, Side A's zoom-out to the whole room's tips was logically sound and rhetorically effective.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantled Side B's "obligation" framing by highlighting that the proposition guarantees neither previous earnings nor schedules, leaving workers with a rate the house posts and potentially fewer hours due to sticker shock.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more systemic case: tipping is not just imprecise but structurally biased and unpredictable, and it embeds a coercive dynamic (harassment tolerance, guilt pricing) that B never fully dismantled—B mostly conceded these harms exist and offered generic "enforce protections" fixes rather than showing how disclosure or a floor removes the underlying incentive problem. A's pressure-question dilemma (floor secures pay vs. doesn't) forced B into a defensive posture, and A's answers (A1/A2) were direct and on-format. B's strongest counters—total compensation uncertainty, worker choice, and the "valuable but not necessary" reframe of tips—are real and not fully answered (A's closing repeated the dilemma without engaging that reframe, a genuine weak spot), but B's own retreat to "restaurants whose workers prefer tips can keep them" softened its opposition to the motion as stated. On balance A's rebuttals did more damage to B's live routes than B's did to A's, giving A a clear but not dominant win. A's clipped opening is a real execution cost, capped in scoring, but did not gut the core case already delivered before the cutoff.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure-question dilemma ("either the floor secures the paycheck and the tip is a second collection, or it doesn't and hidden costs remain") in Turn 5, and A's direct A1/A2 answers in Turn 7, put B on the back foot; B's best counter-punch was B4.5's "valuable but not necessary" reframe, which A never squarely answered in closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully separating the need for a secure wage floor from the necessity of abolishing tipping. Side A's responses to the Pressure Questions cleanly neutralized Side B's attacks on hours and income stability, while Side A effectively exposed that Side B's proposal would flatten earnings and remove an upside that workers value.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 response flawlessly parried Side B's question about hours by pointing out that neither system guarantees weekly income when shifts are cut, maintaining parity on stability while preserving A's advantage on total compensation.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully framing tipping not as a valuable "upside" bonus, but as the actual wage bill outsourced to customer guilt and bias. Side B's dilemma—either the base floor secures a living (making tips a biased, guilt-driven surplus) or it doesn't (leaving hidden labor costs intact)—effectively trapped Side A. Side A defended the "upside" of tipping but conceded that this upside relies on customer generosity, failing to neutralize Side B's critiques of discrimination and social pressure. Side B's rhetorical framing ("the menu price is a fiction," "payroll cannot be a gift") was sharper, and its answers to the pressure questions decisively dismantled Side A's hybrid model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's rebuttal to the "loss of upside" and "cut hours" arguments. Side B effectively argued that an included service charge scales with sales just like tips, neutralizing the claim that higher base pay uniquely risks cut hours, and reframed the "upside" as merely the employer refusing to price labor into the menu, making the loss of tip volatility a feature of reform rather than a bug.
