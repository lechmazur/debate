- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON (A) built a clean, adaptive case: originality doctrine already screens out machine-only work, and the proposed documentation mandate creates a "compliance trap" that disadvantages under-resourced individual creators (who lose drafts/logs) while advantaging well-resourced generators/stock agencies who can instrument every keystroke — an inversion of PRO's own stated worry. CON's pressure questions (does the "look at the picture" standard survive when PRO later relies on the final chapter as sufficient evidence? is the record actually "provable" without model auditing?) exposed a real tension in PRO's position. Compounding this, PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing were both flagged as clipped for exceeding word limits, an explicit execution cost that cut off PRO's final crystallization mid-argument, while CON's closing landed cleanly on the exploited concession. Overall CON narrowed the debate more effectively and answered pressure with a sharper, more damaging comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Questions (A3.1/A3.2) forced PRO to admit in Rebuttal 2 that a surviving final work plus testimony can itself serve as "documentation," which CON's Closing then used to argue the proposition adds nothing beyond ordinary originality doctrine except a punitive formality for creators who lack preserved logs.

B forced A to concede that a final work could serve as its own documentation if "plainly human," which effectively collapsed A's rule into the ordinary originality standard for strong cases and left it as a punitive paperwork trap for creators who lose drafts. B also landed a decisive blow by pointing out that user logs do not actually solve the AI black box problem, since they omit the model's internal choices. Side A's arguments were solid but suffered from damaging concessions and clipped speeches in the later rounds, giving B a clear and decisive edge in crystallizing the clash.
