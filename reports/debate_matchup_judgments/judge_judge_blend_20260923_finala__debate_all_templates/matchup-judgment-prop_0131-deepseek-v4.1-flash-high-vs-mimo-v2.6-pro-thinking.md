### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a clear causal story (unpaid care as an invisible subsidy that is failing structurally) and then used the exchange effectively: it turned CON's "personal budget" alternative into a concession that the real dispute is generosity of payment, not mechanism, and it flipped CON's "delay" worry by pointing out that free labor, not paid labor, is the status quo's actual excuse for underinvestment. CON's structural critiques (two-tier access, fiscal openendedness, supervision) were substantive and well-organized, but CON never fully closed the loop on why a personal budget that hires a relative isn't simply "paying family caregivers more generously" under another name—this definitional move looked more like rebranding than refutation, and PRO's closing exploited that directly. CON's pressure questions were sharp and PRO's answers (A1/A2) were format-compliant and substantive, but they didn't fully neutralize PRO's core reframe. PRO's case suffered a real execution cost: three separate speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits, losing potential content (including at least one full point in rebuttal 1 and the close of rebuttal 2), which is a legitimate ding against polish and discipline. Still, on the core clash—whether paying caregivers more is a live, coherent alternative to the status quo, or is secretly identical to CON's preferred architecture—PRO's framing proved more resilient after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing move pointing out that CON's own concession (a personal budget can hire a relative) collapses CON's "architecture vs. kinship" distinction into a dispute about generosity of pay—exactly the motion's question—while CON's reply (insisting this makes it "not a caregiver wage") never fully answered why that relabeling defeats the proposition as written.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won by defending the motion's actual comparison: compensated family care versus continued reliance on unpaid labor. B showed that personal budgets and formal backup can coexist with generous family-caregiver compensation, while burnout, family luck, weak supervision, and capacity delay already exist more severely under unpaid care. B's repeated clipping was an execution cost, but its visible case and closing still resolved the central clash more convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to the backup-cost challenge was decisive: both paid and unpaid caregivers can collapse, so formal backup is a shared cost rather than a unique disadvantage of payment. B then argued that compensation preserves more care hours and reduces burnout, while A never established why leaving the same labor unpaid would build formal capacity faster.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully grounding the debate in the actual comparison posed by the motion—paying family caregivers versus relying on unpaid labor—while exposing Side B's reliance on an idealized, fully-funded public care system that does not currently exist. Side A suffered some word-limit clipping, but the core arguments were fully delivered and highly persuasive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "fiscal trap/delay" argument. Side A brilliantly flipped this by arguing that unpaid family labor is the ultimate excuse for delay because it renders the crisis invisible and costs the treasury nothing, whereas paying generous wages forces the true cost of care onto the public books, increasing the pressure to build formal capacity.
