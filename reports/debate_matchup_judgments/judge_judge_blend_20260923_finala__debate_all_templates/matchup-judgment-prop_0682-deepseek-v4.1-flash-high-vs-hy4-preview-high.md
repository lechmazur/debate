### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): B won by sustaining the substitution problem: stricter denial may shift demand from visible, conditionable suppliers to opaque rivals rather than reduce spyware circulation. A never adequately grounded its crucial claims that substitutes would be inferior or that lost allied capabilities were merely conveniences. B also persuasively showed that national security depends on controlling any exploit, not specifically preventing transfer of domestic code.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s final response to A’s quality-adjusted scarcity metric was decisive: even a less mature rival exploit can compromise the same devices, while moving the sale removes contracts, logs, update dependencies, and jurisdictional leverage. This directly weakened A’s claimed pathway from stricter regulation to less overall blowback.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won the live comparison: stricter denial mostly shrinks the governable market, not the spyware pool, so blowback tools still reach abusers while allies lose usable capability and the exporting state loses licensing levers. PRO’s irreversible master-key and upstream-denial frame was sharp and stuck through closings, but never escaped the displacement fork after PRO conceded demand may shift; “inferior/our code” and quality-adjusted circulation did not show net fewer compromises or better overall security once rival suppliers fill the gap. CON answered pressure directly and made the decideable trade clearer in later rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s “upstream control only works if we remain upstream / NS does not care whose code compromises the phone” exchange, which undercut PRO’s inferior-rival and quality-adjusted circulation defense of denial.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won the upstream vs downstream weighing. B established each denied export removes a supported zero-click chain and its update pipeline, so our vulnerabilities don't leak even if demand shifts. CON (A) conceded licensed sales still leak and have no pre-abuse revocation proof, relying on paper hooks that B showed fail after the exploit is copied. On the alliance clash, B's distinction between core HUMINT/SIGINT/liaison and commercial convenience was less damaged than A's claim that governed visibility outweighs irreversible blowback.
