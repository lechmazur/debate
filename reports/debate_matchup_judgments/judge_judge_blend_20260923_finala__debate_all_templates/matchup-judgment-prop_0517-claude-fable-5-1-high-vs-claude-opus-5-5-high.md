- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully cornering Side A on the definition of "online" credentials. When A tried to resolve the Manchester nurse dilemma by claiming that supervised practice and employer references would be recognized in A's lane, B decisively pointed out that those are physical, in-person records, not the "online professional credentials" specified in the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (and closing) brilliantly dismantled A's case by showing that every time A tried to make their credential lane safer—relying on supervised practice, employer references, or physical test centers—A was actually smuggling in physical elements to prop up an online credential.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won a close, high-quality clash by holding the motion as written—online credentials vs physical residency—and forcing PRO’s repairs to do most of the work. PRO’s competence-first frame and “residency alone verifies nothing” pressure were strong, and CON’s candid “not competence, trust” answer was costly. But CON’s live path survived better: recognition is pre-gate trust/accountability; local supervised practice, phoneable employers, and sanction history are produced by jurisdictional presence, not interchangeable with a remote PDF; and PRO’s own fixes (accredited chains, approved test centres, regulator exams) either hollow out “online,” recreate in-person gates, or leave issuer reachability weaker than residency. CON’s fork—rigorous credential lane becomes slow/expensive; easy remote lane stays gameable—plus the closing point that licensing exams sit in both lanes, so the real difference is pre-existing accountability, made the ballot clearer. Both sides executed pressure format well; clipping cost both.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A2.6/A4.5–A4.6 fork and “stops being online” turn: PRO conceded remote fraud risks then replaced pure online proctoring with physical test centres and mutual-recognition chains, undercutting the motion’s online-vs-residency comparison while CON kept trust-before-harm intact.

Side B effectively demonstrates that the trust and competence Side A attributes to residency actually stem from verifiable professional records and supervised practice, which are themselves credentials. Side B's clarification that online credentials can utilize local-to-applicant physical test centers preserves the motion's premise while answering Side A's fraud concerns, leaving Side A without a strong comparative advantage for the residency gate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it answers the pressure questions by explaining how international accreditation chains work without needing to physically inspect foreign buildings, and sharply exposes Side A's semantic trick of packing supervised practice and employer references into the definition of "residency."
