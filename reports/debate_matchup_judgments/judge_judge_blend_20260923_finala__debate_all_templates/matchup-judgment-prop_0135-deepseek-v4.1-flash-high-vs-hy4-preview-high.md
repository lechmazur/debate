- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): The decisive clash was whether the motion's "outweigh" standard is a workable, accountable trigger or a discretionary veto disguised as management. A's closing tried to collapse the debate into "CON's tightening tools are caps too," but this rhetorical move never answered the mismatch critique—it just relabeled without resolving why an aggregate, multi-factor "outweigh" ledger is preferable to causally-targeted numeric limits. B's closing image ("a mood with handcuffs") crystallized this live, unrebutted advantage cleanly. A had a strong opening and made reasonable economic arguments linking peak demand to rental conversion profitability, but by the end B had the cleaner, more rigorous path: precise tools already do the metering work the motion claims to need, and the motion's added value is exactly the unaccountable discretion B warned about from Turn 1.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but B's two core lines of attack survived the exchange better than A's defenses of them. First, B's housing argument—that cruise and coach visitors sleep aboard or at home and never compete for a bedroom—directly indicts the motion's own trigger, and A's only answer ("cumulative... immediate protection... slow") never actually explains how capping a stream that doesn't touch housing fixes housing pressure. Second, B's mode-vs-harm distinction (crowding is about bodies at a pinch point, not vehicle type) was sharpened by the 7 a.m. coach vs. noon crush example and never neutralized; A's "pulse" defense conceded that displaced demand becomes smaller dispersed vehicles, which B correctly reframed as evidence that scheduling, not banning, solves the problem. In the pressure-question exchange, A's "exactly the cap" gotcha (once every slot is sold and capacity is still breached, the only tool left is refusal) looked decisive momentarily, but B's rebuttal cleanly distinguished refusing a specific overloaded call from a blanket mode-class cap, preserving CON's alternative. A's closing "spillback" framing was rhetorically effective but arrived late and didn't fully engage with B's broader pinch-point/occupancy-limit proposal, which was not confined to visible tourist plazas. Overall, B kept a consistent, harm-targeted alternative alive through every round and forced A into unresolved concessions on housing and mode-specificity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal 2 answer to A's "exactly the cap" pressure question—distinguishing refusing a specific overloaded call (capacity management) from capping an entire mode—most directly defused A's strongest structural argument and reset the debate onto B's preferred harm-based framing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically exploiting the precise wording of the motion, demonstrating that capping specific modes is a blunt instrument for harms (like housing and peak crowding) that are better addressed by capacity management. Side A's distinction between a vehicle and a peak load proved decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 effectively neutralized the 'housing' aspect of the motion's trigger by pointing out that cruise and bus visitors do not compete for bedrooms, making the proposition's linkage structurally flawed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON successfully demonstrated that the proposition targets the wrong variable. The harms are peak occupancy, emissions, and housing pressure, not the vehicle mode. CON’s persistent distinction between capping a mode versus managing actual harms—combined with the devastating point that cruise and bus tourists are the least housing-intensive visitors—left PRO defending an overinclusive and underinclusive tool. PRO’s admission that the final resort is refusing overload anyway vindicated CON’s alternative of dynamic capacity management, making a mode-specific cap unnecessary and counterproductive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 1 and 2 separation of mode from harm, showing that a vehicle cap blocks off-peak low-impact coaches while ignoring peak-load vans, and that housing pressure cannot meaningfully be blamed on visitors who sleep off-site, decisively undermined PRO’s causal story.
