### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by showing that the proposed caps do not equalize political voice: they advantage incumbents, celebrities, self-funders, and already-organized groups while depriving unknown challengers of early funding. Side B compellingly described donor influence and the money primary, but too often assumed that a large donation constitutes procurement and never adequately answered the displacement of money into independent ads or self-funded candidacies. Side A also exposed tension in banning business treasuries while permitting other institutions to aggregate funds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was its answer to the small-donor cure: unknown candidates need visibility before they can build a donor base, while earned media favors incumbents and restricted donations can migrate into less accountable independent spending or self-funded campaigns. Side B's volunteers-and-persuasion response did not resolve that counterfactual.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by demonstrating that the proposed ban and caps would not create equal citizenship but would instead shift political influence toward incumbents, celebrities, self-funders, and the unions and nonprofits the motion exempts, while silencing challengers who need concentrated early funding to become visible. Side B never resolved how an unknown challenger in a local race could overcome the incumbent’s free media advantage under the motion’s actual mechanisms, repeatedly appealing to small-donor democracy and public matching that are not included in the proposition. Side A also exposed an unresolved asymmetry in Side B’s corporate ban: by leaving other associations free to pool resources while banning only business treasuries, the rule functions as a viewpoint preference rather than genuine neutrality. Side A’s crystallization of the trade-off—official scarcity marketed as fairness—provided the cleaner comparative reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal (A4.3–A4.5), which showed that the small-donor alternative Side B defended was not actually in the motion, and that the proposition would instead empower incumbents and self-funders while muting the very challengers Side B claimed to empower.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON kept two routes alive that PRO never neutralized: banning only business treasuries while leaving unions/nonprofits free is a viewpoint preference because shareholder exit parallels member exit, and low caps without public matching shifts from accountable donations to self-funders/independent ads and hurts unfamiliar challengers who need early concentrated money. PRO's citizenship equality framing was strong but answers on mechanism and neutrality were repetitive rather than resolving.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 reversal on the money-primary and consent—shareholders can sell like members can exit, and a first-time challenger in a one-paper town funded by early large gifts loses audibility while self-funders bypass the cap entirely.
