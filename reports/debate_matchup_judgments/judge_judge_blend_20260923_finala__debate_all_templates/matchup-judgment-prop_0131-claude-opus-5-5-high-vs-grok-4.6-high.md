### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that care needs, not caregiver payments, usually force relatives out of work, so withholding payment adds poverty without eliminating the 3 a.m. burden. Side A made strong points about respite, childless recipients, and opportunity costs, but relied too heavily on treating payment and services as mutually exclusive and subtly shifted the motion from ending unpaid care to ending family care altogether. Side A's repeated clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's most decisive response was that a payment does not cause the caregiver's withdrawal; the relative's unmet needs do. This directly weakened Side A's labor-market trap argument while exposing that promised future services do not compensate the person covering nights and weekends now.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won because it anchored the debate in the present reality that family caregivers already work overnight shifts that services do not cover; the actual choice is paying them or leaving them destitute. Side B’s service-expansion alternative was normatively strong but relied on an idealized counterfactual and a contested zero-sum budget, and B incurred execution costs from three clipped speeches. A’s framing that poverty compounded by exhaustion drives emergency collapses survived B’s pressure, and A’s reading of the motion stayed truer to the text.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s reply that a generous carer wage pays women to surrender the stronger long-term protections of salary, pension, and career progression (Rebuttal 2, A1) was the sharpest single line, but A countered by showing the career was already forfeited where night-sitting does not exist, leaving the caregiver either paid or impoverished.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the central mechanism clash. Both agreed collapse comes via burnout/exhaustion and that unpaid assumption must end. A showed a cheque leaves the 3 a.m. shift unchanged, so it does not prevent the ward admission it fears, while services do. B conceded "a cheque does not lift a patient" and never solved the finite-budget and childless-man pressure or the path-dependency point that paying for existing hours locks in funds and makes the "until" permanent. B's poverty-plus-exhaustion point was strong but does not outweigh A's distinction that paying still assumes families absorb the burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - salary/pension as real anti-poverty and nurses' wages buy new capacity while carer wages pay for hours already worked, plus paid-but-exhausted still collapses, directly neutralizing PRO's burn-out story.
