### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing the unsupported step between appetite suppression and superior athletic performance. Side B showed that GLP-1 drugs might outperform dangerous crash dieting, but did not establish an advantage over properly supervised, legal weight management. Side A also weighed prescription privacy and enforcement costs against that uncertain benefit. Its clipped opening and erroneous reference labels were execution costs, but not enough to erase the substantive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal most decisively reframed the comparison: arriving hydrated and strong is not uniquely drug-enabled, and beating a rival who crash-diets does not prove an unfair edge over disciplined preparation. This left Side B’s case dependent on the unresolved conditional that GLP-1 users preserve more power and recovery.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) wins on burden and comparison. The motion is "may create" - B correctly frames it as precautionary, while CON demands proven results. B's core route - mass decides weight-class/endurance, GLP-1s pharmaceutically abolish hunger/dehydration cost, enabling hydrated athletes to face smaller opponents, invisible and unequal - stays live. CON's net-drag mechanism and privacy-cost rebuttal never defeats the comparative advantage vs the real status quo of crash cutting, and its "properly prepared" benchmark concedes that many rivals still pay the hunger tax B identifies. Pressure answers on circularity and limiting principle give B the cleaner basis for scrutiny-as-disclosure, not ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 comparative vs crash-diet framing - turning CON's lean-mass-loss point into evidence that disclosure is needed to see benefit/harm, and recasting health vs fairness (IV analogy).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core comparative benchmark. Side A's case relied heavily on the argument that GLP-1s provide an advantage because they are "safer and easier than starving and dehydrating." Side B brilliantly reframed this by pointing out that unfair advantage must be measured against legal, optimized preparation, not the sport's most reckless practices. If starvation is the alternative, the solution is better weigh-in rules (which B noted are already happening), not surveilling a harm-reduction drug. If proper supervised weight-making is the alternative, GLP-1s offer no magical advantage and may actually cause a net drag via lean mass loss and underfueling. Side B also successfully defended against the "circularity" charge by noting that clinical sports research can establish plausible benefit without needing to invade athlete privacy first. Side A's limiting principle ("pharmacological abolition of the intake barrier") felt arbitrary compared to Side B's demand for actual competitive effect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of PRO's "GLP-1 vs starvation" benchmark in Rebuttal 1 and 2, pointing out that PRO was measuring unfair advantage against the sport's worst, already-regulated practices rather than legitimate, supervised preparation.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. It tied appetite suppression directly to reduced weight-making costs and power-to-weight advantages, then showed why ordinary clinical oversight cannot assess competitive misuse. Side A offered credible privacy, health, and line-drawing concerns, but its claim that lawful leanness cannot be performance enhancement was too categorical, and anonymized research did not answer B's case for athlete-specific review and deterrence. B's clipped rebuttal incurred a modest execution cost but had already answered both questions and completed the decisive comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was its answer that clinical monitoring tracks patient care and adverse events, whereas sport scrutiny tracks off-label use and competitive effects. This undercut Side A's proposed substitute and made B's moderate monitoring regime seem responsive to a distinct blind spot.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON maintained the decisive line-drawing clash: GLP-1s produce a lawful physique via safer means, akin to gels, altitude tents, and super-shoes, not a new physiology like oxygen or muscle doping. PRO's "less misery is the advantage" [B2.1] was answered by showing many legal, unequally distributed aids also reduce misery [A2.1], and that pharmacy rules plus anonymized lab testing can generate prevalence without mandatory disclosure [A4.1][A4.3]. PRO's pressure about invisibility [B3.1][B3.2] was met directly, while PRO's own R2 was clipped for word limit, blunting its asymmetric-stakes weighing, and its proposal fails to reach the truly unsupervised adolescent it invokes. CON's narrower path - study anonymously, regulate later if needed - better fits the moderate scrutiny motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.2: drug is detectable in blood/urine so anonymized sampling can estimate prevalence and performance link without a suspicious registry, defusing B's circular blind-spot charge and Q1.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by decisively framing the "misery" of weight-cutting as the core physiological constraint of the sport, which GLP-1s pharmacologically override. A successfully dismantled B's analogies by noting that unlike visible super-shoes, invisible drugs require active disclosure to deter misuse. A also exposed the circularity of B's "study first" approach, pointing out that anonymized measurement is not enforcement and cannot stop an athlete dosing for a specific weigh-in. A's risk asymmetry weighing in the final speeches—contrasting the low cost of a disclosure form against the irreversible entrenchment of an invisible advantage—provided a much cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's "anonymized sampling" defense by distinguishing between passive statistical measurement and active scrutiny, proving that surveys cannot deter a hidden athlete dosing specifically for a weigh-in.
