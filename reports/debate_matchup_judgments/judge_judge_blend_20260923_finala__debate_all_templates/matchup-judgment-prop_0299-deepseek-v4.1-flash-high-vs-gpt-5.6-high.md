### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but A's line of attack proved more durable across the exchange: the recursive "who decides eligibility/finite allocation" problem and the observation that permanence itself pre-maps vulnerable communities and creates state leverage were never fully neutralized. B's safeguard architecture (non-exclusive eligibility, staggered boards, withdrawal rights, sunset review) grew more elaborate each round, which A exploited by pointing out these are statutes a hostile successor can simply amend, and by catching the internal tension between "permanent guarantee" and "sunset-reviewed." B's strongest counter—that A's own ad hoc/targeted alternative still requires an official to choose and offers no continuity—landed in the closing but arrived too late to be fully tested, and A's response (plural custody, targeted urgency) only partially answered it. A's Rebuttal 2 was clipped for length, a real execution cost, but the load-bearing dilemma ("if officials refuse to judge, allocations are impossible; if they judge, gatekeeping stands") was delivered before the cut. On balance A pressed the more decisive, harder-to-answer structural objection more consistently.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 turn (A2.1–A2.9), especially the point that every proposed safeguard "recreates the disputed decision one level higher" and the catch that PRO's fund can't simultaneously be a stable permanent guarantee and "sunset-reviewed," did the most damage to B's central design claims and was never fully repaired.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it established that irreversibility creates a unique urgency requiring continuity, while CON's alternative of dispersed private support and ad hoc state contracts offered no reliable floor for the most marginalized and endangered communities. CON landed strong blows on politicization and gatekeeping, but PRO successfully neutralized the comparative force of that objection by showing that charity and private donors already pick winners invisibly without accountability or appeal. The decisive turn was PRO's framing in the closing that the core question is not whether public power can be abused, but who bears the risk when it is absent. CON never fully overcame the structural volatility critique of the private/charity model, and the clipping of CON's second rebuttal compounded the difficulty of closing that gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal neutralizing CON's gatekeeping objection by demonstrating that private philanthropy and universities already select which dialects and communities survive without transparent criteria, public appeal, or legal claim—making invisible gatekeeping less accountable than a statutory fund with published rules and community standing.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but PRO (B) did more to neutralize the opponent's core objection and to convert CON's own proposal into an admission of its thesis. CON's strongest weapon was the "trustee dilemma" (a fund either overrides refusal or stalls), but PRO directly defused this in Rebuttal 2 by designing a fund that respects refusal outright, treating stalled consent as chosen autonomy rather than coerced loss—an answer CON's closing simply restated without engaging. PRO's closing then exploited CON's own answer to the pressure questions (existing institutions, published rules, ready emergency microgrants) to show that CON's "last-resort" alternative already requires the standing capacity PRO advocates, undercutting the distinctiveness of CON's model. CON's honest concession that "no system guarantees" timely reach for unpopular minorities, while intellectually credible, gave PRO a clean line to frame CON as trading a reversible institutional risk for an irreversible cultural loss, and CON's final speech—though technically the last word—reasserted earlier framing ("rather than," the trustee dilemma) rather than directly rebutting this closing move. CON's capture and pluralism arguments remained live and forceful throughout, keeping the debate close, but PRO's tighter institutional safeguards and sharper closing comparison gave it the more resolved, more defensible path to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A1) answering the consent-paradox pressure question—clarifying the fund respects refusal rather than overriding it—directly dismantled CON's "trustee" dilemma, and PRO's closing turned CON's own emergency-backstop model into evidence that standing capacity is unavoidable, a point CON's final speech never squarely answered.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO wins narrowly by sustaining the asymmetry between reversible bureaucratic failures and irreversible cultural extinction, while showing that CON's own alternative of emergency public backstops still requires the pre-standing capacity it claims to reject. CON landed sharp blows on gatekeeping and the "rather than" framing, but its plural-funder model never overcame the public-good problem: unpopular, impoverished minorities lack guaranteed advocates, and CON conceded that no system fully guarantees preservation. PRO's justice and urgency claims remained live after the exchange, whereas CON's capture fears, while plausible, were met with credible institutional design responses (arm's-length administration, statutory separation of funding from recognition, and parallel funding for disputed claims) that PRO successfully framed as sufficient safeguards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing turn that CON's "last resort" emergency model implicitly requires standing public capacity (money, administrators, application routes must already exist), paired with the weighing that reversible bureaucratic errors are preferable to irreversible language extinction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO maintained live route: irreversible loss requires guaranteed capacity; design of arm's-length, community custody and parallel funding answered ownership/capture worry.
