### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON (A) landed the more durable rebuttals. A's repeated exploitation of PRO's own concession that "family involvement continues... is the point" (B2.8-9) forced PRO into a strained "gaps of preference" vs "residual insurer" distinction that never fully answered how capped hours and queues differ from unmet need. A's attack on the pension analogy (bounded cash vs unbounded in-kind care) also went largely unanswered—PRO simply reasserted the analogy in R2 without engaging the bounded/unbounded distinction. On pressure questions, A's Q2 about workforce staffing tradeoffs produced a vague answer ("wages, training, hours give first") that A effectively turned into an admission of unstated tradeoffs, while A's own answer to PRO's Q1 (duration trigger, not means-tested poverty) was a genuinely responsive design move that blunted the "spend-down" charge, even though PRO's closing counter—that the "lighter stretch" is actually the modal case most elders face—was a strong point A never fully rebutted beyond assertion. On balance, CON's throughline (universal promises ration too, just less visibly, and still leave family as the practical backstop) proved harder for PRO to fully neutralize than PRO's core claims were for CON. This edge is tempered by two clipped CON speeches (opening and rebuttal 2), a real execution cost, keeping the margin modest rather than decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated pressure on PRO's admission that families remain involved "as the point" under universalism, escalating through the Pressure Questions into PRO's concession that queues/caps still leave unmet need for the household to fill — a point PRO's "preference vs default payer" distinction never cleanly resolved.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): B won a clear but competitive debate by making the decisive comparison about who is the first insurer of core care. A persuasively showed that universal systems still ration through queues, assessments, and caps, but repeatedly treated any remaining family involvement as equivalent to making families the default payer. B answered that marginal gaps under a public package are materially different from assigning the entire pre-catastrophic period to savings, co-payments, and relatives. B also showed that workforce scarcity exists under either model and that unpaid daughters do not solve it; they merely conceal its cost. A's duration-based catastrophic alternative avoided literal spend-down better than B sometimes acknowledged, but it still conceded substantial household responsibility by design. A's two clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.5 most decisively separated residual family help from residual family insurance and forced A to identify who bears the long period before catastrophic coverage. A later supplied a coherent mixed answer, but that answer confirmed B's central charge that households remain first insurers for a significant portion of care.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO won by showing that CON's floor-plus-catastrophe alternative still makes the household the residual insurer for the long pre-catastrophic stretch of care, recreating the very spend-down, savings depletion, and family burden the proposition rejects. CON effectively argued that universal systems also ration and rely on family gaps, but they never cleanly escaped the charge that their own model leaves the middle class exposed by design during the years of bathing, nights, and moderate dementia. Their attempt to distinguish a care-duration trigger from means-testing did not resolve who pays for those middle years—savings and daughters—which is precisely the lottery PRO identified. Two clipped speeches and a false claim that PRO never answered CON's Q2 added execution costs that made CON's path harder to credit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure questions and the subsequent Rebuttal 2 exchange on the "lighter stretch," which forced CON to concede that middle-class households carry partial cost via co-insurance and savings during pre-catastrophic years, showing that CON's alternative effectively recreated the household insurance model PRO condemns.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): This was a genuinely close, substantive debate. PRO built a strong throughline (uninsurable catastrophic risk, market failure, means-testing cliffs, the "proves too much" attack on CON's labor-scarcity claim, and the recurring equity point that unpaid family care is not "free"). CON matched it with a consistent structural counter (labor scarcity as the binding constraint, "you cannot legislate nurses into existence," family care as preference rather than failure) and, crucially, adapted under pressure by finally specifying a concrete duration/poverty-triggered backstop when directly asked in Pressure Questions — a real example of progression and responsiveness. PRO's closing landed a sharp point exploiting that design (ruin can occur during the "stretch" before the backstop triggers), but CON's final rebuttal offered a coherent distinction between unpaid mixed home care and purchased professional care that blunted, even if it didn't fully erase, that attack. The deciding factor is execution discipline: PRO's Opening, Rebuttal 1, and Rebuttal 2 were all clipped for exceeding the word limit, repeatedly cutting off developing arguments (e.g., ending mid-list or mid-sentence three separate times), which the judging frame explicitly treats as a real cost. CON stayed within limits throughout, executed the A1/A2 pressure-answer format cleanly, and kept its case tighter and more resolved. Given how close the substantive clash is, this consistent execution failure on PRO's side is enough to tip a genuinely competitive debate to CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Pressure Questions exchange was decisive: PRO's Q1 forced CON to finally specify its backstop design (duration/poverty trigger protecting home and modest savings), and PRO's closing then exploited that design by arguing the uncertain "stretch" before the trigger is where ruin actually happens — the sharpest and most consequential exchange of the debate, only partially defused by CON's final distinction between unpaid mixed care and purchased professional care.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by offering a credible duration-and-intensity backstop that protects catastrophic cases without universalizing ordinary care costs. That answer weakened B’s central claim that the only alternatives were universal coverage or ruinous asset testing. A also maintained stronger comparative pressure on scarce labor, fiscal sustainability, and subsidies to those able to pay. B compellingly exposed the hidden costs borne by daughters, but never fully showed that universal purchasing power creates enough net care capacity rather than reallocating scarce workers. B’s repeated clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After B demanded a concrete alternative to means-testing cliffs, A directly specified a need-and-duration trigger with protection for the home and modest savings. B replied that ruin would occur before coverage began, but did not establish why the trigger could not operate early enough or why mixed family and private care necessarily meant professional-rate ruin. This exchange gave A the cleaner alternative to B’s universal-or-destitute framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO maintained that elder-care risk is uncertain and catastrophic, requiring pre-crisis pooling rather than a post-ruin backstop. CON's duration-gate design was conceptually elegant but remained vulnerable to the charge that families burn resources during the initial qualifying stretch before the gate opens, and never fully resolved the tension between shrinking families and continued reliance on family labor. PRO's demographic trap and risk-pooling frame sharpened across rounds, while CON's workforce-scarcity argument, though strong, was blunted by the recognition that CON's own backstop also demands scarce professional hours without a superior recruitment mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing crystallization that CON's duration backstop is "a promise to catch families after they have fallen" — forcing households to front unpredictable severe costs before any public gate opens — decisively shifted the comparison on risk-pooling.
