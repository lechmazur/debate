### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: PRO itself framed model internals (weights, logs, activations) as the binding constraint, and CON showed that coordinated public silicon does not supply that good and is therefore orthogonal to the dependency PRO named. Across rounds CON forced the unique-work test—“what does public ownership do that legal access rights plus funded multi-provider procurement cannot?”—and PRO’s best answers (meter throttling; interoperable national clouds) either collapsed into CON’s regulatory/procurement package or left commercial capacity inside the loop, undercutting the motion’s “rather than.” PRO kept a live residual on enforcement lag and quiet starvation, but never showed why that residual requires replacing firm frontier capacity rather than regulating and buying access to it. Side A therefore owned the comparative and the motion’s distinctive mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON A2.1–A2.2: public compute still does not conjure weights/logs; the scarce good is model access, so the fix is a researcher-access regime, not a multinational cluster—leaving PRO’s core problem orthogonal to the proposition.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully showed that the motion misdiagnoses the binding constraint on independent AI research. While PRO established that firms currently control both the subject and tools of study, CON bifurcated the problem into model access (weights, logs, internals) and compute access, demonstrating that public compute solves only the latter while the true gatekeeping power lies in discretionary model release. CON's alternative—mandated legal access rights plus flexible procurement—was shown to achieve independence without the cost and lag of permanent public infrastructure. PRO's throttling and sabotage fears were plausible but never established as uniquely solvable only by rack ownership, especially once CON pointed out that the same regulatory regime compelling model access could equally bar infrastructure interference. CON's closing crystallization—that "the auditee's power switch was never the power meter, it is the model"—neatly resolved the core clash in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pivot in Rebuttal 1 (A2.1-A2.2) that public compute does not provide model weights or training logs and that the motion therefore prescribes the wrong remedy, forcing PRO to spend the rest of the debate defending why infrastructure ownership matters even if model access requires separate regulation.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the central clash: weight and log access is the binding constraint, and public silicon does not conjure it. PRO never names an audit that a public cluster enables that rented or national supercomputing allocations under the same legal access regime cannot, while CON shows rented capacity can be multi-sourced and contractually protected. That collapses PRO's "meter" argument and exposes the motion as building a lagging public tier that still leaves the real gatekeeper intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A3.1 / A4.1-A4.2 pressing for one uniquely public-compute audit and arguing mandated access plus diversified rental removes the auditee's power switch, which B's long-duration stress-test answer failed to meet.
