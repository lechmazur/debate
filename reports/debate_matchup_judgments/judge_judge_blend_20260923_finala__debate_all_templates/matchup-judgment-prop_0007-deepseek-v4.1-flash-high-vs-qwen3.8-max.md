### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side A effectively distinguished targeted prophylaxis from growth promotion and raised credible outbreak and leakage risks, but its case remained conditional on farms preserving current disease-prone conditions and on later treatment exceeding routine population-wide exposure. Side B directly challenged that assumption through management substitution, fewer exposed animals, and the scale of continuous selection, then weighed irreversible resistance against manageable price and yield costs. Side A overstated B’s acknowledgment that no policy is guaranteed into a concession that the benefit was merely speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the outbreak concern by identifying Side A’s fixed-husbandry assumption and explained why broad repeated exposure of healthy populations is generally a larger resistance reservoir than illness-triggered treatment. This preserved B’s mechanism while strengthening its precautionary weighing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing prophylaxis as a crutch for intensive farming that systematically breeds resistance. Side A's strongest counter—that banning prevention forces delayed, larger antibiotic doses for sick animals—was neutralized by B's argument that a ban forces structural management improvements and inherently exposes fewer animals to selection pressure. B also expertly leveraged the motion's 'even if' clause to weigh the irreversible loss of medical efficacy against A's economic and yield concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal and closing effectively dismantled A's 'targeted' alternative, exposing it as a permission structure that would allow routine dosing to continue unabated for predictable events like weaning.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the decisive clash on what the ban actually forbids versus CON’s “targeted high-risk” alternative. Once weaning, transport, and mixing count as defined risk, CON’s scalpel functions as a standing permission for routine healthy-animal dosing; PRO’s loophole pressure and exhaustible-resource framing made that clearer than CON’s counter that benefits are unproven. CON landed real hits on delayed/concentrated exposure and cross-border leakage, and forced PRO to concede no universal biomass proof, but those points never fully neutralized the motion’s accepted price/yield costs or the irreversibility weighing. Stewardship versus ban stayed live, yet PRO’s comparative route—stop continuous healthy-population selection before resistance is permanent—was the cleaner reason to decide after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure Q1 plus A2.5/A4.5 on “high-risk” swallowing routine weaning/transport prophylaxis; CON never cleanly showed that distinction survives without remaining default practice.
