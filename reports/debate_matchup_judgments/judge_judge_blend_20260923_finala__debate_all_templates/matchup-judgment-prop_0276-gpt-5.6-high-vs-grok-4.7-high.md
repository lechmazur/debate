### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A successfully framed the debate around the burden of initiation, effectively demonstrating that Side B's 'one-click' alternative still defaults to the airline keeping the money if the passenger remains passive. While Side B landed a highly effective technical critique of Side A's 'minutes past the threshold' rule, Side A persuasively contained this as an edge-case issue that should not prevent automatic payouts in clean, ordinary cases.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument that identity and payment paths are mere bookkeeping already held in the reservation system, effectively dismantling Side B's justification for making the debt contingent on a passenger's click.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully showed that PRO’s supposedly automatic system relied on an arbitrary "minutes past threshold" causation rule and opaque coding incentives that merely relocated disputes behind the airline’s screen. While PRO landed strong moral blows about silent passengers losing out, CON demonstrated that liability must be established before payment and that a prefilled, one-click claim with independent review achieves accessibility without the mass errors of automatic payout. PRO never fully answered the sequence-dependence problem or the perverse coding incentive, leaving CON’s comparative case cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sequence-dependence critique in Rebuttal 2 (showing that PRO’s primary-cause rule could make a brief crew issue after hours of weather trigger full liability while the reverse sequence escaped payment), which exposed the arbitrary mechanics of PRO’s automatic compensation trigger.

Side B conceded that in clear-cut cases of airline fault, the carrier keeps the compensation money if the passenger fails to click a confirmation link. Side A capitalized on this to argue that Side B's system still prices reliability as optional and rewards airlines for passenger inaction, effectively neutralizing B's claim that their alternative removes the endurance barrier. While Side B landed strong, logical critiques of Side A's "minutes past threshold" causation rule, Side A's broader point that hard edge cases shouldn't withhold standardized payments in ordinary cases was sufficient to maintain their route to victory. Side A's rhetoric was also sharper, more direct in answering pressure questions, and more memorable in the final clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's concession that airlines retain unclaimed compensation in clean cases, framing the "one-click" requirement as a persistence tax that protects the carrier's cash flow rather than the passenger's rights.
