### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won a highly competitive debate by effectively framing Side B's salary threshold as a "price tag on restraint" that allows deep-pocketed employers to commodify worker mobility.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response that garden leave covers wages but not career (skills atrophy, market value decays) decisively answered Side B's claim that paid restrictions eliminate harm.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the debate on the motion's principle: restraint should be unpurchasable and rare. CON won the low-wage abuse point but that is common ground. The decisive comparison became salary-threshold vs fiduciary/sale status. CON's best hit was that VP titles are cheap, but B's closing clarified fiduciary = court-tested duties, not a card. B's framing that a threshold commodifies liberty - once the price is paid the restraint follows - directly supports "nearly all" plus only earned duties. California as a natural experiment and the gap in evidence of harm at the top was not filled by CON, leaving PRO's path intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Exchange over whether exceptions are purchasable: B2.3/B4.5 threshold as price tag swallowing the rule vs A4.6-A4.8 titles as cheapest thing to hand out, resolved in B5.2 by defining fiduciary as conduct-based duty.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively exposing the structural flaws in Side B's proposed alternative. Side B advocated for a "genuine fiduciary" exception, claiming it was an ungameable, principled line. Side A brilliantly countered that this relies on ex-post, case-by-case litigation to determine status based on "actual authority and conduct." This recreates the exact chilling effect, uncertainty, and litigation risk that makes noncompetes abusive in the first place. Side A's alternative—an ex-ante, objective salary threshold combined with mandatory garden leave and upfront notice—proved much more robust, ensuring employers internalize the cost of restraint while giving workers clear terms. Side A's closing crystallization of "who pays?" effectively sealed the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantled Side B's "genuine fiduciary" exception by pointing out that relying on courts to assess conduct means workers won't know if they are bound until they are sued, exposing it as a highly gameable, litigate-it-later standard rather than a true safe harbor.
