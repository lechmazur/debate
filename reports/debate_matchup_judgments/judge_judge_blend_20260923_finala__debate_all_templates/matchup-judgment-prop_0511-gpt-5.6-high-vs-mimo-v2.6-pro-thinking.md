### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won by effectively defending the definitions of 'housing supply' and 'local public finance' against Side B's attempts to substitute them with 'incumbent affordability' and 'averted spending'. Side A also systematically dismantled Side B's attacks on valuation error and zoning constraints.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively countered the valuation critique by contrasting a broad, recurring base with administrative errors against a transaction tax whose precise, optional trigger actively freezes markets and erases revenue.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A won because it kept the motion’s true comparisons live: incentives to build plus recurring local revenue versus two alternatives that either suppress returns/raise nothing or tax exchange. B’s zoning-constraint and valuation attacks were sharp and forced real work, but B repeatedly treated incumbent rent protection and averted emergency spending as if they were housing-supply and public-finance wins, which diluted rather than defeated A’s cleaner comparative architecture. A answered the decisive pressure honestly and narrowed the clash to manageable administration versus structural distortion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 + symmetry): under rigid zoning neither policy creates a unit, so that tie defeats CON’s claim for caps; LVT still funds infrastructure and does not obstruct assembly, while caps only protect selected incumbents and raise no revenue.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON set a doubly comparative burden that PRO never fully met. By showing that under binding zoning constraints—common in shortage cities—LVT cannot create new units, CON established that LVT ties rent caps and transaction taxes on the constrained supply margin rather than exceeding them. Using PRO's own incidence concession, CON also demonstrated that LVT offers sitting tenants no immediate relief during the long pre-construction window where rent caps preserve occupied homes. On finance, CON's critique of LVT's unobservable site-valuation base and its vulnerability to assessment error that spills onto improvements remained live, while PRO's reply that error is merely "administrative" did not fully neutralize the structural concern. PRO's closing retreat to "wherever construction is legally possible" crystallized the concession that decided the round: LVT's supply incentive is strongest where constraints are weakest and often inert where housing shortages are most severe.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The zoning-constraint exchange in Rebuttal 1, where CON asked "Attractive to whom? A developer who cannot get permission" and concluded that "an incentive that cannot bite on the constrained margin is not supply policy." This substantially damaged PRO's core mechanism by showing the vacancy-to-development channel is frequently blocked by exogenous planning constraints, forcing PRO into the weaker position of defending LVT only "wherever construction is legally possible."

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A won by strictly controlling the comparative metric. Side B successfully proved that LVT might not be a magical supply engine, but Side A expertly used B's own logic (capitalization, friction) to show that LVT is still comparatively better than the alternatives, which actively penalize transactions and rental returns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal exposed the fatal flaw in B's case: even if LVT's incentives are weaker than advertised, B's alternatives act as active deterrents, and A's point that transfer taxes tax "the remedy" while LVT taxes "the problem" was decisive.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): A won the comparative motion cleanly. A’s holding-cost vs. production-tax frame and pressure for a rival mechanism decided the exchange; B’s “LVT helps less than advertised / realistic rivals less bad” case was not enough.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 A1 on capitalization: lower bid price reflects, rather than erases, the recurring idle-holding cost—and capitalization hits B’s preferred transfer tax harder because it taxes the sale remedy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing a direct, durable mechanism through which LVT pressures idle land into use while showing that both transaction taxes and rent caps actively penalize the transactions and returns that produce housing. CON’s sophisticated critique—that LVT capitalizes into land prices and does not alter the incremental construction calculus—was effectively answered by PRO’s distinction between a discounted entry price and a recurring annual holding cost, and by the decisive reframe that transfer taxes tax the remedy (the redevelopment sale) while LVT taxes the problem (the idle site). CON additionally failed to discharge the burden of demonstrating a positive supply mechanism for either alternative when pressed, and their opening was clipped, materially weakening their ability to set out a full comparative case. PRO’s fiscal stability argument also survived largely intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s second rebuttal (B4.2) reframing transfer taxes as taxing the remedy rather than the problem, and noting that CON’s own capitalization argument applies more strongly to CON’s preferred instrument; paired with B4.5–B4.6, which exposed that CON never identified a channel through which either alternative actually increases housing supply, shifting the comparative burden decisively.
