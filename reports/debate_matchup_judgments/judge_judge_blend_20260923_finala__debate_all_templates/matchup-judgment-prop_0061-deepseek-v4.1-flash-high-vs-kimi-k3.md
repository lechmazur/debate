### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won a competitive debate by tying immigration to Japan’s immediate, labor-intensive care and fiscal needs while framing it as a bridge through the dependency peak rather than a permanent cure. Its repeated claim that immigration must accelerate forever also did not adequately answer A’s time-bounded bridge argument. A’s clipped opening and underdeveloped treatment of integration costs narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange answered the demographic treadmill: migrants can contribute for decades during the hardest dependency window, so eventual aging does not negate their present value. Side B kept asserting an ever-expanding inflow despite A proposing a sustained range through the 2050s, leaving the central timing comparison unresolved in A’s favor.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2/Closing conversion of A’s 300–400k answer into the definition of “pillar” plus forever-rebuilt treadmill.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the clash concrete - care, farms, construction need people now where robots fail after 40 years of automation-first policy - and flipped CON's strongest cards. More direct answers to pressure and cleaner weighing gave PRO the path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 sector-strip pressure and B2/B4 response that wage reform only bids workers away in a shrinking pool, plus the enforceable permanence rules, left A's rightsizing/automation answer without a person to lift the patient.
