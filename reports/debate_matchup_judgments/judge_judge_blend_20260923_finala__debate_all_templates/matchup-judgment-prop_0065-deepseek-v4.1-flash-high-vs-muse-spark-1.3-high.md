The debate was close and competitive, but PRO ended with the cleaner, less-contradicted case.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): This was a genuinely close, well-executed debate with strong clash on both sides. B built a compelling normative case (positional goods, "quality control on persons," the inescapability of a purchased genome versus optional tutoring, private eugenics via aggregation) and landed a sharp pressure question exposing that CON's own concession on sex/race selection bans undercuts its claim that no non-disease line can ever be legislated. But CON's central practical objection — that a domestic ban only stops families who cannot afford to travel, turning the ban into "symbolic for the poor, optional for the rich" — was never fully neutralized. B's answer (organ/surrogacy bans still shrink and stigmatize despite evasion) is a reasonable analogy, but it does not resolve the deeper problem that this is precisely the inequality B's own case is built to prevent: the ban leaves the wealthy free to buy the exact positional advantage B decries while the poor are additionally criminalized or shut out even of regulated access. CON kept returning to this "who bears the cost" comparison and used it effectively in both rebuttals and the closing, while PRO's rebuttal narrowed to normalization/scale arguments that answer prevalence but not the equity harm PRO itself foregrounded. That unresolved tension is the most decisive thread left standing, giving CON the cleaner path to a vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's repeated "ban is symbolic for the poor, optional for the rich" line, pressed via Q2 and never fully defused by B's organ/surrogacy analogy, was the most damaging exchange because it turned PRO's own equality rationale for restriction against the practical effect of the restriction itself.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by framing the issue as a collective-action arms race that mere regulation cannot solve. A deftly neutralized B's 'criminalizing families' argument by specifying clinic-level liability, and successfully defended against the evasion argument by emphasizing that domestic bans prevent normalization. B's concession that skin color could be restricted under 'harm-based rules' undermined its own stance against state line-drawing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's employment law analogy (distinguishing the legal act of discarding an embryo from the discriminatory motive of grading it for non-disease traits) cleanly dismantled B2's primary structural attack.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing embryo screening as a collective action trap that requires shared legal boundaries, effectively parrying Side B's evasion and blurry-line arguments with strong analogies to existing exploitation laws and sex-selection bans. Side A's act-versus-motive distinction was a decisive analytical blow that neutralized Side B's defense of standard IVF practices.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it deployed the employment law analogy (declining to hire versus declining to hire for a discriminatory reason) to dismantle Side B's claim that selecting among spare IVF embryos is morally and legally indistinguishable from standard IVF.
