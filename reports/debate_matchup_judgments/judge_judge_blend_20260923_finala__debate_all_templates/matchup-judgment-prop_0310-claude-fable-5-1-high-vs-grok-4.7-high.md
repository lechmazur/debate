### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by tying the motion to universal reach and showing why optional provision predictably misses students without home instruction. Side A raised serious costs, timing, and safety concerns, but repeatedly assumed that core status required a standalone, high-stakes credential. The motion did not require that design, and B persuasively showed that finance, safety boundaries, cooking, and repair could be embedded within existing required instruction. B did not fully resolve opportunity costs, but A never supplied an equally reliable mechanism for reaching everyone.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was its response that A’s proposal to teach interest and repair warnings inside required classes already accepted the motion’s central principle: every student should receive these lessons rather than opt in. A’s distinction between embedded academic content and a life-skill credential was coherent, but depended on a narrower and more burdensome interpretation of “core” than the proposition demanded.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): PRO folded cooking, repair, and finance into existing required courses, neutralizing CON's cost and gate objections. PRO then showed CON's own alternative—teaching interest inside required math and safety warnings inside required classes—was itself a mandatory reach, vindicating the motion's core-status framing. CON never recovered from this concession, and PRO's equity argument that the true alternative leaves the most vulnerable students learning from lenders remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO Pressure Question Q1 and its follow-up in Rebuttal 2, which trapped CON into admitting these skills should reach every student inside required classes, then showed that the remaining dispute was over facilities rather than the motion's actual demand of core status.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A won by decisively trapping Side B in a semantic corner. Side B's strongest argument was that these skills should be embedded as modules within existing required classes (like math and science) rather than standing alone as new diploma gates. Side A brilliantly pointed out in the Pressure Questions and Rebuttal 2 that if these skills are taught inside required classes, they are by definition required for every student—perfectly satisfying the proposition's demand that they be "core" rather than "optional extras." Side A also dismantled Side B's "teach beside the signature" argument by pointing out that the only institution present at the signature is the lender, whose interests are adverse to the student. Side B spent the latter half of the debate retreating to the distinction that embedded lessons aren't the "aim" of the course, which felt like a weak semantic quibble compared to Side A's structural takedown.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they exposed that Side B's proposal of teaching these skills inside existing required classes actually fulfills the proposition's definition of "core," effectively neutralizing Side B's main offensive.

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by turning feasibility into a sharp thick-versus-thin dilemma. Its clipped second rebuttal was an execution cost, but the decisive analysis appeared before the clipping and was crystallized effectively in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange exposed the collision between B’s promise that the welding student “surrenders none” and B’s proposal for a semester or units inside health.
