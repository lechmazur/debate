### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that portability need not transfer every relationship indiscriminately: visible links can move, while sensitive ties require confirmation or remain excluded. Side A persuasively identified contextual privacy and data-multiplication risks, but never established that partial graph transfer would be useless or that its vague alternatives would meaningfully solve network lock-in. B therefore retained the stronger comparative case that regulated, imperfect portability creates valuable exit pressure without requiring wholesale disclosure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered A’s dead-letter-or-forced-disclosure dilemma with tiered transfer and argued that a partial network can still provide critical mass. A’s multiplication-of-risk response remained important, but it did not defeat B’s narrower claim that governed transfer of already-visible links offers meaningful competitive value.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won by successfully framing the social graph as inherently relational and proving that transferring it inherently compromises third-party consent. PRO's 'tiered' defense (moving only public links and deferring sensitive ones) was effectively neutralized by CON's dilemma: if sensitive ties are omitted, the community fails to reconstruct, but if they are moved, privacy is violated. CON's argument that the remedy for incumbent data extraction is 'minimization, not compulsory multiplication' cleanly defeated PRO's strongest weighing mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 provided the decisive moment by answering PRO's pressure question with the 'minimization vs. multiplication' framework and pointing out the re-contextualization problem (that consent on one platform does not equal consent on another).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully defending a pragmatic, tiered mechanism for social-graph transfer that breaks incumbent lock-in without sacrificing sensitive privacy. Side A effectively neutralized Side B's "dead letter versus forced disclosure" dilemma by explaining that a "symbolic quorum" or partial arrival is sufficient to bootstrap a competing network. Furthermore, Side A's comparative weighing—highlighting that Side B's privacy arguments effectively protect the incumbent's unaccountable monopoly over the graph under the guise of safety—provided a cleaner, more decisive reason to vote PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the "dead letter" pressure question, arguing that a "symbolic quorum" and partial arrival are enough to seed a new network and invite the rest, effectively defused Side B's strongest structural dilemma.
