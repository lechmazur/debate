### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by anchoring their case to the absolute wording of the resolution, successfully arguing that mandating continuous telemetry and cybersecurity for "all" assets (including minor rural overpasses and power-grid substations) would incur massive lifecycle costs and divert funds from manual inspection. Side B argued brilliantly for the superiority of continuous data over snapshots, but their attempt to dismiss the universal scope as a mere "boundary dispute" failed to overcome Side A's practical economic clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, which rejected the "boundary dispute" framing by demonstrating that the absolute scope of "all" assets is precisely where the financial and logistical harms of the mandate live.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion’s actual text live—“all new” assets and reliance shift “rather than” main manual inspection—and showing PRO repeatedly defended triage/complementarity instead. PRO’s collapse examples and continuous-detection story were vivid and mostly unanswered early, but never closed the gap on low-consequence assets, lifecycle/cyber/stewardship costs, or false-reassurance at fleet scale. Once CON forced “targeted where risk warrants” as the real operable policy, PRO’s mechanism concessions (inspectors still verify and decide; sensors route them) made the motion-as-written harder to affirm than CON’s risk-based alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained motion-fidelity turn (esp. A2.2/A4.1–A4.5): PRO’s own “multiply occasions / informed response / verification still with inspectors” framing is triage, not the proposition’s universal primary-reliance mandate.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully trapping Side B (PRO) on the text of the motion. PRO spent the debate defending a risk-based triage system where sensors flag issues and inspectors verify them, which CON accurately pointed out is not what the motion requires ("all new assets" and "rather than rely mainly on" inspections). CON's argument that a universal mandate creates unmanageable lifecycle costs (telemetry, cybersecurity, data stewardship) for low-risk assets was never effectively answered by PRO's "rounding error" assertion. Furthermore, CON's closing rebuttal regarding the actual causes of the cited bridge collapses (design flaws rather than inspection frequency) severely damaged PRO's core emotional appeal. Side A controlled the clash, enforced the burden of the specific proposition, and offered a more grounded engineering reality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing argument dismantling PRO's use of the Fern Hollow and I-35W bridge collapses. By pointing out that these failures were due to design capacity and load rating issues rather than a lack of continuous monitoring, Side A effectively neutralized PRO's primary impact calculus and exposed the mismatch between the proposed solution and the actual problem.
