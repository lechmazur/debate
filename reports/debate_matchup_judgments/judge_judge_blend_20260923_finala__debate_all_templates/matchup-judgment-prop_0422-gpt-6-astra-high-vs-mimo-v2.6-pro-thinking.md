- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing child supervision as a non-negotiable safety prerequisite rather than an optional seat preference. Side A convincingly argued that collectively funding this basic obligation is preferable to leaving child safety contingent on a family's ability to pay or a stranger's willingness to swap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly capitalized on Side B's answer to Q1, turning Side B's defense of a 'free option' into a damaging admission that Side B's policy explicitly permits separating preschoolers from caregivers.

PRO framed supervision cleanly as a non-luxury duty and landed a real blow with CON’s admission that residual separation is allowed, but never showed why bundling adjacency into base fares leaves the tightest-budget family better off. CON’s through-line—that the motion abolishes a price, not a cost—grew stronger after PRO’s sold-out answers committed to compensation, free replacement travel, a designated-caregiver link, and enforcement. That turned PRO’s “modest upstream fix” into a compliance regime whose weight migrates into fares and thinner discount inventory. Opening clip cost CON some ground early, yet later comparison and pressure handling still made the clearer reason to reject the mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2 (A4.3): using PRO’s own sold-out remedies—displace paid seats with compensation, free rebooking, caregiver register, enforcement—to prove the proposal is a heavy compliance regime, not a free adjacency fix.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO sustained the core distinction between necessary caregiver access and optional seat preference, and CON's admission that its policy permits a child to be seated apart when gate coordination failed left a concrete harm unaddressed. - **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's operational claims and separating the safety requirement from the billing structure.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which highlighted Side A's concession that late-booking families would need to take the 'next departure'—the exact outcome A had aggressively condemned in their first rebuttal.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON won by splitting the motion’s safety premise from its billing ban.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.7–A4.8: once both frameworks already protect adjacency on journeys sold, PRO’s remaining ban on transparent charges loses its claimed safety payoff and collapses into contested redistribution.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won by anchoring the debate in a powerful moral distinction: monetizing the placement of a young child next to their caregiver is a toll on a safety duty already owed, not a legitimate charge for scarce capacity. While Side A landed real scarcity objections and showed that transparent pricing plus a safe-arrangement mandate could prevent the worst abuses, A never overcame B’s core comparative argument that this safety obligation should be bundled like other carriage duties rather than itemized to a captive group with no exit. B’s rebuttals consistently turned A’s economic framing into a defense of paywalls, crystallized the captive-market problem in the Pressure Questions exchange, and closed by sharpening the choice between carriage and toll.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where B answered A’s pressure questions by showing that late-booking edge cases are absorbed by the operator rather than forcing families to pay, and by exposing the separate reservation charge as a targeted extraction from a captive market rather than neutral capacity pricing.
