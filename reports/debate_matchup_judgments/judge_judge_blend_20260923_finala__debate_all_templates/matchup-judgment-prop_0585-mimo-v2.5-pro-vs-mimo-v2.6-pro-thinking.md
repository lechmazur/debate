### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and executed the pressure-question format cleanly (correct Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 labeling). B's strongest points—that permitting/siting is a legal-political wall money can't dissolve, that transmission's "commissioning day" is just as distant as storage maturation, and that correlated extreme weather (Texas 2021, European wind drought) undercuts the "borrow from a distant surplus" story—were sharp and mostly unanswered on their own terms. But A's central economic rebuttal, that covering multi-day/weekly correlated shortfalls with long-duration storage requires terawatt-hour-scale buildout at costs far exceeding moving already-existing stranded hydro/wind, was never quantitatively engaged by B, who retreated to "both are hard, but storage is faster and modular" without addressing the scale mismatch. A also more successfully turned B's own European drought example into support (partial relief from wires vs. no comparable storage precedent), and framed transmission investment plausibly to include cost-allocation/permitting reform, a point B pressured but didn't fully defeat. A's closing crystallized the asymmetry (storage depends on wires; wires don't depend on storage for first-order benefit) more effectively than B's closing, which mostly restated prior claims without directly rebutting the volume-cost math. The debate was close and both sides argued in good faith with plausible, ungrounded-but-reasonable examples, but A ended with the cleaner, more load-bearing comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's shift of B's European wind-drought example from an attack into support—interconnectors delivered documented partial relief during a multi-week correlated lull, while B could only assert (not demonstrate) that a long-duration storage fleet could do better at that scale—was the most decisive exchange, and B's response never supplied a comparable quantitative counter.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by demonstrating that transmission physically solves the worst-case grid scenarios while storage cannot realistically reach the necessary scale. Side B had an excellent argument about transmission's political bottlenecks, but A correctly pointed out that B artificially narrowed the motion to "buying hardware" while ignoring "regional market integration." A masterfully turned B's own European wind drought example to show that interconnectors provide tangible relief during multi-week lulls, whereas the terawatt-hour storage needed for the same event remains economically speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing turned B's European wind drought example into a devastating comparative metric: interconnectors provided proven partial relief during a multi-week event, while long-duration storage for that timeframe requires impossible terawatt-hour volumes, proving "partial beats speculative."
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping the decisive comparison on the table: one-way dependency (storage needs delivery paths and cheap surplus; wires unlock existing hydro/wind/reserves without waiting on storage) plus energy-volume weighing on multi-week correlated scarcity. B landed real pressure on permitting vs. hardware dollars and modular speed, and forced A to stretch “investment” toward siting/policy levers; but B never neutralized that multi-week LDES at regional scale is as implausible as a fast supergrid, nor that interconnectors still supplied partial real relief in the shared European example. A’s closings and A1/A2 answers resolved the clash more cleanly toward the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s European wind-drought / TWh-volume rebuttal: interconnectors gave partial real relief in week-scale correlated scarcity, while a storage fleet sized for that shortfall is not a serious alternative—undercutting B’s local-dispatchability priority.
