- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): CON's decisive move was forcing PRO to specify what "transparency" actually meant, then exploiting PRO's necessary retreat from public disclosure to "controlled access for auditors/courts/counsel." CON's closing crystallized a clean fork (public disclosure = fraud manual; sealed disclosure = not the motion, and no global instrument needed) that PRO did not neutralize in the space available. Execution-wise, PRO also lost more content to word-limit clipping across three turns versus CON's one, costing PRO some argumentative completeness (e.g., the torture/refoulement analogy was cut off before being developed).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5), tying PRO's concession on withholding "decisive feature set" and "unpublished detection methods" back to CON's opening warning about disclosure architecture, then declaring "PRO wins the abstract principle of reviewability... It loses the proposition as written" — this framing went largely unanswered in PRO's closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A successfully defended a pragmatic model of algorithmic transparency, fending off Side B's attempt to force the motion into a mandate for a public cheat-sheet.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's argument that "treating every lawyer as a cartel is an argument against counsel, not against contestability" neatly neutralized B's attempt to frame controlled legal disclosure as a leak to smuggling networks.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by forcing Side B into a decisive dilemma regarding the definition and implementation of "transparency." ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): A (CON) ran a tight, consistent case: transparency in an adversarial screening domain functions as a "marking scheme" for coaches, a global floor is either too weak to matter or too strong for classified partner-intelligence cases, and the asymmetry between reversible false refusals and irreversible false grants gives the strongest weighing metric in the debate. Crucially, A's pressure question on classified intelligence forced B to concede that even sensitive cases require disclosing something like "a watchlist identity match and a travel-route flag," and A then turned that concession into a devastating rebuttal (coaching memo plus burned method), which B's closing never adequately neutralized. B (PRO) built a coherent humanitarian case—applicants need notice of system-specific errors (mistranslation, identity mismatch) invisible in the raw file—and pushed a reasonable layered-disclosure framework, but repeatedly had speeches clipped for length across three separate turns, a real execution cost that cut off developing arguments before they landed. B's central claim that individualized disclosure adds only "marginal" help to fraudsters was asserted rather than defended against A's marking-scheme framing, leaving that clash unresolved in A's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's use of B's own Q2 answer (that classified cases still require disclosing something like "a watchlist identity match and a travel-route flag") to argue this is itself the coaching memo and burned-source problem was the most decisive exchange, converting B's attempted concession into A's strongest weapon.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won by consistently proving that individualized algorithmic transparency functions as a 'marking scheme' for coaching networks in an inherently adversarial domain. Side A offered strong arguments regarding unseen data errors, but Side B successfully demonstrated that even A's 'calibrated' examples (e.g., revealing a 'travel route flag' or 'family ties') would compromise security and assist fraudsters. Side A also suffered execution costs by repeatedly exceeding the word limit and being clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's attack on Side A's 'calibrated' disclosure for classified intelligence, pointing out that providing a summary like 'travel route flag' is a 'coaching memo plus a burned method.'
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B wins because it successfully proves that Side A's alternative—relying on file review without disclosing principal factors—functionally acts as a rubber stamp for systemic errors. B's concrete example of a wrong identity match (where the applicant doesn't know the system confused them with someone else) decisively answers A's claim that applicants already know their own facts and can just present their papers. While Side A delivers a highly polished, rhetorically sharp case about adversarial gaming and the danger of "false grants," A fails to overcome B's core burden regarding uncorrectable false refusals. Side B's execution cost (three clipped turns) prevents a wider margin, but B's substantive victory on the central clash of error correction and superior performance in the pressure questions secures the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A1), which dismantles Side A's "file review" alternative by explaining that systemic errors (like a name-similarity score or an agency's bad translation) are invisible to the applicant without factor disclosure, leaving the human reviewer anchored to an unexplained flag.
