- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Both sides built a genuinely contested case around the definition of "main source," and B's convergent/divergent taxonomy was the sharpest conceptual move of the debate, landing real hits (the "reward thesis risk is a contradiction" point, the circularity of "who teaches the override," and the closing charge that CON wins only by redefining "main source" as "most comments"). But A answered the pressure questions directly, kept a coherent functional definition of "main source" (what students actually use while drafting) across every speech, and never let the practical bottleneck point drop: late, scarce human feedback cannot be the main developmental force under real classroom constraints. Crucially, B's turns were clipped for exceeding word limits four separate times, including the opening, both rebuttals, and the closing — a repeated execution failure that cost B exactly where closings and rebuttals are supposed to crystallize the clash (the closing was cut off mid-sentence on its final synthesizing line). A never suffered this cost and used its full space efficiently, including landing a coherent final answer to the "redefinition" charge. On balance, B had the more elegant framework, but A executed the format more reliably and gave the cleaner, uninterrupted path to a verdict, which under the judging frame's emphasis on deciding the whole debate and penalizing clipped turns tips a close contest to A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 point [B2.2]/[B2.4] — that A's "checkable" examples (prompt-fit, evidence, coherence) are mastery practice in disguise, and that "rewarding thesis risk" is definitionally incoherent for a rubric-driven tutor — was the single sharpest exchange, forcing A to spend its next two speeches defending the mastery/feedback boundary rather than expanding its case.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side B won by showing that draft-specific feedback on coherence, evidence, and prompt fit remains writing feedback rather than becoming mastery practice merely because it is checkable. Its human-framing, AI-drafting, human-judgment model answered A's strongest concern about machine authority while preserving the practical advantage of timely revision. A offered a memorable distinction between volume and load-bearing judgment, but narrowed “writing feedback” too aggressively and never fully defeated B's realistic classroom comparison. A's repeated clipped turns also imposed a meaningful execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In B's second rebuttal, the sequence of human framing, AI feedback during drafting, and later human judgment directly answered A's concern that students would optimize blindly for the machine. - **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO kept the motion's own distinction alive: convergent, checkable work vs divergent judgment about what a student is reaching for. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Both sides built real cases: CON's scarcity/revision-cycle argument was practically grounded and its pressure on PRO's mechanics-vs-substance line (exploiting PRO's own concession that AI may comment on real drafts) was the sharpest moment of the debate. But PRO never actually conceded the collapse — in Rebuttal 2 it answered the exact pressure question with a coherent, if abstract, threshold ("sets the substantive revision agenda" vs. mere mechanical support) and used a vivid, unrebutted example (the family-obligation essay) to show what a pattern-matching AI misses that CON's "floor beats no floor" reply never neutralized on its own terms. Execution also favored PRO: CON's opening and second rebuttal were both flagged as clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning arguments were literally cut off mid-thought twice, a real cost under the judging criteria, while PRO delivered complete, properly formatted speeches (including clean A1:/A2: answers) throughout. On balance, PRO's cleaner defense of its core distinction plus CON's execution lapses give PRO the edge, though the clash was genuinely competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's move in Rebuttal 1 (A2.3) seizing on PRO's concession that AI may inform feedback on real drafts, paired with PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer (A1/A2) drawing the "substantive revision agenda vs. mechanics" line — this exchange was the fulcrum of the debate, and PRO's answer, while imperfect, was the more complete response to the sharpest pressure raised.
