A also offered the stronger comparison for poor, uncharismatic communities that private donors may neglect. B's transmission and budget-risk challenges kept the debate competitive, and A incurred execution costs from three clipped turns and prematurely alleging silence, but those issues did not overcome A's clearer fit with the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to the custody argument was decisive: scarce private funding also ranks communities, but does so through donor taste, whereas a public fund can use transparent criteria while communities control materials and preserve competing accounts. B's proposed state-capitalized community endowment then substantially converged with that model.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing effectively framed CON's proposed state-funded "community endowment" as a concession of the motion's core premise.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A wins by decisively exposing the practical flaws in Side B's "many hands" charity model, particularly for impoverished minority communities that lack wealthy patrons or diaspora networks. A's pressure questions were sharper, and A successfully trapped B into conceding that the state must provide capital, effectively neutralizing B's semantic distinction between a standing public fund and a state-capitalized community endowment.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it answers B's pressure questions by explaining that public funds can rank by urgency rather than donor taste, and then turns the pressure back on B for failing to name a private actor who would sustainably fund a tiny, marginalized community for twenty years.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by separating public funding from state ownership: communities can retain custody and initiative while receiving durable support. Its public-goods case, cumulative-preservation argument, and comparison against fragile charity remained intact. Side B raised real concerns about gatekeeping and living transmission, but never established that funding necessarily erodes ownership; Ireland showed that money alone is insufficient, not that standing community-directed funds are undesirable. Side B also overread “rather than” as requiring the exclusion of private participation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s “the fund is the paycheck, not the gatekeeper” response was decisive. It directly answered Side B’s state-custody objection, explained how public support enables community-led language nests and archives, and forced Side B to rely on an asserted tradeoff between money and ownership rather than demonstrating one.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by brilliantly turning Side B's defenses into concessions of Side A's core offense. When B defended the fund's legacy as 'banked recordings' and a 'floor,' A used the motion's text ('rather than') to prove B was running away from the state taking over responsibility, and proved that B's 'banked' legacy was exactly the 'embalming' (shelves, not speakers) that A warned about from the start.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 and A5 identifying B's 'what gets banked stays banked' defense as a direct concession to A's 'embalming' argument.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A built a more resilient case by clearly distinguishing between financial responsibility and operational gatekeeping, effectively neutralizing Side B's state-overreach arguments. Side A's weighing of worst-case scenarios (banked memory vs. total extinction) and sharp exposure of the flaws in Side B's reliance on private philanthropy secured the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Side B's pressure questions, particularly dismantling the "plural sources" argument by highlighting that private rejection lacks the transparency and appeal of a statutory fund, and framing "diversified fragility" as still being fragility.
