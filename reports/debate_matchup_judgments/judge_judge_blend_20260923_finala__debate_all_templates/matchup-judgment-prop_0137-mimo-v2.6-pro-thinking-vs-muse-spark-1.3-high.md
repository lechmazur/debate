### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): The debate turned on whether restricting "flagship" status is functionally a concealment (B's position) or merely a marketing-narrative restriction that leaves coverage, recruiting mentions, and the offer conversation intact (A's position). A's clearest win came in Rebuttal 2: B had argued in Rebuttal 1 that "a headline costs nothing," intending to show no budget tradeoff and thus no poison pill — but A correctly flipped this into a fatal admission: if the flagship slot is costless in budget terms, then B's own "poison pill / smaller firms will drop the benefit" argument collapses, since restricting a costless slot costs nothing too. B never directly repaired this internal contradiction in Rebuttal 2 or Closing, instead re-asserting the discoverability claim and the alternative "we won't abandon you" story-reading, which left the contradiction unresolved. A also stayed closer to the motion's literal wording (marketing as flagship, not banning or hiding the benefit), while B's "page 47 / gag rule" framing subtly treated any demotion as total concealment, which is a broader claim than the text supports. B's vagueness objection to "strong" support was real pressure, but A blunted it by noting flagship selection is itself a judgment call, and B never escalated past reasserting vagueness. A's two clipped speeches are a real execution cost, and B's emotional framing ("map not marketing") was rhetorically effective, but it never neutralized the decisive self-contradiction A exposed, giving A the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 point (A4.5) turning B's own "a headline costs nothing" concession into a refutation of B's poison-pill argument — B never directly repaired this contradiction in the following speeches.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side B won by consistently distinguishing access and visibility from flagship status. Side A repeatedly treated the proposition as requiring concealment on page 47, even after B expressly allowed promotion in recruiting materials and benefit discussions. A raised legitimate concerns about discoverability and the vague meaning of strong support, but never proved that egg freezing uniquely needs the top headline rather than clear promotion. B more convincingly framed flagship marketing as an employer signal that is inappropriate when support for actually raising families remains thin. B's two clipped turns were execution costs, but its decisive reasoning remained visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was the response to A's claim that a headline costs nothing. B agreed, then flipped the point: if the headline is not a budget expense, its significance is precisely the story the employer chooses to tell. That undercut A's crowd-out objection while sharpening the motion's focus on flagship signaling rather than coverage.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively demonstrating the practical necessity of 'flagship' marketing, arguing that rare and expensive benefits must be loud to act as a 'map' for job seekers who cannot see benefit handbooks before applying.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4.1/A5.2 takedown of Side B's 'handbook distinction,' pointing out that no job seeker hunts through page 47 of a PDF they cannot see, effectively collapsing Side B's claim that discovery would be preserved without headline marketing.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly within the motion's terms, but CON built the tighter, more sustained pressure campaign. CON's central attack — that PRO never supplies a mechanism by which restricting "flagship" marketing actually redirects money toward leave or childcare ("a billboard is not a budget line") — went largely unanswered beyond an assertion that "reputation is a budget strategy," which was never substantiated with a causal chain. CON also exploited PRO's own concession at A4.3 ("if that means no firm earns that flagship... that is exactly the point") to show the rule's realistic output is simply fewer visible benefits with no compensating gain, and PRO's closing never rebutted that framing, just restated the moral slogan. PRO's strongest original point — the class divide between professionals who can use freezing and hourly workers who cannot — was raised in the opening but never developed or defended against CON's pressure in later rounds. CON's pressure questions (on take-up/awareness and the undefined "strong" standard) landed on live weaknesses that PRO only partially resolved. CON did suffer a real execution cost: both its opening and Rebuttal 2 were clipped for exceeding the word limit, which is a legitimate penalty, but the substantive content that was visible remained sharper and more responsive than PRO's repeated restatements ("fund the crib before you market the freezer").
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's "a billboard is not a budget line" rebuttal (B2.4-B2.5, reinforced in B4.5 and B5.1), which forced PRO to assert an unproven reallocation mechanism it never actually demonstrated, combined with CON's use of PRO's own concession at A4.3 to show the rule's realistic output is subtraction without addition.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by consistently distinguishing access to egg freezing from branding it as the premier path for women. Side A compellingly argued that restricting promotion would not automatically fund leave and could reduce awareness, but it repeatedly treated loss of flagship status as loss of coverage. Side B answered that neutral information and coverage could remain while employers were denied family-friendly credit for privileging delay over present caregiving support. That independent signaling and pressure harm sustained the proposition even where B's reallocation mechanism was uncertain. Side A's two clipped turns were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's portal-versus-billboard distinction was decisive. It directly answered Side A's claim that the policy destroys a useful option: ordinary access can remain, while flagship branding uniquely endorses postponement and communicates workplace priorities.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's causal mechanism. While Side B argued eloquently that restricting the marketing of egg freezing would force employers to invest in real leave, Side A proved that suppressing a cheap benefit does not manifest an expensive one. Instead, the benefit simply loses visibility and funding, leaving women with one fewer tool and no new support.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's decisive argument in Rebuttal 2 and Closing that Side B's rule relies on "deprivation to shame employers into generosity," correctly noting that corporate credibility would simply migrate to another cheap symbol rather than reallocating funds to paid leave.
