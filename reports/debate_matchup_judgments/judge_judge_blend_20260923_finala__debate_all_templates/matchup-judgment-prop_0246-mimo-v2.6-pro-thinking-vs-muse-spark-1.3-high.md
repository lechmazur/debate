### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides built vivid, plausible causal stories, but CON's case proved more resilient under pressure. CON consistently located the actual site of harm (the chronological mentions inbox/raid) and forced PRO to defend an idealized counterfactual platform that keeps notifications, trending lists, quote-posts, and suggested follows but somehow lacks the one feature (ranking) that resurrects and amplifies content — a point PRO never squarely answered in its closing. CON's "sort order vs. social architecture" framing and historical anchor (chronological forums/early Twitter being vicious) sharpened the comparison and was never fully dismantled. PRO's rebuttals were rhetorically strong (bus of strangers, stadium event, gasoline metaphors) and landed real hits on the "brake" argument via profit motive, but PRO's closing retreated to reasserting its central thesis rather than re-engaging CON's strongest late points (fictional restraint, substitution effect, group-chat-organized raids), leaving CON's last word more decisive and better crystallized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2), arguing that chronology's "structural restraint" is fictional because notifications, trending, and quote-posts survive the motion and can still resurrect content — a point PRO never directly answered in closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A won by defending the motion's modest comparative burden: chronology need not eliminate raids or anonymity, only reduce toxicity by adding friction and withholding automatic mass distribution. B compellingly showed that harassment predates ranking and that chronology preserves many toxic affordances, but repeatedly demanded that ranking be the master or decisive cause. B never adequately answered A's distinction between the chronological inbox where abuse arrives and the algorithmic system that recruits and rewards a much larger mob.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal most decisively answered that “nothing stops every raid” because the motion promises less toxicity, not zero toxicity, then contrasted existing harassment channels with algorithmic recruitment, persistence, and cross-tribal amplification. This neutralized B's strongest inbox argument and exposed its excessive burden.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won by successfully proving that algorithmic ranking is the decisive accelerant for toxicity due to its mass distribution of outrage. Side A's distinction between the "mailbox" and the "machine handing out the address" decisively neutralized Side B's strongest argument about chronological mentions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 effectively dismantled Side B's mentions-inbox point by arguing that while the inbox is chronological, the algorithm is what delivers the mob's attention to the target in the first place.
