### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases, but CON (A) more successfully narrowed the dispute to a defensible middle position: it conceded that locally-stored diagnostic access and fault-code clearing must survive a lapsed connectivity plan, while distinguishing that concession from ongoing backend authentication/pairing services that require continuing infrastructure and security labor. This let A directly answer PRO's pressure question with a concrete forbidden practice (A4.2), something PRO's closing (B5) never acknowledged or rebutted, leaving PRO's "CON forbids nothing" charge stale and unresponsive to the actual state of the clash. PRO's core rebuttal—that any charge for restoring repair capability is a "tollbooth" on already-owned hardware—was rhetorically vivid but never cleanly refuted A's key logical point that necessity for a repair does not automatically entail free provision forever (A4.3); PRO's answer to CON's pressure question ("No—may not charge per use... price lifecycle support into the product") committed to an absolute position that A then exposed as forcing uncompensated indefinite labor and cross-subsidization, a cost PRO minimized rather than substantively answered. PRO's analogies (washing machine, key) were memorable but weaker under scrutiny than CON's distinction between static hardware and live backend verification. Both used the required Q/A format correctly, but CON's final speech engaged more directly with the live crux (charge vs. free, necessity vs. entitlement), making the debate easier to decide in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3's "necessity never proves they must be supplied without charge" directly punctured PRO's "functional necessity" test (B4.1), and PRO's closing failed to answer it or acknowledge CON's concrete forbidden-practice answer from A4.2, leaving that exchange unresolved in CON's favor.
