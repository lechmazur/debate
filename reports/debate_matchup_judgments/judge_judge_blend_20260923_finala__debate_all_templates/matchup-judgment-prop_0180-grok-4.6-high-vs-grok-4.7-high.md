### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but B maintained a tighter, more consistent causal thread: cheap intervention does not automatically produce canopy or forest structure, and "steering" abandoned land is not the same as delivering growth. B's sharpest moment came in Rebuttal 2/A1, calling out that A's pressure question ("if those hectares hold more carbon and habitat in total, have you won?") smuggled the conclusion it needed to prove — a real logical gap A never closed. A's case relied on the assumption that succession reliably converts released hectares into carbon- and biodiversity-rich land on the marginal, often dry or isolated European/North American farmland actually at issue, but A conceded in closing that "where seed cannot travel, neither path is free forest," without ever establishing that succession's mechanism holds on the land B kept pointing to (Mediterranean margins, dry hill pastures). B's recurring, vivid lines ("a fence does not put rings on a stem," "steering is not growth," "extra area is not extra tons") kept forcing A back onto the unproven assumption that abandonment reliably becomes forest, and A's later rounds mostly reasserted the per-dollar leverage framing rather than answering that gap. A's opening and initial framing were strong, and the biodiversity clash (common early-successional stages vs. scarce woodland structure) remained genuinely contested without a clean winner, but B's superior handling of the core mechanism dispute and cleaner closing gave it the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1: "The question smuggles the conclusion... Extra area is not extra tons") directly exposed a circularity in A's Pressure Question and reframed the entire debate around whether released land actually converts to carbon/biodiversity gains, a challenge A never fully answered.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by maintaining a relentless focus on the 'per dollar' constraint, effectively proving that the massive cost leverage of passive recovery over a wide area generates more total biomass and varied habitat than expensive, active planting on a fraction of the land. Side A's rhetoric was excellent ("A fence does not put rings on a stem"), but its logic ultimately relied on the overly strict binary that unplanted land yields zero carbon and zero biodiversity value.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side A's 'empty ground' argument by pointing out that scrub is biomass and that planting only buys a young cohort, flattening the messy successional stages that also provide valuable habitat.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): CON undermined PRO's core mechanism by showing that cheaply released hectares often stall into bracken, scrub, or grassland, so they do not deliver the promised forest. By conceding soil carbon rebounds under both paths, CON narrowed the clash to canopy wood and woodland biodiversity, where active planting wins the per-dollar comparison if the alternative never closes. PRO was forced to retreat to narrower favorable conditions, weakening the broad claim, while CON's memorable refrains crystallized that PRO never proved delivery.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's sustained dismantling of PRO's "leverage" framing by insisting that extra hectares are not extra tons when succession stalls, crystallized in lines like "a fence does not put rings on a stem" and "leverage without a forest is cheaper emptiness."
