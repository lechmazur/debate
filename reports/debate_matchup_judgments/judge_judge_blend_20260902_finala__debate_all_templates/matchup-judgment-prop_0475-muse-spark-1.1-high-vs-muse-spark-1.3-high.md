### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but B's case held up better under sustained pressure. B's key strategic move—reframing the comparison from "fermentation vs. the idealized grass-fed cow" to "fermentation vs. the feedlot/dairy system that subsidies actually fund"—effectively neutralized A's central shield (rangeland pastoralists), forcing A to retreat mid-debate from defending pastoralists to defending generic "mixed farms," a visible goalpost shift under pressure. B also answered A's scaling/contamination fear with a grounded, ordinary-knowledge analogy (beer, cheese enzymes, insulin already run sterile fermentation at industrial scale in parallel tanks), and A never directly re-engaged that rebuttal in later rounds, leaving it live for B. A's case was solid on land/energy accounting and offered concrete methane-mitigation alternatives (3-NOP, digesters, genetics), but leaned on some unsupported precision ("pharma fermentation learning curves flat for years," exact reduction percentages) and did not fully recover the pastoralist framing once B exposed it as a mismatch with where subsidy dollars actually go. B did suffer a real execution cost from a clipped rebuttal, but the substance lost there was largely restated and defended later, limiting the damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A2), where the beer/cheese/insulin analogy directly answered A's "one phage trashes 100,000 liters" scaling attack, combined with B's reframe that subsidies target feedlot beef/dairy rather than rangeland pastoralists—an argument A never fully rebutted after initially raising pastoralists as its main shield.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): B won by distinguishing marginal grazing from the feedlot, dairy, and feed-crop systems that subsidy reform could actually target. A raised serious cost, feedstock, contamination, and rural-resilience concerns, but repeatedly treated a major shift as wholesale abandonment and relied on several unsupported claims about current costs, global cattle diets, and fermentation performance. Despite its clipped first rebuttal, B offered the clearer long-run comparison: fermentation can improve with cleaner power and scale, whereas conventional livestock retains inherent methane and land burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.2 was decisive: B conceded that fermentation need not outperform cattle on otherwise unusable scrub, then redirected the motion toward subsidized intensive production and answered the single-vat failure argument with existing parallel-tank fermentation and shared infrastructure. A never fully overturned that targeted comparison.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully dismantling Side B's "pastoralist and grass-fed" shield, accurately pointing out that the vast majority of agricultural subsidies fund intensive feedlots and commodity crops, not arid rangeland herders. Side A's rebuttal on feedstock efficiency—that a cow burns vastly more calories just staying alive than a yeast vat, making precision fermentation far more land-efficient even accounting for sugar inputs—was the decisive clash. Side B's reliance on incremental tweaks (methane inhibitors) effectively conceded the long-term structural trajectory to Side A, and Side B struggled to defend the status quo subsidy distribution against Side A's sharper systemic critique. Side A's minor clipping in Rebuttal 1 was a negligible execution cost that did not impact the core arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's reframing of the feedstock argument in Rebuttal 1 ("farming far less corn to feed vats instead of far more corn to feed animals plus the animal") and the subsequent dismantling of the pastoralist shield in Rebuttal 2.
