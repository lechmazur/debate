### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but B's mechanism story was more complete and better defended under pressure. B's core claim—that strikes trade reversible physical damage for irreversible losses (inspections, trust, hardliner empowerment, proliferation incentive)—went substantially unanswered, especially the historical-precedent point that regimes which gave up weapons programs fell while nuclear states were never struck. A never directly rebutted this. B also exploited A's own concessions (that delay is "temporary" and further force only a "possibility") to argue A's plan is either open-ended preventive war or empty threat, a sharp catch that A's Rebuttal 2 answer ("cameras observe, they do not compel") did not fully neutralize. A's asymmetry rebuttal ("not every militia rocket forces an American escalatory step") felt thinner than B's credibility-auction mechanism, and A's Rebuttal 1 was clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost. B's closing inversion of the "reversibility" frame A introduced ("rubble is the most reversible outcome of all") was the most memorable and logically forceful moment of the debate, crystallizing the clash in B's favor. A's case remained plausible but never fully closed the gaps B kept pressing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 point [B4.3]—turning A's admission that delay is "only temporary" and further force merely "a possibility" into proof that A's plan is either endless re-bombing or an empty threat—was the most decisive exchange, and A's closing never fully recovered from it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively exposing a contradiction in Side A's long-term strategy. Side A argued that the temporary delay bought by strikes could be managed afterward by diplomacy and sanctions, but Side B successfully countered that bombing destroys the very monitoring and political leverage required for those tools to work, trading temporary physical delay for permanent strategic blindness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal cleverly flipped Side A's reliance on future diplomacy and sanctions, asking why the U.S. would first destroy the inspections those tools rely on ("vandalism followed by a plea to negotiate in the rubble").
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by establishing a cleaner comparative frame: the physical damage from strikes is temporary and reversible, while the strategic damage—collapsed monitoring, blinded intelligence, discredited pragmatists, and a galvanized hard-line sprint for a bomb—is lasting and structurally worse. B effectively exposed a tension in A's case: if delay is temporary, A either commits to open-ended preventive war or falls back on diplomacy and sanctions under worse conditions than before. A's "cameras observe; they do not compel" was turned effectively by B into an argument that observation enables precisely targeted compulsion, and that destroying inspections forces future strikes to fly blind. A's regime-survival calculus was answered by B's credible "credibility auction" frame, showing how domestic political imperatives on both sides structurally trap them into escalation after blood is drawn. A mounted a respectable case about buying time and degrading integrated capabilities, but could not fully neutralize B's core claim that the strikes manufacture the larger danger they claim to prevent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4 turning A's "cameras observe; they do not compel" into an argument that blinding ourselves eliminates the very precision that makes compulsion possible, forcing heavier, wider future strikes.
