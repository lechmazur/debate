- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON built the stronger overall case by repeatedly forcing comparison on marginal effects at universal scale, documented fadeout in real programs like Tennessee/Head Start, and substitution deadweight, while PRO relied on limited or boutique examples and reframed "lasting gains" without squarely meeting the "justify universal cost" threshold; clipped later speeches for A compounded execution weakness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 format) directly dismantled PRO's unfalsifiability claim and distal-outcome pivot by re-emphasizing marginal arithmetic and consistent evidence inconsistency at scale.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by exposing a fatal external validity gap in PRO's evidence and a structural cost-benefit flaw in universality itself. PRO's strongest returns came from tiny, intensive, targeted experiments that bear little resemblance to scaled universal programs, and PRO conceded that no large universal system has yet measured Perry/Abecedarian-scale returns. CON's deadweight-loss argument—showing that universality spreads costs across children already attending preschool while gains remain concentrated among the disadvantaged—directly undermined the motion's core cost-benefit test. PRO never offered evidence on the marginal effect for the marginal child, leaving their economic justification extrapolated and unmoored. While PRO landed effective methodological critiques and an unfalsifiability charge, CON answered with a clear falsifiability standard and showed that the evidentiary inconsistency itself is the case against universality. The arithmetic of substitution proved more durable than PRO's appeals to political durability and distal outcomes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dismantling of PRO's cost-benefit case through the deadweight-loss / marginal-child argument, showing that universal subsidies mostly capture families already purchasing preschool and collapsing the return-per-dollar ratio, which PRO never answered with evidence on marginal effects.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by rigorously anchoring the debate to the motion's specific burden: justifying the universal cost. While PRO offered strong rhetorical framing around developmental mechanisms, distal outcomes, and political sustainability, they never adequately solved the marginal-child arithmetic or provided large-scale longitudinal ROI data, ultimately conceding the evidentiary gap under pressure. CON's responses were direct, their weighing consistently tied back to the proposition's exact wording, and their closing crystallized the clash decisively around the word "justify."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 handling of the substitution problem and falsifiability pressure. CON directly answered PRO's questions, set a clear evidentiary bar, and delivered the decisive weighing line: "Targeting captures the benefit. Universality captures the benefit plus a transfer." This reduced PRO's case to an unsolved arithmetic problem that PRO's subsequent speeches could not overcome.
