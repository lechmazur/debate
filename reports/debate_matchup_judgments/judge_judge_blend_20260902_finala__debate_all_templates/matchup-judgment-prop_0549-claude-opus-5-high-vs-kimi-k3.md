### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): A won by pairing demonstrated, direct mortality mechanisms with a stronger five-year comparison. B sharply exposed naloxone's witness limitation and the difficulty of reaching unaware users, but never established that expanded interdiction would reliably reduce synthetic fentanyl availability rather than reroute supply or induce substitution. B's heroin-drought example was distinguishable, while its attribution of the current decline to supply changes remained speculative. A also preserved substantial treatment headroom that B's focus on one unaware-user archetype did not outweigh.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's unwitnessed-overdose attack was the cleanest hit: A's references to supervised sites, test strips, and jail treatment did not fully answer the teenager alone at home. However, A's broader reply that interdiction successes merely reroute synthetic supply was more decisive on the proposition's aggregate comparison.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): PRO wins the central weighing: over five years treatment scale-up has direct mortality evidence and clear headroom in jails and low-threshold buprenorphine, while CON's supply lever lacks any sustained historical mortality decline in the synthetic era. B's best hits are the unwitnessed-overdose witness problem and the unaware teenager, but A answers the China 2019 reroute, distinguishes Australia's crop-based drought from lab-regenerating fentanyl, and frames enforcement as tested at full force while MAT was not, giving a cleaner comparative path to vote PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B1/B2 flip on witness requirement — naloxone is not a shield, it needs someone awake nearby, and majority of fatal overdoses are unwitnessed, directly undermining A2.3 and forcing A to retreat to two supervised sites and test strips.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side A won by decisively shifting the debate's focal point from the chronic, willing user to the unaware, unwitnessed user (e.g., the teenager taking a counterfeit pill). Side A brilliantly exposed the structural flaw in Side B's harm reduction case: naloxone requires a witness, and test strips/supervised consumption sites require the user to know they are using drugs. Side A also neutralized Side B's "iron law of prohibition" argument by distinguishing between powder potency and the physical manufacturing of counterfeit pills via pill presses. Side B's reliance on supervised consumption sites to save a teenager dying alone in his bedroom was a fatal unforced error that Side A exploited perfectly in the final speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's claim that naloxone reaches the unaware user by highlighting the absolute necessity of a witness, culminating in the devastating closing line: "Naloxone without his consent — but not without a witness, and he has none at midnight."
