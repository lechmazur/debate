### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by converting the clash into a cleaner comparison between a reliability floor that mandates standby utilization and vouchers that pay for actual trips. Early B turns were clipped, but the surviving economic mechanism and later weighing still left a more decisive path for CON than A's repeated bluff-call and circular claim that vouchers secretly need the floor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 answers): vouchers buy paid trips; the floor buys idle standby hours — dissolving A's core contradiction and locking the weighing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won the central economic clash. A pressed a contradiction between vouchers being cheap and the floor being costly; B's R2 resolved it cleanly as trip vs standby - vouchers pay only when revenue exists, a floor pays for idle hours between sparse rides. That distinction sustains B's leveling-down-by-growth-path mechanism (300 vs 800) and undermines A's call-the-bluff and pennies-per-mile claims, while A's supply-needed-for-vouchers reply never cures the inter-trip idle cost. Despite clipped openings, B's weighing of scale/safety vs wait-time ratio was clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1-A3 dissolving the cheap-vs-costly contradiction by separating paying for a trip from paying for standby idle hours, then linking it to total fleet shrinkage.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A won by decisively exploiting the tension in Side B's voucher alternative. While Side B offered a strong economic distinction between marginal trip costs and standby costs, Side A's closing effectively demonstrated that vouchers rely on the very supply-side presence the mandate provides; without it, a voucher merely buys a long wait, failing the motion's "reliability" standard. Side A also handled pressure questions better, providing a concrete regulatory definition and dismantling Side B's dilemma, whereas Side B's clipped turns slightly hindered its otherwise strong execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which exposed the contradiction in Side B's claim that vouchers make trips cheap while mandates burn runway, culminating in the sharp observation that a voucher without mandated local supply is just a coupon for a distant car.
