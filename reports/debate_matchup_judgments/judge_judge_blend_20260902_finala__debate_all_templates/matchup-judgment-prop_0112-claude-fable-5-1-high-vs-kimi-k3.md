### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. B created real pressure through queues, budget concentration, and the distinction between coverage and timely treatment. But B's preferred employer model depended on public backstops, subsidized continuation, and capped cost-sharing that substantially break the work-status tie named in the proposition. A also preserved largely unanswered advantages on administrative simplification, churn, job lock, and recession-synchronized coverage loss. B's claim that public budgets necessarily contract with tax receipts was too mechanical, while A plausibly explained deficit spending and political accountability. B's clipped second rebuttal was a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was the response that B's portability fixes concede the need to detach basic access from employment, combined with the “invisible queue” argument that price-excluded patients must count alongside patients waiting in public systems. This prevented B's Canadian-waits comparison from deciding the debate by itself.

PRO’s sharpest pressure—that CON’s patches are tax-funded government guarantees—was answered as a cheaper targeted net plus competing buyers for capacity/speed, while CON’s delivery/queue and recession-scale arguments remained livelier than PRO’s wallet-queue reply. PRO was more memorable on floor-vs-net and invisible exclusion, but repeatedly stretched “single-payer” into any public guarantee, which blurred the proposition; CON’s clipped R2 was a real cost but did not erase the clearer comparative path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.3 catching B’s Germany move as redefinition: a public backstop does not convert work-linked multipayer financing into single-payer, so security no longer forces SP.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO maintained that the proposition targets systems where job loss threatens access, and showed CON's own fixes are government guarantees that make the employer link redundant.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 turn that Germany/Switzerland work precisely because they untether access via state guarantees, so CON's portability defense proves PRO's point and leaves employer link with no distinct value.
