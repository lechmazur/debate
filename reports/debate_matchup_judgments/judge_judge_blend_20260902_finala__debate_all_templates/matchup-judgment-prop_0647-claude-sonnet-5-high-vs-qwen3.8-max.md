### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won by locking the debate to the resolution's frequency/default test for ordinary cities, not showcase exceptions. PRO's induced-demand and opportunity-cost case was coherent, but its examples repeatedly dissolved under pressure into earthquake opportunism or capacity-preserving rebuilds; CON used that consistently to show removal is resource-heavy and non-routine while retrofit/manage stays inside the existing right-of-way. The clipped A rebuttal was a cost, yet CON still made the comparative choice clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON flipping PRO's Big Dig/Embarcadero examples into proof that real demand forces capacity preservation, not deletion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO cannot meet the burden of proving removal should be the *routine* choice rather than the careful exception. CON effectively neutralized PRO's flagship examples: Embarcadero was a post-earthquake anomaly, and the Big Dig preserved (and even expanded) highway capacity underground at massive cost—proving that when real regional demand exists, cities pay dearly to avoid losing throughput. CON also carved out a compelling "retrofit" middle ground (managed lanes, transit conversion, tolling) that undermined PRO's forced dichotomy, showing that preserving and reallocating existing right-of-way is safer for ordinary cities than destruction followed by uncertain redesign. PRO's induced-demand critique of widening remained live, but they never overcame CON's equity and feasibility concerns: removal exports traffic to adjacent surface streets in neighborhoods least equipped to absorb it, and PRO's "design can fix it" response assumed resources and political capital most cities lack. The decisive frame—"which default is safe across ordinary cities?"—favored CON and was never answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turning of the Big Dig example and establishment of the retrofit middle ground. CON showed PRO's cited "removal" success actually preserved capacity underground at astronomical cost, and that managed-lane retrofits are neither widening nor removal—making PRO's frequency claim collapse into an argument for modification rather than destruction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON (Side A) won the decisive example and framework clash.
