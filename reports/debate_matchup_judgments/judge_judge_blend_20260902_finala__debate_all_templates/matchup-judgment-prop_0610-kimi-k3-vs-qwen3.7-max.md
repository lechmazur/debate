### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by showing that installation-level accounting requires real cleaner production and by weighing temporary, self-harming retaliation against persistent leakage and erosion of the EU carbon price. Side A repeatedly asserted that upgrading an EU-serving plant leaves total emissions unchanged, but never supplied the missing causal step: upgrading production lowers emissions unless dirtier output elsewhere expands correspondingly. Its claims of catastrophic and permanent geopolitical damage were also more asserted than demonstrated. B incurred some execution cost from two clipped turns, but still resolved the central clashes more clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the shell-game argument was decisive: A conceded that firms would upgrade facilities serving Europe, yet continued claiming total emissions remain exactly unchanged. B exposed that inconsistency and used installation-level accounting to answer mere relabeling, while A never established why cleaner new capacity would necessarily cause equal additional dirty production elsewhere.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the cleaner path by treating the motion's "even if retaliate" clause as the real weighing rule and showing temporary, self-harming, negotiable friction versus permanent leakage that guts the internal carbon price. Side A raised real geopolitical and Global-South risks but never neutralized the capital-stock/incentive logic that clean capacity once built is not permanently duplicated, nor supplied a credible non-leverage alternative beyond status-quo multilateralism. Side B's progressive narrowing and storm-vs-leak weighing decided a competitive exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's repeated takedown of CON's shell-game (esp. B2.1 + B3.1 Q1 + B4.1–4.2 on installation-level certificates and costly permanent dual lines)
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): PRO wins the central weighing the motion demands.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's [A2.1]/[A5.3] turn of CON's shell-game admission – that diverting "cleanest batches" to Europe concedes a clean plant gets built, which is success not failure, and that permanent parallel dirty lines are economically implausible – directly undercuts B's core atmospheric-equivalence claim.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by showing that Side B's benefits depended on an inadequately defended chain: broad carbon accounting must work, partners must adopt sufficiently equivalent pricing, and retaliation must then fade. Side B repeatedly asserted that adaptation was rational and inevitable without establishing why domestic politics, legal disputes, and measurement problems would not sustain retaliation. Side A also offered a narrower commodity mechanism and sectoral agreements as a more proportionate alternative. A's clipped turns count against execution, and B correctly identified downstream leakage through finished goods, but B never weighed that risk convincingly against the costs of applying uncertain defaults across most industrial imports.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange attacked the claimed off-ramp from retaliation: Side B answered the pressure question with indirect policy changes rather than an example of a major economy adopting equivalent carbon pricing under foreign pressure. A then argued that without an effective off-ramp, B's supposedly temporary retaliation could become persistent. The claimed concession was somewhat overstated, since equivalent policy need not be an identical tax, but it exposed B's unsupported assumption that trade pressure would reliably produce compliance.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON built the cleaner comparative path: certain retaliation and unworkable breadth for complex goods versus PRO's speculative leakage fix and temporary-friction claim. CON neutralized the off-ramp by forcing PRO's own concession that partners may not adopt equivalent pricing, while defending the existing narrow commodity mechanism as a working alternative that already captures most embedded carbon. PRO stayed too reliant on rational adaptation and hand-waved measurement/default-value problems. Clipped CON openings cost some execution points but did not erase the sharper later weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2 / Closing capture of PRO's A1 concession that partners "may not adopt an identical European tax," collapsing the temporary-friction premise.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON wins the decisive tradeoff. PRO's case needed partners to avoid the fee by pricing carbon domestically and to accept default-value accounting, with retaliation fading quickly. Under pressure B showed the off-ramp requires importing Brussels-level energy costs - a political loser - and PRO conceded in A4.1 partners "may not adopt an identical European tax," making the fee and retaliation permanent. B's upstream point - that existing narrow CBAM on steel/aluminum/cement already captures most embedded carbon and complex-goods competitiveness turns on tech not marginal carbon costs - meant PRO's expansion to "most" goods multiplies fraud, disguised tariffs and legal exposure for little extra coverage. Certain retaliation vs speculative dominoes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 A1-A2/A4-A5: turning PRO's "may not adopt identical tax" admission into proof the off-ramp stays shut, so broad CBAM becomes permanent tariff and retaliation becomes permanent, while upstream pricing shows breadth adds costs not coverage.
