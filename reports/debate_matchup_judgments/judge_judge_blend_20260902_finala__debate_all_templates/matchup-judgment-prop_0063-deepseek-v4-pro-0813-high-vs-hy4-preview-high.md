PRO's repeated move—declaring any rule lacking those specifics "not clear enough" and thus outside the motion—functionally smuggled in a third guardrail the proposition doesn't mention, which is close to redefining the motion rather than defending it as written. CON's concrete recurring examples (ink, coffee Subscribe & Save, dog-food autoship, nonrefundable hotel) consistently exposed this gap and were never neutralized; PRO's answers to the pressure questions conceded the ambiguity issue rather than resolving it. CON's tap-as-last-checkpoint framing, reinforced across every rebuttal, gave the cleaner, more textually faithful path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1/A2.2 and B4.3's dog-food/autoship exchange: CON's example showed a rule satisfying "budget and merchant" yet producing an unconsented recurring charge, and PRO's answer had to declare the rule "not clear" for autoship — effectively conceding that the motion's stated criteria are insufficient without an unstated item-level guardrail.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal (A4.3/A4.4) was the decisive blow, dismantling Side B's attempt to bundle item-level specs into 'merchant rules' and proving that Side B was implicitly relying on a third guardrail absent from the proposition.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing that mandatory item-by-item confirmation does not deliver the meaningful consent Side B needs: reflexive tapping converts agent errors into approved errors without catching them, making the tap theater rather than a safeguard. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and with vivid, plausible examples, but CON (A) built the more decisive line of attack: it identified early that PRO's "clear budget and merchant rules" could not, on their own, catch shrinkflation, bundling, subscription traps, or nonrefundable terms, and pressed this through every round. PRO's repeated fix—adding baseline drift-detection, refundability checks, deposit checks, and comparison to the last approved purchase—kept expanding what counts as a "rule," which CON correctly flagged in the final rebuttal and closing as smuggling item-level contract review back in while still claiming to satisfy the motion's narrower wording. PRO's own answer to CON's Q2 (that when rules are silent the user simply "bears ordinary commercial risk") effectively conceded the exact harm CON was pointing to. CON also neutralized PRO's strongest practical objection (rubber-stamping and accessibility harms) by offering low-friction, prefilled, one-tap confirmation, preserving PRO's efficiency argument while keeping a human at contract formation. PRO's lock/vault/coin imagery and autopay analogy were rhetorically strong and kept the debate close, but the closing exchange left CON's motion-fidelity critique unanswered and more clearly resolved the central clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 [A2.1]-[A2.2], calling out that B's detergent fix required smuggling in unit size, bundle, and subscription terms not covered by "budget and merchant rules," set up the decisive theme that PRO's later expansions (baseline comparison, drift detection, refundability checks) were scope creep beyond the motion—crystallized devastatingly in A's closing [A5.1].
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by strictly enforcing the text of the motion. A demonstrated that "budget and merchant rules" cannot account for checkout-level changes like bundles, shrinkflation, or refundability. Side B attempted to survive this by adding "baseline comparison" and detailed product specifications, but A successfully framed this as a concession that the motion's actual rules are insufficient to replace final consent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal cleanly dismantles B's case by pointing out that B's reliance on "baseline drift detection" abandons the motion's limitation to "budget and merchant rules."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won because it successfully showed that user-set rules plus baseline comparison and automated escalation provide more systematic protection than item-by-item confirmation, which collapses into rubber-stamping or exclusionary friction. Side B&#39;s core objection—that budget and merchant rules are too thin—was answered by A&#39;s drift-detection mechanism, while B never adequately resolved the inattention problem in its own one-tap model. A crystallized the clash cleanly: control belongs at the rule layer, attention at exceptions. B&#39;s closing overstated the plan shift and understated the practical equivalence of one-tap and zero-tap for inattentive users.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A&#39;s second rebuttal (A4.1-A4.6), where it directly answered B&#39;s pressure questions by distinguishing bounded baseline comparison from an exhaustive contract specification, and then reversed B&#39;s safety argument by showing that a one-tap approval would not surface a buried nonrefundable clause to a skimming user, while change-detection would.
