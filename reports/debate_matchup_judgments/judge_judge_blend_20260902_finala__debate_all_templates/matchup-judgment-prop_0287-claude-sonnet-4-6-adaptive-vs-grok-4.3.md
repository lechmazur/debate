### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): B won by making the central clash about what democratic legitimacy for constitutional courts means, not just whether courts should be periodically updated. A had a plausible consent and rotation story, but leaned heavily on assertions that fixed vacancies become routine and that compliance improves when courts are reachable. B more directly attacked those mechanisms, arguing that scheduled turnover can normalize constitutional campaigning and that the legitimacy gain is illusory if courts are expected to stand outside electoral pressure. B also landed the stronger independence harm through the mandatory post-service audition window, while A repeatedly answered with reappointment and continued-tenure points that did not fully meet that concern. The debate remained competitive because A preserved a real predictable-turnover legitimacy case, but B gave the cleaner comparative reason that the asserted democratic gain was misdescribed and the independence cost was structural.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was on the audition problem: A argued post-service incentives already exist and reappointment is removed, but B sharply distinguished optional strategic retirement under lifetime tenure from a mandatory, public countdown under fixed terms, showing why fixed terms create a more systematic pressure point.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by proving a substantial legitimacy gain (lowering the existential stakes of appointments and ending dead-hand control) while effectively minimizing the independence harm through real-world examples (European courts). Side B offered a strong theoretical defense of the "audition problem," but struggled to overcome Side A's empirical counter and the framing that non-renewable terms still heavily insulate the actual decision-making process.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response explaining why a mandatory exit date creates a structurally guaranteed audition window compared to discretionary lifetime retirement was excellent, though Side A's framing of confirmation battles as bounded legislative business vs. zero-sum generational contests ultimately carried the comparative clash.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO won by better execution and cleaner comparative weighing. While CON offered a sophisticated theoretical framework that judicial authority requires insulation from electoral cycles, PRO successfully symmetricized the audition problem and showed lifetime tenure freezes democratic consent without eliminating post-service incentives. CON's clipped closing and repetitive R2 left its structural independence claim under-resolved next to PRO's accessible framing of bounded stakes and living consent. The net legitimacy gain remained the clearer live advantage after the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.1 distinction between self-limiting strategic retirement and the structurally guaranteed fixed-term audition window, which sharpened the independence cost despite PRO's symmetry claim.
