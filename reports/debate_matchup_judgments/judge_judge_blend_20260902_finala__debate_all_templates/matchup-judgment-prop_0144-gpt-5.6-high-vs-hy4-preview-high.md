### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively dismantling Side A's causal link between discovery route and vulnerability. Side B successfully argued that navigation friction filters by menu knowledge rather than resilience, and that determined searchers might actually be more vulnerable than casual browsers, undermining Side A's core claim that an opt-in requirement ensures informed consent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's point in B4.6 that search proves desire, not comprehension or resilience, directly neutralizing Side A's claim that making users search filters out the most vulnerable.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B kept the narrow proposition live: default auto-suggest is itself a system act that manufactures intent in new users who lack a model of synthetic intimacy, and opt-in preserves all legitimate uses at trivial cost. Side A’s best moves — benign time-limited scenes and conduct-based safeguards, plus the point that search filters by menu knowledge not vulnerability — were answered by B’s distinction between shrinking the unwitting at-risk pool ex ante versus governing behavior ex post. A never showed what an unrequested romantic prompt adds that intentional opt-in discovery does not, leaving B’s brake vs accelerant comparison more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4’s A2 pairing friction-shrinks-population with search-shows-motivation-not-resilience versus A4’s counter that desire does not equal comprehension — the central clash on whether default suppression is cosmetic or an ex ante brake.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A built a highly persuasive and logically tight case, effectively framing the default suggestion as an active mechanism for manufacturing intent rather than passive visibility. A's central dilemma—that opt-in is sufficient for benign features while defaults are dangerous for risky ones—neatly trapped Side B's conduct-focused safeguards. Side B fought well but struggled to justify why an uninterested newcomer needed to be auto-suggested a romantic feature rather than simply discovering it via opt-in.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they answered the pressure questions by establishing that friction shrinks the at-risk population before safeguards activate, and that a searching user's pre-existing motivation makes them more receptive to disclosures than a user caught by a default prompt on a whim.
