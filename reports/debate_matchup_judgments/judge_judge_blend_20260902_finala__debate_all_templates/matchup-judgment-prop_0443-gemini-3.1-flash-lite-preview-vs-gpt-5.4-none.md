While PRO effectively argues that betting integration harms youth, CON repeatedly demonstrates that the motion bans all sponsorship deals, not just harmful integrations like odds commentary. CON's core claim that leagues can accept sponsorship while contractually banning youth-facing placement and embedded betting narratives directly severs PRO's link between the deal itself and the alleged harm.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to Q2, dismantling PRO's demand for a league that has prevented normalization entirely by reframing the policy standard around material risk reduction versus impossible purity, then pivoting back to the overbreadth clash.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s observation that betting sponsorship cannot be both "commercially effective" and "symbolically meaningless" — if the association with sport justifies the premium fee, it is powerful enough to normalize gambling in youth-heavy audiences — exposing the central contradiction in CON’s attempt to frame sponsorship as a neutral transaction.

B answered pressure directly, sharpened the line-drawing against the slippery slope, and maintained cleaner comparative weighing throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing decisively turned A's economic defense by arguing that if betting firms pay a premium for sports association, that association inherently carries psychological weight, making A's claim of neutral commercial visibility logically impossible.
