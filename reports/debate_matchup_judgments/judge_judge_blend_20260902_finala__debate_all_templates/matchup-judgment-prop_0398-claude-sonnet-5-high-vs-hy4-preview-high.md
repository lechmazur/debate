- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won by defending a cleaner, more consistently applied standard: pay tracks the labor market that actually competes for the worker where they live. A’s either/or trap on geography (A2.1), equity answer rejecting indefinite above-market raises (A4.1–A4.2), and closing charge that B relabels the same local rate “market” for new hires but “monopsony” for movers (A5.1–A5.2) left B’s equal-output claim unstable.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing crystallization that B treats the local rate as legitimate market price for a new hire then as monopsony when applied to a mover doing identical work (A5.1–A5.2), which locked in the inconsistency after B’s entry-vs-incumbency defense.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing that Side A’s national-market premise does not justify preserving the highest-cost headquarters band. B consistently distinguished individual lifestyle choices from regional labor-market premiums and answered the savings and boundary objections. A’s attrition mechanism was plausible, but it never established that rivals would generally preserve coastal pay, and A misread B’s conditional discussion of a national rate as a concession abandoning location adjustment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s pressure question and second rebuttal created the decisive dilemma: if pay is regional, headquarters anchoring follows the wrong market; if pay is national, the national rate still need not equal the headquarters premium. Side A’s response attacked a concession B had not actually made.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that location-adjusted pay for relocated incumbents is market-irrational and leads to attrition. While Side B effectively neutralized A's "employer savings" argument with a strong principled defense of symmetric market pricing, B could not overcome A's "poaching discount" mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly used B's own Pressure Question about new hires to differentiate an incumbent's "revealed price" from a new hire's unproven value, demonstrating exactly why rivals will poach discounted movers.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by keeping the proposition’s real comparison live: HQ bands embed a location premium the mover no longer needs, so the default should unbundle that premium rather than freeze a double windfall. A’s pressure questions exposed CON’s soft spot—calling the market “national” while treating the highest-cost HQ band as the locked entitlement—and A’s A1/A2 answers plus closing unbundling frame resolved that clash more cleanly than B’s attrition/poaching story dissolved it. B landed sharper individual rebuttals (especially national-market challenge and cheap incumbent poaching), but the “PRO abandoned location-adjustment for my position” turn overclaimed A’s national-rate point and never fully justified why HQ bands specifically, rather than a lower remote clearing price, remain the fair general rule. A’s path on fairness, replacement cost, and remote-role sustainability therefore stayed easier to vote for after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Q1 forcing why a “national” market still freezes the highest HQ band, answered in A4.1–A4.2 and crystallized by the skill-vs-location unbundling in A’s closing; B’s R2 poaching/revealed-price reply was the strongest counterpunch but did not erase the HQ-benchmark incoherence.
