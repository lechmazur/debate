- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A won the comparative motion by showing that twenty years of additional construction would enlarge today's housing stock and exert at least some downward price pressure. Side A correctly weighed that surge as common to both counterfactuals and noted that even modest savings satisfy “more affordable.” The clipped rebuttal was a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange answered the in-migration objection through vacancy and marginal landlord competition: new units redirect some bidding away from existing homes unless migration demand is perfectly elastic. Side B asserted full absorption but never established it, leaving A's lower-price counterfactual intact.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side A won by maintaining a disciplined comparative frame that Side B never escaped. By leveraging Side B's concessions about a Miami "glut" and a potential "few percent" price trim, Side A proved that even under Side B's assumptions, the core burden of "more affordable" was met.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R2 handling of Miami, pointing out that a "glut" means supply lowered prices and that the city's current unaffordability would be even worse in the no-tower counterfactual.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO won the decisive comparative framing: the motion requires only more affordability, not solving today's crisis. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by consistently enforcing the correct counterfactual: a growing city with more legal housing versus that same city under tighter supply, not the city today versus itself in 2004. Side B showed that demand, credit cycles, land costs, and migration can blunt supply effects, but its examples of cities that built and still became expensive did not establish that they would have been equally affordable without those homes. Its land-value and induced-demand mechanisms also never demonstrated a net price increase sufficient to defeat the motion's modest directional claim. Side A combined the durable-stock argument with vacancy, filtering, and a strong cyclical-bust-versus-permanent-ban comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the Seattle, Denver, and Austin evidence was decisive: rising prices during strong construction primarily demonstrate surging demand, not that construction failed relative to the no-building counterfactual. Side B never cleanly closed that causal gap.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully defending the comparative nature of the counterfactual and proving that the durable housing stock built before the 2008 crash would exert a downward directional pressure on prices today. Side A's pressure question exposed a flaw in Side B's land-value mechanism, cornering Side B into admitting that banning construction does not lower prices, which left Side A's supply-vacancy mechanism as the decisive differentiator.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly neutralized the 2008 crash argument with 'busts are cyclical, bans are permanent,' solidifying the durable stock argument that won the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping the motion as a same-city counterfactual and making the durable-stock / permanent-ban versus cyclical-bust weighing decisive. CON landed real damage on legal≠built, GFC timing, sprawl-versus-density, and the missing American infill instance, and Rebuttal 1 being clipped cost execution; but CON too often treated “expensive versus 2004” and national demand shocks as if they answered “cheaper than without twenty years of legal denser supply,” while never establishing a net that overturns supply’s direction. PRO’s cleaner comparative frame and answers under pressure made the path to victory easier to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s neutralization of the crash (covers ~5 of 20 years; finished units still house people; busts cyclical, bans permanent) plus the one-way land-value pressure question that forced CON’s options asymmetry without restoring a supply-irrelevant story.
