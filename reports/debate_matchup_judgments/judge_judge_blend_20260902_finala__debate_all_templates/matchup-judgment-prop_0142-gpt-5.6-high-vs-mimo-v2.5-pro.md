### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by establishing a structural dilemma with the proposition's mechanism: labels will either be applied selectively (creating a false aura of authenticity for unlabeled images) or broadly (becoming meaningless background noise). Side A played into this dilemma by embracing defensive over-labeling, which Side B expertly exploited to prove the policy would fail. Side A also suffered significant execution costs by having four out of five speeches clipped for exceeding the word count.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which weaponized Side A's defense that over-labeling is "the opposite of a problem" to decisively prove that the proposed warning would lose all salience and communicative value.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON built the clearer path by forcing the debate onto efficacy and workability of this specific rule. PRO established vulnerability and a real gap in pure aspirational branding without product claims, but never showed the proposed labels reliably inform rather than normalize or create a false authenticity binary. CON's dilemma (over-labeling destroys salience; selective labeling certifies unlabeled constructed images) survived, and PRO's "more disclosure is fine" reply undercut its own mechanism. Vulnerability raised the bar for useful protection; it did not convert an unproven label into a moral default.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2/closing dilemma on over-labeling vs. certification effect after PRO called defensive over-labeling "the opposite of a problem."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON wins the central efficacy/workability clash. PRO argues vulnerability + gap (brand-equity retouching not covered by deception law) + light-touch disclosure, but CON's dilemma sticks: broad defensive labeling = background noise, selective labeling = false aura of authenticity for unlabeled yet constructed images. PRO's concession that over-labeling is "the opposite of a problem" destroys salience and leaves the vagueness of "heavy" and "aimed at minors" unresolved. B's certification-effect and normalized-enhancement mechanism answers A's pressure about being worse than zero-signal status quo, while PRO never shows a label reliably reduces comparison. Vulnerability alone doesn't justify symbolic regulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 / Closing dilemma - if label broadly, warning conveys nothing; if selectively, unlabeled pose/lighting/makeup/surgery images gain undeserved authenticity. Turns A's over-labeling defense into fatal concession and crystallizes why disclosure fails its own informational purpose.
