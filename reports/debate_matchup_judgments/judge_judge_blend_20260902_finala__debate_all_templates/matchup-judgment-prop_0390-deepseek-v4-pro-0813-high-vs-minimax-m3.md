### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON (A) built a cleaner, more consistent path to victory by maintaining a sharp distinction between guilty evaders and falsely accused players, and by relentlessly attacking the circularity of who defines a "serious" ban worthy of portability. The pivotal exchange came when A's pressure question forced PRO to concede that "too few" current platform rulings meet a serious-evidence standard [B4.4]. That concession undercut PRO's entire rescue strategy, since the motion as written asks whether bans should travel now, "even if that raises due-process concerns," not whether an idealized future evidentiary regime should exist first. PRO's closing never resolved this tension — it kept asserting a "properly gated regime" would emerge from portability's incentive pressure, but never explained who verifies seriousness today or why an issuing platform would absorb cost rather than export a flawed label for PR credit, a rebuttal A landed cleanly in [A2.5]-[A2.6] and never lost control of. PRO's incentive-flip argument (platforms get "skin in the game") was clever and original but stayed asserted rather than demonstrated, and A's counter-mechanism (platforms capture enforcement credit while externalizing false-positive costs onto players and rival platforms) was at least as plausible and never neutralized. PRO's rebuttals were also clipped twice for exceeding word limits, a real execution cost that cut off developing arguments before they landed. A's case was more internally consistent across rounds and more responsive to the pressure it received.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 pressure question ("which actual platform ruling today meets that standard?") forced B's concession in [B4.4] that too few current rulings meet the serious-evidence bar — a concession A then used in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to show the motion, as written, would make today's opaque rulings portable rather than some idealized future regime.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the motion as written front and center: more portability under current opaque private moderation multiplies unreviewed error before proof. PRO’s serial-tourist opening and incentive story were strong and memorable, but PRO repeatedly leaned on an idealized “properly gated” seriousness standard after conceding few current rulings meet it, and paid execution costs from two clipped rebuttals plus a muddled A1/A2 answer pass. CON’s cleaner distinction between guilty evasion and false-positive recovery, plus direct answers on blast radius and over-ban incentives, made the decisive tradeoff easier to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 on blast radius and exporter incentives, crystallizing that portability multiplies pre-review damage rather than disciplining careless platforms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing that portability globalizes punishment before proof, not after it. CON kept the debate focused on the circularity of PRO's "serious" evidentiary standard—since the same opaque platforms would define it—and offered a more realistic incentive story in which platforms export dubious rulings for PR while externalizing costs onto players and rival games. PRO's concession that too few current rulings meet a high bar allowed CON to collapse the debate onto a concrete asymmetry: the motion would make existing flawed moderation travel industry-wide, and the safety valve of localized, recoverable error would disappear. PRO's speculative claim that portability would magically generate due-process standards was never sufficiently grounded against this pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal, which reversed PRO's incentive story by showing that portability lets platforms launder unreviewed rulings into industry-wide blacklists while rival games and the banned player absorb the fallout, and that legal risk remains theoretical behind TOS shields—making localization, not portability, the stronger check on careless moderation.
