### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by adhering more closely to the actual proposition. It persuasively distinguished treating targeted digital exclusion as a serious issue from punishing every omission or forcing invitations. Its answers preserved ordinary friendship choices while establishing school concern when coordinated exclusion is deliberately made visible and harms the campus environment. B offered legitimate privacy and enforcement concerns, but repeatedly assumed a formal disciplinary offense that the motion and A did not require. A's clipped opening and slight tension over whether administrators need to view chats narrow the margin without overturning the result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest rebuttal exposed that B's surveillance and forced-inclusion objections depended on treating every private omission as punishable. A directly answered that students may decline friendships and invitations; the school intervenes when exclusion is coordinated, weaponized, and manifested in school.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the framing fight on "purely off-campus." By limiting the claim to targeted, visible, patterned freeze-outs whose harm lands in school, and by explicitly disowning blanket mandatory inclusion, A neutralized B's surveillance and forced-inclusion parade. A's pressure Q1 forced B to concede on-campus manifestations are school business, and A4.1-A4.3 directly answered B's proof and safe-harbor questions with on-campus footprint evidence and "don't weaponize awareness." B's claim that PRO abandoned the motion misreads it—the motion is "rather than purely off-campus," which PRO defends.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 turn that CON already abandoned "purely off-campus" by conceding screenshot-waving taunts are actionable, plus A4's answer that no chat seizure is needed because the cruelty announces itself at school, and that the rule is "don't invite and don't advertise the exclusion."
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully reframing the debate around where the harm lands rather than where the keyboard is located. PRO dismantled CON's slippery slope arguments regarding surveillance and forced inclusion by drawing a clear, plausible line between private friendship choices and weaponized, visible exclusion. CON's attempt to claim a concession in Rebuttal 2 fell flat, as PRO's clarification actually strengthened the distinction between ordinary social dynamics and actionable bullying. PRO's closing effectively exposed the deliberate blindness of CON's purely off-campus stance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 where they answered the pressure questions by explaining that evidence of coordinated exclusion walks into school on its own because children are terrible conspirators, completely neutralizing CON's surveillance and proof objections without needing device seizures.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by isolating what the proposition uniquely adds: jurisdiction over exclusion when no independently actionable harassment can be proved. Side A reasonably narrowed its policy to coordinated, malicious ostracism and answered the questions directly, but its proposed evidence and threshold remained highly discretionary. B showed that provable coordination already supplies actionable conduct, while unprovable silence creates privacy, fairness, and retaliation risks. B's clipped opening was an execution cost, but its later rebuttals decisively resolved the central clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was the proof dilemma in B2 and B4: if schools inspect chats and interview peers, they incur the intrusion and informant costs; if they do not, they infer coordinated malice from distress and social patterns. A offered witnesses, admissions, and contextual judgment, but B persuasively replied that admissions or coordinating acts are already actionable, leaving the motion's distinctive reach insufficiently bounded.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive narrowing clash: what does the motion uniquely add beyond already-actionable harassment, and can it be proved? PRO conceded at [A1.3] that substantial-disruption conduct is already covered. CONconsistently distinguished acts (instruct, mock, recruit) from mere non-invitation, then pressed evidence and threshold questions. PRO's answers – pattern/context and intent without a line, witness accounts from pastoral interviews – confirmed CON's informant-machinery and discretion critique and left the non-uniqueness point (counselling/mediation available today without disciplinary jurisdiction) unanswered. That left PRO with a moral claim about duty of care but no workable adjudication standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 + Rebuttal 2 chain: forcing PRO to pick surveillance vs hunch, exposing "silence with no artefact" as unprovable, and re-framing PRO's own evidence as the surveillance it denied, plus the unique-add framing in closing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively reframing the debate around the epistemic and practical limits of school jurisdiction. A successfully distinguished between actionable harassment (already covered by existing policies) and mere exclusion (the absence of an act), forcing B to defend policing "silence" and non-invitations. A brilliantly exposed B's internal contradiction regarding off-campus jurisdiction, pointing out that B's own opening conceded schools already act on off-campus conduct that causes substantial disruption. A's argument that the motion uniquely adds "jurisdiction over silence" without reliable artifacts, relying instead on informant machinery and adult intuition, was devastating and left B without a coherent answer. B's reliance on "pastoral interviews" to prove coordinated malice played directly into A's warnings about arbitrary enforcement and surveillance. A's rhetorical framing was exceptionally sharp, making the comparative stakes clear and memorable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled B's answers to the pressure questions. A pointed out that B's proposed evidence base ("peer witness accounts" and "pastoral interviews") was exactly the informant machinery A had warned against, and that B's refusal to set a threshold left schools with unlimited discretion to punish the "absence of an act," inevitably harming the wrong students.
