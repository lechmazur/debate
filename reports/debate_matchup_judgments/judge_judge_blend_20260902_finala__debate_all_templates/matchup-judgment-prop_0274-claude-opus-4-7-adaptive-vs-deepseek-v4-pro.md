### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the motion narrow: limits on monetized influencer-style promotion as one layered tourism-management tool for fragile sites already being damaged. B had real arguments on direct management, leakage, and speech discretion, but too often treated geotag restraint as a sole substitute for permits and rangers rather than an added valve. A answered the bright-line and effectiveness pressure more cleanly, while B's key empirical claim about geotag discouragement remained under-supported and did not defeat A's counterfactual marginal-reduction framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest rebuttal was that leaky tools are normal in tourism management: geotag limits need not stop every visitor to reduce a viral spike enough for caps, rangers, and closures to work. That directly blunted B's main "hashtags will replace tags" objection.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by effectively framing the motion as a narrow, commercial regulation akin to existing filming permits, neatly neutralizing Side B's censorship and slippery-slope arguments. Furthermore, Side A's concept of "friction" and counterfactual surges decisively dismantled Side B's claim that the policy is useless if it doesn't stop 100% of the traffic.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized Side B's "leakiness" argument by explaining that friction is not a binary, and comparing geotag restrictions to other successful but imperfect tools like speed limits and fishing quotas.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because it maintained a narrow focus on the proposition's trigger condition (documented damage) and treated geotag restraint as one reversible layer inside proven management systems, while directly answering pressure on enforcement lines and displacement with existing ad rules and marginal reduction. Side A raised valid speech and leakage concerns but widened the frame, over-relied on the Maya Bay management success without rebutting B's "buys time" point, and never squarely weighed resident/ecosystem harm against creator reach, leaving its live burdens less resolved after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2, which used FTC disclosure precedents to answer the bright-line question and reframed visitation data as a partial spike reduction rather than a binary failure, materially damaged A's displacement and censorship routes.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): A won narrowly by making geotag limits a complementary demand-side tool rather than a substitute for capacity rules. Its best point was that viral promotion creates sudden spikes at fragile, often unmanaged sites before turnstile tools can be built, so reducing the amplifier is itself damage reduction. B had strong pressure on enforceability, inequality, and displacement, and A's clipped turns cost it some execution. But B leaned too hard on a false choice between information limits and capacity management, and its displacement argument depended too much on treating demand as fixed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal to B's pressure questions was the key exchange: it answered that perfect suppression is unnecessary if limits reduce the high-speed viral amplifier, and it reframed displacement as something unfettered geotagging already causes.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by effectively proving that viral geotagging operates at a velocity that traditional capacity management (Side B's main alternative) cannot match. Side B falters in the later rounds by leaning heavily on a mischaracterization of Side A's routing argument, claiming Side A wanted to route crowds to the very sites they were suppressing, when Side A clearly specified routing them to the destination's broader managed assets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's distinction in A4 that a single viral reel delivers a million impressions overnight—unlike guidebooks or Google Maps—successfully answered Side B's pressure on enforceability by showing why the unique velocity of influencer content must be targeted.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by establishing that geotag limits regulate speech without controlling actual visitor flows, leaving enforcement gaps and driving displacement to unmanaged sites; Side B failed to resolve the core contradiction of darkening fragile marquee spots while claiming redirection to infrastructure sites, and could not neutralize enforceability or the superiority of direct capacity tools. Side A’s rebuttals and closing crystallized this cleanly after pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A Rebuttal 2 exposed Side B’s unresolved funneling contradiction and weighed turnstile tools against leaky speech limits.
