### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B built a tight, conditional case (limit only mechanics that demonstrably trade quality/trust for engagement) and executed the clash better across rounds. B's Rebuttal 1 caught a real internal tension in A's case—claiming match quality is too subjective to regulate while simultaneously claiming boosts "efficiently optimize" attention—and A never cleanly resolved it. B's pressure questions forced A to specify what boosts are "efficient at, measured how," and A's answer (impression counts, right-swipe rates) inadvertently conceded B's central thesis: that the only thing being measured is engagement, not quality. B's closing crystallized this concession decisively and tied it to the motion's actual burden. A's answer on badged boosts was also shaky (conflating Super Like badges with algorithmic boost visibility) and B's closing correctly flagged this as either an inaccurate claim or a concession that labeling is itself the kind of limit the motion contemplates. A's own case (liquidity, cold-start, network effects) was reasonable but became increasingly defensive and repetitive, and its closing failed to engage with the decisive "impression counts" trap, settling for restated assertions instead of resolving the key clash. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but B used the exchange to sharpen the decisive comparison while A's answers deepened its own vulnerability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q2) forcing A to define what boosts are "efficient at, measured how," followed by A's Rebuttal 2 answer citing "impression counts and right-swipe rates"—which B's closing then used to show A had conceded that only engagement, not match quality, was ever being measured.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO (B) won by keeping the modest conditional motion live, forcing CON to defend features even when they trade quality/trust for engagement, and crystallizing that CON's efficiency claims never left impression/swipe metrics. CON's liquidity and subjectivity points were competent but repeatedly collapsed under the contradiction between "quality unmeasurable" and "boosts optimize," plus weak answers on labeling and measurement proxies. B adapted across rounds with cleaner weighing and pressure; A relied more on restatement of the empty-room risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 (and follow-through in R2/closing) on CON's internal contradiction: if match quality is too subjective to limit features, paid boosts cannot credibly "optimize" or "efficiently allocate" it either.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO kept the debate on the conditional motion – limit only when quality/trust is traded for engagement – and won the measurement clash. CON's claim that boosts are efficient collapsed when it defined efficiency as impressions and right-swipe rates [A4.3], i.e., the engagement metrics the motion targets. B also neutralized the dead-pool argument with existing caps and turned the labeling defense into a concession that a limit preserves trust.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Pressure Q2 forced CON to define "efficient allocation" and CON answered with impression counts and swipe rates [A4.3]; B's closing used that admission to prove boosts count traffic not crossings, leaving CON with no outcome metric while conceding labeling as a viable limit.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON's central attack — that the motion's own "only when" clause demands an administrable trigger that PRO never supplied — was pressed relentlessly and never resolved. PRO's Pressure Questions answer ("the threshold isn't a single arbitrary reply rate; it is determined by whether the platform's algorithmic weighting prioritizes...") stayed vague, and PRO's closing explicitly conceded "we do not need a perfectly precise regulatory metric," which CON then used devastatingly against the motion's own wording. CON also exposed a real tension in PRO's narrative between "addicted captives" and "constantly churning, frustrated switchers," and PRO's attempt to reconcile caps-plus-boosts as complementary remedies was met with a sharp counter (caps make each boost more zero-sum, not less) that PRO never cleanly answered. CON's concrete counter-examples (rural dater stuck at profile twenty, episodic delete-and-return behavior as evidence of cheap exit rather than addiction) gave the debate clearer, more falsifiable content than PRO's evocative but under-defended slot-machine analogy. PRO argued with genuine rhetorical force and a coherent moral framing, but by the second rebuttal and closing it was reacting to CON's frame rather than reclaiming it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question on the swipe-cap/boost interaction ("caps manufacture the scarcity that makes boosts zero-sum — so which half do you abandon?") forced PRO into a hedge that CON then flipped in Rebuttal 2, showing the cap actually makes boosts more disruptive, not less — a contradiction PRO never resolved and CON exploited through to closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON won by forcing the live clash onto the motion's "only when" administrability requirement and the internal conflict between the two remedies. PRO's conflict-of-interest and dark-pattern story was forceful and rhetorically sharp, but never supplied a workable, observable trigger for when features undermine quality/trust; its later pivot to unobservable algorithmic "prioritization" conceded the point rather than resolved it. CON repeatedly showed that PRO's proxies would misclassify successful off-app matches as failures, that free exit undercuts the addiction narrative, and that a swipe cap itself manufactures the scarcity that makes boosts zero-sum—leaving PRO's dual remedies self-defeating. PRO never cleaned these problems; CON's comparative weighing on concrete harms (thin markets, free-tier loss) versus an unmeasurable hope therefore decided the round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A2.3–A2.5 and A4.5–A4.6 showing that PRO's own swipe cap creates the attention scarcity that makes paid boosts zero-sum, so the two halves of the proposition defeat each other.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON won on administrability and remedy coherence — the proposition's own "when" trigger. PRO never supplied a workable threshold, substituting "prioritizes time-on-app" and finally "we do not need a precise metric" [B5.3], which CON framed as concession. CON's twin rebuttals — users as addicted captives vs free-switching shoppers, and caps manufacturing the scarcity that makes boosts zero-sum [A2.1-A2.4] — were not neutralized, while CON gave concrete costs (rural dater at profile 23, free tier loss).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R1 exposing contradictory subject (addicted vs comparison-shopper) and self-defeating remedies where the cap makes boosts more zero-sum — later sharpened in A2 with logging ≠ interpreting and episodic deletion as free exit, not addiction.
