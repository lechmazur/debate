### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B built and sustained a genuine dilemma: PRO's "growing right" is either fully revocable at parental judgment (in which case it is indistinguishable from the discretion model CON already endorses) or it requires specific justification before intervention (in which case it blocks the very monitoring that reveals grooming, self-harm, or dangerous plans in the first place). This dilemma, first raised in B2.1-B2.2 and sharpened via pressure questions (B3.1-B3.2), was never cleanly resolved by A. A's answers (A4.1-A4.2) tried to have both a "default of autonomy" and case-by-case exceptions triggered by signs like "sudden secrecy," but B correctly pressed that such signs are often only detectable through the access PRO's right would restrict (B4.3-B4.4) — a point A's closing never directly answered, instead re-asserting the developmental narrative. B's driving-instructor and training-wheels analogies (B2.3, B5.2) concretely showed how practice and accountability coexist without needing an entitlement against guardians, undercutting A's core claim that practice requires privacy from parents. A's case remained emotionally resonant and consistent, but it repeated its developmental framing across rounds without closing the structural gap B exposed. A's openings and first rebuttal were also clipped for exceeding word limits, a real execution cost. B's tighter, more analytically rigorous argument, better-targeted pressure questions, and cleaner final crystallization give it the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's dilemma in B2.1-B2.2, reinforced by the Q1/Q2 pressure questions (B3.1-B3.2) and hammered again in B4.3-B4.4: PRO's right is either redundant with parental discretion or actively delays detection of danger, and A never fully escapes this trap in A4 or the closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully trapping Side B in a dilemma: if the proposed right is entirely subject to parental revocation, it is identical to Side A's model of parental discretion; if it requires specific justification to revoke, it prevents parents from discovering the very hidden dangers (like grooming) that would justify intervention. Side B struggled to resolve this catch-22, relying on "flipping the default," while suffering from execution errors (two clipped speeches).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, where they pointed out that Side B's triggers for intervening ("sudden secrecy") are often only visible through the very oversight Side B seeks to restrict by default.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B wins by exposing the collapse/information dilemma: if the growing right is conditional/revocable it is CON's discretionary model, if it requires specific justification before checking it blocks early discovery of grooming/self-harm. A’s default-flip answer never resolves how secrecy is detected without monitoring, and was hurt by clipped over-limit turns. CON keeps practice without entitlement cost, aligning responsibility and authority.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.7 and B4.2-B4.5 collapsing PRO into discretion vs unsafe presumption and showing practice does not require secrecy – training-wheels/driving-brake analogy neutralizes A’s development claim.
