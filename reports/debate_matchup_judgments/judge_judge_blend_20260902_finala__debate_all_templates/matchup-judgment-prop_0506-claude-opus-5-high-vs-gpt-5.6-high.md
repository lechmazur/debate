  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Closing attack on Side B's answer to Q1, pointing out that requiring a platform's "own review to confirm" a scam creates a legal subsidy for willful blindness by rewarding platforms that simply do not look at the evidence.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the proposition narrow - repeated, verified, ignored synthetic fraud causing financial loss - and recast vague terms as ordinary negligence: convergence (independent victims + wire receipts + impersonated person's notice) and reasonable response (pause ad, hold payout, ask for consent). CON's knowing-material-assistance alternative collapsed into willful-blindness incentive, which PRO exposed in B5: confirming only if you look rewards not looking, while CON never named a record that would satisfy its own standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Exchange on knowledge: A2.2 that platforms act ex ante before a court links loss (so plaintiff-standing limit doesn't cure chilling) vs B4.2-B4.3 reframing duty as reasonable response not instant deletion plus A4.2-B5 willful-blindness subsidy argument that CON's confirmed-knowledge rule punishes the diligent investigator and lets the inbox-ignorer free.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built a highly effective, narrow tort-based case and brilliantly exploited CON's concessions. PRO correctly identified this as creating a legal subsidy for willful blindness, effectively neutralizing CON's alternative. Furthermore, PRO's rebuttals to the censorship argument—pointing out that liability requires an actual defrauded plaintiff with documented financial losses, which doesn't apply to parody or political speech—decisively blunted CON's strongest offensive line.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing argument exposing that CON's "own review" standard legally subsidizes willful blindness by rewarding platforms that simply choose not to open the files or look at the reports.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won a close but clear victory by consistently grounding its case in the strict requirements of negligence (causation, unreasonableness, actual loss) and exposing the flaws in Side B's alternatives. While Side B argued persuasively that "verified" is an ambiguous standard that leads to over-removal and litigation costs, Side A successfully pointed out that Side B's proposed "regulatory clock" alternative suffers from the exact same epistemic ambiguity. Side B also suffered from repeated word-limit clipping across three of its speeches, weakening its final impact in those rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument in Rebuttal 1 that a regulatory clock still requires a reviewer to decide when notice is valid, effectively neutralizing Side B's core complaint that civil liability uniquely creates ambiguity at the desk level.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the motion narrow to repeated, verified, ignored reports plus causation, meeting CON's vagueness charge with a reasonableness-then standard. The cheapest-cost-avoider point and payment-rails as complement not substitute survived. CON's strong knowledge-tax/over-removal and redundancy critiques were blunted by B's collapse: if clear scams are already removed burden is low, if not deterrence matters, and by direct answers in Pressure Questions. Clipped CON turns added execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2: defined verification as corroborated evidence for a reasonable reviewer, not repetition, denied liability for reasonable mistake, and limited damages to causal loss, directly answering CON's hindsight and accusation-equals-verification claims.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively dismantling the practical mechanics of the proposition's "verified" threshold. A successfully argues that "verified" is a hindsight standard determined by a jury, meaning trust-and-safety reviewers only ever see "accusations." Because the cost of leaving a post up (massive liability and discovery) asymmetrically outweighs the cost of taking it down (none, due to TOS), the proposition functionally mandates over-removal of lawful speech in murky cases. Side B relies on the legal principle that "reasonable mistakes aren't negligence," but A effectively counters that the litigation tax and settlement pressure drive ex-ante behavior. Furthermore, A turns B's claim that "platforms already remove obvious scams" against them, proving the proposition adds no deterrence where cases are clear, and only causes harm where they are murky. Despite minor clipping at the end of several turns, A's core arguments were fully developed, highly original, and rhetorically devastating.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal to Side B's claim that obvious cases are already removed. A points out that if obvious cases are already handled, the proposition's deterrence value is zero, and its only actual bite is in murky cases, where it incentivizes over-removal. This perfectly neutralized B's attempt to minimize the over-removal harm.
