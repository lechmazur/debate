A was also clipped in rebuttals, which slightly hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was most decisive: it answered A's trending/search objection as side doors rather than the main feed, then used the decay versus algorithmic resuscitation comparison to show why chronological feeds would likely reduce net scale even if toxicity persisted.

Side B also suffered from word count execution errors, clipping their R1 and R2.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal, where they distinguish between the 'main hall' of the feed and the 'side doors' of trending topics, cleanly resolving B's strongest counter-example.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by building a cleaner, more consistent causal story around the algorithmic force-multiplier and the natural decay of chronological feeds. While Side A (CON) raised strong points about moderation levers and the acute harm of targeted harassment mobs, PRO effectively neutralized the moderation argument by highlighting the structural conflict between platform profit motives and consistent suppression. PRO's focus on blast radius and scale directly answered the net toxicity burden. Additionally, CON suffered a clear execution cost with two speeches clipped for exceeding word limits, which disrupted flow and left some comparative weighing underdeveloped. PRO's closing crystallized the clash sharply without overreaching.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's moderation shield argument was decisive. By pointing out that the same engagement-optimized engine CON trusts to demote hate is structurally addicted to the outrage that drives dwell time, PRO successfully framed algorithmic curation as an unreliable filter rather than a safety net.
