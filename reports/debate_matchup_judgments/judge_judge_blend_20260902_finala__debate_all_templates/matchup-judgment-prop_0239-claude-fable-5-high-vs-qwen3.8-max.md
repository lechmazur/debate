### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by holding Side A to the proposition’s actual ethical standard rather than A’s narrower test of authorization or rights violation. A repeatedly admitted that consensual terms could be cruel, vicious, or repellent, then classified those moral defects as external to the transaction. B persuasively showed that the boater’s exploitative price was itself a central term of the exchange, so consent could establish authorization without establishing ethicality. B also showed that A’s pollution repair required an independently determined account of affected rights-holders, undermining the claim that consent alone does all the work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal was that A had defended consensual transactions as valid or non-theft, not necessarily ethical. Its comparison between a modest rescue fee and a demand for the victim’s life savings exposed that identical consent can accompany morally different terms, while A never convincingly explained why cruelty embedded in the price was merely outside the transaction.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully demonstrating that Side A's own concessions (calling certain transactions "cruel" or "repellent") proved that moral evaluation extends beyond mere consent. Side B effectively collapsed Side A's distinction between character and transaction by pointing out that "the vice is the price."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which neutralized Side A's defense by pointing out that the boater's cruelty cannot be separated from the transaction because the cruel demand is the central term of the exchange.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won by keeping the burden on sufficiency and forcing CON's own concessions to do the work. CON ably defended authorization-as-non-violation and the worry about observer veto, but repeatedly used evaluative labels (cruel, vicious, repellent) and a "properly scoped" harm map that function as partner criteria, not consent alone. PRO's cleaner weighing—ethics is wider than "not robbery," and scoped consent already imports independent standards—made the proposition easier to affirm after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure/A1 line on the boater: if two equally consensual rescues can differ ethically, and CON still calls the life-savings demand cruel, something beyond consent is doing the evaluation.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won the central definitional clash. Side A expanded consent to include every affected person while narrowing coercion to engineered threats, but never established that informed agreement and mutual improvement necessarily make exploitative terms ethical. Side B persuasively distinguished a transaction being better than abandonment from its being ethically decent and showed that either desperation matters independently or Side A must accept severe exploitation as ethical. Side A sharply challenged the precision of B's fairness test, keeping the debate competitive, but did not defeat that core dilemma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer that a bad offer may be preferable to no offer yet still unethical directly undercut Side A's strongest bystander comparison. Its follow-up dilemma about hunger and voluntariness exposed the cost of Side A's consent-only standard, despite Side A's effective attack on B's proposed fairness floors.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly separating "improves welfare" from "is ethical," neutralizing Side A's powerful "punishing proximity" argument. Side A also fell into a contradiction in Rebuttal 2, claiming starvation doesn't affect voluntariness but then arguing Side B's starvation-based fairness test was just "voluntariness relabeled."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 exposed a core dilemma in Side A's framework regarding whether hunger impairs voluntariness, and successfully argued that a transaction can be the "least bad option" without being ethical.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): PRO won a close, high-quality clash by trapping CON on the hunger dilemma: either bare survival consent counts as voluntary (so exploitation is labeled ethical) or background alternatives/power do the work (so consent alone is not enough). CON’s counterexample burden and bystander pressure were sharp and nearly decisive, but PRO’s reply that “better than abandonment” does not equal ethical, plus the smuggling charge against a thickened consent definition, better resolved the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s hunger/voluntariness dilemma (R2/closing): CON must either permit survival-wage deals as ethical or admit fairness/alternatives, not bare consent, settle the case.
