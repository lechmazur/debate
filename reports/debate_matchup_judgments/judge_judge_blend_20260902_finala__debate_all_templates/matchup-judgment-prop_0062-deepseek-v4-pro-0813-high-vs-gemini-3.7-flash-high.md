### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won a competitive debate by exposing the unsupported premise behind B's net-loss arithmetic: B repeatedly assumed that scalable drafting and analysis make the entire professional service nearly labor-free. A more carefully distinguished automated outputs from tailoring, verification, negotiation, exception handling, and liability, then explained why cheaper services could expand demand for that human layer. B established real disruption risks and a persuasive pipeline problem, but largely asserted rather than demonstrated that oversight would remain thin enough to produce aggregate net job loss. B's absolutist language and nonexistent C-reference labels also weakened its epistemic discipline, though A's clipped opening was an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was its answer to B's 500% demand challenge: it rejected the premise that AI had automated five complete jobs, distinguished drafts from finished accountable services, and turned B's concession of expanded clientele against B's unsupported assumption of near-zero marginal human work.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive clash on what white-collar work actually is. Across rebuttals and the closing, B consistently separated drafts from accountable deliverables and showed that cheaper production makes residual judgment, verification, exceptions, and liability the bottleneck rather than a thin supervisory afterthought. A’s strongest moves—software velocity, bounded specialized demand, and firms free-riding on entry-level cuts—landed real pressure, but B answered the 500% arithmetic and pipeline questions more cleanly: demand need not explode one-for-one if the residual human layer expands, and competitive firms have reason to keep supervised junior capacity. Once net permanent white-collar collapse was not established, A’s case for early collapse-oriented policy lost its burden against B’s flexibility alternative. Slight execution cost to B for a clipped opening did not erase the clearer comparative route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R2/closing answer treating A’s “near-zero marginal human hours for more clients” as true only of the automated slice, not accountability work—converting A’s own demand concession into B’s elasticity win.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won the central elasticity/accountability clash. PRO relied on bounded demand and zero-marginal-human service of new clients [B4.1], but CON reframed the automated unit as draft vs defensible service [A4.1, A5.1] and showed demand is rationed by price, so cheaper drafts expand the human verification/negotiation/liability layer. CON's pressure about priced-out small firms [A3.1] went unanswered except by the implausible near-zero human hours claim, and its pipeline answer [A4.3] countered PRO's market-failure story. Clipped CON opening is a cost, but later adaptation was cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 where CON distinguishes draft from finished accountable work and argues new clients need verification/integration/exceptions, directly undercutting PRO's 500% arithmetic and "zero marginal human hours" claim.
