### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won because the proposition only requires a substantial class to benefit relative to mandatory full-time attendance. A identified experienced individual contributors and gave plausible mechanisms through focus, avoided commuting, and retention. B showed real remote-work costs without establishing that they outweigh A’s gains for the identified class. A’s clipped turns, overstated concession claims, and overly precise study references narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s “scalpel, not sledgehammer” response was decisive: accepting that juniors benefit from pairing or onboarding days does not justify requiring every experienced worker to attend five days a week. B’s reply that hybrid concedes the need for attendance did not defeat a proposition comparing work from home with mandatory full-time office attendance.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B won by holding the motion’s modest burden—on-net gains for many versus a five-day mandate—and forcing A to defend forced attendance rather than only catalog remote frictions. B’s sharper comparative weighing (scalpel onboarding vs sledgehammer mandate; seniors exit burns the tacit capital A prizes) made the ballot easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1–2 on juniors/seniors: grant mentoring costs, price them with structured office days, and show the mandate destroys the senior tacit knowledge it claims to protect.
