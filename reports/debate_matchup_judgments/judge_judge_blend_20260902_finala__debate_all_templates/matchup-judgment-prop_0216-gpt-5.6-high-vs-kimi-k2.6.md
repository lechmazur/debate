Side A's institutional critique and leakage arguments were strong and well-articulated, but Side B's practical enforcement mechanisms (targeting mill chokepoints, allowing provisional claims) and sharper weighing in the final rounds gave it the clear edge.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): CON built the cleaner path by treating both sides as regulators of peat, primary forest, and burning, then forcing PRO to justify the extra hard-cap layer. That extra layer never cleared the dual burden of net environmental gain plus unavoidable equity freeze under weak institutions; PRO’s bright-line claim was repeatedly undercut by CON’s demonstration that caps still require baselines, maps, origin verification, and grandfathering that entrenches connected estates. PRO’s haze and land-grab examples therefore lost force because they were already banned under the targeted alternative. The exchange narrowed to governance realism and smallholder incidence, where CON’s comparative weighing was more consistent and less assumption-dependent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.3 (caps recreate parcel judgment plus ceiling while freezing incumbents; premium markets do not exhaust demand)
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B won the additionality clash. PRO's haze/peat harms are conceded by CON's targeted ban, so PRO needed to justify blocking already-cleared low-carbon expansion. CON showed a hard cap freezes incumbent estates, burdens smallholders who cannot enter, does not escape mapping/verification corruption, and risks leakage to opaque markets or higher-land-use oils because palm is yield-efficient. PRO's bright-line simplicity claim was directly answered and market-access norms require traceability, not a total cap, leaving CON's precision-as-enforceability case as the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3-B4.5 - cap does not eliminate discretion, it entrenches past privilege and still requires mapping/origin verification, while directing rents to incumbents and pushing excluded farmers toward opaque channels.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by decisively dismantling Side B's (PRO) core mechanism. PRO argued that a bright-line cap on expansion is simpler and less corruptible than parcel-by-parcel ecological targeting. CON brilliantly countered that defining the baseline for an expansion cap requires the exact same complex mapping and verification, but instead of punishing harm, it merely grandfathers in the existing unequal distribution of land, locking out smallholders while entrenching corporate estates. CON's framing of the cap as "freezing power's historical gains" was logically robust and rhetorically devastating, securing the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it exposed that PRO's "simple" bright-line cap still requires parcel-by-parcel verification to establish the baseline, thereby recreating the exact corruption vulnerabilities PRO criticized while adding an arbitrary ceiling that disproportionately harms smallholders.
