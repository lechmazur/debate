### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): PRO won the debate's central clash by maintaining a durable and meaningful distinction: evaluating a verifiable result (did the worker meet the SLA, complete the audit, resolve the blocker?) versus continuously tracking an ongoing state (is the status dot green, is the mouse moving?). That distinction survived the full exchange. CON's strongest pressure was the definitional trap—if responsiveness and check-ins count as "outputs," the motion becomes hollow—and the timing argument that leading indicators are needed before damage lands. PRO answered both. On the definitional trap, PRO's result-vs-state framing held: calling a completed peer review an "output" is not semantic inflation; it is a verifiable deliverable that measures business value, unlike a stream of green-dot activity signals. On timing, the SLA framework was a genuine partial answer: measuring whether a worker unblocked a teammate within an expected window is a result evaluation, not constant monitoring. CON's own Rebuttal 2 conceded significant ground by locating acceptable monitoring in "ordinary workflow tools" rather than constant surveillance—an answer that moves closer to PRO's territory than CON acknowledged. The motion's qualifier "mainly" also narrowed CON's real target: the proposition never claimed outputs are the only thing that matters, only that they should dominate over constant presence surveillance. CON repeatedly argued as though the motion said "exclusively," which overstated the burden and weakened CON's case. CON's closing dilemma was rhetorically sharp but did not survive PRO's consistent result-vs-state distinction, which gave "outputs" a principled boundary rather than an infinitely elastic one. The margin is real but competitive: CON's timing argument and definitional trap were genuine threats that PRO addressed but did not eliminate entirely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 A1 answer—distinguishing "measuring a result" (a completed deliverable, an SLA met) from "tracking a state" (continuous status monitoring, green dots, mouse movement)—was the most decisive exchange. It directly neutralized CON's definitional trap by giving "output" a principled boundary that is narrow enough to be meaningful and broad enough to cover process and collaborative deliverables. CON never successfully collapsed this distinction, and their own answer that monitoring should occur through "ordinary workflow tools" implicitly accepted PRO's framing.
