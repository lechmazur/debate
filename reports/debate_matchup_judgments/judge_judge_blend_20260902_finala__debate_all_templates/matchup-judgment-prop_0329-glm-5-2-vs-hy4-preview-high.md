### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but PRO (B) did the better job actually operationalizing the motion's "when" test. When pressed for a threshold, PRO answered concretely: readiness within normal recovery bands, no compressed back-to-back, an equivalent local date preserved, and durable (not one-night) indicators of gain — and paired this with a clean distinction between a reciprocal domestic road game and a discretionary relocated home date. CON's "catch-22" reframe (any conditional test always favors the status quo because benefits are immature at t=0) was clever and original, but CON's own answers to PRO's pressure questions were comparatively vague ("dependence on the home market," "one or two dates is marginal") rather than a real measurable line, which is exactly the specificity CON demanded of PRO. In the final exchange, CON's closing reasserted that PRO's test blocks the "first game," but this mischaracterizes PRO's own clarified "buffered pilot" framing (B4.1/B5.2), which explicitly lets a managed trip proceed and be judged mainly on cost-side criteria knowable in advance, with durable indicators guiding continuation rather than gating the initial trip. CON's last word is rhetorically forceful but doesn't fully engage that narrowing, leaving PRO's threshold as the more resolved, harder-to-dismiss standard. PRO also sustained the more concrete harm story (home-date economics, hidden competitive variable from circadian disruption) throughout, giving it the cleaner, more decidable case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The pressure-question exchange on thresholds: PRO answered with a specific conjunctive test (recovery band, no back-to-back, preserved home date, durable indicators) while CON's answer to the parallel question ("dependence on the home market") stayed comparatively vague, and CON's final closing attack on PRO's "buffered pilot" concept didn't fully engage PRO's earlier clarification that the pilot itself is judged on cost-side criteria, not pre-existing proof of gains.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won by preserving the motion’s conditional distinction: scaling back is required only when the combined costs outweigh the gains, not whenever any cost exists. A supplied workable comparisons, answered both pressure questions directly, and showed that managed pilots remain possible. B’s delayed-benefits critique was forceful, but B ultimately conceded that the motion binds in extreme cases and when international frequency degrades the home product. That concession validated A’s core decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to the catch-22 was decisive: a scaled-back policy can still permit buffered pilots and expansion upon signs of durable demand. B repeatedly treated scaling back as prohibiting the first international game, which A had not advocated.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A wins by cleanly differentiating domestic scheduling burdens from international home-date relocations, neutralizing Side B's attempt to frame the trips as standard variables. Side A also effectively defuses Side B's "catch-22" attack by allowing for a "buffered pilot" to gather data, proving their test is a functional guardrail rather than an impossible veto.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4.3 brilliantly dismantled Side B's equivalence between domestic road trips and international games, pointing out that domestic travel is a shared, reciprocal burden while a London game is a discretionary conversion of a local home date into a neutral-site commercial event.
