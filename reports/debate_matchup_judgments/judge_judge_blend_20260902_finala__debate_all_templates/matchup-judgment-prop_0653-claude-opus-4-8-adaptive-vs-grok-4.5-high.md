### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively flipping Side B's core mechanism. While Side B argued that predictive models create an opaque shield for retaliation, Side A successfully demonstrated that a logged model actually creates a discoverable paper trail for regulators, and that a ban merely forces bad actors back to untraceable intuition. Side A also successfully exploited Side B's concession on aggregate analytics to show the ban would be easily bypassed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively flips the pretext argument by pointing out that a subpoenaed AI model is discoverable evidence of discriminatory motive, meaning a ban destroys the exact paper trail needed to protect workers.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO's ban is both overinclusive and underinclusive. CON showed that the aggregate-versus-individual line is practically porous—managers can derive individual predictions from aggregate attrition rates plus an org chart—so the ban targets a label rather than the leverage. CON also turned PRO's black-box concern against it: a logged predictive model creates subpoena-able evidence of pretext, whereas banning it destroys the paper trail and leaves untraceable managerial intuition untouched. PRO never recovered from the frame that the ban removes the audit trail while missing the harm, and failed to show why regulation of conduct couldn't reach the abuses while preserving beneficial uses like retention interventions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's paper-trail turn combined with the aggregate-loophole argument: showing that PRO's ban would eliminate discoverable evidence of retaliation while allowing managers to replicate the same individual scoring through legal aggregate analytics and untraceable hunches, making the ban a restriction on transparency rather than power.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the instrument clash that decides the debate. PRO argued ranking itself chills and that banning code is easier than proving motive. CON inverted both: a ranking inert without action, an aggregate 30% readout plus org chart is the same individual inference allowed under the ban, and a logged model is discoverable motive evidence that a ban destroys. PRO's answers in B4 that aggregate stops at team rates and detection is strict did not resolve the loophole or the evidence-destruction point, leaving the decisive comparison – ban blocks retention/help and auditable proof but misses intuition-based retaliation – intact for CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 A1/A2 paper-trail inversion: ranking documents pretext for subpoena; banning it leaves only untraceable hunch, plus aggregate loophole shows ban misses harm while killing beneficial uses.
