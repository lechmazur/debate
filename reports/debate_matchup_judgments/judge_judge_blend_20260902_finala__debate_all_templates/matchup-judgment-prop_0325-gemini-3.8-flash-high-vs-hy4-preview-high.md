### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a durable structural argument early — that team/cup/continental rights are sold separately, that broad bundles cross-subsidize smaller clubs and niche sports, and that fragmentation itself fuels piracy via blackouts and login friction. Crucially, CON identified and exploited a genuine dilemma: PRO's Rebuttal 1 concession that "comprehensive tiers can remain" [B2.1] either preserves the bundle as default (conceding the motion's own "rather than forcing most fans" language) or, if targeted passes truly replace the bundle, collapses the subscriber pool funding rights fees. PRO never cleanly escaped this trap in Rebuttal 2 or Closing, instead reasserting the same incremental-revenue and piracy-reduction claims without addressing the free-rider/cross-subsidy mechanism or the NBA-example rebuttal (that single-team packages are bundle-funded add-ons, not standalone replacements). CON's pressure questions targeted this contradiction precisely, and its answers to PRO's questions directly engaged rather than dodged. PRO's case was intuitive and consumer-sympathetic but repeatedly reasserted rather than defended against the structural critique, leaving CON's route to victory clearly live through to the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point [A2.1] catching PRO's "comprehensive tiers can remain" concession as either abandoning the motion's reform or collapsing the funding pool — a dilemma PRO never resolved in later speeches.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): B won by locking A into a live dilemma the motion wording itself creates: if broad bundles stay the default funding floor, most fans remain bundled and the reform evaporates; if cheap team/competition passes become the default, the pooled base funding multi-competition rights and cross-subsidies shrinks. A’s fairness, coercion, and pirate-conversion story stayed rhetorically sharp but never fully neutralized B’s stacking (league/cup/continental holdbacks) and freeriding points, so B left the cleaner reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.5–B5: PRO’s “comprehensive tiers can remain” either concedes the motion or collapses the rights-funding pool that makes cheap passes possible.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a structural dilemma PRO could not resolve: if targeted passes merely supplement bundles, the motion’s reform is hollow; if they replace bundles, the pooled revenue that subsidizes rights fees and smaller clubs collapses, forcing price hikes or coverage cuts. B also successfully turned PRO’s NBA example and showed that even single-team fans need stacked passes for league, cup, and continental fixtures—frustrating the affordability and anti-piracy claims. PRO’s consumer-choice narrative retained intuitive appeal but lost its economic footing after B’s sustained pressure on funding mechanics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s demonstration that PRO’s NBA single-team packages are out-of-market add-ons carved from broad-bundle revenue, not a standalone model, paired with the argument that a single-club fan still needs multiple passes (league, cup, continental) subject to blackouts and holdbacks, making the “cheap pass” illusory.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent economic cases, but the decisive clash was over whether a "cheap" targeted pass is additive revenue or a cannibalizing substitute. CON (A) pressed a genuine contradiction early (B claimed most fans are single-team loyalists yet also claimed cheap passes wouldn't cannibalize existing subscriptions), and PRO's answer to that pressure—that the pass must be stripped of playoffs, restricted to out-of-market games, and ad-laden to avoid cannibalization—handed CON the material for a sharp closing dilemma: either the pass cannibalizes bundle revenue, destabilizing broadcasters, or it is so degraded that ordinary in-market fans (the "most fans" the resolution is actually about) still must buy the broad bundle to get real access. PRO's closing called this "the design, not a refutation," but never squarely showed how mainstream domestic single-team fans, as opposed to diaspora/blackout/pirate fans, actually escape the bundle under its model, leaving the resolution's core promise ("rather than forcing most fans into broad bundles") unfulfilled by PRO's own admissions. PRO's rebuttals on upstream/downstream separation and NESN/YES reframing were solid and kept the debate close, but the final exchange favored CON's tighter, resolution-relevant dilemma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's move in Rebuttal 1/Rebuttal 2 exposing the contradiction between "most fans aren't completists" and "cheap passes don't cannibalize," which forced PRO to concede the pass must be degraded—material CON then converted into a decisive closing dilemma about whether most fans are actually liberated from bundles.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by keeping a cleaner live route: the proposition ends coercion, not bundles, so cheap differentiated passes convert priced-out/diaspora/pirate demand as additive contribution while central pooling can still fund parity. CON’s fixed-rights and cannibalization case was strong but repeatedly treated bundle-plus-tail as bundle-replacement and never fully escaped PRO’s false-dilemma rebuttal. PRO answered pressure more directly; CON’s late “crippled pass” dilemma was sharp but PRO’s design response matched the motion’s “most fans” burden better than CON’s insolvency overclaim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s false-dilemma/bundle-plus-tail replies (esp. A2.1–A2.2 and A4.1–A4.2 product differentiation vs total cannibalization) most damaged CON’s core route.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully reframing the debate as coercion versus choice rather than bundle versus no bundle, and by neutralizing Side B's cannibalization fear with a coherent product-differentiation story that targets non-paying fans without collapsing bundle revenue. Side B landed strong economic challenges but relied on nonexistent reference IDs and overstated Side A's closing stance as a concession, while failing to disprove that segmented passes could be additive rather than purely cannibalistic.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of the cannibalization argument by distinguishing priced-out and pirating fans from existing bundle subscribers, and showing how product differentiation (out-of-market only, ads, no playoffs) makes the cheap pass complementary rather than substitutive.
