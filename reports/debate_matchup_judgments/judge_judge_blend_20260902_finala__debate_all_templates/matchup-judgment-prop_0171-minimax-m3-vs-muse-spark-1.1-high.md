### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that inadequate animal models often coincide with uncertain pathogenesis, weak rescue options, and unknowable long-term harms, making guaranteed infection difficult to justify or consent to. B also offered a coherent threshold permitting well-understood, treatable challenges such as cholera or typhoid. A repeatedly overstated the field-trial comparison, misused the London drug trial as a pathogen-challenge example, and in closing falsely claimed B named no qualifying diseases. A's clipped turns and erroneous reference IDs added execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B decisively answered A's dengue comparison: field trials expose participants only to existing background risk, whereas A's proposal guaranteed infection and even staged heterologous reinfection despite no curative rescue. This turned A's strongest safety comparison into evidence for B's ethical objection.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully dismantling Side A's core safety argument. When Side A argued that a controlled dengue challenge (including deliberate reinfection to test enhancement) is safer than field trials, Side B devastatingly pointed out that logistical control (an ICU) is not biological control (a cure), and intentionally manufacturing enhanced disease crosses a hard ethical line. Side B also successfully defended its threshold for CHIS (permitting it only when a cure exists, like cholera) while Side A inaccurately claimed in closing that Side B had named no acceptable pathogens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which exposed Side A's admission that they would deliberately reinfect volunteers to test dengue enhancement, sharply distinguishing between natural background risk and guaranteed, incurable, manufactured harm.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by keeping the proposition's own trigger ("when animal models are inadequate") as the decisive clash and showing that inadequacy is exactly when pathogenesis, rescue, and quantifiable risk disclosure fail, rendering "controlled" and "informed" hollow. A consistently distinguished intentional guaranteed infection from background field risk, used the dengue ADE/reinfection problem and poor adult-to-child translation to undercut claimed safety and speed gains, and answered B's ban-pressure with a coherent threshold (real rescue + known history ok; novel ignorance expansion not). B's harm-relocation frame and historical examples were forceful but never neutralized the ignorance-trigger mismatch or the category error on TGN1412. Later rounds narrowed cleanly rather than widening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 dengue response: a 30-volunteer CHIS cannot safely test ADE without deliberately manufacturing heterologous reinfection under no real cure, so it is not safer than non-guaranteed field exposure.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3 most decisively answered the blank-check objection by specifying eligibility criteria and clear exclusions.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4/A5 synthesis pointing out that Side B's alternatives to CHIS (like household contact studies and field trials) simply export the same unknown risks to thousands of people who lack quarantine monitoring and bedside rescue.
