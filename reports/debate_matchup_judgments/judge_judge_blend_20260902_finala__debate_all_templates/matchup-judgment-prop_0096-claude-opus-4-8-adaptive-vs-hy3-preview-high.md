### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A masterfully controlled the core clash by forcing Side B to concede that opt-in doesn't solve the underlying power imbalance, then systematically dismantled Side B's remaining fallback arguments.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 flawlessly neutralized Side B's "default-flip" argument by pointing out that a default setting is useless to a frightened newcomer who will be forced to sign "yes" regardless of the format.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won because its rebuttals dismantled PRO's core claim that opt-in protects the vulnerable: CON showed the default-flip only aids actors who already have leverage, the marginalized still sign under duress, and enforcement is identical for disclosure-plus-terms.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.2 and A4.6 directly turned PRO's "informed choice" and "protect marginalized" lines into proof that opt-in shields the strong while leaving the day player exposed.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by successfully demonstrating that procedural changes like opt-in do not resolve the underlying power imbalance for vulnerable actors, who will simply sign the separate form under the same duress. CON’s alternative—conspicuous disclosure plus collectively bargained substantive terms—directly protected the target demographic regardless of leverage. CON effectively turned PRO’s concessions about “informed coercion” and studio “route-arounds” into decisive offensive points, and closed with a highly memorable, crystallizing frame (“Opt-in protects the act of signing; bargained terms protect the person”). PRO’s case relied heavily on visibility and default-flipping but never adequately answered why a powerless actor’s outcome improves when the bargaining dynamics remain unchanged.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing decisively resolved the central clash by showing that flipping the consent default only benefits performers who already have leverage, while CON’s proposed substantive guarantees protect vulnerable actors regardless of what they sign. The line “Opt-in protects the act of signing. Bargained terms protect the human being” perfectly captured the comparative weighing and sealed the round.
