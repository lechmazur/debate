B’s clipped Rebuttal 1 was a cost, but later rounds narrowed and resolved the clash more decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s compensation fork (Pressure Q1 through Rebuttal 2/Closing): mandate either duplicates voluntary demand response or confiscates firm service—A never cleanly chose.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins decisively by exposing the fundamental economic and structural flaws in Side B's mandate. Side A's most devastating maneuver was the "compensation fork" introduced during Pressure Questions and hammered home in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing: if the mandate compensates data centers, it merely duplicates existing voluntary demand-response markets but with worse price discovery; if it does not compensate them, it is outright confiscation that chills the investment needed to build grid capacity. While Side B's physical asymmetry argument (AI can pause, factories cannot) was intuitively appealing, Side A proved that electricity markets already price this exact asymmetry in without needing a blunt, distortive mandate. Side A's Turn 4 was slightly clipped at the very last word, but this was a negligible execution cost that did not leave any key burdens unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's introduction and exploitation of the compensation fork. By forcing Side B to choose whether the mandate pays data centers for curtailment or confiscates their purchased firm service, Side A trapped the PRO side into proving either that the mandate is redundant or that it destroys grid investment. Side B's evasive answer in Rebuttal 2 sealed the debate for Side A.
