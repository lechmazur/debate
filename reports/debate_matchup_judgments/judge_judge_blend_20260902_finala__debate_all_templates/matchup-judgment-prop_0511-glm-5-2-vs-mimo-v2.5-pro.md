### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won on the decisive comparative question. PRO's core case held up across the exchange: LVT creates continuous carrying-cost pressure on idle land while transaction taxes only spike at acquisition and then vanish from the calculus entirely. The capitalization tension B pressed throughout—either the tax is fully capitalized (no new development pressure) or it is not (investment deterrent)—was answered adequately: the structure and timing of obligations matter behaviorally even if total present value is identical, and ongoing annual costs create a different incentive profile than a one-time upfront charge. More critically, Side A turned the capitalization argument back on CON: if capitalization is the problem, it applies to transaction taxes as well, and those taxes add acquisition friction precisely where redevelopment most needs to begin. Side B never resolved this symmetry. The most decisive gap in the debate was Side B's failure to establish a positive supply mechanism for the alternatives. B claimed only a comparative burden—LVT need not be a supply miracle if it doesn't clearly outperform—but under repeated pressure B never explained how transaction taxes or rent caps improve housing construction. Rent caps were effectively conceded on supply grounds (B acknowledged they protect tenants, not supply). Transaction taxes with exemptions were offered as an answer, but that design only removes a problem inherent to transaction taxes rather than creating supply improvement. Side A's clipped speeches (Rebuttal 1, Rebuttal 2, Closing) are a real execution cost, but the preserved content of those speeches was strong enough that the unfinished tails did not cost A the critical arguments. The proposition's claim—that LVT does more for supply and public finance than the named alternatives—survived the exchange intact because B could not offer an alternative mechanism that actually outperforms on supply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was Side A's response to the capitalization contradiction in A4.1–A4.2 and A5.2–A5.3: PRO acknowledged that capitalization lowers purchase prices but argued the ongoing annual obligation still creates distinct behavioral pressure absent in transaction taxes. Side B's counter—that total cost of ownership is unchanged if fully capitalized—was coherent but failed to address the timing and behavioral asymmetry, and PRO successfully turned the same critique against transaction taxes (also capitalized into lower sale prices, but then exerting zero ongoing development pressure). This exchange ended with Side A holding the cleaner comparative claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON built a cleaner path to victory by exposing a sustained internal tension in PRO's supply mechanism: PRO could not reconcile the claim that LVT is capitalized into lower land prices with the claim that it creates new carrying-cost pressure to develop, a contradiction CON showed was double-counting rather than complementarity. CON also demonstrated that PRO's supply story relied on speculative political behavior unsupported by evidence, while PRO's Rebuttal 1, Rebuttal 2, and Closing were all clipped for exceeding word limits, leaving key comparative claims unfinished and creating a real execution cost. PRO's asymmetry argument—that alternatives are worse—was plausible but never squarely resolved the capitalization critique that became the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's capitalization critique in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2, which charged that PRO could not simultaneously claim LVT capitalizes into lower purchase prices and generates new ongoing development pressure, forcing PRO into an unresolved theoretical contradiction that persisted through the closing.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A wins by consistently forcing the debate onto comparative ground, where Side B struggled to demonstrate that transaction taxes or rent caps outperform LVT on the motion's explicit metrics. While Side B landed a sophisticated critique on the tension between capitalization and carrying-cost pressure, Side A effectively neutralized it by showing that capitalization similarly shrinks transaction tax bases while transaction taxes lack LVT's ongoing development incentive. Side B's concession that rent caps do not boost supply effectively ceded half the comparison, and the defense of transaction taxes relied on policy carve-outs that implicitly conceded base-level flaws. Side A's framing—that holding regulatory constraints equal, LVT pushes incentives toward construction while the alternatives push away—provided the cleaner, more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the capitalization pressure (A4.1-A4.3), which clarified that lower acquisition costs and ongoing carrying costs are complementary rather than contradictory, and immediately turned the critique back onto transaction taxes by noting they face similar capitalization effects while adding acquisition friction and zero ongoing use pressure.
