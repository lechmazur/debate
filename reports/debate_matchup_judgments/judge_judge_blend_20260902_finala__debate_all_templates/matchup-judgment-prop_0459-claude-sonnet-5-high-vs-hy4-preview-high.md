### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully defending a distinct alternative to the motion and maintaining independent offense on climate deployment.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing deftly answered Side B's "sunsettable trap" by explaining that if a temporarily guaranteed plant fails, dependency doesn't return because the network—not the individual plant—is the insurance.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the motion’s actual tradeoff live: forced localization via subsidies and local-content rules still leaves input chokepoints, raises costs that slow deployment, and invites silos/retaliation, while allied buffers (stockpiles, recycling, sunsettable first-of-kind support) buy substitutability without that permanent premium. PRO’s strongest line—that CON’s fix is industrial policy in denial, and midstream capacity can be rebuilt—was sharp and forced real answers, but CON’s insurance-versus-underwriting distinction and the embargo-among-allies reply held up better under pressure. PRO also softened pure localization into ally-inclusive content, which blunted the motion more than it saved it. Clear but not dominant edge to B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 / close: public money for narrow buffers ≠ permanent local-content localization; network substitutability, not one subsidized flag-plant, is the insurance (directly answering A’s “same tool” pressure).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by offering a coherent, structurally distinct alternative to the motion’s policy. While PRO effectively pressed that any supply-chain security requires public investment, CON successfully distinguished between temporary, sunsettable insurance mechanisms (stockpiles, recycling, R&D, allied loan guarantees) and the motion’s permanent subsidies with local-content mandates that raise consumer prices, slow clean-tech deployment, and invite trade retaliation. CON’s argument that localization often leaves true input chokepoints intact—whereas allied diversification plus buffers creates real substitutability—survived PRO’s midstream-processing counter, and CON’s deployment-cost impact was under-addressed by PRO. The debate narrowed to whether CON’s plan was “industrial policy in denial”; CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing made the categorical difference clear enough to prefer their path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over whether CON’s buffer strategy was merely a disguised version of PRO’s industrial policy. In Rebuttal 2, PRO argued CON’s loan guarantees and allied capacity-build were subsidies by another name, forcing CON to either admit the motion or embrace theater. CON answered sharply by distinguishing sunsettable insurance and network substitutability from permanent, uncompetitive production subsidies, a distinction it sustained through the Closing.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A decisively won the strategic clash by exposing a fatal contradiction in Side B's case. When B tried to solve the cost of redundant plants by proposing an "allied compact" where allies specialize in different links, A correctly pointed out that this abandons B's core "domestic sovereignty" warrant and collapses into A's own allied-hub model. Furthermore, A's argument that localizing processing still leaves countries dependent on imported upstream ore successfully neutralized B's autonomy claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly combined the upstream dependency argument (magnet plants still need imported ore) with the observation that B's "allied compact" conceded the need for multilateral reliance, completely dismantling B's case for independent domestic sovereignty.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON won a competitive debate by keeping the comparison on the motion’s real cost: whether localization is the right insurance instrument. CON repeatedly forced PRO onto full domestic chokepoint ownership rather than cheaper allied tools, then showed that one domestic link still leaves upstream ore/precursor dependence while baking in a permanent price premium. PRO’s “who can say no / exercisable without permission” framing was sharp and its pressure questions on stranded hubs and expiring stockpiles were strong, but the compact of caps/sunsets/no-export-ban rules looked like a political wish-list that conceded CON’s coordination logic without defeating capture or expected-value insurance. CON made the cheaper contingent path (diversify + reserves + crisis-triggered allied buildout) easier to prefer after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s upstream-dependency / sovereignty-illusion hit (B4.2 into B5.1): localizing one link relocates the chokepoint and still leaves you paying for autonomy you do not fully get.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO's model of domestic localization fails to achieve true supply-chain sovereignty: domestic processing plants still depend on imported ores and precursors, merely relocating the chokepoint upstream while permanently raising costs. PRO never squarely answers this upstream-dependency turn, and CON's alternative—diversified allied hubs with contingent buildout—delivers comparable resilience more cheaply. PRO's "bounded allied compact" concededly adopts CON's multilateral logic while adding an unnecessary domestic tax, undermining the case for unilateral localization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal and closing argument that domestic ownership of a single supply-chain link creates an "illusion of sovereignty" because critical inputs (rare-earth ore, battery precursors) remain imported, permanently raising consumer prices without eliminating the real chokepoint.
