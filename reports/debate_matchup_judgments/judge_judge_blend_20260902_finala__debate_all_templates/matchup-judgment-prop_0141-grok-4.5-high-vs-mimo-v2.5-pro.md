### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A built a clean, consistent causal story (the "first mirror" a minor sees is already algorithmically altered) and defended it through every round: distinguishing default alteration from creative opt-in, distinguishing self-face exposure from magazine imagery, and tying the age-line to standard legal/developmental minority protections. A also correctly followed the required A1/A2 answer format, engaging both of B's pressure questions head-on. B raised the debate's most damaging structural point—that letting platforms self-label which effects count as "face-slimming" invites incentive-driven relabeling—which A never fully neutralized, only reasserting that labeled categories exist. But B undercut its own momentum in two ways: its answer to A's Q1 (how do parental tools/education outrun thousands of automatic exposures before they "kick in") was evaded rather than answered, instead re-litigating the age-line point; and both B's opening and closing were clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning key crystallizing content was never seen. Those are real execution costs under the judging rules. On balance, A's mechanism argument stood up better across rounds, A handled pressure more cleanly, and B's strongest weapon (enforcement/self-adjudication) was a real but partial dent rather than a case-ending blow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's turn 8 point that "the company that names a preset 'face warp' is the best judge of whether it qualifies... but that same company profits from the effect" was the most damaging unresolved hit of the debate; A's closing reply ("ordinary product design... not a guessing game left to their incentives") reasserted rather than refuted it, leaving this as B's strongest live thread even though it wasn't enough to overturn A's core mechanism case.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): B PRO wins on decisive clash: automatic self-face rewrite as first mirror teaches real face as defective, distinct from magazines/other-images.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.4 - CON's profit incentive attack that letting the platform that markets 'face warp' adjudicate its own compliance turns clear product rule into creative relabeling and self-policing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A built a much more compelling and memorable case around the "first mirror" mechanism, decisively defeating Side B's magazine analogy and exposing the "inverted priority" of relying on after-the-fact media literacy. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but B ultimately controlled the decisive clash better. A's "default as endorsement" framing and its claim that a platform-wide opt-in default "eliminates the outlier problem entirely" was the crux of PRO's rebuttal to CON's strongest point (that a natural-face default isolates the unfiltered minor amid a still-filtered peer/influencer/adult environment). B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing directly exposed the incompleteness of that fix — since opt-in minors, non-minors, influencers, and other platforms remain filtered, the "gap" A claimed would vanish does not actually close — and A's Closing only dismissed this as an argument about "scope, not logic" rather than answering it substantively. That left a live, unresolved route for CON. Additionally, A's speeches were repeatedly clipped for exceeding the word limit (opening, rebuttal 1, and closing), a real execution cost per the judging frame, while B stayed within limits and cleanly executed the required Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure throughout. A's pressure questions were sharp and exposed a real tension in CON's case (sticky-toggle vs. friction, sophistication paradox), but B's answers, while not flawless, sufficiently blunted them, whereas CON's own pressure question about isolation was never fully neutralized. On balance, CON provided the cleaner, more resilient path to a decision, aided by cleaner execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) and Closing (B5.1), arguing that PRO's "platform-wide fix" for the outlier problem fails because minors who still opt in, plus non-minor influencers and other platforms, keep the visual environment idealized — a point PRO's Closing acknowledged but never actually rebutted, leaving it as CON's clearest surviving line of attack.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO won the central trade-off. B showed the opt-in costs one sticky tap while a beauty-altering default acts as an endorsement that teaches the real face is inadequate, and distinguished subtle slimming/smoothing from cartoon play. CON's friction/accessibility case collapsed after conceding sticky settings work both ways, and its outlier claim was answered by B's platform-wide fix. A never resolved why the many who dislike filters should bear automatic distortion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.2 + B3.1 pressure: if filters are so wanted opt-in costs one tap, so default must be imposing an unwanted beauty norm — exposing that sticky-toggle logic favors natural default.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A built a significantly more convincing case by framing defaults as active endorsements and exposing the contradiction in Side B's "friction" argument (if opting in is just one tap, it's not a real burden; if it is a burden, the default is coercive). Side A also decisively neutralized Side B's strongest point—the "outlier" effect—by pointing out the systemic, platform-wide nature of the proposed policy. Although Side A suffered an execution cost from being clipped at the end of three speeches, the core arguments were fully developed, logically tighter, and far more persuasive than Side B's defense of the status quo.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A2), where they brilliantly turned Side B's "outlier" argument on its head by pointing out that a platform-wide default change eliminates the peer outlier effect entirely, forcing Side B to weakly pivot to adults and external platforms.
