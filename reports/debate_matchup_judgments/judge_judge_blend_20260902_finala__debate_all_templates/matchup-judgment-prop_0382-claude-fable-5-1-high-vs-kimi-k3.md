B’s clipped opening was an execution cost, but its later comparison and weighing overcame it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s decisive response separated the tailored pre-market exam from post-clearance market terms, then showed that conventional meats already face hazard-specific testing under a shared regulatory framework. This directly undercut Side A’s claim that any distinct test or label necessarily defeats parity.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing response differentiating steers (which cannot drift biologically) from cell lines (which do) decisively neutralized Side A's attempt to equate genomic verification with conventional batch testing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): PRO won a competitive definitional clash by keeping standards (the exam, including novel hazards) distinct from post-clearance basic terms, forcing CON’s “no bans / tailored path / harmonize later” into near-alignment with the motion. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by giving the more natural meaning to “same basic terms”: equal legal standing within a common hazard-based framework, not identical tests for unlike production systems. Side A identified real product-specific controls, but did not show why tailored controls, labels, or pre-market safety review defeat basic parity, especially when the motion expressly conditions legalization on meeting safety standards. Side B also retained largely unanswered benefits concerning competition, animal welfare, land use, and antibiotics. Both sides incurred clipping costs, and Side A mounted a sharp semantic challenge, but Side B made the motion easier to affirm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was its answer that different hazards do not imply a different basic regime: oysters, canned meat, ground beef, and cultivated meat can require distinct controls while sharing inspection powers, truthful-labeling rules, and market access. This directly undercut Side A’s effort to turn every tailored assay or disclosure into proof of unequal terms.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully controlling the definition of "same basic terms," proving that the biological realities of cultivated meat require unique regulatory mechanics that cannot be dismissed as mere paperwork. Side B highlighted specific mechanisms (like cell-line authentication and lack of metabolism) that Side A ultimately failed to fully address.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively dismantled Side A's "feed" analogy by pointing out that conventional feed rules assume an animal's metabolism filters the inputs, whereas in cultivated meat, the medium is the plate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by making the motion text the decisive clash rather than prohibition. After correctly discarding PRO's ban-focused opening, CON consistently showed that PRO's reading absorbed pre-market novel-food gates, distinct mandatory origin labels, specialized batch/medium controls, and split oversight into "same basic terms" until the phrase meant little more than legal sale under some inspection. PRO answered well on framework parity, HACCP flexibility, and CON's own farm-raised/from-concentrate examples, and handled pressure format cleanly, but never stabilized a non-vacuous meaning of "same" against CON's product-structure objections (no metabolism/clearance curve; cell-line and medium rules with no carcass ancestor). CON's later speeches kept narrowing that definitional failure; PRO's closing reasserted that CON's preferred regime equals the motion, which conceded CON's policy substance while still failing to carry the ballot language.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R1 (B2.1–B2.4): catching PRO rewriting "same basic terms" to include the distinct gate, handoffs, and "cell-cultivated" label CON had demanded, then declaring the label clash over.
