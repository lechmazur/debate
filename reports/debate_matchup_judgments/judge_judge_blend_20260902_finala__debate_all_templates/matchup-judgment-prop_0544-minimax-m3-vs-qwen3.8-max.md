### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side B won by consistently separating reduced legal sales from reduced access and deaths. Side A established firearm lethality and a plausible benefit from delay, especially for suicide, but never grounded how many affected young adults depend on their own retail purchase rather than existing household or informal access. Its comparative claims relied on unsupported prevalence assertions and lethality figures that addressed substitution to less lethal methods better than substitution to other guns. Side B kept the burden focused on the policy-specific net effect and showed that Side A had not completed that comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s durability turn was decisive: existing firearms can remain immediately accessible even if new retail purchases decline. Its follow-up that the firearm-versus-pills lethality gap does not establish how often the age restriction actually prevents access directly exposed the missing link in Side A’s case.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON won by keeping the motion’s comparative burden live: PRO had to show net rate reductions larger than added illegal acquisition and substitution, yet mostly proved fewer first legal sales plus a lethality gap. CON repeatedly damaged the retail-chokepoint story (household/borrowed/street access already dominates crisis use; intermediaries are friends/roommates, not just professional straw buyers), and forced the durability point against PRO. PRO’s impulsivity and case-fatality framing was the strongest affirmative mechanism, especially on suicide, but never cleanly showed those gains beat the now-and-informal channel shift CON identified. After pressure rounds, CON’s answers kept magnitude pressure on PRO; PRO’s “meaningful/tractable” retreat left the net comparison unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R1 turn that a 21 limit mainly illicitizes friend/relative transfers (loan/gift/glovebox) rather than stacking pressure on professional straw buyers, converting PRO’s barrier into less-visible illegal acquisition.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won the comparative weighing the motion requires. A established peak-risk window, impulsivity, and an order-of-magnitude lethality gap, and argued retail first-purchases create the household guns that drive crisis access. CON described a plausible shift to loans/gifts/street guns but never quantified it, conceding it is hard to count, while PRO hammered direction-is-not-magnitude in Pressure and Rebuttal 2. A's answer that retail need not be primary, only a tractable chokepoint that shrinks the pool, survived B's traceability challenge and left CON with mechanism only, no net-loss math.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A3/A4 attack on illegal-acquisition magnitude – PRO exposed CON's failure to quantify the increase and reframed household access as created by the very first retail purchase CON dismissed.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A won by supplying the cleaner comparative mechanism: blocked legal access creates delay and friction, while substitution to less lethal methods can reduce deaths. Side B plausibly showed that household and illicit access remain, but repeatedly asserted rather than demonstrated that these channels would absorb enough demand to outweigh the benefit. Its claims that illegal channels are frictionless, untraceable, and already absorb demand were overbroad, and its first rebuttal was clipped. Side A overstated matters by suggesting CON needed perfect substitution and occasionally shifted the affirmative burden, but still gave the stronger net-effects case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s response to the household-access and overdose argument was decisive: household guns are not universally immediate, and overdose generally leaves more opportunity for survival than a firearm attempt. Side B established leakage but not equally lethal or sufficiently widespread replacement.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO won by consistently forcing the comparative onto imperfect friction and lethality gaps rather than total elimination. CON mounted a strong channel-based case (household pathways for suicide; straw/theft pipelines for homicide) and rightly noted the large existing stock, but repeatedly needed near-complete and equally lethal substitution to negate the age barrier. PRO’s replies kept that assumption explicit, answered the pressure questions directly on lethality and marginal buyers, and crystallized the net-death standard more cleanly. CON’s clipped first rebuttal and occasional burden-shifting slightly weakened execution against an otherwise competitive performance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 1 and later crystallizations of CON’s perfect-substitution assumption (vs. real friction/delay/less-lethal means) most decisively protected the net-reduction claim.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO frames the comparative correctly: friction, delay, and less-lethal substitution can save lives even if some substitution occurs. CON requires near-perfect replacement via household closets and illegal markets, but concedes that route now needs another person, locked storage, money, trust, and arrest risk, and never shows equal lethality. CON's R1 clipped for word limit and its A2 in R2 reasserts displacement without answering PRO's lethality gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's R1 [A2.1-A2.6] and R2 answers [A4.1-A4.4] exposing CON's perfect-substitution assumption and turning household access into friction plus showing overdose/other-weapon substitution is generally less lethal.
