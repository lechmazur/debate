### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K2.6 (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins a clear but competitive debate on three live routes. First, the homeostasis-bypass mechanism—that GLP-1s suppress appetite centrally and favour fat oxidation in ways no meal plan can replicate—was introduced cleanly in the opening and never genuinely refuted. CON's counter that appetite suppression causes muscle catabolism that erases the benefit is plausible in theory but sits in tension with PRO's point that athletes can still hit protein targets strategically, and that even partial lean-mass retention outperforms rivals constrained by biological set-points. Second, PRO's demand argument ("if the net effect were negative, demand would vanish, not surge") was introduced in Rebuttal 1 and repeated in every subsequent speech. CON's best answer—athletes use these drugs for medical necessity, sponsor pressure, or weight-class cuts unrelated to endurance—is partially credible but leaves the performance-seeking subset unaddressed and never reconciles why informed elite competitors in cycling, MMA, and rowing would absorb known GI side effects unless the calculus favoured them. Third, PRO successfully maintained throughout that stricter scrutiny ≠ prohibition, directly defusing CON's most emotionally resonant concern about barring sick athletes. CON's TUE-backlog argument (diagnosis can't distinguish legitimate from off-label users) is the strongest counter, and PRO's "fix the paperwork" response is thinner than it should be, but CON never quantified the concrete harm beyond assertion, limiting its weight. CON's best standalone argument—that unregulated dangerous weight-cutting is the real fairness threat—never actually engaged the motion as written; pointing to a worse problem does not defeat the claim that GLP-1s also warrant scrutiny. PRO's grounding is consistently plausible and mechanism-rooted; CON's includes a few overspecific calorie-count asides that add little. Both sides answer the Q1/Q2 format correctly and with reasonable directness. On balance, PRO built a more coherent path through the exchange, answered pressure more squarely, and left more live routes standing at the close.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO edges this debate on the three most consequential live arguments. First, the motion's threshold is "stricter scrutiny," a deliberately low bar—even genuine uncertainty about mechanism argues for oversight rather than against it, a burden CON never squarely addressed. Second, PRO's equity argument ran throughout and was never neutralized: zero-scrutiny status quo already lets wealthy athletes purchase unpenalized GLP-1 use, while CON's counter (TUE systems also advantage wealthy athletes) merely shows imperfect regulation, not worse regulation than none. Third, CON's proposed alternative—hydration testing and same-day weigh-ins—explicitly concedes the weight-class problem while leaving the endurance sports angle entirely unaddressed; PRO correctly called this out and CON never recovered. CON's strongest argument—the body weight continuum making TUE categorically harder than EPO or steroid exemptions—was genuine and creative, and PRO never fully engaged with the "discrete pathology vs. continuum" distinction. Likewise, CON's clinical-population challenge (reduced exertion appears in sedentary or diabetic cohorts, not conditioned elites) was a live methodological criticism that PRO reasserted around rather than answered head-on. These are real gaps for PRO. However, CON's closing strawman—claiming PRO had "admitted" distinct benefits that CON consistently disputed—slightly undermined CON's credibility at a key moment. On balance, PRO maintained more live, unanswered routes to victory: the endurance angle, the equity asymmetry, and the low regulatory bar of "scrutiny." The margin is real but competitive because CON's mechanism challenge and continuum argument had genuine force that PRO left partially standing.
