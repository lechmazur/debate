- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON wins by exposing that the proposition’s safeguards do not actually prevent the central harm of state favoritism among religions. PRO conceded that limited slots mean “every sect need not receive identical slots,” defended by interest and space. Side A argued that religious instruction could be managed like Model UN or band, using neutral criteria like student interest and space. Side B correctly noted that a secular ethics alternative solves religion-versus-secular neutrality but does nothing to cure inter-denominational favoritism. Side A failed to resolve this structural dilemma in the final speeches, relying on repetitive assertions about ordinary elective management. Side B also provided a much cleaner distinction between after-hours facility access (a neutral private forum) and daytime scheduling (institutional integration), neutralizing Side A's logistical fairness arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing exchange on denominational favoritism. When Side A conceded that not every sect needs a slot and relied on "student interest" as a neutral criterion, Side B sharply countered that this simply allows popular faiths to win state-facilitated platforming while small faiths lose, proving the proposition's "neutral menu" fails its own promise of equality.
