### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A won clearly by turning the debate from abstract privacy panic to comparative implementation. B had a plausible core claim that instant identification itself destroys anonymity, but A repeatedly answered that glasses require databases, so upstream licensing, consented galleries, auditing, and conduct crimes target the mechanism better than a hardware ban. A also exposed a major motion problem: B's assistive-use carveout for face-blind citizens was not a professional exception and effectively conceded targeted regulation.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to the enforceability and survivor questions was decisive: a ban must detect invisible, toggled software on ordinary-looking glasses, while targeted regulation can operate at software, database, and logging chokepoints; the ban also disarms compliant vulnerable users while doing less to stop bad actors.

A's clipped rebuttal and later concessions weakened their route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1/B4.5 on A's concession rewriting the professional exception into targeted regulation, neutralizing A's ban framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing that Side A's framework quietly abandoned the motion's text—certified assistive use for ordinary citizens is targeted regulation, not a "narrowly defined professional exception." A's clipped first rebuttal and failure to answer B's central pressures in the closing sealed the loss.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing turning the survivor/abuser symmetry and showing that A's carve-out for assistive use collapsed into B's regulatory framework, while the ban left the vulnerable disarmed and the powerful exempt.
