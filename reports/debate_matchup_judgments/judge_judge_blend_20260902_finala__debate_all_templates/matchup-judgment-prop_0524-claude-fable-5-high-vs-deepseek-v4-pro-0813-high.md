B showed that linking allocations automatically to those shortages can convert failures outside an institution’s control into fewer visas while withholding the revenue that could expand capacity. B’s levy mechanism was legitimate narrow support answering A’s direct question, not an unrelated late case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sequence rebuttal was decisive: where capacity is short, the motion first cuts allocations rather than producing housing or services. A answered that institutions would pre-build because they were motivated, but B showed that motivation already exists and does not overcome local vetoes or financing constraints; the cap instead makes obstruction automatically reduce migration.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive mechanism clash: the motion’s first response to short capacity is a withheld allocation, which removes the tuition/payroll/tax revenue that could fund housing and services and gives councils a quiet automatic kill switch. CON’s later levy detail fairly operationalized an already-stated alternative and better resolved funding sequence and veto asymmetry.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.5): shortfall triggers visa denial first, making obstruction costless and destroying the revenue base for capacity expansion.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B decisively dismantled Side A's core mechanism by exposing a fatal structural catch-22: withholding migration allocations to enforce capacity building simultaneously withholds the very revenue (tuition, payroll, taxes) required to fund that construction. Side B's introduction of a levy in Rebuttal 2 was a legitimate, direct answer to Side A's pressure question, not an unfair late add-on.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing explanation of the "sequence" problem—showing that denying a visa to enforce a housing cap destroys the revenue needed to build the housing, thereby locking in scarcity and making local obstruction costless.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that worker portability severs the proposition's defining link between institutional allocations and local capacity. A persuasively defended fixed-term guarantees against the claim that guarantees must last forever, but its closing reliance on aggregate admissions did not explain how locally measured capacity remains meaningful when migrants can relocate. B also sustained credible pressure on productive-city rationing, metric gaming, and NIMBY vetoes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to portability was decisive: either workers remain sponsor- or place-dependent, creating exploitation risks, or they move freely and the local-capacity allocation no longer tracks where housing and services are used. A endorsed portability without supplying a transfer or reallocation mechanism.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by landing two structural dilemmas PRO never neutralized: the guarantee/cap “scheduled dilemma” and the portability dilemma that makes the capacity tie fiction for mobile workers. PRO’s own closing admission that the cap controls aggregate places not individual movement gutted the motion’s distinctive mechanism. B remained competent on planning realism but lost the decisive comparison after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s portability horn (A2.3/A4.3) plus closing crystallisation of B5.3: free movement dissolves the local-capacity link that is the motion’s whole selling point.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by systematically dismantling the two core mechanisms of Side A's case. First, B exposed the temporal contradiction between a "guarantee" and a moving "cap," forcing A to concede that the guarantee only applies to short tranches, which undermines the long-term planning benefits A claimed in the opening. Second, and more decisively, B trapped A on the portability dilemma. When A embraced worker portability to avoid exploitation, B correctly pointed out in the closing that this severs the "local capacity" tie entirely, as workers can freely move to areas that lack the measured capacity to support them. B's rebuttals were sharper, the pressure questions were perfectly executed, and the closing crystallized the fatal structural flaw in A's model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing on the portability dilemma, where B forced A to choose between worker exploitation and severing the local capacity link, and then capitalized on A's choice to prove the motion's defining feature fails as written.
