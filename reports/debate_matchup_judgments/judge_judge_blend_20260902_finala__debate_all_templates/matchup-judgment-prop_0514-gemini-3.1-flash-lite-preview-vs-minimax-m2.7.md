- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (CON)): A won by making the motion-specific distinction that open standards and portability need not ban integrated solutions, while B repeatedly treated the proposal as forced fragmentation. B never squarely answered the renewal-price, deprecation, and lock-in pressure, leaving A's governance and bargaining-leverage route live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was against the "single throat to choke" claim: it argued that nominal accountability without an exit option is weak leverage, and B's response focused on failure resolution while largely dodging price exploitation and platform capture.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the stronger path to victory by reframing procurement as a governance choice between public control and private dependency. A never squarely answered B's pressure on vendor price-gouging, deprecation, or security-failure risk, and A's closing merely restated the unfunded mandate claim without resolving B's live argument that portability creates negotiating leverage. B's progression from interoperability to democratic accountability to leverage narrowed the clash effectively and left a clearer route to ballot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 inversion of CON's switching-cost argument, showing that high migration costs make lock-in dangerous rather than acceptable and that portability supplies essential negotiating leverage precisely because exit is costly—combined with the reframe that procurement under open standards uses SLAs and conformance testing rather than forcing institutions to become systems integrators.
