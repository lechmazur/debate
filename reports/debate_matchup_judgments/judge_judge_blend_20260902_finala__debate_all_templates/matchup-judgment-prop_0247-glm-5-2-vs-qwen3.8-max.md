### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won by showing that partial reductions in sugary-drink consumption can still produce meaningful health benefits and by weighing the avoidable tax burden against larger, entrenched disease costs. Side B sharply identified regressivity and substitution, but repeatedly treated leakage as if it eliminated all benefit, without adequately grounding that claim or showing its proposed alternatives would replace the tax's price effect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered that prevention need not eliminate every substitute calorie, defended broad coverage and water access as leakage controls, and established that imperfect prevention can still outweigh a modest, avoidable tax cost.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): PRO won by locking the decisive comparison as lesser avoidable tax cost versus greater entrenched disease cost on the same households, then showing CON's substitution and revenue critiques prove only that prevention is imperfect rather than that the status quo is preferable. CON landed real damage with the stacking argument and the point that price sensitivity hits the poorest hardest, but never neutralized PRO's core claim that a visible shelf signal still beats ongoing medical and wage losses, nor showed alternatives fully replace the price mechanism the motion requires.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / closing flip: CON's "certain extraction vs speculative prevention" frame actually condemns the status quo harder, because disease costs are already certain, compounding, and more regressive.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO kept the decisive comparison alive: disease costs are certain, compounding, and more regressive than the tax. CON's substitution = addition-not-replacement attack was sharp, but PRO answered it with the liquid-sugar specificity, broad-base plus water fix, and avoidable vs unavoidable burden framing, which CON conceded is more regressive in principle. CON never closed the gap that even leaky prevention displaces some of the worst harm while tax is avoidable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Rebuttal 2 B4 A1-A2 plus the break paragraph: PRO turned CON's concession that disease is more regressive into the weighing test, and showed substitution is mitigable without losing the core price-signal benefit.
