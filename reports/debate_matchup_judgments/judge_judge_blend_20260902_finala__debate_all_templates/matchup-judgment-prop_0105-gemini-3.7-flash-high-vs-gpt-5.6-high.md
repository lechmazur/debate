### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the live clash on the proposition’s decision rule: temporary, judicial, reviewable removal when credible evidence shows imminent risk, not after conviction. A’s asymmetry (reversible temporary deprivation vs irreversible death), proportionate-middle framing, and threshold-gap argument outlasted B’s strongest attack—that real imminent cases are already covered by arrest/psych holds and red-flag tools are security theater. B repeatedly pressed due-process costs, false positives, and unmanaged residual risk, which forced A to specify safeguards, but B never fully closed the gap that different legal thresholds answer different questions. B’s answers treated unmet criminal/clinical bars as proof of mere suspicion, while A showed why that collapses cases with specific threats/displays that still fall short of confinement or completed-crime proof. Across rounds A adapted better, answered pressure more cleanly, and crystallized a clearer reason to affirm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 / A1–A2 answers: risk reduction by removing the threatened means need not solve every danger to be justified, and unmet crime/commitment thresholds do not make particularized imminent-risk evidence “speculative,” with prompt adversarial review carrying the process burden.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by establishing a coherent "proportionate middle" between total confinement and legally mandated helplessness. While CON landed sharp due-process and security-theater objections, PRO successfully defended that credible, specific evidence of imminent firearm violence can exist even when criminal or psychiatric-commitment thresholds are unmet, and that temporary, reviewable removal under judicial supervision is justified by the asymmetry between reversible error and irreversible death. CON's insistence that all genuine imminent threats already fit existing legal categories was plausible but slightly overstated, and PRO's answers to pressure questions were more complete and less assumptive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 [A2.6] and Rebuttal 2 [A4.4] reframing of the "gap" as a legitimate space for a narrower, less coercive intervention—arguing that if the state can confine the entire person under psychiatric holds, it can certainly impose the lesser restriction of temporary firearm removal—was the pivotal turn that CON never fully neutralize.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept the decisive gap argument live: some credible imminent firearm threats fail criminal and commitment thresholds, and a temporary, reviewable firearm separation justified by sworn, specific facts with prompt adversarial hearing is narrower than full detention. CON’s core counters – ex parte risk and security-theater – were mitigated by PRO’s parallelism point that removal can accompany evaluation and by the irreversibility asymmetry (repairable deprivation vs irreparable death). A’s pressure Q exposed CON’s lack of lawful pre-trigger option; CON’s pressure was answered with concrete safeguards. Despite clipped opening cost, PRO won weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.6/A4.4 – PRO's false-choice rebuttal that criminal charges require completed crime and psych holds detain the whole person, making temporary firearm removal the less-restrictive tailored middle, which CON never resolves in B4.
