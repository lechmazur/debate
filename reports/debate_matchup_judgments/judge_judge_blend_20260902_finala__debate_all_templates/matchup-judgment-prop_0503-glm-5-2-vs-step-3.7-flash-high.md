### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): B won by making the central test clearer: not whether Gulf states spend heavily on non-oil labels, but whether those sectors are becoming self-sustaining rather than continuously fed by hydrocarbon rents. A had a real answer that seed capital does not invalidate diversification, and the Dubai and reform examples kept the debate competitive. But A leaned on many brittle exact figures and made a notably weak analogy to a U.S. oil breakeven, while B used the fiscal breakeven, state procurement, petrochemical-adjacency, and structural-dependence arguments to show why A's metrics could overstate real transformation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal to A's seed-capital framing was most decisive: B conceded spending and some non-oil activity, but argued that continuous oil-funded procurement and high fiscal breakevens show dependence rather than successful diversification.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully proving that the Gulf's "non-oil" metrics are largely accounting illusions masking continued dependence on hydrocarbon rents. Side B's case suffered a fatal blow when it introduced a nonsensical "U.S. federal breakeven oil price" to excuse high Gulf breakevens, which Side A easily exposed as false. Side A's framing of the fiscal breakeven price as the ultimate test of substantive diversification provided a clear and decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which demolished Side B's "U.S. federal breakeven oil price" claim and solidified the fiscal breakeven metric as the definitive proof that Gulf economies remain structurally reliant on $70+ oil.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): CON wins by successfully shifting the evaluative frame from surface-level GDP and revenue statistics to structural fiscal dependency. While PRO assembled a competent case citing megaprojects, policy reforms, and non-oil growth figures, CON's introduction of the fiscal breakeven oil price and the "private on paper, public in substance" framing effectively demonstrated that much of the cited diversification remains circularly funded by hydrocarbon rents. PRO's defense, particularly the unsupported U.S. breakeven comparison and the attempt to dismiss high GCC breakevens as mere "optional spending," failed to sever the dependency link. CON cleanly explained that the "optional" spending is the diversification program itself, proving the circularity. CON's closing was clipped, incurring a minor execution cost per the rules, but the substantive clash was already decisively resolved through superior weighing, metric selection, and direct rebuttal work in earlier rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of PRO's fiscal breakeven defense. PRO attempted to normalize GCC breakeven prices by comparing them to the U.S. and labeling Gulf spending as optional; CON cleanly exposed the false equivalence (the U.S. does not fund core operations on oil rents) and reframed the "optional" spending as the diversification projects themselves, proving the circular dependency that ultimately decided the round.
