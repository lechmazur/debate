### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won by locking the burden to “name a better achievable rival,” then forcing B’s confederation into a no-win: either it presupposes two sovereignties (and is partition-plus) or it is the harder political ask. The Ariel enforcement pressure exposed that B’s “rights-first” model still needs Palestinian jurisdiction over Israeli residents; B’s later answer effectively conceded the two-state core. B’s achievability attack on classic partition was real and sharp, but never established a superior reachable alternative, so A’s least-bad comparison survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1 plus Pressure Q2: confederation = harder partition, then “who arrests the settler in Ariel?” — B’s later “Palestinian police under Palestinian law” reply became A’s decisive closing crystal.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) controlled the comparative framework and collapsed Side A's confederal alternative into a harder variant of partition, not a genuine alternative. PRO's first rebuttal showed confederation presupposes two sovereignties and requires more, not less, of the same Knesset. The pressure question on who arrests a refusing settler forced CON to choose between conceding occupation or conceding Palestinian sovereignty over Israelis—both of which weakened the claim to have found a distinct achievable path. PRO also successfully parried the "thirty years of failure" point by showing that negotiators repeatedly converged on territorial maps, and crystallized the exchange with the memorable line that "zero signatures beats zero attempts." CON made a forceful case that mass evacuation is politically unachievable, but never demonstrated that its own rights-first arrangement had any constituency or clearer path through the Knesset.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal framing that confederation "presupposes two sovereignties" and is therefore "partition with a customs union attached," which recurred through the pressure questions and closing to show CON had not actually named a rival that was both different and easier to achieve.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by decisively neutralizing Side B's alternative. By forcing Side B to specify the enforcement mechanism for settlers in a confederal model during the Pressure Questions round, Side A exposed that Side B's plan either collapses into the two-state solution (via Palestinian territorial sovereignty and law) or demands an even less achievable political concession (Israel voluntarily handing criminal jurisdiction over hundreds of thousands of its citizens to Palestinian police). Side A's rhetorical trap and the crystallization of that clash in the closing speech provided a cleaner path to victory, proving that the two-state territorial framework remains the inescapable baseline for any achievable sovereignty. Side B argued strongly on the physical difficulties of evacuation, but failed to prove their confederal alternative was more politically achievable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they weaponized Side B's answer to the "who arrests the settler" pressure question to demonstrate that B's confederation model either requires an impossible surrender of Israeli jurisdiction or is just the two-state solution in disguise.
