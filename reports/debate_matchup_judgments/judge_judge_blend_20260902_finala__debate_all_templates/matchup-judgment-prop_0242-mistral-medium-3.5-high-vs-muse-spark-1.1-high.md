- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): B built a sharper, more consistent causal story (algorithmic virality removing opt-out and converting apolitical users) and used the pressure round decisively: the UK/Ofcom question exposed a real hole in CON's "cable-first" global evidence. B's case therefore survived the exchange more intact and gave a cleaner, more resolved path to the verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question on UK Ofcom bans ("what cable channel polarized Brexit if not Facebook?") forced A to retreat from cable news to print press in A4.2, which B then used in closing to show CON had effectively conceded that cable news itself didn't drive that case.

Additionally, Side A sharply dismantled Side B's timeline argument by distinguishing between 1990s 'sorting' and post-2010 'hatred/affective' polarization, which Side B failed to adequately answer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the UK/Brexit pressure, pointing out that Side B's reliance on print newspapers to explain UK polarization conceded the motion's premise regarding cable news.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by building a cleaner causal distinction and neutralizing CON's strongest timeline argument. PRO successfully separated partisan sorting in the 1990s-2000s cable era from the post-2010 surge in affective hatred and participatory extremism, mapping the latter to algorithmic social media. PRO also undermined CON's "pioneer beats imitator" frame by demonstrating that social now drives political incentives—cable chases viral tweets, and audiences migrate to platforms when hosts leave. By the closing, PRO's "opt-out" and "you are the broadcaster" crystallizations made the comparative burden clear: cable polarized audiences who chose to watch, while social removed choice and turned passive users into polarizing participants at global scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was PRO's rebuttal distinguishing sorting (1990s-2000s) from the post-2010 hatred spike, directly answering CON's timeline pressure and reversing the empirical burden to show that the most damaging polarization—dehumanization, family estrangement, and violence—accelerated when feeds replaced channels.
