### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won by centering the decisive fact that an unavoidable fee is part of the purchasing price, so displaying only the base figure impairs budgeting and comparison. Side A largely defended clearly disclosed, pre-order component pricing rather than the motion’s late bill-or-checkout revelation, and never established why restaurants could not display an inclusive price while separately explaining its food and service components. Its flexibility argument had some force, but did not outweigh B’s cleaner consumer-facing standard. B’s clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost, though not enough to reverse the result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal directly answered both questions and exposed the weakness in Side A’s “structured transparency” defense: because the charge is non-negotiable, the total is the number diners need for choice and budgeting, while the lower item figure remains partial. Side A answered clearly but continued to assume that inclusive prices necessarily erase cost breakdowns.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) successfully defended that clearly disclosed, separated mandatory service charges are structured transparency—not deception. It showed that Side B’s (PRO) promise of an “exact price” is undermined because tax is still added later, and that bundling everything into one menu figure erases useful cost-structure information and freezes operational flexibility. Side B’s clipped first rebuttal and its reliance on an unproven “dominant impression” psychology left its core claim that separation equals deception insufficiently defended against these direct responses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s demonstration that even under Side B’s proposed system tax would still be added later, exposing that the “price on the menu is the price you pay” standard is incomplete, and that structured lines disclose both the total and the reasoning behind it rather than collapsing them into an opaque sticker.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully framing mandatory service charges as inherently part of the meal's price, making their exclusion from the displayed per-item price deceptive regardless of secondary disclosure. Side A's sharpest rebuttal—that because the fee is non-negotiable, the breakdown between food and labor costs is irrelevant to the diner's purchasing decision—effectively dismantled Side B's primary offensive argument about "structured honesty." While Side B offered a competent defense regarding operational flexibility and multi-part pricing norms, it failed to overcome the intuitive and rigorously defended premise that displaying a partial price for a mandatory total is a bait-and-switch. Side A's minor execution cost (a clipped Rebuttal 1) did not materially hinder its clear path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A2), which argued that because mandatory charges are non-negotiable, separating them misrepresents them as optional add-ons and renders the food-vs-labor breakdown irrelevant to the diner's actual budgeting and purchasing decisions.
