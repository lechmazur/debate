### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): A built a coherent mechanistic case (trade friction as a permanent tax, investment stagnation, labor bottlenecks) but never squarely answered B's central empirical challenge: name a measurable divergence between actual Britain and a Remain counterfactual. B's repeated demand for observable evidence (Germany/France comparisons, post-pandemic growth data) exposed A's argument as unfalsifiable—crystallized devastatingly in B's closing "heads they win, tails we lose" line, which A had no chance to rebut and which A's own R2 language ("tracking peers proves suppressed potential") arguably invited. B also handled the Pressure Questions cleanly with proper A1/A2 answers and used the round to sharpen rather than repeat, notably reframing the counterfactual (a Britain that voted Leave but stayed would face its own investment-paralyzing uncertainty), which A's response only partially deflected. A's rebuttals were solid but often reasserted the mechanism without engaging the comparative data B kept raising, leaving B's core empirical challenge live at the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing turn on A's own concession—"Britain tracked its peers... that is itself proof of harm"—reframed as "heads they win, tails we lose," which decisively undercut the falsifiability of A's entire mechanistic framework and went unanswered.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) wins on the decisive "observable divergence" clash. PRO (B) established a plausible mechanism - friction as tax on trade, investment and labour - but never met A's pressure to show a measurable GDP gap versus peers. A hammered that the UK outgrew Germany since the pandemic and tracked France, that investment flatlined after 2008 not 2016 and was revised up, and that vacancies hit Germany too, while B retreated to an unfalsifiable 'suppressed potential' claim that tracking peers proves harm. B also correctly reframes the counterfactual as 'never happened' vs 'vote ignored', but fails to answer Q2 on where the large compounding cost appears in the data.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 turn on Germany: using B's own excuse (Russian gas, China demand) to prove outcomes are driven by shocks/structure not membership, plus the fixed-cost vs compounding rebuttal and the point that vacancy crises occurred inside the single market, directly undercutting friction-as-cause.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A built a highly effective empirical trap, demanding an observable divergence in macro growth that Side B could not provide without sounding like they were moving the goalposts. Side A's attack on the counterfactual (the political uncertainty of ignoring a 52-48 vote) and the sharp closing ("Heads they win, tails we lose") decisively undermined Side B's case. Side B relied heavily on standard economic theory but struggled to reconcile it with the actual post-pandemic growth data Side A presented.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing rebuttal, which sharply exposed the unfalsifiability of Side B's counterfactual by pointing out that Side B treated both lagging and matching European peers as proof of Brexit's harm.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A built a tightly causal case (trade friction, investment flatlining, sterling depreciation, labour shortages) and consistently converted B's rebuttals into further ammunition: pointing out that if the EU "chains" growth, Brexit should have produced acceleration rather than the observed deceleration; showing the vaccine story predates any legal Brexit freedom; and noting migration hit record highs despite the promised "points-based pivot." A's pressure question on the alleged debt-liability mechanism was sharp and exposed B's weakest, most speculative claim, and B's answer ("own resources ceiling") read as vague, unfalsifiable precision rather than a solid rebuttal. B's own pressure questions were reasonable but less damaging, and its answer to A's "name one indicator beating pre-2016 trend" query effectively conceded the point by retreating to "no European economy beat its trend," which A had already preempted with the "matching the wounded is not strength" framing. B's closing recapped rather than directly engaging that asymmetry. A's closing crystallized the clash (measured losses vs. speculative/hypothetical gains) more cleanly and left B's rebuttal structurally unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 point that EU membership "chaining" Britain to stagnation should predict acceleration after Brexit, not the observed deceleration, combined with the pressure-question exchange on the invented EU debt-liability mechanism, which B never satisfactorily grounded.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO built a measurable three-channel case — trade friction, flatlined business investment, and labour/sterling — and isolated it from common shocks by showing investment and goods-trade lagged peers. CON's counter relied on hypothetical EU liabilities with no identified mechanism and on matching Germany, which PRO turned: matching a Germany hammered by a Russian-gas shock Britain avoided is a wasted advantage, not parity. PRO answered pressure cleanly — vaccine using member-state powers in transition, VC/services booming pre-2016 inside the EU, OBR 4% net of services — while CON never named an aggregate beating its pre-2016 trend. Measurable losses beat imaginary gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2 — Germany explanation as gas/China/car shock not single-market proof, plus pre-2016 record that London VC and services surged inside the EU, reversing burden on never-enacted transaction tax/AI Act and exposing CON's unanswered debt mechanism and trend question.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B wins by systematically dismantling Side A's core comparisons and examples. B's rebuttal that the UK merely matching Germany's growth is actually underperformance, since Germany faced asymmetric shocks like the loss of Russian gas, devastates A's primary comparative metric. B also cleanly neutralizes A's vaccine and services arguments, while A fails to directly answer B's pressure question about beating pre-2016 trends, instead pivoting back to the flawed Germany comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the Germany comparison in Rebuttal 2, pointing out that Germany was hammered by unique energy and demand shocks, so the UK merely matching it represents a wasted advantage rather than parity.
