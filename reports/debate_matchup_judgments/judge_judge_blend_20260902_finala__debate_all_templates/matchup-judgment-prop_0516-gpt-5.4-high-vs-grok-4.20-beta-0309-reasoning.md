### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins because it identified and sustained the decisive mechanism flaw: if a university caps international admits but fills those seats with out-of-town domestic or graduate students who also rent off-campus, the cap relieves no housing pressure. Side B never answered this adequately. In B4.1, PRO claimed "swapping in some domestic students does not erase the net relief" but never explained where the relief comes from if the replacements are equally non-housed. Side B's best move—arguing international recruitment is the uniquely "unconstrained revenue-driven vector"—has some force but does not close the gap, because the harm is still unaccommodated demand regardless of passport. Side A also offered a clear, better-targeted alternative (constrain total enrollment to housing capacity; require beds for growth) that PRO never effectively neutralized—PRO's only response was to call it "neutral rules that let the profit motive persist," which was asserted rather than demonstrated. Side A answered the pressure questions directly with A1:/A2: labels and honestly conceded that total enrollment may need to pause, strengthening its credibility. The "beds not passports" framing crystallized the core comparison cleanly and was never dislodged. Side B had genuine force in the public-duty and externality arguments, but left the central mechanism question—why nationality is the right line rather than housing status—unresolved through every round, and that is the proposition's burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Q1 in the pressure round—"If a university cuts international admits but fills those seats with out-of-town domestic or graduate students who also rent nearby, what housing pressure has your cap actually relieved?"—was the most decisive exchange. It forced Side B to answer the replacement problem directly. Side B's response in B4.1 acknowledged replacements happen but claimed relief still exists without explaining the mechanism. Side A then quoted that answer in A4.5 and landed the unanswered rejoinder: "they never explain where that relief comes from if those replacements also rent nearby."
