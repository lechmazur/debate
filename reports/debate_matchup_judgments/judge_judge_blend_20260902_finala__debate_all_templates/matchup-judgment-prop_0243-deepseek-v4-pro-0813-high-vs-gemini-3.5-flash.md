CON (Side A) offered plausible mechanisms (parking-search elimination, first-mile/last-mile, VMT reduction from car-free households) but leaned on somewhat hand-wavy specifics (algorithmic dispatch, geofenced staging) without engaging the deadheading/circulating-vehicle rebuttal head-on, and its answer to B's Q2 pivoted to VMT instead of directly addressing the dominant-trip-type question, an evasion that weakened its pressure-round performance. B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly around bottleneck location versus citywide totals, leaving CON's periphery-focused defenses looking non-responsive to where congestion is actually produced.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal in [B2.1]-[B2.2], reframing ride-hail's "eliminated parking search" as merely relocated and multiplied curb/road friction (deadheading, staging, circling at hubs), which A never fully answered beyond asserting algorithmic efficiency without engaging the core displacement logic.

CON's counter-narrative – parking-search elimination, algorithmic staging, and car-free VMT reduction – was largely answered by PRO's response that ride-hail keeps moving, multiplies curb friction in nightlife/downtown areas, and mostly replaces non-car trips in the core, not long suburban drives. - **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A dominated the debate by correctly identifying that traffic congestion is a peak-load problem at specific spatial bottlenecks, rather than a citywide average of VMT or car ownership. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON correctly identified that the motion is inherently comparative — "made congestion worse" requires a counterfactual net-VMT comparison, not just a description of ride-hail's visible footprint. CON hammered this framing from the opening through both rebuttals and the closing, and PRO never actually performed the subtraction: deadheading shares and mode-shift percentages describe ride-hail's internal activity but never show that total city vehicle-miles rose net of displaced taxis, private trips, parking search, and avoided car ownership. PRO's strongest original contributions (deadheading, mode cannibalization, curb disruption) were vivid but repeatedly answered with the same unmet burden, and PRO's late introduction of a suspiciously precise "10-15% displacement" statistic in Rebuttal 2 is exactly the kind of brittle, unsupported figure the rules discount, weakening PRO's grounding. CON's pressure questions (on the missing counterfactual arithmetic) exposed a real, unresolved gap that PRO answered with assertion rather than subtraction, while CON's own answers to PRO's questions (conceptually distinguishing fleet-share deadheading from net city-wide VMT) were directly responsive. The debate ultimately turned on whether "presence equals net worsening," and CON's consistent exploitation of that gap, paired with cleaner epistemic discipline, gives it the clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.4, where CON directly rebuts PRO's 10-15%/40% deadheading statistics by showing they measure ride-hail's internal fleet activity, not net city-wide vehicle-miles displaced, exposing that PRO never completes the required subtraction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the decisive clash on physical space: ride-hail replaces stationary parked cars with vehicles circulating empty ~40% and pulls 85-90% of riders from transit/walk/bike, creating net VMT. CON's strongest line was the "overall/net counterfactual" critique, but it stayed largely defensive, never quantifying how ownership avoided or parking search could offset the massive transit-to-car shift PRO quantified in R2. PRO answered B's pressure directly with net arithmetic; CON's answers said surveys undercount avoided cars but offered no counter-math. Live route to victory remained PRO's spatial efficiency degradation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's R1 demand for net arithmetic [B2.1-B2.4] vs PRO's R2 answer giving 10-15% private-car replacement and framing remaining 85-90% as net additions [A4.1-A4.3] – the central net-vs-gross exchange that decided the debate.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully meeting Side B's central burden of proving the net counterfactual. While Side B effectively argued that PRO must subtract displaced private car trips and parking searches, Side A answered this in Rebuttal 2 by quantifying the mode-shift (85-90% of trips replace zero-car modes like transit or walking) and contrasting the continuous 40% active deadheading of ride-hail with the stationary nature of parked private cars. Side B's fallback arguments regarding "avoided car ownership" and "pooled rides" were less empirically grounded for dense major cities and failed to neutralize Side A's decisive framing of physical space and spatial efficiency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered Side B's pressure questions by providing the requested arithmetic: quantifying that only 10-15% of trips displace private cars, meaning the vast majority are net new car trips replacing space-efficient modes, and explaining why continuous fleet deadheading mathematically overwhelms brief private parking searches.
