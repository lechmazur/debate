### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by showing that DPR is physically capped by return flows minus consumptive losses and cannot scale with total urban growth, while diversified imports provide genuinely new volume and independent hydrological redundancy. Side A most decisively exploited Side B's admission that DPR "does not mean eliminating secondary backups," proving imports remain essential infrastructure rather than something DPR can be prioritized over. Side B never adequately answered the arithmetic ceiling on return flows or rebutted the diversification argument, instead repeating assertions about local sovereignty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal and closing, which identified Side B's concession on secondary backups as fatal to the "prioritize over" framing, and sharply distinguished portfolio diversification across independent basins from mere technical redundancy inside a single treatment train.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON's mass-balance ceiling [return flows < intake] and single-node risk were never closed by PRO. PRO's A4.1/A4.6 repeats 'multiplies productivity' and concedes secondary backups remain needed, which CON turns into a prioritization defeat, while PRO's drought/paper-water attack was answered by CON's diversification across independent basins and gradual-failure vs binary-failure framing. Pressure answers favored CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R1/R2 arithmetic rebuttal that DPR is capped by consumptive losses and cannot scale with population/outdoor growth, compounded by exposing A4.6's backup concession as collapsing the 'prioritize DPR over imports' claim.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a highly effective, physically grounded case focusing on the conservation of mass (DPR cannot create new water, only recycles a capped fraction) and risk diversification. Side A systematically dismantled Side B's claims, particularly in Rebuttal 2, where Side A brilliantly trapped Side B into conceding that DPR requires "secondary backups" and "modular" systems to mitigate single-point-of-failure risks. Side A's closing perfectly crystallized this concession: a city cannot prioritize a capped, supplemental source over the very diversified backups it admits it still needs. Side B argued well on the ecological and political risks of importing water, but failed to overcome the fundamental arithmetic and portfolio-diversification deficits of relying primarily on DPR.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which expertly turned Side B's answers to the pressure questions (admitting the need for modular plants and secondary backups) into a fatal concession that DPR is a supplement to, rather than a priority over, imported water.
