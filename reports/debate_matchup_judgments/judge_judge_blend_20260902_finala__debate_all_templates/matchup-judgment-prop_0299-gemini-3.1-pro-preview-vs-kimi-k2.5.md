- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins by establishing that cultural preservation is a public good subject to systematic charitable and market failures, while successfully neutralizing CON's sovereignty concerns. A's argument that private philanthropy structurally favors capital projects and metric-driven outputs over the long-term payroll stability required for living transmission directly answered the core question of how endangered languages actually survive. A also effectively neutralized B's "right of exit" defense by framing it as a death warrant for critically endangered communities, turning B's autonomy argument into a liability. While B landed strong blows regarding the political vulnerability of public funding and the ideological nature of cultural memory, A's statutory entitlement model and critique of foundation gatekeeping provided a clearer, more enforceable path to preservation under the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's turnaround of the "bureaucratic mandate" clash in Rebuttal 2 was decisive: by pointing out that private foundations impose rigid logic models, quantifiable outputs, and branding alignment, A successfully flipped B's state-control offense and reinforced why public statutory funding better supports organic, community-led transmission.
