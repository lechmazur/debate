- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO's trilemma framing was the structural anchor of the debate, and CON never fully neutralized it. CON's best counter—that criminal threat statutes already authorize preemptive seizure—is substantively weaker than PRO's response: those statutes require different processes, target a narrower set of relationships, and the fact that 19+ legislatures enacted separate red-flag statutes is strong circumstantial evidence of a perceived gap. CON's answer ("political momentum") was the weakest move in the debate and left the gap argument essentially standing. PRO's analogy to restraining orders—which also involve ex parte initial orders, no pre-deprivation cross-examination, and no criminal conviction—was apt and CON never cleanly defeated it. Crucially, CON's objections throughout pointed to implementation failures in some state statutes, which PRO correctly identified as conceding the principle of the motion rather than defeating it. The multiple clipped turns on PRO's side are an execution cost but do not change this fundamental assessment, as the core arguments were visible in each clipped speech. CON was sharper on format discipline but did not turn that into argumentative superiority on the decisive clashes.

CON never offered a workable alternative for this scenario, dismissing law enforcement preference for red-flag laws as mere "institutional hand-waving" without demonstrating that existing tools are actually adequate. That live unmet burden is the strongest route to victory in this debate. PRO also answered the pressure questions cleanly and directly — confirming a clear-and-convincing standard at extension hearings and describing full civil remedies for wrongful removal — which neutralized CON's two sharpest attacks. CON's strongest moments were real: the driver's license analogy is properly distinguished (driving is a privilege, not a constitutional right), "upheld ≠ warranted" is clever and was never fully buried, and the false-positive base-rate argument lands. But CON's attempt to distinguish domestic-violence restraining orders actually backfired — noting DVROs are themselves prone to perjury and abuse confirms the system has error-correction mechanisms, which supports rather than defeats PRO's framework. CON's rhetorical closing was the debate's most memorable line ("the citizen has already lost the year, the stigma, and the presumption"), and CON had fewer clipped speeches (a real execution cost for PRO in Rebuttal 1 and the Closing), keeping the margin competitive. Overall, PRO holds the cleaner path because the unmet gap argument is a live, consequential burden CON never discharged, while PRO's affirmative safeguards story held up under direct pressure questioning.

CON landed the sharper individual blow rhetorically, but PRO's comparative challenge — what does CON do when psychiatric commitment criteria are not met? — remained unmet through the closing and is the key reason PRO holds the winning position.
