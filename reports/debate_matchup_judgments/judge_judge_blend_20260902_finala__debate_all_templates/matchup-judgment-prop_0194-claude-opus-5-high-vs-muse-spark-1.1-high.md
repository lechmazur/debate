### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by showing that the same constitutional commitment needed to establish and protect a commissioner could instead enact binding substantive duties enforced preemptively by appealable courts. This preserved future protection while avoiding the commissioner's discretionary interpretation, legitimacy problem, and anti-building asymmetry. Side B powerfully framed structural presentism, but never resolved why its institution was less vulnerable to later narrowing or repeal than A's alternative. A's repeated clipping was a real execution cost, though its decisive arguments remained visible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest response was to Side B's claim that courts cannot stop harms made legal by present legislatures. A conceded that precommitment is necessary, then argued that the same constitutional moment should create public substantive rules rather than an official empowered to define and veto contested tradeoffs. Side B's closing repeated the lawful-theft argument without answering that institutional comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by decisively collapsing the debate down to the shared requirement of a "constitutional moment." A successfully argued that if a society can muster the political will to install a commissioner, it could instead codify binding rules enforced by courts. A then used B's mid-debate clarification on nuclear waste to prove that a commissioner would inherently exercise arbitrary, shifting discretion rather than applying fixed law.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly flipped B's argument that "courts only work if the legislature first binds itself" by pointing out that installing the commissioner requires the exact same self-binding moment, which A argued is better spent writing fixed, appealable laws.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash clearer and unresolved in A's favor: a binding veto either becomes pure discretion under contested "future" criteria or collapses into the same constitutional moment that could simply enact public, general duties enforced by courts. B's supermajority dilemma, pro-build irreversibility flip, and especially the mid-debate reinterpretation of "without disposal plan" exposed real mechanism defects; A's closing crystallization of legalized presentism was sharp but did not neutralize the alternative of ratified rules plus early injunctive relief. Clipping hurt B's polish, but not the comparative path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal flipping A's "legislature must bind itself" concession into the same-cost choice of ratified general rules vs. one interpreter's stop button, cemented by the disposal-plan redefinition.
