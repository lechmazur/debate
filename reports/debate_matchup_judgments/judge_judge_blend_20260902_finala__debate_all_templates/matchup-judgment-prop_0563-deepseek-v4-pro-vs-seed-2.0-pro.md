### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): B won a competitive debate by giving the cleaner incentive story: uncertain recurring votes reward Putin for waiting, while a durable commitment removes that path and reinforces wider alliance credibility. A effectively stressed capability and exposed B’s tendency to equate flexible support with abandonment, but repeatedly inflated reduced resources into gutted readiness or empty stockpiles without establishing that magnitude. A’s claim that uncertainty beneficially pressures Russia was also less convincing than B’s response.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal most decisively reversed A’s uncertainty argument: Putin benefits from the possibility of Western cutoff rather than fearing it, and military capacity has limited deterrent value if allies and adversaries doubt the willingness to endure costs.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built the cleaner path by locking the debate on the incentive structure and cascading credibility costs: standing aid removes Putin's core bet on outwaiting democracies, and capacity without proven will is empty. Side B pressed the capacity erosion and "even if" tradeoff well but never fully neutralized A's point that abandon-and-hoard proves partners cannot rely on us, and B's opening was clipped. A's answers to pressure and closing crystallized the known-bounded cost versus irreversible precedent more decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 and A1/A2 on incentives: quarterly uncertainty is Putin's winning card, not pressure that forces early negotiation; standing commitment ends the forever-wait strategy while capacity without demonstrated follow-through fails to deter.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by more cleanly resolving the central credibility-capacity tradeoff. While B effectively argued that a rigid commitment risks hollowing out readiness for other theaters, A successfully framed credibility as the prerequisite that makes all other capability meaningful. A’s pressure questions exposed an unresolved burden: B never directly explained why condition-based aid would avoid the reputational damage of abandonment, and B’s opening was clipped for exceeding the word limit, leaving A’s core incentive-structure narrative more intact. A also handled B’s pressure more directly. The margin is razor-thin because both sides argued with real analytical force, but A’s execution was cleaner and its crystallization of the clash more memorable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 1 flipped the opportunity-cost argument by contending that every Pacific priority rests first on the belief that the United States will not abandon a partner under attack, turning B’s capacity concerns into a reason to prefer the commitment. This exchange remained live throughout the debate and determined the decisive clash.
