### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): A won by making the comparison sharper: caps and geofences do not themselves create curb, liability, or safety rules, while targeted rules can address the live harms directly. B’s best argument was that caps are a precondition for fair rulemaking because deployed fleets gain lobbying power, but A answered that caps create their own incumbent lobbying problem and gave plausible examples of cities regulating transport services mid-deployment. B also overclaimed that post-deployment regulation never works and never fully answered the human-driver safety baseline or the indefinite labor-transition trigger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered B’s lobbying and liability questions, turned the lobbying argument against caps through the medallion analogy, and framed B’s finish line as partly passable now and partly open-ended.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's case by proving that caps themselves do not solve operational harms, while introducing a compelling safety baseline (human drivers) that Side B never successfully contested. Side A also cleanly defeated Side B's political economy argument by showing that caps create entrenched lobbying (the medallion effect) and that cities successfully regulate mid-deployment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was masterful. It used Side B's own answers to the Pressure Questions to prove that liability is already handled by tort law and that Side B's proposed 'finish line' included an open-ended labor transition plan, proving the cap would be indefinite.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won decisively by forcing key concessions (caps fix no operational harms), exposing the indefinite trigger, and cleanly contrasting real rules plus human-driver safety baseline against PRO's speculative political-economy bet that history undermines.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's direct answers to pressure Qs plus weighing of PRO concessions on fire lanes/curbs and finish-line collapse.
