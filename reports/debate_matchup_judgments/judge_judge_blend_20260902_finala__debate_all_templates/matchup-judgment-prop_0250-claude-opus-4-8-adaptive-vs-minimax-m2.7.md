While Side A raised compelling philosophical concerns about consent and the distinction between raw turnout and actual governmental responsiveness, Side B effectively neutralized these by framing mandatory voting as a structural correction to systematic demographic exclusion. Side B countered decisively by noting the state need not judge sincerity; the recorded act itself solves the "invisible absence" problem, and the arithmetic inclusion of millions of previously silenced voters vastly outweighs a small fraction of blanks. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): B wins narrowly because its central mechanism survived: voluntary turnout predictably skews toward older and wealthier voters, so politicians rationally cater to that electorate, while compulsory turnout makes the whole public electorally relevant. But A's alternative of publishing abstention data did not answer B's incentive point, since visible nonvoting still gives politicians no votes to compete for.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively neutralized Side A's 'legible signal' argument by showing that publishing ward-level turnout provides the exact same transparency without resorting to coercion.

Side A's second rebuttal was decisive, tracking Side B's rhetorical retreats from concrete policy outcomes to theoretical mechanisms and showing that compulsion's sole surviving benefit was already obtainable under voluntary systems. Side A's closing crystallized this cost-benefit tradeoff cleanly, leaving Side B's case as an unproven structural promise weighed against a certain civil penalty. Side B argued competently but never squared the burden of proving that coercion yields a unique, tangible democratic good.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which dismantled Side B's "legibility" defense by showing that publishing ward-level turnout data already makes disengagement visible to politicians without coercion, while simultaneously mapping Side B's case evolution from mandatory voting to compelled attendance to unnameable structural mechanisms.
