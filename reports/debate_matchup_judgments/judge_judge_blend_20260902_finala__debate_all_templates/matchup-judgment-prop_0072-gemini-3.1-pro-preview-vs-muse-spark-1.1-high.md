### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but CON's rebuttals did more damage to PRO's live routes than vice versa. CON's distinction between Germany teaching a "disowned regime's" crimes versus PRO's proposal to mandate a continuing state affirm its own ongoing criminality ([B2.1]) was never squarely answered — PRO's reply ([A4.4-4.5]) restated that codifying abuses "disowns" them without explaining why legal compulsion, rather than voluntary teaching, is necessary or safe. More decisively, CON cleanly defused PRO's central pressure trap (the "trustworthy state" contradiction) in A2 of Rebuttal 2 by distinguishing low-discretion civic-attachment framing from high-discretion mandated moral verdicts, while PRO's own answer to CON's parallel pressure question about who defines "atrocities" and what stops partisan capture ([A4.1]) was thin — it simply asserted institutional continuity, which CON then turned against PRO by noting that if the same boards flip after elections, the "guaranteed floor" flips too. This "legal whiplash" argument was sustained cleanly into CON's closing and never neutralized. PRO's intuitive point that local majorities will not voluntarily teach their own group's abuses remains real and unresolved, which keeps PRO competitive, but CON's answer (a menu of decentralized correction mechanisms: courts, press, litigation, competitive markets) at least offers a plausible alternative causal story, whereas PRO never fully explains why legal mandate solves capture/politicization better than it creates it. Overall CON's rebuttal work was sharper, its central contradiction argument more originally framed, and its pressure-question answers more directly resolved — giving it a clear but not dominant edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [B2.1]-[B2.2] distinguishing Germany's disowned-regime Holocaust education from PRO's proposal to mandate a continuing state affirm its own "atrocities," combined with CON's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 ([B4.2]) cleanly defusing PRO's "trustworthy state" pressure trap by separating developmental civic attachment from compelled moral verdicts — both points PRO never fully neutralized.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by consistently exposing and exploiting PRO’s core design contradiction: the state is too dangerous for civic health so it must force atrocity study, yet that same state is entrusted with legally defining the atrocities and compelling every district.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Germany response [B2.1–B2.2] plus the trust/contradiction turn [B2.5/B4.4]: it reframed PRO’s key example and collapsed the “same boards” defense into political capture.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO built a coherent, principled case (state PR duty, democratic competence, prevention) anchored in concrete comparative examples (Germany's inclusion vs. Turkey/Japan/Russia's omission) that CON never directly rebutted except by distinguishing Germany's post-collapse context—leaving the broader pattern of omission-breeds-denial largely intact. CON's strongest weapon was the "fox gets a bigger weapon" critique and the sharp closing hit that "courts don't write curricula, legislatures and boards do," which exposed real softness in PRO's enforcement mechanism (the evolution/creationism analogy was a stretch). PRO's closing did not directly answer this last point, giving CON the final word on that specific thread. However, PRO's broader framing—that curricular discretion already exists and is currently used to omit, so a documented-atrocity floor narrows rather than expands political control—survived the exchange better than CON's alternative of "trust local/decentralized control," which never squared with PRO's negative national examples of decentralized or state-controlled denial. On balance, PRO's constructive case and use of comparative evidence gave it a more resilient path to victory, though CON's mechanism critique kept the debate close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The clash over "who actually enforces the floor" — PRO's claim that historical consensus, courts, and evidentiary records constrain politicization (A4.3), directly countered by CON's closing insistence that "courts do not draft curricula; partisan legislatures and boards do" (B4.4/B5.2) — was the most decisive exchange, and CON landed the sharper final blow on it, though PRO's broader omission-vs-inclusion framing remained largely unrefuted.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO built the cleaner path by fixing the status quo as already-curating omission, then defending a constrained "floor" of documented state-perpetrated cases (slavery, internment, Trail of Tears) that shrinks discretion rather than invents it. CON's weaponization and Germany-exception attacks landed repeatedly but never fully neutralized the cynicism-from-discovery or fox-already-runs-the-henhouse pressure; later speeches restated mechanism fears without showing why optional local practice prevents erasure better than the proposed test. PRO's comparative weighing (brittle identity vs. reformable agency) therefore decided the motion more convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.4: floor of consensus + state-action + temporal distance leashes the fox and blocks live-policy redefinition while CON's un-leashed omission does not.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built a much stronger comparative case by successfully reframing the mandate as a constraint on state discretion (a "floor") rather than an expansion of it. Side A's analogies (evolution vs. creationism, the "fox" guarding the henhouse) were highly effective in neutralizing Side B's weaponization concerns. Side B's retreat to "trust local educators" was effectively dismantled by Side A's observation that local control is exactly what allows historical amnesia to occur in the first place. Side A's rhetoric was sharper, and their closing perfectly crystallized the central clash, proving that optional truth in a compulsory system guarantees the very cynicism Side B fears.
