### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A consistently pressed the "name a victim" test and Side B never produced one that survived scrutiny. When pushed on relative-position harms (height being zero-sum), A's pressure question exposed an inconsistency: if positional disadvantage from another family's embryo choice is a harm, then ordinary heredity (tall couples having tall kids) should be equally harmful, yet B exempted natural reproduction as "a lottery, not a marketplace" without a principled reason why the mechanism (money vs. genes) rather than the outcome (relative disadvantage) is what matters. A also successfully reframed the caste/commodification rhetoric as metaphor rather than identified harm, and neutralized the organ-market and cosmetic-surgery analogies by pointing out disanalogies (no third-party exploitation; the analogy proves law can permit gradients, not ban them). B's "genome vs. wealth" redistribution argument was rhetorically strong but never engaged A's core point that screening only changes which existing possible child is born, not that it creates unprecedented biological stratification beyond what assortative mating already produces. B's closing repeated the caste framing without directly answering A2.1-A2.8's dismantling of the victim requirement. A's answers to pressure questions were sharper and more directly responsive (A1/A2 format followed cleanly), while B's answers, though formatted correctly, relied on the lottery/marketplace distinction that A had already preemptively questioned and then further undercut in the following round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 in Pressure Questions ("are shorter children of naturally tall parents victims as well") which exposed that B's positional-harm theory proves too much and was never fully resolved by B's "natural lottery vs. marketplace" answer, since B never explained why the money mechanism rather than the outcome is what makes a harm cognizable.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO kept a collective-action path alive that CON never fully neutralized: height/IQ as positional goods creating biological inflation and a purchased baseline that cannot be redistributed, plus a workable medical-vs-enhancement line. CON's sharp no-third-party victim and travel-evasion points damaged the organ analogy but the clipped opening and reliance on "inequality already exists" left the arms-race/commodification core standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.5 organ-market rebuttal - ranking own embryos harvests no third party, so trafficking analogy fails - was the cleanest damaging hit, but B's inability to answer it in B4 left CON's fairness point live yet not enough to overturn PRO's zero-sum infrastructure argument.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won decisively by exposing Side B's fundamental conflation of embryo selection with genetic modification. Side A rigorously defended the mechanism of screening as a choice among already-created embryos, effectively dismantling Side B's "manufacturing," "customizing," and "altering the blueprint" rhetoric. Side A's rebuttals, particularly the destruction of the organ market analogy and the relentless demand for a specific victim of private reproductive choice, were logically airtight and rhetorically sharp. Side B relied heavily on emotive "biological caste" metaphors but failed to philosophically justify why the state should police the selection of a couple's own embryos when natural heredity, IVF markets, and assortative mating already produce the exact same inequalities without state intervention.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1, specifically dismantling the organ market analogy by pointing out that embryo ranking harvests no third party, and clarifying that screening is selection rather than modification, which fatally undermined Side B's "design" and "trafficking" framings.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but PRO (B) built a more coherent throughline directly tied to the motion's key clause "even if the technology becomes more reliable," arguing that reliability sharpens rather than dissolves the harm by turning refusal into visible negligence. B's pressure question exposed that CON's rebuttal had leaned entirely on cognition-as-health while ignoring height/appearance, forcing a recovery (A1.1) that relied on a strained "metabolic robustness" link for height—an answer that felt like a stretch rather than a clean rebuttal. B also offered a workable narrower restriction (bar composite elective ranking, but permit viability/disease data), which CON's closing continued to mischaracterize as total "redaction of medical files," letting a live clarification go unaddressed. CON's strongest points—the IVF "lottery" reframe and the pleiotropy challenge—landed well and were answered only adequately by B, but CON also silently dropped its own tourism/inequality rebuttal after B's counter in Rebuttal 1, weakening its case on inequality. Overall B's closing crystallized the clash more precisely around the motion's reliability condition and ended with a sharper, more memorable framing ("selling the prettier embryo"), giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Question Q1 ("you never defend height or looks... have you quietly dropped half the motion?") was the most decisive exchange, forcing CON into a strained health-based defense of height/appearance that weakened its overall consistency.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the decision rule on purchasable hierarchy vs medical screening. B answered the two core CON attacks - that restriction is a random blindfold and that pleiotropy makes line-drawing impossible - by noting viability/disease screening remains allowed and law can bar composite beauty/IQ scores while reporting medical risk. B's Q1 forced CON to finally defend height/appearance as health via weak metabolic link, which B exploited in closing. CON's autonomy/tourism framing never overcame the positional-goods + arms-race account and relied on a strawman lottery.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R1/R2 dismantling of the tutoring/prenatal-care analogy into choosing which embryo exists vs acting on a living child, plus the A1/A2 answers that restriction is not a random lottery and pleiotropy does not require a storefront, directly neutralized CON's best offense.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a clear, defensible line between medical screening and commercial trait ranking, effectively neutralizing Side A's "dystopian ledger" slippery slope. B's distinction between enhancing an existing child (tutoring) and selecting which child exists based on a status sheet dismantled A's parenting analogy. Furthermore, B's pressure questions forced A into a weak, biologically strained defense of selecting for height and appearance, exposing a critical gap in A's case and allowing B to crystallize the clash decisively in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of the parenting analogy ("A parent hiring a math tutor does not discard the sibling who scored lower") and the clean, practical parry to A's pleiotropy pressure ("Withhold the ranking product, not the health warning").
