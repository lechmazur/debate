### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): Side A built the stronger path to victory through three moves that CON never fully neutralized. First, the "prioritize" reframe: PRO established early that the motion is a marginal investment question—core channels and emergency capacity survive, so the real comparison is between a new traditional channel and short-form/creator spending. CON repeated the "every pound" argument throughout but never squarely addressed this framing. Second, PRO's most durable claim—"public value not encountered has no civic effect"—remained live after the full exchange; CON's "visibility vs. value" distinction concedes that short-form can carry real content but insists it cannot convert to depth, yet PRO reasonably answered that self-contained civic value (correcting a false claim before an election) is itself a mission success, not a failure. Third, PRO's pressure questions exposed a genuine gap: CON never gave a satisfying answer to what specific relevance problem a new linear channel solves for youth who already aren't watching scheduled TV. CON's best answers—"young audiences aren't monolithic," "family shared screens"—are real but weak against the specific burden. CON's strongest counter-thrust was the "all entrance and no hall" argument—short-form parasitically depends on journalism it starves—and rhetorically this landed well throughout; CON's closings were consistently more memorable. But CON's creator-governance pressure question, while sharp, received a plausible rejoinder (commissioned talent managed like freelancers always have been), and CON's alternative to platform presence remained underdeveloped. The multiple clippings are an execution cost for Side A but did not prevent the core arguments from coming through clearly enough to carry the exchange. The decisive asymmetry is that CON's live burden—explaining how expanded channels specifically reach youth who opt out of linear TV—was left unanswered, while PRO's key burdens were addressed with at least adequate responses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive rebuttal exchange was PRO's pressure question Q1 ("what specific relevance problem does adding another traditional channel solve for young audiences who are not choosing scheduled TV?") combined with CON's inadequate answer in Rebuttal 2 (invoking family screens and a non-monolithic youth audience). CON's response failed to engage the actual specificity of the question—it gestured at edge cases rather than showing how a new traditional channel closes the youth-reach gap. This left the core burden of CON's case undefended at the critical exchange point.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): Side A wins by cleanly isolating the comparative in the motion: the choice is between short-form and new channel expansion, not short-form and existing journalism.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 and Rebuttal 2 effectively dismantle Side B's claim that short-form "starves journalism," correctly pointing out that the true opportunity cost of the motion is the massive expense of channel expansion, which would only duplicate a failing model.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): Side A won by establishing relevance as contact with young audiences via adaptive formats, directly answering pressure on mechanisms and governance with plausible editorial controls and routes to depth while preserving core infrastructure. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO executed cleanly across all five turns while CON suffered clipping penalties in three of five speeches—including the critical conclusion of the Opening's big framing move ("The real choice is between [clipped]") and the closing line of Rebuttal 1. That is a real execution cost. More importantly, PRO's motion clarification held up under pressure: the proposition targets new expansion, not dismantling existing capacity. This framing, introduced in A1 and sustained through the closing, let PRO absorb CON's universal-service argument (existing spectrum and free digital streams already carry emergency, minority-language, and children's content) without conceding the core claim. CON's strongest analytical move—B4.4, catching that PRO's own safety-net answer implicitly makes owned capacity the indispensable layer—was genuinely sharp and PRO never fully resolved the logical tension it created. But CON's response to PRO's Q2 (what metric justifies traditional expansion when it reaches a shrinking audience?) remained philosophical rather than concrete, and CON never clearly demonstrated that new traditional channel investment generates greater civic impact than youth-oriented digital investment. After the full exchange, PRO's live route—young audiences are behaviorally absent from linear TV; political sustainability requires the next generation's buy-in; new capital should follow them—was more clearly intact than CON's, which required showing that expansion, not just preservation of existing owned capacity, deserves priority. "Heritage site awaiting closure" and "pension for equipment" were memorable, rhetorically effective crystallizations that CON never quite defused.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.4) was the most analytically damaging exchange: catching that PRO's own A1 answer ("None" of the core public-service functions would be subordinated because existing channels handle them) implicitly concedes that owned public capacity is the indispensable foundation, which logically should make it the priority, not a safety net for a creator-partnership strategy. PRO's closing did not squarely resolve this contradiction, instead pivoting to political sustainability—a real concern but not a direct answer to the structural logic CON exposed.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by cleanly separating 'traditional channel expansion' from 'existing public capacity' and 'owned digital apps', which neutralized Side B's warnings about losing emergency or minority broadcasts. Side B tried to frame Side A's reliance on existing infrastructure as a concession, but this failed because owned apps are not traditional channels. Side A's framing that relevance requires reaching future taxpayers to ensure institutional survival remained the most compelling metric.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal directly answered Side B's pressure by clarifying that halting traditional expansion does not mean dismantling existing public capacity, exposing Side B's core equivocation.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing that existing public capacity already supports core universal functions while new resources should target short-form reach to secure future taxpayer buy-in and mandate sustainability; this directly neutralized B's pressure on subordinated services and accountability by referencing owned digital fallbacks. Side B offered coherent warnings on platform incentives and the need for durable institutional guarantees but struggled to rebut the demographic exclusion and reallocation logic, with incomplete later turns weakening comparative force.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 responses that reframed priority as protecting existing spectrum/streaming while shifting expansion capital to youth platforms, directly addressing both B questions without concession.
