B's clipped Rebuttal 1 is a genuine execution cost, but it did not prevent B from carrying the decisive substantive thread through to a clear, resolved closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing fire-crew analogy ("does not gain a moral right to let a burning building next door collapse while protecting a smoke alarm") most sharply exposed the weak point in A's assignment/fiduciary defense, and A's attempted counter-use of the same analogy did not fully answer the severity mismatch it raised.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully separating the administrative necessity of states ("who must act") from the moral weight of the claims they adjudicate ("whose need matters"). - **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by maintaining a clear conceptual distinction that Side B never fully neutralized: administrative assignment is not the same as moral weight. A effectively turned B's heart analogy against B in the closing, showing that an instrument privileging membership over survival fails its own purpose. B's associative-duty and fiduciary arguments were strong, but they largely rested on conflating who must answer with whose claim is stronger, which A repeatedly exposed. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, competing frameworks — CON's "loyalty does the operative work, welfare is only a boundary" versus PRO's "loyalty is bounded/derivative, hence weaker." The pivotal exchange came over the ICU/vaccine/booster cases: CON tried to convert PRO's concession that welfare permits local trustee preference into proof that loyalty "does the moral work," but PRO's Rebuttal 2 and closing cleanly split this into an equal-stakes/unequal-stakes structure, showing that welfare authorizes local preference only instrumentally and still wins whenever stakes diverge. CON's closing reasserted "a floor doesn't make welfare stronger" and "guardrail vs road," but never squarely engaged PRO's equal/unequal stakes distinction, leaving PRO's final "tie-breaker vs scoreboard" framing as the last word on the decisive clash. PRO also handled the Pressure Questions round more crisply, offering a concrete allocation criterion (severity, capacity, effectiveness) rather than relying on definitional assertions. CON's arguments about institutional reciprocity and determinate claimants were solid but ultimately conflate frequency of operation with moral priority, a conflation PRO exposed and CON did not fully repair.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1) reframing the ICU/local-trustee concession as "welfare authorizing local preference for its own ends" rather than loyalty winning, later crystallized in the closing's "tie-breakers vs scoreboard" distinction — CON's closing response did not directly rebut the equal/unequal stakes split that made this work.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by proving that universal human welfare acts as a structural upper bound on national loyalty. Side B used the pressure questions to force a concession on asymmetric resource allocation (vaccines), proving that universal welfare overrides loyalty when stakes are unequal. B also brilliantly absorbed Side A's strongest argument (life-and-death local preference) by framing it as a division of labor authorized by universal welfare itself, cementing the conclusion that loyalty is a derivative, and thus weaker, claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which absorbed Side A's ICU bed argument by framing national preference in equal-stakes scenarios as universal welfare authorizing "reliable local trustees" to avoid the inefficiencies of a global lottery.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO established that loyalty operates within side-constraints set by universal welfare: CON conceded that grossly disproportionate tradeoffs like a healthy compatriot’s booster versus a stranger’s life-saving first dose must yield to welfare. PRO successfully reframed local allocation (ICU beds, rescue) not as loyalty outranking welfare but as welfare authorizing local trusteeship when stakes are equal, preserving the hierarchy. CON’s counter that negative constraints are not positive welfare duties created pressure but did not neutralize the booster concession or the floor framework, leaving PRO’s comparative case intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 answers to CON’s pressure questions: the trustee distinction clarified that local preference is a division of labor authorized by universal welfare when stakes are equal, while welfare overrides loyalty when stakes are grossly unequal. This neutralized CON’s attempt to cite life-and-death allocation choices as loyalty victories and sharpened the decisive floor framework.
