- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and handled the Pressure Questions round properly with labeled Q/A. However, B's throughline—that incremental fixes buy only eroding percentage reductions that climate-driven rainfall will progressively undermine, forcing perpetual re-investment, while separation retires the sewage-dumping design flaw once—directly engages the motion's core tradeoff (upfront cost vs. continued overflow management) and was never squarely defeated. A's answers to B's pressure questions (cost-per-percentage-point, opportunity cost) were solid but did not directly rebut the escalation dynamic B kept pressing, leaving that route live for B through the closing. B's distinction between pathogen-driven harms (ended by separation) and lesser stormwater pollutants (metals/nutrients) adequately answered A's strongest counter, even though a small unresolved tension remains around nutrient-driven ecological harms B cited in its own opening. On balance, B kept tighter focus on the actual cost-tradeoff question the motion poses and left A with an unclosed liability argument, giving it a narrow but real edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange around A2.1 (pointing out separation still permits untreated stormwater pollution) and B4.1 (distinguishing pathogen-carrying sewage from lesser pollutants) was the pivotal clash — B's answer blunted the attack but did not fully resolve the tension with its own opening claim about nutrient-driven ecological harms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a durable frame that persisted through the exchange: the question is whether cities should keep engineering raw sewage into waterways. A’s cost-effectiveness rebuttal, while rational, conceded too much moral ground on public health and never fully overcame B’s permanence argument. B’s closing crystallization—asking whether a city keeps designing sewage dumps into its rivers or stops—provided the cleaner reason to affirm the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s distinction between combined sewage (pathogens from human waste) and separated stormwater (metals/nutrients), which blunted A’s “separation creates new pollution” argument and protected PRO’s core public health claim.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built and sustained the argument most directly responsive to the motion's actual challenge—whether a much higher upfront cost is justified—by consistently framing the debate as risk-averted-per-dollar and showing that hybrid gray-green systems capture 80-95% of the benefit PRO celebrates at a fraction of the cost. A's pressure question ("what exact extra illnesses or beach-days justify the multi-billion premium") struck at PRO's central vulnerability: PRO never quantified why eliminating the last increment of harm justifies the cost differential, instead repeatedly asserting that any raw sewage is categorically unacceptable. PRO's severity-over-frequency argument (sewage vs. mere stormwater) was a genuinely strong qualitative point and kept the debate competitive, and PRO's answer that sanitary lines can be climate-buffered while storm lines "safely" overflow was a reasonable response to A's second pressure question. But PRO's closing still leaned on absolutist rhetoric ("not a debate") rather than engaging the cost-effectiveness comparison that the motion explicitly poses, while CON's closing crystallized the cost/benefit tradeoff cleanly. PRO's opening was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost that lost some of its foundational case-building. Overall CON kept tighter engagement with the resolution as written and exploited PRO's failure to quantify the marginal benefit, giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Question ("If residuals are already rare after 80-95% capture, what exact extra illnesses or closed beach-days justify the multi-billion premium for total elimination?") exposed PRO's core weakness: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer reasserted severity and permanence but never quantified the marginal benefit against the marginal cost, letting CON's cost-effectiveness framing dominate the rest of the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by framing the proposition as a ruinously expensive purity test. They showed that modern tunnels, sensors, and green infrastructure already reduce raw-sewage overflows by 80–95 percent, leaving only rare extreme events, and that separated systems would still be overwhelmed by those same climate-driven storms—merely converting sewage surcharges into stormwater surcharges—while demanding multi-billion premiums, years of gridlock, and foregone investments in higher-return priorities. PRO’s severity framing remained rhetorically vivid but never overcame the marginal analysis; the upfront premium bought vanishingly little extra risk reduction, and unsupported claims that single-event fines could exceed separation capital costs weakened PRO’s credibility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal directly answered PRO’s pressure questions by showing the rare residuals occur in the same extreme storms that would overtop separated pipes, so separation does not eliminate overflow events—it only changes their content—while hybrid systems lock in gains and scale adaptively. This neutralized PRO’s core permanence and severity arguments.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): CON won the weighing clash. PRO framed raw sewage as categorically intolerable, but CON showed 80-95% capture via tunnels/real-time/green already turns chronic overflows into rare extremes that even separated storm pipes will exceed, so separation buys near-zero marginal health gain for multi-billion debt, gridlock, forced laterals, and foregone housing/lead work. PRO's permanence claim was damaged by climate-design and infiltration rebuttals, while PRO's opening was clipped and never quantified the last-percentage benefit without relying on absolute rhetoric.
