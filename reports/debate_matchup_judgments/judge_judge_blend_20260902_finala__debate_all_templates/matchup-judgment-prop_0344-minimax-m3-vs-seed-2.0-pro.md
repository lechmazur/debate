### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A won on the two most decisive clashes. First, the motion's scope: A consistently and correctly defended "prioritize" as ordering tradeoffs, not eliminating devices, keeping labs, library periods, and targeted digital citizenship units as fully compatible with the proposition. Side B's counter-read—that prioritizing means "cut the device program first, so you never implement 1:1 at all"—was an overreach that weakened B's own position by attacking a more extreme version of the motion than the one on the table. Second, the sequencing argument: A's foundation-before-load-test logic was coherent and developmentally plausible. B's supervised-practice rebuttal had genuine force, but it never answered A's sharpest pressure (Q2): why does teaching device management require universal all-day 1:1 rather than structured targeted lessons? B's Rebuttal 2 answered this by calling lab-cart time "an afterthought," but that strawmans what adequately resourced, scheduled digital literacy lessons could look like. B's closing swimming analogy was rhetorically strong but logically cut against itself: you teach swimming through supervised progression into deeper water, not by throwing non-swimmers into the deep end—which mirrors A's sequencing point rather than defeating it. The equity exchange was roughly even; B's "already have devices at home so school is the only supervised window" argument had real purchase, but A's "counterbalance, don't mirror" framing held. The two clipped speeches are a modest execution cost for A, but the core arguments in both were delivered before the clips and remained unanswered. B's case was genuinely resonant but repeatedly overclaimed on what the motion required, leaving A's cleaner, more internally consistent path standing at the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure question Q2—asking what concrete decision B's reading of the motion actually prohibits—exposed B's core overreach. B's motion-scope argument (prioritize = never implement 1:1) was the load-bearing move that justified its whole "there is no later" framing. A's question forced B to defend that interpretive leap, and B's answer in Rebuttal 2 (that the alternative degrades to "uneven last-resort lab time") never engaged the actual distinction between poorly resourced labs and well-designed structured digital literacy programs. This exchange neutralized B's most emotionally potent argument at its logical root.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A wins a clear but competitive debate primarily because its Q2 pressure question set a trap that B walked into. When asked how adults absorb new workplace tools so quickly, B answered that adults succeed because they already carry the cognitive and social infrastructure built earlier in life — which is precisely A's core thesis. A's closing correctly identified this as a concession, and B's attempt to walk it back in its own closing ("that foundation is built through devices too") was asserted without the support needed to neutralize the concession. A's central argument — prioritize the irreversible developmental window at thirteen because digital skills can be scaffolded quickly once the foundation exists — survived the entire exchange largely intact. B's strongest counter-moves were the selection-bias challenge on budget causation (legitimate and somewhat damaging) and the recurring dyslexic/English-learner example (vivid and pointed), but neither cleanly neutralized A's framing. B's first rebuttal was also clipped, an execution cost in the round where B had its best momentum. B's reframe that devices are attention tools rather than rivals was creative and partially landed, but the zero-sum resource reality A kept returning to was never genuinely answered: B consistently argued from a world of unlimited options rather than the constrained budget cycles A specified. A's closing was tighter and crystallized the live clash more effectively despite being shorter. The margin reflects that B ran a genuinely competitive race — the vulnerable-student argument and the selection-bias challenge were real pressure — but the decisive Q2 exchange tilted the round to A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Q2 exchange was decisive. A asked how working adults successfully learn brand-new workplace tools every year if digital skills cannot be caught up later. B answered that adults learn tools quickly because they already have the baseline cognitive and social infrastructure. A then crystallized in closing: "That is our entire case." B never cleanly recovered, because the answer confirmed that the foundation precedes and enables tool acquisition rather than the two developing simultaneously — exactly what A had been arguing about the early secondary window all along.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by trapping Side A on the timeline of digital fluency. Side B's second pressure question exposed a tension in Side A's case, leading Side A to explicitly concede in Rebuttal 2 that "the device layer can be added later." Side B capitalized on this flawlessly in closing, neutralizing Side A's otherwise compelling point about devices being the intervention for vulnerable students.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Closing cleanly weaponizing Side A's Rebuttal 2 concession that foundational skills make tool-learning easy and devices can be added later.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A built the stronger case by consistently defending prioritization under real budget constraints, directly answering pressure questions on baselines and causality, and crystallizing the irreversible developmental window trade-off that Side B never neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 answers) exposed Side B's confound claims and forced the debate back onto live trade-offs.
