### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): Side B wins: stronger core clash on control vs. sophisticated dependence for critical uses, better handling of pressure questions on operational continuity, cleaner weighing that insurance beats theoretical superiority in crises. Side A’s obsolescence and 85-vs-100 arguments were answered directly without being neutralized. Clipped turns slightly hurt both but did not change comparative outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1/A2 answers directly neutralize A’s supply-chain and second-best attacks by reframing to acute-phase control and continuity-over-peak, preserving B’s insurance route.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): PRO won by establishing that the motion concerns insurance against heavy dependence, not replacement of frontier private cloud. CON's strongest attacks—obsolescence and adversarial inferiority—assumed sovereign compute must supplant all private use, but PRO explicitly defended a hybrid model where domestic capacity guarantees continuity and operational control during crises. PRO's "reliable second-best you control" framing, combined with sharp rebuttals showing that multi-cloud and cryptography manage rather than eliminate dependence, created a cleaner comparative case. CON's emergency-powers answer to operational control conceded the danger of dependence, and their closing attempt to redefine sovereignty as access to the best tools slightly evaded the motion rather than defeating PRO's core logic.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's first rebuttal (B2) exposed CON's category error by distinguishing commercial efficiency from mission-critical control, establishing the decisive "reliable second-best you control versus best-in-market you may not" frame, and showing that CON's multi-cloud and confidential-computing alternatives were mitigations of dependence rather than substitutes for sovereignty.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): CON wins by successfully turning PRO's core "insurance" frame and providing a more realistic mechanism for crisis control. While PRO effectively argued that guaranteed access ("a controllable 85") beats theoretical peak performance during ruptures, CON sharply countered that in adversarial AI domains like cyber defense, running on obsolete hardware guarantees failure ("a breached network"). More decisively, CON answered PRO's crisis-dependency fear by pointing out that governments possess statutory authority and emergency powers to compel or seize domestic commercial infrastructure, achieving operational control without the massive cost and rapid obsolescence of state-built silicon. PRO dismissed this as "borrowing sovereignty," but failed to substantively explain why legal command authority is inferior to owning depreciating hardware that still relies on foreign supply chains. CON's weighing of capability over ownership, combined with practical alternatives (multi-cloud, cryptography, emergency powers), offered a cleaner, more cost-effective path to securing public-interest AI.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's response to PRO's "controllable 85 vs unavailable 100" frame was decisive. By arguing that an 85-capability system cannot defend against a 100-capability adversarial attack ("it guarantees a breached network"), CON shifted the weighing from mere continuity to effective security. Additionally, CON's use of statutory emergency powers to answer PRO's crisis-rationing fears provided a concrete, lower-cost alternative that PRO could not fully neutralize, effectively collapsing the necessity of the sovereign build-out.
