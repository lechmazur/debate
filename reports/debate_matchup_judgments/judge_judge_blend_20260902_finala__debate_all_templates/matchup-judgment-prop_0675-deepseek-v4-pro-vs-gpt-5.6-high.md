### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): A (CON) built a cleaner, more disciplined case: export controls are a tax that a determined state can route around via stockpiling, smuggling, prioritization, and domestic substitution, while the damage to U.S. firms compounds through lost ecosystem influence and accelerated rival self-sufficiency. A's key dilemma—if substitutes stay weak, militaries simply triage scarce chips to priority programs while civilians absorb the pain; if substitutes mature, controls lose bite while U.S. industry has already lost customers—was never squarely answered by B, who mostly reasserted "the shortage is extreme" without a new mechanism. B's case leaned heavily on specific multipliers ("three versus fifty runs," "four or five times longer," "a factor of ten") that A correctly flagged as asserted rather than demonstrated, which cost B on grounding and epistemic discipline. B also suffered a real execution cost: four of its five speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits, including its closing, which cut off mid-argument and denied it a clean final weighing statement. A's pressure questions exposed the incidence problem sharply, and A's answers to B's questions, while not airtight, engaged the "when does delay become decisive" issue more honestly than B's answers engaged the incidence problem. Overall A did the better comparative weighing work across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 point that PRO's specific multipliers (three vs. fifty runs, tenfold gap, 4-5x slower retraining) were asserted rather than demonstrated, combined with the unanswered incidence dilemma (military triage vs. adequate substitutes), was the most damaging and unresolved exchange of the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that PRO never demonstrated chip scarcity lands on priority military programs rather than civilian users, and by converting PRO's unsupported quantitative claims into an unproven marginal delay that cannot outweigh demonstrated cumulative harm to U.S. ecosystem leadership and long-run competitiveness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s first rebuttal called out PRO’s brittle fake precision—claims like “three instead of fifty training runs” and “4–5× longer retraining”—as asserted-not-demonstrated, reframing the alleged structural ceiling into a marginal, bypassable delay and deflating the core military mechanism.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by decisively dismantling Side B's core causal mechanism. Side A's sharp distinction between training compute (affected by bans) and edge inference (where the actual battlefield OODA loop occurs) neutralized Side B's main impact. Furthermore, Side A won the prioritization clash, convincingly arguing that a state actor will shield its military programs from chip scarcity at the expense of its civilian sector, rendering Side B's "structural deficit" claim unproven. Side A's "adaptation dilemma" in the closing perfectly crystallized the competitiveness trade-off, leaving Side B without a strong comparative weighing mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where it separated frontier-model pretraining from battlefield inference and exposed Side B's failure to prove that chip scarcity falls on priority military programs rather than civilian users.

Both sides answered pressure questions with correct A1/A2 formatting, but B's answers did more actual work distinguishing counterfactuals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A2), distinguishing "a blank check accelerates effort relative to doing nothing, not capability relative to the real counterfactual" — this directly undercut A's core boomerang mechanism and A never adequately answered it in the following speeches.

Crucially, Side A suffered severe execution costs, with four of its five turns clipped for exceeding the word limit, leaving its final arguments incomplete and severely weakening its overall rhetorical impact and flow.
