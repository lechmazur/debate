### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won a close, high-quality clash by forcing PRO into a live dilemma: either retirement remains residual to growth (an open-ended treadmill with no one obliged to deliver clean firm power) or PRO’s own “delivered megawatts” off-ramp becomes a binding schedule that treats industry as planned around exit rather than growth-first residualization. CON’s institutional weighing—dates and liquidated damages drive procurement; PRO’s trigger obliges nobody—survived pressure better than PRO’s bridge-vs-cliff frame, and the closings crystallized who is actually on the hook. PRO answered B’s pressure questions cleanly and kept motion fidelity live, but never fully neutralized the incentive/permanence critique or the continuous-emissions and competitiveness costs of prolonging the dirtiest units. Two clippings cost CON some content, yet the comparative path stayed clearer for B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R1–R2 squeeze: PRO’s “schedule tied to delivered firm clean power” either stays open-ended (license/treadmill) or, once made bankable with conditional retirements, walks off the motion into CON’s date-first ordering.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON wins the trigger debate. PRO's "until delivered firm power exists" [B2.1/B2.6] leaves procurement optional, which A shows matches India's history of quietly extended pollution deadlines. A answers pressure directly: bind the sunset first with lead time, liquidated damages, and public override, so Gujarat keeps power but someone is on the hook to deliver clean firm power [A4.1-A4.4]. PRO concedes retirements even when a factory says it wants this year's coal [B4.1] and funds retrofits by taxing manufacturers [B4.3], collapsing into CON's ordering while hurting the workers and export competitiveness CON emphasizes. Bridge vs cliff is reframed as treadmill with no forcing mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2/A4 turning PRO's off-ramp into an open-ended license - "Who certifies that replacement exists?", extension quiet/retirement loud asymmetry, and that dates drive procurement vs procurement driving dates, exposing that PRO's own A1/A2 answers require CON's binding sunset.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively exposing the logical flaw in Side A's "bridge" framework. B demonstrates that making coal retirement conditional on the arrival of replacement capacity removes the forcing mechanism to actually build that clean capacity, turning the bridge into a permanent treadmill. B's analogy of fixing a construction deadline to force procurement is highly effective. While Side A attempts to patch this by adding conditions like enforcing retrofits, B successfully argues that the political economy of delayed retirement inherently undermines those very conditions. Side B's rebuttals were sharper, and their framing of the core clash was more compelling, despite minor clipping at the very end of two speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where they dismantle Side A's "bridge" metaphor by showing that without a fixed retirement date, there is no forcing mechanism to procure clean energy. B's observation in Rebuttal 2 that Side A's answers to the pressure questions actually adopted Side B's ordering (retiring plants even if factories want them) was particularly decisive.
