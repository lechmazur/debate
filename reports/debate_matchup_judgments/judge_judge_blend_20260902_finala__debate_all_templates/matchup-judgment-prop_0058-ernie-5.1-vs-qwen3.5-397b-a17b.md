The balance of grounding, metric discipline, and rebuttal sharpness favors PRO.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side B won by sustaining a coherent asymmetry argument across all rounds: savings are immediate and visible; capacity damage is slow, compounding, and irreversible in the specific ways government differs from the private sector. This framing was introduced early, maintained under pressure, and sharpened in closing. Side A's core responses relied on specific mechanisms—legally mandated reinvestment earmarks, risk-adjusted review panels triggering human oversight—that felt invented to answer the pressure questions rather than grounded in how any known efficiency drive actually operates. Claiming "most DOGE-style reviews are required by law to produce a reinvestment plan" audited by GAO is the kind of brittle precision the judging criteria discount. By contrast, Side B's counters (political incentives favor showing savings not rebuilding; government hiring rigidity makes recovery years slower; institutional memory is an ecosystem, not a machine) relied on plausible, epistemically honest general reasoning. Side A's best moments—the cost-of-doing-nothing argument and the distinction between targeted efficiency drives and broad austerity—were real contributions but never dismantled Side B's core asymmetry. Side B's Q1 pressure question ("what binding mechanism prevents legislators from pocketing the surplus?") was the most decisive single exchange: Side A answered with specific procedural claims that sounded authoritative but were not clearly grounded, and Side B's closing response ("paperwork does not preserve expertise") cleanly neutralized them. The category-error rebuttal on private-sector comparisons also went unanswered adequately. On balance, Side B's case remained the more durable and epistemically honest path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Q1 about binding reinvestment mechanisms (B3.1) was the sharpest exchange. Side A answered with claims about legally mandated earmarks and GAO audits (A4.1) that read as invented specificity rather than grounded knowledge of actual DOGE practice. Side B's closing response—"paperwork does not preserve expertise"—then cleanly collapsed the procedural answer, leaving the phantom-reinvestment line essentially intact as a live route to the PRO verdict.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side B won by making the core comparison clearer: visible short-term savings versus slower, compounding losses in institutional knowledge, oversight, resilience, and service delivery.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was against Side A's dashboard and reinvestment claims: it argued that spreadsheets miss contextual oversight value and that political incentives make promised reinvestment unreliable, which directly attacked Side A's main mechanism for making DOGE-style cuts safe.
