- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B won on the strength of its mechanism dilemma, which was the decisive argument in the debate. Side A never escaped this fork. Worse, Side A's own Rebuttal 2 concessions deepened the trap: introducing resident vetoes (not in the original plan) was a patch-up that Side B correctly identified as "fatal"—if neighborhoods can rationally opt out, dispersal cannot be a general rule for popular tourist cities, which is exactly what the proposition requires. Side B's pressure questions were also sharper than Side A's; Q2 on the mechanism was the most important question in the debate, and Side A's answer (gentle marketing produces a "15–20 percent shift") was both speculative and insufficient to solve core overload. Side A had live arguments—the equity case for service workers, the spillover-already-happening observation, the access-for-working-class-travelers point—but Side B's responses were adequate: "labor policy, not erasing the off-season" and "spillover proves need for better containment, not deliberate spreading." Side B's clipped Rebuttal 2 is a real execution cost but the key arguments appeared before the cut. On balance, Side B made the debate easier to decide by offering a single, clear comparative frame that Side A could not cleanly answer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's mechanism dilemma in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3–B4.5): PRO's plan is either gentle enough to be harmless but too weak to relieve overloaded cores, or strong enough to matter but then it exports tourism into more neighborhoods and erodes the off-season.
