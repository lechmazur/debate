### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B won by focusing the debate on “mainly” and distinguishing crowds from a durable nightlife economy built on sustained spending, jobs, and year-round operation. Side A plausibly showed that street food can attract and broaden crowds, but repeatedly relied on those crowds later filling indoor venues, reinforcing B’s claim that trucks are valuable complements rather than the primary economic anchor. A’s responses on bad weather and reliability remained especially weak.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange exposed the tension between A’s cheap-access model and its regulated-reliability model: fixed hours and supporting services increase costs, while minimal obligations preserve affordability at the expense of dependable year-round presence. B then tied this fork to A’s concession that trucks feed indoor rooms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a structural inconsistency in PRO's case: street food could not simultaneously be the cheap accessible backbone, the reliable regulated corridor, and the feeder that fills indoor venues. CON's "cost fork" and "garnish vs. plate" framing made the decisive clash clear. CON showed that every version of PRO's revival story still ended with spending converting inside staffed rooms, meaning reliance on indoor zones remained primary. PRO never supplied the demanded example of a district running mainly on street food, and conceding "modest operating costs" let CON close the price-advantage fork. CON's rebuttals were sharper, more consistent, and better at crystallizing the tradeoff.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 (B2) introduced the decisive "fork" that street food could be either cheap-and-unreliable or reliable-and-no-longer-cheap, and that PRO's own descriptions kept making trucks useful only as feeders to the indoor venues the motion asked cities to stop relying on. This exchange defined the rest of the debate and was never fully neutralized.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): B won on internal-consistency and weighing. PRO's own "feeds the indoor rooms" language conceded the anchor is indoors, and B's cheap-vs-reliable fork survived - once you mandate fixed late hours, restrooms, trash and security, the price advantage collapses. PRO never named a district that runs mainly on street food after direct Q1 pressure, and never resolved how a sidewalk queue beats a warm room in January. B's payroll/tax-base/year-round metric gave a cleaner definition of a nightlife economy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's double hit - calling out [A2.2] as a concession that trucks are a feeder not the engine, plus the fork that reliability regulations erase the low-cost story - which A4 then conceded as "modest operating costs" on razor-thin margins.
