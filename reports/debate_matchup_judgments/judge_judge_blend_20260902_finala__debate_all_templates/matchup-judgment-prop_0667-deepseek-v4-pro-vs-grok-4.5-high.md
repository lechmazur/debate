### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by offering the cleaner comparative mechanism: universality need not eliminate every workaround to meaningfully contract reliable formal employment, while wages, recruitment, and reorganization mitigate lasting shortages. Side A identified plausible evasion and profiling risks, but repeatedly misstated E-Verify as checking identity rather than employment eligibility records and asserted without support that most unauthorized work would survive. Its discrimination case established a possible incentive, not the large nationwide increase required, and both clipped speeches imposed execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response to the escape-hatch argument was decisive: residual identity fraud and off-book work do not make the policy cosmetic if nationwide checks materially raise their cost and remove many formal openings. Side A answered mainly by asserting that the vast majority would persist, without grounding that scale.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by providing a superior causal mechanism for how E-Verify affects discrimination. While Side B argued that a standardized check reduces profiling, Side A successfully countered that the fear of audit-triggering mismatches would rationally drive employers to use pre-hire screening. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's labor adjustment arguments by pointing out that a nationwide mandate, unlike state-level experiments, would drain the same labor pool simultaneously.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which definitively explained why the liability of a mandatory post-offer check creates a stronger incentive for pre-hire profiling than an optional paper I-9 system.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a much stronger and more logically coherent case, particularly regarding the mechanics of employer behavior. Side A's argument that the liability and audit flags associated with a failed E-Verify check incentivize pre-hire discrimination based on names and accents effectively neutralized Side B's claim that the system is "color-blind." Side A also sharply criticized the structural limitation of basic E-Verify (verifying identity/SSN match rather than work authorization), which Side B failed to adequately answer beyond asserting that it "raises the cost." While Side A suffered minor execution costs from being clipped at the very end of two speeches, the core arguments were already fully developed, and Side A's rebuttals were far more decisive, directly dismantling Side B's psychological and structural assumptions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1), which explained that mandating the check increases the employer's liability for a mismatch, thereby rationally driving them to screen out "foreign-sounding" applicants pre-hire to avoid the flag, directly dismantling Side B's claim that a post-offer electronic check reduces discretionary profiling.
