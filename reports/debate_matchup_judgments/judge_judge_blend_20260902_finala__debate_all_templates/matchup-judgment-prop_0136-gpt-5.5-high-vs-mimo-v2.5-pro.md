### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by crystallizing the empirical record of failed East Asian bonus policies (South Korea's record lows despite billions) against cross-country patterns where structural support sustains higher fertility, while directly answering pressure on thresholds and forward-looking behavior with timing and reach distinctions. Side B's practicality and certainty arguments were undermined by repeated reliance on "not enough" or "too uncertain" without neutralizing the track record; clips on multiple B turns added execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 directly answered both Pressure Questions with France/Nordics comparisons and a timing reframing that sharpened the decision-point clash, outperforming B's counter on uncertainty and non-comparability.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO built a more convincing case by showing that ongoing structural costs are the binding constraint on fertility and that one-off bonuses arrive too late in the decision timeline. When CON argued South Korea's bonuses were overwhelmed by structural headwinds, PRO turned that concession into support for structural reform. PRO also answered pressure questions more decisively, using cross-country comparisons to establish a falsifiable threshold and reframing leakage as institutional reliability. CON's argument about marginal certainty and speed was plausible but could not overcome the intuitive timing problem or explain away the regional track record of cash transfers failing to raise fertility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's turn of CON's South Korea example: if structural pressures overwhelmed bonuses, then structural reform—not larger bonuses—is the necessary lever.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO wins by successfully framing fertility decisions as a long-term calculus driven by recurring costs, making one-off bonuses structurally mismatched to the problem. While CON raised valid points about implementation uncertainty, leakage, and cultural differences in the Nordic/France comparison, CON never fully resolved why a one-time payment would overcome the exact same overwhelming structural headwinds. PRO's rhetorical framing ("precision at the wrong moment is worthless") and cleaner weighing of the empirical record gave it the decisive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing turn of CON's admission that "structural pressures overwhelmed cash policies" into a direct proof of the proposition, paired with the recurring "precision at the wrong moment" framing, decisively collapsed CON's certainty-at-the-margin argument.
