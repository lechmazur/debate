### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won by showing that partial but systematic preservation is materially better than preventable loss. Its release-triggered model, controlled access safeguards, and distinction between capture and later curation answered the selection and rights objections reasonably well. Side B's technical-burden case was strong, but it relied too heavily on an unsupported perfection standard under which nonfunctional or incomplete preservation has little value. It also conceded that voluntarism cannot reliably prevent loss without proving that imperfect mandatory deposit would be worse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was against Side B's claim that a release trigger would miss obscure works later delisted or server-killed. A legal obligation triggered at initial distribution captures them before that later disappearance, whereas merely having a public footprint does not ensure volunteers preserved them. This weakened Side B's proposed alternative at the central point of market failure.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate as a choice between actionable preservation ('evidence over oblivion') and an impossible 'perfection standard'. Side A made a compelling ontological argument that software is a performance rather than an artifact, but suffered a fatal logical error in Rebuttal 2 when arguing that a deposit triggered at launch would somehow miss apps that die quickly. Side B effectively used the book/film analogy to show that early films and manuscripts also lack immediate public accessibility, neutralizing Side A's primary offense.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which smoothly clarified the scope of deposited server code, dismantled Side A's false dilemma regarding universal vs. retrospective selection, and delivered the round-winning "perfection standard" framing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): PRO kept a live route: capture-now-curate-later via release triggers mirrors book deposit, imperfect client/assets/owned-server-code under embargo still counts as preservation like closed-stack film, and volunteers admittedly don't stop loss [B4.1]. CON's core 'corpse/museum label' critique was sharp but demanded fully runnable, server-complete experience — a perfection standard that would also invalidate early film/manuscript preservation. CON's A1 that a launch trigger doesn't close the gap misfires, and its cost/rights burden on small creators was never weighed against PRO's embargo/redaction model. Cleaner comparative: evidence over guaranteed oblivion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.4 turn exposing PRO's concession that deposit is 'not a promise of public functionality' [A2.4] as preserving only a museum label, not the exhibit, and PRO's later rehabilitation via controlled-access analogy in A4-A5.
