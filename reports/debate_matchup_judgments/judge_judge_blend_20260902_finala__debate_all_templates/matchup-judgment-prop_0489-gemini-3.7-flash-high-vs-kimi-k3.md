### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that independent pre-deployment review adds a distinct layer of prevention before borrowers suffer irreversible denials. Side A raised strong concerns about drift, capture, compliance costs, and ambiguous fairness standards, but repeatedly treated auditing and continuous monitoring as substitutes. Side B persuasively answered that both can coexist and grounded audits in existing less-discriminatory-alternative principles. Side A never established that imperfection, possible capture, or certification costs outweigh catching discriminatory models before launch.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest response was that model drift proves the need for continuous monitoring in addition to, not instead of, pre-deployment auditing. This undercut Side A’s certificate-versus-surveillance framing because post-deployment review cannot prevent the first wave of harmful decisions.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won a competitive debate by forcing a clean comparative: certificate versus continuous surveillance. A's opening established no single audit standard, model drift, auditor capture, and chilling of credit-invisible inclusion. After B conceded pre-deployment and monitoring are "mutually necessary," A made that concession decisive—existing fair-lending doctrine and outcome-testing already do the real work, so the issuer-paid stamp adds sedation and cost without preventing harm. B's irreversibility frame stayed powerful but never squared how audits avoid capture or settle the signal/proxy problem better than courts already do.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2/closing use of B's "mutually necessary" concession: once continuous monitoring is indispensable either way, the motion's one-time paid certificate must justify additive value and fails.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON won the central clash. PRO relied on irreversibility and proxy risk, but CON showed no auditable standard exists (fairness metrics conflict), pre-deployment certification doesn't solve drift, and issuer-paid audits recreate rating-agency capture. PRO's answer that the standard is existing "less discriminatory alternative" doctrine conceded CON's point that courts/regulators already do this, and its "mutually necessary" concession let CON frame audit as redundant certificate vs indispensable continuous surveillance. CON also turned irreversibility into omission harms to credit-invisible borrowers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2/B4 sequence that zip code is simultaneously signal and proxy, that "mutually necessary" concedes surveillance does the work, and that audit logs don't prevent capture as rating agencies showed, was the debate's decisive turn.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by presenting audits as an additional independent checkpoint rather than a replacement for continuous enforcement. B plausibly explained how existing backtesting, less-discriminatory alternatives, and material-change thresholds answer A’s concerns about business necessity, model drift, and constant re-auditing. A’s historical-data critique was incisive, but it did not establish that audits must reproduce old approval patterns or inevitably reject inclusive models. Its liability-shield and innovation-cost claims also remained more asserted than demonstrated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal directly answered both pressure questions: historical validation can test predictive necessity before deployment, while materiality thresholds distinguish meaningful model changes from routine recalibration. This substantially weakened A’s claim that audits are either technically impossible or required after every minor update.

Prefer the side that made the vote easier after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 A1/A2: backtesting already used for model pricing/validation, plus ordinary materiality lines, turning CON's own questions into concessions while keeping the detection asymmetry live.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the decisive feasibility and necessity clash. A answered business necessity via backtesting [A4.1], provided materiality thresholds from existing SR 11-7-style validation [A4.3] to defang the tollbooth claim, and pressed Q2 that if regulators can detect bias post-deployment from loan files, the same test works pre-deployment.
