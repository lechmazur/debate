### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the motion's cost qualifier decisive. Side B had a strong moral and public-health case that lead harm is irreversible and source removal is best in principle, but it leaned on overconfident claims about guarantees, some brittle exact figures, and did not adequately answer affordability harms, disconnections, weak municipal fiscal capacity, or the need for treatment during long replacement transitions. Side A offered the clearer comparative alternative: enforced corrosion control plus targeted replacement gives faster risk reduction without triggering fiscal shocks that can worsen health outcomes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the perfection challenge by showing full replacement also needs years of treatment and oversight, then turned Side B's debt mechanism by arguing it can weaken the compliance capacity needed to prevent Flint-style failures.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal cleanly exposed Side A's reliance on "federal assistance" as an evasion of the motion's core premise, and brilliantly pointed out that the decades-long transition to full replacement would still require the very chemical treatments Side A claimed were inherently prone to failure.

CON also demonstrated that full replacement still requires chemical treatment during a decades-long transition, making PRO's "permanent solution" framing misleading, while targeted replacement delivers faster protection to highest-risk households without the fiscal shock that causes disconnections and offsetting harms. PRO's case remained morally urgent but never answered the real-world tradeoffs the motion demanded.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON catching PRO's concession that federal assistance could mitigate costs ([A2.2]), then pressing in questions ([B3.1]) and rebuttals ([B2.1], [B4.4], [B5.1]) that the motion explicitly demands replacement *even when* such help is unavailable.
