### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully centering the debate on the pedagogical purpose of coursework. While Side A proved that disclosure offers better visibility and enforceability, Side B effectively argued that assessing editing is categorically different from assessing generating, rendering Side A's "visibility" irrelevant to the core educational goal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's distinction between tutors (who scaffold existing student labor) and AI (which substitutes for it) effectively dismantled Side A's strongest equivalence argument.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO won by making the enforcement/visibility clash decisive and comparative: bans offer zero observation or honest-incentive gradient, while disclosure-plus-accountability creates free disclosure + costly concealment plus partial surfaces (logs, random oral probes). CON's process-is-the-learning and scaffolding-vs-substitution points remained live and forceful, but PRO narrowed them with mixed-policy allowances, tutor/writing-center parallels under supervision, and the "compared to what" standard that left CON defending unenforceable norms and faith in culture. PRO's direct A1/A2 answers on incentive asymmetry and concrete (if imperfect) mechanisms converted pressure into a clearer reason to prefer the proposition as written; CON's later culture and "sees the wrong skill" replies were coherent but never recovered a workable alternative mechanism or equalized other undisclosed advantages.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 on disclosure-compliance incentives (honesty free, concealment risky) vs. ban (any use is the violation, so everything hides) plus imperfect logs/orals still better than zero surface.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins because it defended the core educational purpose of coursework—developing thinking through drafting—and showed that disclosure shifts assessment to editing machine output, making PRO’s prized "visibility" a procedural distraction that assesses the wrong skill. It also offered an unanswered distinction between tutoring (which amplifies student cognition) and AI (which replaces it), undermining PRO’s equity and enforceability claims. PRO’s pragmatic case for incentives and detection was sharp, but it never squarely answered why assessing editing skill is better than preserving the norm of generative thinking, even imperfectly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s rebuttal distinguishing AI substitution from tutoring/scaffolding, and its attack on PRO’s spot-check mechanism as superficial theater that evaluates editing rather than the generative reasoning the assignment was meant to test.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a highly consistent and practical framework. A successfully counters B's 'drafting is reasoning' argument by biting the bullet that AI can author the first draft, provided the student is accountable for defending the final product. A also effectively exposes B's demand for a perfectly auditable system as an unrealistic standard, noting that universities already rely on imperfect safeguards like honor codes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly answers B's pressure by conceding that first drafts could be AI-authored, effectively neutralizing B's 'initial synthesis' attack by shifting the focus to the final, accountable product and exposing B's 'perfectionist' standard.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won by consistently collapsing PRO’s core safeguards: disclosure cannot audit extent of AI use and “accountability” for reasoning is incoherent for students still learning the material. The tutor/writing-center distinction (initial synthesis vs. feedback on existing work) landed repeatedly and neutralized A’s main analogies; B’s later introduction of process-based verification (timed writing, tracked histories, oral defenses) as a superior alternative answered the visibility challenge without relying on self-report. A’s status-quo-is-worse pragmatism and professional-preparation path remained live but secondary once the proposition’s internal conditions were shown unenforceable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 dismantling of the tutor analogy: AI generates first synthesis on demand; tutors react after the student has already attempted the draft.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON undermined PRO's core policy by showing that disclosure without verifiability is false confidence, not accountability. The distinction between AI replacing student reasoning versus refining it, combined with a concrete process-based alternative, gave CON a cleaner path to victory. PRO's "imperfect transparency" frame could not recover once CON demonstrated the safeguard's inability to distinguish minimal from maximal AI use, and PRO's clipped closing left key clash unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal exposing that disclosure creates only a "paper trail" equally compatible with five keystrokes or a fully generated essay, while offering process-based assessment as a genuinely verifiable alternative.
