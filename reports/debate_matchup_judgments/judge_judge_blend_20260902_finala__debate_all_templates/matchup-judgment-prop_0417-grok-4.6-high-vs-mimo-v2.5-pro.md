However, B's opening and closing were both clipped for exceeding word limits, costing B the full articulation of its final synthesis exactly when it needed to consolidate its strongest late-round point. Combined with A's tighter logical handling of the pivotal mechanism dispute and consistent framing (the "moment after last call," "vehicle that is not theirs"), A ends the debate with the cleaner, more resolved path to victory, though B's root-cause framing kept real pressure on throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answer to its own Q1 ("If last call stays put, transit pours nothing") directly dismantled B's core reframe that transit extensions function as extended drinking time, forcing B into a weaker, more speculative fallback about "behavioral pressure" that never fully recovered the lost ground.

- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A built a much stronger and more logically sound case, effectively dismantling Side B's primary argument that transit extensions increase intoxication by pointing out that trains do not extend venue serving hours ("the train does not unlock the tap"). - **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent causal stories, but B executed more cleanly and won the decisive substantive clash. A's opening, both rebuttals, and closing were all clipped for exceeding word limits, repeatedly cutting off its arguments before the judge could see them — a real execution cost given the format's explicit rules. Substantively, B's "duration-and-dose" framing directly answered A's compression/displacement mechanism: B argued that extra licensed hours are extra pours after people are already impaired, not merely relocated drinking, and that a later train lengthens the window in which an already-parked driver's judgment erodes rather than removing the car.
