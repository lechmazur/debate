PRO's rebuttals narrowed the dispute cleanly; CON's later speeches largely recapped rather than adapted. The margin is clear but not dominant because CON's political-backlash evidence is real and PRO's five-year claim rests on administrative optimism that remains somewhat unresolved.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins on the two most decisive clashes. First, the category-error argument — the motion covers Libya, Sudan, and Yemen alongside the UAE and Saudi Arabia, and PRO never adequately addressed states with no unified administration, civil-war displacement, or fractured governance. Pointing to Gulf digital infrastructure proves nothing for conflict-affected North African states. PRO's responses either conflated wealthier Gulf states with the rest or offered generic assurances that "registries can expand," which CON repeatedly showed was insufficient. Second, CON caught and sustained a damaging internal contradiction in PRO's plan: if Year One delivers universal cash via existing IDs, there are no targeting savings — that is just another universal program. But if Years Two through Five narrow to targeted transfers, that narrowing is exclusion by design. PRO's Rebuttal 2 asserted "exclusion is impossible," then immediately described a system that starts universal and narrows — a direct self-contradiction. CON exploited this in both the second rebuttal and closing with clarity and persistence. CON's Iran reframe — a demand drop of a third is a price shock hitting transport and food, not a gentle adjustment — also partially neutralized PRO's showcase example, and PRO's defense (blame sanctions) didn't fully close the gap. PRO's moral case against subsidies as regressive is sound and was never fully answered, and the broader reform argument has real force. PRO's opening was strong. But PRO's Rebuttal 2 was notably thin (well under the cap) and left both pressure questions only partially answered, ceding ground CON needed for the win. CON's closing was clipped, which is a real execution cost, but the portion visible consistently crystallized the live clash rather than reopening new ground. Overall CON built a cleaner, more durable path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to PRO's Q2 answer: PRO claimed "exclusion is impossible" under the phased plan, then described Year One as universal cash — meaning no targeting savings initially — followed by narrowing over Years Two to Five. CON caught this as a direct self-contradiction: universal cash is just another universal program, the fiscal benefits require the later narrowing phase, and narrowing is exclusion by design. This single exchange crystallized why PRO cannot simultaneously promise full coverage and significant fiscal savings within the five-year window, and it remained unresolved through the closing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON demonstrated that the proposition’s five-year timeline is unworkable across the full heterogeneity of Gulf and North African states, with fragile and conflict-affected countries lacking the administrative capacity to build targeting systems. CON also exposed a decisive internal contradiction in PRO’s phased plan: year-one universal cash transfers carry none of the promised targeting savings, while the later narrowing to targeted support is exclusion by design. PRO’s regressivity and fiscal arguments retained force but were outweighed by the unresolved implementation gap and the risk of leaving the poorest behind in precisely the states most unable to manage the transition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal, which identified Libya, Sudan, and Yemen as states incapable of building targeting systems in five years and showed that PRO’s year-one universal cash program eliminates the promised fiscal savings while later narrowing to targeted transfers is exclusion by design.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): CON wins by successfully exposing a fatal logical tension in PRO's adaptive response. When pressed on exclusion, PRO pivoted to Year 1 universal cash, which CON correctly identified as defeating the promised fiscal savings and contradicting the motion's targeted requirement within the five-year window. CON also effectively leveraged regional heterogeneity, showing that a blanket five-year deadline collapses in fragile or conflict states lacking basic governance or ID systems. PRO's closing failed to resolve the coverage-versus-savings contradiction or address the specific state-level barriers, relying instead on moral urgency and repetition. CON's alternative of sequenced reform and quantity caps provided a credible off-ramp that sharpened the comparison and made the ballot clearer.
