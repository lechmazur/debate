### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by consistently comparing capped-credit outcomes with the realistic alternatives rather than evaluating high-interest cards in isolation. Its description of payday loans as bounded was especially damaging and conflicted with its own account of worse substitutes. Side B’s two clipped turns impose an execution cost, but the visible material still supplied a clear and substantially unanswered comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal decisively turned Side A’s answers: delaying the repair could threaten employment, family or mechanic credit was speculative, and calling payday loans capped and bounded undermined Side A’s claim that removing cards prevents worse borrowing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive clash: same emergency, regulated high-APR card versus the stipulated no-card world. PRO’s extraction rhetoric was strong in isolation, but it repeatedly attacked cards alone and never showed mechanic installment, family aid, delayed repair, or payday outperforming a disclosed revolving line. CON exploited PRO’s own concessions (hardship; cards “drive” borrowers to worse products) and the Rebuttal 2 pivot that treated payday as a gentler “small, capped” advance. Format clips cost CON some polish, but the live comparison still favors A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2/Closing on B4.1–B4.2: weak named alternatives plus the contradiction of defending payday after four speeches against 30% rates.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won because it successfully showed that capping rates eliminates the least-bad emergency option for high-risk borrowers without removing the underlying emergency, and exposed Side A's failure to demonstrate that its alternatives would actually improve outcomes. Side A's morally vivid case never recovered from its concessionary description of payday loans as "small, capped advances" or from Side B's demonstration that delaying essential repairs can cause job loss. Side B's rebuttals were sharper, its pressure questions more pointed, and its closing effectively flipped Side A's "bounded" framing against it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 (Turn 4) established that Side A attacked the credit card in isolation without ever showing that the post-cap alternatives were better for the borrower, and showed that Side A's own causal story—high-rate cards drive borrowers to payday lenders—implied that removing the card would simply accelerate that path.
