### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the decisive comparison cleaner: a guaranteed, compounding rise in execution costs against an unproven stability benefit. A repeatedly forced the empirical pattern from France/Italy/Sweden (migration into substitutes, wider effective spreads, no clear crash prevention) and showed B's replies mostly relabeled unfavorable data as "toxic churn exiting" or gestured at a "properly designed" FTT without a workable enforcement mechanism. B kept a coherent purpose-of-markets frame and landed that circuit breakers are ex post, but never neutralized the cost-record clash or the scalpel-vs-sledgehammer alternative. After the full exchange A left the easier path to reject the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's France/Italy turn that if HFT were pure predation, FTT removal should improve institutional execution—yet spreads and implementation shortfall rose—undercutting B's phantom-liquidity theory.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON wins by demonstrating that the empirical record of FTTs undermines PRO's theoretical case and by exposing a decisive logical tension in PRO's account. PRO argues HFT liquidity is purely predatory "phantom liquidity," yet when FTTs removed that activity in France and Italy, effective spreads and implementation shortfall rose rather than fell. CON repeatedly presses this point, showing that the liquidity removed was not merely toxic churn but genuine market-making that benefited ordinary investors. PRO never supplies a mechanism to reconcile this data with their theory, instead relabeling the spread widening as the exit of "subsidized quote flickering" without proof. Additionally, CON's instrument-migration argument—that HFT simply moves to untaxed derivatives and CFDs—goes largely unanswered by PRO beyond a speculative assertion that a "properly designed" tax could reach foreign derivatives, which CON correctly identifies as a promise rather than a design. CON's alternative of targeted ex-ante rules (order-to-trade ratios, minimum resting times) remains comparatively superior because it distinguishes pathology from genuine liquidity provision, a distinction PRO never overcomes. PRO's case remains elegant in theory but empirically and comparatively hollow by the end of the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 that if HFT liquidity were truly illusory predation, removing it via FTTs should have lowered institutional execution costs, yet France and Italy saw spreads and implementation shortfall widen—exactly the outcome consistent with HFT providing real liquidity, not merely extracting it.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B wins the core trade-off comparison. A theorized phantom liquidity and flash-crash prevention but never showed an FTT preventing crashes. B cited France 2012 as a narrow modern FTT and showed volume migrated to untaxed substitutes (futures, CFDs) without leaving co-location, and that effective spreads and implementation shortfall rose - a measurable cost to pension funds. B's targeted alternatives (order-to-trade ratios, minimum resting times) hit cancel-heavy quoting ex-ante, undermining A's ex-post circuit-breaker characterization. A's answers in R2 asserted a "properly designed" FTT would cover derivatives and relabeled adverse spread data as toxic churn exiting, without a discriminating mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.3 plus B4.3-B4.4: instrument-substitution and spread-widening rebuttal that turned A's phantom-liquidity claim into a cost for real investors and exposed A's evasion defense.
