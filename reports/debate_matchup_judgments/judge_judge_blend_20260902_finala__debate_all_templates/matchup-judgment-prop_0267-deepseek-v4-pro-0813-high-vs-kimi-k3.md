### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON built a single, consistent causal throughline—vendors follow demand created by fixed anchors, they don't generate it—and pressed it relentlessly through rebuttals, pressure questions, and closing. Its Pressure Questions exposed a real fee dilemma (low permit costs vs. funded 2 a.m. enforcement) and a definitional concession (PRO's own "complement fixed anchors" language), and CON's answers to PRO's reciprocal questions were direct and well-labeled (A1/A2), converting the "night-shift nurse's diner" example into a point for its own side by correctly noting that indoor hospitality on the motion's terms is not limited to cocktail districts. PRO's late pivot to "hospitals, stations, and warehouses are not indoor hospitality zones" in the closing looked like a definitional retreat rather than a rebuttal, which CON called out cleanly. PRO's case had real substantive content (unserved late-night demand, spillover, distributed risk) and adapted with a pooled-enforcement mechanism, but never fully resolved the mobility-vs-commitment tension CON raised, and its own concessions kept being repurposed against it. CON's opening was clipped, a real execution cost, but its argument had already stated its thesis and the subsequent rounds more than compensated with sharper, more damaging engagement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2's move ("complement fixed anchors" concedes the whole motion; pinning vendors down reinvents the venue "minus its kitchen, seating, and winter trade") combined with the fee-dilemma pressure question was the decisive exchange—PRO's later answers (pooled enforcement, hospital/night-bus demand nodes) never escaped the trap that its own examples all presupposed a fixed anchor already generating foot traffic.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by forcing and then deciding the live burden of "rather than rely mainly." Across rounds it turned PRO's own framing (vendors complement anchors; trucks go to clubs, hospitals, stations, bus stops) into a causality/follow-demand win, and blocked the late definitional shrink of "indoor" to cocktail zones by keeping diners and other leased premises on CON's side of the text. PRO had a real alternative route—thin, odd-hour demand that full indoor investment skips, plus managed distributed permits—but never showed a clean trucks-first mechanism for genuinely dark blocks or a nightlife economy beyond a food point, so the motion's main-strategy claim stayed underproven. A's clipped opening was a real but non-decisive cost; later clash control still made the easier ballot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's conversion of B's "complement fixed anchors" line into a garnish/status-quo concession, then extended in A4/A5 against hospital/station relabeling of anchors.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a decisive logical trap around causality, successfully arguing that street food always follows fixed demand generators (clubs, hospitals, stations) rather than creating it, rendering trucks secondary "garnish" rather than primary revivers. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's late-stage attempt to separate hospitals from "indoor hospitality zones" by pointing out that fixed indoor premises like diners serve the same thin demand better and carry the necessary leases and accountability. Despite a minor clipping issue in the opening, Side A's rebuttals were exceptionally sharp, controlled the clash, and made the comparative burden impossible for Side B to escape.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, specifically the "garnish" trap and the dismantling of Side B's redefinition of anchors to include hospitals, proving that trucks still rely on fixed infrastructure to generate foot traffic and therefore cannot be the primary strategy for dark blocks.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, competitive cases, but PRO (A) sustained a broader, more resilient throughline: accessibility/reach, entrepreneurship, safety-through-presence, and cost/flexibility, and it repeatedly turned CON's own examples and vocabulary against it (the "food pod" concession in Rebuttal 1, and later reframing CON's own "amplifier" coinage in the closing to argue permits work from a "standing start" of latent demand). Side B's rhetorical choice to embrace the amplifier metaphor in the closing backfires when Side A logically points out that an amplifier cannot fix a dead circuit without underlying infrastructure and a primary signal.
