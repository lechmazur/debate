- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side B wins on the strength of two live arguments that Side A never fully neutralized. Second, and most decisively, Side A's A1 answer in Rebuttal 2 conceded that the right activates not when land physically vanishes but at "systematic uninhabitability" from events like aquifer intrusion. This collapsed the "bright, scientifically objective line" that was the load-bearing element of Side A's opening. Side B immediately recognized and exploited this concession, correctly pointing out that distinguishing "expensive to adapt" from "legally uninhabitable" is a policy judgment, not a physical fact, and that the same logic extends to drought and desertification—validating the slippery-slope concern exactly. Side A's closing did not sharpen this exchange or offer a workable threshold; it largely repeated the moral framing from earlier rounds. Side B's enforceability argument—that a right no state honors at scale teaches states that legal commitments are optional, eroding the broader asylum framework—also remained live throughout. Side A's strongest counter (bilateral agreements without legal rights reduce displaced people to supplicants with no anchor) is genuine and CON never fully dispelled it, which keeps the margin competitive rather than dominant. The clipping of Side B's closing is a minor execution cost but the visible portion had already crystallized the key clashes. Overall, Side B controlled the most consequential technical exchanges and forced the concession that mattered most.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO built and maintained the stronger path to victory across the debate. Its central architecture—that voluntary bilateral and multilateral cooperation can fail, leaving displaced populations with no legal floor—was never adequately answered. PRO's Q1 ("what happens when no state volunteers to sign?") was the most decisive moment: CON's response pivoted to "multilateral frameworks," which is simply another form of voluntary cooperation and repeats the exact failure mode PRO identified. The justice argument (causation creates obligation) and the legal-gap argument were never squarely rebutted. PRO also handled the "individual vs. collective" pressure by noting a limited legal right can operate collectively, which CON characterized as a damaging concession but was better read as clarification. CON's strongest contribution was exposing the heat-trigger scale problem—wet-bulb conditions affect hundreds of millions and are seasonal, not binary—and PRO's sustained-uninhabitability clarification in R2 was reasonable but left some residual vulnerability. CON's slippery-slope and system-collapse arguments were plausible but undercut by PRO's comparison to existing 100-million-person conflict displacement. Two clipped speeches for PRO are real execution costs but did not destroy the core logic; key arguments were preserved each time. CON's closing repeated earlier points without meaningful progression and overstated the "PRO conceded" framing. Overall, "obligation follows harm, not leverage" remained live and unanswered, giving PRO the cleaner reason to vote for the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 ("if no state volunteers a bilateral treaty, are displaced people left stateless by diplomatic default?") and the follow-through in R2: CON's answer—"multilateral frameworks"—was simply more voluntary cooperation, which PRO correctly identified as repeating the same failure. This exchange crystallized the core asymmetry: CON's entire alternative rests on goodwill that may never materialize, while PRO's legal right creates an obligation that survives diplomatic indifference.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): CON won by showing that asylum is structurally mismatched for collective environmental displacement, requiring individual border-crossing and adversarial litigation that extinguishes sovereignty. PRO's moral urgency and diplomacy-failure argument were potent, but CON's sharper institutional analysis and superior rebuttal execution created the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1-A4.6) framing PRO's collective recognition redefinition as a concession that traditional asylum is structurally unfit, paired with the Tuvalu-Australia treaty example demonstrating a superior proactive alternative to reactive litigation.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): CON wins by successfully exploiting the structural mismatch between individual, adversarial asylum courts and collective climate displacement. CON's alternative of state-led relocation better addressed scale and sovereignty, while PRO's "legal floor vs. diplomatic luck" framing, though strong, could not overcome the mechanism deficit. CON's rhetorical contrast between forcing citizens to sue foreign governments versus preserving dignity through diplomacy proved decisive.
