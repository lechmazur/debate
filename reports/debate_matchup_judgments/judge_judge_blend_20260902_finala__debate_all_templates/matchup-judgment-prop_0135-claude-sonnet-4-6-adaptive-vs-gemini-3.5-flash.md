### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side B wins a close but clear debate. Side A made strong points about vague thresholds, regulatory capture, housing misattribution, and targeted alternatives, and often had the sharper line-by-line rebuttal. But Side B better defended the motion as written: the cap is triggered only when crowding, pollution, and housing pressure outweigh revenue, and B persuasively framed high-volume cruise and bus arrivals as a physical-capacity problem that pricing, zoning, and piecemeal management may not solve. A's repeated claim that B had shifted from economic balancing to physical capacity was somewhat overstated, because the motion itself asks to weigh physical and social harms against revenue. B's holistic preservation frame ultimately gave the cleaner reason to vote PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's best exchange was attacking the remedy mismatch and pressing why physical saturation should not be handled by targeted capacity rules. Side B's decisive answer was that the motion already weighs physical and social harms against revenue, and that absolute spatial limits make some arrival cap necessary once the city is being overwhelmed.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): B won by consistently exposing the mismatch between the motion's economic trigger and the physical harms described, showing alternatives address volume without blunt caps, and capitalizing on A's concession in R2. A defended problems well but failed to justify the specific remedy or answer precision concerns directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 rebuttal on the economic trigger vs. physical capacity inconsistency, which forced A to concede and undermined the motion as written.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): B won by exposing that A defended severe urban problems without adequately justifying the specific remedy in the motion. B demonstrated a damaging tension in A's case: A simultaneously framed the trigger as holistic physical-social reality while the motion text required comparing incommensurable harms against revenue on a single scale. A never satisfactorily answered why a hard economic-balancing cap was preferable to targeted alternatives like staggering and site-specific routing, and B's pressure questions successfully pinned A on the missing mechanism linking day-tripper caps to renewed overnight demand. While A's imagery of physical saturation in medieval squares was vivid and rhetorically effective, B's analytical pressure on indeterminacy, regulatory capture, and blunt instrumentation remained live and largely unanswered after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1-B4.8], where B used A's own answer to the first pressure question to show that A's reframing of the trigger as physical-social undermined the motion's actual economic-balancing wording, crystallizing that A had defended problems rather than the proposed cap mechanism.
