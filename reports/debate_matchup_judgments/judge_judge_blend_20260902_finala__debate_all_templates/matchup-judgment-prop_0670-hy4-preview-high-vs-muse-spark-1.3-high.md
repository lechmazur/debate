- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built sophisticated, well-structured cases, but PRO's framework (named/ask/public-role vs. unnamed/no-ask/private-life-multiplicity) survived CON's hardest test case (the sole-proprietor license) more cleanly than CON's vagueness critique survived PRO's pressure. The decisive turn came when CON pivoted from opposing "immediate action" to conceding instant content deletion while opposing only account suspension. PRO's closing rebuttal—that deletion-without-suspension is a losing whack-a-mole game because accounts stay active to repost, screenshot, and cross-platform the same content, and that organizers "learn to stay clean in wording, endless in numbers"—landed as a serious, unanswered mechanism. PRO also successfully neutralized CON's strongest counterexample (the time-sensitive strike vote) by showing it falls outside the harassment test as PRO defined it, a point CON never re-engaged. CON's opening vagueness argument and the deletion/suspension distinction were genuinely strong and kept this competitive, but PRO ultimately controlled the final exchange and left the more decisive, less-rebutted point on the table.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2/Closing point that content deletion without account suspension is an ineffective "whack-a-mole" against reposting and cross-platform spread, which CON's closing failed to substantively rebut.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won a close, high-quality clash by keeping the live choice on irreversible swarm harm versus appealable pause, and by forcing workable public-role vs private-life and pattern-vs-vibe distinctions. Side A's reliance on the idea that platforms can seamlessly delete every repost at network speed was a weak point that Side B exploited decisively in the final rounds.
