Side A's narrative remained more coherent and responsive throughout.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO wins on three live routes that CON never neutralized. First, PRO's "who pays now" reframe—that industrial meat's externalities are already borne disproportionately by low-income households through water contamination, air pollution, and public health costs—was stated in the opening and never adequately answered by CON across any subsequent round. CON implicitly accepted the externalities are real but never explained why the current hidden cost structure is preferable to a corrective tax. Second, PRO repeatedly pressed that CON offered no alternative to internalize these massive externalities, leaving CON defending a status quo that PRO had recharacterized as itself a regressive tax on the poor. Third, PRO's pressure question Q1 exposed a genuine logical tension in CON's case—the claim that tax revenue will dry up (consumption falls) and the claim that poor families will simply pay it (consumption stays high) cannot both be true simultaneously. CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to this was clever—calling it an "inescapable dilemma"—but turned against CON: if consumption stays "substantial for decades," revenue is stable and subsidies are fundable, which is what PRO argued. CON's strongest individual point was the time-poverty/preparation-cost barrier for plant-based substitution in Rebuttal 2, a genuinely specific and plausible claim PRO never fully answered. But this was introduced late and underweighted in CON's closing. CON also leaned heavily on emotional rhetoric ("economic violence," "starving the working class") which, while vivid, sometimes substituted for engagement with the structural argument PRO was making. PRO's case was more coherent across rounds, better adapted to the evolving clash, and left fewer live burdens unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question Q1 was the most decisive exchange: it exposed that CON's two primary claims—(1) the tax eliminates its own revenue base and (2) poor families simply pay the penalty—are mutually exclusive. CON's Rebuttal 2 response tried to reframe this as an "inescapable dilemma" for PRO, but in doing so, CON admitted that consumption will remain substantial, validating PRO's revenue stability argument and undercutting the urgency of CON's "starving families" rhetoric.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by landing sharper rebuttals that exposed the logical contradiction in B's position: B conceded consumption would remain substantial for decades to preserve revenue, which A correctly framed as making the tax either environmentally ineffective or a permanent regressive burden. A directly addressed pressure questions with the sin-tax dilemma and practical substitution barriers, while B's answers relied more on restating design features without fully neutralizing the tradeoff. A's closing crystallized the immediate harm versus speculative benefit comparison more cleanly than B's focus on hidden costs already paid.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly leveraged B's concession on continued consumption to dismantle both environmental and equity claims.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully framing the status quo as a hidden, regressive burden and defending the revenue-recycling model against CON’s sin tax dilemma. While CON delivered sharper rhetoric and raised a strong practical point about time poverty and food preparation, PRO’s clarification that Pigouvian taxes aim to internalize costs rather than eliminate consumption neutralized CON’s central contradiction claim. PRO more directly engaged the motion’s even-if premise, offering a cleaner comparative path on net fairness, revenue stability, and long-term harm reduction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2 turn exploiting PRO’s admission that meat consumption will remain substantial for decades to argue the tax becomes a permanent regressive surcharge was strategically sharp. PRO’s response successfully reframed this as a feature of stable externality pricing that funds offsets while still reducing marginal harm, cleanly resolving the clash.
