### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by making the clearer comparative case that core status is justified by universal exposure and unforgivability of leaving school without manipulation resistance, not by technological complexity or academic difficulty. Side A’s best points on opportunity cost, domain knowledge, and bureaucratic dilution were strong, but they leaned heavily on defining “core subject” as a standalone specialist course with standardized exams and credentials. Side B plausibly reframed core status as mandatory priority, repetition, and habit formation around permanent verification principles, which answered obsolescence and softened the resource objection. Side A’s embedded-assessment alternative was credible but did not fully overcome B’s point that optional or loosely embedded approaches are too easily skipped and do not build an automatic defensive reflex.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s most decisive rebuttal was the response to obsolescence: the curriculum need not teach current AI artifacts, but durable habits like source-checking, emotional manipulation awareness, and verification before action. That undercut Side A’s opening claim that rapid technological change makes core treatment misguided.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): B won by establishing that universal, life-or-death cognitive defenses deserve guaranteed priority over optional embedding that has empirically failed. PRO’s "permanent rules" and "the pause" framework successfully rebutted CON’s obsolescence and siloing objections, while CON never fully answered why embedding would succeed now after years of neglect or why behavioral reflexes do not warrant core status. B’s closing crystallized the clash cleanly; A’s closing, while competent, recycled the "lightweight" turn that B had already reframed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s second rebuttal flip that students indeed cannot evaluate fabrications without domain knowledge—"They can't. That is exactly why this works"—which turned CON’s pressure question into support for the standalone subject by framing it as the missing defensive reflex that applies knowledge students already possess.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully reframing the justification for core subject status from academic depth to universal necessity. Side A built a coherent structural case that core subjects require stable, rigorous content and that siloing critical thinking strips it of domain context. However, Side B effectively neutralized this by arguing that the embedded model has empirically failed for over a decade because it gets dropped under testing pressure, and by clarifying that the goal is not teaching fleeting tech tricks but drilling a permanent defensive reflex. When Side A pressed on teacher pipelines and argued that a lightweight habit does not merit core status, Side B directly answered the pipeline concern and flipped the metric in the closing: core status is awarded for what is unforgivable to graduate without, not for academic weight. Side A's alternative (embedding via standardized assessments) never squarely answered why it would succeed where prior embedding mandates failed, leaving Side B's necessity-and-coverage framing as the cleaner, unrefuted path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 into Closing. Faced with pressure on teacher recruitment and the claim that teaching a pause is too lightweight for a core subject, Side B answered directly (use existing PD, no new pipeline) and then decisively reframed the clash: core status guarantees universal repetition of a life-saving reflex, not academic rigor. The seatbelt analogy crystallized this weighing and materially damaged Side A's category-error argument, resolving the debate on necessity versus depth.
