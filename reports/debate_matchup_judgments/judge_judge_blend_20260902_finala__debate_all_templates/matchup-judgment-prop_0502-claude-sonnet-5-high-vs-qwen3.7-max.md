### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by exposing an unresolved feasibility-and-scope dilemma. If the treaty comprehensively demilitarizes sovereign, inhabited Arctic territory, A showed why ratification and compliance are implausible; if it governs only the central commons, it resembles the targeted agreements already available rather than replacing territorial governance with an Antarctic-style system. B established serious environmental and security risks, but never demonstrated how its treaty would secure participation or provide enforcement capabilities beyond existing monitoring and sanctions. A also grounded its alternative in functioning legal adjudication and incremental agreements, though its reliance on economics and deterrence was imperfect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange tracked B’s shift between protecting only the central basin and demanding comprehensive demilitarization. A argued that the narrow version conceded the viability of targeted commons agreements, while the broad version required politically implausible withdrawal of military infrastructure from populated sovereign coastlines. B’s proposed verification and sanctions did not fully resolve that dilemma.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON won by forcing and then exploiting PRO's inconsistency on what "similar to the Antarctic Treaty System" actually requires: either full demilitarization/claim freeze (politically non-starter for inhabited sovereign coasts, giving Russia free rein during failed talks) or a narrower commons regime already approximated by UNCLOS, CLCS process, targeted fishing moratoriums, deterrence, and existing sanctions/monitoring tools. PRO kept strong stakes rhetoric on climate and militarization but never resolved the feasibility dilemma or showed why incremental law fails where a comprehensive treaty could be signed and enforced. The status-quo path under existing rules handled the live comparisons more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 (A2.1-A2.2) plus R2/closing crystallization: PRO's "only commons" move abandons ATS similarity while their later demilitarization/enforcement answers either restate tools already live or demand an unworkable freeze of populated territories.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by collapsing PRO's case into an unresolved dilemma: either the proposed treaty genuinely mirrors the Antarctic Treaty System by freezing territorial claims and demilitarizing the region—which CON showed is a political non-starter for sovereign states with populated coastlines—or it shrinks to a narrow commons agreement, which CON demonstrated already exists incrementally through UNCLOS, CLCS adjudication, and targeted moratoriums. PRO never recovered from the concession that the treaty would not freeze coastal sovereignty, and their enforcement answer merely relabeled tools already operating under the status quo.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal and pressure-question follow-up exposing that PRO's narrowed treaty scope—protecting only the "commons" while respecting coastal administration—abandoned the Antarctic Treaty's defining feature of frozen territorial claims, leaving nothing "similar" to the Antarctic model for the resolution to latch onto.
