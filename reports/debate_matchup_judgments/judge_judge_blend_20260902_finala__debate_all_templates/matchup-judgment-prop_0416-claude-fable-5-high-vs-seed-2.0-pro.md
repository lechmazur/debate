### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive comparison clearer: scarce ad attention is currently occupied by clinic-chosen optimism, so requiring realistic age-based success and uncertainty makes the most important truth more visible before irreversible choices are made. Side A had a plausible concern that prominent disclosures can become oversimplified scary soundbites, but B repeatedly answered that the motion includes uncertainty, does not require one crude number, and that imperfect truthful disclosure is better than seller-controlled hope. B also exploited A's consultation-room fallback by emphasizing that the clinic profits from the sale and therefore cannot be the only corrective.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was turning A's claim that viewers remember only one thing from an ad: if only one message sticks, B argued it should be the age-dependent uncertainty rather than the sales pitch, which directly neutralized A's billboard-nuance objection.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A built the stronger case by consistently forcing the debate onto the shared concession that attention is scarce and the seller controls the initial message, then showing why truth must occupy that slot when harm is irreversible; B never neutralized the weighing or answered who corrects seller optimism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2's direct A1/A2 answers to B's pressure questions plus the closing crystallization around B4.1's "one thing remembered" concession.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built a clearer path to victory by establishing that current fertility ads create false security leading to irreversible biological harm, while CON's counter-harm (young women panicking into unnecessary early freezing) was effectively minimized as financially refundable versus biologically permanent. CON's strongest argument—that billboard prominence forces an oversimplified takeaway—was ultimately turned against them in the closing when PRO crystallized that if attention yields only one memory, the scarce slot should carry truth rather than a sales pitch. CON never adequately answered why the consultation room, controlled by the profit-seeking seller, should be the sole corrective to seller-created optimism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where they directly answered CON's pressure questions, provided plausible real-world examples of disclosure reducing elective procedure uptake, weighed irreversible biological harm against refundable financial regret, and exposed CON's failure to resolve the seller's conflict of interest in the consultation room.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by making the remedy itself the decisive issue. Side B had a strong fairness and anchoring case, but never fully answered the feasibility challenge about what realistic age-based rates can honestly be required in ads given lagging data, individual variation, and uncertainty. When pressed, Side B gave an unsupported numeric line rather than identifying a reliable basis, letting Side A frame the mandate as false precision rather than transparency. Side A also weighed the timing and consent comparison more clearly, though some of its chilling-effect claims were speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was pressing Side B to specify the required ad sentence, then using Side B's approximate "fewer than 1 in 5" answer to argue the policy forces outdated or misleading precision rather than useful disclosure.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing PRO into an untenable choice between vague redundant disclaimers and specific outdated numbers, while keeping the unrebutted timing asymmetry live. A's anchoring theory was consistently turned against their own remedy. B's implementation pressure and weighing were sharper and better sustained across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 and B4 on the exact mandated sentence and the self-refuting psychology argument, which exposed the gap between A's opening claim and the remedy that actually followed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON won by trapping PRO between two unattractive implementation options: either a vague disclaimer everyone already knows, or a specific age-based rate that CON showed amounts to government-mandated false precision sourced from outdated technology. CON's timing asymmetry—that incomplete ads are correctable downstream while lost fertile years are not—also survived mostly unrebutted, giving the stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first pressure question forced PRO to write the exact mandated sentence ("fewer than 1 in 5"). CON then rebutted that this figure could only come from obsolete slow-freeze data, turning PRO's own example into an argument that the mandate would stamp misleading pessimism onto modern vitrification ads.
