### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON (A) built a tighter, more sustained case: the scale/learning-curve argument against fragmented production was never fully neutralized, and CON's definitional pressure—that PRO kept defending allied/multilateral diversification rather than the motion's actual "countries...localize" language—landed and was never squarely resolved. PRO's answer (that allied coordination "is" the industrial policy called for) is plausible but doesn't fully explain why that isn't just a smaller-scale version of CON's own diversification alternative, undercutting PRO's distinctiveness. PRO's chokepoint and weaponization arguments (gallium/germanium, Russian gas analogy) were strong and genuinely pressured CON, but CON's stockpile/multilateral-guarantee answer offered a lower-cost way to address the same risk without conceding full national localization, and PRO never cleanly showed why that alternative fails. Execution also favored CON: its closing was fully delivered and crisply crystallized the clash, while PRO's rebuttal 2 and closing were both clipped for length, cutting off the final weighing sentences precisely where PRO was trying to close the sale on tail-risk framing. That is a real, penalized execution cost per the judging rules.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON's pressure question ("you are defending allied diversification, not national localization—will you concede this isn't the actual proposition?") and PRO's answer that allied coordination via subsidies "is" the industrial policy in question. PRO's answer was reasonable but never explained why CON's own diversification/stockpile alternative doesn't satisfy the same logic at lower cost, leaving CON's framing largely intact through the closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by forcing a live definitional burden the proposition requires individual countries to build domestic critical chains (redundant full-stack capacity with permanent scale loss), then showing PRO repeatedly defended a milder allied-specialization version instead. PRO had stronger risk-asymmetry and chokepoint material, but clips left those incomplete and the pressure unanswered; CON’s permanent-cost/climate-weighing crystallized cleaner after the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1 bait-and-switch: PRO proved need to break upstream chokepoints yet refused to defend the actual national localization vs allied diversification distinction.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): Side A built a much stronger comparative case by effectively weighing the catastrophic tail risk of supply chain weaponization (the Russian gas analogy) against the manageable costs of industrial policy. Side B relied too heavily on a pedantic semantic trap, insisting that "localize" meant every single country must achieve total autarky, which Side A easily parried by pointing out that allied coordination and friend-shoring are standard features of modern industrial policy. While Side A suffered minor clipping at the very end of two speeches, it did not obscure any substantive arguments, and their overall framing of the stakes was far more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where they dismantled Side B's "diversification" argument by showing that moving final assembly to other countries doesn't solve upstream Chinese chokepoints, and successfully defended their interpretation of localization against Side B's autarky strawman.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): PRO opened strongly with a vivid frame (insurance premium, hospital metaphor) and a concrete 2022 polysilicon example, but CON's Rebuttal 1 landed the decisive blow: PRO's own concession to limit localization to "the most critical inputs" [B1.7], later formalized as a "60% concentration" test [B4.1], let CON argue that PRO was defending a narrower, more selective policy than the sweeping "industrial policy to localize critical clean-tech supply chains" of the motion. PRO never squarely rebutted this framing (e.g., by pointing out the motion itself says "critical" chains, which would have blunted CON's attack), leaving that structural critique live and unanswered through the closing. CON also offered a sharper systemic counterfactual—that broad localization would have blocked the ~90% cost collapse that drove record clean-energy deployment—and PRO's closing repeated the existential-stakes framing without directly re-engaging this counterfactual or the scope-narrowing critique. CON's pressure questions were more incisive (exposing lobbying/incumbency risk and the definitional slippage of "critical"), and CON's answers to PRO's questions were direct and format-compliant. PRO's answers, while clear, didn't neutralize the central comparative weighing CON built across rounds. CON's closing was clipped for length, which is a real execution cost, but the visible content still crystallized the decisive clash effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point [A2.1]-[A2.2], turning PRO's "critical inputs only" concession into an admission that the motion as written is overbroad, was the single most damaging and unrefuted exchange of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON (A) built the cleaner path by converting PRO's own narrowing into a live concession that the motion is overbroad, then weighing permanent deployment drag and cumulative emissions against one-off shocks. PRO's insurance framing stayed vivid but never fully neutralized the cost-collapse counterfactual or the localization-vs-diversification distinction, leaving CON's comparative easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A5 forcing PRO's 60%-plus test to admit the motion overreaches while claiming surgical redundancy already is CON's position
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B wins by effectively distinguishing between localization and diversification, and by trapping Side A into conceding that broad localization is overbroad. Side B's counterfactual regarding cumulative emissions from a slower, more expensive transition serves as a decisive weighing mechanism that Side A fails to adequately overcome. Side A's use of brittle fake precision in Rebuttal 2—inventing exact percentages and timeframes for a limiting principle on the spot—is a notable grounding flaw. While Side B's closing was clipped, their substantive dominance in the earlier rounds secures the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantle Side A's invented limiting principle as a concession that the motion is overbroad, and effectively weigh the cumulative emissions of a slower transition against the one-off emissions of a temporary supply shock.
