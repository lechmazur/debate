### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the proposition as written easier to reject than accept. PRO opened with a sharp insider/outsider logic, but under pressure its protection design drifted from universal/employer-delinked to contribution locks, portal gateways, and a tax-funded floor plus earmarked top-up. CON kept showing that the floor for the 90% does not require the tenure trade, that individual top-up deposits do not fund that floor, and that PRO’s final conditionality is protection-first sequencing rather than an exchange. PRO’s strongest counter—need formal payers and locked implementation—never fully recovered that comparative point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal and closing on design drift: once PRO made the floor tax-funded and flexibility conditional on a working portable account, the live bargain collapsed into CON’s sequencing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON won by proving that PROs bargain collapsed into the negatives preferred sequencing. PRO opened with universal, state-backed, employer-delinked protection in exchange for flexible contracts, but across rounds reframed it into an employer-deposited, contribution-conditional scheme where flexibility only activates after the protection portal is built, funded, and verified. CON showed this is no longer the exchange the motion demands; it is protection first, flexibility later. CON also landed the decisive fiscal point that the universal floor for the informal ninety percent must be tax-funded either way, while the employer top-ups are earmarked individual savings that do not cross-subsidize that floor. Combined with credible skepticism about state capacity and the evidence that flexibility reforms have already outpaced protection, CON left PROs path looking like a promise that workers surrender tenure first and hope for a safety net later. PRO kept the debate competitive with a compelling insider-outsider diagnosis and clever mechanism design, but could not overcome the core tension: the affirmative ended up defending the negatives sequencing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CONs closing argument that PROs conditional lock converts the motion into protection first and flexibility later, and that employer-deposited individual accounts cannot fund the tax-based universal floor for the informal sector, decisively undermined the affirmative path to victory.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by systematically dismantling the mechanics of Side A's proposed bargain. Side B successfully forces Side A into two major concessions: first, that the "universal" protection relies on a tax-funded floor (meaning it does not actually require trading away tenure to fund), and second, that flexibility must be conditional on the protection portal being fully functional (meaning protection must come first, conceding Side B's sequencing). Side B's closing masterfully points out that earmarked contributory accounts do not fund a universal tax base, collapsing Side A's core justification for the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it exposes that Side A's "floor plus top-up" design means the universal floor is tax-funded regardless of flexibility, and that Side A's "lock" mechanism effectively concedes Side B's "protection first, flexibility later" sequencing.
