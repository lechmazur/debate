### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B narrowly won by keeping the comparative question alive: imperfect but enforceable standards create escrow, insurance, parentage certainty, counsel, accreditation pressure, and a paper trail, while prohibition risks pushing cross-border demand into less visible markets. Side A’s enforcement critique was sharp and rhetorically powerful, especially on coerced consent and inaccessible remedies, but it overstated that all paper protections serve only buyers and did not fully answer B’s point that bans remove even the limited protections available to surrogates and children.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was the attack that signed attestations and parentage paperwork cannot verify free consent across borders. Side B’s decisive answer was that enforceability need not read minds; it can impose structural preconditions and create claims, records, insurance, and market-access sanctions that are still better than a grey-market ban.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): A built the stronger case by consistently showing that PRO's paper-based tools protect buyers far more than the surrogate's actual consent, exposing the motion's enforcement gap as unfixable across borders while A's domestic alternative narrows the power differential where remedies exist.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly answering Q1/Q2 by showing the attestation fails to verify free consent and that domestic enforcement reaches the woman while cross-border cannot.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): CON successfully narrowed the debate to the motion’s keyword—“enforceable”—and demonstrated that the paper-trail safeguards PRO offers (parentage orders, escrow, attestations) primarily serve buyers and agencies while leaving the surrogate’s lived coercion invisible and unreachable across borders. PRO’s grey-market rejoinder lost force once CON specified an alternative of supervised domestic arrangements, and CON’s memorable line that “a coerced woman signs that attestation too” crystallized the decisive gap between documenting consent and verifying freedom. PRO’s clipped speeches and incomplete development of the “families excluded domestically” point left a lingering burden unanswered. After the full exchange, CON had the cleaner, more damaging route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 response to PRO’s answer on independent counsel and signed attestation [B4.4–5], arguing that a coerced woman signs the attestation too, exposing the paper trail as an agency defence rather than genuine protection for the surrogate, which PRO never fully recovered from.
