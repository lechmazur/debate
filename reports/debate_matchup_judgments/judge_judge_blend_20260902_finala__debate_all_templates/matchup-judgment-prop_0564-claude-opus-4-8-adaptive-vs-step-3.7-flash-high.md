### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by making the central comparison clearer: if a city can raise new broad-based tax revenue, spending it to replace fares has a real opportunity cost compared with using it for frequency, coverage, and reliability. B had a plausible equity case, but leaned on unsupported precise claims and never fully answered why universal free rides beat targeted fare relief plus service investment, especially when initial ridership gains are modest. A also pressed the funding mechanism more effectively and turned B's farebox share argument into a dilemma: if fares are small, the equity gain is limited; if they matter, the lost revenue matters too.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response to B's less-than-20-percent farebox point was decisive: break-even replacement does not create better service, and the same tax money could fund the night bus or missing route instead of making existing rides free.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by relentlessly pressing the opportunity cost of fare-free transit. Side B argued that replacing fares with taxes "leaves the budget intact" for service, but Side A cleanly flipped this by showing that "break-even" means zero new tax dollars go toward service expansion, proving the "free versus good" tradeoff was real.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4.1 response perfectly answered Side B's first pressure question by explaining that the "service loss" is the opportunity cost of the new tax revenue: "every improvement we forgo permanently by spending scarce new revenue to make an already-running bus free."
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A built the stronger case by consistently highlighting opportunity costs and zero net service gains from break-even replacement, directly answering pressure questions, and crystallizing the free-vs-good comparison without evasion or new dumps. Side B's responses were weaker on funding realism and political framing and were clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which opened with labeled A1/A2 answers to the exact questions and then weighed the forgone service against speculative coalitions.
