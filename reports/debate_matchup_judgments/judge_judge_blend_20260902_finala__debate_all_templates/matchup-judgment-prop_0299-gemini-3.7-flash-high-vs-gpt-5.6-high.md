### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO built a clearer and more resilient path to victory. Its core asymmetry—political/administrative risks are governable and correctable, while linguistic extinction is permanent—was introduced early, defended under pressure, and never squarely neutralized. PRO's pressure questions exposed a genuine gap in CON's case (what reliably serves poor, remote communities absent public funding), and CON's answer ("decentralized community trusts, diaspora networks, independent endowments") asserted alternatives without showing they would actually materialize for marginalized groups, effectively dodging the mechanism question. PRO also pre-empted and then directly answered CON's "state orthodoxy/custody" objection with specific safeguards (confidentiality exemptions, community consent, funding multiple competing dialects, ownership retained regardless of defunding), while CON's rebuttals mostly reasserted the same abstract claims (bureaucratic orthodoxy, coercive monopoly, cultural sovereignty) across rounds with limited new engagement with PRO's funding-versus-custody distinction. CON's attempt to leverage PRO's "no statute can prevent future legislatures" admission as a fatal concession was cleanly defused in PRO's closing (defunding does not retroactively destroy work already done or seize community-held material), leaving CON's strongest rhetorical moment answered rather than decisive. Both sides avoided fake citations and stayed within reasonable epistemic bounds, but PRO's incremental, responsive argumentation made the debate easier to decide in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's answer to CON's pressure question (A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2), distinguishing funding from custody and showing that defunding cannot retroactively erase completed preservation work or seize community-owned material, directly undercut CON's central "state coercion" narrative and was never adequately answered in CON's final speeches.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): B won by making the decisive comparison clearer: extinction is irreversible while political and design risks are governable and voluntary grants need not equal custody. A repeatedly hit political vulnerability and used B’s legislature concession well, but never answered how poor or remote communities get durable resources and often rehashed orthodoxy/coercion points without neutralizing underprovision. B’s replies on restricted ownership, viewpoint-neutral criteria, and optional public funding as an add-on route kept the live clash resolved for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4’s A1/A2 plus asymmetry: safeguards and community custody answer control fears, while CON still offers no reliable private mechanism before permanent loss.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it established a durable public-goods framework—markets and charity systematically underprovide preservation, and poor remote communities lack reliable alternatives—while showing that state-control risks are governable through arm's-length design. CON landed real blows on political vulnerability and gatekeeping, but never satisfactorily answered how marginalized communities would reliably access resources without public funding, repeatedly falling back on vague "trusts and networks." PRO's framing that governance risks can be corrected while extinction cannot, and that sovereignty without capacity is merely the freedom to lose one's heritage, provided the cleaner comparative lens through the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 (B2) reframed CON's position as "making poverty the preservation policy," exposed that private funders also impose rules without democratic accountability, and crystallized the decisive asymmetry: implementation risks can be amended, but extinction is irreversible.
