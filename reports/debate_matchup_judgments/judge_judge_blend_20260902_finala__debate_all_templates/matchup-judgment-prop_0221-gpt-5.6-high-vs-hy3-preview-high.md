### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) showed that Side B (PRO) never made a like-for-like comparison of total gains per total dollar, instead comparing selected tutoring recipients against diluted per-student class-size gains while ignoring aggregate reach. CON’s pressure questions and rebuttals exposed this mismatch and the marginal decay of tutoring at scale, while PRO’s reliance on unsupported precise figures and failure to answer the aggregation problem left its burden unmet.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal (A4.1–A4.3), which dismantled PRO’s “year versus week” hypothetical as question-begging, rejected its brittle fake precision, and demonstrated that selection effects cannot prove systemwide per-dollar superiority.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the like-for-like per-dollar clash. PRO's late quantitative claims in B4.1-B4.2 were unsupported brittle precision that CON directly neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal A4.1-A4.3 exposing PRO's selective 15% / 1.2 years vs 0.5-1 week comparison as a selection-biased, unsupported input that fails to prove system-wide gains per total dollar.

Side B consistently compared the concentrated gain of a single tutored student to the marginal gain of a single student in a smaller class, ignoring that the cost of the teacher is spread across the entire roster, generating aggregate gains for all students simultaneously. Side A also successfully highlighted that Side B's tutoring returns relied heavily on selection bias (targeting the highest-need students), which fails to prove the proposition's claim of system-wide superiority at scale.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantled Side B's hypothetical math by exposing the selection bias inherent in comparing a targeted 15% subgroup's gains against a universal intervention's aggregate reality, effectively neutralizing Side B's core per-dollar comparison.
