- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): Both sides argued competently with clear causal stories and memorable lines. PRO's decisive advantage lies in the "final moment gap": when credible intelligence flags a place and time but not a named carrier, CON's enumerated alternatives (high-visibility containment, behavioral stops, violence interrupters) do not mechanically remove a concealed weapon from a calm individual who has done nothing visibly suspicious. PRO pressed this directly in Q1, and CON's A1 response was plausible but incomplete—none of the listed tools physically searches a waistband in that scenario. CON's structural-incentive argument is plausible but not demonstrated; PRO correctly notes it would indict most discretionary policing powers. PRO's counter-trust point (gang control of streets also destroys trust; victims have civil liberties too) was never squarely neutralized by CON. PRO was clipped three times, a real execution cost that narrowed the margin, and CON's circular-causality framing (the gap is self-created by the policy) is sophisticated. On balance, PRO's simpler mechanistic story—targeted authority closes a genuine operational gap that conversation alone cannot close—survives the exchange more cleanly than CON's longer causal chain survives PRO's pressure.

PRO's reframe was not fully neutralized. The margin is clear but genuinely competitive because CON's core intelligence-trust argument retains real purchase even after PRO's responses.
