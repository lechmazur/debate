### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins a close but clear debate on the strength of three live routes that CON never fully neutralized. First, PRO's empirical counter that digital goods already cost as much as physical media undermined CON's central tradeoff claim—that licensing delivers lower prices—and CON's response pivoted to theory about what would happen under ownership rather than engaging the present reality. Second, PRO's concrete harm case (platform closures, delisting, silent edits, vanishing libraries) was answered only by CON's weak "revocations are rare" claim, which consumers would widely recognize as false. Third, PRO's answer to the server-dependency argument—ownership creates a legal lever to compel offline functionality or server-software release, with community servers as a real precedent—was plausible and at least partially responsive. CON's sharpest blow was the pre-approval versus post-enforcement distinction in the authentication exchange: physical law punishes theft after the fact while digital validation requires permission before each transfer, which PRO never squarely answered. That point meaningfully limits PRO's "all ownership needs validation" response. CON also pressed the frictionless-duplication economics argument credibly. But CON's case ultimately defends a system where consumers bear all the risk of platform failure while being told they have purchased something, and CON never articulated a satisfying answer to why that asymmetry is just. PRO's normative frame—sovereignty and dignity over purchased culture—stayed live throughout; CON's "functional access beats abstract rights" framing was rhetorically appealing but rested on the undefended premise that licensing actually delivers on its promises. PRO's closing clipping is a minor execution cost that did not materially change the outcome given the visible content was already strong. Both sides argued with discipline and without fake citations, producing a genuinely competitive exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that digital goods already cost as much as physical media without offering ownership rights was the most decisive exchange. CON's entire tradeoff narrative—accept licensing, gain lower prices and convenience—depends on consumers actually receiving something valuable in return for surrendered rights. PRO exposed that the price half of this bargain does not materialize in practice, leaving only platform control, and CON never effectively answered this empirical counter.
