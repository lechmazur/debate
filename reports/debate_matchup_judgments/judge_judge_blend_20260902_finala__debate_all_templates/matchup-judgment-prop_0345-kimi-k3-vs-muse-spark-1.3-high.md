- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly with plausible examples and no fabricated sourcing, but PRO (B) built the more resilient structure. PRO's core move—showing that every CON justification (hallway mentorship, junior proximity, "floor is alive") was itself a nameable, checkable mechanism—was repeated effectively and never fully neutralized; CON's own pressure-question answer (the payroll specialist/density point) actually supplied the sentence PRO asked for, which PRO then used as a decisive meta-concession in rebuttal 2 and closing. CON's administrative-paralysis worry was also undercut by PRO's job-family/2020-sorting/hospital analogies, which CON's rebuttal reasserted rather than refuted. On balance, PRO answered more of the decisive pressure points and left CON's case resting almost entirely on one unresolved dilemma, while PRO's own vulnerabilities were only partially exploited.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal 1 move of taking CON's own analyst/culture examples and rewriting them as one-sentence role-specific justifications ("this team develops juniors through proximity") was the pivotal exchange—CON never escaped the trap that every defense of broad mandates it offered was itself convertible into the very role-specific reason the motion demands.

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (CON)): A won by making the decisive clash easier to decide: every time B explained why presence matters, B produced the very role- or function-level mechanism A required (juniors, hallway help, floor density, crisis response). ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely strong cases, but CON (Side A) won the decisive clash. B's opening and R1 leaned on the orchestra/violinist analogy to show role-specific reasons are concrete and falsifiable, but conceded that such reasons "include the network" — and A seized on this concession relentlessly (R1, both PQ rounds, R2, and closing), showing that a reason whose content is "everyone else" collapses the role-specific/culture distinction the motion depends on. B's "choreography" rebuttal (specific schedules are narrowable/falsifiable even if network-based) was a reasonable counter but never fully closed the gap, and A's closing turn on the violinist ("downbeat comes from the conductor, listen left means the violas") landed as an unanswered final blow since A spoke last. A also pressed a second live line — that a determined employer can satisfy the rule with "three true sentences" padding a still-broad mandate — which B never squarely answered. B's strongest material (market attrition punishes the powerless first; concentrated labor markets blunt the "just take another job" answer) was good but A's rebuttal that testing happens "at the offer stage" via competing employers, while not airtight against concentration, was enough to keep that line contestable rather than lost. On balance A controlled the central definitional fight and closed more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The recurring orchestra/violinist exchange: B introduced it to show role-specific reasons are concrete, but conceded the reason "includes the network," and A used that concession across three subsequent speeches (R1, PQ, R2, closing) to argue the role-specific/culture distinction collapses — a thread B's "choreography" reframing never fully neutralized.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): A won by showing that role-specific justification need not atomize work: employers can identify the people, tasks, frequency, and coordination that make presence valuable. This preserved genuine network benefits while exposing why broad culture claims cannot distinguish necessary attendance from pretext. B's enforcement fork created real pressure, but overstated the choice between a useless memo and costly litigation, while its market remedy left existing and less-mobile workers bearing the harm first.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's orchestra response was decisive: timing, tuning, and real-time cues are concrete network-based reasons, demonstrating that coordination can be expressed role-specifically without collapsing into a vague culture assertion.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on enforceability and network interdependence. The fork (toothless memo vs. litigation) and the padded five-day mandate point forced A’s “modest transparency” case into either already-existing law or costly post-decision process that caregivers can least use. A’s specificity and pre-harm framing stayed strong, but B’s comparative weighing—claim to litigate vs. competing offer that actually pays childcare—cleared the choice more cleanly after full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s reply that once A concedes each role’s reason “includes the network,” role-by-role accounting collapses into culture/choreography by another name, capped by the padded-mandate case that still passes A’s test.
