CON's comparative framing sharpened the decision: both approaches require enforcement, but only PRO's surrenders the secondary market to a single corporate gatekeeper. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by showing that the resale premium can migrate off-platform through side payments followed by legitimate ticket transfers. A offered a compelling fairness case and exposed platform conflicts, but its zero-spread mechanism ultimately depended on restrictive transfer rules not clearly required by the proposition and did not explain how platforms would detect private payments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A’s barcode-control claim by distinguishing off-platform payment from the subsequent official transfer, then explained why bots could still capture capped tickets and monetize scarcity elsewhere.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won because it exposed persistent enforcement gaps in Side B's mechanism. While PRO's "zero spread" theory is elegant, CON showed that off-platform side payments combined with standard in-app transfers (or credential sharing) allow arbitrage to continue around platform caps, and that bots can sweep capped listings to flip them off-platform. PRO never squarely resolved how platforms would police payment coordination outside their systems without trapping ordinary fans in rigid non-transferable structures. CON's scarcity-driven black-market argument therefore remained live after the full exchange, while PRO's idealized platform control was shown to leak under real-world circumvention.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over whether a price cap eliminates arbitrage or merely displaces it. In A4.1, CON revealed that sellers can take off-platform payments (e.g., via cash apps) and then execute a nominal face-value transfer through the platform's own system, undermining PRO's barcode-control enforcement. PRO's response (B5.3) that off-platform buyers risk voided tickets failed to address this specific in-app transfer workaround, leaving a critical hole in PRO's route to victory.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): CON keeps a live route that physical scarcity survives a cap. After full exchange, its claim that sellers/bots will sweep $100 capped listings and flip them off-platform for $700 using the platform's own transfer/share-credential flow was not neutralized. That leaves fans paying the same market price but without fraud guarantees, and forces PRO into draconian non-transferable restrictions that harm ordinary fans with emergencies. PRO's strong conflict-of-interest and "spread hits zero" framing is undermined because the spread only hits zero on-platform, not off-platform.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 A1/A2 and bot-sweep arbitrage: zero on-platform spread does not kill incentive, it displaces it off-platform, requiring trapping fans in non-transferable systems - directly undercuts PRO's central mechanism.
