### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by systematically turning Side B's own examples and demonstrating a fatal flaw in the automaticity mechanism. Side A convincingly proved that conditionality is what makes the threat of licensing effective in the status quo, and that applying an automatic threshold to small-population drugs (like Side B's Trikafta example) would break the mathematical viability of their development.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 decisively resolved the debate's core clashes by proving that a strict threshold destroys the R&D math for rare diseases, and that automaticity cannot shield weak states from trade retaliation because retaliation responds to the seizure itself.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully turned PRO's empirical examples by showing they occurred under discretionary licensing, and demonstrated that automaticity eliminates the conditional threat that drives voluntary deals. CON's closing crystallized an asymmetry: every cited access benefit belongs to discretion, while automaticity introduces unique costs without proving a distinct gain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 showing that all of PRO's cited access victories were earned under discretionary licensing, and that automaticity replaces a conditional bargaining threat with an unconditional seizure that destroys the incentive for voluntary deals, launch, and technology transfer.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by decisively framing the clash around conditionality versus automaticity. While PRO effectively argued that discretionary licensing favors diplomatically powerful states, CON demonstrated that removing the conditional threat destroys the incentive for voluntary negotiation, technology transfer, and market entry. CON successfully turned PRO's flagship examples (Brazil, Thailand, MPP) by showing they succeeded precisely because of discretionary leverage, not automatic triggers. PRO also walked into CON's OECD/Trikafta dilemma, conceding that wealthy markets would be capped, which undermined their innovation safety net. CON's mechanistic explanation of how automatic triggers lead to launch boycotts and collapsed development math for rare diseases was more rigorous and ultimately unresolved by PRO. PRO's clipped second rebuttal further hampered their ability to fully answer the conditionality charge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 dismantling PRO's alleged contradiction and establishing the conditionality mechanism. CON showed that investment flight (ex ante) and launch avoidance (ex post) are rational responses to the same automatic rule, and that discretion works precisely because it preserves a bargaining chip that automaticity destroys. This neutralized PRO's core empirical examples and mechanism.
