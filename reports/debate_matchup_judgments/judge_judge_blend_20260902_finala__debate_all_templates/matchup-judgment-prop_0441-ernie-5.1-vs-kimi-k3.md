### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the comparison tied to the motion: jail reduction and violent-crime rates under reform versus cash bail. Side A raised legitimate concerns about bias, confounding, and underfunded supervision, but never grounded those concerns in a case where reform increased violent crime and repeatedly converted the motion into a demand for universal reliability or certainty. Side B's causal evidence was not conclusive, but its jurisdictional examples, comparative mechanisms, and direct answers substantially outweighed Side A's largely hypothetical objections. Side A's clipped closing was an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions immediately, distinguished predictive validation from perfect fairness, and explained that the safety standard is comparative rather than a guarantee of zero offending. It then turned Side A's supervision critique by noting that cash bail can free dangerous wealthy defendants under equally weak conditions, while Side A still supplied no violent-crime counterexample.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won by maintaining a relentless focus on the resolution's specific metrics (jail populations and violent crime rates) and backing their claims with concrete examples.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's first rebuttal cleverly flipped CON's post hoc argument, noting that if crime was already falling nationally, New Jersey served as a perfect control group to prove mass release didn't bend the trend line upward.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by strictly anchoring the debate to the proposition's specific metrics: overall jail populations and violent crime rates. Side A provided concrete, plausible examples (New Jersey, D.C., Cook County) demonstrating these exact outcomes. Side B's critiques regarding algorithmic racial bias and underfunded supervision, while conceptually sound, failed to directly refute the motion's specific claims about aggregate violent crime. Side A's pressure question exposing Side B's inability to name a single jurisdiction where violent crime rates actually rose after reform was decisive. Furthermore, Side A effectively neutralized the bias argument by contrasting auditable algorithmic disparity with the unauditable, wealth-based disparity of cash bail. Side B's clipped closing served as an additional execution cost in an already losing position.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, where they dismantled Side B's perceived contradiction by clarifying that cash bail's disparity is the unauditable mechanism itself, and sharply pointed out that Side B's bias argument critiques the distribution of detentions rather than the motion's core metrics of overall capacity and violent crime rates.
