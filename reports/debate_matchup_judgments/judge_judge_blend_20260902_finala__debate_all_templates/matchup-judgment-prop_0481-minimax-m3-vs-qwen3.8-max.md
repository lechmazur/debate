### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): B won by keeping the motion's "nearly every" burden live and showing CON's best cases still collapse into governance, correction, and off-chain truth problems that favor administrable databases. A had real force on no-single-operator settings and portable verification, but its opening was clipped, and it never fully neutralized B's correction/administrability pressure or the claim that permissioned ledgers become costly wrappers once parties can govern. B's comparative frame stayed clearer across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 on who lawfully corrects false/private records, then B4 turning A's multi-party answer into "if governance exists DB wins cheaper; if not, freeze is not coordination."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO successfully framed the core dilemma that non-currency coordination requires administrability—correction, revocation, and legal accountability—while blockchains either recreate the centralized governance they claim to avoid or freeze errors immutably. CON identified plausible structural advantages in cross-border and multi-party contexts, but PRO's "expensive notary" and "wrapper vs. stalemate" frames consistently showed that in CON's own examples the blockchain was anchoring claims rather than making them true, and that federated databases with contractual governance could capture the coordination benefits without the immutability costs. CON's opening being clipped added an execution cost, and CON never fully neutralized the governance dilemma that PRO sharpened across rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's answer to CON's pressure questions in Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.2), articulating that a joint operating agreement need not solve every dispute before code, and that if governance is possible a shared database coordinates more cheaply whereas if governance is impossible a blockchain merely freezes disagreement rather than coordinating.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO (A) won the sweep debate. A framed non-currency coordination as administrability - correctable, revocable, private, legally accountable - and showed land, health, supply-chain, voting, identity all fail that test on a blockchain, which cannot make claims true and freezes errors. CON's strongest niches - cross-org audit, portable credentials, provenance - were answered: federated append-only logs with multi-sig and contracts give tamper-evidence without consensus overhead, and blockchain's claimed survivability still depends on verifiers, law, revocation and off-chain truth, recreating governance. A's answers to Q1 circularity and Q2 unique work questions were more direct, keeping the "nearly every" burden met.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 + Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: federated DB provides tamper-evidence cheaper, and if parties can't agree minimal governance blockchain freezes disagreement rather than coordinates; plus portable/provenance examples still need governance to correct false entries.
