### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the counterfactual harder and more concrete. Rather than comparing the euro with painless monetary sovereignty, B argued from Europe’s repeated experience with floating currencies and unstable pegs, then showed that non-euro states also suffered devaluation, rescues, and austerity. A persuasively established that the euro intensified asymmetric shocks and political resentment, but never sufficiently demonstrated that a no-euro EU would avoid comparable crises while retaining today’s joint borrowing, banking backstops, cohesion, and geopolitical leverage. B also offered stronger observable tests—continued membership, accession, public support, and expanded common institutions—while A too readily dismissed these as sunk-cost effects.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s “crisis architecture” argument was decisive: it accepted the euro’s costs but insisted they must be netted against a realistic alternative containing currency and banking crises without the ECB, ESM, or established joint-debt capacity. A’s claim that political willingness alone could recreate those tools did not overcome B’s argument that the common currency generated the mutual stake necessary to build them.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's counterfactual. While Side A made a brilliant conceptual argument about 'unadjusted accounting' and the euro being a 'fragility machine,' Side B grounded the debate in historical reality (Europe's past failures with floating rates and pegs) and successfully proved that the crisis-management tools Side A dismissed (like NGEU) could not exist without the mutual stake created by a shared currency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 explanation of why 27 floating currencies could not issue joint debt due to exchange-rate risk directly neutralized Side A's claim that NGEU just required 'political willingness.'
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by forcing the clash onto a realistic counterfactual and then winning it: Europe’s repeated return from float to snake/EMS/ERM undercut B’s calm adjustable-rate baseline, while non-euro 2008 cases (Hungary/Iceland/Latvia) and Latvia’s later euro entry blunted B’s adjustment mechanism. A’s “inspect vs. imagine” netting—same shocks, fewer tools without NGEU/ESM/euro sanctions leverage, plus observed support and zero exits—outweighed B’s sharp but incomplete “unadjusted accounting” frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1–2 baseline attack: treating Europe’s actual peg-after-float pattern plus 2008 non-euro rescues as the honest alternative, which left B defending idealized sovereignty tools that history and the crisis tests had already diluted.
