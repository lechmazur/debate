  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A2: fixed sessions beat risk-triggered checks because only the former does not depend on the engagement-optimized system correctly flagging its own failure.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live, bias-proof rationale: a fixed checkpoint is the only mechanism that doesn't rely on the chatbot to flag its own failure, which directly answers CON's risk-triggered alternative and marketing-regulation pitch. CON's strongest routes – arbitrariness of the number and the scarcity/bottleneck harm – were substantially blunted when PRO clarified in [B2.3]/[B4.1] that handoff is a brief triage review, not termination, allowing return to the tool. The arbitrariness objection lingers, but PRO made the choice easier to decide as cheap, bounded human look vs indefinite unsupervised care.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3 – PRO's A2 that a risk-triggered rule still asks an engagement-optimized system to detect its own inadequacy, making a fixed count uniquely immune to that incentive failure, which CON's drug-surveillance analogy never fully resolved.
