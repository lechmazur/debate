### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won a close, substantive clash by preserving the distinction between suspicious algorithmic parallelism and horizontal agreement. Side A compellingly showed how a vendor could facilitate coordination, but repeatedly treated nonpublic pooling, recommendations, and adherence as inherent features of the entire category. Side B offered a more discriminating rule: infer conspiracies from mutual reliance, expected adherence, monitoring, or discipline without automatically treating shared-tool use as direct coordination. B’s closing overstated A’s position on defection, since A allowed habitual rejection to rebut coordination, but the underlying overbreadth objection remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered A’s pressure questions by identifying the missing proof as reciprocal reliance and an adherence mechanism, while explaining that this could be established circumstantially rather than through a written promise. A turned that into the decisive weighing point — if posting and not posting both trigger scrutiny, independence becomes impossible and scrutiny becomes punishment for tool use, proving overbreadth.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / Closing using B's A1 admission that isolated defection is mere "leakage" to show PRO's standard leaves no path to prove independent pricing.

Through sharp pressure questions, CON forced PRO to concede that a landlord who actively rejects the algorithm's pricing still faces "direct coordination" scrutiny. CON brilliantly weaponized this in the closing to show that PRO's rule collapses the distinction between independent and concerted action, making the software subscription itself the offense rather than requiring proof of a horizontal agreement. While PRO offered highly intuitive and rhetorically polished arguments about "outsourcing the cartel," CON's tighter grasp of the antitrust mechanics and decisive rebuttal of PRO's overbreadth secured the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's closing argument highlighting PRO's concession that "isolated defection" does not clear a landlord of direct-coordination scrutiny, proving that PRO's standard targets software use rather than actual price coordination.
