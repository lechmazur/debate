### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): CON (Side B) wins a competitive but clear debate on the strength of three sustained arguments that PRO never fully neutralized. First, CON weaponized PRO's own numbers: the 15% reduction at Kiyomizu-dera and the 20% Prague shift leave 80-85% of demand exactly where it was, and PRO's "proof of concept that compounds over time" answer was unsubstantiated hand-waving rather than a mechanism. Second, CON's housing-policy unbundling—resident displacement in Venice accelerated via short-term rental platforms, not tourism clustering per se—stripped one of PRO's three core pillars and PRO never recovered it. Third, CON's unanswered infrastructure question from the pressure round (what stops secondary neighborhoods from developing identical rent spikes once tourist flows arrive?) exposed a real mechanism gap: PRO's answer ("dilution, not relocation") named a desired outcome rather than a causal process. PRO's "bandage vs. cure" framing was rhetorically vivid and kept them competitive, and their point that managed concentration still leaves icons under pressure (admitted by CON) was live throughout. But CON correctly replied that "facing pressure" is not the same as "failing," and that concentrated footfall funds the conservation revenue dispersal would dilute. CON's clipped closing is a real execution cost in the final round but does not undo the substantive leads built in Rebuttals 1 and 2. PRO's somewhat suspicious numerical precision (exact percentages presented without sourcing) was also a minor credibility cost throughout. On balance, CON's combination of evidence attacks, mechanism critique, and economic counter-argument outweighs PRO's symptom/cause framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 attack on the 15% figure—"fifteen percent is not a solution; it leaves eighty-five percent of demand concentrated exactly where it was"—was the most decisive exchange. It turned PRO's flagship evidence into a liability, forced PRO onto the weak "proof of concept / trajectory" defense in Rebuttal 2, and was reinforced by CON's Q2 pressure question, which PRO never answered with a credible compounding mechanism. This sequence materially damaged PRO's central causal story without PRO offering a substantive repair.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON)): Side B wins by effectively turning Side A's own statistics against them, arguing that a 15-20% shift leaves the vast majority of tourists exactly where they were, proving that core management remains the necessary solution. Side A also loses track of the debate's details, claiming Side B never addressed cumulative wear on stonework despite B explicitly explaining how capacity caps solve this, and mistakenly attributing their own Barcelona example to Side B in the pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which directly dismantles Side A's claim that site management only fixes queues by pointing out that capacity caps directly reduce physical footfall on historic stonework, while arguing that a dispersed economy starves conservation funds.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by consistently pressuring PRO's core mechanism and evidence. CON successfully framed PRO's 15-20% dispersal figures as marginal hedges rather than solutions, exposed the infrastructure and relocation risks of spreading demand, and cleanly separated resident displacement from tourism concentration by pointing to housing and short-term rental policy. PRO's "dilution" and "trajectory" arguments were rhetorically smooth but lacked concrete scaling mechanisms, leaving CON's managed-concentration alternative as the more empirically grounded and decisive path. Both sides followed format rules well, but CON's rebuttals did more damage to the opponent's live route to victory and crystallized the clash more sharply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 sharply answered PRO's pressure by distinguishing tourism concentration from housing policy failures, turning PRO's "dilution" claim into an unproven hope, and demonstrating how targeted caps directly reduce physical wear while funding conservation at the necessary scale.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO built and sustained three durable advantages across the exchange. First, the "CON's tools are the proposition" argument—that timed entry, transit upgrades, and seasonal pricing all redirect visitors toward available capacity—was introduced in Rebuttal 1 and never cleanly escaped by CON. CON's best counter, the temporal-versus-geographic distinction, arrived late (Rebuttal 2) and was only partially convincing: timed-entry slots do create off-peak windows that meaningfully reduce peak density, which is at least adjacent to the motion. But CON failed to press this distinction systematically through the closing. Second, PRO's "cities exist for citizens" framing in A4.4 was the most decisive single move in the debate; CON never directly addressed it, leaving the stakeholder hierarchy in PRO's favor. Third, CON's own concession in B4.4—that Airbnb displacement persists whether tourists are concentrated or spread—effectively neutralized CON's claim that concentration better serves residents, without PRO even needing to press it. CON relied on vague "city data" for dispersal-pilot failures that PRO correctly flagged; PRO's own examples (Venice population decline, Dubrovnik walls, Gion's deliberate promotion) were more grounded even if imprecise. PRO's two word-limit clips are execution costs but did not eliminate core arguments before the clips occurred. CON's closing was repetitive and did not sharpen the live clash. The margin is real but competitive because CON's temporal-versus-geographic distinction is a genuine point that partially limits PRO's definitional absorption move.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 argument that CON's preferred tools—timed entry, transit upgrades, capacity investment—are themselves mechanisms of demand redistribution and therefore constitute the proposition rather than rebut it. This framing put CON on the defensive for the rest of the debate and was only partially answered by the temporal-versus-geographic distinction introduced belatedly in CON's Rebuttal 2.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing the debate around the well-being of residents rather than tourists, and by pointing out that Side B's alternative of 'better management' naturally relies on the redistribution (especially temporal/seasonal) that the prompt advocates. Side B fought back well on the geographic front, but ultimately failed to answer the seasonal aspect of the prompt and lost the stakeholder weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 provided the decisive weighing: 'Cities exist for their citizens. Tourism is a visitor to that city,' which structurally neutralized Side B's reliance on falling visitor satisfaction scores.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully framing CON's "manage concentration" alternative as functionally identical to the proposition's call to spread demand across seasons and neighborhoods. PRO's pressure questions exposed CON's reliance on unsourced "city data" and pilot claims, which CON never adequately grounded per the prompt's evidence rules. While CON made a valid distinction between temporal management at a single site and geographic dispersion, PRO effectively noted that the motion explicitly covers both seasons and neighborhoods, and that CON's own tools (timed entry, transit routing, overflow capacity) inherently redirect demand. PRO's stakeholder weighing ("cities exist for their citizens") and "managed failure" frame crystallized the clash more persuasively. CON's case grew repetitive and leaned on brittle empirical assertions that PRO successfully neutralized, leaving PRO with the cleaner, more adaptive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 handling of the pressure questions, particularly A2, which flipped CON's tourist-vs-resident weighing by arguing that seasonal or timed shifts merely inconvenience visitors while concentration permanently displaces residents. Combined with the strategic folding of CON's management tools into the proposition's framework, this exchange decisively narrowed the dispute and broke CON's core comparative advantage.
