### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that the motion’s long-term-cascade rationale does not justify banning every kinetic test, especially very low-altitude intercepts whose debris rapidly decays. Side A plausibly argued that even temporary clouds create collision risk, but never established that this residual risk is large enough to produce the long-term cascade named in the proposition or outweigh a tailored altitude or debris-lifetime rule. B’s factual overstatements and clipped closing narrowed the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively distinguished transient conjunction risk from a persistent debris cascade and argued that A had shifted from defending the motion’s long-term harm to defending against any temporary collision risk.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing that the motion's stated harm—long-term orbital debris cascades—does not require banning all kinetic tests. CON demonstrated that low-altitude intercepts decay within weeks and cannot breed generational cascades, and that a blanket ban eliminates the incentive for compliant states to test responsibly, leaving only violators who have no reason to shoot low. PRO was forced to retreat from the motion's justification and redefine the harm as transient collision risk, which CON exposed as a standard that would also indict ordinary launches. CON's incentive argument and its turn of the arson analogy remained materially unanswered, giving it the cleaner live path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn of PRO's arson analogy into a "stove vs. arson" distinction, paired with the incentive argument that a blanket ban deletes the harmless low-altitude testing option and leaves only violators who will test recklessly anyway. This combination undermined PRO's core claim that "all" was necessary and exposed the blanket ban as counterproductive.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON wins the central tailoring clash. The motion justifies a ban to prevent long-term cascades, but CON shows low tests in 2008 and 2019 decayed in weeks/months and cannot breed fragments, while China 2007 and Russia 2021 did. PRO never names lasting harm from the clean tests and shifts to "months of dodging," which CON frames as abandoning the motion and indicting all reentries. CON's incentive argument – a blanket ban deletes the clean compliant path and gives a violator no reason to shoot low, while an altitude/lifetime ceiling preserves it – survives PRO's replies, and PRO's self-grading worry contradicts its own verifiability claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 perverse-incentive and verification contradiction: if radars can catch a test they can watch its cloud vanish, so a ceiling gives a clean provable path; an ALL ban removes that path so the only tester left is a law-breaker who won't shoot low – plus the swap from "long-term cascade" to "collision lottery."
