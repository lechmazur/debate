### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by exploiting the proposition’s categorical burden. A persuasively established irreversibility but repeatedly asserted that all apparent certainty is equivalent, without identifying a plausible error mechanism in B’s narrow caught-at-the-scene, multiply corroborated cases. B also answered A’s proportionality question cleanly and showed that life without parole creates its own punishment ceiling. B’s claims about abolition inevitably producing release were overstated, but A’s martyrdom claims were similarly absolute and less decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal distinguished a jury’s confidence from evidence configurations where the familiar causes of wrongful conviction are absent. A kept repeating that every wrongful execution once seemed certain, but never squarely answered B’s concrete narrow category, leaving B a live exception sufficient to defeat universal abolition.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B kept a live proportionality route that A never neutralized — LWOP flattens 1 vs 77 murders — while materially damaging A's central irreversibility route. A argued fallibility proves abolition; B distinguished verdict-label certainty from mechanism-absence certainty (mistaken ID, coerced confession, informant) and turned the trust argument: threshold needs trust only that no live doubt exists case-by-case, abolition needs permanent trust that LWOP and no hostage-trade will ever erode, with Norway/Breivik as concrete practice. B answered pressure honestly (no zero-error category) and held advantage on martyrdom/hostage clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2 trust inversion and failure-mode filter — admitting no zero-error record but arguing wrongful convictions share mechanisms absent in filmed, on-scene, boasting cases, and flipping A's "can't trust state to kill" into "abolition demands permanent future trust" — which undercut A's absolute irreversibility claim.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger, more tailored case by narrowing the death penalty to cases of incontrovertible guilt (where standard error mechanisms like mistaken identity do not exist) and highlighting the tangible dangers of keeping mass murderers alive (hostage trading, prison radicalization, and the flattening of proportionality). Side B relied on generic irreversibility arguments and failed to effectively counter Side A's mechanism-based defense or the proportionality "ceiling" argument. Side A's rebuttals were sharper, more original, and decisively won the key clashes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the innocence argument by distinguishing between subjective jury certainty and the objective absence of error mechanisms (e.g., a gunman caught on camera, DNA-matched, and confessing), a distinction Side B repeatedly ignored in favor of repeating that "every wrongful execution was considered certain at the time."
