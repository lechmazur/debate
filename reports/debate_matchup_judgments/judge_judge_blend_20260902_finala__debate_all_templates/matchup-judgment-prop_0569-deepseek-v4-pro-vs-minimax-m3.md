### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a stronger cumulative case across the debate. The "deaf machine" framing from Side B was compelling and generated its most dangerous pressure question (Q1: if the bot can't detect buried despair, how does risk-based escalation ever fire for quiet deterioration?), but Side A answered it with genuine substance—multi-signal longitudinal trajectory monitoring is richer than keyword matching—rather than deflecting. More decisively, Side A's queue argument was never adequately answered: a universal session-N mandate floods scarce clinicians with low-acuity cases regardless of need, worsening access for genuinely high-risk patients. Side B's response was essentially "fix the capacity problem," which Side A correctly identified as hopeful theory against a documented structural reality. Side A also landed the sharpest logical turn in the debate: the fixed number does not cure the bot's deafness, it merely bounds its duration. Every session before N is still in the blind window; the rule tolerates the deafness rather than ending it. This directly reversed Side B's own "deaf machine" framing. Side B never squarely answered Q2 about the arbitrariness of N, offering only a circular regulatory-ceiling justification. Side A's closing crystallized the decisive comparison cleanly—"one triages on signal, the other floods on schedule"—while Side B's closing still relied primarily on the blind-window concern without addressing the queue damage. Both sides had competent openings and the "deaf machine" concern is real, keeping this competitive, but Side A won on the weightier arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's "bounds the duration of deafness" turn in Rebuttal 2 and Closing: by pointing out that every session before the handoff remains invisible to the machine, Side A directly reversed Side B's own framing—the fixed number does not give the bot hearing, it merely caps how long no one hears. Combined with the queue-flooding argument (universal mandate sends low-acuity users to the same scarce clinicians, worsening access for the genuinely at-risk), this was the decisive exchange because Side B never provided a substantive answer to either point.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side B built the stronger path: repeatedly showed that fixed handoffs manufacture queues and disengagement for low-risk users while still leaving a blind window before N, directly answering pressure questions and crystallizing the risk-based vs calendar tradeoff. Side A never neutralized the queue harm or explained why a rigid count outperforms calibrated triggers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.7 and B4.3 on disengagement costs and queue flooding, which directly damaged A's "review not termination" claim.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully turning Side B’s core premise and offering a cleaner comparative framework. While B consistently argued that chatbots are “deaf” to silent deterioration and require a fixed human checkpoint, A effectively demonstrated that a fixed session cap merely tolerates that deafness for N sessions while flooding scarce clinical queues with low-risk users. A’s risk-based triage model directly answered B’s pressure about wait times by distinguishing targeted escalation from universal mandates, and A’s closing crystallized the tradeoff memorably. B’s safety case was principled but struggled to resolve the practical bottlenecks and the pre-N blind spot, leaving A with the stronger, more complete path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over clinician queues and triage. B pressed A on how risk-based escalation avoids the same wait times A criticized, but A cleanly distinguished a triaged queue (reserved for flagged high-risk cases) from a flooded queue (created by mandating every low-acuity user to see a human at session N). This comparison decisively shifted the practical weighing to CON.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): PRO built a more durable path to victory around a single well-sustained structural argument: vendor engagement incentives will always corrupt any escalation threshold the vendor controls, and only an external, calendar-bound floor is immune to that manipulation. The "who controls the gate" framing held across all five rounds, and the closing line—"independent audits inspect a corpse, not a living patient"—was the debate's most decisive rhetorical and argumentative moment. CON's best counter—"the session counter doesn't notice crisis, it just counts"—is genuinely strong and was well-executed, but PRO answered it credibly (the human after the handoff provides individualized judgment; the AI's role is bounded support, not differential diagnosis). CON's risk-based alternative suffered from a structural vulnerability PRO exploited cleanly: in CON's own framework, the vendor's algorithm sets the clinical thresholds, making "external auditing" a post-hoc check rather than a real-time gate. CON landed a useful trap by exposing that PRO's "handoff" softened into a documented referral while chatbot use continues, but PRO's response—that a licensed professional with professional duty formally touching the record is categorically different from no referral—held well enough. Both openings and first rebuttals were clipped, imposing roughly symmetric execution costs. PRO's pressure questions were sharper and more revealing, forcing CON to articulate exactly who performs the capacity assessment—an answer (tiered validated screening) that is reasonable but still leaves the threshold-setting problem with the vendor. CON's questions were competent but somewhat redundant with existing clash. Overall PRO argued the proposition as written with greater consistency, better addressed its own concessions, and closed more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 response to CON's Q2 pressure question: the observation that "clinical markers are only as good as the assessor" and that in CON's framework the vendor's algorithm—with a financial interest in continued engagement—is that assessor. This flipped CON's strongest technical claim (risk-based escalation is more precise than a session counter) into an argument for PRO's position, and set up the closing "who controls the gate" frame that CON never fully neutralized.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built the stronger case by showing the calendar trigger supplies the only external floor independent of vendor incentives; CON's risk-based system never resolved the assessment conflict or external-enforcement gap raised in pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 directly answering B's pressure Qs and crystallizing the gate-control clash in closing.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully framing the decisive clash around vendor incentives versus external oversight. CON's alternative (risk-based escalation with independent audits) was clinically nuanced but left a critical vulnerability: the thresholds and data feeding those audits remain vendor-controlled, optimized for engagement, and only reviewed post-harm. PRO convincingly argued that a fixed-session calendar trigger is the only vendor-proof mechanism, reframing it as a documented review floor rather than a care-terminating guillotine, which sufficiently blunted CON's access and disruption offense. PRO's closing crystallized this tradeoff with sharp, substantive rhetoric ("safety by permission vs. safety by law," "it depends on Tuesday"), making the comparative path to victory clear. CON executed well but never fully resolved the incentive conflict at the heart of PRO's case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing on vendor incentives. When CON leaned on "independent audits" and "mandated risk protocols," PRO sharply noted that vendors still set the underlying thresholds and control the engagement metrics, meaning audits "inspect a corpse, not a living patient." This turned CON's alternative into a structural liability and cemented the calendar trigger as the only mechanism that fires before vendor incentives get a vote, decisively shifting the weighing.
