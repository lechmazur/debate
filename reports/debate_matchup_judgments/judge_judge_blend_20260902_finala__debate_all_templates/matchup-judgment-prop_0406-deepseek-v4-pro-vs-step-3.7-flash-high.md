### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the stronger cumulative case across the exchange. Its opening constructed a concrete, causally tight harm chain—fines pile debt, towing destroys the last roof, displacement blocks employment and stability—that CON never fully neutralized. PRO's most durable technical move was consistently distinguishing sleeping-specific penalties from the full body of parking, environmental, and fire-code enforcement that the motion leaves intact; CON kept restating a "blanket ban" framing that PRO correctly and repeatedly deflated. The most damaging moment came when CON's own Rebuttal 2 [A4.1] acknowledged that "existing policies exempt people actively experiencing homelessness from towing when no shelter exists"—an admission PRO correctly identified in its closing as confirming the motion's principle and cracking CON's stated rationale for opposition. CON's strongest live argument—no verification mechanism means anyone can claim the exemption—was addressed by PRO with a plausible self-selection mechanism (vehicle living is so punishing that people with genuine alternatives rarely choose it) combined with manageable administrative tools analogous to disability permits. That answer was imperfect but adequate. CON's late wheelchair-ramp example was vivid but arrived in the closing without earlier development, and PRO had already explained that standard traffic ordinances still cover that scenario. Format costs hurt PRO (three clipped speeches vs. CON's one), but the arguments PRO did complete were sharper and better adapted to the motion's qualifying language ("no safe alternative"), making the comparative case cleaner overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1] cleanly parsed that the motion bans sleeping-specific penalties while general parking, fire, and environmental codes remain fully operative—then PRO's closing exploited CON's own concession in [A4.1] that targeted enforcement already avoids harm when no shelter exists. This one-two combination directly undermined CON's central enforcement-gap claim and was never adequately answered.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by cleanly differentiating between punishing the act of sleeping and enforcing standard parking/safety codes. Side B's entire case relies on the assumption that the motion bans moving vehicles blocking fire lanes or wheelchair ramps, which Side A successfully neutralizes. Side B also makes a fatal concession in Turn 8 by admitting that targeted enforcement already avoids towing when no shelter exists, effectively agreeing with the proposition's core principle.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Turn 7 (A4.1) distinction that communities keep every existing parking ordinance and simply cannot penalize the status of being unhoused, directly neutralizing Side B's chaos impacts.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by successfully narrowing the motion to a prohibition on status-based punishment while preserving neutral parking, health, and safety enforcement. CON overreached by characterizing the motion as a blanket ban, repeatedly conflating vehicle dwelling with tent encampments, and failing to show why standard traffic laws could not address the spillovers it predicted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s second rebuttal answering CON’s pressure questions, distinguishing penalties for sleeping status from neutral parking violations and showing that safe-lot compliance is maintained by loss-of-spot rather than fines or towing. This directly neutralized CON’s central claim that the motion would make safe parking ungovernable and spill over into residential neighborhoods.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO wins primarily because it landed and sustained the "cruel contradiction" that CON never adequately resolved: CON explicitly admits shelter beds are currently insufficient to house all vehicle dwellers, yet insists that fining and towing those same people is a "compassionate lever" toward shelter. The lever only works if there is somewhere to push people, and CON's own concession destroys that premise. PRO's second core line—that towing and fining merely displace people and their associated sanitation problems to the next neighborhood rather than solving them—was also not squarely neutralized; CON's answer (displacement breaks up clustering) conceded the displacement while ignoring that the underlying problem simply moves. PRO's framing of the motion as "punishment for a systemic housing failure" was consistent and progressive across rounds. CON's strongest arguments—the unworkable exemption standard and the sanitation risk—had genuine force, but PRO reasonably answered both: the standard is already used in shelter prioritization and outreach, so the enforcement gap is about moment-of-contact procedure rather than structural incoherence; and the sanitation argument cuts against CON since enforcement without accompanying services simply relocates waste. CON's repeated claim that enforcement "creates the encounter" that connects people to services is circular when its own pressure-question answer admitted shelters are too full to absorb them. CON also suffered minor execution costs from a clipped opening and clipped second rebuttal. PRO's progression was cleaner: each round narrowed the dispute and sharpened the same comparative frame rather than widening or retreating.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's pressure question B3.2 and its Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's Q1 together form the most decisive exchange. PRO forced CON to confront the admission that shelter capacity is already inadequate, then showed that fining and towing people into non-existent beds is not a lever but a punishment loop. CON's answer—that enforcement creates the encounter for services—is hollow when the destination for that encounter is full, and PRO kept this contradiction live through the closing without CON ever providing a satisfying resolution.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won by exposing a fatal contradiction in Side B's case: B argued that enforcement serves as a 'compassionate lever' to push people into shelters, while simultaneously acknowledging that shelter beds are insufficient. Side A effectively hammered home that you cannot 'lever' someone into a non-existent bed, making enforcement purely punitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's decisive point that fining people with nowhere else to go as a 'compassionate lever' into non-existent beds is a cruel contradiction.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO won by exposing a decisive contradiction in CON’s case: CON admitted shelter capacity is insufficient, yet defended routine fines and towing as a “compassionate lever” to push people into those same scarce beds, which PRO effectively framed as punishment for poverty rather than genuine help. PRO’s rebuttal that the “no safe alternative” standard is already used for shelter prioritization and housing vouchers directly undercut CON’s vagueness objection, while PRO’s pressure questions forced CON to concede that enforcement relies on penalizing people who cannot be verified at the point of contact. CON’s public health concerns were plausible but met by PRO’s argument that displacement merely shifts blight and that ending wasteful enforcement frees resources for outreach. CON was further hampered by execution costs: a clipped opening, a clipped second rebuttal, and burying the A2 answer label rather than leading with it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s rebuttal that the “no safe alternative” standard is already operational in shelter prioritization and housing vouchers nationwide, which defanged CON’s “blanket amnesty” argument and shifted the burden back onto CON to justify punishing people under an administratively workable standard.
