### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a tight focus on the comparative burden of the motion ("reduce" vs "eliminate"). Side B fatally misreads the prompt in Rebuttal 2 by arguing the black market "does not need to become larger to defeat the motion; it only needs to survive," which concedes Side A's point that legal supply would reduce it. Side A also successfully defends its life-years math by pointing out that a massive financial incentive will induce net new supply that outpaces altruistic crowd-out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's first answer in Rebuttal 2, pointing out that the "premium lane" Side B warns about is actually just an exact description of today's status quo, effectively neutralizing B's core mechanism for black market persistence.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the motion comparative to today's prohibition and making both prongs easier to award: residual queues/brokers do not defeat "reduce," and tiny screened donor risk versus multi-year recipient gains supports net life-years even with partial substitution. A landed real pressure on political caps, rejected-seller stock, and additionality/crowding-out, but too often treated remaining harms as a loss rather than neutralizing B's status-quo baseline. B answered the pressure more cleanly and crystallized the choice more decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: legal supply makes the old $3k/$150k hotel-clinic premium uneconomic at scale and reframes donor-risk counting so prohibition fails both motion prongs.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively attacking the "additionality" of the proposed legal market. By arguing that paid donors would largely substitute for altruistic donors (crowding out intrinsic motivation) and that a politically capped monopsony would leave queues intact (sustaining the black market as a premium lane for the wealthy and a dumping ground for medically rejected sellers), Side A effectively neutralized both prongs of the motion. Side B's reliance on the assumption that paid and altruistic donors are entirely distinct pools was insufficiently defended against Side A's sharp economic and behavioral critiques. Side A's closing crystallized this "no net gain" argument perfectly, showing that without additionality, the life-year gains vanish while the harms of commodification remain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they systematically dismantled the assumption of additionality, showing that if paid kidneys merely displace altruistic ones, the net life-year gains vanish while the harms of a surviving black market and commodification are added.
