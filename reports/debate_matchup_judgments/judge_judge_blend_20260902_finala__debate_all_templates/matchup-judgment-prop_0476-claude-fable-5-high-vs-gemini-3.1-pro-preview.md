### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by making the decisive clash about whether bonds actually change orbital safety or merely move money after failure. Side A had a strong externality and polluter-pays case, especially on bankruptcy and residual cleanup funding, but B effectively pressed the pricing dilemma, the current immaturity of active debris removal, and the late surety-bond pivot. B's argument that sureties rescue affordability only by weakening A's original incentive story was the cleanest comparative hit. A's responses were plausible but did not fully establish that forfeited funds can reliably buy physical removal or that bonds outperform direct pre-launch engineering mandates.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal against the surety pivot was most decisive: it argued that shifting exposure to a guarantor undercut A's claimed operator incentive while still leaving the debris physically in orbit because removal capacity is not yet available at scale.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by exposing a fatal tension in PRO's model: a bond large enough to fund actual debris removal would crush startups and trigger regulatory flight, while a bond small enough to be affordable becomes a mere "pollution license" that leaves lethal debris circling overhead. When PRO pivoted to surety bonds to escape this cost dilemma, CON showed the move gutted PRO's own incentive story by shifting financial pain to third-party guarantors, creating moral hazard and evaporating the direct engineering discipline PRO had relied on. CON's alternative—direct physical mandates like decay-guaranteed altitudes, passive deorbit hardware, and design-for-demise—provided a cleaner mechanism that protects the sky even when companies go bankrupt, whereas PRO's model ultimately changed a ledger entry while the debris remained in orbit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pricing dilemma in Rebuttal 1 [A2.1], completed by the moral-hazard turn in Rebuttal 2 [A4.5], which forced PRO to choose between an unaffordable bond and one that contradicted the opening incentive claim.

The line "money moves, debris doesn't" crystallized the debate, exposing that forfeiting a bond does not physically remove orbital junk. CON also decisively exploited PRO's mid-debate pivot to surety bonds, showing it contradicted PRO's original "operator skin-in-the-game" incentive argument and functionally reduced the proposal to the insurance model PRO had initially dismissed. While PRO offered plausible arguments about market access and catalyzing an active debris removal industry, those claims relied on speculative chains of causation. CON's emphasis on pre-launch engineering mandates and physics-based decay provided a more grounded, prevention-focused alternative that directly answered the bankruptcy scenario without relying on a nascent cleanup market.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 dismantling PRO's surety pivot. CON showed that shifting the financial burden to a third-party guarantor to save startups from capital lock-up simultaneously destroyed PRO's opening incentive mechanism, creating a fatal internal contradiction. Paired with the "money moves, debris doesn't" frame, it neutralized PRO's cleanup fund argument and forced PRO onto the defensive for the remainder of the debate.
