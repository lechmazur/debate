### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won the central fiscal comparison. A asserted that individual policies would cover catastrophic misconduct while taxpayers funded only baseline premiums, but never adequately established coverage for the intentional or egregious conduct most likely to generate major payouts. B persuasively explained that denied or insufficient coverage leaves either victims uncompensated or municipalities paying anyway, now alongside premiums and insurer costs. B also gave stronger responses on claims-volume pricing, de-policing incentives, institutional responsibility, and private insurers becoming de facto disciplinary authorities. A presented a coherent accountability mechanism, but too often answered these concerns by asserting ideal underwriting behavior rather than demonstrating it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s insurance-coverage claim was decisive: if carriers exclude or contest the worst intentional misconduct, the city must still indemnify to preserve victim compensation, undermining A’s promised taxpayer savings while adding baseline premiums and insurer profit.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): B won by proving the plan fails its own stated goal. CON showed that standard liability policies exclude intentional or willful acts—exactly what excessive-force claims allege—so carriers would likely deny coverage for the worst misconduct. That forces cities to indemnify victims anyway, leaving taxpayers to pay the judgment plus baseline premium stipends and insurer profit. CON also demonstrated that actuarial pricing based on complaint frequency would penalize lawful proactive policing and outsource discipline to unaccountable private firms. PRO never adequately answered the coverage-gap problem or proved the savings would materialize, and relied on a misreferenced claim. CON kept compensation and accountability where they belong: on the public institution that creates the risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 1 showing that individual liability policies typically exclude intentional or willful acts, which would void coverage for excessive-force claims and force taxpayers to pay anyway plus a new middleman premium.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing the fatal mechanical flaws in Side B's insurance proposal. Side A effectively argued that personal liability policies standardly exclude intentional acts (which excessive force allegations inherently are), meaning the insurer would deny coverage. This forces the city to indemnify anyway to compensate the victim, resulting in taxpayers paying the judgment plus the new insurance premiums and stipends. Side A also convincingly argued that actuarial pricing relies on loss frequency (including nuisance settlements), which would penalize proactive officers in busy precincts and cause de-policing. Side B's responses relied on idealized, unsupported assumptions about how individual underwriting would perfectly discern fault from frequency and bypass standard exclusions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the insurance payout mechanism by highlighting the "intentional act exclusion" and distinguishing between municipal policies and a thin individual market, which completely undercut Side B's claim that personal insurance would cover catastrophic payouts without costing taxpayers more.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): B won the central financial clash. A repeatedly compared one officer's modest premium with a multimillion-dollar judgment, overlooking that premiums collected across the whole force must cover expected claims, overhead, and profit. A showed possible deterrence and better risk tracking, but never established that officer-paid surcharges would cover enough of aggregate losses to stop taxpayers bearing the main cost. B also effectively challenged pricing based on settlements and offered public decertification as a cleaner accountability mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's actuarial response was decisive: predictable premiums may smooth municipal costs, but they do not inherently reduce them. A's transparency answer did not resolve who ultimately pays, and its closing again treated a small individual premium as cheaper than aggregate judgments.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): CON successfully undermined PRO's economic case by showing that insurance overhead and union bargaining would leave taxpayers paying misconduct costs upfront plus profit margins, failing the motion's core test. While PRO offered a compelling status quo critique, CON's demonstration that the proposal fails on its own terms and substitutes market mechanisms for democratic accountability provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4) exposing that PRO's concessions on union bargaining and insurance overhead mean taxpayers would pay misconduct costs plus corporate profit, effectively converting the plan into a more expensive version of the status quo rather than a relief mechanism.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO built a stronger, more resilient case by directly addressing the financial mechanics of the prompt. When CON argued that unions would simply negotiate premium reimbursements, PRO successfully pivoted to highlight that this would make the costs transparent in a labor contract rather than hidden in unpredictable liability judgments. PRO also effectively neutralized CON's reliance on statutory decertification by pointing out that the current fragmented system fails to prevent wandering officers, making the insurer's "national memory" a practical necessity. CON's arguments were competent but relied on idealistic alternatives that dodged the financial reality of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal to CON's union bargaining argument, shifting the impact from "taxpayers pay more" to "taxpayers pay transparently rather than suffering hidden hits to public services," was the most decisive exchange in the debate.
