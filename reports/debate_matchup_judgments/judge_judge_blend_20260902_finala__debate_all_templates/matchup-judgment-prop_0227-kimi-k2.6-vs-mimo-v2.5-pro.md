### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A made strong arguments about underground evasion, lost investigative trails, data-extortion harms, and moral hazard, but B gave the cleaner comparative answer: ransom payment offers only uncertain victim protection while certainly financing and normalizing the next wave of attacks. B also plausibly argued that a ban need not stop every payment to matter, because dismantling insured, institutional, lawful payment channels changes default incentives at scale. A's intelligence and privacy points remained live, but were not weighed as persuasively against B's structural incentive case. B's clipped speeches and some overconfident claims narrow the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's data-extortion rebuttal was decisive: paying for deletion relies on criminal promises, often fails, and rewards the shift toward data theft, so A's “chance at suppression” was outweighed by the broader incentive harm.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully exposing the structural flaw in CON's defense of the status quo: that paying ransoms only buys a 'chance' at suppression while guaranteeing the capital for the next attack. PRO effectively counters CON's evasion arguments by distinguishing between marginal underground evasion and the collapse of the institutional, insured payment apparatus, proving that a ban would dry up the bulk of syndicate funding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 A2 reframes CON's defense of payment as a shield against privacy carnage, pointing out that paying for a speculative chance of mercy is exactly what underwrites and guarantees the exponential growth of data extortion.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO won by establishing that payments reliably fund escalation and rarely secure data, with support mechanisms shifting from criminal revenue to recovery infrastructure. CON's intelligence and enforcement objections were directly engaged but not neutralized, as institutional channels dominate volume and prohibition removes normalized supply without worsening core victim harms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answers to A's pressure questions on evasion and privacy harm, showing institutional payments as majority volume and that payment validates rather than mitigates data theft.
