- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): B won the immediate-crisis comparison. A persuasively attacked permanent vacancy-linked caps and advocated adding beds, but never established that universities can reliably create thousands of beds by the next intake. B’s revised policy remained consistent with the motion: pause growth during severe scarcity, then lift the cap when vacancy improves or guaranteed new beds exist. A’s own bed-tracking model functionally conceded that recruitment cannot remain unconstrained when housing is unavailable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure question and second rebuttal exposed the central contradiction: if enrollment may grow only alongside guaranteed beds, then it is effectively capped by housing capacity. A’s “floor on beds, not ceiling on students” distinction was rhetorically sharp but did not solve the immediate shortage when new supply cannot arrive in time.

B effectively proves that A's unique contribution—the market-veto cap—cuts off vital housing revenue and gives cities an excuse not to build.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (B4.3/B4.4) brilliantly distinguishes the two models, arguing that A's pivot to a guaranteed-bed trigger is just B's model with a destructive 'vacancy veto' bolted on.

When Side B shifted from a city-controlled vacancy target to a university-controlled bed guarantee to avoid the "permanent cap" trap, Side A brilliantly pointed out that this conceded Side A's own model ("scale beds with enrollment") and reduced the proposition to a mere "vacancy veto." Side A proved this veto causes the exact harms they identified (lost revenue, letting cities off the hook for zoning) without solving the crisis, as seen in Canada. Side A's analogies were highly effective, and their defense of alternative housing adequately answered Side B's pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's revised trigger, showing that the "bed guarantee" branch was just Side A's model, and the "vacancy" branch was an indefensible veto that produces layoffs without rent relief.
