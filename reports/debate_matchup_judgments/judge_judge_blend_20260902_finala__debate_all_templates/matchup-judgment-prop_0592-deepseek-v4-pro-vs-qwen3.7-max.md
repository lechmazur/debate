### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the stronger cumulative case across the exchange. Its decisive structural move—turning CON's own Hungary example against them—was never cleanly neutralized. CON's core claim is that unanimity produces upfront consensus that guarantees enforcement, yet the Hungarian oil embargo, the very case both sides kept returning to, delivered late, loophole-ridden, pipeline-exempted sanctions: exactly the porous outcome CON attributes uniquely to QMV. CON's response (visible legal exemptions are capped and monitored, whereas QMV would produce invisible bureaucratic sabotage) is the debate's most creative moment and genuinely shifts the terrain, but it ultimately concedes that unanimity also produces diluted sanctions—it only argues the dilution is more visible. PRO's rejoinder that an outvoted state still faces binding EU law, immediate reputational costs, and peer pressure—and cannot write itself textual carve-outs—was adequate, if not airtight. On the structural wedging argument, PRO's point that capturing a 35-percent blocking minority is structurally harder than cultivating one veto ally was never fully answered; CON's counter that only one or two infrastructure-critical states suffice is an empirical qualification, not a structural defeat of the principle. PRO also handled both pressure questions more directly: CON's Q1 about infringement speed was answered by distinguishing the value of stripping the veto upfront from the enforcement litigation step, and CON's Q2 on solidarity was answered with the solidarity clause and constructive abstention. CON handled PRO's questions competently but the visible-versus-invisible sabotage distinction, while sharp, implicitly surrendered the core claim that unanimity reliably produces enforceable outcomes. Side B's multiple clipped turns are real execution costs that tighten the margin, but the core arguments within the visible text were clearly advanced and well-answered.

CON's later response (visible legal exemptions are capped and monitored) was clever but conceded the foundational claim by admitting unanimity also produces diluted sanctions, relocating the dispute to a secondary question about visibility rather than quality of enforcement.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built a stronger cumulative case and adapted better across rounds. The decisive pivot was PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to Q1: the speed advantage is front-loaded at the financial freeze stage—central bank asset locks, SWIFT bans, and elite wealth freezes operate through major EU financial hubs that comply with EU regulations directly, not through national customs machinery. CON's entire enforcement narrative rested on "customs halls and port inspections," which is where bureaucratic foot-dragging operates, but that is not where the critical initial shock of modern economic sanctions is delivered. CON never closed that gap. CON's answer to PRO's Q2 (deadlock-breaking mechanism under unanimity) was also weak—"political cost of being lone obstructionist" is circular and does not address the real-world scenario of a state with sufficiently strong bilateral interests simply refusing for months. PRO also correctly noted that QMV still requires 55% of states and 65% of population, which CON's "Brussels diktat" framing failed to rebut. CON made real points about enforcement being in national hands and about resentment fueling euroscepticism, and these arguments retained some traction. But two clipped speeches (Turns 2 and 4) imposed execution costs, and CON's core enforcement argument was narrowed rather than eliminated by PRO's front-loading distinction. PRO's closer crystallized the decisive clash cleanly; CON's closer was stronger rhetorically but largely repeated the enforcement theme without resolving the financial-vs-customs distinction PRO had already opened. On balance, PRO made the debate easier to decide in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 A1: the "front-loaded speed advantage" response. PRO argued that the decisive initial shock—freezing central bank reserves and blocking elite financial flows—is delivered through major EU financial institutions that comply with regulations on day one, making the capital paralysis already locked in even if a resentful outvoted state later drags its feet on physical customs. CON had no adequate reply: it kept citing "customs halls and port inspections" as if those were the primary mechanism of sanctions efficacy, leaving PRO's financial-freeze distinction standing and materially undermining CON's enforcement narrative.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate focused on the motion’s core comparative: faster action against aggressor states. B gave a plausible mechanism for why unanimity creates exploitable delay and why QMV creates a broad but non-vetoable mandate. A’s strongest answer was that imposed sanctions may be under-enforced by resentful states, and A did serious damage to B’s overly strong claim that EU law automatically guarantees compliance. But B’s later front-loaded “day-one financial shock” response was decisive: even if some later customs enforcement is imperfect, QMV still enables immediate freezes across most of the EU, whereas unanimity can mean no sanctions at all during the critical adaptation window. A’s alternative mechanism—peer pressure and eventual burden-sharing—was less convincing on speed and conceded too much delay. A also had clipped turns, which slightly hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 best answered A’s enforcement attack by distinguishing later leakage from the initial financial freeze, preserving the key QMV speed advantage while exposing unanimity’s weak deadlock mechanism.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO won because it successfully narrowed the decisive clash to the front-loaded financial shock that QMV delivers on day one, and showed that this critical phase relies on centralized banking and asset-freeze mechanisms rather than the decentralized customs enforcement CON emphasized. CON mounted a strong enforcement-gap critique, but never adequately answered how peer pressure breaks a months-long deadlock under unanimity, and its inability to distinguish between financial sanctions (swift and centralized) and trade controls (slow and border-dependent) left its core counterattack bleeding. Two clipped speeches compounded CON's execution costs, while PRO steadily sharpened the weighing across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 distinction between immediate financial/asset shocks and later physical customs enforcement (A4.1–A4.2), which defanged CON's "paper tiger"/enforcement-gap offense by conceding the lag where it mattered least while securing the speed advantage where it mattered most.
