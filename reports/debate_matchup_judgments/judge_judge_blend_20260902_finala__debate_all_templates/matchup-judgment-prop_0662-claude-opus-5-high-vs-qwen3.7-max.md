### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by exposing two unresolved design failures: earnings telemetry records what happened but not whether lost work was involuntary, and intermittent workers may contribute without clearing unemployment-insurance thresholds. Side B repeatedly substituted health and pension accrual for an answer about unemployment eligibility, while its constructive-dismissal response depended on employment-law concepts not clearly applicable to nonemployees. Side A's clipped rebuttal was an execution cost, but its comparative case remained substantially clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's threshold exchange was decisive: when asked whether the eight-hour courier could actually qualify for unemployment insurance, Side B answered with health and pension benefits instead. Side A correctly identified that as a concession rather than a resolution of the central burden.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the motion's fit problem live: UI needs verifiable involuntary separation and earn/continuity thresholds platform work routinely lacks. A never named a threshold the typical intermittent contributor clears, repeatedly retreating to pensions/health, and the constructive-dismissal answer created a clean dilemma (either ordinary employment already covers them, or the state must litigate "why" from telemetry that only shows "what"). A's fairness and data-pipe points stayed competent but did not neutralize those decisive burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's what-not-why / constructive-dismissal dilemma after A's R2 answers (telemetry cannot settle fault; invoking constructive dismissal either makes the bespoke pipe redundant or makes adjudication unworkable).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by demonstrating a structural mismatch between platform labor and unemployment insurance. B effectively pressured A to name a UI threshold that a typical part-time platform worker could clear; A never answered, retreating instead to pensions and health and effectively conceding that the median contributor would pay into UI without ever being able to claim it. B’s “what versus why” critique—showing that telemetry records income drops but not whether separation was voluntary—remained intact, and B’s dilemma that constructive dismissal doctrine either renders the bespoke system redundant or creates impossible adjudication for non-employees was never resolved. While A scored points on integration versus parallel welfare, the central mechanism of the motion was shown to be either inaccessible or unworkable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 and Pressure Questions exposing that standardized earnings records capture income drops but never causation (deactivation versus voluntary log-off), and the subsequent constructive-dismissal dilemma that either makes the bespoke pipe redundant or adjudication impossible at scale.
