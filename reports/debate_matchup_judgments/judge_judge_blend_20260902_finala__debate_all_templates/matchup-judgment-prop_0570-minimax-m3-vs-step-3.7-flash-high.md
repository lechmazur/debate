### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side B won by making the practical comparison sharper: under-resourced students lose immediate, iterative feedback if AI cannot be the main writing feedback source, while Side A's promised teacher bandwidth was under-supported and partly speculative. Side A had a coherent convergent-versus-divergent distinction, but leaned too heavily on repeated voice-flattening examples and an unsupported 60-70% claim. Side B more effectively weighed access, revision cycles, and classroom scarcity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was the attack on Side A's equity flip: the motion does not add teachers, shrink classes, or guarantee conferences, so restricting AI as the main feedback source risks removing a real tool in exchange for hoped-for human time.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side B won by dismantling Side A's "freed time" mechanism and realistically weighing the classroom tradeoffs. Side B effectively demonstrated that removing AI as the main feedback source leaves under-resourced students waiting weeks for feedback rather than magically creating more developmental conferencing. Side A also committed a major unforced error in Turn 5 by attributing its own equity argument to Side B and attacking it, which Side B efficiently weaponized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal exposing the flaw in Side A's time-saving math, pointing out that an exhausted teacher grading essays without AI assistance will simply be slower, not freed up for conferencing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing PRO’s central equity claim—banning AI frees teachers for developmental feedback—was an unfunded institutional hope, not a policy mechanism; the same overworked teacher simply loses a tool without gaining guaranteed conferencing time. CON also landed a decisive internal contradiction: PRO needed AI to be reliable for discrete writing mastery (thesis, evidence, structure) yet unreliable for those same elements in full essays, leaving no principled line for the proposition’s carve-out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 ([A4.1]–[A4.5]), where CON exposed PRO’s equity argument as sleight of hand and identified the “moving target” contradiction that undermined PRO’s mastery-practice exception.
