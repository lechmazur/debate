### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON built a more coherent and better-grounded case. Its central mechanism—that a 60-day EAD does not meaningfully deter someone already banking on 3-5 years of legal work before a merits hearing, because credible-fear screening is a low pass-through rather than a truth test—was never squarely answered; PRO's "60-day vetting window" claim in rebuttal 2 was mostly assertion. CON also won the definitional fight over "public assistance," successfully broadening it beyond SNAP/Medicaid to shelters, emergency Medicaid, and schooling, and then exploited PRO's costly concession ("total system costs are irrelevant") to show PRO was answering a narrower question than the one on the table. PRO's case leaned heavily on an unsupported, repeated "30-40% reduction" statistic that reads as fake precision rather than grounded reasoning, and PRO never rebutted the "interim work is the prize" framing, instead reasserting that permanent status is the "primary incentive" without engaging CON's mechanism. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but CON's answers did more actual work refuting the opponent's live claims, while PRO's answers mostly restated priors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.6), which used PRO's own concession that "total system costs are irrelevant" to argue PRO had implicitly conceded the debate by narrowing the proposition's scope, directly undercutting PRO's central empirical claim.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled both halves of Side B's case. A proved that a 60-day wait is no deterrent compared to a multi-year work payoff, successfully arguing that interim work becomes the prize. Furthermore, A decisively won the framing on 'public assistance' by showing that a surge in total caseload would overwhelm per-capita federal benefit reductions with massive local shelter costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 completely neutralized the distinction between 'day-one' and '60-day' authorization by framing the 60 days as mere processing time compared to the months of travel and the 3-5 years of interim legal work.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B built the cleaner path to victory by forcing the incentive clash and the public-assistance definition onto the proposition’s own terms: interim work as the actual prize for years of pending cases makes 60 days trivial processing rather than a deterrent, producing material non-meritorious filings, while per-person SNAP savings cannot count as reduced reliance once total shelter/system costs explode. Side A’s repeated narrowing of “public assistance” plus ungrounded 30-40% claims never neutralized that dual failure; later rounds largely restated the initial frame instead of answering the live pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 A1–A2 and closing: day-1 vs 60-day is irrelevant math for a 3–5 year work payoff after months of travel, and declaring total costs “irrelevant” loses the proposition on its face.
