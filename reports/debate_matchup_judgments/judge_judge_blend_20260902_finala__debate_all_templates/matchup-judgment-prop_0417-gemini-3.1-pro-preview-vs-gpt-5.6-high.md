PRO's bottleneck story remained vivid but was narrowed to a single causal pathway that did not outweigh CON's comparative reach.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's demonstration that PRO bundled venue-hour extensions with transit extensions (A2.1, A4.3), undermining PRO's claim that late transit alone would stagger departures and recentering the comparison on the motion as written.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the live comparison on what still works when drinkers do not follow the hoped-for script: extensions separate intoxication from driving and break synchronized street surges more reliably than a closing bell, which merely ends one venue type and still leaves workers/patrons needing a ride. A’s root-cause/prevention framing was sharp and rhetorically clean, but it never securely closed displacement (preload, private continuation, faster service) or supplied a better transport alternative for those already out; B’s answers and weighing exposed that gap more cleanly than A’s replies dismantled B’s.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s A1/A2 and follow-on weighing in Rebuttal 2 (transit need not extend venue hours; lower impairment still leaves an impaired driver and no safe ride, while convenience lets people leave the car home).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won because it built a more robust comparative case. Side A’s prevention framework depended on an uncertain causal chain: that earlier closing reliably reduces total intoxication rather than shifting it to private preloading or house parties—a displacement that Side B repeatedly exposed. Side B showed that transit directly removes driving exposure for patrons and necessary workers, disperses crowds over time, and operates independently of venue hours, undercutting the claim that late service necessarily enables marathon drinking. Side A never squarely answered why a less-intoxicated but still impaired crowd would avoid drunk driving without safe transport, and its “last mile” objection remained narrow against Side B’s broad car-free advantage. Side B’s crystallization that “closing bell ≠ sobriety bell” and “lower impairment is not safe transport” resolved the decisive clash in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal, where it decoupled transit extensions from venue hours to undercut the marathon-drinking claim, exposed Side A’s assumption that every extra minute equals more alcohol, and reframed the comparison as uncertain sobriety displacement versus direct removal of the dangerous journey home.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A kept the comparative clearer: earlier closing hopes to lower BAC but does not guarantee less consumption given preload/private continuation and still requires a ride home, while late transit directly removes the driving trip and staggers departures across time/routes. B's prevention framing and trapped-disorder point are strong but overclaim guaranteed sobriety and rely on standard transit handling a synchronized surge. A's A1/A2 answers and driver-vs-rider weighing leave the more direct mechanism live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5-A4.6 exposing CON's error that a closing bell = sobriety bell vs transit = marathon drinking, capped by 'a somewhat less intoxicated driver is still impaired; a transit rider is not driving.'
