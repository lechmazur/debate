### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (no reasoning) (CON)): B won by making the comparative burden clearer: seasonal and sectoral pathways may reduce some labor-driven irregularity, but they do not reach distress migrants, overstayers, uncooperative employers, or people outside narrow visa criteria. A had a strong root-cause story about labor demand and visibility, but relied on broad assertions that pathways would capture the economic majority and sustainably shrink irregularity. B more effectively pressed the limits of substitution and argued that enforcement has direct purchase on residual irregular presence. A's closing partially answered the enforcement-concession point by distinguishing border militarization from administrative tracking, but not enough to overcome B's broader structural objections.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was most decisive: it answered A's pressure by conceding enforcement has churn but nonzero deterrence, then turned A's visibility argument into a claim that pathways still require enforcement to reduce noncompliance.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (no reasoning) (CON)): Side B won by consistently exposing the structural limits of Side A's model, specifically highlighting distress migrants and visa overstayers as populations that sectoral pathways cannot resolve. Side A's heavy focus on labor demand left these gaps largely unaddressed, allowing Side B to convincingly argue that enforcement remains the necessary and more direct tool for the residual irregular population.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal decisively severed Side A's attempt to merge economic and distress migration, correctly noting that employer-sponsored agricultural visas offer no solution for families fleeing violence.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (no reasoning) (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): CON exposed three durable gaps in PRO’s case: sectoral pathways do not reach distress migrants, scaling up risks increasing overstays that convert into irregular presence, and PRO’s own Rebuttal 2 conceded that pathways need enforcement mechanisms to manage non-compliance. PRO never showed that the subset captured by labor visas exceeds the residual population left untouched, nor that pathways outperform enforcement on reducing existing irregular presence. CON’s comparative framing—enforcement acts directly on irregularity as it exists, while pathways shape future intentions for a narrower subset—proved more durable through the final exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 turn on PRO’s admission that pathway visibility enables “targeted enforcement on the few bad actors,” which CON leveraged to show that enforcement remains the operative instrument of reduction even within PRO’s preferred model.
