### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Both sides ran competent, engaged debates, but PRO (Side B) built the stronger path to a win for three reasons. First, the objectivity-versus-validity distinction [B4.3] is the most decisive philosophical point in the debate: CON's entire defense rests on the claim that a clean, scalable metric is inherently justified, but PRO correctly shows that a perfectly objective measure of the wrong thing is still not a valid justification for outsized sorting power. CON's response—"without objectivity, validity is impossible to verify at scale"—is a reasonable practical point but concedes the validity problem rather than resolving it. PRO's closing correctly crystallizes this: CON's position reduces to "no, but it's cheaper and easier," which is not the same as justification. Second, PRO's pressure question about ambiguity and the subsequent [B4.4–B4.5] development—"you cannot measure tolerance for ambiguity using a tool structurally designed to eliminate it"—is the sharpest logical move in the debate. CON's response (the tool measures the underlying trait, not the surface format) is coherent but asserted rather than demonstrated, and PRO never had to abandon the point. Third, PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralizes CON's strongest equity argument: if both test scores and portfolios are influenced by wealth, the relevant criterion for choosing between them is which one actually measures what the job or course requires. CON never recovered from this because its "raw potential" vs. "polished presentation" distinction is contestable and not developed. CON does score real points on the anti-nepotism argument and the g-factor-as-foundational-capacity claim, keeping the margin competitive rather than dominant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's [B4.4–B4.5]: "Standardized cognitive assessments are explicitly designed to have zero ambiguity; there is always one predetermined, correct answer hidden in the matrix. Real-world innovation is the exact opposite… You cannot measure a person's tolerance for ambiguity using a tool structurally designed to eliminate it." This directly exposed the internal tension in CON's fluid-intelligence defense, and CON's reply—that the tool measures the underlying processing capacity regardless of format—was asserted without bridging the structural gap PRO identified.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): CON built the stronger path to victory through the exchange by establishing and defending two decisive comparative claims. First, CON reframed the test's "narrowness" as a feature rather than a flaw: isolating fluid reasoning strips away pedigree noise and measures capacity to learn rather than what an applicant has already been privileged to learn. This capacity-vs.-competency distinction emerged in Rebuttal 1 and was sharpened each round without meaningful counter. Second, and most decisively, CON turned PRO's own proposed alternatives against them. When PRO cited coding tests and teaching demonstrations as superior replacements, CON correctly observed that passing a coding test requires prior computer access and programming instruction, and giving a teaching demonstration requires prep-school-style exposure. This meant PRO's alternatives systematically advantage affluent applicants even more than fluid reasoning tests do. PRO never recovered from this turn; the closing simply reasserted that tests are culturally biased without answering why work samples would be less so. PRO's initial framing was rhetorically strong—real-world success requires creativity and collaboration—and the "ceiling not baseline" reframe had genuine force. But PRO never squared the key burden: if the alternatives are more biased against marginalized applicants, the motion's claim that the current role is "unjustified" collapses. CON's pressure questions were also sharper, exposing the circularity in PRO's structured-task proposal. PRO's questions were reasonable but gave CON manageable answers. Both sides maintained epistemic discipline, avoiding fake citations, which kept the comparison clean.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 exploitation of PRO's own coding-test example [B4.3]: CON noted that practical skill demonstrations require prior exposure to the tools and training involved, meaning PRO's proposed alternatives privilege applicants who already had specialized instruction—exactly the affluent insiders PRO claimed to protect the excluded from. PRO introduced this example in its own answer to CON's pressure question and never addressed the vulnerability CON exposed. This was the single most damaging exchange in the debate.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side A built the stronger path by consistently showing that alternatives to cognitive tests amplify privilege and fail to measure learning capacity, neutralizing PRO's bias and narrowness claims while clarifying the baseline role.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4-A4.6 on capacity vs. competency directly dismantled B's work-sample push and exposed mobility costs.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): CON successfully reframed IQ tests as measures of underlying capacity to learn rather than trivial puzzles, then landed a decisive comparative turn: PRO's proposed alternatives—structured work samples, coding tests, and situational judgment tasks—inherently require prior access to specialized training and resources, making them more exclusionary than the cognitive baselines they seek to replace. While PRO effectively showed that narrow metrics risk false negatives and hard cutoffs, they never fully recovered from CON's "capacity versus competency" distinction, which demonstrated that broadening assessment as PRO advocated would systematically privilege candidates who had already bought educational access. CON's sharper closing crystallized this tradeoff with the memorable "engine versus fuel" metaphor and left the clearer live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4) dismantled PRO's advocacy for work samples by distinguishing capacity from competency, arguing that coding tests and teaching demonstrations require prior exposure that under-resourced applicants lack, thereby turning PRO's equity argument against them.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully turning the equity clash and crystallizing a decisive capacity-versus-competency framework. While PRO effectively argued that IQ tests miss multidimensional traits and can act as hard cutoffs, CON consistently demonstrated that PRO's proposed alternatives (work samples, practical tasks) inherently measure past training and exposure, which disproportionately advantages privileged applicants. CON's rebuttal that standardized tests isolate raw learning capacity independent of pedigree directly undermined PRO's core normative appeal. CON also handled pressure questions cleanly, defended the "baseline" model against the "outsized role" charge, and maintained tighter argumentative discipline throughout. PRO's closing relied on repetitive metaphors without resolving the structural paradox in their own alternatives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 dismantled PRO's work-sample alternative by distinguishing fluid reasoning (capacity to learn) from task performance (past competency), showing how removing cognitive baselines would actually cement socioeconomic barriers rather than dismantle them.
