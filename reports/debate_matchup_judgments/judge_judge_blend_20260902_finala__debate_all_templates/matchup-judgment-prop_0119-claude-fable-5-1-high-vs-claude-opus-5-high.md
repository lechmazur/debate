Side A also decisively won the mechanical clash by proving that converting tips to service charges transfers ownership of the surplus from the worker to the house under existing law.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive in catching Side B inventing a late legislative fiat to solve the 'house revenue' problem, and brilliantly resolving the nurse comparison by pointing out the lack of a discretionary surplus at a hospital bedside.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won the broader comparison: transparent pricing, predictable wages, and reduced customer leverage outweighed B's strong concern about lost upside. A answered worker exit as a transition and selection problem and openly defended redistribution, while B repeatedly generalized A's concession about top earners into an unsupported claim that median workers must also lose. B never fully neutralized the hidden-price, bias, or harassment arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest answer was that wage workers have one boss, while tipped workers have that same boss plus every customer controlling part of their income. This directly undercut B's claim that replacing tips merely concentrates power in management, because managerial power already exists under tipping.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by effectively proving that moving to fixed wages shifts all leverage to the employer, reduces total worker compensation, and replaces income volatility with hours volatility. Side B makes a strong moral case against tipping's inequities, but Side A brilliantly leverages B's concession about pay cuts to show the motion ultimately harms the workers it aims to protect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal that fixed wages incentivize employers to aggressively cut shifts on slow nights, successfully flipping Side B's argument about income predictability by showing that volatility of hours is worse than volatility of rate.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the distribution fight. PRO framed tipping as hidden risk and bias but never recovered from two live pressures: (1) service charges belong to the house while tips belong to the server, creating opacity, and (2) full-minimum-plus-tips states show servers still choose tips, undermining the coordination-failure defense. A's Q2 forced B to own that a $30/hr server takes a cut, and A crystallized that as "higher base pay" becoming a visible pay cut plus concentration of power in one boss and stronger incentive to cut hours. PRO's less-restrictive alternative (raise the tipped minimum) went unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 pivot - using B's own admission that the top earner takes a cut to reframe the motion as floor-plus-Saturday vs floor alone and exposing the owner-capture of the service charge.
