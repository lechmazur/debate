### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A built the clearer comparative path: volunteer recruitment is already failing, while conscription has produced mobilizable reserves in several exposed European democracies. A also exposed that Sweden's selective reinstatement still supports the proposition. The erroneous C-reference IDs and some overstated causal claims modestly reduce A's edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was the attack on B's retention model: a reserve drawn from departing volunteers remains downstream of an inadequate recruitment pipeline. B identified an existing pool of trained leavers, but did not establish that it could reach the scale required, while Sweden's return to a draft remained damaging evidence against volunteer-only recruitment.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively weaponizing the Sweden example to neutralize Side B's alternative model. A consistently demonstrated that B's solution (retaining professional outflow) fails because it is downstream of a broken recruitment pipeline, a fatal flaw B never resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing crystallization that B's reserve model is "a pipeline drawing from a dry well," exposing that retention cannot solve a structural failure in initial recruitment.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won a competitive debate by forcing a single live decisive comparison: Europe needs a trained mobilization base for deterrence, and only A's model has a working European production mechanism (Finland/Baltics/Sweden's reversal). B's capability-and-fairness critique was serious and often sharp, but its alternative—pay, retain, and refresh volunteer outflow plus civilian specialists—remained downstream of the recruitment failure A kept proving with Sweden and empty European pipelines. A answered B's pressure on billet assignment and cost more cleanly than B answered the export of Sweden's threat trigger or the source of reserves. Cohesion was closer; readiness decided it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Sweden-as-failed-volunteer-experiment line ([A2.1]–[A2.2]), then pressure Q2 and closing “dry well” crystalization against B's retention/outflow reserve—B never fully neutralized the recruitment-source burden.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by turning A’s flexible model into a sharp tradeoff: universal service imposed major costs and training dilution, while selective or predominantly civilian service weakened either the cohesion claim or the direct defense benefit. A plausibly defended civil resilience and shared obligation, but never adequately resolved the opportunity-cost objection or established that coercion would improve cohesion. B’s targeted-capability alternative and liberty weighing therefore provided the cleaner decision rule, despite some unsupported cost language and erroneous reference IDs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered A’s questions: targeted volunteer capacity costs less because it recruits only those needed, while A’s universal civilian-track model risks displacing skilled workers and diluting its defense rationale. A’s resilience response remained plausible but did not fully defeat that comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by trapping Side A in a structural dilemma: a universal draft is too costly and dilutes military capability, while a selective draft fails to build generational cohesion. When Side A adapted by proposing a universal obligation heavily focused on civilian tracks, Side B successfully argued this would displace skilled professionals and abandon the core defense readiness mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which deftly pointed out that Side A's "repurposed payroll" mechanism would result in untrained twenty-year-olds displacing professional nurses and firefighters, while undermining the military deterrence goal.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing and sustaining a decisive consistency trap: universal service dilutes readiness/funding while selective service kills the cohesion cross-section, and PRO's civilian-track pivot then made the defense rationale evaporate.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1–B2.2 converting PRO's Sweden reply into a fatal either/or (universal dilutes vs selective kills cohesion), later clinched by B4.5 on "most" in care tracks.
