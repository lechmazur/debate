### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON won by showing that LVT's theoretical supply advantage collapses in the most supply-constrained markets, where PRO conceded it merely ties transaction taxes and rent caps. CON turned this into a decisive filter: where housing need is greatest, LVT delivers no marginal supply benefit while imposing unique systemic costs—chronic valuation failures, compounding carrying costs, and displacement pressure that deferral only postpones. PRO's local-finance argument went comparatively underengaged, and the weak "not inherently harmful" reply on displacement undermined PRO's equity claims. CON's sharper rebuttals and cleaner crystallization of the core clash—bounded trade-offs versus unbounded systemic burdens—provided the stronger path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of PRO's deferral defense, showing that postponing tax liability to sale or inheritance is still forced displacement repackaged, and that PRO's concession on zoning-constrained markets evaporates LVT's claimed supply superiority where it matters most.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO kept the comparative ballot clear: valuation and liquidity problems are fixable with reassessment, appeals, and deferral, while transaction taxes must suppress productive exchange and rent caps must suppress returns to function. On zoning, PRO's tie-not-loss framing answered CON's strongest hit - where building is illegal all tools tie, where it is legal LVT alone leaves construction untaxed and captures publicly-created value for stable local finance. CON scored on residual circularity and permitted-use neutering but never showed alternatives create more homes, and suffered four clipped turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's R1 attack that valuing at permitted use neuters LVT's intensification signal in the most constrained cities, answered by PRO's pressure Q1 and A1 framing that this is at worst a tie since transaction taxes and rent caps also create zero homes there.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A built a much stronger structural case, effectively neutralizing Side B's zoning and displacement arguments by highlighting the economic incidence of LVT and the comparative failures of the alternative policies. Side A's rebuttals were exceptionally sharp, particularly the point that Side B was using areas where all policies fail to defend policies that remain worse where building is possible. Furthermore, Side B suffered severe execution costs, being clipped for exceeding the word limit in four out of five speeches, which left several of its final thoughts incomplete and disrupted its rhetorical flow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's zoning argument by pointing out that "CON thus turns a place where all tools are blocked into an argument for tools that remain worse once building is possible," perfectly crystallizing the comparative flaw in Side B's case.

### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO established a durable structural asymmetry: LVT taxes idle holding while transaction taxes penalize reallocation and rent caps suppress returns. CON landed serious causal challenges on the housing-supply link, but PRO’s political-economy reframe—that LVT turns landowners into pro-density allies by making scarcity expensive—provided a second live mechanism that CON answered only with speculation. On finance, CON showed implementation friction but never proved transaction taxes or rent caps outperform LVT; reserves and bonding merely mitigate transaction-tax volatility rather than reversing the comparison. PRO crystallized the decisive clash more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s rebuttal to the zoning constraint by arguing LVT reshapes landowner incentives toward supporting upzoning, because annual holding costs make density rational rather than scarcity profitable.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO wins the comparative despite a concession. CON's best hits - that LVT capitalizes into lower prices and turnover != net supply, and that deferrals/appeals break collection stability - reduce LVT's magnitude but PRO answers Pressure Q1 honestly (compresses timeline, doesn't make infeasible feasible) and adds the political-economy mechanism that holding-costs shift owners toward upzoning. CON's infrastructure-via-transaction-taxes is indirect and admits volatility that requires reserves/borrowing, while rent caps are conceded as non-revenue, losing half the proposition. Taxing idle holding vs taxing reallocation/exchange remains the cleaner structural win on both supply and finance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure-vs-production / capitalization critique and A's honest A1 concession plus reallocation argument - LVT doesn't create unviable projects but makes delay costly while transaction taxes penalize the assembly needed for development.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A built a more cohesive comparative case, successfully turning Side B's own examples (like fragmented ownership and the need for fiscal reserves) against them. Side A's argument that transaction taxes penalize the very reallocation needed to assemble land, and that LVT alters the political economy of zoning, provided decisive original impacts that Side B could not fully neutralize. Side A's closing also crystallized the clash more cleanly without getting clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal, where they pointed out that transaction taxes make the consolidation of fragmented parcels (which Side B raised as a real-world constraint) more expensive, effectively using Side B's own evidence to undermine their case.
