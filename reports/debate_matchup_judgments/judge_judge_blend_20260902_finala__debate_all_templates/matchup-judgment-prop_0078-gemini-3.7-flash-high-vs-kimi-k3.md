### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON built a durable analytical fork early (the "expectation" is either redundant with existing law or an unenforceable, vague social verdict enforced by stigma) and sustained it through every round. PRO never escaped this: when pressed on what "core civic norms" concretely add, PRO's answer collapsed into naturalization requirements and civic education that already exist (a concession CON exploited directly). PRO's second pillar—that expecting integration is what "politically compels" funding—was repeatedly challenged to supply a causal mechanism, and PRO's final answer ("politically compelled") was an assertion restated rather than a mechanism, which CON correctly flagged. CON also offered a concrete counter-mechanism (funded classes, translation, legal aid, shelters) that addressed PRO's strongest surviving point (informal, non-criminal coercion in enclaves) about as well as an opening framework can without conceding the motion. PRO's case remained thematically consistent and normatively appealing (shared civic space, democratic deliberation) but stayed comparatively abstract and repetitive across rounds, never sharpening the mechanism gap CON kept pressing. Both sides executed the Q&A format correctly with clear A1/A2 answers, but CON's answers were more concrete and its questions extracted a more damaging concession from PRO than vice versa.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.2–A4.4), which noted that PRO's own answer to the "what is the expectation" question reduced it to already-existing naturalization/civic education institutions, and that PRO's funding causal chain was "an assertion, restated, never a chain" — this cut directly at PRO's two load-bearing claims and was never adequately repaired in PRO's closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by locking the debate onto a live operational question PRO never solved: what the “expectation” uniquely adds beyond existing law, naturalization, civic education, and funded access. The redundancy/vagueness fork repeatedly forced PRO either into already-binding legal norms or into soft stigma with no fixed adjudicator, while CON offered concrete alternative levers—classes, translation, shelters, exit options—for every harm PRO named. PRO’s democratic-compact case was coherent and rhetorically strong, but its key causal claim (expectation politically compels funding) stayed asserted rather than mechanized, and later speeches mostly restated the opening rather than closing that gap. Shared ends made the lever comparison decisive, and CON owned it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.2: CON used PRO’s narrowed definition of “core norms” to collapse equality/rights into preexisting law and leave only residual “tolerance,” exposing the motion as either redundant or an open license for majoritarian judgment.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON won the decisive clash on what the expectation adds. PRO defines core norms as equality, rights, tolerance - B shows first two are already law, third is unjudgeable, leaving redundancy or vague social penalty. B's resource lever (classes, translators, shelters, exit options) directly answers A's harms of exploitation and gray-zone coercion, while A never gives a causal mechanism from 'expecting' to budget, retreating to 'politically compelled'.
