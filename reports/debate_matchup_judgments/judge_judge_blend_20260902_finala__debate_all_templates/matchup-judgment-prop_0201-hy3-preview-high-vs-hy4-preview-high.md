### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): The debate turned on whether a freeze can be treated as functionally equivalent to an adjudicated debt ripe for judicial execution. B built and sustained a sharper legal distinction—restraint (reversible, conservatory, executive/political) versus execution (irreversible, requiring adjudicated liability or waiver of immunity from execution)—and used it to expose the weakest joint in A's case: that a sanctions freeze, which is political and reversible, cannot substitute for a court judgment establishing liability. A's central move ("the freeze already crystallizes liability") was repeated across rounds but never squared with this distinction; A's answer that "adding due process... to an executive freeze" cures the legitimacy gap did not engage B's point that immunity from execution is a separate, stronger protection than jurisdictional immunity. B also deployed a concrete, apt counter-model (the Gulf War future-flow oil fund) to show a lawful, multilateral alternative that preserves principal and leverage, and A's attempt to claim this analogy supported PRO ("both reject absolute immunity") glossed over the multilateral/unilateral and stock/flow distinctions B had drawn. B's leverage argument—that spending the principal forecloses any bargaining chip for a ceasefire, whereas a freeze can be lifted as part of settlement—was never neutralized by A, who could only assert "leverage unused is leverage wasted" without a causal account of why spending produces better outcomes than continued pressure. Both sides complied with the Q&A format, but B's answers more directly dismantled A's framing. A's moral urgency framing (arsonist analogy, impunity) was rhetorically effective but rested on assertions that did not survive B's legal rebuttals. Overall, B's case was more internally consistent, better grounded in real legal distinctions and a concrete historical alternative, and more responsive to the decisive pressure points, giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
