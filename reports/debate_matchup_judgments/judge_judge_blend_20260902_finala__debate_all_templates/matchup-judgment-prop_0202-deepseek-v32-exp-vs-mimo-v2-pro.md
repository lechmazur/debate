### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V3.2 (CON)): Side A wins a clear but competitive debate. A established the central contestability mechanism—interoperability reduces network-effect lock-in and gatekeeper control—and gave plausible reasons why security risks can be managed through standardized, regulated APIs rather than treated as catastrophic. B had a strong line that weakest-link risks, API ossification, and degraded user trust could make mandates self-defeating, but too much of this was asserted as inevitable rather than demonstrated. A’s framing of “structured openness” versus incumbent veto power better matched the proposition’s comparative burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first rebuttal was decisive: it attacked B’s binary security model, argued that APIs can use sandboxing/authentication/permissions, and reframed the comparison as managed risks versus ongoing monopoly entrenchment.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V3.2 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's analogies (email and banking) and establishing a clear, unrefuted causal link: degrading security and quality through forced APIs drives users to seek the safety of incumbent platforms, actively destroying contestability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively neutralized the banking analogy by contrasting closed, verified financial transfers with the massive threat surface of mandated social data APIs.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V3.2 (CON)): Side A won by demonstrating that security risks from interoperability are graduated and manageable through well-designed standards, successfully turning B's email analogy by showing that protocols like SPF and DKIM evolved within open frameworks to improve security. While B's "self-defeating retreat to incumbents" mechanism was clever, it remained speculative and unsupported compared to A's concrete precedents from finance and telecom. A answered pressure questions more substantively and maintained cleaner comparative weighing, whereas B's reliance on asserting that email improved "despite" interoperability was special pleading that failed to answer A's causal challenge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's takedown of the email analogy in Rebuttal 1 and the subsequent Pressure Questions exchange, where A showed that interoperable standards enabled iterative security improvements, leaving B unable to answer beyond repeating the opposite conclusion.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V3.2 (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining a cleaner comparative frame throughout the debate. While Side B effectively argued that security degradation could ironically entrench incumbents (the "self-defeating" mechanism), Side A consistently mitigated this by detailing how regulated APIs, sandboxing, and compliance timelines manage risk and force security innovation. Side A’s rebuttals directly answered pressure questions without evasion, and its closing crystallized the clash around "structured openness" versus gatekeeper veto power. Side B repeated its "net loss" framing without fully overcoming A’s technical mitigation or addressing why regulated interoperability couldn't adapt like other industries. A’s weighing of certain monopoly harms against manageable, speculative security risks provided the clearer path to victory on the motion’s explicit trade-off.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over the email and banking analogies in Rebuttal 2. Side B pressured A on why mandated, fast-tracked integration wouldn't create immediate exploit gaps compared to organic evolution, and why banking's threat surface compares to social platforms. Side A answered directly by arguing that mandates act like GDPR, forcing rapid security engineering leaps, and that a unified regulated API framework actually raises the baseline above today's chaotic voluntary connections. This exchange decisively clarified whether security risks are inherent and fatal or manageable and catalytic, which became the hinge of the debate.
