### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by using the motion’s “ban or heavily restrict” language to offer a proportionate policy: ban unsupported facial and vocal inference while imposing validation, disclosure, human review, and alternatives on potentially valid questionnaires. Side A answered opacity with a credible enforcement model, but repeatedly framed the proposition as a categorical ban and never showed why B’s ex ante safeguards were unjustified. B’s comparison between pre-use validation and forcing rejected applicants to uncover black-box discrimination after harm provided the clearer decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s A1 response in Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it converted Side A’s claimed Big Five “concession” into a tiered policy expressly permitted by the motion. Side A continued calling this overbreadth without adequately confronting that heavy restriction, rather than prohibition, was itself the proposition.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won decisively by correctly leveraging the 'ban OR heavily restrict' wording of the proposition. Side A wasted significant time arguing that Side B's willingness to allow 'heavily restricted' questionnaires was a fatal concession, ignoring the plain text of the motion. Furthermore, Side B cleanly defeated Side A's enforcement mechanism by demonstrating the impossibility of applicants proving disparate impact against a black box prior to discovery.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1 and B4.2 cleanly dispatched A's main offensive push by pointing out that 'heavy restriction' is explicitly part of the proposition as written, turning A's perceived 'gotcha' into a direct endorsement of the motion.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A effectively utilized the "ban or heavily restrict" language of the motion to neutralize Side B's overbreadth arguments, proposing to ban opaque facial-analysis tools while heavily restricting self-report questionnaires. Side A also won the decisive clash on enforcement, demonstrating that Side B's reliance on post-hoc disparate impact litigation fails when applicants cannot access the black-box criteria needed to establish a prima facie case. Side A's polygraph analogy and focus on the erasure of the "why" in automated rejections provided a cleaner, more compelling path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they cleanly answered the pressure questions by mapping their policy directly to the "ban or heavily restrict" text and exposing the practical impossibility of Side B's enforcement-only model for black-box algorithms.
