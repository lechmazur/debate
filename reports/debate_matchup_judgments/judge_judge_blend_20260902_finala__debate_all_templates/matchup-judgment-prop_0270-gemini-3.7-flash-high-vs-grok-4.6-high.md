### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): CON identified and sustained the decisive weakness in PRO's case: the "rather than" framing cannot address daytime harms (predatory contact, gambling mechanics, autoplay) that have nothing to do with sleep, boredom, or bedrooms. PRO's strongest claim—that a screen-free bedroom "categorically neutralizes" harm—was exposed as narrow and overstated, since CON repeatedly pointed out that content and contact harms occur during waking hours regardless of bedtime discipline. CON also defused PRO's core obsolescence argument by distinguishing between chasing individual apps (a losing game) and naming durable categories of harm (unmoderated contact, loot boxes, autoplay)—a distinction PRO never adequately rebutted. In Rebuttal 2, CON caught PRO in a self-contradiction: PRO's own answer ("delay personal smartphones") is itself a device-specific rule, undermining PRO's claim that no app/device-level guidance is needed. PRO's closing repeated its architecture framing without closing the daytime-harm gap that CON kept pressing. CON's closing line ("a child can sleep eight hours, climb trees, and still open an unmoderated chat on the walk home") crystallized the clash memorably and left PRO's position looking incomplete rather than wrong in principle—sleep/boredom/play are goods, but not substitutes for named refusals, which is exactly the motion's disputed "rather than."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that content harm and predatory contact "do not clock in at 9pm" [B2.1-B2.2], combined with the Rebuttal 2 exposure that PRO's own "delay smartphones" answer is itself device-specific advice, most decisively undercut PRO's central "rather than" thesis.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): B decisively exposed the proposition’s “rather than” as a false substitution. A showed that developmental baselines can reduce displacement and family conflict, but never adequately answered harms that persist despite sufficient sleep and play, such as predatory contact or gambling mechanics. A’s claim that boredom tolerance dismantles those risks was especially weak. B also answered the obsolescence objection by distinguishing rapidly changing apps from durable risk categories.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response that “the design is the weapon” and displacement merely “the wound” directly defeated A’s attempt to treat sleep, boredom, and play as a complete architecture. The later walk-home chat example made the remaining gap concrete and unanswered.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON successfully bifurcated digital harms into displacement versus contact/content/compulsive design, showing PRO's three-pillar framework addresses only the former. CON's category-level refusals neutralized PRO's surveillance objection while preserving specific guidance, and CON exposed that PRO's "intentional exception" screen policy requires forbidden-product rules that PRO refused to supply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal (Turn 8 / A4), which answered PRO's surveillance burden with category-level guidance and showed that PRO's "intentional exception" framework collapses without named refusals.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A built a consistent structural case (scarce institutional authority is better spent on stable, platform-agnostic priorities than a mutating blacklist) and sustained it across every round, culminating in the memorable "venue, not a running catalog" line. B's strongest moment was the pressure-round example of a well-slept, well-played child still being groomed in twenty minutes, which genuinely threatened A's "not distinct losses" claim. But A answered this directly and specifically (A4.1) by distinguishing safety/consumer-protection duties with existing reporting channels from the "core digital-wellbeing" framing at stake in the motion, and B's final rebuttal and closing largely reasserted "safety and wellbeing are inseparable" without dismantling that distinction or explaining why existing channels are insufficient. A also answered the pressure questions cleanly with required A1/A2 labels and used the exchange to sharpen rather than dodge, while B's closing was more recap-heavy than clash-sharpening. Both sides argued in good faith with plausible, non-fabricated grounding, but A left the cleaner, more resolved path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.1 answer distinguishing "safety work with existing channels" from "core wellbeing framing" directly neutralized B's strongest grooming/self-harm example, and B never fully re-engaged with why that channel distinction fails.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won the proposition’s comparative framing: stable developmental goals provide durable rules across changing apps, while specific literacy can remain supplementary. Side B established that acute platform-specific harms exist, but repeatedly treated A’s proposed core as an exclusive framework and overstated that removing devices from bedrooms offers no operational leverage. B’s eventual shift from app-by-app guidance to teaching recurring mechanics also weakened its defense of the stated alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal directly answered both questions: qualitative dangers can be handled through parallel safety duties, while venue-based rules neutralize bedtime hooks regardless of redesign. Side B’s response that app-specific guidance really teaches transferable mechanics effectively conceded A’s central objection to rotating platform battles.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won because it showed that scarce pediatric and school authority is better invested in stable, non-negotiable developmental goods—sleep, boredom, and free play—than in app-by-app guidance that expires with the next redesign. CON landed real pressure on daytime qualitative harms and predatory platform mechanics, but PRO successfully framed those as separate safety duties and argued that venue-based rules outlast feature catalogs. CON never fully neutralized the structural critique that tactical literacy cannot scale faster than platform mutation, and its pivot to recurring architectural mechanics was cleanly dismantled in PROs closing as still an expiring catalog.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PROs second rebuttal (Turn 7, B4.1–B4.6) was decisive: it rejected CONs collapse of safety into wellbeing, argued that venue-based rules survive redesigns while feature autopsies expire, and crystallized the clash with the phrase The leverage is venue, not a running catalog of features.
