### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making total lifetime value, rather than immediate reach, the cleaner comparison. Its premium-cascade argument explained how theatrical revenue, event marketing, later windows, and stronger IP can be additive. Side A plausibly emphasized convenience and recurring engagement, but repeatedly assumed without sufficient support that delayed viewers become permanently lost subscribers and did not show why streaming must replace rather than follow theatrical monetization. B remained somewhat assertion-heavy, so the debate stayed competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal most effectively challenged A’s subscriber arithmetic by arguing that immediate streaming changes and devalues the product being counted. This forced A to establish that reach, data, and retention outweigh lost premium revenue and cultural heat, a burden A answered mainly through broad claims about the convenience majority.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing the theatrical window not just as a revenue channel, but as the value-creation engine that manufactures "appointment viewing." Side B convincingly argued that without this scarcity, the product is fundamentally devalued into "convenience content," effectively neutralizing Side A's claims about lifetime subscriber value.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A2) effectively neutralized Side A's argument about losing audiences to competitors, pointing out that exclusivity creates "appointment status competitors cannot duplicate," meaning audiences will still seek it out later.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): PRO maintained a live total-value cascade (scarcity -> opening heat -> free marketing -> higher VOD/streaming price) and pressed the decisive causality gap. CON never answered A3 Q1: if lower budgets follow theatrical underperformance, how does killing the runway not cause that underperformance. CON's answers asserted households already skip theaters and that discussion migrates, but did not weigh total lifetime ARPU vs quarter-headcount. A gave the cleaner metric and weighing: a devalued non-event title lowers IP value for every window.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 reframing that CON counts more one-time convenience views while shrinking the value of what is viewed, plus Q1 exposing CON's reversed causality on budgets/underperformance, which CON then sidestepped with 'scarcity already fails.'
