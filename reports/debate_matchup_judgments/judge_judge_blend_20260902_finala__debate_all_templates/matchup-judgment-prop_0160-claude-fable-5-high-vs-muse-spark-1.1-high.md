### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by drawing a razor-sharp distinction between consumer safety standards (bench-testing a device to ensure it physically cannot exceed 4mA) and medical regulation (requiring clinical efficacy trials, prescriptions, and gatekeepers). Side A also successfully weaponizes Side B's LASIK analogy to prove that the medical pathway would effectively ban daily, take-home use.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's distinction in Rebuttal 2 between checking if a toaster can electrocute you (FCC/UL) versus requiring a prescription to buy a toaster (FDA) brilliantly dismantled Side B's attempt to conflate all safety regulations with the medical pathway.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by narrowing the clash to who must validate neurological safety of plasticity-nudging protocols and by crystallizing the recoverable-vs-irreversible error asymmetry; B’s engineering/toaster and training-wheels replies never fully neutralized the need for brain-level monitoring of slow judgment drift or the engagement-incentive risk. Both sides engaged tightly, but A’s path remained more decisive after pressure and weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal-2/closing sequence: premarket power-cap validation is still neurological (not mere bench UL) once durable plasticity is claimed, so CON’s “consumer rails” recreate the medical pathway and Instagram-style delay is permanent for brains.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully collapsed the debate to a single decisive asymmetry: medical pathway delay is recoverable, while undetected neural harm from consumer-market devices is not. CON's regulatory alternative never fully answered who validates neurological protocols for long-term safety before shipping—the "bench engineering" distinction addresses hardware limits but cannot assess cumulative mood or plasticity effects. While CON landed strong blows showing that medicalization would de facto ban the routine healthy enhancement uses at the heart of the debate, PRO's framing around consent, the "organ doing the detecting being the one altered," and the recoverable-vs-irreversible weighing in the closing provided the cleaner route to victory. The training-wheels and toaster analogies were memorable but did not fully neutralize the invisible-harm and consumer-incentive risks that PRO kept central.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 either-or dilemma forcing CON to choose between "placebo" and "meaningful neural intervention," and the subsequent closing crystallization that a decade-late consumer-regulator response is irreversible when the brain itself is the target, was the most decisive exchange. It put CON on the back foot for the rest of the debate, forcing them to accept both durable benefits and transient risks from the same device without a fully adequate reconciliation.
