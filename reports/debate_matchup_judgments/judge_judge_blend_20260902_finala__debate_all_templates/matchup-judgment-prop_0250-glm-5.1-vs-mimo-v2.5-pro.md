### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but PRO maintained a more consistently offensive posture around the live burdens. PRO's selection-bias argument—voluntary electorates skew systematically toward the wealthy, older, and ideologically extreme—was introduced early, never squarely refuted, and reinforced throughout every round. More damaging to CON, PRO repeatedly pressed that CON offered no alternative mechanism to close the representational gap, and CON's responses (remove barriers, make politics worth showing up for) remained at the level of sentiment rather than policy throughout. PRO's most effective move was the symmetric-noise-versus-directional-bias comparison: even if compelled participation adds random noise, that noise is less distorting than the systematic skew of voluntary turnout. CON's best counter—that disengaged voters cluster around incumbents and default options rather than distributing randomly, converting supposed noise into a different kind of entrenchment—was genuinely strong and PRO never directly addressed it. However, this clustering argument arrived only in Rebuttal 2, leaving PRO no clean rebuttal opportunity, and CON did not develop it with sufficient depth to fully overturn PRO's mathematical frame. CON's categorical distinction between expressive acts (voting) and functional civic duties (jury service, taxes) was philosophically interesting but PRO's response—that the ballot itself remains wholly free, and that jurors similarly exercise independent judgment under compulsion—sufficiently neutralized it as a decisive divide. PRO also landed the sharper irony in closing: voluntary voting already delivers mandates from 30–40 percent of citizens, producing the very false-mandate problem CON claimed to fear. CON's consent-centered case was coherent but ultimately came down to a principled objection with no constructive alternative, which PRO exploited consistently. On balance, PRO built the more durable path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's symmetric-noise-versus-directional-skew distinction in Rebuttal 2 [B4.2] was the most analytically decisive exchange. By conceding that some donkey and random ballots exist under compulsory systems but showing that random noise washes out in aggregate while voluntary-voting selection bias compounds systematically in one direction, PRO reframed CON's donkey-voting critique from a fatal objection into an acceptable tradeoff. CON's counter—that disengaged voters actually cluster around incumbents and defaults rather than randomizing—was meaningful but arrived too late and too briefly developed for CON to capitalize on it.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by building a clearer path to the motion via selection bias correction with Australia/Belgium grounding, strong analogies to other duties, and direct pressure answers showing noise is minor/symmetric while voluntary skew is systematic. Side A pressed consent hard but offered no effective alternative mechanism and saw its "abstention as signal" and "clustered defaults" claims neutralized without full rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 sequence directly neutralized A's noise objection with symmetric washout math and reframed the participation choice without evasion.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B wins by maintaining a clearer comparative framework tied directly to the motion's goal of representative outcomes. While Side A delivered potent philosophical framing on consent and successfully distinguished the jury duty analogy, Side B consistently forced the debate back to practical policy comparison. Side B's weighing of symmetric noise versus systematic skew provided a cleaner reason to prefer mandatory turnout, and Side B effectively turned Side A's false mandate concern by demonstrating that voluntary voting already manufactures mandates from a smaller, wealthier, and more extreme pool. Side A's alternative mechanism remained aspirational and historically unproven, leaving Side B's structural fix as the only concrete path to solving the selection bias both sides acknowledged. Side B's rebuttals did more comparative damage to Side A's live routes to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's systematic skew versus symmetric noise comparison in Rebuttal 2, combined with the closing turn of Side A's false mandate argument. This sequence crystallized why voluntary voting structurally fails the motion's standard worse than compulsory voting's alleged flaws, and exposed Side A's lack of a concrete alternative mechanism.
