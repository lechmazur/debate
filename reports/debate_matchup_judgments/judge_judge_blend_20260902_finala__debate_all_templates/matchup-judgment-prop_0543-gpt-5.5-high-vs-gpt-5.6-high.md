### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO successfully defended that default privacy settings create measurable harm reduction through direct mechanisms (restricted unknown contact, location off, reduced discoverability) while reframing the censorship concern as audience consent rather than speech suppression. The decisive "asymmetry" argument—that minors can opt into public reach while predators cannot opt them into exposure—resolved the central tension. CON's age-verification trilemma and invisible-censorship points generated real pressure but relied on speculative surveillance overreach and an overbroad definition of censorship that PRO persuasively distinguished from ordinary safety friction. Clipped speeches from CON constituted a real execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's distinction between intended speech and unwanted exposure in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, establishing that default privacy places the choice with the minor rather than the stranger, directly neutralizing CON's claim that inertia burdens lawful communication symmetrically.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won the central trade-off. It answered PRO's pressure Q2 with concrete universal safeguards (location-off by default for all, anti-scraping limits, rate-limiting suspicious first contact) that achieve harm reduction without age-gating distribution, exposing that PRO's benefits are not unique to an age mandate. It sustained the contradiction that defaults cannot be powerful enough for safety but trivial for speech, and showed significant censorship includes state-imposed loss of findability/audience measured by non-events that PRO's opt-out metrics miss. PRO's safe-harbor / actual-knowledge answer to the verification trilemma remained underinclusive by design and undercut its measurability claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 - A1/A2 answers to pressure: reframes censorship as distribution burden and offers specific alternative architecture (universal location controls, scraping limits, quarantine of stranger messages) proving harm reduction without making minors presumptively invisible, directly neutralizing B's Q2.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing a fatal contradiction in Side B's case: the inertia paradox. Side A convincingly argues that if default settings are powerful enough to protect minors through behavioral friction, that same friction necessarily burdens lawful speech and findability for minors seeking audiences. Side B struggles to resolve this, relying on the claim that opting out is a modest inconvenience, which Side A effectively counters by highlighting the unmeasurable non-events where mentors and communities never connect. Side A also provides a strong, less restrictive alternative of universal anti-scraping and location controls. Despite minor clipping at the end of two turns, Side A's arguments were fully developed and logically superior.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal and closing, where they crystallize the inertia contradiction, pointing out that the friction protecting the uncertain child equally deters the determined journalist, and that the resulting censorship manifests as unmeasurable non-events rather than formal appeals.
