### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): This was a genuinely competitive debate. PRO built a coherent three-legged case—existential payoff, market failure, and spillovers—and its strongest moment came in Rebuttal 2, where the ignition/net-energy-gain point gave a concrete, grounded answer to CON's sharpest pressure question ("at what point does investment become unjustified?"). The market failure argument—private capital will not fund decades of basic plasma physics—was never fully dismantled; CON's drug-development analogy is weaker because pharmaceutical economics are far more predictable than fusion's. These are real residual credits for PRO. However, CON's case was more consistently grounded. The opportunity cost argument was anchored to a real-world example (ITER cost overruns diverting funds), and PRO's "false dichotomy" counter—claiming science and deployment budgets are fully separate—was not convincing enough to neutralize it. CON's most durable achievement was challenging PRO's "only permanent solution" framing with concrete, currently-deployed baseload examples: France, China, and South Korea running grids on fission, and geothermal expanding via new drilling. PRO's dismissals ("intractable opposition," "geographically constrained") came across as more hand-wavy than CON's responses, and PRO never adequately addressed the economic-viability assumption—that fusion might not close commercially even if the physics work. CON's "three unproven assumptions" closing framework was cleaner and more decisive than PRO's "failure of imagination" counter. On balance, CON held more live arguments after the full exchange, particularly on opportunity cost and the working-alternatives point. The margin is narrow because PRO's market failure argument is structurally sound and underrebutted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to PRO's Q1 (why no private consortium has funded a demo plant) was the pivotal exchange. PRO expected the question to silence CON, but CON pivoted cleanly: private capital stays out not merely because the timeline is long but because the commercial economics remain uncertain—and no amount of public "valley of death" framing changes that if the returns ultimately don't close. CON simultaneously used France, China, and South Korea to concretely undercut PRO's framing of fusion as the "only permanent solution," leaving PRO's core framing damaged on both the viability and alternatives axes in the same speech.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by consistently pressing opportunity costs and unproven assumptions about fusion's economic viability and alternatives' limits, directly neutralizing B's questions with examples like France/China fission deployment; B's "endgame only" framing and market-failure arguments were not fully resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 directly answered both pressure questions, exposing B's indefinite commitment and overstatement of alternatives as stopgaps.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing that Side B's case rests on three unproven assumptions: that fusion will prove economically viable, that working baseload alternatives will hit hard physical limits, and that the climate timeline permits decades more waiting. A sharpened the decisive clash on opportunity cost, showing with concrete examples that fusion investment diverts funds from deployable alternatives. A's rebuttals were sharper, particularly the drug-development comparison defusing B's private-market argument and the Apollo contrast undermining the "forcing function" defense. B left core pressure unanswered by treating fusion as the "only permanent solution" without adequately engaging A's evidence that advanced fission and geothermal are already scaling, and by offering no limiting principle for investment beyond physical impossibility. A's closing crystallized the gamble more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 answers to B's pressure questions, where A used the drug-development comparison to show private capital funds long-term projects when economics close, and corrected B's strawman by showing proven baseload alternatives are already scaling—shattering B's "only stopgaps" framing.
