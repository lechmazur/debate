- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): A built a more precise and resilient case by separating the legal right to childlessness from a blanket moral verdict, and by consistently exploiting the gap in B's "harms no one" definition of selfishness. B's case remained forceful on dehumanization and stigma-as-coercion, and its tax-subsidy point was a fair counter, but A neutralized it by noting parents pay the same taxes while bearing the added direct costs. B largely reasserted its "you can't separate description from stigma" claim rather than engaging A's distinction between aggregate diagnosis and personal blame. Overall A adapted more effectively across rounds and left fewer of its opponent's live points unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3's "shared chore" analogy, showing selfishness can exist without deprivation of an enforceable claim, directly undercut B's core "harms no one" definition and was never squarely rebutted.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing that PRO’s categorical claim relied on definitional fiat. PRO could not adequately answer CON’s pressure question about leisure-motivated childlessness, artificially narrowing “selfishness” to direct rights violations while ignoring collective burdens. CON also successfully distinguished aggregate demographic concern from individual coercion, showing society can recognize childlessness as a collective problem without stigma, whereas PRO’s structural-reform argument conceded that reforms do not create people and dodged the physical necessity of generational renewal. The sharper rebuttals and cleaner handling of pressure questions gave CON the live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s pressure question and subsequent rebuttal on selfishness (B3.1, B4.3–4), which exposed PRO’s inability to countenance any childlessness as self-regarding and showed PRO simply defined the problem away rather than engaging with intergenerational burden-sharing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully decoupling aggregate demographic concerns from individual moral stigma, dismantling Side A's core argument that treating childlessness as a societal problem inevitably results in coercive shaming. Furthermore, Side B effectively challenged Side A's overly narrow definition of selfishness, proving that free-riding on the intergenerational contract purely for leisure can indeed be self-regarding. Side A remained repetitive on the "biological engines" and "stigma equals coercion" points, failing to adequately address Side B's nuanced middle ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it cleanly answered the pressure questions by distinguishing between aggregate policy and individual blame, and dismantled Side A's restrictive definition of selfishness using the "shared chore" analogy.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): B built a cleaner, more resilient case: autonomy plus a specialization/reciprocity model (taxes, essential work, and caregiving already satisfy social obligation) that directly rebutted A's "free-rider" framing. Crucially, B's pressure questions exposed the decisive gap in A's case — A never specified what reproductive duty is owed, how many children discharge it, or why cultural pressure would reliably produce willing rather than resentful parents whose children bear the cost. A's rebuttals restated the "biological necessity" and "financial abstraction" framing across rounds without closing that gap, and largely ignored the harm-to-child argument B raised repeatedly. B answered A's own pressure questions (tragedy of the commons, migration ethics) with direct, structured A1/A2 responses, while A's answers to B's questions dodged the core "how much/why" problem. B's case did suffer real execution costs — both its opening and closing were clipped for exceeding word limits, denying the judge full visibility into its concluding synthesis — which tempers the margin, but the substantive clash was resolved in B's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q1/Q2) asking what principled reproductive duty is owed and why cultural pressure would produce willing rather than reluctant parents — a challenge A's Rebuttal 2 never squarely answered, instead reasserting "shared obligation" without specifying its content or scope.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins because it successfully defended the reasonableness of childlessness while exposing the fatal gap in Side B's case: the missing bridge between aggregate demographic trends and an individual moral duty to reproduce. A showed that contribution takes many forms, that cultural pressure operates through stigma and harms children by creating reluctant parents, and that collective challenges require policy solutions rather than converting citizenship into reproductive obligation. B's free-rider argument remained abstract and unprincipled, unable to specify who owes how many children or why taxes and essential labor do not count as reciprocity. B also strawmanned A's migration points and never recovered from A's quota critique.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to the free-rider charge, demonstrating that reproduction cannot function as a standardized duty because there is no principled quota (one child? two? three?) and that labeling non-parents as free-riders erases their actual tax, labor, and caregiving contributions while ignoring the private benefits parents receive.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins by systematically dismantling Side B's "free-rider" argument and exposing the flaws in B's proposed "cultural expectations." A effectively demonstrates that modern society relies on pooled, specialized contributions, logically countering B's claim that taxes cannot satisfy the social contract. Furthermore, A decisively attacks B's defense of cultural pressure, pointing out that such "expectations" inherently rely on the exact stigma the prompt condemns and risk harming children born to reluctant parents. While B delivered memorable rhetorical punches (e.g., "taxes don't perform surgeries"), B failed to logically explain how financial and professional contributions fall short of civic duty or define a principled reproductive quota, leaving A with the cleaner, more robust path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the "free-rider" charge, specifically the argument that society routinely pools different specialized contributions without requiring every citizen to personally manufacture every public good they use, and the sharp exposure that B's "cultural expectations" are enforced through the very moral stigma the proposition rejects.
