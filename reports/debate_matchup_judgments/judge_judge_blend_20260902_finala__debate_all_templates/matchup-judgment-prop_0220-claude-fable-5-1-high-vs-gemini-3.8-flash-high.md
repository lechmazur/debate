- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): B won by locking the debate onto CON's own lifetime net-balance-sheet metric and showing that larger deductions deliver roughly zero dollars to the paycheck-to-paycheck non-saver CON itself treated as decisive. A's liquidity/debt harm story remained speculative against opt-out and was undercut by its own inertia premise, which B turned into a confession that voluntary deductions reach almost nobody inert.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure-to-rebuttal sequence (Q1/Q2 then A1/A2): inertia strong enough to block opt-out is strong enough to block researching/opening a deductible IRA, so CON's harm story simultaneously proves deductions abandon the very households CON claims to protect.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins on CON's own metric - lifetime net balance sheets. PRO established deductions are worth ~$0 to low-income non-savers and only reshuffle existing savings, while auto-enrollment jumps participation from ~half to ~90% with largest gains among vulnerable groups. CON's core harm claim (22% debt vs 7% return) was directly answered by PRO's inertia-is-salience distinction and highest opt-out among lowest earners, plus no observed debt worsening. CON never answered A-Q2 dollar-value challenge, conceding its policy leaves the target household at zero. Design issues like 3% anchor and leakage are fixable (escalation, auto-rollover) vs unfixable zero.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's paired pressure Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 answers - showing if inertia traps workers in auto-enrollment it equally prevents them ever using a deduction, and that a leaky positive balance still beats CON's zero where deduction value is $0 - neutralized CON's leverage story.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins decisively by exposing a fatal contradiction in Side A's (CON) case. CON argued that lower-income workers are too behaviorally inert to opt out of auto-enrollment when facing financial strain, yet simultaneously relied on those same inert workers to actively research, open, and fund voluntary retirement accounts to claim tax deductions. PRO effectively neutralized CON's liquidity trap argument (22% debt vs. 7% returns) by highlighting that ticking a single opt-out box when a payment bounces requires far less effort and hyper-rationality than navigating the tax code to claim a deduction. Furthermore, PRO hammered home the decisive point that tax deductions have a literal dollar value of zero for low-income workers with little to no tax liability, meaning CON's policy offers absolutely nothing to the demographic most in need of financial security. PRO's framing of the debate around marginal impact and net new savings was much cleaner, and their rebuttals sharply dismantled CON's core harm story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7) and Closing (Turn 9), where they crystallized the inertia contradiction. PRO effectively explained that inertia governs subtle decisions, but genuine financial hardship (like a bounced payment) is not subtle, and ticking an opt-out box is vastly easier than opening an IRA, thereby turning CON's own behavioral premise against them.
