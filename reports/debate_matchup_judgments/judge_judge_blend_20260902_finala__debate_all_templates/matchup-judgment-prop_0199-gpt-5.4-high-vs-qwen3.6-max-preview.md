### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): Side A won by making the decisive clash clearer and landing the stronger tu quoque. PRO correctly identified from the opening that the motion already includes threshold-based safeguards, neutralizing CON's "publish now, regulate later" framing before it took hold. The most decisive sequence: PRO's pressure question asking what concrete standard would ever satisfy CON's release condition (Q1) forced CON to articulate a "phased, empirically validated access" answer in Rebuttal 2. PRO then exploited this in both its A2 answer and closing: if capabilities emerge unpredictably and evaluations are backward-looking (CON's own premise), CON's phased access standard relies on the same imperfect pre-release predictions they use to attack PRO's thresholds. This is a genuine internal inconsistency CON never resolved. PRO's concentration argument—that fewer external eyes is worse when capabilities are hard to predict, not better—also remained largely unanswered; CON's response that "concentration is governable through mandates" was less developed than the problem it addressed. CON's strongest contribution was the irreversibility/"cannot license a torrent" argument, which PRO only partially answered by pointing to chokepoint regulation. That argument retains some force. But PRO's overall framework was more internally consistent, had a clearer limiting principle, and correctly showed that CON's alternative does not escape the epistemic problems CON raised against PRO. The "not yet becomes never" crystallization in the closing was effective and unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 sequence (A1/A2) exposed that CON's own "phased empirically validated access" standard relies on the same pre-release predictions CON attacked as unreliable, while also showing that if a model can be pushed over a threshold with routine fine-tuning it should be treated as already crossing it—making CON's critique of gaming mostly a design question for PRO's framework rather than a fatal objection.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving a structural flaw in the proposition: open-weight distribution is a "one-way door," making post-hoc safeguards impossible to enforce. Side B argued effectively that Side A's standard would lead to indefinite corporate concentration, but Side A decisively weighed this by arguing that concentration is a governable policy problem, whereas irreversible proliferation of a torrent file is an ungovernable physical reality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of Side B's "chokepoints" argument by pointing out you cannot license a decentralized swarm or recall a torrent file.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): Side A won by showing the proposition supplies a concrete, limiting principle—legal default with specified danger thresholds—while Side B’s precautionary standard demanded an impossible negative proof that would freeze openness indefinitely. A directly defused B’s strongest objections in Rebuttal 2 with workable answers on derivative enforcement and adaptation-aware thresholds, then closed by crystallizing that uncertainty is better governed by targeted rules than by unreviewable concentration in opaque incumbents.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4’s direct answers to B3 on derivative regulation and adaptation-aware tripwires, which neutralized the irreversibility objection and turned the pressure back onto B’s lack of a limiting principle.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): CON built the stronger path to victory across the full exchange. The decisive turning point came in Rebuttal 2: when A4.1 defined PRO's "clearly specified" thresholds as fixed around "standard open-source toolchains, bounded fine-tuning windows, and typical consumer compute," CON correctly identified this as a self-defeating concession. Either the protocol stays narrow and dangerous models pass its lab snapshot, or it expands to cover plausible scaffolding and becomes exactly the contestable forecast PRO claimed to have solved. CON crystallized this cleanly in the closing. The irreversibility asymmetry — CON's core frame — remained live throughout: PRO's counter (regulatory ratchet prevents un-banning) was plausible but abstract, while CON answered with the concrete mechanism of statutory sunsets and review. PRO's "collective patching" argument was partially neutralized by CON's point that malicious forks and stale mirrors do not update when the community discovers a flaw. PRO had genuine strengths — regulatory capture, permissionless red-teaming, containment theater — but left too many of CON's live lines unanswered, and its own threshold answer in Rebuttal 2 handed CON its best closing material. CON's distinction between "universal possession" and "controlled access with broad scrutiny" was never fully answered and remained a live route to victory at the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exploitation of PRO's Rebuttal 2 A1 answer in B4.3–B4.4: PRO defined thresholds around "standard open-source toolchains, bounded fine-tuning, and typical consumer compute," and CON immediately turned this against them — either the protocol is narrow and dangerous models pass, or it expands to cover real-world scaffolding and becomes the very contestable forecast PRO claimed to have eliminated. This turned PRO's own answer into CON's best closing argument.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's key mechanisms. A forced B to define how "clearly specified thresholds" would work in practice, then used B's answer (fixed lab protocols) to prove that real-world misuse would bypass the test. Additionally, A successfully neutralized B's "collective patching" argument by pointing out that offline, safeguard-stripped forks do not receive community updates.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal, which turned B's answer about "fixed evaluation protocols" into a devastating dilemma: either the test is a narrow lab snapshot that dangerous models bypass, or it expands into the vague forecast B claimed to avoid.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): CON wins because PRO never resolved the central threshold dilemma: either standardized tests are narrow lab snapshots that miss real-world misuse via scaffolding and non-standard compute, or they expand to cover plausible boosting and become the vague forecasts PRO promised to avoid. This undermines the "clearly specified" requirement of the proposition. CON also maintained the irreversibility asymmetry effectively—while PRO argued restrictions calcify, CON showed that legal defaults can include sunset and review mechanisms, whereas published weights cannot be recalled. CON's distinction between mandated auditing access and universal possession further blunted PRO's transparency advantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal ([B4.3]–[B4.4]), where CON demonstrated that PRO's own description of standardized stress-test protocols ("standard open-source toolchains," "bounded fine-tuning," "typical consumer compute") concedes that real misuse occurs outside the regulator's canned setup, collapsing PRO's claim to "clearly specified" thresholds into either false precision or a contestable forecast.
