- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (CON)): CON won by exposing a structural tension PRO never resolved: PRO conceded frontier capabilities are emergent and unpredictable, yet insisted pre-deployment licensing could certify safety. PRO’s answers to pressure questions about definitional thresholds and sandbox metrics remained vague, and CON’s alternative of regulating chokepoints and release modalities stayed live.

PRO decisively undermined CON’s physical chokepoint strategy in the closing by demonstrating how frontier models lower barriers to bypass traditional controls (e.g., using household materials instead of regulated DNA synthesizers). PRO also handled pressure questions cleanly, defending pre-deployment testing as capability-threshold mapping rather than perfect prediction. CON’s closing mostly reiterated earlier points, included garbled reference tags, and failed to answer PRO’s final comparative push, leaving PRO’s "single point of origin" framing as the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s closing turn effectively dismantled CON’s downstream chokepoint argument by demonstrating how frontier models bypass physical controls, shifting the weighing decisively toward upstream licensing.
