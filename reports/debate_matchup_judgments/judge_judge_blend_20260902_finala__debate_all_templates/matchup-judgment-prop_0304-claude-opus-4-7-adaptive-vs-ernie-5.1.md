### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by successfully shifting the metric from composition to causal effect. A's argument that elite universities cause upward mobility for low-income students but merely ratify preexisting wealth for affluent students went unanswered and decisively won the net-effect clash. A also cleanly won the policy clash by demonstrating that Side B's endowment tax would destroy the very financial aid that enables this mobility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's hospital analogy in Turn 4 cleanly dismantled B's denominator argument, and A's causal counterfactual (rich kids stay rich, poor kids become rich) decisively won the net-effect weighing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON built the stronger path by consistently centering causal per-student mobility effects and exposing PRO's failure to rebut the relevant counterfactual (admitted low-income students vs. alternatives). CON answered pressure questions directly and used them to sharpen the net-effect comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.5 directly neutralized survivorship and denominator claims while forcing PRO to defend why a fixable intake problem defines institutional identity.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON wins by successfully separating causal mobility from compositional ratification. While PRO effectively argued that elite universities structurally favor the wealthy, CON demonstrated that hosting affluent students who would remain affluent anyway does not constitute reproducing hierarchy in a causal sense, whereas the income convergence for low-income admits is a direct institutional effect. CON also turned PROs reform proposals into a concession that inheritance is a removable policy layer rather than an essential identity, and clearly weighed the policy tradeoff: taxing endowments would defund the very aid that enables mobility. PROs systemic framing was coherent but ultimately circular on causation and failed to answer the counterfactual or the policy harm to low-income students. CONs closing crystallized these burdens cleanly despite a minor clip in R2.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CONs response to PROs survivorship and structural claims by introducing the reproduction without causation versus mobility with causation distinction. This reframed the entire debate, showing that elite schools ratify pre-existing wealth rather than create it, while actively causing upward mobility for low-income admits. This directly neutralized PROs core metric and gave CON a decisive weighing mechanism that PRO never squared answered.
