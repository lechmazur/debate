### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): CON won on the decisive structural clash. The motion requires PRO to prove that industrial confinement of octopuses constitutes *indefensible* cruelty—meaning no welfare framework can make it defensible. CON consistently pressed this burden, noting that pilot farms already operate with low-density, enriched, solitary systems meeting PRO's stated welfare concerns, and that regulation has improved outcomes for other cognitively complex species. PRO's main counter—that industrial economics inevitably undermine welfare standards—was plausible but never rose above assertion; CON correctly noted this is a scalability challenge, not proof that all welfare-compliant farming is impossible. CON's wild-catch argument also survived: PRO's response that farming adds demand rather than replacing wild catch was labeled speculation by CON (fairly), and PRO's fallback of "just reduce consumption overall" goes beyond the motion's scope. PRO's closing burden-flip ("the weight is on those who wish to confine consciousness") was rhetorically sharp but didn't erase CON's earlier structural advantage. PRO's strongest uncontested argument—that you cannot regulate away the psychological experience of captivity for a problem-solving mind—was real and CON never directly dismantled it, which kept the margin narrow. But CON's clean framing that a ban locks in the far more destructive unregulated wild harvest, while eliminating the only production pathway subject to oversight, remained live through the close and PRO's answers to it were persistently weak. CON's closing crystallized the live clash well; PRO's closing was more rhetorical than analytical, leaning on "Band-Aid on a bullet wound" rather than resolving CON's surviving burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's consistent deployment of the burden argument—that PRO must show ALL welfare-aligned octopus farming is indefensible, not merely unregulated industrial farming—combined with the "ban locks in wild catch harm" line, formed the debate's most damaging sustained attack. PRO's responses to this combined pressure (wild death is natural; we should reduce all consumption) were insufficient: the first distinction is contestable, and the second goes outside the motion, leaving CON's argument live at the close.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the debate centered on the motion's moral claim: octopus cognition and solitary biology make industrial confinement inherently cruel, especially for a luxury product. B's wild-catch backfire argument was the strongest CON route, but it often shifted the metric to total seafood harm and relied heavily on unsupported claims about existing low-density pilot farms, marginal costs, and enforceable welfare systems. A gave the clearer comparative frame: farming would add and normalize systematic captivity rather than solve wild harvest harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer that wild-catch cruelty is not a justification for building a second, industrialized cruelty pathway most effectively blunted B's main policy turn.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO successfully focused the debate on the specific incompatibility between cephalopod biology and industrial economics. PRO effectively neutralized CON's pilot farm examples by pointing out that industrialization requires density for profit, and defeated the wild-catch substitution argument by arguing that farming expands rather than replaces the market. CON struggled to defend the viability of scaling ethical farming and misread PRO's cost argument in the second rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's argument that industrial scale inherently demands volume and density, which neutralized CON's reliance on subsidized, low-density pilot farms as proof of ethical viability.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): PRO built and maintained a coherent three-part chain throughout the debate: documented cognitive complexity entails genuine suffering capacity; industrial conditions make that suffering severe and chronic; luxury demand provides no sufficient public necessity to justify it. CON's strongest countermoves were the inspectability argument ("tanks are monitorable, oceans are not") and the comparative harm argument ("a ban outsources cruelty to unregulated wild capture"). Both are real arguments and kept the debate genuinely competitive. However, PRO landed two decisive blows CON never fully answered. First, the necessity gap: CON's food security claim for coastal communities was underdeveloped and PRO's characterization of octopus farming as luxury-oriented was never seriously refuted, meaning the "defensible cruelty" threshold was never established on CON's terms. Second, PRO's answer to CON's sharpest pressure question (Q2: why is research confinement ethical?) was clean and genuinely distinguishing—limited, individually authorized use for critical public benefit versus industrial mass confinement for trivial gain—rather than evasive. CON's own comparative harm argument cuts against itself logically: showing that the alternative is also bad does not establish that industrial confinement is defensible, only that both may be indefensible; CON needed to affirmatively show the cruelty is defensible, not merely that the comparison is unfavorable. CON's "luxury margins fund welfare" claim was the weakest link—plausible in theory but undermined by the historically grounded industrial farming pattern PRO cited, and CON never supplied a credible enforcement mechanism that addresses the systematic profit-incentive problem. PRO's clipped Rebuttal 1 is a real execution cost that compressed their early development, but subsequent rounds recovered the core arguments adequately. Margin is clear but competitive: CON made genuine comparative pressure that PRO had to work to address.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 response to CON's Q2 (why is research confinement permissible if confinement is inherently indefensible?) was the most decisive single exchange. PRO cleanly distinguished the EU directive's narrow, individually approved, non-industrial research standard—requiring minimal suffering for critical public benefit—from mass industrial confinement serving trivial luxury demand, turning a strong CON pressure question into a clarification that actually sharpened PRO's core necessity argument rather than damaging it.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side B wins a clear but competitive debate by making the central comparison easier to decide: inspectable, regulated farming versus uninspectable wild capture. Side A built a strong cruelty case around cognition, solitary behavior, confinement, and lack of necessity, but its answer to the wild-capture tradeoff was weaker and somewhat inconsistent: it argued global farm welfare enforcement is impossible while relying on global wild-harvest enforcement through market leverage. Side B’s regulation model was somewhat optimistic, but its fixed-infrastructure inspection, trade-standard, and net-suffering framing directly damaged the case for a global ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response that tanks are inspectable through fixed infrastructure, cameras, and import standards while oceans and dispersed vessels are not was the most decisive exchange, undercutting Side A’s paired-ban-and-wild-rules escape route.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON)): Side A won by effectively combining the biological reality of octopuses (solitary, mobile, complex) with the economic realities of industrial farming, proving that the two are inherently incompatible. Side B's defense relied on the premise that 'luxury margins' could fund solitary housing at an industrial scale, but this conflicted with B's own claim about the industry providing local protein security, and failed to overcome A's point that scaling inherently demands cost-cutting.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized B's reliance on the EU research directive by correctly distinguishing between narrowly tailored, public-benefit research and mass industrial confinement for luxury demand.
