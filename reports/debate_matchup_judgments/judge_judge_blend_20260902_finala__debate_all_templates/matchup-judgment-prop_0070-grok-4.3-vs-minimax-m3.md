- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): CON won by successfully exploiting the central contradiction in PRO's position: a mandate that must remain flexible enough to handle projection uncertainty cannot reliably differ from the voluntary frameworks PRO says are failing, and PRO's proposed fix—an external review body approving periodic revisions—opened directly to CON's rebuttal that this merely adds a bureaucratic layer around the same deferral incentives. CON's pressure question on this paradox was the sharpest move in the debate, and PRO's A1 answer was adequate but not damaging to CON. On the Isle de Jean Charles example, CON's reading held: slow processes reflect the inherent difficulty of moving communities, not the absence of a mandate, and compulsion multiplies compensation disputes rather than dissolving them. PRO's strongest running argument—the enforceable starting duty breaks the deferral cycle—was genuine and left partially alive, but CON's counter (officials who ignore voluntary timelines will seek variances and litigate mandatory ones) was plausible and never cleanly rebutted. PRO also underutilized the word cap across every speech, leaving burdens insufficiently developed, while CON's closing crystallized the clash more sharply despite the Rebuttal 1 clipping cost. Overall, CON delivered a cleaner comparative case: targeted adaptation and buyouts do real work now, a mandate adds legal friction and misallocated resources, and PRO's own answers revealed the mandate's flexibility paradox without resolving it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question B3.1—asking how a "flexible" mandate differs from the voluntary frameworks PRO says are failing, and whether revision clauses simply enable the same deferral—was the most decisive exchange. PRO's A1 answer (external body approving periodic adjustments) was honest but weak, and CON's Rebuttal 2 turned it into a net negative for PRO by showing it just adds bureaucratic years without changing political incentives.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Side A won by successfully dismantling Side B's core mechanism.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 systematically dismantled Side B's mechanism by pointing out that 'flexible mandates' overseen by 'external bodies' just create another layer of bureaucratic delay, directly answering B's pressure questions while turning B's own logic against them.

CON also effectively turned PRO's flexibility claim against it, arguing that irreversible property acquisitions and legal commitments cannot be easily revised as projections improve, while an "external review body" merely adds years of process. The sharpest exchange was CON's Rebuttal 2, which used PRO's own Pressure Question answers to collapse the distinction between mandatory and voluntary frameworks. - **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side A built a durable path to victory on the back of one core move that Side B never fully neutralized: the sacrifice-zone label already exists via private insurance non-renewals, so the CON fear of stigmatizing neighborhoods is describing the status quo, not a consequence of planning. A's closing crystallized this with "the only thing worse than a labeled sacrifice zone is an unlabeled one," which is both rhetorically sharp and argumentatively grounded in everything already on the table.
