### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B built the stronger path to victory by identifying and holding a single decisive structural flaw in the proposition: "AI" is a bad proxy for the real moral concern. This argument was planted in the opening ("too broad and too narrow"), sharpened through Q1 in the pressure round ("if a non-AI system does the same harm, do users lose this right?"), and then fully weaponized when Side A answered Q1 by conceding, "If a non-AI system did the same harm, the principle would still apply." That concession was never recovered from. Side B's closing crystallized it cleanly: if PRO admits AI is not the moral boundary, the motion is misframed. Side B also landed an unanswered dilemma: if disclosure means only an "AI inside" notice, it is symbolic and useless; if it means substantive ranking factors, the gaming and safety costs are real. Side A never resolved this dilemma, and two clipping penalties (Rebuttal 1 and Closing) compounded the execution gap. Side A had genuine strengths—compelling human stakes, the collective-action rebuttal, and memorable rhetoric ("control without transparency is coercion")—but the logical weakness at the heart of the proposition was exposed and never repaired. Side B's pressure questions were the most decisive moment: they forced the concession that broke the motion's framing, while Side A's questions, though reasonable, generated only ordinary defensive responses from Side B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Q1 in the pressure round ("If a non-AI rules system demotes single parents or racially steers users in exactly the same way, do users lose this 'right to know' just because no AI is involved?") was the most decisive exchange. It forced Side A into a concession in Rebuttal 2 ("If a non-AI system did the same harm, the principle would still apply"), which Side B then used in both its second rebuttal and closing to demonstrate that the proposition is misframed around AI as a trigger. That concession became the linchpin of Side B's victory.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2, which capitalized on PRO's answers to the pressure questions to show that PRO had conceded the motion's focus on 'AI' and escalated to demanding full ranking transparency, walking into CON's gaming arguments.
