### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides argued competently, but CON built and sustained the more analytically rigorous case. PRO's normative "duty attaches to the relationship you own" framing was emotionally compelling but never squarely answered CON's collective-action critique: that unilateral refusal shifts revenue and competitive strength to non-refusing rivals while leaving total youth exposure roughly unchanged, and even weakens the refusing league's capacity to invest in youth programs. CON also successfully distinguished the tobacco analogy (uniform legal prohibition removing competitive penalty) from a voluntary, vague "large share" threshold, exposing a real disanalogy PRO never fully closed. PRO's strongest counter—that "own-team association" is a uniquely potent, non-substitutable channel—was asserted repeatedly but not quantified or weighed against the cost side CON kept raising, especially in the closing where CON's "selects against compliant leagues" point went unanswered. CON's Rebuttal 2 was clipped for length, which is a real execution cost, but the substance already delivered (A1/A2 answers plus the content-vs-container distinction) carried the round, and CON's closing cleanly crystallized the decisive comparison. This makes the debate easier to decide in CON's favor, though not by a dominant margin given PRO's coherent normative framing throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) distinguishing tobacco's uniform legal-prohibition context from betting's voluntary, competitively asymmetric threshold, combined with the closing's "the complying league is poorer, the child sees the same exposure" framing, most damaged PRO's central mechanism claim without being adequately neutralized.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by establishing that refusal directly severs the uniquely powerful association between a young fan’s own team and betting. Side B’s displacement argument assumed that rival sponsorship leaves exposure essentially unchanged, but never adequately answered why partial, relationship-specific protection is worthless. Its claims about lost youth investment, regulator delay, and near-total competitive harm were comparatively speculative, while its threshold objection was weakened by A’s point that leagues already use audience data for commercial decisions. Side B remained competitive through its coordination argument, but the clipped rebuttal and overstatement that refusal protects “no one” reduced its force.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer to the substitution objection was decisive: exposure through rival teams or ambient advertising does not recreate endorsement by the child’s own club and heroes. This turned Side B’s demand for coordinated, comprehensive protection into an implausible claim that incomplete protection has no value.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully reframing the debate around the qualitative difference in exposure, arguing that 'own-team association' causes unique psychological harm that ambient rival advertising does not. This cleanly neutralized Side A's strongest point about substitution and competitive disadvantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively answered Side A's Q1 by isolating the specific reduction in harm: severing the emotional fusion between a child's own heroes and betting, even if rival ads persist.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): A's decisive move was distinguishing sponsorship (permanent brand fusion: jersey crests, stadium naming) from mere advertising (timeable, formattable spots), arguing this is why the specific remedy the motion demands—refusing sponsorship deals—cannot be replaced by B's toolkit of timing codes and age gates. B never squarely answered how ad codes or age-gated accounts touch a permanently branded kit or stadium name; B's closing repeated that "timing codes... already isolate the real risks" without confronting that gap. B's consistency argument (energy drinks, combat sports) and workability concerns about "large share" were live and forced A to clarify (qualitative legal thresholds, baseline vs. playoff spikes), but A's answers were adequate and B's uniqueness concession in the pressure round (B4.2, granting gambling "can uniquely engage adolescent reward pathways") handed A rhetorical ground it exploited in Rebuttal 2 and Closing. A's case suffered a real execution cost from repeated clipping across four of five speeches, but the core substantive arguments were delivered before each cutoff, so the comparative case still favors A, tempered to a moderate rather than dominant margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's sponsorship-vs-advertising distinction (A2.3-A2.4, sharpened in A4.3-A4.4) against B's reliance on ad codes/age gates as sufficient remedies—B never explained how timing restrictions or age verification affect a permanent jersey crest or stadium name, leaving this clash unresolved in A's favor.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by distinguishing transaction controls and ordinary ad restrictions from sponsorship’s persistent integration of betting into sports identity. Side B offered legitimate concerns about causation, revenue, and line-drawing, but did not show that its narrower tools address jersey, stadium, and broadcast branding. It also repeated the playoff-fluctuation objection after A proposed season-level audience baselines. A’s repeated clipping and occasional overstatement of concessions narrowed an otherwise clear win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal directly answered both pressures: qualitative thresholds can use stable season-wide demographics, while sponsorship requires refusal because timing and format rules cannot remove embedded branding. B largely asserted that this distinction was invented rather than neutralizing its mechanism.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by drawing a decisive distinction between advertising (which can be restricted by Side A's 'narrow tools') and sponsorship (which structurally embeds gambling into jerseys and stadiums). Side A repeatedly asserted that ad codes are sufficient but failed to explain how they resolve jersey sponsorships, and also ignored Side B's explanation of how baseline metrics solve the threshold issue.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively distinguished between ad codes that govern 30-second spots and the structural permanence of jersey sponsorships.
