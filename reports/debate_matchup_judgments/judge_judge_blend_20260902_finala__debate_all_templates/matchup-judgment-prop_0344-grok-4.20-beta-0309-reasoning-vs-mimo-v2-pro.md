  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 distinction that using digital platforms for anxious students mistakes 'accommodation for growth,' sharply defending the need for unmediated practice.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by demonstrating that PRO's (Side B) case depended on an idealized "screen-free baseline" that does not exist. Instead, it strips away pedagogical guardrails, leaving students to unmanaged digital exposure. CON effectively showed that one-to-one programs enable responsive differentiation and scaffold social participation for quieter students, while schools have a duty to teach digital literacy in supervised settings. PRO's sequence argument and social atrophy claims were coherent but could not overcome the "managed integration versus unmanaged exposure" frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 (A2.1) and its development throughout the debate: the argument that the "screen-free classroom" is a myth because students already possess personal devices, which reframed the debate from "devices versus no devices" to "managed pedagogical integration versus unmanaged exposure." This directly undermined PRO's foundational "wiring window" and "sequence matters" arguments by showing the protected developmental window PRO advocated was already impossible.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): CON wins by successfully problematizing PRO's reliance on a protected, low-distraction baseline. CON also better defends digital collaboration as a scaffold for social growth rather than a replacement, while PRO's low-tech differentiation alternatives felt less responsive to the dynamic needs CON highlighted. Both sides were disciplined and clear, but CON's pragmatic reality check and tighter weighing provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response to the wiring window pressure, arguing that adolescent brains are already wiring within a digital ecosystem and that schools avoiding integration are not protecting attention but abdicating the chance to teach management in a supervised context. This successfully neutralized PRO's core developmental sequence argument and crystallized the reality-versus-idealization clash.
