- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner comparative case on the motion’s own terms (poverty reduced per dollar): direct gap-closing, near-universal reach without leaky chains, lower overhead, and the 2021/2022 pattern even in a tight labor market. Both executed well under format constraints, but A answered pressure more sharply and kept the comparison narrower.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure on the responsive share + closing use of B’s “cannot responsibly assign a share” concession, which undercut the multiplier claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO wins because it framed monthly cash as a superset of childcare subsidies—delivering equivalent or superior poverty reduction for every category of poor families with far lower overhead—while CON never overcame the arithmetic that one childcare slot costs enough to lift multiple families via direct cash. CON’s leverage argument (subsidies unlock earnings) was blunted by its admission that it could not estimate the share of poor families for whom childcare is the binding work constraint, leaving its key multiplier unquantified. The 2021 expansion and 2022 snapback gave PRO concrete empirical support that CON’s accounting-frame explanations failed to neutralize. CON correctly distinguished reach from efficiency, but could not show that concentrated childcare spending converts to more poverty reduction per dollar given high per-family costs, bureaucratic leakage, and a limited responsive population.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s slot-cost arithmetic and superset frame in Rebuttal 2, which showed childcare subsidies are merely cash-with-conditions-plus-bureaucracy for most recipients, and PRO’s exploitation of CON’s admission that it could not estimate the share of poor families facing a binding childcare constraint.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO won the cost-effectiveness comparison. Cash was shown to close poverty gaps directly with low overhead and universal reach across school-age, night-shift, disabled-parent, and rural families, while childcare requires a slot, eligibility, and a labor-market conversion. PRO neutralized CON's fixed-benefit waste claim by noting thresholds scale with family size and depth reduction counts, and matched it with childcare's own deadweight on already-working families. CON's core leverage claim collapsed after pressure Q2 when CON conceded "I cannot responsibly assign a share" [B4.2] for the binding-constraint group, leaving the multiplier speculative. Bucket analysis [A5.3] made the trade clear: childcare equals cash at best, zero where constraint doesn't bind, and only wins where leverage exists in unknown size.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Closing bucket/superset clash: A forced B to admit no share estimate for binding-constraint families [B4.2], then showed childcare = cash face value for already-paying families [B4.3] plus bureaucracy, and zero otherwise, while cash can be spent on care when needed. This turned CON's selectivity-as-virtue into a reach disadvantage.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive round by making the decisive cost-effectiveness metric easier to judge: per public dollar, childcare concentrates on enrollees while A conceded a broad monthly credit sends likely a minority of dollars below the poverty line. A landed real damage on excluded households and on B’s invented one-for-one “floor,” but never escaped the leakage concession or fully forced B to prove the employment multiplier occurs often enough. B’s arithmetic framing, seizure of A’s admissions, and cleaner close outweighed A’s flexibility case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R2/closing use of A’s admission that “likely a minority” of credit dollars reach poor households, turning A’s own per-dollar metric against the proposition.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won because it made the targeting arithmetic decisive under the agreed metric of poverty reduction per public dollar. Side A conceded that under the monthly credit it defended, likely a minority of dollars reach households below the poverty line, and its retreat to a hypothetical steeply targeted design clashed with the actual policy named in the motion. Side B also turned Side A's displacement argument into a floor-at-parity framing that preserved childcare's employment upside as pure surplus. While Side A landed real blows on administrative costs, price capture, and the non-working poor missed by childcare subsidies, Side B's cleaner crystallization—culminating in the "rent, or a way across" distinction—provided the stronger comparative reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's inversion of Side A's displacement argument in Rebuttal 1 [B2.1-B2.2], turning "much subsidy spending replaces private payment" into proof that childcare's floor equals cash's ceiling while preserving employment upside, was the most decisive exchange. It reframed the central comparison and forced Side A into a defensive retreat on targeting that persisted through the closing.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): PRO wins the efficiency comparison. B's powerful floor=ceiling and threshold-lumpiness claim was materially damaged by A's sustained zero-floor counter for unemployed/disabled/night-shift/kin-care poor and by administration/price capture, plus A's fix that incremental refundable expansion can concentrate dollars at the bottom from zero earnings with zero marginal tax on the next poor dollar. B's concessions on double-counting provider wages and reliance on a contingent employment chain left cash's direct, broad gap-closing mechanism as the cleaner per-dollar poverty reducer under equivalent spending.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's attack on the floor=ceiling premise — that a subsidy frees nothing for families with no available/eligible slot or who weren't already paying out-of-pocket unless it contingently creates new employment — combined with administration/price shaving, which forced B into the weak 'dollars aren't spent on non-enrollees' retreat that ignored eligibility exclusion of the deepest poor.
