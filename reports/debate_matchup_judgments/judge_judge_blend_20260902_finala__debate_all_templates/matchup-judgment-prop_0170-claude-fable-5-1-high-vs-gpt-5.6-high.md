### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively winning the framing around the word 'primarily' and grounding the debate in the empirical reality of water usage. By showing that most regions are nowhere near the ceiling of conservation (due to massive leaks and agricultural usage), B successfully positioned A's case as a distant hypothetical and effectively weaponized A's own 'insurance' analogy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal, which hijacked A's 'fire station' analogy to point out that nobody spends *primarily* on insurance premiums, cleanly neutralizing A's reserve-capacity argument.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B wins on the word "primarily." A describes desalination as insurance/reserve capacity and concedes cheap leak repair/reuse should get first funding [A4.1], which B uses to show supplemental cannot be primary. B's cost-per-secure-liter metric, inland agriculture mismatch, reuse as cheaper rainfall-independent source, and stranded-asset examples (Australia idle, Cape Town demand cuts working in months while plants take years) directly undercut PRO's terminal-drought guarantee. A's creation-vs-reallocation frame is strong but never overcomes the conceded point that most regions have not exhausted cheap measures.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R1 exposing PRO's shift to "insurance/reserve capacity" [P2.5-P2.6] as supplemental by definition, rebutting the fire-station analogy with mortgage/idle-plant cost, and arguing the ceiling for conservation is theoretical while cheap gains remain — turning PRO's own hierarchy against primarily investing in desal.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively controlling the definition of "primarily" and weaponizing Side A's own concessions. By forcing Side A to admit that cheaper conservation measures (like leak repair and reuse) should receive funding first, Side B proved that Side A was actually advocating for conservation as the primary investment until a hypothetical ceiling is reached. Side B also effectively neutralized Side A's "guaranteed supply" argument by demonstrating that reallocating a small fraction of agricultural water (which accounts for 70% of withdrawals) provides a faster, cheaper, and more abundant buffer for essential municipal demand than slow-to-build coastal desalination. Side A's reliance on a "terminal arithmetic" that almost no current region faces left their case detached from practical policy prioritization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 and Closing, where B brilliantly trapped Side A by highlighting A's own descriptions of desalination as "insurance" and "reserve capacity," and using A's admission that conservation should be funded "first" to collapse PRO's stance on the motion's core word, "primarily."
