### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by tying liability to platform control, profit, scalable prevention, and the practical impossibility of pursuing anonymous users. Side A persuasively defended fault requirements and warned of over-filtering, but its decisive claim that the proposition necessarily imposed strict liability was too rigid: Side B consistently described shared liability as apportioned according to knowledge, control, and reasonable care. Side B therefore preserved a credible negligence-style policy distinct from automatic liability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal most decisively answered the strict-liability and parody objections: it distinguished shared from automatic liability, limited prevention duties to reasonably knowable copying, and contrasted scalable platform precautions with ineffective user-by-user enforcement. Side A’s existing-law response remained plausible but did not fully answer the incentive and proof problems created by platform-scale ignorance.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by cleanly demonstrating that the motion as written imposes strict liability, which would suppress lawful fair use. Side A effectively neutralizes Side B's best examples by showing that existing contributory infringement doctrine already catches willful blindness and knowing abuse, whereas Side B is forced to defend a "reasonable care" standard that softens the actual proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly answers the pressure questions by explaining that the law already treats willful blindness as knowledge, collapsing Side B's main justification for needing a new standard.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively exploiting the gap between the proposition's plain text (strict liability "when that content infringes") and Side B's softened, fault-based gloss. Side A consistently demonstrated that the motion as written would force over-filtering and destroy fair use, while existing contributory infringement law already covers the willfully blind platforms Side B worries about. Side B's rhetorical flair and analogies were strong, but they ultimately conceded the overbreadth of the motion by smuggling in "reasonable care" and "apportionment" requirements not present in the text, allowing Side A to control the final clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they nailed Side B for adding "reasonable care" and "apportionment" to the motion, exposing that PRO was defending a rewritten, narrower rule rather than the strict liability standard actually proposed.
