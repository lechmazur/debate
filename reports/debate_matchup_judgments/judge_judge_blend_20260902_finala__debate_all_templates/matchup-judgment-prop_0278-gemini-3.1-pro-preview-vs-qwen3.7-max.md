- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): Side A won by successfully anchoring the debate to the motion's actual standard—"least-bad achievable"—and exposing a devastating, unrebutted contradiction in Side B's case. Side A's Q1 in the pressure round asked: if relocating settlers triggers a civil war, why is it more achievable to demand that those same Israelis voluntarily dissolve the Jewish state's demographic majority? Side B never adequately answered this. Their A1 response distinguished "forced evictions" from "civil rights struggle," but this distinction evades the core burden: the Israeli Jewish public that supposedly cannot absorb settler relocation would need to accept the political dissolution of their nation-state, which is at least as politically impossible and arguably more so. Side A hammered this contradiction through Rebuttal 2 and the closing, and it remained live throughout. Side B's achievability attack was the right theoretical frame—the motion does require achievability—but it was fatally undermined by proposing a one-state alternative that is even less achievable than what it attacked. Side B also left its counter-framework structurally vague: "fighting for civic equality" and "paradigm shift" never acquired enough institutional specificity to be weighed against Side A's two-state architecture. By contrast, Side A's responses to the demilitarization and Jerusalem pressure questions were adequate: the PA Security Forces plus international forces model is a plausible structural answer, and invoking the Clinton Parameters framework for Jerusalem is grounded background knowledge rather than invented authority. Side A's false-equivalence rebuttal on Gaza—distinguishing unilateral disengagement from a negotiated comprehensive agreement—was clean and accurate. The margin is clear but not dominant because Side B's demilitarization contradiction point was real and only partially addressed, and the geographic fragmentation argument was substantively sound even if its proposed remedy was underdeveloped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Q1 in the Pressure Questions round, subsequently developed in Rebuttal 2: if relocating West Bank settlers would fracture Israeli society, then demanding those same Israelis voluntarily surrender their demographic majority and dissolve the Jewish state is orders of magnitude more politically impossible—making Side B's "achievable" alternative self-defeating on its own terms. Side B never found a satisfying answer to this asymmetry.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO's core comparative logic held up better across the full exchange. The decisive move was the internal contradiction argument: CON dismisses partition as impossible because Israeli political will is absent, yet their single-state civil-rights alternative demands something even more radical—Israel dissolving its own Jewish-majority statehood and voluntarily enfranchising millions of Palestinians. CON never squared this asymmetry. The South Africa parallel was PRO's to rebut, and PRO's response (Western security integration, nuclear deterrent, absence of the isolation vulnerability that crushed apartheid) adequately narrowed its force without needing fake precision. CON's best work was the diplomatic-alibi / permanent-waiting-room argument—genuinely original and harder to shake—but PRO's answer was solid: dropping the two-state demand removes leverage from Palestinians rather than adding it, since no alternative legally-recognized mechanism compels Israeli enfranchisement. CON's answer to PRO's pressure Q1 ("paradigm shift" unlocks accountability) was the weakest moment in their case; the mechanism was asserted rather than grounded, while PRO's parallel mechanism (land swaps, evacuation precedent, aligned national baselines) remained more specified. CON's physical-vs-political distinction is real and lands on the contiguity sub-question, but PRO absorbed this without conceding the broader achievability comparison. PRO's framing held through the closing: both paths are agonizing, but the two-state model demands mutual compromise whereas CON's demands unilateral capitulation by the stronger party, which has no demonstrated enforcement path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's sustained internal-contradiction argument across A2.4 and A4.3–A4.4: if the same Israeli intransigence that makes settling-evacuation impossible also makes full enfranchisement impossible, CON cannot simultaneously cite Israeli non-compliance to rule out partition while relying on international pressure to force something even harder. CON's South Africa answer was never anchored to a plausible pressure mechanism that accounts for Israel's Western integration, leaving PRO's comparison standing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A wins because it better neutralized CON's core "physical impossibility" route by showing that CON's own single-state mechanism requires even greater impossibility (dissolving Israeli sovereignty via leverage that the debaters agree does not exist), while preserving a live compromise path. CON's South Africa analogy and paradigm-shift answer to pressure questions remained ungrounded and did not damage A's weighing of compromise vs. capitulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.4 directly answered B's pressure questions and exposed that CON's leverage claim has no mechanism once the two-state frame is dropped.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by establishing that the two-state solution is physically unachievable due to permanent demographic and infrastructure facts on the ground, and therefore cannot be the least-bad achievable outcome. CON's core distinction between physical impossibility and political difficulty proved decisive: partition would require the violent uprooting of hundreds of thousands of settlers and the dismantling of billion-dollar infrastructure, whereas a civil-rights framework operates within the existing single-state reality. PRO's land-swap and Gaza-withdrawal counters were insufficient against CON's specific territorial objections and scaling argument (8,000 versus 700,000 settlers). PRO never squarely answered how a contiguous, sovereign Palestinian state could emerge from the current matrix of control, and CON successfully turned PRO's civil-war fears against the demographic engineering that partition would require. CON's "diplomatic alibi" and "phantom limb" reframes held through the exchange, giving them the cleaner live route to victory after the full clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's scaling takedown of PRO's Gaza withdrawal precedent and the resulting physical-versus-political-impossibility distinction, which reframed partition not as a difficult political compromise but as a logistical demolition project requiring the forced extraction of 700,000 entrenched citizens.
