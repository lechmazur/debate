### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but A (CON) constructed and sustained the decisive dilemma of the debate: either the "retained advantage" test has no limiting principle (making liability effectively permanent, as the Yale-charter and "social disadvantage never ends" points showed), or the proposed remedy collapses into ordinary need-based aid once it is made facially neutral to survive legal scrutiny. B (PRO) never fully escaped this trap. In Rebuttal 2, B's attempt to define a "bounded obligation" (ending when "durable funded repair" occurs) was circular, and its retreat to neutral criteria (need, first-gen, neighborhood) conceded the very point A pressed — that repair becomes indistinguishable from aid universities already provide. A's closing crystallized this cleanly ("PRO needs continuity for guilt but not for limits"), while B's closing largely reasserted earlier claims without resolving the tension. B had strong opening framing and good original phrasing, but A's rebuttals did more damage to B's live route to victory and left the debate easier to decide in A's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2/A4's dilemma — "either genuinely reparative and lineage-based (illegal under SFFA) or neutral and indistinguishable from existing need-based aid" — pressed in Rebuttal 1 and sharpened after Pressure Questions, which B's "facially neutral but still repair" answer never cleanly resolved.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): B won by connecting retained institutional advantages to ongoing material duties and showing why acknowledgment alone leaves the relevant distribution unchanged. A raised a meaningful concern about indefinite liability, but its central dilemma falsely treated lawful, targeted, additional aid as either generic charity or an unlawful ancestry proxy. Its repeated SFFA claim was also too categorical.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s answer that repair can use additional place-based, first-generation, and harmed-pipeline commitments most decisively undercut A’s lineage-or-generic-aid dilemma. A exposed some vagueness in B’s endpoint, but did not show why neutral criteria cannot be genuinely reparative when tied to specific institutional harms.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by making the live choice decidable. B’s continuity/retained-advantage frame was morally forceful and well sustained, but A repeatedly forced the decisive collapse: either repair is lineage/proxy-based and legally/ethically unstable after SFFA, or it is need/first-gen/neighborhood aid and therefore rebranded ordinary programming rather than the proposition’s distinctive remedy. A also landed the better limiting-principle attack—prestige, campus, and long social disadvantage supply no clean exit—so B’s “bounded duty” never became operational. B answered pressure more cleanly on design mechanics than on when obligation ends, while A’s fiduciary/mission weighing better matched the motion’s practical burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s false-dilemma collapse of B’s scholarship design (lineage/proxy vs generic rebranded aid), sharpened across Rebuttal 1–2 and Closing.
