### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): CON won by exposing that PRO's plan to prioritize mass immigration as Japan's primary tool depends on a supply of migrants that Japan cannot realistically attract. PRO never overcame the global talent competition problem: every wealthy aging nation wants the same skilled workers, and Japan faces structural disadvantages in language, wages, and licensing. PRO's retreat from "stabilizing" demographics to merely "softening the crunch" conceded that immigration cannot carry the primary load, while CON's "workable beats aspirational" frame crystallized that automation and targeted adaptation align with Japan's actual capacities. PRO's best argument—that payroll contributions require living workers—was blunted by CON's counter that immigrants also face multi-year integration lags before contributing, and that tax structures can capture productivity-based output in the interim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal on global talent competition and scalability [A2.4, A4.6-A4.8], which showed that PRO's call to "prioritize" immigration offered no mechanism to outcompete Anglophone economies for the same scarce skilled workers, reducing PRO's plan to an ungrounded wish rather than a policy.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully pressuring the feasibility and timeline of PRO's immigration model. PRO effectively anchored the clash on Japan's statutory payroll pension system, arguing that only human heads can fund it now. CON neutralized this "immediate contributor" advantage by demonstrating that recruiting, training, and integrating skilled migrants at scale also carries a multi-year lag, mirroring PRO's own timeline objection to fertility policy. More decisively, CON's sustained pressure on the global talent competition exposed PRO's reliance on "prioritization" as an aspirational label rather than a concrete mechanism to overcome Japan's structural disadvantages (language, stagnant wages, licensing) in a bidding war against Anglophone rivals. CON's alternative path—automation-driven productivity, adaptable tax structures, and targeted migration—presented a more workable route that directly answered the motion's burden without assuming away supply constraints. PRO's case was rhetorically vivid but ultimately circular on how Japan actually secures the required inflows.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A2 in Rebuttal 2, which flipped PRO's timeline pressure on fertility policy by showing that mass immigration faces nearly identical recruitment, credentialing, and integration lags before generating payroll taxes at scale. This cleanly neutralized PRO's "immediate fix" advantage and shifted the decisive weighing to feasibility and global supply constraints, where CON held clearer ground.
