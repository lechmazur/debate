### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing two unresolved burdens. A never adequately separated the default's causal effect from coordinator and retrieval infrastructure, and its claim that family consultation prevents ethical harm did not answer B's concern about the unknown wishes of the decedent. A offered a coherent clarity-based defense, but relied too heavily on asserting that uncertain families represent hidden donor preferences. B's clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost, yet its overall comparison remained sharper and better grounded.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's decisive response was that A needs uncertainty to produce the additional organs: the same family hesitation that blocks retrieval under opt-in permits it under presumed consent. This directly challenged A's attempt to claim both a meaningful transplant increase and no added ethical cost, while the point that family assent cannot establish the decedent's preference further weakened A's safeguard.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by systematically dismantling the proposition's conjunctive burden. CON successfully argued that the transplant gains are driven by infrastructure rather than the default (a point PRO struggled to refute after conceding isolated testing is rare). Moreover, CON built an inescapable logical vise on the ethics/distress pillars: if the new default changes family answers, it does so by converting a family's hesitation into state-sanctioned retrieval, meaning the transplant gain and the ethical cost/distress stem from the exact same mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal [A4.3-A4.5] definitively cornered PRO by demonstrating that if soft presumed consent works, every extra organ comes from families who would have otherwise hesitated, meaning the gain is built entirely from ambiguity rather than affirmative consent.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): CON won the triple-burden fight. A showed Spain's rise tracked coordinators not the 1979 law, Wales as B's own soft blueprint underdelivering, and forced B to concede in B4.1 that an isolated default effect "almost never exists." On ethics, A's distinction between foregone benefit vs active wrong and distributional reach, plus the viselike dilemma — if soft enough to keep family veto it moves little, if powerful it harvests hesitant unfamiliar families — was never neutralized. B's majority-preference framing explains a nudge, but A made the decisive clarification that the extra organs are precisely cases of unchanged ignorance scored as yes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4/A5 vise that the gain and the ethical wrong are the same set of cases — same daughter, same ignorance, opposite outcome under opt-in vs opt-out — plus the Wales soft-test and admission of confounded evidence.
