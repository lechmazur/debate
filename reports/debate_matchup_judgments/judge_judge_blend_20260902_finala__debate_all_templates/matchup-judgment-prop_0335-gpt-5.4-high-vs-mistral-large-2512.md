- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won the decisive clash of the debate: whether ordinary fraud law can generate ex ante duties that interrupt harm before the money is gone. CON's counter was "just strengthen existing enforcement," but they never demonstrated the mechanism by which ordinary fraud statutes would require bot disclosure, off-platform friction, or romance-pattern transfer holds. Critically, CON's own Rebuttal 2 answer (A1) effectively conceded the point by saying fraud law "doesn't need" ex ante steps—the exact gap PRO was exposing. PRO's pressure questions were sharper and exploited CON's core weakness; CON's questions, while reasonable, tended to reinforce PRO's framing. The "patchwork" objection was CON's strongest real argument, but PRO flipped it cleanly: role-specific duties are not clutter, they are the only way each actor owns prevention rather than externalizing risk. CON's closing repeated the tagline "fraud is fraud" without resolving the structural prevention question. PRO's closing correctly crystallized that CON never answered what ex ante tool ordinary fraud law provides.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive exchange. CON's pressure question asked how generic fraud stops grooming before the first transfer—and PRO openly conceded it cannot, then used that concession to sharpen their own argument: a framework aimed at proving fraud after loss is structurally too late, which is precisely why a distinct consumer-protection frame with ex ante duties is needed. This turned CON's own question into a winning point for PRO.

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 3 (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins because its core argument held up under the full exchange: the motion rises or falls on whether a romance-specific category unlocks protection that general anti-fraud frameworks cannot deliver, and PRO never demonstrated that. The "machinery vs. costume" crystallization in CON's closing efficiently encapsulated why the romance label does less work than PRO needed it to do.
