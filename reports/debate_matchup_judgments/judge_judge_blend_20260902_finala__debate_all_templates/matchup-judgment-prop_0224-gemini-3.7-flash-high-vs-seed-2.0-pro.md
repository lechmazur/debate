### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): B won by showing that the short window rewards modular deployment and marginal fossil displacement, while new nuclear creates substantial portfolio drag. A established that preventing reactor closures has real value, but its decisive arithmetic depended on unsupported claims that 80% of spending goes to extensions and that those extensions yield six times the displacement. B also correctly challenged A’s assumption that renewables require fully redundant battery capacity to reduce emissions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was its answer that wind and solar need not replicate nuclear’s firm output megawatt-for-megawatt: their generation can immediately displace marginal fossil power. A never squarely answered that challenge and instead relied on blanket queue claims and an ungrounded portfolio ratio.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the motion’s combined nuclear portfolio and per-public-dollar metric central. Life-extension speed, firm capacity, and sunk-cost leverage were never fully neutralized; the 80/20 portfolio weighing (extensions carry even if new-build is weak) repeatedly forced the comparison back onto B. B’s velocity and opportunity-cost attacks were strong on new-builds and outages but treated the proposition as mostly new greenfield and under-quantified against A’s cost/capacity claims. A answered pressure more cleanly and crystallized a simpler reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s portfolio rebuttal (A2.1/A4.2 and closing): even granting new-build near-zero abatement, extensions-dominated spending still beats renewables on the motion’s metric; B never ran competing numbers against that weighting.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by successfully isolating the most favorable part of the motion (life extensions) and building a resilient comparative framework around sunk costs and the "per public dollar" metric. B effectively argued that even if new builds were a total write-off in the 2026-2036 window, the massive cost-leverage of extending existing reactors would still carry the portfolio. Side A made strong points about deployment velocity and the capital drag of new builds, but struggled to overcome B's core economic premise that preserving existing zero-carbon baselines with sunk capital is vastly cheaper per public dollar than building new renewable+storage capacity from scratch. B also effectively used pressure questions to expose A's weak handling of avoided emissions from plant closures.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they compartmentalized new-build delays as a minority portfolio weight, answered the pressure questions directly, and forced Side A to address the real-world transmission interconnection queue backlogs that A had previously ignored.
