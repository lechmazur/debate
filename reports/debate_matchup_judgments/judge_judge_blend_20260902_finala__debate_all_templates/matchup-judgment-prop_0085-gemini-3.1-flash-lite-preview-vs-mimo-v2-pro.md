### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) accumulated a narrow but consistent advantage across three key clashes. First, the "shield" reframe in Rebuttal 1 was the single most effective conceptual move in the debate: it transformed CON's teacher-autonomy argument from a strength into a burden, arguing that a school-wide policy removes teachers from the line of fire rather than constraining them. CON's response ("conflict just shifts") was partially true but never fully dismantled the institutional-backing value PRO identified. Second, PRO's Pressure Q2 — smartphone quality varies, so doesn't relying on personal devices also widen the achievement gap? — was a genuine reversal that muddied CON's strongest resource argument. CON's rebuttal ("creating a guaranteed inequity vs. a potential one") was decent but didn't resolve the tension PRO had introduced. Third, and critically, PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 drew a substantive distinction between formal, institution-managed accommodations (IEPs, 504 plans) and daily instructional judgment calls — the very type of discretion a ban would reduce. CON's A1 in the same round largely ignored this distinction and repeated the "exceptions prove rigidity" point without engaging the actual line PRO drew, which was the most important live answer PRO gave under pressure. CON had real strengths — the resource/access gap argument was consistently live, the edge-cases pressure was creative, and the world-preparation point was never fully answered — but these lived in partial response rather than full refutation of PRO's core narrative. PRO's framing was cleaner throughout: the core question of whether schools should provide institutional structure rather than leave teachers to negotiate individually was argued more coherently and defended more effectively when challenged.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's "shield" reframe in Rebuttal 1: recharacterizing teacher discretion not as professional autonomy but as an administrative burden that leaves individual educators exposed. This set the terms of the debate favorably for PRO and CON never fully neutralized it — "conflict just shifts" acknowledged part of the critique but did not rebut the institutional-backing value PRO identified.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON)): Side A won by making the cleaner comparative case that teacher-by-teacher rules create ambiguity, haggling, and enforcement burdens, while a default school-wide ban creates a clearer baseline for attention. Side B had real offense on educational uses, access gaps, and exceptions, but its exception argument overstated the damage because documented accommodations do not collapse back into ordinary teacher-by-teacher discretion. A also answered the pedagogy/autonomy framing effectively by arguing that phones impose a systemic attention problem teachers should not be individually forced to solve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer that administrative medical or disability exceptions are categorically different from daily classroom negotiations was the key exchange, blunting B’s strongest attack that a default ban becomes unworkably inconsistent.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON)): Side A won by showing a default ban shifts enforcement from subjective daily teacher negotiations to a clear school-wide standard, while preserving necessary accommodations through defined administrative processes rather than classroom discretion. A neutralized B's autonomy objections by demonstrating that individual rules burden teachers unsustainably, and that smartphones' primary function as distraction portals outweighs their occasional utility. B's resource-gap and exception objections were blunted by A's principled distinction between institutional compliance and arbitrary judgment, and by A's compelling structural framing of the proposition. B repeatedly mischaracterized A's accommodation stance as inconsistent, while A maintained cleaner argumentative discipline throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal distinction between documented administrative accommodations and daily subjective teacher discretion, which neutralized B's claim that medical or language exceptions render a default ban unworkable or inconsistently applied.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite Preview (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully framing the default ban as an administrative shield that protects teachers from exhausting, subjective negotiations, rather than a restriction on their professionalism. B's distinction between documented institutional accommodations (IEPs/504s) and daily instructional discretion effectively neutralized A's consistency attack regarding edge cases. While A raised strong practical concerns about resource constraints and the digital access gap, B's principled stance that relying on personal devices obscures school funding duties and creates wealth-based tiers held firm. B's closing crystallized the core tradeoff more cleanly: institutional structure that reclaims attention versus abandoning teachers to fight addictive algorithms alone.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over whether a ban protects or burdens teachers. B's framing of the ban as a "shield" that removes subjective haggling, followed by the sharp distinction in Rebuttal 2 between administrative medical protocols and teacher-led instructional discretion, decisively blunted A's consistency and autonomy attacks.
