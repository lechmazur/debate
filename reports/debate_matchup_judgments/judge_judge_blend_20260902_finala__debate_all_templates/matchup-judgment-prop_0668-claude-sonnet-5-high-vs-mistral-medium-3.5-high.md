### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by converting the debate into a clean comparative test of outcomes for activists rather than pure moral gesture. Its core route—ban severs licensing trails, blacklisting, and leverage while transferring market share to zero-accountability suppliers, and the “poor HR record” trigger is inherently selective realpolitik—survived every round and became decisive. PRO’s strongest cards (documented weaponization of Pegasus/facial recognition and failures of toothless conditionality) landed early but never neutralized the market-shift or selectivity problems; its later claims that pivoting is hard and forensics suffice were undercut by ordinary counter-examples already in the exchange. CON’s later speeches sharpened the leverage vs. zero-leverage weighing while PRO largely restated complicity, making the path to vote for CON clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 (and close) destroying B’s “forensics still work regardless of vendor” answer: technical detection alone cannot restore the sanction/blacklist/sue chain that actually pressures behavior once Western suppliers exit.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by showing the blanket ban would sever the Western accountability chain—licensing, courts, blacklisting, and press scrutiny—that actually creates consequences for abuse, while pushing regimes toward Chinese and Russian vendors who answer to no democratic institution. CON also turned PRO’s concession that the human-rights trigger is “no more arbitrary than existing sanctions” into a decisive critique that the ban becomes selective geopolitical theater rather than principled policy. PRO’s case remained morally resonant but never recovered these two central burdens, and CON’s conditional alternative demonstrated it could protect both activists and legitimate policing where PRO’s ban protected neither.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal demonstrating that PRO’s ban destroys the Western accountability chain that actually deters misuse, and turning PRO’s concession on selective sanctions into proof the ban would be geopolitical theater.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B built a much stronger pragmatic case, demonstrating that a blanket ban severs the accountability chain (licensing, courts, blacklisting) and pushes the market to unaccountable authoritarian vendors, making abuses invisible and unpunishable. Side B also effectively exposed the selectivity of Side A's proposed human-rights trigger, forcing a concession that the ban would likely be applied as realpolitik. Side A's moral arguments about complicity were rhetorically polished but failed to overcome Side B's decisive comparative weighing on actual outcomes for activists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side A's reliance on Citizen Lab forensics by pointing out that detecting an infection after the fact is useless for accountability if the vendor is in Moscow or Shenzhen and beyond the reach of Western courts and sanctions.
