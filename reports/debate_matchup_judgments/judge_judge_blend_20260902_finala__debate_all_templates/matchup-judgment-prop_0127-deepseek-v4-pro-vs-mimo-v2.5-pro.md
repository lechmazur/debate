### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO maintained the cleaner path to victory across the full exchange. The "donate or discount" mechanism did genuine argumentative work: the discount channel uses existing infrastructure, requires no separate cold chain, and converts a disposal cost into at least partial revenue—a point CON never fully neutralized. CON argued mandatory markdowns sometimes destroy value when handling costs exceed recovery, but PRO correctly pressed that even that worst-case outcome is still less costly than paying landfill fees plus bearing unmetered methane and municipal waste externalities. That externality argument—methane, municipal disposal costs, taxpayer-funded food aid—was PRO's most durable live route, and CON consistently sidestepped it in favor of attacking the direct compliance costs to grocers. CON's strongest point was the Good Samaritan liability argument under compelled versus voluntary conduct, which was genuinely novel; PRO's admission that "regulatory clarity" would be needed handed CON a real concession. CON's Q2 (why single out supermarkets?) also created a principled gap, but PRO's chokepoint-and-feasibility answer was adequate: retail is where consumer-ready food concentrates at scale, making it both the highest-impact and most administratively tractable target. CON called PRO's "shared cold-storage" vision wishful thinking, but that characterization was unfair since PRO's actual safety valve was the discount shelf, not charity logistics. Overall, PRO's dual-path framing survived the exchange with its core logic intact, while CON's operational and legal objections were each addressed by one horn of the "or" or the other.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's Q1 (the yogurt pallet problem) was the most decisive exchange. CON's question tried to show the mandate creates an unsolvable capacity crisis. PRO answered that the same pallet that can't reach a food bank today still has the discount channel as a fallback—turning a dumpster run into at least some revenue recovery—and that a mandatory framework gives stores strong incentives to build standing partnerships to avoid last-minute scrambles. This directly neutralized CON's strongest operational objection and reframed who bears the cost in a way CON never convincingly rebutted.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A won because it kept the debate centered on the proposition’s built-in alternative: donation or sharp discount before disposal. B raised strong concerns about logistics, liability, undefined discounts, and compulsory inefficiency, but A gave the cleaner comparative answer: disposal already has private and public costs, discounting uses existing supermarket infrastructure, unsafe food can still be discarded, and supermarkets are a practical chokepoint for regulation. B’s best objections showed the mandate is not costless, but did not outweigh A’s externality and feasibility case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s food-bank-capacity argument was decisive: if donation capacity fails, the discount channel prevents the mandate from becoming a charity-logistics trap. B’s reply that discounts may be inefficient was real but less damaging because it underweighted the avoided disposal and public externality costs.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by maintaining the proposition's core logic across rounds, directly answering B's pressure questions in rebuttal 2 with the discount fallback and chokepoint targeting that neutralizes unpredictability and operational concerns while emphasizing uninternalized externalities like methane and public waste costs. B raises valid points on liability and sector targeting but overstates the "nightmare" and fails to rebut the flexibility of the "or" clause or A’s direct responses, leaving A’s comparison stronger overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's structured answers in Rebuttal 2 to the yogurt pallet and supermarkets-only pressure, using the discount channel as a built-in safety valve to turn disposal cost into recovery.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO built a durable case around a clean market-failure framework and a carefully scoped two-pathway policy (donate or discount). The most decisive structural move was identifying the internal contradiction in CON's case: CON simultaneously claimed voluntary action already works because it serves margins, and that codifying that same behavior would trigger catastrophic costs. CON's response—that selective voluntary action differs from a blanket mandate—was reasonable but never fully resolved why decades of voluntary progress have left food waste at industrial scale. PRO exploited CON's own concession word "selectively" across multiple rounds with increasing effect. PRO's answers to the pressure questions were more direct: the market-failure explanation for why food still gets wasted even under voluntary systems was coherent and grounded, and the observation that infrastructure costs fall on large retailers (not low-income consumers) directly countered CON's strongest humanitarian objection. CON's best argument—the cannibalization dynamic, where deep markdowns train customers to skip full-price purchases—was genuinely original and somewhat underanswered by PRO, but CON never quantified this effect convincingly or showed it would overwhelm the clear per-item recovery value of discounting near-waste items. CON's French counter-claim (fresh offerings reduced, biogas gaming) was asserted with more confidence than the evidence warranted, while PRO's more cautious reliance on France—sector did not collapse, donations increased—held up better under scrutiny. The clipped format notes affected both sides roughly equally, so no decisive asymmetry there.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's "fatal contradiction" rebuttal in Rebuttal 1 [A2.1–A2.2] was the most decisive exchange. By forcing CON to choose between "voluntary action already works so mandates add nothing" and "voluntary action is incomplete so mandates are needed," PRO created a live burden CON never fully escaped. CON's "selective vs. blanket" distinction was the best available answer but still conceded the incompleteness PRO needed, and PRO converted that concession ("selectively") into a running theme through closing.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by making the central market-failure story clearer: supermarkets discard edible food because disposal is frictionless while donation and discounting impose private costs despite large social benefits. B had a plausible cannibalization and logistics objection, but leaned too heavily on speculative catastrophic effects, overstated the mandate as a blanket rule, and repeated weakly grounded claims about France. A better used B's concession that donations happen only selectively to show the voluntary system leaves the core waste problem unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was turning B's "selective" voluntary donation defense into an admission of incompleteness and market failure; B's cannibalization response narrowed the banana example but did not defeat the donation option or the broader social-cost weighing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A built the stronger case by exposing the contradiction between B's voluntary praise and persistent waste, directly resolving pressure questions on selective actions and costs with targeted responses, while B's harm claims remained less responsive to the core market failure. Clips penalized execution equally but did not offset A's clearer clash resolution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answering B's Qs by leveraging the "selectively" concession and differentiating friction costs from systemic harm.
