### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully dismantling Side A's core dichotomy between "securities regulation" and "light supervision." B smartly used A's own examples (forex, commodities) to prove that strong intermediary oversight exists without forcing a misfit securities classification. Furthermore, B capitalized on A's admission that hybrid venues should meet standards "for their securities activities," effectively proving that applying the securities label to the entirety of "most" platforms was an overreach.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized A's strongest point (that securities rules provide necessary platform-level disclosures) by noting that those are generic intermediary duties, not unique justifications for a securities classification.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by forcing a decisive clash on the word "most" and demonstrating that Side A's responses narrowed to targeted rules on security-like activities rather than full platform reclassification, while showing strong tailored oversight could address risks without misfit. B's pressure questions exposed this gap effectively, and later speeches crystallized it without new evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 quoting A's [A4.1] concession to activity-by-activity standards and arguing it abandons the proposition's breadth while highlighting bad fit for non-security trading.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO won by successfully reframing securities regulation as a robust framework for platform-level conduct (custody, solvency, conflicts) rather than just issuer disclosures, directly neutralizing CON’s “bad fit” argument. PRO also dominated the pragmatic weighing, framing CON’s tailored regime as a speculative promise that leaves investors unprotected during years of legislative delay, while effectively arguing that venue-level regulation prevents the classification arbitrage that has historically enabled crypto fraud. CON’s attempt to trap PRO on activity-based regulation was rhetorically sharp but failed to dismantle PRO’s core case that regulating the platform itself is necessary to close loopholes. PRO’s closing crystallized this tradeoff more decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 answer to CON’s Q2, which pivoted from issuer-centric disclosures to platform-level obligations (solvency, custody, conflict controls), effectively defanging CON’s central “category error” claim and showing how existing securities rules directly address failures like FTX.
