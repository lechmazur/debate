### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A won by consistently pressing the structural asymmetry between public and private schools. While Side B offered excellent rhetorical framing around family agency and "who owns the dollars," Side B failed to adequately address Side A's argument that private schools face no mandates to accept high-need or SPED students. This allowed Side A to successfully frame the resource drain as a guaranteed harm to the most vulnerable students that is not offset by unproven academic gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly exposed Side B's failure to answer the pressure question about private school admission mandates, cementing the "cherry-picking" argument as a decisive, unmitigated harm.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): A built a stronger case by anchoring every speech to the proposition's conceded mixed evidence and resource losses, showing how those guarantee net harm to the 90% in public schools (especially marginalized students) without offsetting gains. B's repeated reframes to choice/ownership never neutralized the accountability gap or timing of fixed-cost impacts. A's direct answers in Rebuttal 2 and consistent weighing made the tradeoffs clearer after full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1/A4.2) directly answered B's pressure questions on fixed costs and "rightful funding" while re-centering the cherry-picking and collective-good problems that B never resolved.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): CON successfully anchored the debate in the motion's conceded weaknesses: mixed academic evidence and guaranteed resource loss. By demonstrating that fixed costs, legal mandates for special education and transportation, and selective private-school admissions create immediate, structural harm to the public system serving the vast majority, CON showed that PRO's trade-off was a gamble with no proven upside and severe downside. PRO's family-agency frame was rhetorically powerful but never overcame CON's rebuttals on cherry-picking, accountability gaps, and the impossibility of rapid district consolidation. The weighing favored CON because protecting a stable system serving nine in ten students outweighs expanding an unproven program that structurally undermines it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal dismantling PRO's "competitive pressure" argument by showing private voucher schools face no mandate to serve high-need learners, disclose outcomes, or accept students with disabilities or behavioral challenges—converting PRO's competition narrative into a mechanism of resource extraction that leaves public schools with the costliest students and fewer dollars.
