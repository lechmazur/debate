CON's final speech never directly answered this "no third option" charge despite having the last word, letting a decisive point stand unrebutted. CON also gave a notably vague, non-concrete answer to PRO's direct pressure question about what threshold makes capped borrowing worse than lost output, which was a real execution cost given that PRO explicitly asked for concreteness. PRO's answers to CON's questions, while not perfect, were more responsive and tied back to its core asymmetry argument, making PRO's case easier to follow and harder to dislodge by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing move (B5.3) showing that CON's "pre-authorized contingency fund" alternative collapses into either automatic stabilization or recreates the very delay the policy is meant to solve—left unanswered in CON's final speech despite having the last word.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built the more durable case by isolating the motion's real vulnerability: an unemployment trigger cannot distinguish demand shortfalls from supply constraints or fiscal fragility, and PRO never neutralized this. The decisive moment was the pressure-question exchange: CON's Q2 asked whether the automatic mandate still applies when borrowing costs are already surging, and PRO explicitly answered "yes" (A2 in B4), locking itself into an unconditional commitment that CON then exploited in Rebuttal 2 and the closing ("future tax receipts cannot rescue a government whose refinancing pressure is immediate"). PRO's own answer to CON's Q1 (monetary policy will "counter residual inflation") was flagged by CON as a concession that debt is incurred while the stimulus is offset—an unanswered structural problem. CON also gave a concrete, non-evasive answer to PRO's timing pressure (capped contingency fund, days-long multi-indicator deadline, existing UI as bridge), which blunted PRO's core "speed" advantage without abandoning rapid support. PRO's case remained a strong general Keynesian argument about cascading demand collapse and multiplier effects, but it repeated the same demand-side story across rounds without adapting to the supply-shock and debt-distress counterexamples, and its closing mostly restated rather than resolved that gap. CON's closing crisply reframed the stakes as "option value": calibration preserves the ability to use cash correctly, while automaticity destroys the ability to avoid it when wrong—a cleaner final decision rule than PRO offered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Q2 in Pressure Questions ("does your rule still mandate automatic expansion when borrowing costs are already surging?") forced PRO's explicit "yes" in A2, which CON then used in Rebuttal 2 and the closing to argue PRO's rule is fiscally reckless precisely when it matters most.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's universal claims by distinguishing between supply and demand shocks, and successfully separating baseline unemployment insurance from the proposed marginal expansion. B was forced into a corner during the pressure questions, biting the bullet that governments must automatically borrow even during surging debt crises. A expertly punished this concession, showing B had no mechanism to prove that hypothetical output gains would outrun immediate insolvency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 expertly used B's own answers to the pressure questions (conceding residual inflation and biting the bullet on surging borrowing costs) to prove B's rule was an irresponsible macroeconomic gamble.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B wins on calibration vs autopilot. A’s timing-vs-scarring core was answered by B’s preauthorized contingency fund + rapid multi-indicator assessment that keeps baseline safety nets. A’s answers to pressure conceded key harms — monetary offset for inflation and mandatory borrowing even when costs surge — letting B weigh supply-shock inflation and debt-distress as decisive. B preserved protection without binding to one trigger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 A1/A2: shows rapid preauthorized activation without fresh bill, then uses A’s own “monetary can counter residual inflation” and “yes, still mandate when borrowing surges” as concessions proving autopilot risks debt without guaranteed jobs.
