### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate focused on proportionality and the current excess rather than whether removal is ever legitimate. Side A repeatedly treated B’s narrow concession—that boards may remove genuinely unsuitable titles when lesser remedies fail—as if it defeated the proposition. That misread left B’s central case largely intact: age-banding, relocation, and opt-outs can protect younger students without blanket exclusion, while vague majoritarian standards risk viewpoint-based removals. B’s claims about the current wave could have used more concrete grounding, but A mostly defended legitimate review in principle rather than rebutting overreach in practice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal directly answered both questions, conceded legitimate removal under limited conditions, and then showed why that concession did not answer a motion about whether removals have gone too far. A’s response that the concession made B’s case “collapse” was the decisive failure because it substituted “boards may sometimes remove books” for “the present wave is not excessive.”
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the motion's actual test—whether current challenges/removals have gone too far—alive after conceding that some age-inappropriate titles can be removed. PRO's proportionality frame (placement, restricted sections, opt-outs vs. blanket removal) and repeated demand for a limiting principle against majoritarian overreach damaged CON more than CON's democracy/in loco parentis and "removal is not a ban" replies repaired. CON was competent and exploited the board-authority concession, but too often defended challenges in principle rather than proving the present wave is not excessive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 answers plus B5: concedes legitimate removal then shows CON still lacks a firewall against overbreadth, so CON's rule proves too much.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO held the proposition's core — even conceding some titles are age-inappropriate, the current wave is disproportionate. PRO won the remedy clash: grade-banded shelves, restricted sections, opt-outs and notes protect younger students without blanket removal, and exposed CON's lack of limiting principle to stop a temporary majority labeling LGBTQ+/minority viewpoints "explicit." CON's "not a ban, available elsewhere" and "democratic oversight" collapsed under PRO's class-bias and captive-choice rebuttal and admission that formal process can still be censorial.
