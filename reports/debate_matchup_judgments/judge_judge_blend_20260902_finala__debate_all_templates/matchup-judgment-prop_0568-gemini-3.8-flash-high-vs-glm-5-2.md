### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A won by keeping a narrower, more decideable clash: a certain, concentrated access failure for people who already hold cash versus B’s more diffuse and partly speculative costs, closures, and inclusion bridges. A repeatedly forced comparison on who is left out today (undocumented residents, abuse survivors, non-adopters), answered B’s pressure with the required A1/A2 structure, and crystallized that kiosks/cards are optional private workarounds while the mandate is a public access floor for essentials at major retailers. B landed real pressure on recurring cash logistics, thin margins, and the store-closure downside, but never fully neutralized A’s point that open cashless stores still exclude cash-only buyers and that major retailers already run cash infrastructure. The live route that survived was A’s immediate access guarantee under a deliberately narrow policy, not B’s claim that cheaper automated formats plus cash-to-card bridges are superior substitutes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 weighing of “few cents per transaction” against “100% of the purchase price” for excluded cash holders, paired with the false-binary rejection of forced closures for major retailers that already handle cash.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): PRO won on the narrowed scope - major retailers already have cash infrastructure, so mandate maintains status quo rather than building new. Its weighing of a few cents spread vs 100% exclusion for unbanked, elderly, undocumented proved durable. CON's cost-pass-through, crime, and food-desert/automated-store future were more speculative and answered by existing controls and competition, while CON's own kiosk alternative conceded cash users need a bridge but offered a less guaranteed one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R1/R2 comparison that the real price hike is not marginal overhead but inability to buy food at all, plus framing that closure already gives zero access to cash-only customers, directly damaged CON's regressive-tax and store-closure routes.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by establishing a decisive weighing mechanism: the concentrated, certain harm of total exclusion (100% cost to the unbanked) vastly outweighs the diffused, speculative marginal costs of cash handling. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's store closure and kiosk arguments, pointing out that major retailers already possess cash infrastructure and that private kiosks are not a guaranteed substitute for public mandates. Side B's case relied heavily on speculative closures and late-stage kiosk solutions that failed to overcome A's access guarantee.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled Side B's reverse-ATM kiosk alternative by highlighting that private, optional kiosks can break or be removed, contrasting it sharply with the guaranteed access of a cash mandate, and framing the cost comparison as pennies per transaction versus 100% of the purchase price.
