On the pivotal unsupervised-space clash (hallways, lunch, subs), A pressed repeatedly for a concrete proactive alternative, and B's answer ("situational awareness," "move safely and respect others") stayed vague and never matched A's concrete "shared, invocable norm" mechanism. B's closing reframe ("who owns the norm") was rhetorically sharp but mostly recapitulated rather than resolved the burden-shift argument. A's own closing was clipped for exceeding the word limit, which is a real execution cost, but the substantive case built across rebuttals and pressure answers had already established the more coherent path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Q1/A1 exchange on "phone-present by default": A's claim that discretion leaves phones present unless a teacher actively suppresses them, and B's rejoinder that a teacher's baseline can simply be "phones away," which inadvertently conceded A's patchwork-inconsistency point rather than defeating it.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the decisive clash on the practical baseline rather than abstract trust: absent a schoolwide rule, phones stay present unless each teacher repeatedly manufactures focus, and non-class spaces stay normless.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 A1/A2: short teacher “yes” under default-off is permission, not renegotiating the baseline, while B’s model multiplies suppression work across periods/subs and leaves public spaces without a shared norm.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): B won by establishing that only a schoolwide default can set a coherent baseline in unsupervised spaces and under substitute teachers, whereas teacher-level rules leave predictable gaps.
