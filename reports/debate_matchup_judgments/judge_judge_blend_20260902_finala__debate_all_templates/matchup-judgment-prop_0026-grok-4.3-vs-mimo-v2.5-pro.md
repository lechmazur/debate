### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully pressing the grounding question throughout the debate and executing a decisive turn in Rebuttal 2. The core move was identifying that autonomy is parasitic on dignity: every safeguard PRO claimed for autonomy—verifying genuine consent, rejecting duress, ensuring informed decision-making—imports standards of what persons deserve, which are dignity norms. PRO's own A1/A2 answers supplied the evidence: when PRO said duress "negates genuine autonomy," PRO was implicitly invoking a prior standard of what persons are owed, i.e., dignity. Side B correctly identified this as autonomy borrowing from the very principle it claimed to supersede. Side A never answered the grounding question—why persons have autonomy rights in the first place—and its repeated assertion that autonomy is primary functioned as assertion rather than argument. Side A's practical arguments (dignity claims pressure patients in clinical settings) were genuinely strong and not fully neutralized, and its examples (cancer, dialysis) were concrete and effective. The asymmetry charge in B2.6—that PRO gave dignity its crudest reading while giving autonomy its most sophisticated—was never adequately addressed. Side B was clipped twice, a real execution cost, but the theoretical core survived the clips and was reproduced clearly enough to do decisive work. The vehicle-vs.-foundation framing in the closing was the cleanest crystallization of the key clash. On the comparative question the motion actually poses—which carries *more* weight—Side B's argument that the vehicle cannot outweigh its own foundation is more compelling than Side A's assertion that decision-making authority should simply rest with the patient.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 turning PRO's own pressure-question answers: when PRO explained that duress negates "genuine autonomy," PRO was implicitly relying on a standard of what persons deserve free from coercion—which is a dignity norm, not an autonomy-internal criterion. This move converted PRO's apparently competent answer into a concession that autonomy cannot operate without importing dignity, directly vindicating CON's central claim.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully arguing that autonomy is philosophically parasitic on dignity. Side B effectively used Side A's defense—that autonomy handles exploitation via procedural checks for "duress"—to prove that defining duress requires a prior dignity-based baseline of human worth.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly seized on A's answer regarding duress, pointing out that recognizing and caring about duress requires a prior standard of what people deserve (dignity).
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully collapsing Side A's procedural defense of autonomy. B demonstrated that autonomy's core safeguards (verifying informed consent, identifying duress, rejecting manufactured choice) inherently rely on normative baselines about human worth, making autonomy functionally parasitic on dignity. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): PRO built and sustained a cleaner path to victory across the full exchange. The core structural argument — that dignity inevitably functions as a third-party veto over the person whose body is at stake — was introduced clearly, never neutralized, and sharpened through each round. Three things decided it. First, the eugenics reversal in A2.6 was the most damaging single rebuttal: PRO correctly identified that forced sterilization was an exercise of collective power overriding individual bodies, often justified by dignity-based language, making it autonomy's defeat rather than its consequence. CON's counter in B2.5 and B4.2 — that sterilization programs "began by redefining whose lives possessed sufficient dignity" — partially conceded the point rather than answering it. Second, the "whose authority decides" pressure question (A3.1) went to the structural heart of CON's position, and CON's A1 answer in B4.1 was essentially a restatement of the original claim rather than an identification of any mechanism or constraint. CON never located who resolves the dispute when the patient flatly rejects the dignity framework applied to them. Third, PRO's honest partial concession on BIID in A4.1 — acknowledging autonomy finds it difficult, then correctly pivoting to the comparative claim across the full domain — was both intellectually credible and strategically effective. CON's pressure questions (B3.1 and B3.2) were decent but the answers they received were adequate: autonomy does not compel physician cooperation, and professional discretion plus capacity review handle the edge cases without requiring the structurally dangerous move of declaring the patient's judgment unworthy of respect. CON's closing was noticeably shorter and weaker, repeating prior claims rather than crystallizing the clash. The clipped turns are a modest execution cost for PRO, but the arguments that survived were consistently stronger than CON's unrebutted core vulnerability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's reversal of the eugenics argument in A2.6: correctly identifying that forced sterilization programs used state power to override individual bodies, frequently justified by appeals to collective or population dignity, making them a demonstration of autonomy's defeat rather than a cautionary example of autonomy run amok. CON never adequately answered this, and its attempt in B2.5 to say sterilization began by "redefining whose lives possessed sufficient dignity" actually illustrated PRO's point about dignity being manipulable by whoever holds authority.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side A won by relentlessly pressing the pragmatic question of who gets to interpret "objective" dignity. Side B failed to adequately answer this, relying on the assertion that dignity is a self-evident moral fact. Side A also handled Side B's pressure questions beautifully, conceding the BIID edge case to win the broader rule and cleanly distinguishing a patient's right to choose from a mandate for doctors to comply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 successfully neutralized Side B's strongest pressure by explaining that autonomy empowers the patient without compelling the physician, and conceding the BIID edge case while pointing out that autonomy better handles the vast majority of medical decisions.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): PRO wins by successfully framing dignity as an unaccountable third-party veto and forcing CON onto the defensive regarding interpretive authority. PRO’s practical weighing (consent, capacity, professional opt-outs) resolved CON’s pressure questions cleanly, while CON’s reliance on abstract “objective status” failed to answer who decides when patient and institution clash. PRO’s strategic concession on edge cases correctly narrowed the debate to general moral weight, securing a clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s R2 directly answers CON’s pressure on BIID and physician coercion by distinguishing patient rights from medical mandates (opt-outs) and conceding the edge case without ceding the broader weighing, while simultaneously exposing CON’s evasion of the “whose dignity prevails?” question. This exchange crystallized the structural advantage of autonomy.
