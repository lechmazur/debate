### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B built a tighter, more internally consistent case: a moderate "floor not ceiling" framing, a clear trap between blank-check and blanket-exclusion positions, and repeated exploitation of a real contradiction in A's opening (successful religious schools already meet secular academic standards, so why frame the floor as destructive?). B's Pressure Questions forced A to either name an acceptable standard or default to unconditional funding, and A's answer in A4.1 (accepting fire safety and financial transparency) let B close by showing A had already conceded the state may attach conditions, collapsing the "forced assimilation" framing to a dispute over where the line sits rather than whether one exists. A's strongest material—the "comparable equals identical secular curriculum" reading and the vague European defunding claim—was asserted repeatedly across speeches but never textually grounded, and B directly rebutted it each time (theology alongside science keeps funding; only doctrine mislabeled as science is barred). A's mosque analogy and hypocrisy points landed initially but were effectively neutralized by B's "funded service vs. faith" distinction. Both sides executed the Q&A format correctly, but B's questions did more real work exposing an unresolved burden, and B's answers to A's questions were more precise and directly responsive. B's closing crystallized the debate's decisive move; A's closing largely re-asserted prior claims without resolving the conceded contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's [B2.1]–[B2.2], pointing out that A's opening simultaneously claimed religious schools already outperform public schools academically and that the motion's standards would destroy their core identity — a contradiction B kept pressing through the Pressure Questions and final closing, and which A never fully resolved.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a tighter, more strategic case around the motion's own text. Its pressure question forced PRO into a genuine dilemma: either "comparable to public schools" is honored literally (secularizing hiring/curriculum) or PRO's actual answer (faith-based hiring allowed for "religious roles") is a carve-out that concedes the standard isn't truly comparable. CON exploited this concession relentlessly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, and PRO never recaptured the word "comparable" or offered a clean alternative reading that would neutralize the dilemma. CON's second question — who judges whether minority doctrine counts as "teaching inferiority" — also landed hard; PRO's answer ("the same authorities... decide, and schools that refuse forfeit funding") essentially confirmed CON's "majoritarian tribunal over doctrine" framing rather than defusing it. CON also offered an underdeveloped-but-real alternative (funding follows the child, analogous to religious hospitals/universities) and an equity point (poor families lose access while rich families buy religious schooling) that PRO dismissed as a "category error" without substantively engaging the voucher-style mechanism. PRO's core principle — public money shouldn't fund discrimination — remained emotionally resonant throughout, and its opening was solid, but its rebuttals grew repetitive and its Rebuttal 2 was clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost. Overall CON progressed more sharply across rounds, converted pressure into decisive leverage, and closed with the cleaner, more memorable framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question forcing PRO to choose between "comparable" hiring standards (secularization) or carve-outs (hollowing the motion), followed by CON's Rebuttal 2/Closing exploitation of PRO's carve-out answer as a de facto amendment to the proposition.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully turning the text of the proposition ("comparable to public schools") into a structural trap that Side B fell into. By forcing Side B to concede that funded religious schools would retain religious roles and a religious ethos, Side A proved that Side B was defending a carve-out rather than true comparability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 weaponized Side B's answers to the pressure questions, devastatingly pointing out that since public schools have no religious roles, Side B had essentially conceded the standard was not comparable.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON won the central textual clash. PRO's core principle that public money shouldn't subsidize discrimination remained live, but CON's pressure question Q1 in B3 forced the dilemma: either "comparable to public schools" bans faith-preference hiring and secularizes, or the carve-out PRO conceded in A4.1 makes the motion hollow. PRO's clipped Rebuttal 2 admitted religious roles plus ethos, and never reclaimed the word "comparable," validating CON's charge of amendment. On the second clash, PRO answered that the state decides what counts as teaching inferiority and defunders dissenters, confirming CON's tribunal worry. With execution cost from clipping and no clean answer to the two-tier reversal, CON had the clearer live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 teacher-preference dilemma - showing PRO's "yes for religious roles, no for core subjects" concedes public schools have no religious roles, so the standard as written is either secularizing or rewritten to be hollow.
