### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully proving that financial dependency intrinsically causes self-censorship, making explicit coercion unnecessary. Side B also landed a decisive logical blow by pointing out that Side A's alternative—banning secret veto clauses—is impossible to enforce without the mandatory disclosure Side A opposes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing argument that you cannot police NDAs or secret veto clauses without mandatory reporting effectively dismantled Side A's reliance on targeted enforcement.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO wins by defining dependency itself as the coercion that undermines independence and showing that CON's alternative leaves hidden influence undiscoverable. CON's strongest line—replacement funding for capped programs—was answered directly by PRO's argument that an 80-percent-funded center is already a branch office, not independent scholarship, and that survival without a patron is the test of real autonomy. CON never squarely answered PRO's pressure question about how journalists or faculty discover NDA-bound veto clauses without mandatory disclosure, retreating instead to the claim that the system "self-corrected." That gap undermined CON's targeted-enforcement alternative. Additionally, CON's closing was clipped for exceeding the word limit, leaving its final weighing incomplete. PRO progressed cleanly across rounds, distinguishing disclosure as low-cost transparency and caps as anti-dependency levers that only bite where concentration exists, while CON's attempt to collapse the motion into a "Norway equals North Korea" strawman was effectively rebutted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that ITAR, EAR, and CFIUS govern technology transfers and equity stakes but do nothing to stop a dean from accepting a foreign ministry's veto over speakers or curricula, combined with the pressure question exposing that voluntary reporting cannot uncover NDA-hidden contract terms—CON never recovered from this gap in its targeted-enforcement alternative.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built a more robust and logically consistent case for the proposition. PRO successfully defended the uniform cap by explaining it only impacts structural dependency (where one funder exceeds the threshold) while leaving benign, dispersed grants untouched, and effectively argued that uniform rules avoid the politicization of targeted enforcement. When pressed on the collateral damage of defunding heavily dependent programs, PRO bit the bullet and convincingly reframed the clash: a program overwhelmingly funded by a foreign ministry lacks academic independence by definition, so shrinking it protects the university's integrity. Side A (CON) landed strong points on the practical costs of compliance and the loss of scholarship, but struggled to explain how targeted contract review could work without baseline disclosure to overcome NDAs, and failed to answer how to break structural dependency without politicized targeting. Side A's clipped closing was a minor execution cost but did not alter the outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to Side A's pressure question about replacement funding for capped programs. By asserting that a closed propaganda arm is more honest than an open one pretending to be scholarship, and defining independence as the ability to lose a funder, PRO effectively neutralized CON's strongest practical attack and reclaimed the normative high ground on the motion's core metric.
