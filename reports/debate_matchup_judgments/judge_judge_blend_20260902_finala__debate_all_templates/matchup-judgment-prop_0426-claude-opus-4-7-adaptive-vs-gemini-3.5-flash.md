### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): A won by making the comparative decision clearer: the marginal dollar should reach people when and where burnout or isolation actually hits. B had a plausible structural frame, especially on predictable time off, but leaned too heavily on labeling apps as cosmetic and gatherings as restorative without adequately answering A's exclusion, timing, and scalability objections. A successfully reframed serious wellness platforms as continuous, clinically grounded, and at least partly systemic through training and analytics, while showing that B's gatherings are episodic and design-dependent and that time off can compress work rather than solve it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was the answer to B's structural-versus-band-aid framing: A argued that B's tools do not reliably cap workload or reach isolated workers, while apps uniquely provide immediate support and can include systemic levers. That undercut B's central contrast.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): B built the stronger case by consistently showing that continuous, accessible tools reach more employees in crisis than episodic structural interventions, directly answering pressure on exclusion and timing while exposing PRO's over-reliance on idealized design assumptions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.4-2.7 and B4.1-4.4 on scalability/exclusion, which neutralized A's structural claims with practical reach comparisons.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by building a cleaner comparative frame that better survived the exchange. PRO's central dichotomy—structural interventions versus individualized digital band-aids—was effectively dismantled when CON showed that predictable time off does not necessarily cap workloads but merely compresses them, and that serious wellness platforms include system-level tools such as manager training and workload analytics. This reframed the debate around reach and shape of suffering: burnout and isolation strike continuously and unpredictably across distributed workforces, making episodic, geographically narrow gatherings and fixed PTO ill-suited to match the harm. PRO never adequately answered the exclusion problem for caregivers, remote hires, and night-shift workers, and their retreat to "localized and tailored" gatherings conceded the scalability issue. CON's pressure questions exposed real burdens about workload compression and exclusion, and PRO's answers did not fully resolve them. CON's closing crystallized the decisive clash—match the spending to the shape of the suffering—while PRO's closing largely repeated the structural framing without repairing the leaks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 2's collapse of PRO's "structural vs band-aid" framework by arguing predictable time off compresses rather than caps workload, and that labeling one intervention structural and the other cosmetic is branding rather than analysis.
