### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases and executed the pressure-question format properly, but A's line of attack held up better under scrutiny. A's central claim—that upfront, population-scale drug costs outrun narrow, delayed medical offsets within a five-year window—was reinforced by a plausible cost/uptake calculation and, crucially, A turned B's "discontinuation caps exposure" point into a liability rather than a mitigator: patients who quit early still generate wasted spend with no lasting benefit, a point B never squarely answered. B's rebuttals repeatedly asserted that marker improvements "arrive within months" and "sever" costly event cascades, but this stayed largely qualitative and didn't engage A's specific claim that fixed organ damage and hardware (dialysis trajectories, joint replacement) limit how much near-term events can actually be avoided. Overall, A gave the cleaner, more internally consistent account of why costs would outrun savings inside the window, while B's optimistic mechanism, though plausible, left key vulnerabilities from the pressure round unresolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turning B's "discontinuation caps costs" argument around—showing that early discontinuers still generate wasted spend with negligible lasting benefit—was the most decisive exchange, since B never directly neutralized this reframing in the closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively winning the arithmetic of the prompt's 5-year window. Side A successfully weaponized the 50% discontinuation rate, proving that while it 'caps exposure' (as B argued), it still results in substantial uncompensated upfront costs that drag down the net savings of the entire cohort.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 handling of B's discontinuation argument (A4.2) decisively showed why partial courses are pure uncompensated cost, neutralizing B's attempt to frame dropouts as a mathematical advantage.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q2 exposed B's tension on multi-year costing vs high discontinuation [A3.2-B4.2]; B's answer that discontinued courses are upfront waste and continuers drive persistent costs preserved CON's net-increase ledger and left PRO's high-utilizer savings claim under-quantified.
