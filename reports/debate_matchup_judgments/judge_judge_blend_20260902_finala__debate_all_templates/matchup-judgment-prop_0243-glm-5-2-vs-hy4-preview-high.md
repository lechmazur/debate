- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): The decisive clash was over the counterfactual: what trip did ride-hail actually displace? PRO's rebuttals repeatedly and correctly pressed that in the specific dense, transit-rich cities the debate names (Manhattan, SF Loop, Chicago), the realistic counterfactual is often transit or walking, not a parking-hunting car — and CON's own [A4.3] response conceded "this answer defeats PRO only if transit and walk loss dominate," essentially admitting the vulnerability without closing it. CON's answer to PRO's first pressure question was also evasive, pivoting to "burden isn't mine" rather than substantively engaging the arithmetic PRO asked for, which the judging criteria treat as a real cost. PRO's rebuttal 2 answered pressure questions more directly (A1/A2) and kept pressing the same unresolved crux. PRO's closing was clipped for exceeding the word limit, which is a genuine execution penalty and slightly narrows the gap, but the substantive trajectory of the debate — PRO successfully identifying and sustaining pressure on CON's key unproven assumption — gives PRO the more convincing path to victory overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question 2 ("If the rider instead came from a train or sidewalk, what parking-circling mile is actually eliminated?") followed by CON's partial concession in Rebuttal 2 that the displacement argument "defeats PRO only if transit and walk loss dominate" — this exchange exposed the load-bearing assumption in CON's case that was never fully secured.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by showing that B's congestion savings depended heavily on ride-hail replacing private cars that would circle for parking. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases—PRO's deadheading/substitution mechanism was vivid and consistently applied, while CON's counterfactual framing and offsetting-mechanisms strategy directly targeted the motion's demand for a net verdict. The decisive turn came late: PRO's hedged concession that "often around a third" of core trips came from sustainable modes was meant to anchor its case, but CON's final closing weaponized that same number, showing that if the remaining two-thirds were taxi/for-hire replacements, PRO's own logic (taxis already cruised) makes those trips miles-neutral rather than additive, collapsing PRO's "worse overall" claim to only a fraction of trips. Because CON had the last word and this reductio went unanswered, it left the debate on CON's terms: PRO never supplied a full net accounting, only a mechanism operating on part of the trip mix. PRO's case remained strong on mechanism but leaned on unsourced "survey" framing for its key empirical share, a mild grounding weakness CON avoided by staying in hedged, non-numeric language throughout. This narrow but real edge, combined with CON's more disciplined evidence use and the last-mover advantage in a closely argued debate, tips the result to CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's final closing (A5.1–A5.3), turning PRO's own "roughly a third" concession into a reductio—arguing the other two-thirds are either parking-search-offsetting (personal cars) or miles-neutral (taxis), per PRO's own logic—leaving only a fraction of trips as clear net additions and denying PRO the "worse overall" verdict it needed.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. It offered the stronger marginal mechanism: ride-hail adds deadheading and pickup friction, and trips shifted from transit, walking, biking, or no trip are unambiguously additive. Side B raised legitimate uncertainty about substitution shares and parking offsets, but vehicle reuse and reduced fleet ownership did not directly establish fewer vehicle-miles. Its closing arithmetic also faltered: if a meaningful share is additive and many taxi substitutions are neutral, that supports worsening unless the claimed offsets are shown to outweigh it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s closing answer to Side B’s two-thirds argument was decisive. It separated taxi substitutions, which provide no parking-search benefit, from personal-car substitutions and showed that Side B had not demonstrated enough congestion-reducing trips to outweigh the clearly additive sustainable-mode and induced trips.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by making “worse overall” require a real net counterfactual and then exploiting PRO’s own modal-share concession.
