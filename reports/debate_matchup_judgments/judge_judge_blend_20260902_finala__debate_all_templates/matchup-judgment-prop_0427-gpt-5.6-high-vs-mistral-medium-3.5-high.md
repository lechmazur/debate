- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully proving that loneliness and violence require substantive relational solutions rather than just navigational instructions.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the midnight cyberbullying example demonstrated that offline relationships provide an enduring safety net (someone to message), while Side A's mute button only stops a notification but leaves the victim alone.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): PRO won by making the decisive comparison clearer: loneliness and violence stem from missing durable relationships, roles, and supervision, which funded programs can supply directly while literacy mostly equips teens to manage an absent or hostile environment. CON repeatedly overclaimed teen power to “dismantle/rebuild/rewire” platforms via mute/report, and treated access barriers as disqualifying while treating classroom delivery as effectively universal. PRO answered pressure more cleanly (funding removes barriers without guaranteeing perfection; location of harm ≠ best site of protection) and used the motion’s “than campaigns alone” language better. CON stayed alive on digital primacy but never neutralized the capacity gap PRO kept pressing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 (esp. A4.3–A4.5): even if digital is primary site, literacy cannot create loyal friends/trusted adults or stop platform incentives; midnight cyberbullying mute helps less than offline belonging already in place.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): A won by demonstrating that loneliness and violence are rooted in missing relationships, roles, and belonging, not merely in misunderstanding digital platforms. A sharply turned B's accessibility critique into a case for public funding to remove barriers, and established the decisive asymmetry that funded programs can incorporate digital guidance while literacy cannot manufacture embodied community, mentorship, or nonviolent status. B repeatedly asserted that digital spaces are the primary engine of harm, but never overcame A's argument that knowing about a harmful environment is a weaker remedy than creating a healthier alternative. A also neutralized B's midnight-cyberbullying example by showing that offline belonging continues to protect after hours, whereas a mute button does not reduce isolation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal turning the accessibility objection into a pro-funding argument and establishing the information-versus-infrastructure asymmetry: clubs can include digital literacy, but literacy cannot generate repeated contact, mentorship, or earned belonging.
