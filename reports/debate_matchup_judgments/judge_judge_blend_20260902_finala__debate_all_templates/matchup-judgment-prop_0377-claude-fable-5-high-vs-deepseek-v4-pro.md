### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's case by turning B's own mechanisms into concessions. When B argued that dynastic wealth is deployed early through trusts or acts as a backstop for parental spending, A correctly pointed out this proves lifetime resource deployment is the true driver of unequal opportunity, rendering the death transfer a mere "trophy ceremony."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal, which seized on Side B's admission that advantages are deployed "long before the will is read" to prove that lifetime income deployment is the actual live threat.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully framed the debate around timing and mechanism, showing that the decisive advantages (elite schooling, tutoring, internships) are purchased during childhood with parental income, while inheritance typically arrives decades later. PRO's attempt to link childhood spending to the "expectation" of dynastic wealth remained unsupported and was directly contradicted by CON's observation that high-earning professionals without fortunes already dominate the positional arms race. By demonstrating that PRO's proposed remedy progressively shifted toward taxing lifetime gifts and income flows, CON undermined the motion's core comparative claim. PRO never satisfactorily answered CON's empirical challenge to name a jurisdiction where aggressive estate taxes dismantled dynasties, and the "silent engine" theory collapsed under its own speculation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 showing that every harm PRO identified (trusts, tuition, internships) was actually a lifetime income deployment, not an inheritance-at-death phenomenon, and that the "expectation effect" was unsupported psychology—culminating in "You don't tax a psychology."
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully trapping Side B (PRO) on a timing and mechanism dilemma. CON's core frame—that opportunity is rigged during childhood via income deployment, while inheritance arrives decades later—forced PRO into a concession trap. When PRO argued that trusts and gifts deploy advantage early, CON sharply noted that these are lifetime transfers funded by income, meaning a tax at death misses the harm. When PRO expanded the policy to a "unified" tax catching lifetime gifts, CON correctly argued this abandons the motion's focus on inheritance and concedes that lifetime flows are the actual threat. PRO's "silent engine/backstop" theory was plausible but relied on speculative behavioral claims that CON effectively rebutted with the grounded "two-surgeon household" example. CON's rhetoric was crisper, their weighing was consistently comparative, and they cleanly crystallized why PRO's own mechanics undermined the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 masterfully turned PRO's mechanism against them. By highlighting that PRO's own examples (trusts at birth, tuition checks, internship subsidies) occur during the parent's life and are funded by income or lifetime gifts, CON proved that PRO was either defending a tax that arrives too late to matter or quietly rewriting the motion into a tax on lifetime resource deployment. This dilemma neutralized PRO's comparative burden and decided the round.
