### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by showing that imperfect accreditation can still enforce escrow, insurance, market access, and adult liability without denying the child recognition. B powerfully exposed the weakness in A's original passport threat, but then conceded that adult-focused sanctions can bite under prohibition, which also supports enforcing conditional legality. B established likely leakage and some volume reduction, but not that a ban protects residual surrogates better than a regulated legal channel.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned the child-hostage dilemma. A retreated from denying entry and proposed nonautomatic parentage plus adult and agency penalties; B showed this weakened the price-deterrence claim, but B's own reliance on fines and broker liability confirmed that enforcement can bite without stranding the newborn.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully showed that the motion’s core condition—enforceable standards—cannot be satisfied in a cross-border context. PRO’s parentage-lever enforcement was caught in a decisive dilemma: either it denies recognition and strands a child (the Baby Manji outcome PRO invoked), or it blinks and grants a slower route, which CON demonstrated undermines deterrence and channels demand to cheaper, unaccredited clinics. PRO’s “slow lane” concession in Rebuttal 2 that “the child comes home” gutted the passport threat, and CON crystallized this by showing the motion fails on its own terms: a condition that cannot be met is permission, not protection. PRO’s verification mechanism was also effectively rebutted as paperwork-versus-practice, with consulates verifying stamps, not clinic conduct. PRO’s closing risk-comparison was strong, but CON’s framing that prohibition at least does not lend moral cover to the trade—and narrows rather than licenses the market—held up. The organ-sale analogy provided a coherent alternative framework, and CON’s sharper progression on the enforceability clash gave the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s “toothless or cruel” dilemma dismantling PRO’s parentage lever: showing that denying recognition either strands a newborn or, once PRO conceded “the child comes home” through a slow adversarial route, becomes an empty threat that price-sensitive clients will ignore—reviving the cost-driven migration to unaccredited clinics.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully defending a plausible, home-state-based enforcement mechanism (escrow, insurance, parentage fast-lanes) and decisively attacking Side B's prohibition model. While Side B posed a sharp dilemma regarding the passport lever (strand the baby or blink), Side A's pivot to a "fast lane vs. slow lane" customs-style friction was plausible. The decisive clash occurred when Side A forced Side B to admit that prohibition offers no contractual or legal mechanism for surrogates already in the trade. Side A's closing crystallization of the risk asymmetry—concrete protections for actual women versus hypothetical protections for "never recruited" women in a black market Side B conceded merely relocates—provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it forces Side B to admit prohibition offers no mechanism for surrogates already in the trade, and then weighs the concrete protections of regulated surrogacy against the hypothetical "never recruited" women of a relocating black market.
