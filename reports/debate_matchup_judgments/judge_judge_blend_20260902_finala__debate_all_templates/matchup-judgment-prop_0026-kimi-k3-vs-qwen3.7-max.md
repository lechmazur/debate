### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B built a coherent, testable framework (determinacy, historical track record, error asymmetry) and sustained it under pressure, most notably by biting the bullet on the healthy-limb amputation hypothetical rather than dodging it, and by exposing that A's "objective" dignity standard ultimately reduces to "tribunals decide," never escaping the very indeterminacy critique B opened with. A raised genuinely hard cases (organ sale, incapacitated patients, mutilation) that forced real engagement, and its incapacitated-patient line was the strongest pressure point in the debate, but A's own pressure-question answers (A4.1-A4.2) mostly asserted conclusions ("this doesn't reach abortion because it doesn't commodify") without rebutting B's historical counter-examples or identifying a harm independent of a worth-theory, which was exactly what B's Q2 demanded. B's closing sharpened this into a clean, memorable line ("blank check with a signature line... who applies it? Tribunals"), while A's closing mostly restated its framework rather than answering that specific charge. The clash was live and substantive throughout, but B controlled it more decisively in the second half.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to A's amputation question (A2 in Turn 7), openly conceding the harm is real but arguing the patient's will determines which harm she bears (via the mastectomy analogy), combined with B's closing point that A never named a decision-maker or standard beyond "tribunals" — this pairing exposed the structural gap in CON's case that was never repaired.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): Side A won by making the comparison decisively about who may authorize bodily intervention. Side B's appeal to boards and courts applying objective integrity standards then reinforced A's concern that dignity becomes a tribunal-supplied veto.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by successfully exposing Side A's "dignity" standard as a blank check for third-party tribunals to override patient wishes.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the capacity pressure question (A1), sharply differentiating the factual, patient-centered assessment of capacity from the moral, judge-centered assessment of dignity.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but CON (A) built and sustained the more decisive structural argument: that autonomy's moral force is itself borrowed from dignity, so dignity cannot be outweighed by what it grounds. CON repeatedly turned PRO's own vocabulary ("profound violation," "captive subject," "active moral authorship") against them, showing PRO could not explain why overriding a choice matters without smuggling in dignity-laden concepts. The most damaging moment was CON's Rebuttal 2 catch that PRO's mechanism/content distinction (capacity vs. dignity) collapsed the instant PRO grounded autonomy's weight in "active moral authorship" — itself a content judgment. PRO never answered this in the closing, instead repeating the shield/sword framing without repair. PRO's fork argument in Rebuttal 1 (alignment = redundancy, conflict = tyranny) was a strong opening gambit, but PRO failed to press it further after CON's counter, leaving it live but unexploited. CON's closing cleverly co-opted PRO's own "shield" metaphor, sharpening the final comparison, while PRO's closing mostly restated position without confronting CON's strongest unanswered point. This gives CON a clear, if competitive, edge on rebuttal quality, originality, and rhetorical closure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 point that PRO's "capacity = mechanism, dignity = content" distinction self-destructs because PRO's own justification for autonomy's weight ("active moral authorship") is itself a content-based claim — a contradiction PRO never resolved in the closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B built the stronger comparative case: dignity explains why autonomy matters, covers patients unable to choose, and distinguishes meaningful agency from exploitation. B also showed that A repeatedly grounded autonomy in moral authorship, intrinsic worth, and protection from objectification—all concepts B plausibly identified as dignity. A never adequately answered B's patient-indexed account of dignity and instead kept reducing dignity to observers' aesthetic discomfort despite B's explicit distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive. It answered the forced-feeding challenge by distinguishing patient-indexed dignity from counterfeit appeals to observers' comfort, then turned A's own language about authorship and objectification into evidence that autonomy derives its moral force from dignity.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by successfully subsuming Side B's entire case. A persuasively argued that autonomy is merely procedural and derives all its moral weight from the intrinsic worth of the person (dignity). When B tried to defend autonomy's importance by citing 'moral authorship' and the wrongness of 'reducing someone to an object,' A sharply pointed out that B was relying entirely on dignity's vocabulary to generate impacts, decisively winning the core weighing of the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.1 and A4.3), which beautifully contrasted living organ donation with organ markets to show why autonomy needs dignity's filter, and then exposed how B's defense of autonomy relied entirely on dignity's vocabulary.
