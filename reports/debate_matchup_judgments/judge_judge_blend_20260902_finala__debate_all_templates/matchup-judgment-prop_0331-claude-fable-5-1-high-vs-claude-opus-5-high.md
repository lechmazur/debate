### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won narrowly by showing that cash-out is not necessary for the central harm: children still stake real money under casino-like reinforcement and chase unequal outcomes. Side A powerfully exposed verification costs, overbreadth, and the availability of ex ante consumer rules, but never fully explained why minors should retain access to the randomized transaction itself once the conditioning mechanism was conceded. Side B also plausibly distinguished digital loot boxes from physical collectibles through unlimited, frictionless repurchasing. Side A's two clipped turns were a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's answer to the cash-out argument was decisive: it shifted the focus from resale value to repeated spending and variable-ratio conditioning, then distinguished physical card packs through digital loot boxes' low friction and unlimited redraws. Side A exposed definitional overreach but did not neutralize that harm comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON consistently cornered PRO into structural dilemmas. When PRO tried to distinguish physical trading cards from loot boxes using "friction" and "delivery," CON successfully framed these as design variables suited for consumer law. The debate was decided on the feasibility of the regulation: PRO argued their gambling age-gate could simply rely on the "stated age" of a platform account, allowing CON to devastatingly point out that actual gambling regulators do not accept self-declared ages. This trapped PRO between an ineffective checkbox and the extreme identity-verification regime they had just denied wanting.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A1) decisively dismantled PRO's proposed age-verification mechanism, proving that a "stated age" fails the standard of actual gambling law.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a highly effective trap around the practical implications of "gambling regulation," specifically the requirement for positive ID verification rather than mere self-declared age. Side A also successfully exposed Side B's shifting definition of gambling (moving from stake-chance-prize to friction, then to tiers/near-misses) to avoid capturing physical blind-box toys. Side A's closing crystallized the verification dilemma perfectly, securing a clear victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1), where it dismantled Side B's reliance on platform "stated age" by pointing out that gambling regulators require positive proof of adulthood, creating an inescapable dilemma for Side B's enforcement mechanism.
