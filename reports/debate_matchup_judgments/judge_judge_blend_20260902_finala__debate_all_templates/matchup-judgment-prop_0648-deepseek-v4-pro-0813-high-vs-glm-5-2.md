### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO won by decisively establishing the structural talent and currency deficits of standing military units, which CON never fully neutralized. PRO successfully parried CON's main definitional attack (that keeping a command cadre violates the motion) by drawing a persuasive analogy to conventional military reserves, proving that a country can shift its primary operational reliance to reserves while maintaining a standing command skeleton.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1 and A4.5) dismantled CON's supplementation/substitution dilemma by pointing out that every reserve system has a permanent command and administrative staff, exposing the absurdity of CON's standard that any standing element equates to "relying on standing units."
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): CON wins by exposing that PRO cannot satisfy the substitutionist burden of the motion. PRO’s civilian-reserve model retains a standing command cadre for continuous watch, incident authority, and lawful command. CON successfully framed this not as trivial overhead but as the essential operational core of cyber defense, showing that reserves supply only episodic, borrowed capacity for a continuous threat. PRO’s talent/currency/cost advantages remain live but accrue to a supplemental architecture, not the “rather than” replacement the motion requires. CON sharpened this into a clean dilemma in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, while PRO’s attempt to minimize the standing core as a mere “skeleton” never overcame CON’s domain-specific point that in continuous cyber operations, the skeleton is the muscle.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 (A4) and Closing (A5), which crystallized the decisive dilemma: either the standing cadre retains command, watch, and continuous authority—in which case the reserve has not replaced standing units—or the reserve lacks assured command, making it a talent pool rather than a primary force. This framing directly answered PRO’s pressure questions and made the supplementation vs. substitution clash unambiguous.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live path that CON never neutralized: standing units cannot recruit/retain/currency-refresh at scale, so reliance fails on talent and breadth. CON's strongest route - assured command and continuous 24/7 watch - was substantially answered by legal activation obligations and by PRO's force-vs-cadre distinction that reframes "rely on" to mean the bulk operational workforce. CON's definitional move that any standing skeleton equals reliance on standing units was implausible and made substitution impossible by fiat. Execution costs from clipped openings affected both but cost CON more early, and PRO still crystallized substitution at the practitioner layer for defensive surge, which is the bulk of national need.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1 rotation-as-assured-availability vs A2.2 rotatable availability - CON turned PRO's availability attack by showing a watchbill is taskable any hour while a reservist on the books may be legally bound but practically blocked by employer conflict; PRO's reply in A4.3-A4.4 about episodic activation only partly recovered.
