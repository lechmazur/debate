### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the gap between defending an individual choice and denying that widespread childlessness can be a social problem. Side A’s claim that no opt-out rate changes the analysis effectively abandoned the motion’s societal clause, while its taxes-and-wages response never fully answered B’s point that money cannot supply workers who were never born. B also directly answered both pressure questions and crystallized the intergenerational timing mechanism more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was its answer that a new child first supports adults already alive, while that child’s later needs fall to a subsequent generation. This directly answered A’s worker-plus-pensioner “package” objection and preserved B’s claim that opting out can shift replacement costs onto parents and their children.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won by decisively dismantling Side A's 'ledger' defense. Side A argued that declining to have a child avoids adding another net claimant to the system, and that paid taxes fund future care. Side B systematically neutralized this by pointing out the generational offset: the childless adult already exists as a future claim, meaning they need a younger worker to offset themselves, and money cannot conjure a caregiver if someone else hasn't borne the cost of raising one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to Side A's 'package' argument (that a child is both a future worker and dependent) was masterful: 'Parents add a taxpayer for today's elderly; the child's own old age is paid by the generation after... Refusing the package is refusing to staff your own claim, not declining a drain.'
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a clean two-track path: selfishness requires wronging an existing person, which non-creation does not, and the "problem" framing fails because fixes are institutional not natal. CON's relay/commons case is powerful but was damaged by the timing reply that each child is both future worker and future pensioner/claimaint, and by conceding no forced births [B1.8], leaving only stigma as the tool to solve it - exactly what A framed as conscripting intimacy. CON's rate pressure [B3.1] landed, but A's individual-vs-demographic distinction plus net-contributor reciprocity answered the more important pressure about being a free-rider.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's tax-reciprocity rebuttal [A2.2]/[A4.3] - childless pay for schools and buy care, they don't draft "someone else's children" - directly undercut B's core extraction claim [B1.2][B2.3] and was never fully neutralized.
