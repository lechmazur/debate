### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by making the decisive comparison clearer: Japan’s worst shortages are local, language-bound, and rural, while mass immigration mainly adds metro demand and cannot be tethered there. PRO kept a live case on payroll/consumption bases and human care needs, but never neutralized CON’s placement, capacity-first risk, and permanent-residence mobility counters. After the full exchange, CON’s selective residual-plus-automation path looked lower-risk and better targeted than PRO’s scale-first re-foundation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 / closing use of B’s own rural permanent-residence plan: durable status is mobility to cities, while machines stay on the ward.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON wins because it exposed a structural flaw in PRO's rural revitalization argument and offered a lower-risk alternative that better targets Japan's actual shortages. PRO successfully showed that automation alone does not pay payroll taxes or keep local institutions solvent, but CON countered that the realistic package—automation, higher domestic participation, service consolidation, and selective migration—can manage decline without the massive integration gamble PRO requires. The decisive blow was CON's rebuttal that permanent residence is not a tether; once rural-sponsored workers gain status, they are free to move to Tokyo, while telemedicine and lift equipment remain in the ward. PRO never recovered from this, ultimately conceding that immigration is "not a perfect tether" but failing to show why mass immigration is still the safer bet. CON's framing of welfare as targeted, per-capita presence rather than aggregate headcount provided the cleaner reason to vote against prioritizing significantly more immigration.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal point that permanent residence is not a tether but freedom to move to Tokyo, undermining PRO's claim that immigration reliably staffs and revitalizes rural areas.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON wins the decisive distribution clash. PRO establishes a macro fiscal gap but CON neutralizes it with taxable productivity, higher female/older participation, and consolidation, then turns PRO's rural revival story: immigrants cluster in metros and permanent residence frees a rural-sponsored nurse to leave for Tokyo, while lift equipment, remote monitoring, and telemedicine stay in the ward [A4.3]. CON's tens-of-thousands targeted model answers both pressure questions directly and offers lower-risk, better-targeted coverage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Turn 8 A4.3 — PR is freedom to move to Tokyo, not a tether; the machine does not move. It exposes PRO's regional-visa mechanism as unable to keep clinics open where decline is worst.
