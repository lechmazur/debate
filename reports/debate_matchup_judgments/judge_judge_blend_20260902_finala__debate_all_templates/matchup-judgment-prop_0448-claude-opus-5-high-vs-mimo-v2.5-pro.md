### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by making the relevant comparison marginal rather than utopian: even a capped visa gives some refugees a safe route and moves some processing away from dangerous crossings. Side B identified real capacity, duplication, and political risks, but repeatedly assumed without sufficient support that rejected applicants would still cross and that backlash would outweigh the concrete gains. Side A answered the pressure questions directly, while Side B's repeated clipping and unsupported precision modestly weakened its case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned whether the visa substitutes for or supplements border processing. Side A candidly conceded supplementation, then showed that this still produces marginal reductions in dangerous journeys and emergency processing. That answer undercut Side B's claim that anything short of full replacement defeats the policy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO defended a capped, imperfect safe channel as a strict improvement over a status quo where the only path to asylum is illegal and often deadly. CON landed strong systemic objections—two parallel systems, political backlash, and scalability—but could not show that anyone is concretely worse off under the proposal, while PRO successfully framed CON’s case as prioritizing speculative political forecasts over certain drownings. PRO’s reframe of rationing-by-drowning versus honest queues, and the crystallizing line that nobody drowns in a consulate queue, gave the cleaner comparative reason to vote for the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s challenge for CON to name a single person made worse off by a refusal, and the accompanying reframe that a consulate provides accurate signals while the status quo outsources rationing to smugglers and the sea. This exchange blunted CON’s moral-hazard and systemic-backlash arguments by shifting the burden to concrete harm.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by identifying political willingness to admit refugees, rather than the absence of an application channel, as the binding constraint. A established that even a rationed visa could save some lives, but never adequately answered how successful applications would translate into actual admissions when member states have resisted binding distribution. B also persuasively argued that processing refusals could legitimize harsher treatment of later irregular arrivals. A's repeated clipping and unsupported assumptions about approval rates and future eligibility further weakened its execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal decisively answered A's qualified-majority mechanism: the prior relocation scheme was adopted and legally upheld yet still faced noncompliance, showing that formal voting authority does not solve practical willingness. B also reversed A's immediacy claim by contrasting lengthy EU implementation with unilateral resettlement expansion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by proving that the binding constraint on safe refugee admission is state willingness, not the absence of a legal channel, and showing that a unified EU visa would fail against that constraint. The 2015 QMV relocation precedent—adopted by majority vote, upheld in court, and simply ignored by member states—demolished PRO's claim that EU institutional mechanisms could enforce participation. CON further exposed unaddressed harms: rejected applicants would be stranded with sunk costs and harmful paper trails, while the visa would give governments a moral alibi to harden borders and legitimize pushbacks. PRO's ethical urgency and smuggling-reduction theory were never able to overcome these feasibility gaps and perverse effects, and CON's alternative of incrementally expanding resettlement emerged as the only plan that actually puts specific vulnerable people on planes without requiring fantasy-level solidarity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's dismantling of PRO's institutional feasibility claim using the 2015 relocation scheme—adopted by qualified majority, upheld in Luxembourg, and flatly ignored by Hungary and Slovakia—which proved that voting rules do not create actual admission obligations and left PRO's unified visa as either a paper right that would teach refugees to wait for fulfillment that never arrives, or a disguised voluntary scheme that resettlement already provides.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON wins the decisive clash on the binding constraint. PRO argues any legal channel beats zero, but CON shows the visa is not a free add-on: it relocates applicants to transit, creates a rejection paper trail, and gives interior ministries the alibi "you had a safe route" to legitimize pushbacks and harden the sea. On feasibility, PRO's QMV answer fails because B proves QMV was already used in 2015 relocation and was simply not complied with; a right to apply is not a duty to admit. PRO never answers why voluntary resettlement's 1-2% failure is solved by an instrument that depends on even more solidarity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 three mechanisms why a rejected visa applicant is worse off — sunk-cost relocation to transit, pre-screening-out paper trail, and moral alibi for non-rescue — plus the QMV non-compliance example that collapses PRO's enforceability claim.
