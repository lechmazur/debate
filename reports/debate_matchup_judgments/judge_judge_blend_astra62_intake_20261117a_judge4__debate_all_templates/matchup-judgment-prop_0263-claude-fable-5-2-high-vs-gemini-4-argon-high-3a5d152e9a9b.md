### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won a close, high-quality debate. A's opening frame (deletion of exclusives = erasure, not scheduling) was never broken, and A's remedy explicitly permitted sale or relicensing, which absorbed CON's most sympathetic scenario. In Rebuttal 1 A turned B's own cost list into a confession: the fees B cited are payments to creators, making deletion the "off switch on creator pay." B's best weapon — recasting A's "priced in at greenlight" as a chilling-effect concession — was blunted by A's stronger analytical points that studios already gamble entire production budgets and that the risky-originals boom predated the write-off wave. B's ZIRP answer was plausible but left the chilling effect speculative, while A's harms (permanent loss, subscriber bait-and-switch) were certain and concrete. B's worst moment was the subscriber remedy of "canceling a month-to-month membership," which A correctly exposed as a concession that the show is simply gone; B never recovered. B's textual argument that the motion's "licensing reasons" clause forbids relicensing was strained against A's natural public-availability reading, and B's unanswered dark-window friction was too narrow to carry the round. A's clipped Rebuttal 2 is a minor execution cost, but it still opened with proper A1/A2 answers and completed its key weighing. Both sides argued cleanly without fake sourcing; A simply made the decisive clash easier to decide and won the weighing battle: bounded, foreseeable cost versus unmanageable, permanent loss.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The pivotal exchange was B2.3–B2.5 (conditional union fees as deliberate risk-sharing, plus the "priced in at greenlight" concession claim) against A4.3–A4.7, where A reversed it: if fees are conditional on hosting, deletion is the switch that cuts creator pay after the work is done, and moving that cost to the greenlight table makes it visible and bargained — while studios that risk full budgets are not undone by a fixed fractional tail.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): A wins a competitive debate by making the opportunity-cost comparison stick. B established genuine access harms and successfully defended availability through other distributors. But A corrected the claim that removal evades already-owed compensation, answered the tax argument, and explained how guaranteed carrying obligations affect marginal commissions. B conceded some lost greenlights without adequately overcoming A’s point that these represent irreversible cultural and employment losses too. A’s repeated misreading of the licensing option and exaggerated claims about prepayment and escrow limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing directly undermined B’s “cost versus loss” distinction: once B conceded that some borderline projects would be rejected, A could identify lost works and creator wages on its side as well. This crystallized an opportunity-cost objection that B’s emphasis on predictable, bounded obligations never fully answered.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the central clash clearer: a bounded, foreseeable carrying cost versus the irreversible disappearance of finished originals. B effectively answered the licensing objection by distinguishing platform hosting from continued public availability, and it exposed A’s subscriber remedy—canceling membership—as conceding the loss. A’s chilling-effect case was plausible and rhetorically strong, but remained more speculative and depended on an aggressive reading of the motion that B adequately rebutted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal distinguishing public availability from platform-specific hosting blunted A’s licensing contradiction argument, while B’s follow-up pressure on the deleted subscriber left A with only “cancel membership,” which B turned into a concession of irretrievable loss.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B built the cleaner route to victory. The "redundant when the market works, coercive when it does not" dichotomy survived the exchange, and B's answer to A's sharpest pressure question (the double standard on priced-in costs) held: a bargained exit fee and a compulsory multi-year publishing duty are genuinely different liabilities. Most decisively, B turned A's own amortization argument — if the write-down windfall dies after year five, conditioning the write-down kills the motive on day one for every title, including licensed originals A exempted, without conscripting anyone — and exposed the year-six residual-severance loophole A never closed. A fought well, with a strong double-standard pressure round, a plausible three-to-five-year mechanism, and a late escalation on union residual waivers, but its labor-protection story was undercut by its own storefront answer (residuals only when consumers buy) and B's flat observation that bargained removal payments compensate workers regardless of viewership.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal: turning A's concession that the tax incentive dies once book value amortizes [A4.2] into the argument that conditioning the write-down achieves A's goal on day one, for all titles, without a publishing mandate — then adding that the residual-severance motive A cited in its own opening never amortizes, leaving the vault lawful in year six.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A wins a competitive debate. B established the access problem and showed that rights reversion cannot guarantee distribution. However, A better distinguished targeted tax and compensation remedies from compulsory ongoing exhibition costs, while defending its forward-looking commissioning-risk argument. B’s claim that three to five years permanently prevents disappearance relied on overconfident accounting assumptions. A also exposed the continuing residual-saving incentive after the window expires, undermining B’s claimed permanent solution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Against B’s sharp question about both policies increasing costs, A conceded some pricing-in but distinguished negotiated exit payments and rights reversion from mandatory clearance obligations. B correctly challenged the description “open-ended,” but did not establish that these different obligations impose the “exact same cost.”
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A narrowly wins by turning PRO’s own accounting explanation into the decisive point: if early purges are driven by unamortized-book-value write-downs, conditioning write-downs on release or reversion targets the motive with less burden than a compulsory multi-year availability mandate. A also gave a cleaner answer to PRO’s price-in pressure by distinguishing bounded exit fees or tax conditions from an ongoing publication duty. B’s labor and creator-burden attacks were strong, but they did not fully defeat A’s narrower-remedy route or resolve the residual-motive problem after the minimum window expires.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal used PRO’s three-to-five-year amortization admission to argue that write-down conditioning cures the purge motive immediately, while separating fixed removal payments from PRO’s open-ended duty to keep publishing and maintain clearances.
