### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly. B's empirical pressure that A had shown non-collapse rather than a measured transplant increase was strong, but A rebuilt a causal path through the modal family: no objection, no paperwork, no certainty. In that common case, opt-in converts uncertainty into refusal while deemed consent converts it into donation, without overriding objectors. A also exposed an asymmetry in B's ledger by forcing the waiting-list patient's harm into the comparison. B's distress and acquiescence worries remained speculative, while A's soft-default/family-veto structure plausibly addressed the no-added-harm clause.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's cleanest hit was that non-collapse is not a measurable increase. A answered it by refocusing on the modal family mechanism: same grief, same knowledge, different outcome, generating extra transplants without overrides. That kept A's causal route alive.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A (CON) won a razor-thin debate by holding B to the motion's two promises. On the causal prong, B never produced a measured increase in completed transplants — the strongest offer was consent conversion that "has not collapsed" and no known system where transplants fell, and A's "non-collapse is not the motion" landed squarely. B's fallback (the law and its publicity/complement package are inseparable, and no RCT can exist) was clever weighing but arguably conceded that the default alone cannot be generalized to "most patients." On the harm prong, A's burden reversal was effective: the motion promises no increase in violations or distress, and B offered a plausible mechanism (rebutting a presumption is lighter than authorizing) plus the guessing-symmetry argument, but no measurement, while correctly noting A's distress claim was also unmeasured — yet the burden sits with PRO. B was more original (the modal-family story, the dialysis-patient ledger turn, "a refusal nobody felt able to authorise") and rhetorically superior, but A's asymmetry-of-errors answer and consistent burden framing kept both prongs unresolved in B's favor. A's clipped Rebuttal 2 was a minor execution cost but its substance mostly landed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's [B4.3] modal-family argument ("a donation nobody opposed... needs nobody overruled") against A's [A4.6]-[A4.7] response that "a donation nobody opposed may be a donation nobody felt able to oppose" and that B had downgraded both promises — measurable gains to absence of decline, and no added distress to an unmeasured assertion. A's burden-reversal there neutralized B's strongest route to victory.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): CON won on the motion as written. PRO offered a sharp mechanism and a memorable “which way should the law guess?” frame, but never showed that the legal meaning of silence itself measurably raises completed transplants for most patients, and then downgraded that promise to non-collapse while folding publicity and coordinators into the reform. CON kept both burdens live: confounded systems do not isolate the default, and silence-as-authorization is not ethically the same error as leaving the body intact. Dialysis deaths supply urgency, not that demonstration. PRO was rhetorically stronger and the clipped rebuttal costs CON, but not enough to erase the unmet conjunction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal, holding that “has not collapsed” is not an increase and that treating publicity and coordinator changes as inseparable from the law blocks any claim that the default itself works for most patients.
