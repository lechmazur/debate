### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A better met the motion's burden: reducing damaging viral demand, not making places undiscoverable. It directly answered the loophole and jurisdiction questions, while its residential-lane critique exposed how B's vehicle restrictions and conduct fines leave pedestrian volume unresolved. B offered credible arguments about legal warnings, deterrence, and navigation, but repeatedly treated surviving directions as making limits useless. Its closing also treated promotion limits as replacing physical protection, although A had explicitly defended combining them. A's incremental, upstream protection case therefore survived the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to the caption loophole preserved its central mechanism: reducing one-tap convenience can discourage impulse visits without preventing every determined visitor. B identified genuine workarounds but did not establish that they erased this benefit. A then sharpened the comparison by showing that ticketing blocked doorways addresses misconduct without necessarily reducing damaging crowd volume.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger comparative case: viral harm depends on cumulative volume, so reducing one-tap discovery and commercial amplification can mitigate damage without stopping every determined visitor. A raised serious loopholes involving captions, comments, organic creators, and enforcement, but B answered them directly and exposed inconsistencies in A’s claims about platform cooperation and visitor behavior. A’s spillover and warning-based alternatives remained plausible, yet its closing overstated B’s position as rejecting physical management and used incorrect reference IDs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal decisively paired A’s earlier claim that municipalities could not secure platform changes with its later reliance on platform warnings and rideshare blocks, then contrasted supposedly relentless trespassers with visitors supposedly stopped by those same blocks.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by consistently framing pin limits as friction to reduce casual viral volume, not perfect secrecy or censorship. CON's spillover and caption loopholes were answered by the convenience-versus-determination distinction, while PRO exposed CON's reliance on the same platform cooperation CON denied, and showed patrol-only protection leaves fragile sites exposed between sweeps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 contradiction: CON claimed towns cannot make platforms alter pins, then relied on platforms for Sold Out flags, transit redirects, and rideshare blocks, conceding the cooperation PRO's policy needs.
