### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): A built the stronger path by separating portable credentials from unspent training accounts, then using PRO's own admissions against it. PRO conceded that a worker leaving after one month would take the full account, which A convincingly reframed as a recruiting bounty likely to reduce future training. PRO's claim that portability would spur employer investment was ungrounded, while A's alternatives—vesting and pooled levies—were more plausible and better tied to PRO's own examples.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's pressure on the one-month rule. PRO admitted full portability after one month, and A turned that into a poaching bounty: Hospital B could poach staff by promising to honor their old balances, undermining PRO's claim that portability would expand training.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): A won by cleanly separating portable credentials from unspent employer-funded balances, then using B’s admission that even a one-month employee receives the full account to expose the windfall and recruiting-bounty problem. A also turned B’s compensation analogies toward vesting and consistently explained why firms might stop creating training balances. B identified a genuine lock-in and immediacy concern, but relied on unsupported claims about portable systems increasing training and never neutralized A’s incentive mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, corrected the signing-bonus analogy, and combined B’s one-month concession with the unspent-balance contradiction to show why portability could produce a one-off transfer at the cost of future training.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by separating already-portable credentials from unspent employer funds, then forcing PRO to defend transferring a full account after one month. That concession destroyed PRO's earned-compensation frame and left its claim that firms would train more as assertion against B's clear incentive mechanism and pooled-levy alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: signing bonuses commonly have clawbacks, one month does not earn a full training budget, and vesting preserves accrued fairness without creating a transferable recruiting bounty firms will avoid funding.
