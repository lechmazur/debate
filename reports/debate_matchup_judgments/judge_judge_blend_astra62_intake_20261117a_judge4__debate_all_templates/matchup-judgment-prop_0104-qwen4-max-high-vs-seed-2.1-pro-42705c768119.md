### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by narrowing the debate to the decisive categories and forcing honest weighing. B built a clear causal mechanism (impulsive youth suicide, curious young children, family-sourced school weapons) and repeatedly pressed the suicide category, which A dodged for two rounds before conceding in Rebuttal 2 that blocked teens would substitute an "equally lethal" method — a factual and strategic blunder B demolished in closing by noting firearms are uniquely lethal and delay itself is the intervention. A's burden case (DV survivors, high-crime households, renters) was vivid and consistent but became repetitive, never quantified defensive losses against child-access deaths despite B's direct challenge, and leaned on brittle specifics (10–20 second delays, screwdriver attacks, permanent disabilities from misdemeanors) presented with more certainty than a closed-book debate supports. B's rhetoric ("inconvenience against a coffin," "waiting is the intervention") made the stakes memorable without substituting for argument. A's closing was mostly recap and the "real laws vs. model law" framing, which B had already answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing turn on A's method-substitution concession: A claimed a blocked teen would substitute an "equally lethal" method; B countered that firearms are the one method that is not equally lethal, meaning the lock's value lies precisely in changing what a teen reaches for — converting A's own answer into proof of the benefit. B's Q1 lockbox contradiction also did durable damage that A only partially repaired.

B’s unsupported youth-suicide majority claim, minimization of potentially serious defensive costs, and premature accusation that A ignored questions limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing directly attacks A’s claim that blocked teenagers simply substitute equally lethal methods: substitution can still reduce fatalities when alternative methods are less lethal. This preserves the suicide-prevention mechanism rather than assuming a lock must prevent every attempt to be beneficial.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by consistently turning Side B's own concessions into contradictions. A caught the benefit estimate shrinking from "thousands" to "hundreds," exposed the seatbelt/pool-fence analogy's key flaw (storage is unverifiable before harm), showed PRO needs delay to be decisive for suicide but negligible for home defense, and landed the sharpest blow in Rebuttal 2: PRO defended the burglary benefit by requiring a bolted safe while pricing the burden at a cheap lockbox — "one law cannot be priced at both." When pressed for the metric the motion's "more than" demands, PRO answered "categorically outweighed," which A correctly noted would justify a ban as easily as a lockbox and therefore never tested this law. B's strongest surviving route — irreversible child deaths versus trivial one-time costs, with the suicide-impulse-delay mechanism — kept the debate competitive, and B answered pressure questions directly with proper A1/A2 format. But B never resolved the verifiability problem, the teen-who-knows-the-code point, or the safe-price tension, and B's opening statistic (three out of four) folded most youth deaths into the storage gap when homicides away from home dominate, a factual overreach A exploited on the merits. B also relied on an overstated theft-tracing claim. A's rebuttals were surgical, cautious, and grounded; B's were competent but increasingly reassertive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.7): PRO defended the burglary benefit by requiring bolted, installed safes while simultaneously calling the burden a trivial cheap lockbox — exposing that PRO's benefit and cost estimates rely on two different laws. This pricing contradiction, combined with the refusal to supply a metric for "more than," was the decisive exchange that B's closing never answered beyond reasserting the asymmetry.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): A wins by exposing inconsistencies in B's benefit-burden comparison. B offered plausible child-access and suicide-prevention mechanisms, but its unsupported prevention totals shifted, and its hardware assumptions changed while burdens remained supposedly trivial. A also pressed the important distinction between safer storage and additional compliance caused by legislation. A overstated the enforcement objection and unnecessarily demanded a numerical ratio; qualitative weighing could suffice. Nevertheless, B never adequately reconciled its expanding benefits with its minimized costs, while A developed sharper comparative pressure across the rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest rebuttal targeted B's response about theft: free trigger locks and inexpensive portable boxes supported the low-cost claim, but bolted storage supported the anti-theft claim. A exposed that mismatch directly. B continued calling the burden trivial without explaining whether its inexpensive compliant options delivered the theft-prevention benefits it claimed.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): A won by turning the motion’s comparative requirement into the decisive clash and exposing unresolved tensions in B’s case: prevention depended on robust storage while minimal burdens depended on cheap, portable devices; delay was decisive against youths but negligible for defenders; and “categorically outweighed” avoided the requested weighing. B presented a plausible child-safety mechanism, but relied on several unsupported empirical claims and largely repeated its asymmetry assertion rather than answering A’s enforcement and comparison challenges.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both pressure questions, distinguished checkable car-seat and pool-fence compliance from unobservable home storage, and caught B raising the required burden from free locks or cheap lockboxes to installed, bolted safes while still calling the cost trivial.
