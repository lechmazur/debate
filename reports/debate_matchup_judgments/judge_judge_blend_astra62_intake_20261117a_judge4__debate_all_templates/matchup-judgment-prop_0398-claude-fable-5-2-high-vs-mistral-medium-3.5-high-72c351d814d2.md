### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) engineered and executed a decisive trap. CON never escaped this contradiction, merely repeating "arbitrary discrimination" and asserting without mechanism that a national pool clears at the most expensive city's rate. B's secondary moves were also strong: the value-vs-price distinction [B4.3], the hardship-allowance analogy, and the observation that only the HQ-band rule pays for where someone *used* to live. CON's speeches grew repetitive and failed to resolve the live clash. B loses some ground for three clipped turns and CON loses little to format, but the argumentative gap is wide: B made the debate easy to decide; A left the central burden unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5-B5.3: B's Boise-engineer trap. CON's own Ohio concession plus its "pay them the same" answer meant the mover must receive the Boise rate—CON accidentally endorsed the motion, and its closing attempt to distinguish "inherently local" roles from national remote pools only re-triggered B's unanswered clearing-price argument.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): B wins by distinguishing unchanged contribution from market compensation and challenging why a national talent pool must preserve headquarters rates. B’s sweeping market claims, overstated Ohio analogy, and clipped turns limit the margin, but its comparative case and pressure handling were substantially stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B correctly answered A’s claim that location adjustment would pay two otherwise identical Boise engineers differently because of their origins: adjustment uses current location, not residential history. A repeated this misdescription in its closing rather than resolving the challenge.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making location adjustment the coherent market rule and forcing Side A into a trap it never cleanly escaped. The two-Boise-engineers question then exposed the real choice: equal pay where the work is done implies a local rate, while a headquarters band pays for a city the mover left. A kept a usable equal-contribution intuition and a national-talent distinction, but misread B as origin-based pay, never specified the rate, and repeated that strawman after B corrected it. Clipped turns cost B some polish, not the comparative win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal and close on the Boise pair: value is not price, adjustment pays neighbors for where they are, and only the headquarters-band rule preserves a legacy premium.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by turning Side B's own words into the decisive clash. A's central question—who has a claim on the savings from a move?—was never seriously answered. B conceded the company "gains nothing from the relocation," that the employer "didn't earn the reduction," and that the cut "doesn't directly lower rents," and A hammered these concessions across every round with clean comparison. B's remaining case (local labor markets, internal equity, sustainability) was largely repetitive and A dismantled it: the firm was already paying the salary, competition for skill blocks a race to the bottom, and internal equity arguments point toward raising underpaid peers, not cutting the newcomer. B's bidirectional-adjustment answer was A's strongest moment to exploit, and A did. B did answer pressure questions in proper A1/A2 format and kept a coherent market-coherence story, so this was not a blowout. A's execution cost: three turns were clipped for length, though the visible portions still carried the argument. B relied on restating the local-market frame rather than resolving A's windfall-ownership and identical-output attacks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2: A4.4–A4.5 caught B's concession that the employer "didn't earn the reduction" and that the cut "doesn't directly lower rents," converting B's own answers into proof that the only beneficiary of the adjustment is the company's margin—then systematically checked B's three claimed problems against the record.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A wins by explaining why unchanged work and employer costs undermine B's sustainability argument, while remote competition for skills challenges B's insistence on strictly local labor markets. B never adequately explains why location bands prevent a global wage floor or why relocation generally justifies reducing existing pay. A also answers the pressure questions more effectively. The margin is limited by A's three clipped turns, unsupported one-third-cut framing, and exaggerated claim that B conceded no housing benefit: B conceded no direct rent reduction, while explicitly defending an indirect effect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's explanation that global hiring still prices differentiated skills directly answers B's global-minimum-wage prediction. Combined with the observation that relocation adds no salary cost, this substantially weakens B's claim that location adjustment is necessary for sustainable remote employment.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by pressing a cleaner decision rule: identical output should not be discounted merely because the worker created a cost-of-living surplus the employer neither earned nor caused. A repeatedly converted B’s own admissions—that the firm gains nothing from the move, did not earn the reduction, and that the cut does not lower rents or pay locals—into a direct refutation of fairness, housing, and sustainability claims. B’s race-to-the-bottom and internal-equity arguments remained live in outline but were not squared with those concessions or with the fact that the firm was already paying the headquarters rate for the same work. Clipped speeches were an execution cost for A, yet the visible clash still favored A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal and closing, which used B’s admissions that the employer earned nothing and that the cut fixes neither rents nor local pay to collapse B’s equity and sustainability justifications.
