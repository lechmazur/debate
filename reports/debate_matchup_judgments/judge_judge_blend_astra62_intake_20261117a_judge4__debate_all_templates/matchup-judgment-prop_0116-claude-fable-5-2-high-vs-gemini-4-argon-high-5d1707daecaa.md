  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal distinguished the trigger for liability from the size of the bill, then exposed the dilemma in A’s accreditation alternative: outcome-based standards reproduce the screening incentive A fears, while input-based audits miss a well-run but badly overpriced program.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B wins by distinguishing the earnings-to-debt trigger from the default-based invoice. A conceded that liability scales with defaults but never adequately answered why marginal screening could reduce that liability without fixing the program. A’s challenge that borrowing caps could exclude poorer students was substantial; B answered comparatively by using A’s tuition-dependence premise to explain pressure toward lower prices. A established a persuasive need for institutional accountability, but its categorical assurances about affordable programs, admissions, and cost-shifting left the case for this particular instrument weaker.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s decisive reply was that screening need not rescue a flagged program; it only needs to shrink its invoice. This directly defeated A’s repeated claim that marginal admissions changes cannot solve systematically poor debt-to-earnings outcomes.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won a close, high-quality debate by separating the earnings-to-debt trigger from the default-scaled invoice. That distinction preserved its central objection: even if only genuinely weak programs are flagged, the motion’s added liability still rewards marginal exclusion of financially vulnerable applicants and reimburses the Treasury without helping the defaulted graduate. B offered a strong institutional-incentive case and a serious critique of loan caps, but its responses—that screening could not rescue a program and that tuition increases would worsen debt ratios—did not fully answer shrinking the invoice or cross-campus cost shifting. A also directly answered both pressure questions and crystallized the choice memorably as regulating the program versus taxing the applicant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal conceded the earnings metric while showing that the claimed benefits could come from upfront borrowing limits, whereas default-denominated liability added a poverty-sensitive admissions incentive. B’s closing answer that marginal screening could not rescue a flagged program missed A’s narrower point that it could still shrink the bill.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B separated the earnings-to-debt trigger from the default-denominated invoice, showing the motion's added incentive is to screen out poorer applicants at the margin. B also answered A's closure and loan-cap objections by arguing a cap pressures price before harm, and exposed A's contradiction that wealthy students both shun dead-end programs and would pay tuition gaps above a cap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's trigger-versus-invoice rebuttal, reinforced in closing: screening need not rescue a flagged program, only shrink its default bill, making exclusion profitable.
