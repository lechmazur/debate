### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO more cleanly defused CON's central feasibility attack. CON argued AI-targeted labeling either swallows all advertising into meaningless wallpaper or requires an undefinable line. PRO answered with an objective technical trigger and reframed widespread labels via the Sponsored analogy: a passive badge can still tell users they were algorithmically singled out and cue warranted skepticism. CON's off-platform and human-buyer escape had force, but PRO convincingly argued those channels lack major platforms' real-time behavioral feedback loops. The harm-based alternative was plausible but did not squarely neutralize PRO's unique coverage of lawful, non-fraudulent micro-targeted manipulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to the dentist-vs-influence-operation dilemma was decisive: it converted CON's line-drawing objection into a workable automatic trigger and used the Sponsored analogy to show universal labeling need not be wallpaper.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A better showed that an automatic technical trigger labels a near-default ad practice, producing a low-information ritual rather than Sponsored-style clarity, while compliance costs and off-platform workarounds remain even if users ignore the badge. Side B usefully stressed platform-scale targeting and answered the dentist question directly, but never fully neutralized the limited audit value, bypass, or two-audience cost points.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's split-audience answer to the wallpaper-versus-potency contradiction, then its use of Side B's own concession that both the dentist and the influence operation wear the badge.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B wins because it turns PRO's answers into concessions: labeling both the dentist and covert operators confirms universal labeling of a default practice, while the audit remains only that optimization occurred. The Sponsored analogy therefore fails to show usable, actionable transparency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 split-audience answer: users can ignore the badge while advertisers still bear stigma, liability, and migration incentives, simultaneously rebutting A's strongest pressure question and preserving the wallpaper and evasion critiques.

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing a fatal workability problem in Side B’s labeling trigger. B tried to draw the line at message variation keyed to inferred traits, but A showed standard conversion AI makes routine bakery A/B tests diverge across inferred micro-groups, while a single fear-framed ad hunting anxious users could escape without message variation. B’s closing conceded the gluten-free tart would “light up” the label and called the single-variant case a “compliance gap,” confirming A’s false-positive/false-negative inversion. B’s moral case for disclosure was strong, but A gave the cleaner comparative answer: existing Sponsored menus and ad libraries can surface variant information without adding an AI-persuasion badge that dilutes synthetic-media warnings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used B’s own divergence trigger to show a bakery’s routine variant routing would be labeled while single-variant AI targeting escaped, and B later effectively conceded that asymmetry.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the definitional fork live: any trigger that catches ordinary conversion A/B tests cries wolf and dulls synthetic-media labels, while a variation-based trigger lets single-message psychographic targeting escape. B’s closing conceded the bakery lights up and treated the fear ad as an unsolved gap, and never showed why disclosing competing frames requires an AI-persuasion badge rather than existing sponsored or ad-library tools. B kept a real symmetry intuition, but A made the operational choice easier to reject.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 broke B’s log-based line by showing standard conversion optimization already routes bakery variants to inferred micro-groups, while a single fear-framed ad hunting anxious users has no “message variation” and walks free; A’s closing then seized B5.3’s concession of both horns.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B exposed the mandate's trigger as simultaneously over- and under-inclusive: standard delivery routes bakery variants to inferred micro-groups, while single-message psychographic targeting escapes. B also showed that revealing another version exists neither reveals the inferred vulnerability nor requires an AI-persuasion badge, leaving dilution and false-authenticity harms as the mandate's distinctive effects.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 answered A's boundary claim by showing modern optimization makes the bakery trigger fire while a single fear-framed ad hunting anxious users evades it; A's closing concession let B crystallize this as a backwards trigger.
