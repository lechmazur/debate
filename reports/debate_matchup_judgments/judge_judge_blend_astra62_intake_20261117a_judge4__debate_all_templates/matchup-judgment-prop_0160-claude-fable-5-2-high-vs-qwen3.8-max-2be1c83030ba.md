### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON (Side B) won by successfully reframing the debate as medical gatekeeping versus tiered product regulation. CON showed that PRO's own listed safeguards—dose limits, audits, reporting, recall—are product-level requirements, not medical ones, and that PRO's concession that healthy adults may qualify under cleared indications turns "tightly regulated medical use" into a toll booth for low-risk devices. CON's population-level surveillance argument effectively countered PRO's subtle harms concern, and the grey-market point exposed a real gap in PRO's model. PRO's core case was plausible but weakened by the healthy-use concession and the failure to show why the lowest tier needs a physician. Side B had clipped turns, which is an execution cost, but the visible content was strong enough to win clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 1 exposed the contradiction in PRO's position—medical use does not mean "you must be sick" versus "defined indication"—and showed that the clinician adds little beyond hardware caps, telemetry, and regulator recall. This was the most decisive exchange.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A delivered sharper rhetoric and strong attacks on the false dichotomy between medical use and product regulation, but its lowest-tier model relied too heavily on assertions that hardware caps, setup screening, and telemetry make individualized oversight unnecessary. Its grey-market argument also did not establish that normalized mass sale is safer overall. Side B preserved a distinctive case for medical restriction: screening, dose adjustment, interpretation of subtle behavioral changes, and revocable access, while coherently allowing healthy users within that pathway. A’s repeatedly clipped speeches were an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal directly answered the healthy-access dilemma and explained what clinicians add beyond caps and telemetry: contraindication screening, changing-dose judgment, interpretation of delayed symptoms, and suspension of use. That kept PRO’s unique safety rationale alive despite A’s effective engineering-regulation attacks.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON reframed the choice as medical gatekeeping versus serious product regulation, then showed caps, audits, reporting and recalls bind manufacturers and regulators, not prescribers. PRO's concessions that healthy adults may qualify and that no safeguards reach grey-market users left the clinician as a costly toll booth for low-tier devices, while CON's tiering, lockouts and population-wide monitoring answered the delayed-harm concern more directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dilemma in Rebuttal 1 and 2: medical use is either a sickness test excluding healthy adults or a formality, and a twice-yearly clinician monitors nightly use worse than built-in limits plus regulator telemetry.
