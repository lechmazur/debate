### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B wins on the decisive clash, though not by much. PRO had a real pull on the motion as written. It argued that CON's own levers (hours, rent, proximity) were justified by friendship, which already concedes friendship as a "policy concern." CON's closing line that "reasons belong in the room" arguably gives up some ground here. CON's answer was stronger, however, because it drew a concrete line between friendship as a stated reason and friendship as an institutional objective with an owner and a metric. It then defended that line with a "survives failure" argument: hours are still hours and rent is still rent even if no friendships form. That argument held up through every speech. PRO's best addition was the "tiebreaker" claim: naming friendship as a goal would tip close calls such as hybrid work or stable teams. CON turned this effectively in Rebuttal 2. An institution breaking ties toward friendship will favour the friendships it can see, and the caregiver who needs swappable shifts pays the cost. PRO's closing never answered that turn and was clipped, which is an execution cost. PRO also leaned on "word game" and gag framing, which CON neutralized directly ("Nothing in my case gags anyone"). PRO's opening evidence was plausible but somewhat loosely sourced (surveys, surgeons general, comparable mortality). CON's evidence was more cautious.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3–B4.4 response to PRO's A1 tiebreaker carried the debate. PRO argued that naming friendship breaks remote-vs-hybrid ties toward stable contact. CON replied that this tilts toward office-visible bonds and away from the neighbour, the school-gate parent, and the sister two towns over, and that stable teams hurt the parent who needs swappable shifts. This turned PRO's most concrete new argument into evidence for CON's claim that institutions can only score the friendships they can see.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by turning A's strongest concrete example into a cost. A argued that naming friendship as an objective changes tradeoffs, but B showed this privileges institutionally visible relationships—office attendance, stable shifts—over invisible bonds held by remote workers and caregivers. A never repaired that turn. B also answered the measurement pressure better: cohesion surveys are diagnostic, not a deliverable, and budget cycles would force attendance proxies. B's time-and-housing levers survive even if friendship gains fail, while A's program would leave only a badge and a survey. A's closing was clipped, slightly weakening its crystallization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal used A's hybrid-work tiebreaker against it: making friendship an institutional objective favors countable contact and lets employers decide whose relationships count. A did not dispatch this turn.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): A won the central clash over what naming friendship as a policy objective adds. B effectively showed structural conditions matter and offered a tiebreaker argument, but A distinguished speaking friendship as a reason from making it an accountable deliverable, showed how measurement substitutes legible attendance for chosen bonds, and argued shorter hours and affordable housing remain valuable even if friendship does not result. B's clipped closing repeated the tradeoff point without neutralizing the visible-versus-invisible friendships objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turned B's employer tiebreaker against PRO: privileging office contact rewards institutionally visible relationships over the neighbour, school-gate, and family friendships institutions cannot count.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp, but PRO (A) built the more durable path to victory. A's core claim was that the conditions for friendship (stable schedules, open shared spaces, unhurried time) are already set by institutions. A then supported the claim that the friendship frame changes decisions with a concrete example: a library justified by literacy closes at five, while one justified by friendship stays open after six. CON (B) ran a strong opening on acquaintance versus friendship and on dispositional causes. Its "redundant or corrosive" dilemma was well framed. But B increasingly granted A's actual policies and contested only the label. B never explained why amenity logic alone would produce the late-hours decision. B's falsifiability tests were answered credibly. To the falling-hours trend, A replied that predictability and access matter more than totals. To the hold-price-at-zero test, A replied that time cost is not monetary cost. B's added time-use claim was a slight late widening. A's final point exposed a real tension: B described the frame as inert as a lever, yet B's own conditional-funding example shows it reallocating money. B's corrosion worry about friendship metrics remained live but speculative. On balance, A made the decision easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange began with B's Q1, which pressed A on how anyone would know the policy worked. A answered in A4.1 by naming condition and uptake metrics and in A4.2 by offering its own falsification test, then turned the unfalsifiability charge back onto B's "demand-side" account. B's attempt in B4.5 to absorb a successful result as ordinary labor or amenity policy was clever. But it left unanswered A's tiebreaker point: naming friendship as the purpose changes what gets built.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A wins. A successfully framed friendship as dependent on necessary environmental conditions, not as something institutions must manufacture. The acquaintance objection was absorbed rather than fatal: A showed that slack, predictability, and shared unguarded settings are the missing inputs. A's rebuttal of B's trend argument was decisive in distinguishing average hours from schedule distribution, and A offered a concrete falsification while exposing B's demand-side account as harder to test. Most importantly, A's tiebreaker example gave the friendship frame a real policy effect: literacy funding can justify closing at five, while friendship funding can justify staying open after six. B's strongest line was that this is ordinary amenity policy or compliance accounting, but A's conditions-and-uptake success model was a credible third path that did not require auditing intimacy. B often attacked a stronger version of the case involving mandated sociability or KPIs, which A repeatedly disavowed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's tiebreaker rebuttal was the most decisive: it turned B's redundancy charge into a specification argument by showing the same library budget can produce an opposite decision depending on whether friendship is named as the purpose. This forced B to concede the policy while relabeling it, weakening the claim that the frame adds nothing.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO sustained the necessary-conditions case: institutions already control predictability, proximity, slack, and opening hours. Its library example gave a concrete specification difference, and its closing showed that a friendship frame acts as a real tiebreaker in funding decisions. CON's “no third thing” dilemma was sharp, but ultimately conceded that this tiebreaker changes outcomes, undercutting the redundancy claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it offered a concrete falsification test for predictable rosters and explained why falling average hours can coexist with more unpredictable schedules, directly neutralizing CON's trend argument while exposing the unfalsifiability of the preference account.
