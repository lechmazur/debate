### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it drove the decisive clash and narrowed it in its favor. Its central claim is that any targeted scheme must leave a price in place for children the school has not yet identified, and that this price can deter a hungry child even when billing is confidential and meals are never denied. It made the point concrete with the "identical accounts" comparison and the "packed sandwich versus skipped lunch" question. CON argued with honesty and discipline. Its opportunity-cost argument stayed live throughout: money that replaces a comfortable family's payment cannot also fund breakfast or holiday meals. But CON made two damaging concessions. It accepted that staff cannot detect hidden hunger before the first missed lunch. It also withdrew its claim that need on the eligibility list is "deeper." That left its alternative uses of the money as plausible but unquantified, just as PRO's benefit was. Both sides correctly recognized the evidentiary symmetry. PRO then supplied the cleaner rule for deciding under uncertainty: place the risk of misidentification on the budget rather than on the child. PRO also showed that administering targeting erodes the claimed savings. CON's closing reply, "the budget is not an alternative victim," was sharp. However, it rested on speculative reallocation that CON had already weakened, so it did not overturn PRO's structural point. The edge for PRO is clear but competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 in the pressure round and its follow-up at B4.2–B4.3 forced CON to concede at A4.1 that confidential, provisional assistance cannot reach a child before the first missed lunch, because "a waiver tomorrow" requires someone to know first. This turned PRO's strongest mechanism into an agreed fact. CON was left defending only an unquantified opportunity-cost tradeoff.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B powerfully showed that confidential billing and provisional aid still require hardship to be identified before help arrives. A consistently turned the extra cost for affluent families into foregone breakfast or holiday support and showed that invoking an invisible child proves a risk, not its scale or proportionality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it honestly conceded the first-missed-lunch problem, then used B's own concession on hunger reduction and demanded comparative evidence from both sides, leaving universal provision as a valuable benefit rather than a justified default.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the central clash easier to resolve: universal meals remove an unavoidable price and identification barrier that can leave unidentified children hungry during the school day. Side B’s opportunity-cost case was strong, but it remained too dependent on unproven assumptions that redirected funds would reliably prevent more hunger than universal meals, especially after conceding that staff cannot always detect quietly skipped meals. A’s answers to pressure, cleaner risk allocation, and more memorable framing gave it the clearer path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s pressure question exposed that targeting cannot reliably identify a child who quietly skips lunch before harm occurs, and Side B’s concession gave A the decisive practical edge.
