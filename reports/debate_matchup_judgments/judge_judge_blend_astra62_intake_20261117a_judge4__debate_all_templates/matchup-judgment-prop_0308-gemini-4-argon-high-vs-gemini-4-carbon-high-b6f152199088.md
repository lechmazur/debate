### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Both sides were competent and well structured, and the decision is close. A set out a clear conceptual frame: populism is defined by an anti-pluralist moral monopoly, and its rise both signals representative decay and erodes trust in democratic norms. A's "pathogen versus cure" closing was memorable. B adapted more effectively across the rounds. - **Circularity charge:** B argued that A succeeds in labelling successful anti-elite movements "reformers" only by defining populism around its autocratic outcomes. A's A1 reply ("method, not outcome") was reasonable but did not fully dissolve the charge. - **Decay versus elite failure:** B argued that equating party convergence with "institutional decay" collapses the very distinction the motion draws between institutional decay and elite failure. This blunted A's claim that B had conceded the warning-sign half of the motion. - **Containment:** B's answer to A's containment argument held up. Every ambitious faction is safe only under checks, so "benign only when restrained" is a standard no actor passes. - **Turned concession:** B turned A's admission that Syriza and Podemos were "boxed in" by coalition math into support for B's claim that the normal democratic environment filters out overreach. A's closing somewhat overstated B's Poland concession by calling backsliding populism's "natural trajectory," when B had framed Poland as an outlier. B's weakest moment was its A2 answer on what stops attacks spreading from regulators to election referees, which leaned on assertion. B also offered limited evidence for its "most populists" claim. On balance, B left the cleaner live route: most populist movements usually supply agenda pressure inside guardrails that hold.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B2.3/B5.1 response to A's containment argument was the most decisive exchange. Branding populism dangerous because guardrails must restrain it applies a test that presidents, majorities, and lobbies would all fail. This reframed A's strongest "benign only when contained" point as a feature of constitutional democracy rather than an indictment of populism.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B controlled the decisive turn: Side A admitted Poland was real backsliding and located populism's safety in external coalition and constitutional guards. B converted that into proof that populism itself is not the corrective, only contained by institutions it erodes. B also effectively distinguished populist anti-pluralism from pluralist anti-elite reform, undercutting A's circular-definition charge. A's counter that all factions are restrained by checks was responsive but did not neutralize the point that populism attacks the legitimacy of neutral rules and opposition, not merely the policies of elites.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pathogen/immune-system turn was decisive: if populism's natural trajectory when unconstrained is backsliding, then the fact that democratic guardrails usually contain it means the system survived a threat, not that populism was medicine. This directly used A's own admission that coalition rules filter out autocratic overreach.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by turning Side A's central defenses against it: party cartelization is institutional decay of representative mediation, and populism being safe only when coalitions and guardrails contain it does not make it a healthy corrective. Side A offered strong counter-examples and the point that all factions need checks, but never squarely answered B's distinction that populism attacks the legitimacy of referees and opposition, or explained how years of court-packing in Poland fit a corrective account beyond conceding it was backsliding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and closing containment turn: Poland shows survival of an illness, not medicine, and a movement contained by guardrails it erodes is a warning sign, not a cure.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A wins a competitive debate. A set out a clear ideational definition of populism in the opening: the anti-pluralist claim that one movement alone represents the "real people." That definition is not based on outcomes, so B's tautology charge never fully landed against it. A then made two moves that held up. First, A turned B's "most populists never gain unchecked power" into evidence that guardrails, not populism, deserve the credit. The "contained hazard" and "autoimmune" framing captured this well. Second, A used B's own admission that "cartelized elites ignored voters first" to show that a populist surge diagnoses decayed mediating institutions. That speaks directly to the "warning sign" wording of the motion. B's strongest material was real. The original U.S. Populists, who coined the term yet were absorbed peacefully, are a good counterexample. B's "definitional shell game" charge also had bite once A began reclassifying peaceful challengers as "pluralist reformers." B's "division of labor" framing was creative. But B had gaps. It never engaged Hungary. It dismissed Poland as a "rare exception" without much support. Its answer on presidential systems (federalism and primaries "similarly channel" surges) was asserted rather than shown. And it widened the meaning of populism to include labor unions and primary challenges, which let A argue that B was borrowing the record of pluralist reform. A's late references to the U.S. and Brazil were slightly broad but stayed tied to live points. On balance, A made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's Pressure Question Q1 and the Rebuttal 2 follow-up, which seized on B's concession that coalitions and courts must "filter out illiberal impulses" and that Poland only went wrong under an outright majority. A argued that this concedes populism is a hazard which institutions contain, not a cure: "an immune response that attacks vital organs whenever it reaches fifty-one percent is autoimmune disease." B's reply that every faction needs guardrails was reasonable but did not restore the claim that populism itself is the healthy corrective.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B wins by holding a non-circular definition of populism centered on anti-pluralist claims to represent the authentic people, rather than merely authoritarian outcomes. A's strongest attack was that this definition rigs the baseline, but B answered it by distinguishing ordinary pluralist reform from populist politics and by showing that peaceful populist examples often depended on guardrails containing them. B also turned A's concessions into evidence: mainstream cartels were framed as mediating institutional decay, and the need for courts and coalitions to filter out illiberal impulses made populism a contained hazard rather than a cure. A's empirical appeal to 'most' peaceful cases was undercut because B broadened backsliding to include loser's consent and norm erosion, which A never fully neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive turn was in Rebuttal 2: accepting that coalitions and courts must filter out populism's illiberal impulses, while dismissing Poland because populists won an outright majority, concedes that populism is itself hazardous rather than a healthy corrective.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won the decisive definitional and causal clash: populism was defined upfront by exclusive moral ownership of the people, not retroactively by authoritarian outcomes, making peaceful policy reform a different category. B also turned A's concessions that populism needs guardrails to filter illiberal impulses and arises after parties stop representing voters into evidence of both decay upstream and hazard downstream. A's Greece, Spain, Finland, and nineteenth-century examples showed containment is possible, but B more convincingly assigned credit for that containment to institutions rather than to populism itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 autoimmune-disease turn: if populism is only safe when coalitions and courts filter it and becomes dangerous with an outright majority, it is a contained hazard rather than a healthy corrective.
