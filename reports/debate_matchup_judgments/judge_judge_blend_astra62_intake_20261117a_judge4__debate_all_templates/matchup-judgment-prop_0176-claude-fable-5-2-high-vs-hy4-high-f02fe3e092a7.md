  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s decisive rebuttal was that A had abandoned pure direct distribution on each hard problem by reaching for a saved reserve, making A’s model a fund by another name rather than an alternative to SWFs.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): B won the substantive comparison: pooled savings protect households against commodity shocks, limit boom-time spending, and preserve resources beyond depletion. A's strongest contribution was its citizen-creditor model, making missing payments personally visible. But its eventual protections relied on withheld revenue held offshore and released by rule, while B persuasively explained that binding citizen payouts can be designed into a fund. A therefore established a valuable payout principle without establishing why direct distributions outperform the saving institution. B's clipped endings were an execution weakness, but the decisive argument remained clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B challenged A to explain what protects poor households when revenue-linked cheques collapse. A answered with a precommitted floor financed from boom savings. B then showed that the saved pool supplies the protection, while citizen entitlement concerns how that pool pays out—not whether it is needed.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the motion’s developing-country setting central: weak institutions make capture and discretion the decisive risks, and a mandatory per-capita claim with a locked residue gives citizens a visible, non-substitutable creditor status that a general sovereign fund’s optional payout design does not. Repeated clipping of Side B’s later speeches left that ownership distinction less fully neutralized and made the choice easier to resolve for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A1/A2 in Rebuttal 2, distinguishing a sole per-capita creditor and automatic bust-year floor from a discretionary fund whose dividend is merely an option.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won the decisive weak-institution clash. B established the SWF's macro advantage, and A's concession on smoother stabilization was real, but A showed that advantage is conditioned on a rule being enforced. B's answer to "who enforces not now" named central banks, auditors, and legislators—the very actors whose capture defines the problem in the target states—and A turned this into a clear asymmetry: a cash flow is publicly watched and loses a slice, while an SWF stock can be quietly emptied. A's failure-to-failure comparison was cleaner and more memorable than B's symmetry claim. A's clipped turns were an execution cost, but not enough to overturn the core case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's follow-up to "who enforces not now" was the decisive exchange: B named central bank/custodian/auditors, and A showed that answer assumes away the weak institutions the motion is about, while distinguishing a skimmable public flow from a whole stock capturable in private.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): A wins the institutional comparison: shared collection risks do not eliminate the additional exposure from accumulating a centrally controlled stock, while visible distributions can leave durable household assets. B established the fund’s stabilization advantage, but its enforcement answer relied heavily on the institutions under dispute and insufficiently answered A’s asymmetric-risk argument. A overstated the inevitability of fund failure, and its two clipped turns count against execution. Nevertheless, its sharper comparative rebuttals outweigh B’s surviving macroeconomic case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange combined the stock-versus-flow distinction with its response to B’s raided-fund answer: a “visible hole” and audit trails are not substitutes for household assets already acquired. This exposed the gap between B’s promised institutional accountability and actual retained value.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by tying the motion to weak institutions and showing that a fund’s macro advantages depend on enforcers those states lack, while cash’s failures are public, partial, and leave household assets. Side B pressed procyclicality and the conditional concession well, but never fully erased the unequal failure modes of quiet stock capture versus a visible monthly flow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2: the share-based dividend auto-adjusts without borrowing, an emptied fund smooths nothing, and a “visible hole” is not a substitute for roofs, livestock, or schooling.
