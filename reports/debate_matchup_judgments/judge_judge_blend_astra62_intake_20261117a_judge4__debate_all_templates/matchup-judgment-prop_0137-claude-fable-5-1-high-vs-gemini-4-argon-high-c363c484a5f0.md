### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A more consistently honored the motion's actual distinction: it did not ban or hide egg freezing, only challenged making it the flagship while deferral's later costs are unfunded. A turned CON's strongest admission into a decisive point: if removing the headline makes the benefit disappear, it was a recruiting advertisement rather than reliable support. B raised real practical and time-sensitivity concerns but too often collapsed the motion into a ban/drop/hide mandate and never fully resolved A's collective-action problem of who pays for thaw, IVF, and leave.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's crystallizing use of B's "ban the headline, destroy the business case" admission was the most decisive exchange, though B's cost-gap attack on the IVF/leave comparison was the strongest counter.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) controlled the decisive clash throughout. Its core reframe—that a benefit existing only for recruiting return is an advertisement, not a benefit—absorbed CON's strongest threat ("firms will drop it") and turned it into a reason to vote PRO. B also maintained the crucial distinction between disclosure (benefits pages, offer letters, onboarding) and flagship branding, directly answering CON's time-sensitivity and applicant-transparency arguments. B's pressure question ("which employer in that market ever pays for the actual baby?") exposed a real hole in CON's "future employer" chain, and CON's segmentation answer conceded that flagship-marketing firms fund nothing downstream. CON landed genuine points on cost asymmetry (small-utilization retrieval subsidy vs. broad recurring leave/childcare costs) and the "policing motives vs. protecting material outcomes" closing frame was its best moment, but B had already reframed the motion as restricting branding, not the benefit itself, blunting the affordability attack. Both sides followed format, answered pressure questions with clean A1/A2 structure, and avoided fake sourcing. B simply made the debate easier to decide with sharper pressure, cleaner weighing, and a memorable crystallization ("eggs in a locker are not a child in a nursery").
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1-B4.5): answering CON's affordability question by recharacterizing it as "a priority, not a budget constraint," then showing that CON's market model—retrieval funded for recruiting, thaw/leave/childcare deferred to "a future employer"—means nobody funds the baby. This exchange broke CON's central "benefit will vanish" threat and set up B's closing kill shot.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won by keeping the motion's actual scope: do not flagship egg freezing without the family floor, while still allowing the benefit to be offered and disclosed. B landed the stronger cost distinction and a serious backfire story, but kept arguing as if the rule banned or hid freezing, and never showed why job postings and recruiter disclosure were not enough. A's conversion of B's own "ban the headline and the line item dies" claim into proof that the perk was recruiting advertising stayed live through the close; B's closing misread that as A's concession rather than neutralizing it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal and close, which used B's recruiting-return concession to show the billboard firm funds deferral and passes the baby to a future employer, while separating flagship branding from ordinary disclosure.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing A into a live dilemma: if factual public disclosure is enough, banning “flagship” marketing is mostly tone-policing; if marketing visibility matters, muting it risks the time-sensitive benefit. A’s benefits remained speculative market pressure and honest signals, while B kept a more concrete harmed applicant in view. B also turned A’s closing admission about itemized benefits into an inconsistency and neutralized the “future employer” point by showing frozen eggs are portable assets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing exploited A’s claim that applicants read itemized coverage: either banners do not fool careful readers, making the rule empty, or they do affect notice, making the rule harmful. A never escaped that dilemma.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) edged a very close debate by defusing CON's only concrete victim: the itemization-vs-flagship distinction (coverage listed on job postings is permitted; signature branding is not) meant the 34-year-old engineer still discovers the benefit, collapsing A's "woman who never learns" harm into a marginal emphasis effect. A's counter-dilemma ("either marketing matters or it polices tone") was escaped by B's coherent third channel — emphasis shapes brand impressions and honest market signals, not notice — which B maintained from B2.1 through the closing. A's "name one beneficiary" pressure was answered adequately with two concrete groups (applicants seeking family-friendly employers; workers gaining upgraded benefits), and A's dismissal of these as "mechanisms, not persons" was more rhetoric than rebuttal. B also landed the sharpest late blow: A's own A4.6 concession that the engineer would "thaw under a future employer's plan" fed B's half-a-bridge and outsourcing-motherhood narrative. A was rhetorically superior throughout — "empties both pans," "triage," "eggs travel with her" — and A5.1's contradiction charge was clever, but it rested on conflating two different women (the retrieval-seeker vs. the family-planner), and A's closing still leaned on a notice harm B had largely neutralized. B's core normative case (flagship delay-branding without motherhood support is bait-and-switch that distorts labor-market incentives) survived the exchange more intact than A's information-loss route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.3: B's pressure-round answers drew a workable line between permitted itemized disclosure and forbidden flagship branding, directly dismantling A's central harm (the woman comparing offers who "never learns the money exists"), then B5.1 converted A's "thaw them under a future employer's plan" concession into evidence that the startup's flagship benefit outsources the cost of motherhood elsewhere.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by forcing a cleaner ledger. PRO's flagship-versus-listing distinction never escaped the dilemma that either emphasis does not matter, making the rule mere tone-policing, or it does, costing notice of a time-sensitive benefit. CON also showed the rule guarantees no extra leave, IVF, or childcare, while PRO's claimed gains stayed speculative pressure and signals. The closing contradiction—careful readers both find coverage and are fooled by branding—left PRO's path less decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal and closing, which turned PRO's own careful-reader concession into an inconsistency and reduced the proposed beneficiaries to unproven market pressure rather than any concrete gain in leave or fertility care.
