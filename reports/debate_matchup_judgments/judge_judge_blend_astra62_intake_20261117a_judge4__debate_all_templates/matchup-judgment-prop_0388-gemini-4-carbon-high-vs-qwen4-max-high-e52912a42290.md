### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): This was a close, high-quality exchange. CON narrowly won by controlling the instrument comparison. CON's strongest moves: - It pinned the dispute on whether protection should attach to the account or the product. - It showed that a ceiling binds an account, not a child. - It argued that PRO's own fallback, transparent fixed-price cosmetics, is itself a design rule. - It used PRO's admission that a shift to ads or adult spenders is "vastly fairer" against PRO's later claim that design rules destroy free access. PRO had real successes: - The default-lock answer to the privacy honeypot was smart. - The point that gift cards do not verify age was correct. - PRO correctly exposed that CON drifted toward a universal ban on paid randomization. That sits awkwardly with CON's opening defense of free-access revenue. PRO's weaknesses decided the round: - It conceded that the parent-profile bypass exists and called it "rare." - It then framed blocking the "honest kid" as the win. CON converted this into the claim that the policy blocks the lowest-risk spender while missing the highest-risk one. - PRO's "mechanics mutate" rejoinder, citing hybrid passes and nested bundles, was asserted more than developed. - PRO never argued that caps and design rules could coexist, which would have neutralized CON's counter-model. CON's closing reframe also landed. It argued that the gate, not the ceiling, does the protective work, and that the gate follows a borrowable identity rather than the purchase. CON's drift and its partial concession on gift cards keep the margin thin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.2–B2.3, extended in B4.5–B4.7, was the most decisive exchange. It argued that a ceiling follows the account, not the child, and that PRO's own fixed-price fallback is a design rule. This forced PRO into conceding the bypass is "rare" and that blocking the honest kid is the win. CON then turned that concession into "protection that follows the mechanic beats protection that follows the paperwork."
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a close, high-quality exchange. B's case stayed anchored to the motion as written, and its default-lock architecture absorbed A's best attacks: the gift-card teen is locked at zero on their own account, and the liquor-cabinet analogy reasonably answered the borrowed-parent-profile bypass. Both sides followed format honestly; B simply made the debate easier to decide on the motion's own terms.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): PRO had the more vivid framing and an early win in pointing out the tension between "revenue is drawn overwhelmingly from adults" and "caps crush studios." CON, however, built the more durable route to victory: a verification dilemma. Truly youth-targeted defaults require either parental opt-in, which already exists, or identity gates on every player, which cost adult funders. CON also pressed a concrete refund-exploit problem, and PRO effectively let that point go. PRO's mechanism kept moving under that pressure: - verified age and bans on minors buying randomized pulls; - then payment-channel attestation; - then a universal ceiling liftable with one click; - then, in the closing, a ceiling liftable only with the adult's own credential. CON exploited the one-click version well. In closing, CON also correctly noted that default ceilings on adult cards break PRO's promise that "every adult keeps every flexible option." PRO's "your best evidence limits work is a limit" line is clever, but it doesn't rescue a plan that can no longer target youth without the costs CON identified. On balance, CON made the decisive clash clearer and held its position more consistently, though PRO's rhetoric keeps this close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3–B4.4 did the decisive work. PRO had shifted to treating adult-named cards as attestation and letting "anyone" raise the cap with one confirmation. CON showed this leaves the saved-card household unprotected and lets a teen simply click through. That forced PRO to revise the mechanism yet again in its closing, rather than defend the motion's youth-specific protections.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a sharp, well-matched exchange. B's early contradiction catch — revenue "overwhelmingly from adults" versus "crushing studios" — forced A into a defensive posture A never fully escaped, since A defended the revenue threat only as verification hassle rather than lost money. B's decisive move was the "cap the money, not the face" reframe: payment-channel ceilings avoid the ID-gate dilemma, and B consistently answered A's saved-card attack with a real distinction between per-purchase prompts (toggleable for convenience) and aggregate monthly ceilings (lifted only with the adult's credential). B also turned A's gift-card point into a concession that caps work. A executed well — catching B's quiet abandonment of "verified age" and cooling-off refunds, and exposing tension between universal ceilings and B's "adults keep every option" promise — but A's closing dilemma ("duplicates existing locks or kills free access") was weakened by B's aggregate-cap-versus-prompt distinction and the minimal one-confirmation friction for adults. Both sides showed strong epistemic discipline with plausible mechanisms and no fake sourcing; B simply made the debate easier to decide and landed the cleaner weighing: an extra click for adults versus unbounded midnight spending by minors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 ([B4.1]-[B4.6]): the payment-channel cap answer to A's verification dilemma, plus the gift-card concession turn ("CON's strongest evidence that limits work is a limit. Their strongest objection is a click.") — this collapsed A's core trade-off and set up B's closing defense of the saved-card household.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A wins the implementation clash. B made a compelling case for protective defaults, but its adult-card attestation and one-click overrides undermined protection for its central example: children using saved adult cards. B's closing partially repaired this with credential-gated ceilings, materially changing the mechanism rather than fully defending it. A also distinguished restrictions on unverified adults from lost youth revenue, answering B's repeatedly alleged contradiction. A's universal-ID and paywall predictions remained overstated, keeping this a competitive rather than dominant win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal directly connected B's proposed safeguards to their vulnerability: an adult-named card would attest adulthood for the very child whose spending B wanted restricted, while a one-click override supplied no meaningful barrier. This forced B's consequential closing revision.
