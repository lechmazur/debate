### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B won by turning PRO's own concession that labour costs shift into wages into a devastating choice: the mandate's apparent employer match is the worker's own pay redirected, and its only remaining edge over a default-enrolled pre-tax portable pool is compulsory locking. A had a plausible structural case, but B answered the main pressure more cleanly and gave the clearer reason to reject the mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's threshold dilemma was the decisive exchange: PRO could not maintain both a payer-blind threshold and its substantial-income targeting without either hitting the ten-hour student from the first dollar or reintroducing payer-side steering.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won the decisive clash. B's strategy of embracing A's concession that "labour costs shift into wages" allowed a consistent reframe — both models are worker-funded, so the real question is who decides — and B defended that frame honestly under pressure (admitting no real match, answering A's Q1 directly). A's design drifted under pressure: to keep the threshold invisible, A moved to a levy on all pay from the first dollar, which B showed abandoned the opening's targeting promise and left sub-threshold workers paying in without the pooled cover. Both sides showed strong epistemic discipline with no fake sourcing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.5-B4.10]: by showing that A's threshold fix ("a small percentage on everything") sacrifices the opening's targeting and then posing the unaddressed top-up dilemma (payers-top-up means steering returns; state-top-up is B's own model), B converted A's own design patches into the decisive reason to reject the mandate.
