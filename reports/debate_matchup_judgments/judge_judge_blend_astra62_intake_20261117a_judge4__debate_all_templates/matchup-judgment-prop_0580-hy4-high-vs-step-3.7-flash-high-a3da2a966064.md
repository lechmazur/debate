### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won decisively. It built a clear causal contrast: cash reaches the poverty gap directly, while a subsidy dollar must pass through a chain of conditions (an open slot, fitting hours, a real job offer, net earnings gains) before it reduces poverty. PRO then pressed that contrast with sharp, accurate questions about CON's double standard: a lapsing credit set against a permanent subsidy tail, and the coverage gap for families with no job or a disabled parent. CON's case rested on a hypothetical $15,000 raise and a "4-to-1 return," plus unsupported figures such as "60% rely on informal care" and an exact 20% misuse rate. Its Rebuttal 2 then effectively conceded PRO's entire framework. It called its own asymmetric modeling a "sleight of hand," said subsidies deliver "zero benefit" to excluded families, called fungibility "a feature," and said the conditional subsidy gain cannot outweigh guaranteed cash. CON's closing then accused PRO of misreading CON's own words and repeated the very lapse asymmetry it had already conceded. This collapse in internal consistency left PRO with the only coherent path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 and Rebuttal 2 exposed CON's stacked comparison: the credit was modeled as expiring after one year while the subsidy earned a permanent earnings floor. PRO's insistence that both policies either continue or face repeal neutralized CON's horizon argument. CON's A1 then openly conceded the point, and PRO's closing quoted those concessions back to seal the decision.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A dominated every layer of the debate, and Side B suffered a total self-contradiction in Rebuttal 2. Even before Turn 8, Side A built the far cleaner comparative case: under equivalent spending across all poor children, refundable monthly cash directly closes the income gap through existing machinery, whereas childcare subsidies must survive a leaky multi-step chain (provider supply, schedule fit, job availability, and wage gains) whose proposed fixes consume the same budget. Under pressure from Side A's sharp questions in Turn 5, Side B completely flipped in Turn 8 ([B4.1]-[B4.4]), explicitly calling its own horizon modeling a "sleight of hand," admitting subsidies deliver "zero benefit at all" to excluded households, and concluding that monthly cash delivers more poverty reduction per dollar—only to falsely deny its own verbatim record in Closing [B5.1].
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "$15,000 raise / 4-to-1 return" claim and asymmetric expiration assumption in [A2.2]-[A2.5], sharpened into Pressure Questions [A3.1]-[A3.2], exposed the conditional fragility and double standard of Side B's model so thoroughly that Side B conceded its entire case in [B4.1]-[B4.4].

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins clearly but competitively. PRO had the stronger empirical anchor in the 2021 CTC, but leaned on brittle figures it could not support: the recurring "40%" claim, "almost 50%," and "doubled in a single month." It also never supplied an equivalent-spending comparator, and its answer to the pressure question on that point ("never come close") effectively admitted the comparison had not been made. PRO also repeated its coverage-gap point across speeches with little development, and its closing was clipped. CON consistently attacked the motion's real decision rule, durable poverty reduction per net dollar. It exposed PRO's chosen metric (official headcount per gross dollar) as one that excludes earnings and fiscal-offset pathways by construction. It reframed the post-expiration poverty spike as evidence of non-persistence, and it separated "coverage" from "yield." CON's weakness is that its earnings-unlock mechanism stays largely theoretical, and it conceded that no clean trial exists. Even so, it handled the pressure questions directly and narrowed the clash more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A2.1 and A4.4 were the most decisive moves. They turned PRO's own evidence, that child poverty jumped when the checks stopped, into proof that the effect was rented suppression rather than durable reduction. They then seized on PRO's A2 answer ("never come close" in real-world U.S. implementation) as an admission that the same-budget childcare comparator was never built.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B won solidly by controlling the cost-effectiveness framework and answering pressure far more cleanly than Side A. Side B showed that Side A's metric—official poverty headcount per gross dollar—ignores unavoidable childcare bills, ongoing earnings from maintained work, and fiscal offsets from payroll taxes and lower benefit use. Side B also drew a decisive distinction between universal coverage and per-dollar anti-poverty yield, exposing Side A's contradiction in criticizing subsidies for reaching above-poverty households while praising CTC spending up to 200% of the poverty line. Side A asked good pressure questions and kept a plausible intuition about non-working and informal-care households, but repeated earlier claims verbatim in later speeches, dropped key counterarguments, and had its closing clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A argued in [A2.1] that child poverty doubling immediately when the 2021 CTC lapsed disproved Side B's claim that the CTC's effect was temporary, Side B turned that point into a concession in [B2.1]: an immediate rebound when checks stop proves the reduction had no durable momentum and was merely "poverty suppression rented month by month," unlike subsidies that help parents retain jobs and ongoing earnings.

Its coverage-versus-yield distinction, critique of measuring only official headcount per gross dollar, and insistence on an equivalent-spending comparator directly challenged PRO’s route to victory.
