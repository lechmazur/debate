### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly without invented sourcing, but CON ultimately exposed a structural gap PRO never closed: PRO's defense that "lecture recordings" textually confines the recording mandate to lectures does not extend to captions and accessible materials, which the motion applies across all instructional settings — so CON's "seminars/labs/studios still get the full bundle" objection survived into the closing largely unanswered. CON's pressure questions forced PRO to invent ex ante administrative machinery (published criteria, accessibility offices ruling in advance) that CON correctly noted concedes the presumption is not self-executing or cheap, undercutting PRO's "low-cost, routine" framing from the opening. CON's closing frame — that producing artifacts (a recording, an auto-caption, a converted file) is not the same as guaranteeing functional access — sharpened the whole debate's stakes in a way PRO's "locked door" metaphor did not fully rebut. PRO's strongest and most consistent weapon, the "accommodation lottery" critique of disclosure-based access, remained a real point, but CON blunted it by showing its own model also provides proactive core materials without disclosure, leaving only individualized services gated behind request — a symmetry PRO never fully dismantled. CON did suffer an execution cost from its clipped opening, which cost some rhetorical polish, but it recovered fully in subsequent rounds and ended with the cleaner, more decisive framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 2 (B4.5) catching that PRO's "lecture recordings" textual narrowing does not limit captions and accessible materials, forcing PRO's closing to leave that gap unaddressed, was the most damaging and unresolved exchange of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A successfully proved that a universal presumption mandates artifacts (like uncorrected auto-captions) rather than genuine access, while introducing bureaucratic costs that compete with individualized support. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's ex-ante exemption mechanism by showing it cannot handle spontaneous sensitive disclosures.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively flipped Side B's pressure questions, pointing out that an ex-ante exemption bureaucracy cannot stop a recording during a spontaneous trauma disclosure, whereas an instructor-led policy can.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a clearer path: presumptive lecture capture/captions/accessible materials ends the disclosure-driven lottery and serves beyond diagnosed disability, with "lecture recordings" textually limiting recording to lectures and a strong pedagogical reason plausibly covering candor/confidentiality conditions. CON's elastic-exception and quality/resource critiques landed, but its alternative still requires disclosure/waiting and differentiated judgments, leaving PRO's planned-default weighing intact despite A's clipped opening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1 elasticity attack that a workable rule can't guess how far "pedagogical" stretches vs B4.2/B4.3 institutional ex-ante answer that mere inconvenience fails and exemptions are decided before term — resolved core exception-scope clash in PRO's favor.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides built developed, plausible cases, but A's core structural point survived the exchange better than B's. A's timing/latency argument—that needs-based systems still require disclosure, diagnosis, and processing before any support arrives, during which learning is lost—was never actually answered by B; B's A1 answer asserted an "expedited timeline" and "enforceable duty" without addressing why establishing need would be faster than default provision. Meanwhile, A's strongest counter to B's "garbled auto-captions / box-checking" line—that the motion requires genuinely accessible materials, so unusable outputs are simply noncompliant rather than evidence against the presumption itself—reframed B's central mechanism as an implementation/enforcement worry rather than an objection to the normative claim in the motion, and B never cleanly rebutted this reframe. B's "floor eats the ceiling" budget-crowding mechanism was original and consistently developed, and its privacy/candor point about ordinary seminars was sharp, but A's repeated charge that CON's own alternative is equally vulnerable to cheap, slow, or dishonest compliance (the asymmetric-assumption critique) was never squarely answered, leaving that a live, decisive point for A. A's opening being clipped is a real execution cost, but the content was substantially reconstructed and defended across later speeches, limiting the damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.6) turning B's "binding rule" question back into "unusable outputs remain noncompliant," combined with A's unanswered pressure question about what guarantees timely, accurate support before cumulative learning is lost—B's A1 answer in Rebuttal 2 never actually resolved the latency problem, leaving A's timing argument as the most damaging unaddressed point in the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON won by clearly establishing the mechanical tradeoff of universal mandates: forcing schools to process every single class mathematically necessitates cheap, automated solutions, which eats the budget for the intensive, human-reviewed accommodations disabled students actually need. PRO effectively pointed out the bureaucratic delays in CON's system, but CON successfully proved that PRO's baseline would simply result in a useless, auto-generated compliance checkbox.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 perfectly answered the budget question, explaining that a universal mandate becomes a 'first charge on fixed budgets,' forcing bulk automated output and leaving intensive human review as an unaffordable extra.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO won the budget and burden clash. CON's floor-eats-ceiling mechanism is plausible but depends on schools counting unusable auto-captions/scans as "accessible," which conflicts with the motion's wording. PRO exposed asymmetric accounting - CON assumes its needs-based system is well-resourced, fast, and confidential while assuming the presumption will be cheap and surveilling - and framed the failure comparison: a defective baseline can be challenged, while a student waiting for accommodation loses irrecoverable weeks. That gave PRO the cleaner live route despite the clipped opening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.5 / B4.5: floor-not-ceiling and asymmetric accounting - showing CON's cheap-compliance fear applies equally to its own system, but its system adds gatekeeping delay while the presumption keeps individualized supports.
