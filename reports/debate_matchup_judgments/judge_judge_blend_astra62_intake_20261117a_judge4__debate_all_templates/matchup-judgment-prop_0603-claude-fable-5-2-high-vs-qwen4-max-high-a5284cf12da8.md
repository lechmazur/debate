### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B conceded the core baseline-first principle but won the mechanism clash. A's irreversible-loss point was powerful, but B showed a conditional approval standard can achieve the same pre-approval verification without a blanket pause. B's sharpest move was converting A's answers into concessions: A said a contractor meeting an independently verified standard would get a decision, and A said "Nothing" protects non-ISA seabed. That let B argue the pause adds only delay while risking defunding and regulatory leakage. A's order/precedent concern remained live but B gave a meaningful answer: the ISA can pre-commit to a checklist and an applicant-funded, ISA-appointed panel before any approval. Overall B's path was cleaner and more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's use of A's pressure-question answers was decisive. A's Q1 exposed that no dedicated independent verifier exists, but B turned that into a funding argument: a pause builds nothing, whereas an approval standard with an applicant-funded, ISA-appointed panel creates the verifier with a budget. B then seized A's "Yes" to the checklist question and "Nothing" on non-ISA mining to frame the pause as merely a padlock on the application window.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a tight, high-quality exchange. Side A's best move was the convergence trap: forcing B to admit that a contractor meeting a verified checklist "gets its decision," seemingly collapsing the pause into A's approval standard. But B absorbed the hit and sharpened the real residual difference: no certifying body exists today, so under A's model the first applicant funds and summons the verifier mid-application, letting deadline pressure and sunk capital define what "robust" means — the pause is the construction phase before that pressure exists. A's reply that precedent pressure survives either way was real but did not neutralize the asymmetric institutional argument. A also landed the unpledged-public-funding and non-party-leakage points, but B defused the latter cleanly ("approving does not purchase jurisdiction") and blunted the former by distinguishing paying a verifier from summoning one into existence. B's pressure questions extracted the two concessions that decided the framing (no verifier, no uncompensated revocation rule), and B answered A's trap honestly rather than dodging. Both sides were well-grounded without fake sourcing; B's closings ("You can revoke a licence. You cannot revoke the loss of the 'before'") crystallized the stakes more memorably.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's pressure question — "if a contractor meets your checklist next year, does it get its decision?" — met by B's direct "Yes" in Rebuttal 2, immediately reframed as a dispute over order: applications judged now by a regulator that cannot certify its own controls versus a verdict rendered by a verifier that exists first. B then compounded it by noting A never named a certifying body or an uncompensated revocation rule, converting A's "drafting problem" into an unenforceable promise.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): A narrowly wins on the proposition as written. Its pause permits exploration and ends when qualifying baselines are independently verified. B repeatedly attacks refusing to consider applications, rather than pausing approvals; its eventual requirement of verification before any approval substantially accepts the motion. B handles funding and revocation pressure better, while A overstates B's willingness to approve prematurely and delays its A2 answer. Those weaknesses keep the result close but do not resolve B's central mismatch with the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's explicit willingness to approve a qualifying application undercuts B's indefinite-blanket-pause objection. B then confirms that its own model permits no approval before independent certification, weakening its claimed alternative to the motion.
