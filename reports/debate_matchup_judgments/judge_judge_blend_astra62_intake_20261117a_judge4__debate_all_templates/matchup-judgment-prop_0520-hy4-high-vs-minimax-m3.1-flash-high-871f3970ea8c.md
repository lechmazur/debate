- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won clearly through sharper comparative framing, stronger institutional fit, and cleaner execution. Side A’s closing largely repeated that harms occur only at hiring and characterized the test as an employer-written proxy, without squarely neutralizing that supply-side argument. A was more polished, complete, and rhetorically incisive, while B’s repeated clipping keeps the margin narrow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A’s demand for a mechanism linking eligibility to displacement, followed by B’s answer that an expanded eligible pool changes employer leverage and substitution incentives even before any particular funded post is filled; B’s closing then exposed A’s post-by-post audit as largely reactive.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B won solidly through sharper institutional framing, superior sequencing logic, and cleaner execution. Side B persuasively separated occupational licensing (which tests competence) from immigration policy (which tests whether enlarging labor supply is justified on fair wage and recruitment terms), exposing Side A's repeated objection that job offers do not prove clinical skill—persisting into Closing—as a category error. A offered credible portability and safety arguments, but never squarely explained why preliminary recognition and candidate registries could not address matching without making credentials the basis for admission. A’s clipped late speeches also left its case incompletely crystallized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, turned A’s public-infrastructure concession into support for a tested gate, and answered the circularity objection with preliminary verification and registries.
