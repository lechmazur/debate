### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined and format-compliant, and the debate narrowed cleanly to two live clashes. On the fairness/queue-jumping route, A's "zero visa slots" answer was never truly neutralized—B's "parallel door, not a queue" restated the objection rather than defeating it, and the harm remained abstract. On the internal-contradiction route, A's pressure questions forced B into real concessions ("report, not the job," rooted residents as "residue" surviving on aid and cash work), which A exploited concretely in rebuttal and closing. B's strongest surviving material was the recurring-regularization incentive and the memorable "a lease is not a visa" framing, and B answered A's statute-of-limitations analogy effectively. But B's defense ultimately asked the judge to prefer principle over a documented practical collapse, and A made the consequences of that collapse easier to see. A wins a clear but competitive debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's Q2 pressure on how anti-retaliation rules can coexist with punishing employers for hiring unauthorized workers. B honestly conceded the state protects "the report, not the job," and A's closing turned that concession into a vivid practical contradiction: a whistleblower loses the job and cannot lawfully find another, handing predatory employers absolute leverage.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B kept the central objection live: the pathway makes duration and records created during unlawful residence the source of legal title. Side A showed legalization would be useful and exposed real harshness in non-status enforcement, but its record defense narrowed to eligibility fraud and its queue answer never justified a parallel door over applicants waiting abroad. B also answered the retaliation and survival pressure with a coherent, if uncomfortable, distinction between protecting reports and granting work status.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's second rebuttal on records and the cutoff: leases and filings prove duration, not lawful priority, while a retrospective date plus future enforcement is a promise that future residents can invoke again—captured in 'a lease is not a visa.'
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO made the practical consequences of CON’s alternative decisive: without mass deportation, denying work authorization creates an unavoidable underground economy and hollow labor protections. CON kept a principled fairness/deterrence objection, but its workarounds (firewalls, voluntary departure, anti-retaliation without work permits) became less credible under pressure, especially the admission that remedies protect “the report, not the job.” PRO answered pressure questions directly and tied its case back to the motion’s conditions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s pressure on anti-retaliation forced Side A to concede that an unauthorized whistleblower would be protected in reporting but not kept in lawful employment; B then turned that into the clearest proof that CON’s model cannot realistically deter exploitation.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A razor-thin win for PRO. Both sides were competent and format-compliant, with B landing the most rhetorically vivid moments (the "countdown to the next regularization demand" framing and the closing dilemma over enforcement). However, B's central contradiction charges partially dissolve on inspection: A consistently distinguished mass removal of settled, screened residents from ordinary enforcement against ineligible recent arrivals, and A's new-hire penalties target future inflows rather than the settled cohort, so "both cannot be true" overstates the tension. Meanwhile, A's live route survived the full exchange: B's enforcement-only endpoint still leaves settled residents in a poorer shadow economy, and B's "gradual voluntary return" mechanism remained asserted rather than demonstrated. A answered both pressure questions directly with the required A1/A2 structure, and the records rebound (if no usable records exist, workplace verification cannot work either) was never fully neutralized—B's employer-audit asymmetry answer helped but did not close it. B also cited nonexistent reference IDs ([P1.2, P1.3]), a small execution cost. B's closing was sharper than A's, but over the whole debate A's case held together slightly better under pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's Pressure Question Q1 (what happens to those who miss the cutoff) colliding with A's A4.1 answer distinguishing mass removal of settled screened residents from ordinary enforcement against ineligible arrivals. B's B4.3 tried to crystallize this as a fatal contradiction, but A's population distinction held, while A's counterpressure—demanding the mechanism by which job loss yields "voluntary return" rather than a poorer shadow class—went only weakly answered (B's "financial purpose" assertion) and remained a live route to victory for PRO through the closing.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner live route: a cutoff either relies on enforcement A says drives people into cash work, or cannot stop cash work and will be overtaken by the next settled cohort. A answered with cumulative proof and a smaller residual, but never fully resolved that concession, the replenishment/vacuum incentive, or diversion from lawful applicants. A's registration-versus-limbo case remained strong, yet B turned A's own cutoff enforcement into evidence that enforcement could work without rewarding unlawful persistence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and closing dilemma was decisive: using A4.1's removal/voluntary-departure concession to argue that if ordinary enforcement can hold a cutoff tomorrow, it can address limbo today, while if it cannot, the cutoff is illusory.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive clash about state capacity and present harm: a conditional pathway creates records, screening, and a bounded lawful class, while CON’s enforcement-only model leaves settled residents in exploitable limbo. Side A’s pull-factor, queue-fairness, and cutoff-contradiction arguments were sharp, but its voluntary-return mechanism stayed speculative and it never fully answered the point that enforcement becomes easier once the population is registered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 records-dilemma rebound: if evidence is too weak for pathway screening, it is also too weak for CON’s enforcement; if it is enough for enforcement, it can support conditional screening.
