### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won the comparative clash. PRO built a vivid case that self-censorship is invisible, cumulative, and models fear, but A repeatedly pressed a dilemma: if suppression is truly invisible, students cannot learn the claimed lesson, and if it is visible through documents, complaints, or patterns, it is bounded enough to inspect and challenge. A also sustained the more direct harm comparison: a delivered one-sided lesson reaches captive children and cannot be unheard, while an omitted unit, though real, is content loss that can be identified and repaired. B's final reframe to hidden permanent gaps was rhetorically strong but overgeneralized and did not resolve the invisibility/visibility problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.3-A4.4 was the decisive exchange: PRO cannot make invisibility deepen the harm while using visible documentary pressure to prove it; if the suppression is visible, it becomes contestable, and if it is invisible, the extra student-level lesson in fear fades.

A also answered the accountability-versus-coercion question directly. B's closing weakened its progression by reviving the disputed claim that students learn fear from unseen omissions and simply declaring content-related injuries limited to a term.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A challenged B's reversibility asymmetry: the curriculum records B proposed for detecting suppression also offer a route to contesting omissions, while correcting a delivered lesson does not necessarily undo humiliation or lost trust.
