### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the decisive clashes. A dismantled the Iran example (B conceded it was kidney-only and "imperfect," yet still banked its results), broke the firefighting analogy with the sharp point that in organ sale "the injury is the transaction," and constructed a dilemma from B's own concession about refusing fees that override judgment: either the capped fee persuades the poor into irreversible surgery (price doing the pressuring) or it fails and the deaths continue while the market infrastructure is built anyway. B's "that is the safeguard working" response was the best available recovery and the "we choose the hospital" frame was memorable, but it never restored the lives-saved payoff that B's case depended on. B also leaned on confident Iran specifics ("eliminated entirely, outcomes matching Western programs") without ground to verify, while A's mechanisms (screened-out donors staying with brokers, cleared list ≠ supply) were derived from B's own design. A also landed the point that B's own scheme cannot supply hearts, blunting the "alternatives can't supply hearts" attack. Both sides followed format, answered pressure questions with A1/A2, and crystallized well in closings; the margin is real but B kept the black-market exploitation route alive throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 exploitation of B's concession: "You cannot claim lives saved by an inducement you promise never to offer." B's closing counter—that a system that can refuse a coercive price is the safeguard working, not a flaw—was B's strongest moment, but it left B's supply-side benefit largely unclaimed.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B built the clearer path to victory by challenging both Iran’s evidentiary value and the assumption that legal donation displaces brokers, then turning A’s fee cap into a dilemma: either payment drives supply by pressuring poor donors, or the promised supply gains disappear. B’s alternatives did not fully answer residual waitlist deaths, keeping the debate competitive, but B’s comparison was sharper and better developed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal decisively exploited A’s capped-fee answer: A could not bank lives saved from an inducement it promised to withhold if it became coercive. A’s closing called this a safeguard, but did not resolve the supply-versus-consent tradeoff.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won the central clashes: Iran failed the motion's strict-regulation test while being banked for results, screening plausibly leaves broker clientele underground, and B's capped fee created a dilemma—either it induces poor donors or it saves no extra lives. ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the cleaner path to victory. B consistently forced the comparison the motion actually poses — a regulated lawful system versus the status-quo waiting list and black market, not versus an altruistic paradise — and gave a decision ruler (net organs transplanted, net donors harmed). Side A's strongest pressure, the monopsony/coercion question, was answered directly in B's Rebuttal 2 (the state keeps no margin, is auditable and suable, unlike the broker). A's crowding-out pressure was neutralized by B's arithmetic about the related-donor pool versus stranger supply. Meanwhile, A's answer to B's death-count question — refusing to name any metric — was honest but left B's charge of an unweighed status-quo body count standing, and B's closing ("a symbol and a surgeon") crystallized that clash memorably. A's later speeches leaned heavily on reworded earlier points (monopsony, screening-manages-risk-not-coercion) without narrowing the dispute, and the closing was largely recap. B answered pressure questions with required A1/A2 labels, adapted across rounds, and made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to the crowding-out question: A demanded proof that altruism survives commodification, and B replied that one-for-one displacement is arithmetically impossible because altruistic living donation draws mostly from matching relatives and friends — a small fraction of the list — while compensated donation recruits the strangers who constitute the actual missing supply. This defused A's one quantitative route to victory and left A retreating to a purely deontological frame B had already flagged as refusing to count the dead.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A built the stronger comparative case: prohibition leaves poverty and unsafe underground sales intact, while regulation offers screening, accountability, and enforceable follow-up. A answered both pressure questions directly and turned B’s refusal to weigh dialysis deaths into a decisive liability. B’s coercion and state-monopsony objections were serious and well expressed, but B mostly restated them without showing that prohibition protects donors better or that its alternatives suffice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure question asking what dialysis death toll B would accept, followed by B’s refusal to set a number and A’s closing contrast between symbolic state innocence and concrete donor protection, most clearly exposed the weakness in B’s position.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner comparative case: prohibition leaves poverty and black-market sales intact while regulated compensation adds screened supply, enforceable follow-up, and need-based allocation. CON's desperation and monopsony objections were serious, but CON refused to weigh dialysis deaths or show alternatives could meet need, and repeated that regulation would fail rather than proving a legal system worse than the unregulated one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2: the broker-versus-state accountability distinction and the scale argument against one-for-one crowding out, followed by exploiting A's refusal to name any acceptable cost for prohibition.
