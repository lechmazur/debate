### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Both sides built genuinely strong, mechanism-rich cases with honest epistemics. A's best assets were the suicide lethality asymmetry, the private-sale loophole point (A2.3), and forcing B's concession that weapon substitution carries zero lethality cost. But B did more damage to A's core routes over the round: the bartender analogy (A4.2) was knocked out by the licensed/inspected vs. invisible parking-lot sale distinction (B4.6); the "same single gun, delayed" claim was met with the untraceable-handgun substitution answer (B4.1) and the observation that a cousin is no slower than a store; and A's closing "cost pool bounded by benefit pool" was directly refuted by B's heads-vs-deaths sorting argument (B5.2), which went unanswered. B also won the burden exchange: A admitted only a "modest" reduction it would not distinguish from zero, which sits poorly with a motion framed as "reduces more than." A's suicide case survived B's pressure better than A's homicide case survived B's, but the decisive clashes late (enforceability of the private-sale margin, magnitude, sorting) all broke toward B. B's three clipped turns are a real but minor execution cost since the arguments were mostly complete. Narrow-to-clear win for CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The A4.2 bartender-vs-B4.6 parking-lot exchange over whether casual private sellers would card under-21 buyers, sealed by B's closing turn of A5.3 ("same pistol, same table, felony attached to a sale nobody sees") plus B5.2's sorting-by-obedience refutation of A's pool bound — the sequence that dismantled A's central friction mechanism and endgame frame.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by pinning the motion's "more than" burden and keeping it live. Side A landed real hits—private handgun sales mean this is not a rifle-only law, less-lethal substitution is not a cost, and purchases can later become the drawer—but never showed an overall-rate drop large enough to clear admitted illegal diversion. Side B's selection point (the law sorts by obedience, so deterred buyers are not the deaths that move the rate), the unfinished dealer-ban experiment, and the unenforceability of invisible private sales were not squarely neutralized. Clipped turns cost Side B some finish, but the closing still made the cleaner vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing break of Side A's pool bound: the motion counts deaths, not deterred heads, and Side A's own "same unrecorded pistol" concession meant neither the weapon nor the timing had been shown to change.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): PRO effectively reframed substitution as a benefit and identified the private-handgun-sale margin, but CON more convincingly showed that margin is small and poorly enforceable, that existing dealer restrictions already failed to prevent disproportionate handgun violence, and that PRO's admittedly modest reduction could not be distinguished from zero. CON's closing sorting argument also broke PRO's claim that costs are bounded by the benefit pool: deterred buyers are disproportionately law-abiding, while determined buyers retain access through faster, untraceable illegal channels.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's closing response to PRO's benefit-pool bound was decisive: the motion counts deaths, not deflected buyers, and the law preferentially deters the least dangerous buyers while dangerous buyers shift to the same handguns through invisible private or straw channels.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): This was an exceptionally close, well-matched debate. Side A (PRO) landed the sharpest individual blows: the "self-defeating" turn in rebuttal 1, the durability reversal, the scope correction on private handgun sales, and masterful exploitation of CON's "roughly none, directly" concession, capped by memorable rhetoric ("a hunter's paperwork violation is not a funeral"). However, Side B (CON) did the more disciplined work on the motion as written. B systematically narrowed the benefit case: handguns already barred at the counter, young killers mostly off-counter, young suicides reaching household guns, and the stacked-conditions portrait of the "margin of a margin" person CON conceded exists but rarely. Critically, when PRO dismissed non-lethal leakage as mere "receipts," CON correctly noted the proposition explicitly names increased illegal acquisition as the cost side, and PRO never defended its commensuration frame against that textual challenge, instead reasserting it in closing. CON also repaired the apparent self-contradiction cleanly in B2.1, blunted the mass-shooter examples as a tiny share of overall rates, and effectively doubted the seller-liability enforcement analogy. PRO's superior polish and concession-hunting kept this razor-thin, but CON's structural narrowing plus the unanswered motion-literalism gives it the deciding edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange began with PRO's Pressure Q1 forcing CON to admit the rerouted lawful buyers cause "roughly none, directly" deaths. PRO rode that concession to the closing ("lives versus receipts"), but CON's B4.1/B5.1 response — that the motion as written weighs increases in illegal acquisition, not bodies, and that "for lawful buyers leakage is total by construction" — was the reply that kept CON's path to victory alive and went essentially unanswered on the merits, deciding the frame fight that determined the debate.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the motion’s comparison intact: a thin effect on overall firearm death rates against a structural increase in illegal acquisition. It separated killers already outside legal retail from lawful buyers pushed off-book, showed the handgun counter limit already exists, and made a store purchase a poor fit for an impulsive crisis. Side A’s sharper close treated nonlethal leakage as irrelevant, which restated a different weighing rather than answering that burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s split between killers never at the counter and lawful buyers migrated into illegal transfers flipped A’s claim that B’s case defeated itself, and that comparison controlled the rest of the debate.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B powerfully narrowed the benefit: dealer handgun sales are already restricted, many offenders obtain guns illegally, and many suicides use household guns. But Side A won the central comparison by forcing B to concede that rerouted lawful purchases cause roughly no deaths directly while the person saved by friction does exist. A also answered the background-check bridge by noting that the unchecked private channel already exists and seller liability would restrict rather than create it. B's transaction-counting interpretation never explained why paperwork leakage should outweigh prevented deaths.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's use of B's pressure answers in Rebuttal 2 and the closing: B admitted the off-book hunter causes roughly no deaths and that the impulsive buyer blocked by the law is real, crystallized as lives versus receipts.
