### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A narrowly wins by preserving the stronger marginal-dollar mechanism: targeted civilian contact can interrupt retaliation before an imminent shooting plan forms, while retaining police for emergencies. A directly answered both pressure questions and explained how support infrastructure could extend credible insiders’ reach. B’s insistence that emergency referrals permanently destroy trust was unsupported, and its claimed fatal concession confused complementary enforcement with comparative superiority. However, B effectively exposed A’s inconsistent treatment of post-shooting prevention, and A inadequately answered targeted policing rather than general patrol.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s distinction between ordinary mediation and a specific imminent threat undercut B’s central trap: calling police for emergencies does not establish that additional police outperform earlier civilian prevention. B’s strongest counter was that hospital intervention, like incapacitation, can prevent subsequent shootings despite occurring after an initial victim.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by forcing and then exploiting Side A's concession that imminent armed retaliation must be handed to police. That concession hollowed out the opening's flagship mechanism, left scaled intervention as lower-yield rumor and logistics work, and made the trust-destruction and non-scalable credibility problems decisive. Side A kept a coherent upstream theory and rightly noted the missing numerical comparison, but never repaired how a disclosed police trigger preserves voluntary bedside access or how apprentices substitute for scarce credible mediators. After the exchange, fewer shootings per dollar favored officers' deterrence and incapacitation over a program that outsources live crises and risks blinding itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first and second rebuttals trapped the "fire alarm" rule: the cousin with the gun is either an imminent threat that must be reported—outsourcing prevention and burning trust—or a threat withheld to protect mediation. Side A's answers confirmed the handoff and narrowed scaling to support staff, which Side B then used to collapse the cost-effectiveness claim.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly because it gave a more direct marginal-dollar case, answered the pressure questions cleanly, and kept the focus on cheaper early intervention across conflict chains. B landed a sharp dilemma about imminent threats, but overstated that it automatically collapsed PRO’s case and never supplied a concrete cost-effectiveness comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s trap forcing A to say whether the “cousin with the gun” must be reported, and A’s reply distinguishing a narrow emergency “fire alarm” from a general informant pipeline.
