### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B wins through stronger comparative rebuttal: identifying information does not itself secure medical cooperation or identify siblings, while A's proposed enforcement leaves informal arrangements accessible and risks penalizing children. A retains a meaningful identity-autonomy argument, but repeatedly declares it fundamental rather than explaining why it outweighs B's child-centered harms. Its closing incorrectly reduces those harms to adult convenience. B overstates how inevitably shortages produce wholly unprotected conceptions, and its three clipped turns cost execution credit, but its direct answers and sharper comparisons still establish a solid advantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B corrected A's attack on “static” medical files by pointing out that its proposal expressly required ongoing updates. Its subsequent distinction between anonymity to offspring and anonymity to clinics directly answered A's pressure question, while showing that disclosure of a name cannot itself compel cooperation.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B won by turning PRO's own harms: a stipulated supply fall pushes conception to unscreened private or overseas routes with worse medical records, incest risk, and identifiability. B also showed clinic-held updated medical files and mandatory registries outperform a mere name, which compels no reply and lists no siblings. A’s rights principle remained largely symbolic, and its proposed regulations either could not reach private arrangements or punished the child.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 and 2 correction that it proposed updated, clinic-relayed files and mandatory registries—not static voluntary ones—while a donor’s name neither compels medical disclosure nor reveals half-siblings, leaving PRO’s practical remainder as a symbolic entitlement.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the central rights claim live: donor-conceived adults have an autonomous entitlement to identifying information that cannot be traded away merely to preserve donation supply. B answered the main pressure with regulatory guardrails, critiqued CON’s alternatives as donor-controlled and incomplete, and used the adoption analogy effectively. Side A made a strong practical case that reduced supply would push conceptions into less regulated channels and that clinics, registries, and limits could address many harms, but it never fully neutralized the rights frame and suffered repeated clipping execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The sharpest exchange was A’s clinic-mediated updates/registry challenge versus B’s reply that evasion and partial effectiveness do not justify surrendering a fundamental identity right; B’s rights-over-evasion framing preserved PRO’s decisive route.
