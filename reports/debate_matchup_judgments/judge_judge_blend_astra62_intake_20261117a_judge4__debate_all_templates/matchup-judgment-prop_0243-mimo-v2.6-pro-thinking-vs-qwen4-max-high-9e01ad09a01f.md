### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO (Side A) wins because it built a positive causal account that stayed standing, while CON (Side B) increasingly fell back on "net unproven." PRO's case had three load-bearing pieces: - Deadheading adds miles with no passenger. - The uncapped fleet grew far beyond the old taxi medallion ceiling. - Trips pulled from transit, walking, and new travel are pure additions. PRO also used the near-saturation and curb-stop mechanism to explain why a small share of regional miles can matter heavily at downtown bottlenecks. CON raised legitimate offsets: taxi displacement, parking search, and shed household cars. The ownership point was its strongest. But CON never offered any positive evidence that the net effect was neutral or beneficial. Its handling of PRO's Q2 ("name one city where delay fell") was a burden dodge rather than an answer. The decisive moves: - PRO's A2 argued that every offset CON names draws on a fixed, pre-existing stock, while platform supply had no ceiling. - PRO's closing showed that CON's own list of trip sources includes transit, walking, and induced trips. Those terms have no road-space counterpart, while CON's offsets merely trade road space for road space. CON's replies were that this term was unsized and that some displaced trips were bus rides. These were fair but partial. The bus point is weak, because the bus still runs regardless. CON's attempt to turn the stopping-events metric (a parked car sits all afternoon) was neatly answered: congestion is the lane, not the parking lot. Both sides stayed epistemically disciplined. PRO's San Francisco planners reference is plausible and used modestly, though CON fairly noted it is a single city. Overall, PRO's directional reasoning beats CON's agnosticism under a proposition that asks which outcome is more likely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing argued that CON's offsets (taxi cruising, parking search, household car legs) merely swap road space for road space. By contrast, the transit, walk, and induced-trip conversions that CON itself acknowledged have nothing on the opposite side of the ledger. This turned CON's "the ratio decides" move into a directional argument. CON's only reply was that the term was unsized, which did not neutralize it.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B wins through the stronger qualitative causal comparison: trips diverted from transit and walking add road traffic, while deadheading and lane-blocking intensify congestion at bottlenecks. A correctly rejects any obligation to prove improvement and damages B's bounded-offsets versus unbounded-growth argument. Nevertheless, A's ownership and parking savings remain speculative, and its claim that the San Francisco attribution ignored displaced miles is unsupported. B does not establish precise net magnitudes, but its mechanisms and qualitative evidence make worsening more persuasive than A's repeated insistence that missing ratios prevent any verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing distinguishes vehicle-for-vehicle substitutions from genuinely additional trips and directly defeats A's parked-car comparison: occupying parking space is not equivalent to blocking a traffic lane. This sharpens the net comparison after A's legitimate objection that offsets can scale with adoption.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO built the cleaner net mechanism: deadheading plus trips drawn from transit, walking, and new travel add road load with no corresponding road saving. CON showed offsets are real, but never showed they plausibly outweigh that uncancelled term, relying mainly on unmeasured-net skepticism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and closing were decisive: taxi, parking, and ownership savings are bounded or road-for-road swaps, while transit/walk/new-trip conversions add a vehicle and remove none, breaking CON's scaling objection.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): PRO wins a competitive debate on the strength of its causal story. It is coherent and plausible: deadheading, substitution away from transit and walking, induced trips, and curb friction concentrated at peak bottlenecks. PRO also anchored this early in the general direction of urban research on added VMT, and CON never directly disputed that point. PRO's key move in Rebuttal 1 was to argue that congestion is set by peak bottlenecks rather than metro-wide averages. That substantially neutralized CON's "small share" denominator argument. CON was the sharper rebutter. Its best points were: - the confounders (downtown job growth, delivery vans) concentrate in the same cores, which undercuts PRO's within-city "control"; - reverse causation; - choke-point nonlinearity also amplifies any relief, not just harm. Still, CON's affirmative offset rests on the private-car-plus-parking-search counterfactual. PRO's closing correctly identified that this is not the main growth market. CON's netting example also glossed over the fact that ride-hail makes return trips too. CON ultimately retreated to "unproven is not proven." That is a legitimate burden argument, but it feels thinner against a mechanism that is widely accepted and largely unrebutted on its own terms. PRO's closing leaned on assertion more than new weighing, which keeps the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.4–B4.5 was the sharpest exchange. It argued that downtown job growth and the delivery boom concentrated exactly where pickups clustered, so PRO's within-city exposure comparison was "the same correlation twice." This seriously damaged PRO's best attempt at causal isolation. PRO recovered only partially by returning in its closing to the question of what ride-hail actually replaced.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): A narrowly wins by exposing the gap between B's plausible congestion mechanisms and its overall net conclusion. B effectively defeated the regional-share defense, but A undermined B's claimed causal comparison and challenged its unsupported assumption that transit, walking, and induced trips dominated substitution. B demanded demonstrated offsets without comparably establishing its own net balance. A's parking and airport savings were also speculative, keeping the margin narrow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest rebuttal showed that downtown job growth and delivery traffic concentrate in the same corridors as ride-hail pickups. B therefore could not treat lower-exposure corridors as controls merely because they shared a metropolitan economy. This directly damaged B's attempted causal grounding rather than simply demanding more statistics.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the motion net, network-wide, and causal. It exposed Side A's unweighted substitution assumption, supplied plausible parking, taxi-matching, and chained-trip offsets on the bottleneck terrain itself, and showed that downtown exposure also tracks concentrated jobs, construction, and deliveries. Side A's deadheading and curb mechanisms remained plausible but did not prove they dominated overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: short dense-core deadheads and removed parking searches turned Side A's nonlinearity both ways, while the point that confounders concentrated in the same downtown cores dismantled Side A's claimed within-city control.
