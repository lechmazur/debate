### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): This was a close, high-quality exchange. CON's strongest work was its framing. It separated "allowing" stabilizers to operate from "expanding" them, pressed PRO for a stopping threshold, and showed that unemployment triggers limit eligibility rather than total liabilities or interest exposure. Its supply-shock benchmark rebuttal was also sharp. PRO still held the cleaner path to victory on the motion as written. The motion asks whether expansion should proceed despite *elevated* debt, not despite a financing crisis. PRO's answer that only market closure justifies withholding expansion therefore fits the proposition rather than conceding it away. PRO also made the comparative point that CON's alternative still involves added borrowing. CON's alternative (prompt targeted support, contingency buffers, borrowing with a servicing path) converged toward PRO's position. It differed mainly by routing aid through discretionary procedures, which PRO showed brings delay and no automatic exit. CON never gave a concrete version of its "credible limits" that distinguished it in practice. PRO's scarring irreversibility argument was the more developed and more concrete harm. CON's counter-irreversibility (a financing squeeze forcing abrupt cuts) stayed largely hypothetical. CON also paid an execution cost for two clipped speeches. The margin is thin because CON's threshold pressure and supply-shock points were real and only partly neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange came after the pressure questions. CON's Q1 demanded a fiscal stopping point, and PRO answered "short of outright market closure." CON turned this into an "unlimited borrowing license" argument, noting that triggers bound eligibility, not liabilities. PRO countered that CON's own alternative "still grows the liability stock" while adding political delay. That kept PRO's comparative advantage intact, because CON had already conceded that added borrowing for protection is acceptable.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): A wins a clear but competitive debate. B’s macroeconomic case for automatic stabilizers was vivid and initially strong, but A turned the decisive threshold clash: B effectively said no debt-service or rollover stress short of outright market closure would justify withholding expansion, which made elevated debt irrelevant to the motion. A then showed that unemployment triggers bound eligibility and wage replacement, not total liabilities, interest costs, or rollover exposure. A also neutralized B’s supply-shock answer by exposing the pre-shock income benchmark and preserved a cleaner bounded-protection alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s rebuttal 2 exploited B’s “short of outright market closure” answer to show B had declined the very fiscal discipline the motion demands, and that unemployment triggers cap eligibility but not the debt liabilities or interest costs that elevated debt makes relevant.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B established meaningful benefits from preventing benefit cliffs and economic scarring. But its explicit refusal to withhold expansion short of market closure allowed A to win the financing-constraint clash: automatic expiry does not eliminate accumulated debt or rollover exposure. A also directly defended timely, conditionally financed protection, which B continued to portray as necessarily delayed or contractionary. A's two clipped turns count against execution, but its central objections remained insufficiently answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal distinguished wage-replacement caps from limits on fiscal exposure and dismantled B's supply-shock answer: keeping demand below pre-shock income does not prevent inflation when production has also fallen. These directly exposed the limits of B's proposed safeguards.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): PRO built the cleaner path to victory on the motion as written. Its core comparison held throughout: temporary, rule-bound support versus a deeper slump that shrinks the GDP denominator and erodes revenue. It also drew a credible line between debt that is merely elevated and acute loss of market access. CON's strongest material was the nonlinear sovereign-risk story and the debt-ratchet asymmetry. Both, however, leaned on framing PRO's case as "uncapped autopilot," which PRO plausibly rejected as a strawman. CON also asserted, rather than demonstrated, that risk premia reliably spike whenever debt is high. PRO's pressure questions exposed a real gap. CON's "explicit review" listed indicators but offered no decision threshold and no protection against delay or veto. CON's answer that discretion "does not cause a deeper slump" was a bare assertion. Reprioritizing a fixed budget was never shown to offset an economy-wide collapse in revenue. CON scored on PRO's growing list of safeguards, framing them as concessions. Its "published cliff" dilemma, that a distress trigger either fires too late or invites front-running, was the sharpest single argument, and PRO's closing never answered it directly. That keeps the result competitive. PRO's opening was clipped, a minor execution cost, though only the final sentence was lost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.5 turned CON's demand for exact crisis thresholds back on CON. It pointed out that "explicit review" has no specified test, timing, or veto protection, and that discretion is where political delay actually occurs. CON's best counter was B4.4/B5.3: statutory distress triggers either fire after yields spike or invite selling ahead of a published cliff. PRO left this partly unanswered, but it applied mainly to acute distress rather than to the general case of elevated debt.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner comparative case: elevated debt justifies bounded, inflation-sensitive design rather than suspension, and CON never supplied a workable threshold or showed discretion would be more timely or politically reliable. CON's sovereign-risk and nonlinear-trigger attacks were sharp, but PRO exposed the strawman of uncapped autopilot and repeatedly forced CON to answer why delay and reprioritization would protect the tax base better. Despite a clipped opening, PRO's weighing of counterfactual damage versus possible crisis was more persuasive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that CON demands an exact crisis threshold from automatic rules while offering none for explicit review, and never explains why legislatures would deliver recovery surpluses more reliably after a deeper slump, was the most decisive exchange.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): B wins by making the stronger comparison between bounded automatic support and the economic costs of discretionary delay. A raises legitimate inflation and nonlinear financing risks, but does not establish that elevated debt generally makes those risks outweigh stabilization benefits. B directly answers the pressure with inflation-sensitive rules and financing safeguards without abandoning automatic expansion as the default. A supplies indicators for review, but its assurance of rapid legislative action remains unsupported, and budget reprioritization never adequately answers falling aggregate revenues and demand.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal was that leftover borrowing does not establish failed stabilization: debt sustainability must include the GDP and revenues preserved relative to withholding support. A answered with possible yield spirals, but leaned too heavily on treating elevated debt as evidence that those spirals would dominate.
