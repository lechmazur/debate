That sharpened A’s broader objection that selective historical signals can displace the quantity and price facts most useful to shoppers. B’s responses were competent but relied too heavily on asserting that notices prompt comparison rather than bias.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The promotional-price exchange was decisive: A’s Q2 elicited B’s base-to-base rule, and A4.3 then exposed its perverse treatment of identical $3.50 purchases. A5.3 completed the point by noting that any later sale-ending price rise would itself be visible.

A persuasively framed the notice as a prompt to compare rather than a verdict against the cheaper product, leaving B's steering and implementation objections narrower than A's habit-exploitation case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's first rebuttal exposed the central weakness in B's alternative: displaying “450 grams” does not tell a repeat buyer anything changed, whereas a shrinkflation flag breaks autopilot and triggers the current-price comparison B favored.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the central problem clearer: shrinkflation works because habitual shoppers and one-tap reorders do not notice unchanged headline prices paired with smaller packages.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the pressure questions with a plausible base-price and permanent-replacement mechanism, while turning A’s autopilot concession into the main reason special shrinkflation disclosure is needed.
