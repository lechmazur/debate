### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B won because it kept the debate focused on the motion's core variable: adaptive feedback. B convincingly argued that engagement-optimized feeds learn a teenager's emotional weak points and escalate comparison, conflict, or dread in a way chronological and non-personalized feeds cannot. A's peer-panopticon and supportive-community case was plausible, but B neutralized its strongest benefit by showing algorithms optimize attention, not welfare, and that A's retention metrics do not distinguish supportive use from compulsive anxious use. A's churn/diversity defense ultimately rested on platform incentives rather than what the architecture actually rewards, leaving B with the cleaner comparative route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question about distinguishing an anxious teenager scrolling compulsively from one scrolling because they feel supported exposed the soft spot in A's satisfaction argument. A's answer listed shares, saves, and multi-day returns, but those are all compatible with compulsive return to distressing content, which B then used to close off the off-ramp defense.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly: adaptive reinforcement supplies the stronger comparative causal mechanism, while A’s distinction between retention and wellbeing undercuts B’s main safety argument. B correctly challenges the finite-feed premise and offers plausible discovery benefits, but overstates how inevitably alternative feeds impose peer surveillance or sensationalism. A’s repeated finite-feed claim, nonexistent reference IDs, and incomplete response to diversity and satisfaction signals prevent a clearer victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest rebuttal explains that shares, saves, and repeated visits can reflect compulsive distress rather than support. This materially weakens B’s claim that durable retention protects wellbeing, although A does not fully answer B’s separate satisfaction-signal and content-diversity responses.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live comparative mechanism: engagement feeds learn an individual's dwell signals and cumulatively escalate comparison, while chronological and non-personalized feeds are static or generic. CON's peer-panopticon and viral-outrage alternatives were strong, but its off-ramp depended on speculative satisfaction and diversity safeguards. PRO directly showed that returns, shares, and saves cannot distinguish supported scrolling from compulsive, anxious scrolling, leaving CON's central defense unproven.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's answer to CON's retention defense was decisive: multi-day returns, shares, and saves are fully compatible with dread-driven compulsive use, so they do not show that adaptation distinguishes healing content from content that merely holds attention.
