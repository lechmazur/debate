### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A won because it made the cost-control contradiction the decisive unresolved clash: the motion binds insurers to cover but does not bind manufacturers to accept value-based terms. Side B's preferred-competition answer did not restore real leverage once the right to say no was removed, and the outcomes contracts either pay out when short-term weight loss is achieved or abandon the long-term outcomes that justify the price. Side B's disease-access case was strong, but Side A's sharper rebuttals and clearer comparative weighing made the spending risk more vivid. The clipped rebuttals were a modest execution cost but not enough to flip.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dilemma was the most decisive: if prior authorization and step therapy survive under the mandate, the mandate changes little; if it bites, it exposes tens of millions to indefinite treatment. Reinforced by the point that the motion binds the buyer, not the seller.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the motion's cost-control half under pressure that Side A never closed. The mandate binds insurers to cover, not manufacturers to accept biting terms; short-term outcomes the drugs reliably hit would not trigger refunds, and the long-term offsets that might justify the price were left uncontracted. Class competition for preferred status already exists alongside the right to decline, and Side A never showed a must-cover class adds leverage once walk-away power is removed, especially with few dominant sellers. Side A's clinical case was clear, but it did not establish that the proposition as written controls spending. Clipped rebuttals were an execution cost for B, not enough to offset that unanswered structural gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's fork that the mandate cannot be both harmless to bargaining and transformative for access, pressed through the preferred-position exchange: A never showed competition inside a must-cover class restrains price once the right to say no is removed.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON won the central mechanism clash. PRO argued class-wide competition and utilization controls preserve leverage, but CON's dilemma held: if prior authorization and continuation rules really contain use, the mandate changes little; if it bites, the broad label, reliable response, and indefinite treatment create price-times-population-times-duration costs. CON also never got a satisfactory answer to why a manufacturer must accept outcome terms when only the insurer is legally bound, especially since preferred-product bidding already exists without a mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal was decisive: PRO's gates merely confirm a label covering tens of millions, non-response removal cannot contain spending if the drugs reliably work, and the proposed refund thresholds have no compelled signatory under a one-sided mandate.
