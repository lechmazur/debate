### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won the decisive clash over the "primarily" lens. A made a strong geopolitical case that import threats must be checked to preserve capacity and leverage, but B showed why the threat lens remains self-defeating: it raises affordability barriers that have driven actual green backlash, protects incumbents, and cannot guarantee cheaper domestic EVs. B effectively turned A's own concessions—welcoming localized plants and accepting climate targets as the end—into proof that tariffs are instruments for an opportunity policy, not the primary frame. The final dilemma on duty revenue undercut A's rebate mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's exploitation of A's affordability admission was most decisive: A said tariffs would fund household rebates, and B answered that duty revenue exists only while taxed imports keep arriving—so the plan either fails as protection or abandons affordability. This exposed the internal tension in A's threat-first case.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won a high-quality debate by controlling the decisive word "primarily." From the opening, B conceded calibrated tools while contesting the governing lens, which let it absorb most of A's specific policy points (duties, local content) as instruments rather than concessions. B's strongest material: the solar precedent reversal ("lost a commodity segment, gained an energy system"), the flow-vs-stock distinction that blunted A's Russian-gas analogy, and the closing's means-vs-purpose frame showing A itself called industrial defense a "precondition" for decarbonization. B also landed two unanswered contradictions: A welcoming Chinese-owned plants with shared know-how, and the rebate paradox (duty revenue funding rebates exists only if imports keep arriving). A performed well — the leverage argument for localization, the irreversibility of industrial ecosystems, and direct A1/A2 answers kept real routes open — but A's clipped opening cost it evidentiary depth, and its reliance on "political consent" predictions was effectively countered by B's "backlash is cost backlash" point. B made the debate easier to decide and closed the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 flow-vs-stock response to the Russian gas analogy ("a car is a stock... generates for decades whatever Beijing decides") combined with the concession trap on joint ventures, later crystallized in B's closing rebate paradox: A's plan to fund household rebates from border duties fails the moment the wall actually works.

Side A's irreversibility and dependency case stayed live, but the clipped opening and the weaker answer on scale versus demand left B the cleaner vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B turned A's localization concession and rebate plan: if Chinese plants with European workers are the desired end, tariffs are bargaining tools under an opportunity purpose, and duties cannot both block imports and finance household rebates.
