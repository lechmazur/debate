### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by combining the leakage and fair-competition case with a plausible phased model of published minimum outcomes, review, remediation, and EU-funded support. Side B raised serious verification, diversion, and distributional risks, but repeatedly treated the policy as immediate blanket exclusion despite A explicitly proposing targeted restrictions after remediation. B also never fully answered A's comparison that investment expands producers' capacity while enforceable access supplies the incentive to reform. Both sides incurred clipping costs, somewhat more consequentially for B in its second rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest response was that diversion limits leverage without eliminating it, while finance and conditionality are complements rather than substitutes: finance changes what producers can do, and market access changes what persistent abuse can earn. This undercut B's proposed alternatives and exposed its lack of a reliable incentive for recalcitrant producers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won the weighing clash. While PRO established that unconditional access rewards abuse, CON showed the proposed cure imposes immediate, concentrated costs on small compliant exporters via discretionary audits and held shipments, while large abusive producers can divert to looser markets. PRO conceded diversion limits gains and answered with promissory pooled certification and EU-funded support arriving after the trade restriction bites. CON's A4/A5 crystallized that as fast, certain poverty costs vs slow, contingent climate/labor gains, and never got a concrete mechanism that prevents the diversion escape or funds safeguards ex ante. That distributional + effectiveness critique survived pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal point that diversion is not a footnote but the central fact – that abusive multinationals keep revenue by rerouting while small co-ops lose their single buyer, and PRO's pooled certification/appeals are unfunded annexes after the order moves [A4.4-A4.5].
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively dismantling PRO's enforcement mechanism. While PRO built a strong normative case against profiting from abuse, CON successfully demonstrated that the practical application of mirror clauses disproportionately harms small, compliant exporters in developing nations through immediate trade restrictions, while large, abusive producers simply divert trade to non-EU markets. CON's distinction between the immediate reality of the restriction and the "promissory notes" of EU-funded mitigation was decisive in the final weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it sharply contrasted the immediate, certain harm of trade restrictions on small exporters with the slow, contingent nature of PRO's proposed mitigation, effectively neutralizing PRO's "design duty" defense.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by sustaining a sharp implementation dilemma that Side A never resolved. If compliant firms can enter regardless of national policy, the proposal becomes targeted firm-level due diligence rather than partner-level conditionality. If national policy can block those firms, innocent exporters bear costs they cannot control. Side A persuasively exposed weaknesses in soft engagement, but did not sufficiently show that broader exclusion would improve labor or climate outcomes rather than divert trade and displace workers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal most decisively exposed the contradiction in Side A’s answer: A first admitted individually compliant exporters, then restored a national veto for governments that outlaw unions or subsidize coal. B showed that this oscillation preserved either the motion’s overbreadth or its departure into narrower due diligence.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON won the interpretive clash. PRO described mirror clauses as firm/shipment certification that rewards compliant exporters, but the proposition requires the trading partner itself to meet standards. CON's pressure (A-Q1/A-Q2) forced PRO to admit that a state banning unions or subsidizing coal blocks certification, reintroducing country-wide exclusion that punishes workers PRO claimed to protect. The measurement objection — product attributes vs institutional conditions — and the innocent-exporter dilemma were never dissolved, while PRO's "engagement failed so burden shifts" did not show exclusion improves rights. Targeted enforcement remains distinct from an open-ended market veto.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R1/R2 distinction between verifying a shipment's attributes and verifying freedom of association / national climate enforcement, plus the firm-vs-country dilemma that PRO's A1 answer confirmed.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively exposing the logical and textual contradictions in Side A's mechanism. The motion requires conditioning access on "trading partners" meeting standards. Side A attempted to defend this via firm-level certification, but Side B correctly identified that this either abandons the motion's partner-wide conditionality or unjustly punishes compliant firms for their government's failures. Side A fell into this trap during the pressure questions, explicitly accepting that compliant firms in non-compliant states would be excluded. Side B effectively weaponized this concession to prove the policy is disproportionate and incoherent, while successfully distinguishing targeted product-level enforcement from the broad, open-ended conditionality proposed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantles Side A's answer to Q1. By forcing Side A to admit that firm-level compliance is overridden by a "national veto," Side B proves that the mirror clause either functions as mere firm-level due diligence (escaping the motion's text) or inflicts unjust collateral damage on innocent exporters.
