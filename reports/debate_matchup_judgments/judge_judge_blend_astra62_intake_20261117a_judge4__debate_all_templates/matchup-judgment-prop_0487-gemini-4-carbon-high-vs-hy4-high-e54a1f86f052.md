### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp, and CON was often the more quotable debater, with lines like "a hammer on a shelf is not a trajectory." CON also made real use of PRO's concession that no targeting step is eliminated. However, PRO answered that concession coherently. If every characterization step still consumes years, then eliminating multi-year fabrication beforehand is the decisive margin. To defend the four-to-six-year window, CON had to move to a "crash campaign" built on scouts launched immediately, pre-qualified parts, warm production, launch-on-call, and an impactor built in parallel. That position drifts toward standing operational readiness rather than a research niche. PRO's closing exposed the problem: parallel building also commits to generic hardware before characterization, only rushed. CON's reply that a deployed bus is equally generic is symmetric, and it leaves readiness ahead on time. CON's geometry objections against Lagrange-point staging were good, but PRO's pairing of pad-ready interceptors with deep-space craft blunted them. Because the motion contrasts deployed infrastructure with a research niche, CON's concessions narrowed the gap in PRO's favor. Grounding was mostly reasonable on both sides. PRO's "roughly a decade" and "three to seven years" claims were somewhat confident but plausible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B3.2/A4.2 into B4.1 and A5.2. CON extracted the concession that no targeting step is eliminated. PRO turned it by arguing that fabrication is the only compressible margin. When CON answered with parallel building from pre-qualified parts, PRO showed that this concedes generic pre-built hardware anyway, just rushed rather than ready.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive comparison between saving fabrication time and resolving the actual pacing problems of characterization, geometry, calibration, and authority. B directly answered both pressure questions with a parallel crash campaign and concrete launch payloads, then showed that pre-positioned generic hardware also commits before characterization. A presented vivid stakes and a plausible medium-warning case, but relied increasingly on asserted timelines and staging-orbit capabilities without establishing a unique advantage over adaptable readiness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A1/A2 directly, used A’s concession that no targeting step is eliminated, and turned the parallel-construction objection against A’s own pre-characterized, pre-positioned hardware.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B narrowly won by making the decisive solvency burden clear: permanent deployed impactors do not eliminate reconnaissance, characterization, geometry, or authorization, and therefore mainly save fabrication time. B’s pressure questions forced A to concede that no physical targeting step is eliminated, then used that concession to argue that warm production, launch contracts, and parallel building can capture much of the readiness benefit without locking in brittle, wrongly placed hardware. Side A’s middle-window urgency and “parallel build is still generic” turn were strong, but B retained the cleaner comparative edge by stressing adaptability, opportunity cost, and interception geometry.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure question forcing A to admit that standardized scouts and impactors eliminate none of the targeting steps, followed by B’s closing weighing that stored hardware buys fabrication time but not the scarcer post-characterization interception conditions.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): This was a close, competent debate, and PRO edges it. PRO's core route held up: lead time is the scarce resource, and a maintained detection-to-launch pipeline protects it. The decisive squeeze came in Q1 and A4.4. PRO showed that CON's "research niche" quietly assumed sustained surveys, notification protocols, priority launch slots, and requisition authority. PRO then argued that guaranteed, funded versions of those things are effectively the proposition, while unguaranteed versions are only hopes. CON's answer, that commercial spaceflight already provides this capacity and only dedicated hardware and standby overhead are excluded, was coherent but left the "fire-engine factory isn't a staffed fire station" point largely standing. CON's strongest weapon was the parallel-clock argument: requisition and authorization can run alongside the characterization bottleneck, so pre-building saves no drift time. That is a genuinely sharp point. However, it rested on the asserted "weeks, not years" for integrating an untested commercial bus with a tailored payload, and on a confident "decades of warning" claim that does not cover every threat. PRO's dual-use answer, a front-loaded incident-command rulebook, also neutralized CON's geopolitical turn reasonably well. CON never squarely showed that a rulebook breeds suspicion. Overall, PRO framed the burden more clearly, while CON's clock argument made the margin thin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The parallel-clock exchange was the most decisive. CON (B4.2/B5.2) argued that faster tasks running beside the characterization-and-tailoring bottleneck save zero time. PRO (A4.2/A4.4/A5.3) countered that a commercial production-line bus is not a deflection-ready bus, and that teams, interfaces, contracts, and legal authority go stale, so the avoidable bottlenecks are not reliably shorter than the unavoidable one. CON's unsupported "weeks, not years" left PRO with the slight edge.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B narrowly won by isolating the marginal value of permanent standby capability and answering A’s lead-time case with a concrete critical-path alternative: procurement, authorization, and data-sharing could run in parallel during characterization and payload tailoring, using existing spaceflight capacity. A’s maintained-pipeline framing was coherent and its pressure answers were strong, but its closing largely reasserted that avoidable bottlenecks matter without showing they shorten the critical path or justify standing costs. B’s claims of zero time savings and reliably decades-long warning were overly categorical, keeping the contest close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.2 and B5.2 were decisive: “clocks run in parallel” directly answered A’s second pressure question and exposed why clearing secondary tasks does not necessarily preserve additional post-launch drift time.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the focus on the operational gap between research knowledge and a launch-ready deflection pipeline. It forced B to import priority launch, requisition, notification, and standing capacity into the “research niche,” then argued that those guarantees are exactly what permanent infrastructure supplies. B’s critical-path and parallel-bottleneck rebuttals were sharp, but its claims about decades of warning and weeks-long requisition were less well grounded and did not fully erase A’s front-loaded legal, technical, and diplomatic readiness advantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 claim that B was smuggling operational capability into a research niche, followed by B’s reply that characterization and payload tailoring make standing kits buy zero drift time, was the decisive exchange; A’s front-loaded institutions answer held slightly better.
