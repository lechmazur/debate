### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp, and the debate stayed focused. PRO wins on the central clash over whether the league's contract adds value as a control lever. PRO's strongest move was showing that CON's safeguards mostly limit hazards the sponsorship itself creates. If the deal is refused, there are no child-kit marks or league live prompts left to police. Public regulators still oversee licensed operators either way. CON's reply, that regulators do not recreate league placement rules, never escaped this circularity. There is no placement to govern once the deal is gone. CON's best route was the comparative burden. It argued PRO never proved a net fall in youth demand once odds clips, group chats and shifted ad spend are counted. It also posed a megaphone-versus-ecology dilemma. PRO answered adequately: refusal only needs to remove a major, prestige-laden contributor, not erase the whole ecology. This makes the dilemma look like a false choice. CON landed real hits elsewhere: - PRO's "zero backfire" was an overclaim. - PRO's threshold answer was loose, with "roughly a fifth" plus a family-marketing trigger. - CON's point about spend shifting to affiliates and influencers was fair. PRO partly absorbed that last point by arguing that hero endorsement on family broadcasts is uniquely potent compared with ordinary ads. Overall, PRO's causal story about league identity being the asset being sold was clearer and more memorable. CON's case rested more on burden-of-proof pressure than on a positive account of why the retained logo is worth keeping.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 and A4.3 exchange did the most damage. PRO asked why CON bans betting marks on children's kits if passive logos are harmless, then argued that "conditional deals" praise a contract for partly curbing hazards that exist only because the league signed it. CON's answer, that adult logos are a "residual cost" of keeping the channel sanctionable, conceded the harm without showing that the controls outweigh it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by keeping the core harm live: league betting sponsorship fuses gambling with hero identity that large youth audiences actually see, and Side A's proposed conditions never remove that on-screen endorsement. Side A pressed hard on substitution, accountability, and threshold vagueness, but its answer to the key pressure admitted the residual logo is left in place and mainly shifted the burden rather than showing why that association is acceptable or confinable. B's stewardship comparison therefore remained the cleaner reason to accept the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that barring marks on children's kits and similar limits still leaves superstars wearing sportsbook branding on the broadcasts and highlights youth watch, so "conditional" deals do not cure the endorsement being sold.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): A won a close, high-quality debate by keeping the motion focused on whether leagues should lend their institutional prestige to betting around young fans. Its pressure questions exposed the limits of B’s safeguards: removing child-kit marks and youth-directed prompts leaves the central hero endorsement intact. A also answered the threshold and substitution challenges directly, then clarified that refusal need not eliminate every betting channel to remove a distinct, league-created harm. B offered excellent comparative pressure on net demand and lost contractual controls, but its recurring “megaphone versus ecology” dilemma was a false choice: both can matter, and under-inclusiveness does not establish that refusal is futile.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s challenge to B’s children’s-kit safeguard, developed in A4.3 and the closing, was decisive: contractual restrictions partly curb hazards created by the sponsorship itself without curing the endorsement being sold. B’s strongest counter was the unanswered demand for proof of net youth-demand reduction, but its dilemma did not fully neutralize A’s narrower mechanism.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): PRO built a crisp, memorable core: sponsorship sells the league's seal, so in a youth-heavy audience the endorsement itself is the harm. Its sharpest move was a dilemma. Either the badge is visible enough to be worth buying and children see it, or it is hidden enough to be safe and is barely sponsorship at all. CON answered that dilemma more effectively than PRO expected. It argued that an official mark placed only in age-gated apps, adult feeds and hospitality still has commercial value, because it steers adult bettors toward licensed operators. That leaves PRO's residual harm resting on thin spillover such as "partner lists" and "fan talk." CON also kept several live routes running that PRO only partly answered: - **Integrity leverage.** CON argued bookmakers need no league data to price public games. PRO's reply that integrity monitoring "can continue" through data access did not engage this point. - **The ratio trap.** CON argued a share-based cutoff penalizes free-to-air family leagues while sparing paywalled ones that may have more child viewers in total. PRO's "more caution, not less" reply did not fully address that comparison. - **Displacement.** CON argued refused budgets shift into unpoliced in-telecast betting pitches. PRO's answer that those slots already exist was reasonable, and this point stayed contested. PRO's closing was elegant but largely restated the dilemma rather than closing these gaps. CON wins a close but real edge on breadth of surviving routes and direct engagement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The A4.1 versus B4.1/B4.3 exchange was decisive. PRO argued the badge cannot be both commercially powerful and invisible to children. CON responded with its "two gates" framing: placing the mark behind age verification still steers adults at login, and the remaining youth exposure is reduced to glimpses of partner lists. CON added that PRO's concession, that adult-channel deals carry "no league sponsorship badge," still leaves the motion banning sponsorships that create no youth hook.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B more cleanly won the core clash: a betting sponsorship sells team-linked endorsement that leaks past ID gates through partner status, coverage, and fan talk, so it cannot stay both commercially potent and harmless to youth-heavy audiences. Integrity and data aims can be separated from marketing rights, while Side A's displacement and ratio points remained more speculative than decisive. Guardrails did not neutralize the association harm the motion targets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's rebuttal that an official badge cannot be valuable enough to steer adults and justify a fee yet sealed from youth fan culture; stripping every public trace abandons sponsorship rather than saving it.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B earned a narrow win by challenging PRO’s central premise that commercially valuable sponsorship must produce youth-facing repetition. Fixed-fee deals confined to adult channels remain sponsorship, so A increasingly relied on redefining them as mere data or hospitality contracts. A’s endorsement-before-login argument was forceful and its treatment of outside advertising was disciplined, but residual partner-list glimpses were not clearly shown to justify categorical refusal. B also kept the youth-ratio penalty and oversight costs live, though its budget-displacement claims remained somewhat speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s false-dilemma argument was decisive: adult-only official status can retain commercial value without broadcast repetition, and dismissing those deals as non-sponsorship narrows the motion rather than answering it.
