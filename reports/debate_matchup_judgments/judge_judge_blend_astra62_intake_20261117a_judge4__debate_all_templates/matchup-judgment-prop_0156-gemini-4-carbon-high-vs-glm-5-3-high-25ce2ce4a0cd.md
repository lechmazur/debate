### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO wins a competitive debate. It built a clear two-track account of distrust, vertical (Troika overrides, the 2015 referendum) and horizontal (the North-South morality play), and kept it consistent through every round. Its decisive move was turning CON's "station for 2020" claim. If the ESM was so stigmatized that states refused its pandemic credit lines, and deposit insurance stayed blocked, then the post-2010 machinery left distrust that Europe had to route around. PRO also answered CON's Q2 directly with a concrete alternative: upfront restructuring, direct bank recapitalization and an early ECB backstop. CON's counterfactual framing was strong and memorable. The "joint credit card," "staircase" and "scaffolding" images were effective, and its sequencing defence (avoiding a second Lehman) was its best rebuttal. But several things weakened it: - CON conceded early that trust "frayed." - Its "outgrown, not quarantined" reply to the ESM-stigma point is assertion rather than proof. - Its populism-outside-the-euro comparison was blunted by PRO's AfD and referendum-override distinctions. - Its closing overstated PRO's position as a concession. - Two clipped turns, the opening and the second rebuttal, were a real execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over the ESM in 2020. CON had claimed in A2.3 that post-2010 institutions "built the station" for joint recovery. PRO replied in B3.1 and B4.5 that the ESM was so politically radioactive that pandemic recovery bypassed it, and deposit insurance remained blocked by Northern suspicion. CON's "you retire scaffolding" answer reframed the point but never showed that the stigma reflected solidarity rather than lingering distrust.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B more convincingly established that avoidable design choices—delayed private losses to shield creditor banks, moralizing, harsh conditionality, and democratic overrides—manufactured distinctive North-South and vertical distrust, while “solidarity” was mostly fear-enforced survival and incomplete risk-sharing. Side A’s counterfactual and sequencing replies were serious but did not show that later institutions vindicated the response or that distrust was outweighed; ESM toxicity and the missing deposit guarantee remained live evidence that distrust prevailed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal after questions, which distinguished euro-specific ballot overrides and bailout resentment from general populism and showed that 2020 bypassed, rather than crowned, the post-2010 machinery.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won by framing the motion around political trust and democratic equality rather than mere euro survival. It tied the post-2010 response directly to deepened distrust through conditionality, overridden referendums, North-South moralizing, and the later toxicity of the ESM. A made a strong counterfactual and institutional-risk-sharing case, but treated non-exit and later elite integration as proof of solidarity without fully neutralizing B’s democratic-humiliation and ESM-stigma arguments. A’s clipped speeches also cost execution.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins a clear but competitive debate. B (PRO) framed the stakes memorably: "containment is not community," the 2015 Greek referendum, and populist backlash in both creditor and debtor states. B also pressed a fair point that A's metrics, such as staying in the euro and new members joining, could be measuring exit costs rather than trust. However, B's opening was clipped, which is a real execution cost. A neutralized B's main routes more effectively: - **Timeline:** A showed relief began by 2012, not "a decade late." - **"Radioactive" mutualization:** A noted that unanimous 2020 joint borrowing sits uneasily with that claim. - **Brexit:** A pointed out that Britain was never in the euro, so it is weak evidence about eurozone policy. - **AfD:** A reframed creditor resentment as evidence that creditor leaders spent political capital to deliver rescues anyway. A also answered B's Q1 honestly, conceding there was no permanent transfer union. A then explained why preserving solidarity did not require one and pointed to banking union and ECB backstops. B's closing claim that A "never once offered a measure of trust" overstated the record. A had cited rebounding euro support, pro-EU Greek majorities, and creditor parliaments repeatedly choosing to share risk. B's A1 answer offered plausible alternatives such as symmetric adjustment, but it softened B's earlier attack on conditionality. B also never directly answered the voluntary-entry and creditor-commitment evidence beyond relabeling it as self-interest. Overall, A left the cleaner comparative weighing: distrust spiked and plateaued, while the institutions endured.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4 exposed a tension inside B's own case. B had described the eurozone as a "shield against storms" that Croatia rationally joined, while also claiming "nobody believes the union would catch them if they fell." A argued that a shield outsiders freely join for protection is itself solidarity. A then turned B's "self-interest" dismissal into an unreasonable demand that solidarity must hurt to count. B's closing restated the exit-cost framing but never resolved this contradiction.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins because it better met the motion's comparative burden. It treated solidarity as democratic risk-sharing built from a no-bailout baseline, then used creditor political capital, voluntary accession, euro support rebound, and 2020 joint borrowing to show that distrust, while real, did not outweigh preserved solidarity. Side B's containment frame was sharp, but it never fully defused those behavioral measures and ended up treating the euro both as a protective shield outsiders freely joined and as a union nobody trusted to catch them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal exposed the contradiction in Side B's claim that outsiders joined the euro as a "shield against storms" while also insisting nobody believed the union would catch a member who fell.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins narrowly by giving the more decision-ready comparative: distrust was real but politically contained, while solidarity was shown through voluntary creditor commitments, institutional risk-sharing, continued membership, and 2020 joint borrowing. Side B had the sharper normative frame and memorable rhetoric, but its opening clipping, its tendency to reduce all cooperation to fear, and its failure to fully answer A’s voluntary-commitment and Croatia/shield contradiction slightly weakened its path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal answered the pressure questions directly and exposed Side B’s tension between calling the eurozone a protective shield while claiming nobody believed it would catch them, undermining B’s containment-only framing.
