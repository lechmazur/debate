### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively neutralizing Side B's two main attacks: the burden on charities and the 'toothless vs. dangerous' dilemma. Side A consistently reminded the judge that sharp discounting was a co-equal compliance pathway, which dismantled Side B's claim that charities would inevitably become the 'escape valve' for unwanted food. Furthermore, Side A successfully flipped the ordering-discipline argument by pointing out that making disposal more burdensome incentivizes better forecasting, whereas Side B's logic on this point was contradictory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 effectively dismantled the 'toothless vs dangerous' dilemma by pointing out that ordinary legal duties routinely combine required outcomes with contextual defenses, proving that a law can be flexible without being meaningless.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a consistent, bounded duty – only safe edible food, only where reasonable recipient/route exists, with discounting as alternative – which neutralized CON's safety and charity-burden catastrophes. A exposed CON's inconsistency: liability risk applies equally to the voluntary donation CON endorses, and requiring private profitability before saving food begs the question. CON's sharp toothless-vs-harmful dilemma was memorable but did not survive PRO's answers that recovery costs incentivize better ordering and that disposal remains permitted where impracticable, leaving PRO with the cleaner weighing: bin destroys remaining value, recovery preserves some.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 in Pressure and A2.1 – showing CON's food-safety liability objection applies equally to voluntary donation and that the mandate does not lower tests, only changes the default for food that already passes them – was the most decisive exchange.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins because it successfully exposed the practical vulnerabilities of Side A's "reasonable effort" mandate. While Side A established a strong moral and theoretical case that supermarkets shouldn't externalize waste costs, Side B's sharp analysis of corporate compliance incentives—specifically that a mandate creates "defensive donation" and turns charities into an "escape valve" for paperwork—was more decisive. Side B's distinction between handling rules and volume/selectivity in the liability clash was particularly damaging, and Side A's closing failed to fully resolve the tension between a mandate strong enough to work and soft enough to avoid harming charities.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A1), which distinguished between safety rules and the volume/selectivity of donations, proving that a mandate pressures charities to accept marginal food as an "escape valve" for corporate compliance.
