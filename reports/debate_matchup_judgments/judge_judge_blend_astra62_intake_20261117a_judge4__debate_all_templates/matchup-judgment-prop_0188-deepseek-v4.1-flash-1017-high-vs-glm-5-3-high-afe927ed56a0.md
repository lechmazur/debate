### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): A built the stronger mechanism for changing publication incentives and ultimately explained how exploratory research and execution review fit the mandate. B convincingly established that repositories can inform evidence synthesis, defeating A's dismissal of them as effectively invisible. However, B did not adequately establish why outcome-blind commitments would undermine quality control when acceptance remains conditional on competent execution. A's incentive advantage therefore survived. Its initial narrowing of the mandate and exaggerated attacks on repositories keep the win competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's distinction between outcome-blind acceptance and unconditional acceptance was decisive: reviewers could check execution against an explicit protocol rather than abandon standards. B answered with plausible concerns about leniency, but never overcame that distinction or directly neutralized the reward for publishing sound null results.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): CON more convincingly showed that the bundled mandate relocates rather than removes discretion and incentive problems. PRO identified a real bias and career-incentive gap, but its qualitative narrowing and “execution review still has teeth” defense reintroduced the same contested judgments CON targeted, while the publication entitlement’s leniency risk was never fully neutralized. CON’s alternative—mandatory reporting, audits, and open data without guaranteed journal space—remained a live, cleaner comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing on the publication guarantee: design-stage commitments judge promises, contested “competent execution” escape clauses give journals reasons to treat deviations as minor, and the resulting incentive is a defensible-looking paper rather than trustworthy evidence.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side A won by turning the debate around the core incentive problem: publication bias is not merely hidden results, but a reward structure that punishes null findings. A made the publication guarantee central as the mechanism that changes researcher and journal incentives, while B’s repository-and-audit alternative never fully answered how it would make null results career-relevant or literarily influential. B raised strong concerns about quality gates, qualitative fit, and contestable execution judgments, but A’s conditional-execution Registered Reports defense was enough to keep the guarantee plausible. A’s closing crystallized the clash more cleanly: imperfect accountability that changes incentives beats documentation that leaves incentives intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing together answered B’s execution-quality pressure by stressing conditional acceptance and protocol-aware review, then reframed B’s repositories as a “file drawer with a search bar” that records results without changing the payoff for producing them.
