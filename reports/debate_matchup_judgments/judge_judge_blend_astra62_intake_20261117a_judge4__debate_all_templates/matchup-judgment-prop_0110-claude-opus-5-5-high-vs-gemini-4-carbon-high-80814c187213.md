### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO built a serious structural inefficiency case, but CON shifted the decisive clash to Switzerland and the Netherlands. Those are predominantly private insurance systems with peer-level outcomes, and CON plausibly argued they buy that health at comparable cost, especially against Sweden and Denmark, while recasting America as a half-public outlier whose flaws are optional. PRO never fully restored a clear private cost penalty after CON answered the Norway oil point. Since the motion requires public systems to deliver better health per dollar, CON's equal-coverage/comparable-cost framing left PRO short of the burden. A's clipped first rebuttal is a real but minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's use of PRO's peer-level concession for Swiss and Dutch systems, combined with the claim that the Netherlands spends in the same range as Sweden and Denmark, was decisive. It turned PRO's denominator argument into a tie rather than a public-system win.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by narrowing the debate to the motion's actual burden. B's case selection (Switzerland and the Netherlands as genuine predominantly-private-insurance systems) forced A to concede "peer-level" health outcomes, converting the fight to cost alone, where B's wealth-adjustment arguments (labor costs track national income, Norway's oil distorts the denominator, Netherlands spends in the range of public neighbors) materially blunted A's per-dollar edge. B then landed the decisive burden framing: the motion requires "better," and equal outcomes at contested cost plus one half-public outlier (America) is at best a tie. A fought back well — the amenable-mortality test was a genuinely principled answer to B's lifestyle objection, and the "failure modes at their best and worst" asymmetry in Rebuttal 2 was A's strongest structural move — but the Switzerland cost gap, the crux of A's per-dollar case, was never firmly re-established after B's adjustment. A's mechanism story (middleman deadweight) survived as plausible but B contested its magnitude credibly. B's closing crystallized the vote cleanly; A's closing relied on a denominator claim B had already contested. Execution cost for B: Rebuttal 1 was clipped mid-sentence at the word limit, which slightly hurt an otherwise strong turn. Grounding was solid on both sides: no fake citations, plausible specific knowledge, and cautious precision where warranted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's double bind in Rebuttal 1 ([B2.5]-[B2.6]): if lifestyle explains America's lifespan deficit, then Mediterranean longevity cannot prove public financing — turning A's own Spain/Italy evidence against A and forcing the metric shift to amenable mortality. A's Rebuttal 2 asymmetry framing (best-case vs worst-case comparison of both models) was the strongest counter, but B's final convergence-plus-burden argument ("equal numerator, comparable denominator... that is not 'better'") closed the door.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash clearer: it kept Switzerland and the Netherlands in-category as regulated but predominantly private systems, used A’s amenable-mortality answer to establish peer-level outcomes, and argued that contested or comparable costs meant PRO had not shown “better” health per dollar. B’s clipped first rebuttal was a real execution cost, but its later pressure handling and closing were cleaner.
