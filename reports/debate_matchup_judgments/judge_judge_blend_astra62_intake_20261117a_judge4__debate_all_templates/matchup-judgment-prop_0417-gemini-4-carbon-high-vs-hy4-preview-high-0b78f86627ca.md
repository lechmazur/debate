### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner path to victory by tying both harms to peak intoxication and exhaustion, then showing earlier closing reduces that cause everywhere, including car-dependent areas where late transit cannot reach. A's journey-home framing was vivid and relevant, but it narrowed the comparison to dense egress and conceded that earlier closing lowers the dose. B turned that concession into the central explosive-charge argument and exposed a real tension in A's position: A treated impairment as already gone by 1 a.m. while expecting a far drunker 4 a.m. patron to make a complex train-to-taxi choice. A's front-loading and displacement claims were plausible but more speculative, and B answered them with ordinary pacing and the fact that most tired patrons go home rather than to packed private flats. B also better preserved the motion's general scope by showing lower intoxication cuts crash risk on every road, not just near rail corridors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that impairment is a steep curve rather than a switch was decisive: it undermined A's claim that earlier closing changes nothing where transit never reaches, because driving at 1 a.m. less drunk and less exhausted is materially safer than driving at 4 a.m. This directly neutralized A's strongest coverage objection and reinforced B's universal dose-reduction case.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B established the stronger comparison: earlier closing can combine existing transport with lower intoxication, reduce exposure to venue violence, and lower crash risk even when patrons still drive. A conceded dose reduction but repeatedly treated impairment as binary and narrowed the proposition to curbside dispersal. B answered the pressure questions directly; A mostly repeated its transport mechanism without resolving the coverage and intoxication objections. B's sweeping assumptions about drinking compensation and transit availability limit the margin, but its central comparative argument remained substantially stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B decisively challenged A's claim that earlier closing changes nothing unless it retrieves the car or supplies a ride. Whether someone drives and how dangerous that drive is are different questions; A's concession that earlier closing lowers consumption left B's risk-reduction argument unanswered.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side A offered a vivid, plausible egress-and-safe-ride mechanism, but its case narrowed excessively to the curb and repeatedly treated earlier closing as changing nothing where patrons still drive. Side B made the stronger full comparison: lower intoxication and fatigue can reduce both violence and crash risk even without perfect ride substitution, including beyond transit networks. B also turned A’s concession that earlier closing lowers the dose into a lasting advantage and directly answered the parked-car and synchronized-exit arguments. A’s rhetoric was memorable, but its closing largely restated the bottleneck frame without neutralizing those points.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal decisively separated a parked car from drunk-driving risk: neither policy retrieves the car, but a patron driving earlier, less intoxicated, and less exhausted can still be safer. Combined with the point that extensions do nothing outside transit coverage, this exposed the largest gap in A’s case.
