### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by reframing CON's strongest point against it: if Android's benefits were truly unknowable ex ante, that is a reason to put the risk on the acquiring platform, not consumers. PRO also built the cleaner asymmetry—a blocked deal with real synergies can be recut, while a cleared deal that absorbs a future rival cannot be undone. CON's post-hoc Android defense and Within enforcement example never resolved who should bear the uncertainty, and its error-cost argument was fuzzy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 turned CON's admission that Android's benefits were "inherently unpredictable" into support for the presumption: if contemporaneous proof is absent, the law is being asked to ratify permanent competitive loss on an after-the-fact story.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) consistently did the comparative work the motion demanded. Its core reframe—that the presumption asks firms to substantiate contemporaneous, documentable consumer benefits rather than "prove a counterfactual"—neutralized B's workability attack and turned the Android example into A's own case: if the benefit was real, it existed in integration, distribution, and engineering records the acquirer controls. A's irreversibility asymmetry (a blocked deal can be re-cut and re-documented; a cleared absorptive deal cannot be undone) directly blunted B's "permanent false positive" argument and gave the cleanest reason to vote PRO. Side B (CON) relied heavily on repeated Android/Parse/Onavo recaps, conceded that transformative benefits are "inherently unpredictable" ex ante without resolving who should bear that uncertainty, and leaned on the FTC/Within case as proof existing law works—a specific factual claim that is actually wrong (the FTC lost the Within challenge), and which A's Q2 pressure exposed conceptually even without naming the error. B's closings were largely recap, while A's crystallized the live clash. Format was clean on both sides, but A adapted and narrowed the dispute each round while B widened it with repetition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's Rebuttal 2 answer on Android (A4.1): rather than denying the example, A specified exactly what contemporaneous evidence Google could have produced—mechanism, integration plans, why alternatives were unavailable—showing the standard demands substantiation, not prophecy. B's reply that transformative innovation is "inherently unpredictable" effectively conceded that consumers must bear unknowable risk on an incumbent's after-the-fact story, which A then crystallized in closing with the irreversible-absorption asymmetry.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): A consistently framed the presumption as an information-forcing rule rather than a ban, answered both pressure questions with a concrete contemporaneous-evidence standard, and won the comparative claim that doubtful deals can be revised while absorbed rivals are difficult to restore. B repeatedly relied on Android’s retrospective success without overcoming that answer, and its central assertion that the FTC successfully blocked Meta’s Within acquisition was inaccurate. B’s closing largely repeated these claims and did not address A’s revised-deal asymmetry.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it specified the engineering, distribution, capital, integration, and product evidence Android’s acquirer could have supplied without predicting the future, exposing B’s reliance on post-hoc benefits and making the proposed burden appear administrable.
