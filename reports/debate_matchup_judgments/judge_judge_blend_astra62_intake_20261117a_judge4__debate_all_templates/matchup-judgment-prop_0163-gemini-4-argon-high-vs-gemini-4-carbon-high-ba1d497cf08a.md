### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a competitive, high-quality exchange. B built a layered containment case (split drives, island isolation, verified chemical susceptibility, clinical backup) and, crucially, answered A's best pressure directly: the "bait-and-switch" charge was met with a coherent explanation that self-limiting trials generate genuine open-air data without pre-approving autonomous drives, and the chemical fail-safe contradiction was repaired by distinguishing resistance classes. B's catch-22 framing survived: A's only escape was late-introduced mesocosms, which B plausibly showed cannot replicate open, multi-year dynamics, leaving A's position looking like a permanent bar. A landed sharp rhetorical blows (the insecticide fail-safe catch, "no recall button," and the closing isolation-vs-real-world contradiction), and the recallability concern remained live, but A's counter-mechanisms (recombination relinking split components, immunity rebound) grew increasingly speculative while B's worst-case framing (reversion to baseline, island-scale clinical care) kept them contained. B answered both pressure questions cleanly and with required format; A also complied. B's closing resolved the island-capacity dilemma more convincingly than A's final contradiction framing, which slightly overstated what B had claimed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1-B4.2) was decisive: it defused A's bait-and-switch by explaining that split drives express the same trait in wild mosquitoes while diluting in mainland populations, and reframed the recallability attack by noting halting releases lets genes breed out naturally—converting A's strongest irreversibility argument into a manageable, reversible trial design.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A better preserved a concrete on-motion pathway: bounded island trials of self-limiting drives can generate open-air ecological data and local effects while genetic dilution and geographic isolation limit runaway spread, so blocking them demands the impossible certainty the motion rejects. Side B pressed real tensions on logistics, persistent niche and evolutionary harms, and recallability, but did not fully collapse the distinction between enclosed mesocosms and isolated outdoor sites or the claim that split-drive trials need not pre-approve autonomous releases. A nonexistent closing reference slightly weakened Side B's execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, which answered the bait-and-switch by showing split-drive island trials can yield real competition and trait data and dilute from stowaways without authorizing unsplit continental release, exposing an all-or-nothing ban.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A narrowly but clearly prevailed by preserving a workable middle position: split or threshold drives on isolated sites could generate useful ecological evidence without preauthorizing continental self-sustaining releases. A directly answered both pressure questions and effectively exposed B’s increasingly categorical position as a permanent prohibition. B delivered sharper rhetoric and a serious realism-versus-containment challenge, but too often treated every trial as an uncontained autonomous release and relied on overstated claims about inevitable resistance, guaranteed eradication failure, and permanent ecological damage. B also never adequately explained why trials and conventional controls must be mutually exclusive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 best answered the central proxy-versus-runaway dilemma: bounded split-drive trials could test traits and vector ecology while dilution supplied a stopping mechanism. B’s closing realism-versus-isolation contradiction was the strongest counterattack, but it did not eliminate the informational value of an informative yet imperfectly representative field trial.
