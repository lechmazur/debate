CON's opening was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost that cut off its health-argument point before the judge could see it. Overall PRO built the cleaner, more consistently weighted path to victory and closed it out more decisively.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Both sides built strong, well-grounded cases anchored in plausible regional knowledge (Bogotá's BRT-to-metro trajectory, Santiago's bus electrification). CON's strongest thread—that e-buses don't pull drivers out of cars while rail is the only mode that beats car travel time, and that near-term corridor/financing decisions lock in decades—was genuinely sharp and repeatedly weaponized via the "BRT already proven to cap out" line. But PRO ultimately won the framing fight that matters most for this motion: "near-term decarbonization strategy" is most naturally read as a test of near-term emissions reductions, not decision-horizon trajectory, and PRO's closing explicitly and convincingly called out CON's reframing ("deciding to build is not reducing emissions"). PRO also neutralized the Bogotá precedent by converting it into a sequencing concession (protect alignments, advance justified rail, but bus-first as the near-term main strategy) rather than letting it stand as proof that bus-first was a historical error, and it pressed a real soft spot in CON's "mandate" solution (unfunded mandates don't buy chargers, depots, or grid upgrades) that CON's rebuttal answered only vaguely. CON never fully closed that funding gap or fully engaged PRO's scope-limiting reply that BRT saturation applies only to the busiest corridor, not the whole network. Both sides suffered a clipped speech (B's opening, A's final rebuttal), so execution costs roughly cancel. The result is a close but discernible edge to PRO on framing control, rebuttal precision, and resolving the central tradeoff more cleanly for a neutral reader.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing turn on CON's "near-term" reframe—"deciding to build is not reducing emissions, and a promised trajectory cannot substitute for delivered cuts"—combined with its earlier conversion of the Bogotá example into a sequencing concession, most decisively reset the debate's deciding question in PRO's favor.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing the decisive clash onto mode shift from cars/motorcycles (the growth source) and irreversibility of corridors/habits, reframing “near-term” as a decision horizon rather than a pure accounting window. PRO kept a coherent speed/breadth case and rightly stressed contingent rail benefits, but never neutralized the concession that e-buses alone leave journey quality (and car pull) unchanged; its lanes/BRT reply was effectively dismantled via Bogotá. Fiscal asymmetry (mandate steers ordinary fleet renewal; rail needs priority) and the “get both” weighing further favored CON after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1 framing that every PRO e-bus advantage is merely tailpipe (same slow trip, zero car pull) plus the Rebuttal 2 pressure answer converting PRO’s lanes/frequency reply into the already-saturated BRT instrument.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): B wins on the motion as written: near-term operational cuts. B's tranche-by-tranche vs zero-until-opening timing argument survived, and B's pressure on unfunded mandates weakened A's "prioritize rail and get both" claim. A's car-growth/mode-shift and irreversibility points are strong for the long trajectory but required redefining near-term as a decision horizon, which is less faithful to the proposition and never answered the core timing deficit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 on timing and funding - metro delivers no cuts while under construction, and mandating e-buses without fiscal priority risks delay/service cuts, vs A's claim that fleets renew anyway.
