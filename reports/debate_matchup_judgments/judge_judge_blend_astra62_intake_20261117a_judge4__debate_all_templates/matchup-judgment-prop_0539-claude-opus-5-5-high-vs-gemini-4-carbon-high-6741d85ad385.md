### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won the sharper exchange. CON also turned PRO’s own organ-ban reasoning against it: surrogacy depends on IVF clinics, hospitals, civil registration, and passport infrastructure, so prohibition can shrink supply rather than merely push it underground. PRO’s repeated clipped speeches weakened its ability to answer those pressures, and its closing overrelied on the “filter vs. wall” point without resolving the sovereignty and coercion problems CON had made live.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO won a close debate by turning CON’s sharpest attack into its own demand-side case. CON’s enforceability critique was powerful: pre-clearance and audits cannot sit in a clinic daily, and host states have bad incentives. But PRO answered that pressure with a workable enforcement story—home-state pre-conception certification, direct inspections, independent escrow, mobility rights, and payment structures—while showing CON’s ban still leaves the embassy dilemma unresolved. The decisive move was PRO’s use of CON’s own border mechanism: non-compliant clinics get no automatic parentage, while the pre-cleared pathway gives intended parents the certainty they want, starving rogue brokers. CON repeatedly said this added only a legitimacy stamp, but did not fully neutralize that behavioral steering argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 A2 was the most decisive: it accepted post-birth custody review for non-compliant clinics, then turned that concession into proof that conditional legality changes buyer behavior before pregnancy by reserving automatic parentage for pre-cleared clinics.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won a close, high-quality debate. B's decisive move was flipping A's own "the child must come home" point into a home-state enforcement mechanism (pre-conception clearance, consular interviews, a revenue kill switch), which directly answered A's core claim that cross-border standards are unenforceable. B also answered both pressure questions cleanly with the required A1/A2 format and gave structural answers to A's strongest point about daily coercion during pregnancy: independent escrow removes clinic leverage over pay, mobility rights bar confinement, and any hospital can verify care. A's best route — that both systems are identical on rogue clinics, so PRO only adds a "legitimacy stamp" — was clever but ultimately blunted by B's closing explanation that automatic parentage is guaranteed only at pre-cleared clinics, which steers demand toward audited clinics and starves rogue brokers. A's "nine months in between" framing remained live but B's design response was substantive, not merely "better paperwork." A also carried execution costs: three clipped turns (opening, both rebuttals), which left some arguments truncated. Both sides argued the motion as written with strong grounding and memorable rhetoric; B simply did more damage per exchange and resolved more of the live clashes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's pressure question "who inspects the clinic in Tbilisi on day forty?" versus B's A1/A2 response in [B4.1]-[B4.3]: home states hold the kill switch over foreign revenue via pre-conception clearance and escrow access, lax hubs lose buyers, and non-compliant parents face custody review without automatic parentage. B's follow-up in [B5.2] — that certainty-seeking parents steer toward pre-cleared clinics — then neutralized A's "identical systems / legitimacy stamp" framing.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the motion's word "enforceable" as the live burden. Side A built a serious buyer-side design—pre-clearance, independent escrow, inspections, and a payment kill switch—and showed that a ban also leaks because states will not abandon newborns. But Side B's questions forced the concession that non-cleared cases still get nationality and post-birth review, so the systems converge on rogue clinics, and then showed that outside audits cannot occupy the months when the host clinic controls daily care. That narrower comparison was the cleaner reason to reject conditional legality. Clipped speeches cost Side B, but did not erase the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal and closing turned Side A's admission that non-compliant clinics still receive nationality and custody review into proof that conditional legality mainly adds a hard-to-police legitimacy stamp, then pinned the gap on day-to-day coercion between pre-conception paperwork and after-the-fact audit.
