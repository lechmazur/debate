- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins a clear but competitive debate. PRO's decisive move came early and held throughout: a transit extension does not require extending venue licences, so CON's repeated charges of "extra two hours of heavy drinking" and "marathon drinking" import costs from not adopting earlier closing rather than from transit itself. CON's answer, that a comparison means venues stay open, has some force. It also eventually added a plausible enabling mechanism, that people drink longer when they know a train exists. But both arrived late and were asserted rather than developed. PRO's "impairment times exposure" framing and its "no substitute" point were never directly neutralized. Earlier closing still releases an impaired crowd all at once with the car still in the plan. PRO's weighing of failure modes, manageable platform friction versus scattered impaired car trips, gave a clean decision rule. CON had real assets. The parked-car problem and the outer-suburb coverage gap were its strongest pressure points. Its general claim that rolling back trading hours cuts assaults is plausible background knowledge, and PRO somewhat brushed it aside. PRO did answer the parked-car and coverage points directly in A1/A2 by arguing that reliable late service changes mode choice before the trip. CON then largely repeated its points with rising intensity rather than engaging that answer. CON's grounding also weakened later on: - It relied on unsupported "exponential" spike claims. - It made a dubious assertion that standard transit is still running at 1 AM. - It cited nonexistent reference IDs ([P4.1, P4.5]). CON's closing restated "pharmacological root" and "optional ride" without answering why an optional substitute loses to no substitute.
