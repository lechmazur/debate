### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A built the stronger path to victory. A answered B's two strongest lines—hidden rewilding costs and stalled succession—directly and with clean A1/A2 labels, conceding all catalyst costs into the denominator while landing the decisive asymmetry: failed succession loses little sunk capital, while failed plantings lose massive upfront nursery/machinery investment. A also shredded B's fragmentation geography by noting Appalachia and the Canadian Shield are heavily forested and dehesas are oak savannas—a counter B never answered. A's definitional pressure (does grassland/wetland work count as "reforestation"?) forced B into a partial dodge. B's answers to A's pressure questions were evasive (walking back the volunteer claim, reframing the definitional trap), and B's later speeches largely recycled the "lottery ticket / ecological autopilot" framing without new weighing, while A's closing crystallized the cost-bind clash sharply. Both sides were grounded without fake sourcing; A simply did more comparative work and neutralized more of the opponent's live routes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 [A4.1]-[A4.5]: A directly priced every rewilding catalyst into the denominator, then flipped B's failure-rate attack into the sunk-capital asymmetry argument, and dismantled B's isolation examples by pointing out that Appalachia, the Canadian Shield, and dehesas are forested or oak-savanna landscapes—a blow B never recovered from.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the motion's comparator tighter: most reforestation programs, judged per dollar of carbon and biodiversity, not per hectare or against idealized restoration. Side B answered the cost and failure questions directly, showed active planting pays heavy upfront capital plus the same stewardship bills, and used failure-cost asymmetry plus a geography correction to damage CON's isolation and lottery frame. CON's reliability and timing points were real, but later speeches partly redefined reforestation into grasslands, wetlands, and boutique repair, and never fully neutralized the acreage-per-dollar multiplier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal: every catalyst enters the denominator, stalled invasive monocultures cost less capital to correct than dead saplings, and CON's Appalachia, Shield, and dehesa examples do not show isolation from seed sources.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner per-dollar comparison: costly nursery stock, machinery, planting, and maintenance versus cheaper landscape-scale catalysts, with smaller sunk losses when interventions fail. B landed a genuine challenge that cheap acreage is not necessarily cheap ecological outcomes and exposed A’s optimistic assumptions about seed sources and succession. However, B increasingly relied on assertions that active programs deliver reliable, immediate outcomes while passive land predictably stalls, and blurred reforestation with grassland and wetland restoration without resolving the cost comparison. A answered both pressure questions directly and exploited those weaknesses more effectively. Both sides overgeneralized heterogeneous landscapes, keeping the win competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure questions and Rebuttal 2 were decisive: they forced B to defend grassland and wetland work as “most active reforestation,” accounted explicitly for rewilding’s management costs, and contrasted low-cost corrective interventions with the larger capital losses possible when planted stands fail.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B controlled the decisive clashes. First, B exposed a real internal tension in PRO's case: PRO sold biodiversity via open mosaics, grazers, and beaver ponds, then progressively retreated to "optional on a minority of sites" and "wet hollows and hedgerows"—habitat features B noted active planting also preserves. Second, B trapped PRO on costs: A's answers to pressure (deer control, targeted weed removal, "limited seed-source planting") steadily eroded the near-zero marginal cost claim, and B capitalized by arguing rewilding had converged on the very island-planting model PRO dismissed as boutique. Third, on the scope dispute, B's "wins either way" framing—global tropics per the motion as written, or the same Ohio field—gave B a safety net, while A's venue-change rebuttal was A's best moment but only neutralized half of B's position. Both sides argued with plausible mechanisms and no fake sourcing; A's "shape of the bill" framing and direct A1/A2 answers were competent, but A spent the whole debate conceding ground under pressure, and B's rebuttals landed the more damaging blows.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3: B seized on A4.1's concessions—deer control, weed removal, and seed-source planting—to argue rewilding had copied the "boutique" island-planting model it had earlier dismissed, collapsing the per-dollar cost advantage that was the core of PRO's case. A never fully recovered, retreating in its closing to a bottleneck-spending frame that B had already recharacterized as planting under another name.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by forcing Side B off cheap walk-away succession and into deer control, weed removal, and seed-source planting, then showing that realistic active planting uses those same tools to close canopy sooner, cut recurring weed costs, and keep hedgerows and wetlands. That damages both the cost gap and the carbon-versus-biodiversity tradeoff on the same land, so A still wins even if the global-budget reading is set aside. B's invoice-for-every-stem frame never closed that concession.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 use of B's own concession that rewilding needs deer control, weed removal, and seed-source planting, which collapsed the near-zero-cost claim against spot planting that shades weeds and accelerates woody carbon.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B built the stronger comparative case under both readings of the motion: global reforestation offers a formidable per-dollar comparator, while same-site planting can also target seed and browsing bottlenecks without planting every stem. A’s cost-asymmetry argument remained plausible, but increasingly depended on treating severe regeneration barriers as exceptional and active planting as uniform, whole-field establishment. B answered the pressure questions more completely and better dismantled A’s claimed biodiversity advantage by noting that planting can retain the same hedgerows and wet hollows.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal decisively resolved A’s “free recruitment” challenge: planted canopy trees supply seed sources, perches, and shade, after which understory recruitment can be free. B then used A’s concession of targeted seed-source planting to narrow the claimed cost gap and showed that active planting need not sacrifice the mosaic habitats A claimed exclusively for rewilding.
