### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side A won by exploiting the motion’s modest demand for compensation to occur “more automatically,” not universally or infallibly. A offered a plausible trigger based on existing cause records and protected itineraries, conceded that mixed cases still require appeals, and clearly weighed that against the status quo’s systematic exclusion of uninformed passengers. B raised credible classification, fare, and route-cost risks, but repeatedly treated the proposal as requiring automatic resolution of all ambiguous cases and never showed why those risks outweighed automatic payment in clear cases. Both sides suffered clipping costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was that both systems retain appeals for hard cases, but only automatic defaults pay clear claims without requiring passenger persistence. B’s concession that forms often add nothing about causation and that uninformed passengers often remain unpaid substantially reinforced that comparison.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing the current claims process as deliberate friction rather than a necessary fact-finding tool. Side B's pressure questions decisively cornered Side A into admitting that forms often provide no new causal information and that uninformed passengers currently go unpaid. While Side A made strong, precise arguments about how automation could scale classification errors and raise fares, Side B's closing weighing—that hard cases can be appealed under either system, but only the proposition ensures easy cases get paid automatically—effectively neutralized Side A's concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing crystallization of Side A's answers to the pressure questions, which perfectly highlighted that the current system's "redundant proof" acts primarily as a filter against marginalized passengers.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): PRO won the central burden shift. A established that airlines already have the facts and decide controllability, so "more automatically" just flips the default to pay when own logs show internal cause on protected itineraries, keeping appeals for mixed cases. CON's best points on messy causation and fare/route costs were real but did not neutralize PRO's decisive comparison: current forms add "often nothing about causation" [B4.1] and the least-informed "often... is not paid" [B4.2], so status quo guarantees failure while auto-pay only risks smaller invisible errors. A used CON's concessions in pressure and closing to crystallize the trade-off.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 + Closing turning CON's A1/A2 concessions — "Often nothing about causation" and "Often that passenger is not paid" — into the filter argument that easy cases should be auto-paid on internal vs external codes while hard cases keep appeals.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by keeping the central comparison clear: automatic payment removes pointless friction in established cases while confining review to genuinely disputed ones. Its notification-and-one-step alternative also moved substantially toward the proposition rather than clearly negating it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered that no regime can eliminate factual disputes, so the relevant gain is quarantining them rather than forcing every eligible passenger through a claim.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, where it used Side B's example of "holding for a late crew" to show that automatic liability would perversely incentivize airlines to strand a few connecting passengers rather than delay the whole cabin and trigger mass payouts.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by narrowing the benefit to a saved click in easy cases that notification plus enforcement could already capture, while keeping live the perverse-incentive costs.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/R2 holding-flight rebuttal: turning every delay code into a payment switch makes holding a departure for 6 connectors risk payouts to 100+ aboard, so airlines will leave them behind or cancel, increasing missed connections PRO wants to reduce.
