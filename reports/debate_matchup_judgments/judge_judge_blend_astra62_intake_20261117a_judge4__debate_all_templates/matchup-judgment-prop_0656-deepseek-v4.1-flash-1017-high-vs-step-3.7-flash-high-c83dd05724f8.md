### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by exposing a fatal structural contradiction in Side A's case. A argues that a fixed threshold provides privacy while preventing large-scale illicit use. B counters that a digital threshold is trivially defeated by automated structuring (smurfing) due to the lack of physical friction. When A attempts to defend the threshold by proposing rolling sender-recipient limits and cluster detection, B decisively points out that tracking relationships, frequencies, and volumes over time requires a transaction graph—which fundamentally violates the proposition's mandate for anonymity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2, which systematically dismantles A's proposed enforcement mechanisms by pointing out that a "per-sender-recipient rolling limit" inherently requires knowing and linking the sender and recipient over time.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by locking the decisive clash on whether a fixed legal threshold can remain enforceable under mandated anonymity. PRO's privacy and cash-analogy case was initially forceful, but CON repeatedly showed that true issuer-blind anonymity makes structuring trivial while any workable velocity, cluster, pair, or wallet-linking control reintroduces a transaction graph and discretionary checks that abandon the proposition as written. PRO's later controls (rolling per-pair limits, identity after N counterparties, non-identifying cluster detection) confirmed rather than escaped the dilemma, and the clipped Rebuttal 2 further weakened execution. CON's pressure and A4/A5 crystallizations left no clean route for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 dismantling B4's cluster detection and per-sender-recipient rolling limits as requiring the very linking/identity graph that anonymity forbids, turning the "fixed threshold" into a velocity test.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): CON won the central clash on enforceability. PRO never resolved that a fixed per-transaction anonymity threshold is defeated by splitting across wallets/mules, and PRO's own fixes (cluster detection, per-pair rolling limits, identity check after 10 counterparties) require linking senders/recipients over time — conceding B's point that it is pseudonymous surveillance, not anonymous P2P. A4 was also clipped for length, leaving burdens unanswered. B's cash-friction vs scriptable CBDC distinction made the trade-off clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.2 and B4.3: if the issuer can flag 50x $90 transfers or enforce a 30-day per-recipient limit, it must see, link, and graph wallets — which is surveillance and negates mandated anonymity; if it cannot, structuring is invisible.
