### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO made a clear equity-based case that cashless retail harms unbanked cash users and that major retailers already bear much of the relevant cash-handling cost. But CON built the stronger comparative path after the exchange: the proposition only preserves an option for people who already have cash, while those lacking both cash and digital access remain excluded. CON also directly corrected PRO’s repeated claim that targeted regulation must be slow or bureaucratic, showing targeted measures can move quickly and pair cash access with broader payment inclusion. PRO’s cost rebuttal was real but did not overcome CON’s best-means/underinclusion challenge, so CON wins cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON’s Rebuttal 2 answer to PRO’s pressure questions: it showed targeted regulation need not begin with a slow survey and that universal cash acceptance only creates legal permission to tender cash, not actual access for the most excluded households. This undercut PRO’s main comparative advantage and left the mandate looking incomplete rather than clearly justified.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): B narrowly wins because requiring cash acceptance directly protects customers who possess cash but lack usable digital payment options. A repeatedly objected that the mandate does not provide money, without defeating that narrower benefit. Its cost mechanisms were plausible, but their significance and its alternative’s comparable coverage remained underdeveloped. However, B’s unsupported CVS and food-bank narratives, repeated invented concessions about implementation delays, and inadequate response to compliance concerns substantially narrow the victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal distinguished accepting cash from requiring customers to use it: the policy can remove a checkout barrier without solving poverty or replacing banking reforms. A continued pressing the lack-of-money objection after that distinction, weakening its central attack.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): B won by separating permission to use cash from actual access to essentials, showing the mandate helps only existing cash-holders and leaves the neither-cash-nor-digital group untouched. B also exposed A's negligible-cost claim and false premise that targeted rules must delay protection, while A repeatedly mischaracterized that alternative as a conceded months-long lockout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it reframed the burden as justifying nationwide compulsion, answered the delay charge with automatic targeted triggers, and noted a universal rule faces the same definitional delays A attributed only to targeting.
