### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner live route by narrowing the motion's marginal instruction. A's best case was credibility insurance, but B turned the post-pandemic record into evidence that visible tightening plus patience, not engineered unemployment, brought inflation down while expectations held. B then used A's own answer to show that once persistence or wage pressure appears, both sides would act, leaving the motion to add layoffs mainly in the no-spiral/no-drift case where inflation would fade. A's reply that that calm is itself produced by the anchor was plausible but less grounded than B's actual trigger rule and recent evidence. B also answered pressure questions more directly and carried the scarring/asymmetry tradeoff convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it took A's answer that persistent three percent inflation would mean pass-through and wage pressure, then showed the two sides converge in the dangerous case, making the motion's distinctive instruction a purchase of unemployment in the remaining no-spiral/no-drift case where it is unnecessary.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A wins narrowly by supplying concrete tightening triggers and distinguishing conditional patience from abandoning the inflation anchor. A sometimes miscast the motion as requiring layoffs or a rigid deadline, limiting its advantage, but its later policy comparison and direct pressure answers were stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A most effectively challenged B's assertion that persistent three-percent inflation necessarily demonstrated wage propagation.
