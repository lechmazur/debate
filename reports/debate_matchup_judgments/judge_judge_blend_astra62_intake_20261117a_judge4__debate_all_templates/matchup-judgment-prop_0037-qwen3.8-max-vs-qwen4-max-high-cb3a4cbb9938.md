### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A (PRO) built and defended a coherent categorical case: pricing's success metric is vehicle flow, so it preserves the roadbed and cannot fund durable subtraction (tram tracks, widened pavements, trees); "zero is a different category from cheap." Side B's strongest route — that "entirely" collapses into a permit system — was answered cleanly by A's "default with named exceptions, like a pedestrian street that admits ambulances," and B never resolved A's sharpest turn: B's own case keeps borrowing exclusion instruments (bollards, corridor closures, loading windows) while leaving the discretionary paying driver in the street. A's "my nurse is exempt from traffic; his is exempt from the invoice" went unanswered. B's tunability/funding case was solid and the "PRO's case becomes our case" framing in Rebuttal 2 was the best CON moment, but B did not neutralize A's point that a reversible dial makes every capital investment provisional. Both sides grounded arguments in plausible general knowledge (London, Ghent, Oslo) without fake precision. A's rhetoric ("A price decides who may drive; a ban decides what the street is") made the clash easier to decide, and B's closing was strong but arrived at a comparison A had already framed. Clear but competitive win for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5): exposing that every instrument B proposed to deliver the desired outcome — bollards, loading windows, corridor closures — is an instrument of exclusion, culminating in "He is defending a ban with a payment option for the people who need it least." ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly and avoided fake sourcing, but B controlled the decisive clash. B's opening move — that the load-bearing word is "entirely" — forced A into a cascade of concessions: exceptions for emergencies, disabled access, essential night workers, drop-off zones, even "a small fee attached to a ban." B tracked each concession and showed PRO had arrived at a priced, permitted, managed core indistinguishable from what high congestion pricing plus redesign delivers. A never fully answered "what does 'entirely' buy beyond a charge set high enough?" — the default-rule/entitlement framing was vivid but B's closing reply (same volume means same damage; the only difference is who decides) blunted it. B also handled the equity attack well: a price can be rebated or income-capped, while categories sort by wealth too (registered van, central address, lawyer) and "you cannot rebate a 'no.'" A answered the pressure questions directly with A1/A2 structure, which was a genuine strength, and A's rhetoric ("privilege with a receipt," "PRO builds a different street") was memorable. But A's redefinition of the motion from a full ban to "removal of private through-traffic as the governing default" was motion drift that B punished repeatedly, and A's funding answer relied on speculative land-value capture that B correctly noted pays out years later. B made the debate easier to decide and won the comparison at every key juncture.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.5]: cataloguing A's concessions (night shuttle, small fee, essential-worker exceptions, drop-off zones) to show "PRO has arrived at my design and kept only the word 'entirely'" — this collapsed A's case into B's and left the motion's key term undefended.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B wins on comparative necessity: pricing can reduce traffic and coexist with street redesign, whereas A repeatedly asserted that charging preserves car-dominated geometry. A's expanding exceptions weakened its defense of an entire ban, and B supplied stronger flexibility and funding comparisons. B overstated bans' inflexibility, but A never established why eliminating ordinary paid entry adds enough benefit to justify the additional exclusions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B directly challenged A's claim that pricing “merely monetizes” traffic by explaining demand reduction and its compatibility with reclaimed street space. A subsequently conceded that pricing reduces traffic but never repaired its central claim that spatial transformation requires a ban.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won by forcing the clash onto the motion's word "entirely." A wanted certainty, redesign, and equity, but its humane exceptions, night shuttle, and small access fee collapsed the ban into the managed core B said pricing plus redesign already delivers. B then showed the ban's unique remainder is permanent refusal of unforeseen trips, categorical inequity that cannot be rebated, a structural funding gap, and ring-district spillover, while a charge can be raised, earmarked, and loosened. A's ownership close was vivid but did not restore a benefit pricing cannot match.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: A's nurse exception, shuttle, drop-offs, and "small fee attached to a ban" conceded a priced, permitted core, leaving "entirely" as damage rather than a distinct instrument.
