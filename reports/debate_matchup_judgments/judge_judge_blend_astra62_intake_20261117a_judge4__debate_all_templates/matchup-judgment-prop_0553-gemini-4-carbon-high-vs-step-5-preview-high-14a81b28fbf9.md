### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive tradeoffs in the India-specific context. PRO's ownership and total-cost framing was appealing, but CON showed that India's existing informal repair sector already supplies cheap component-level fixes, while a national mandate would either keep repairs tied to costly factory-authenticated parts or widen pairing access in ways that threaten payment security. PRO never fully escaped the accreditation bind: tight certification locks out the neighborhood technicians it claims to help, while loose certification invites tampered displays and credential theft. CON also made a plausible affordability point that changing who installs an expensive factory module does not make the module cheap. These live burdens outweighed PRO's more speculative promise that genuine parts and competition would lower total costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON turning PRO's concession that authenticated screens and batteries already exist into a two-ended problem: official parts remain uneconomical for aging budget devices, while broad certification for independent shops creates real payment-security risk. That rebuttal directly attacked PRO's promised savings and its calibrated-access middle ground.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A wins the comparative affordability clash. B repeatedly treated expensive authorized repairs as implying forced replacement, without adequately answering A's alternative of existing, inexpensive component-level repairs. A also distinguished access to genuine assemblies from making those assemblies economical, weakening B's promised savings while sustaining a plausible compliance-cost objection. B's certified-access proposal credibly challenged the blanket security objection, and A overstated some inventory requirements and security risks. Those weaknesses keep the result competitive, but B's repeated monopoly framing did less work than A's targeted rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A conceded that authorized quotes can exceed resale value, then explained why that does not establish forced replacement: local technicians can repair individual components using compatible parts. B largely relabeled this alternative as salvage and copied batteries rather than answering the distinction, leaving its central affordability comparison weakened.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that India’s existing informal repair market already delivers the cheap post-warranty fixes PRO treats as unavailable, while genuine authenticated parts remain uneconomical for depreciated budget devices. PRO never cleanly escaped the resulting binds: broad access versus payment-security risk, quality standards versus affordable improvisation, and a common floor versus design and compliance distortions. Those practical contradictions outweighed PRO’s ownership principle.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it showed that swapping who installs a costly factory-authenticated module cannot make aging budget repairs affordable, while open certification either locks out informal technicians or exposes payment credentials.
