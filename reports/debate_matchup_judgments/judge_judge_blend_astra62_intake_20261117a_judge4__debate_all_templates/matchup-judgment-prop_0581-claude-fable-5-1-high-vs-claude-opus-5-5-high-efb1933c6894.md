### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A built a plausible concentrated-market harm case and pressed hard for an example of consumer-friendly consolidation, but B exposed the central problem: once A limited strict enforcement to visible/illegal harms, the extra blocked deals would be an ambiguous mixed class. B gave concrete efficiency costs—vertical double marginalization, startup exits, plant productivity—and answered A's no-example challenge with MillerCoors. A's closing shift to "a presumption doesn't block; it shifts the burden" then weakened the motion, and B correctly identified that if the presumption blocks nothing, A is defending a different proposition. B's "hindsight is a verdict" crystallized why broader enforcement cannot selectively catch only harmful mergers, while A's irreversibility point was sufficiently answered by failing-firm and lost-exit costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's decisive rebuttal was the motion-consistency dilemma and the claim that a burden requiring ex ante proof of efficiencies that mainly appear in retrospectives is "not a burden shift; it is a verdict." This directly neutralized A's final reframing of strict enforcement as merely shifting proof burdens.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Both sides were well-grounded and disciplined, but A (CON) controlled the decisive frame: the motion asks whether blocking MORE deals nets out positive, so the marginal blocked deal is by definition the ambiguous one. B's attempts to escape this dilemma (targeting only concentrated markets, "a presumption shifts the burden, doesn't block") were met with A's sharpest replies — concentrated-market harms are already illegal, and a burden that can only be discharged with hindsight is "not a burden shift; it is a verdict." A also won the vertical half of the motion, which B largely abandoned after the initial exchange (A's hold-up answer to "just use contracts" was never re-engaged). B landed real pressure with Q2 (name one concentrated-market merger that lowered prices), and A answered it directly with the hedged but plausible MillerCoors example; B's closing counter ("as I understand the retrospective work, prices still rose") was appropriately cautious but came late and slightly brittle. B's error-asymmetry weighing was strong and never fully refuted, keeping this competitive, but A's cleaner progression from opening dilemma to closing crystallization gave the third-party reader the easier path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The B-Q2 / A-MillerCoors / B-closing sequence was the most dramatic clash, but the decisive rebuttal was A's final crystallization: B's claim that "a presumption doesn't block; it shifts the burden" either defends a different motion or concedes that the newly blocked deals are exactly those whose savings cannot be substantiated in advance — and since B admitted enforcers lacked foresight on hospitals and airlines, foresight fails in both directions, making the burden a de facto verdict on the entire ambiguous class.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the marginal-deal dilemma the central clash: if strict enforcement only stops clearly harmful deals, it is not materially more enforcement; if it blocks more, it likely blocks ambiguous deals with mixed evidence. A also kept vertical efficiency and productivity costs live, answered the key pressure question with a concrete horizontal example, and exploited B’s closing tension between “burden-shifting” and actually blocking more deals. Side B was strong on categories of missed harm and error asymmetry, but never fully proved that the additional blocked deals would produce net consumer-price and innovation gains large enough to outweigh lost vertical and productivity benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal answering Q2 with MillerCoors and framing B’s presumption as condemning a mixed class of ambiguous deals was the decisive exchange; B’s burden-shift answer was clean but never fully reconciled with the motion’s requirement to block more deals.
