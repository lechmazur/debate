### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won on clarity of burden. From the opening, B separated "routine delegation" from "parental absence" and argued that A's three harms (reciprocity, co-regulation, friction) all quietly assume an absent parent. B then demanded that A name which mechanism fails when the parent stays on the bed. A made two real hits. The "shell game" contradiction between the active father and the depleted mother was sharp, and A rightly pressed that the motion covers conversation, not just narration. But B neutralized the contradiction by recasting the two parents as a range that a blanket rule must survive. A's best answer to B's Q1 was to say an engaged father "is not outsourcing." B turned that into a charge that A's rule had become unfalsifiable. A's claim that the parent's voice is a biological safety cue was a genuine mechanism, but it came late and was thinly developed. A's closing line, "you don't have to leave the room to vanish," stayed an assertion that B explicitly flagged as lacking a mechanism. A's concession that a five-minute cuddle beats twenty minutes of synthetic chatter was turned by B into "nobody has to choose; your rule just subtracts the story." That gave B the cleaner comparative weighing. B also handled the night-shift and dyslexic-parent cases more concretely. A kept the motion's plain-language intuition alive, so this was competitive, but B made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 response to A1 decided the debate. A had argued that a father who pauses and points "is not outsourcing." B called this "a definition, not a finding": every arrangement where the child thrives gets relabeled. B paired that with turning A's cuddle concession into "subtraction sold as protection." Together these exposed that A never identified a harm that occurs while the parent remains present.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A won the practical comparison between assisted routines and what depleted parents could realistically provide without assistance. B correctly distinguished conversational replacement from simple narration and challenged the teddy-bear analogy. However, its claims that AI necessarily sidelines a present parent or permanently captures an attachment window exceeded its support. A more convincingly explained how narration, touch, and limited parental engagement can coexist. A’s tendency to treat the motion as an absolute ban, and to understate B’s present-parent objection, keeps this competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer to “five minutes of human comfort beats twenty minutes of AI” was decisive: the parent can retain the cuddle and brief engagement while adding the story. B’s closing called this a hijacked connection but did not adequately establish why that loss follows in the constrained household A described.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B separated delegation from parental absence and kept that distinction alive throughout. Side A never explained which developmental mechanism fails when a present parent holds, points, and shares attention, instead relabeling successful co-use as not outsourcing or assuming a silent parent. B also showed A's five-minute cuddle versus twenty-minute story was a false choice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: depleted is not absent, listening and touch remain, and A's rule removes the story while leaving the cuddle the mother was already providing.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO wins a clear but competitive debate. It set the routine-versus-occasional framing early and held it. It also built a coherent causal story that explains why its harm outweighs CON's. Bedtime routines act as a "sensor" for a young child's state. Outsourcing the default slot erodes that sensor, and frictionless devices tend to take over the default. CON's strongest material was its realistic-counterfactual move and its point that constraints like shift work and newborns recur every night. Turning PRO's "one page flatly" line against PRO was effective. However, CON overclaimed AI as a "massive developmental upgrade" and as genuinely responsive turn-taking, with little grounding. The "hundreds of hours" ledger was also inflated rhetoric. In the questions round, CON's answer to PRO's Q2 (satisfied versus abandoned silence) missed the point: saying the device "always answers" does not address detection. PRO then exploited this. PRO's presence/absence dilemma forced CON to specify that the parent "steps out" after five minutes, and PRO converted that into the closing concession. CON's closing reply, that every parent eventually leaves and that freed bandwidth makes the bedside minutes better, was clever. But it came late and asserted reinvestment without answering PRO's earlier "no reinvestment guarantee" point. PRO answered its pressure questions more directly and weighed more cleanly through its asymmetric-risk framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 set up a dilemma for CON. If the parent is in the room, nothing is saved. If the parent is away, nobody senses the child's state, so CON "needs presence for its sensor and absence for its savings." This forced CON to admit the parent steps out after five minutes, which PRO then made the decisive concession in its closing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A wins by establishing that recurring constraints can justify recurring delegation, not merely emergency use. Its bounded handoff preserves attentive parental contact while outsourcing narration, and its bandwidth argument explains why rest and completed chores can improve care. B compellingly distinguishes attention from word delivery, but never establishes why bedtime narration is uniquely necessary for detecting subtle distress. A overstates AI’s ability to satisfy emotional bids, but its narrower case survives that weakness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A directly breaks B’s presence-versus-savings dilemma: parents can provide an attentive tuck-in and then delegate the story. B’s closing treats the subsequent departure as decisive without adequately answering A’s comparison to an exhausted parent who also leaves after a short interaction.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A sustained the routine-vs-occasional distinction and turned CON's retained hug into a presence-versus-savings dilemma: parents cannot both leave to save bandwidth and remain to sense quiet drift. A also answered the literacy and exhaustion cases without denying hard nights, while B's closing reused the one-page example A had already identified as a floor rather than a standard. B's real-world counterfactual and vocabulary gains were strong, but A made the cost of routinely losing the settling window clearer and weightier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 sensor-versus-alarm and presence dilemma was decisive: CON must claim parental presence for emotional detection and absence for bandwidth savings, exposing the weakness in the five-minute handoff model.
