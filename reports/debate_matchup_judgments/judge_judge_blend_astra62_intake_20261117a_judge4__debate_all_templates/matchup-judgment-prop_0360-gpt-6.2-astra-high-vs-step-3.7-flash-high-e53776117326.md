### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by converting the debate from vehicle class to the actual harm received. A's core claim was that factory-built performance cars create concentrated external costs that uniform rules miss, but B showed a common behavioral limit at the receiver's end handles the same harms without a double standard: if 75 dB is acceptable and 92 dB is not, the same decibel line separates them regardless of the car. A never specified a principled differential threshold or showed why model-specific scrutiny beats watching high-risk locations and maneuvers. B also exposed a real inconsistency in A's claim that a 92 dB factory exhaust is legal under an 85 dB threshold, which weakened A's grounding. On acceleration, B's crossing hypothetical was decisive: capability to launch fast does not show dangerous behavior, and a minivan starting late can be more dangerous than a coupe starting safely. A's later speeches largely repeated the design-capability point without answering that pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was turning A's own decibel examples against it: if 75 dB is acceptable and 92 dB is harmful, that same line regulates both cars; class-specific limits are unnecessary. The parallel move on acceleration, where a common hard-launch rule catches every actual violation, neutralized A's built-in-capability argument.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that common behavior- and place-based limits automatically concentrate tickets on the worst offenders without excusing an equally harmful pass from an ordinary car or substituting model predictions for observed maneuvers. Side B never justified a different legal noise level or launch standard by vehicle class, and its factory-loophole and patrol-efficiency claims were neutralized: a nighttime or approach rule can catch stock exhausts and hard launches, while cars that cannot produce the prohibited harm consume no enforcement under that rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal: a shared cutoff separates 75 from 92 regardless of badge, and a hard-launch ban at the crossing constrains the coupe before the light while still catching a later van surge that selective model-watching would miss.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner path to victory: uniform limits on harmful noise and dangerous maneuvers, enforced at hotspots, would naturally burden performance cars most without excusing equally harmful ordinary vehicles. B directly answered both pressure questions and exposed A’s failure to justify either asymmetric noise limits or badge-based patrols.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange showed that A’s 75/92 dB comparison supports one common receiving-end noise limit, while a rule against hard launches constrains capable coupes automatically and still covers dangerous minivans.
