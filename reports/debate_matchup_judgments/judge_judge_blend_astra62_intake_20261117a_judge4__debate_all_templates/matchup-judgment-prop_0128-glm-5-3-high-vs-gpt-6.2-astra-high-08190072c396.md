### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by keeping a workable distinction between degrees as positive evidence and as automatic vetoes. The physician answer showed the necessity test can still preserve true barriers, while selective verification and equal checkboxes answered the cost and hidden-screen objections without requiring exhaustive review. Side B landed sharp hits on breadth, verification asymmetry, and triage, but treated broad removal of unnecessary requirements as a refutation rather than the motion’s point, and the clipped reply left that weighing less cleanly resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answer that a licensure-bound physician is a real necessity case, while a payroll checkbox can compete equally for one of five interviews, undercut both the “abolition” charge and the claim that any access gain must be an unmanageable cost.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by sustaining the central distinction between a degree as evidence and a degree as an automatic veto. Side A persuasively raised triage, verification, and vague-necessity costs, but never showed why a hard requirement beats crediting degrees alongside equivalent experience. B answered with published alternative routes, equal interview slots, and verification only for finalists, while A's claim that this abolishes requirements nearly everywhere largely restates the proposition's intended scope.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's pressure and Rebuttal 2 on the cost/hidden-screen dilemma: if screening is unchanged there is no added burden, and if an experienced clerk's file is now read, that extra attention is exactly the access the reform buys.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B kept the debate focused on the decisive distinction: a degree can be weighed as evidence without being an automatic exclusion. Its payroll example, small-firm answer, transparency response, and closing weighing were cleaner and more direct. A raised real pressure on necessity, cost, and hidden preferences, but too often conflated requirements with preferences, overstated the hidden-screen claim after B proposed published alternatives, and gained little from the physician example beyond a formal law-versus-performance dispute. A’s clipped rebuttal was also a small execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal 2 answered A’s hidden-screen and discrimination-proof pressure by saying employers should publish alternative qualifications and verify finalists, turning A’s transparency concern against degree-only gating while conceding only reasonable review costs.
