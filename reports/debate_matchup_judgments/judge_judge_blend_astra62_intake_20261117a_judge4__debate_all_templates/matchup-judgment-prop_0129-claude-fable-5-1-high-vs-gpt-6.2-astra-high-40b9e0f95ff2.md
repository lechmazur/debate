Side B also exposed internal contradictions in A's enforcement claims and turned A's prediction that remaining buyers pay less, showing that coerced women facing fixed quotas must see more buyers on worse terms while traffickers still extract from victims already under their control.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won a remarkably sharp debate by proving that even under Side A's cleaned-up policy design—repealing brothel-keeping offenses, decoupling immigration, funding unconditional support, and avoiding doorstep policing—criminalizing the purchase inherently degrades safety relative to full decriminalization. Side B demonstrated that buyer liability drives away screenable, record-leaving clients while leaving violent predators untouched, pushing remaining transactions into cash, anonymity, and riskier locations. Side A stayed competitive by isolating offstage coercion and smartly arguing that shrinking aggregate demand lowers the incentive to recruit additional women (even catching an economic overreach by Side B in B4.1). However, Side A ultimately had to concede real safety costs to independent sellers and harsher conditions for currently coerced women, while Side B showed that full decriminalization better exposes private coercion by making clandestine-only operations stand out and letting non-criminalized clients report abuse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: When Side A proposed police decoy advertisements in A2.4 to avoid surveilling sellers, Side B immediately flipped the fix in B2.6 and B3.1 by asking whom decoy ads actually deter—forcing Side A to concede in A4.1 that its enforcement mechanism weeds out cautious, screenable men while missing violent predators.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): A won by consistently shifting the comparison from prohibition to full decriminalization: reporting, screening, shared premises, and support are available there without making every client anonymous. B's invisible-coercion deterrence argument was strong, especially on recruitment, but B conceded the loss of screenable clients and decoys missing predators, while A showed buyer criminalization makes private, untraceable bookings universal and removes clients as potential witnesses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A Rebuttal 2: legal buyers make private-only control stand out and can report what they repeatedly see, whereas criminalized buyers are least likely to call; it also undercut B's purchase-evidence leverage because violent offenders would avoid leaving messages.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the decisive comparison clearer: the real alternative is full decriminalization, not prohibition. B convincingly argued that criminalizing buyers still forces transactions underground, removes screenable clients, makes coerced sellers harder to identify, and leaves only the harms of illegality attached to the seller’s income. Side A had a plausible demand-deterrence case and some strong lines about hidden coercion, but conceded that safe clients would be lost and could not fully answer how visibility under full decriminalization would be replaced.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s “quiet collapse” point in Rebuttal 1, followed by Rebuttal 2’s claim that A’s message-evidence leverage only reaches screenable clients, sharply undercut A’s safety case.
