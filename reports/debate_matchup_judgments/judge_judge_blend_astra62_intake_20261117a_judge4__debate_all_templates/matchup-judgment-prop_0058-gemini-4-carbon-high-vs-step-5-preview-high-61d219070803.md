### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won the core net-comparison after a closely fought exchange. B's decisive line of attack was sequencing: once A conceded payroll was "the wrong ledger," real savings had to come from IT consolidation, data matching, and procurement discipline — work that requires the very specialists (engineers, auditors, procurement officers) that DOGE-style freezes, buyouts, and oversight cuts purge first. A's counter ("pressure creates the deadline, and the deadline creates the migration") was a faith-based mechanism that B explicitly called out and A never substantively repaired. B also landed a real internal contradiction: A conceded legislative paralysis while claiming executive cuts would force legislatures to sunset duplicate mandates. A's strongest route — disruption measured against a decaying baseline — was persistent and rhetorically powerful, and B answered it directly (B4.1, B5.4) by arguing that pre-existing strain magnifies rather than masks marginal harm. A loses points for execution only in the sense that its answers to sequencing were mostly reframing ("the gap is the status quo") rather than resolution. B's own costs: a clipped, citation-heavy, survey-style opening that did less argumentative work than it should have, and an alternative remedy (targeted consolidation) that A effectively painted as utopian. A was more quotable and consistent across turns, but B made the decisive clash clearer and answered the most important pressure more honestly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4/B5 versus A4/A5 on sequencing: B forced A to admit savings come from complex execution, then showed hiring freezes and buyouts remove the people who execute, crystallized as "Deadlines do not write code, merge databases, or police contracts; specialized people do." A's "Deadlines do not write code, but neither do GAO reports" was a clever riposte but left the burden of who actually builds the systems during a freeze unanswered.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): A supplied the stronger causal comparison: removing specialists while legal workloads persist can worsen both service delivery and waste before replacement systems function. B identified plausible savings through shared services and administrative consolidation, but inadequately explained how pressure substitutes for the capacity needed to execute them. Its closing also ignored A’s explicit argument that cuts worsen an already deteriorating baseline. A’s clipped, authority-heavy opening and overconfident predictions limit the margin, but its later speeches answered the central pressure more convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to “pressure creates the deadline” was decisive: deadlines do not supply the engineers and watchdogs needed for successful consolidation. B’s proposed interim controls partially answered the sequencing challenge, but pointing to existing fraud did not establish that removing existing safeguards creates no additional harm.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B better proved the proposition by tying DOGE-style upfront cuts to adverse selection, statutory workload, and a sequencing gap: the savings CON promised require specialized staff and time, while the drives remove those guardrails first. Side A’s baseline-decay framing was strong, but its net-gain case relied too much on speculative pressure-induced reform and did not fully answer how services and fraud controls avoid acute damage during a multi-year transition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal directly answered the pressure questions and turned A’s legislative-paralysis point against CON: if statutes remain and legislatures stay deadlocked, early cuts risk starving mandated services rather than repealing duplication.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) sustained its core asymmetry—shallow, finite spreadsheet savings versus deep, compounding loss of tacit capacity—across every round, answered both pressure questions directly with labeled A1/A2 responses, and landed the decisive definitional blow: CON's increasingly surgical reform program (blanket exemptions, telemetry, real-time waivers) is not the blunt, politically-driven DOGE-style drive the motion describes. Side A's rebuttals were often sharper in isolation—especially the fragility flip (workflows dependent on one analyst are dysfunction, not capacity), the retirement analogy PRO never fully answered, and forcing B to concede telemetry catches idle licenses. But A's opening was clipped mid-sentence, consisted mostly of citation-stacking (Brookings, Cato, GAO, Manhattan Institute) that B correctly dismantled as "citation is not mechanism," and its case progressively morphed into a competent-reform fantasy that B successfully framed as off-motion. B's adverse-selection answer to the redundancy contradiction and the "service failure is the last symptom" point survived the exchange intact, while A's retirement point remained live but underweighted. B wins a clear but competitive debate on framing discipline and unanswered permanence arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3–B4.5: PRO's answer to the redundancy pressure question—adverse selection means the most marketable staff exit first, so "genuine redundancy" does not protect capacity, and by the time queue metrics fall the knowledge has already left—directly resolved A's sharpest contradiction and set up the closing's "auditor's fantasy" frame that CON never fully escaped.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins a competitive debate by undermining A’s central comparison: genuinely idle recurring expenditures can generate lasting savings, not merely temporary accounting gains. B also answered the screening pressure with concrete carve-outs and usage checks; A mostly asserted that such safeguards cannot characterize DOGE-style drives. A’s objections about tacit knowledge and delayed feedback remain persuasive, but A never adequately rebuilt its net-harm comparison after its savings argument weakened. B’s clipped, authority-heavy opening and overconfident claims about restoring expertise keep the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s recurring-savings challenge produced the decisive exchange. A answered that canceled contracts and licenses merely transfer necessary work elsewhere; B distinguished genuinely unused purchases, and A subsequently conceded telemetry can identify idle licenses. That materially damaged A’s repeated temporary-savings-versus-permanent-harm framing.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A wins despite a clipped, over-cited opening because its later case gave the cleaner decision rule: identifiable zero-service waste can produce compounding baseline savings, while personnel harms are detectable and correctable through exemptions, queue metrics, waivers, and codification. Side B’s state-capacity argument was vivid and grounded, but it leaned too heavily on permanent-collapse rhetoric, did not fully answer the routine-retirement and telemetry pressure, and tried to exclude A’s mitigations as not truly DOGE-style rather than defeating them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal directly answered the pressure questions with upfront carve-outs, telemetry, and rapid correction pathways, materially blunting Side B’s claim that tacit capacity is irreversibly lost before any fix can engage.
