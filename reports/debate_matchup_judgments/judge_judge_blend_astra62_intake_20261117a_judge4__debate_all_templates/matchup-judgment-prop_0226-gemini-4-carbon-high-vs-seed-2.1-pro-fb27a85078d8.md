- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A built the stronger comparative case: enforceable last-resort protection, restricted to lethal conditions and unavailable domestic refuge, versus discretionary pathways that can disappear. B's assertion that existing frameworks already guarantee protection was also insufficiently established. A occasionally overstated its opponent's contradictions, but more consistently answered pressure and narrowed the debate to the people adaptation cannot protect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A directly answered B's claim that regional hazards lack an individualized screening hook: safe internal relocation defeats eligibility. Its subsequent exclusion of mere poverty and overcrowding further weakened B's mass-caseload argument. B identified remaining adjudication difficulties but did not substantiate the leap from difficult cases to systemic collapse.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A won by keeping the motion’s limited design at the center of the clash. Objective habitability triggers plus an internal-relocation screen gave a workable answer to floodgates, while B’s concession that states must not deport people to certain death left revocable humanitarian pathways looking inadequate. B raised serious political and incentive risks, but did not show they outweigh an enforceable, narrowly drawn floor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which supplied concrete eligibility and internal-refuge limits and then used B’s own non-deportation concession to expose the weakness of relying on revocable pathways.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A built the cleaner path to victory by tying the limited right to physical uninhabitability and exhausted internal relocation, then directly answering B’s scale, administrability, and backlash objections. A’s closing effectively exposed the tension between describing those protections as legally sufficient yet revocable, making enforceable protection the decisive comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s concession against deporting people to certain death was decisive: A showed that revocable humanitarian pathways do not supply the durable legal protection B claimed already exists, while binding obligations better address the same hostile-government scenario B invoked against the proposal.

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by exposing an unresolved gap between A's capped admissions mechanism and its promises of guaranteed safety and predictable borders. Under direct pressure, A asserted that advance processing eliminates excess arrivals rather than explaining what happens when eligible people exceed available places. B also challenged A's inconsistent internal-relocation threshold and answered the status-quo accusation with adaptation and island-mobility alternatives. B overstated the inevitability of backlash and mass claims, but A's closing concession to incomplete protection never resolved the central implementation challenge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3 decisively answered A4.1: processing claims early does not ensure sufficient resettlement capacity.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal, which forced the choice between deporting verified claimants or letting courts smash quotas, and showed A’s Dhaka heat example either guts eligibility or explodes it.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by exposing two unresolved contradictions in A’s case: capped quotas are difficult to reconcile with an enforceable individual right to asylum, and the Dhaka example makes the extreme-heat and internal-relocation tests threaten either mass eligibility or near-total exclusion.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: early processing cannot ensure that verified claimants never exceed quotas, and wet-bulb heat does not depend on birthplace, so A’s origin-based distinction did not escape the Dhaka eligibility dilemma.
