- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): PRO won the class claim on "nearly every" non-currency use. CON's live route—adversarial multi-party settings with no trusted host—was real and well pressed (trade finance, captured land registries, hosting chokepoints), and the clipped opening was a real cost. PRO still made the debate easier to decide: multi-party trust needs shared governance and external checks either way; once those exist a signed quorum-replicated database does the coordination without consensus overhead and immutability traps; blockchain does not conjure independent validators or solve oracles; and CON's strongest cases either collapse into permissioned databases under a friendlier label or make correction worse. CON (Side A) landed a sharp adversarial case around land title and cross-border trade finance where no single administrator is trusted, but PRO absorbed these into jointly governed, quorum-replicated databases with external audit, persuasively arguing that where independent validators exist they can audit such databases without consensus overhead, and where they do not, blockchain does not conjure independence. CON's opening was clipped, and CON never answered PRO's health-record and voting examples, leaving PRO's "nearly every" claim standing after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's turn that CON's "jointly governed database" is structurally indistinguishable from a permissioned blockchain but adds consensus overhead, cost, and immutability traps—so if the label changes nothing, the simpler mechanism wins.

Side B's distinction between "infrastructure" and "coordination" in the closing failed to overcome Side A's point that infrastructure control is the core of the coordination problem in adversarial environments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they introduce the physical hosting chokepoint to answer Q1, effectively showing that a "jointly governed database" still relies on a single server or cloud provider, whereas a distributed validator set removes that operational and jurisdictional vulnerability.
