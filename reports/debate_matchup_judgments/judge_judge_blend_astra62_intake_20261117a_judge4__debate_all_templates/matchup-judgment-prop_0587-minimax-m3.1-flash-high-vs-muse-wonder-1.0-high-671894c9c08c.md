Side B also persuasively demonstrated that within five years, foreign heavy-industry exporters can more cheaply shuffle existing clean output or redirect shipments than overhaul furnaces and power contracts. Side A offered coherent theoretical mechanisms and adaptive policy fixes, but underplayed domestic reductions as global reductions and could not overcome Side B's sequencing trap.

Side B’s leakage theory was coherent, but its fixes for price legitimacy and plant-average charging added time and contestability rather than proving faster global abatement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions by showing that Side B’s benchmark, default, and plant-average fixes deepen data and litigation problems, so the short-run responses remain paying, discounting, or sorting rather than cutting furnace emissions.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A built the cleaner, more internally consistent path to victory on the global five-year metric. While Side B pressed a smart distinction between an immediate price signal and slow capital turnover in heavy industry, Side A squarely answered it by showing how high default values and shipment-level fees reward immediate operational adjustments at existing foreign plants while preventing domestic cuts from leaking abroad. Side B weakened its own position by repeatedly conceding that a border adjustment complements domestic policy (nearly granting the proposition's comparison against relying on domestic measures alone), borrowing A's own trade-diversion defense in [B4.3], and claiming scrap and clinker shifts can work within five years under product standards while dismissing those same operational levers as unproven under a border fee.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answers in [A4.1] and [A4.2] decisively blunted Side B's best attacks: A overcame the five-year capital-lag objection by specifying existing-asset operational levers (furnace dispatch, scrap use, clinker ratios, fuel switching, and methane fixes) within contract cycles, and dismantled B's claim that imports would shift to equally dirty third-country mills by explaining that shipment-based fees with high defaults mean a dirtier mill cannot win by changing its mailing address.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that ordinary product standards count as domestic regulation and can block dirty imports without a carbon border adjustment, so Side B never proved the fee would produce larger, faster global cuts inside five years. Side B’s leakage story was strong against uncovered imports, but its closing treated those standards as a concession rather than answering them, leaving the burden unmet.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 answer that recycled-content, clinker-ratio, and efficiency standards can exclude noncompliant imports as regulation, not a border fee, directly closed the leakage gap Side B treated as decisive.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a close, substantive debate by neutralizing PRO’s central leakage argument. Side B effectively explained how shipment-based fees discourage rerouting and could induce operational cuts, but never established that those foreign responses would outpace domestic measures within five years. A’s import-covering product standards directly answered the leakage pressure without conceding that a carbon fee is necessary, and B’s closing mischaracterization of that answer did not restore the comparative advantage. A therefore left PRO’s key five-year effectiveness claim unproven.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal answered B’s leakage questions with enforceable product standards applying to domestic goods and imports, then distinguished when a fee signal begins from when emissions actually fall. B’s closing concession framing was memorably answered by A’s clarification that such standards remain domestic regulation.
