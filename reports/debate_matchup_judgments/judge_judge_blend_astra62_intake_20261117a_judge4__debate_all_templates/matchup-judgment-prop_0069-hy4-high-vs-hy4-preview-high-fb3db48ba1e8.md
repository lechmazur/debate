### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON opened strongly by framing the right as a veto over consequence, using the assaulting driver and the rent-skipping tenant. PRO defused this early by limiting the right to fused inference rather than direct, substantiated evidence, so those examples no longer bite. CON then pressed a real dilemma: either the "fact" travels, making disconnect cosmetic, or nothing travels, letting bad actors escape. PRO answered that dilemma with a concrete test of source and use. A finding must be a dated event with a stated rule, an identified decision-maker, disclosed evidence, and a chance to answer, while a weighted probability built from sensors, ratings, and proxies does not qualify. CON's reply was largely assertion ("no such rule exists"). It treated a deactivation probability as already meeting PRO's criteria without engaging the evidentiary and answerability requirements. CON's best independent point was that scores may be replaced by cruder eviction records. PRO's answer was serviceable: those records already exist, the algorithmic score is an additive second gate, and the fix is eviction-record reform. CON also never squarely answered PRO's claim that even accurate inferences can be the wrong reason to deny housing or work, such as a disability-related pause. That claim undercuts CON's "the injury is only bad data" frame. Both sides were disciplined and clear, but PRO left the cleaner limiting principle standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 [A4.1] was decisive. It answered CON's "banned score with decimals rounded" challenge by defining a substantiated finding through source and process: a dated event, a disclosed rule and evidence, an identified decision-maker, and a chance to answer. Under that definition, a weighted probability falls on the banned side of the line. CON's follow-up [B4.1–B4.2] restated the collapse claim rather than showing why a model output would meet those criteria.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A won by defending a workable distinction between direct, reviewable evidence of an event and portable model-generated character inferences. B’s consequence-escape and crude-record objections were forceful, but A answered that contestable ratings can add an invisible second gate rather than replace legal records. B’s later claim that no limiting rule exists largely asserted the central dispute, while contestability never answered why accurate but contextually irrelevant inferences should travel.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its source-and-use test answered the “score with decimals rounded” dilemma, and its “invisible second gate” answer blunted B’s eviction-record comparison. B’s response mostly repeated the fact/score conflation.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by drawing a workable line between answerable, context-specific evidence and portable algorithmic verdicts. CON pressed the “rounded score” problem hard, but PRO’s source-and-use test gave a clearer limiting rule and preserved safety-relevant facts. CON’s fallback that disconnect would force reliance on cruder records was plausible but less developed and somewhat speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 answered the rounded-score pressure with a concrete source/use distinction and handled the eviction-record objection by arguing disconnect removes an extra algorithmic gate rather than worsening existing records.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): PRO opened with a sharp distinction between portable, fused cross-domain scores and sector-specific, contestable records, and the delivery-worker example made the stakes vivid. CON's key advantage was showing that this distinction was unstable under the motion as written. PRO's own carve-out relied on objective provenance rules, and CON argued that those rules are institutional admissibility limits rather than a personal right to disconnect. That dilemma ran through B2.1, B3.1, B4.3 and B5.1: if provenance is objective, use rules do the work and the personal switch adds nothing; if it is not, the switch also swallows the ledgers and complaint databases PRO promised to keep. PRO's best remaining route was timing, the claim that only a disconnect right works before the harm. CON answered it directly by recasting use rules as front-end conditions of lawful operation. CON also noted that a personal off-switch depends on the worker knowing the vendor exists. PRO's closing then described the right as "the institution proves a named, same-domain source," which effectively conceded CON's framing. Both sides kept good epistemic discipline and followed the format cleanly. CON narrowed the clash more effectively and left PRO without a distinct mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came in B4.3 and B4.5, which answered PRO's "provenance, not packaging" in A4.1. CON argued that an objective provenance test can be enforced by institutions without a personal veto. It added that connective judgments in shared ledgers (matching, deduplication, recency) force PRO to choose: either those count as records, which is CON's rule, or they count as inference, in which case PRO's off-switch erases the evidence it promised to preserve. PRO never escaped this fork.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side A presented a compelling account of portable, opaque scoring and initially drew a plausible distinction between fused inference and contestable primary records. Side B, however, more effectively tested that distinction against the motion as written. Its provenance dilemma remained standing: if records can be objectively classified, institutional admissibility rules can bar fused scores before use; if they cannot, a personal disconnect right cannot reliably preserve ledgers and safety complaints. A’s closing embraced institutional proof of a named, same-domain source, allowing B to characterize PRO’s remedy as B’s public gate rather than an individual right to disconnect. B also answered the ex ante harm pressure directly, while A’s closing continued treating B’s pre-use conditions as merely retrospective remedies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered both pressure questions, then turned A’s “provenance, not packaging” test into a dilemma between an enforceable public admissibility rule and an unstable personal veto. B’s closing crystallized that concession cleanly.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won by turning B’s provenance distinction into a public admissibility rule, showing that front-end source, relevance, and appeal conditions could block opaque cross-domain scores without creating a personal veto. B’s ex ante concern and dossier framing were compelling, but A exposed a stable dilemma: if provenance is objective, use rules suffice; if not, B’s disconnect right risks swallowing the domain records it promised to preserve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answered B’s pressure questions directly with a pre-ingestion provenance test, then turned B’s “provenance, not packaging” rule into either CON’s public gate or an overbroad private switch.
