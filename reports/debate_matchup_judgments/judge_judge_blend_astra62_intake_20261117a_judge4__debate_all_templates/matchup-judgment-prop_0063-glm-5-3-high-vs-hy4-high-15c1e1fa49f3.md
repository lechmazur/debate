### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): B better establishes why reusable authorization can replace repetitive approval while preserving confirmation for exceptions. A’s distinction between rule-matching and contextual judgment identifies genuine residual risk, but does not justify denying optional delegation even for the safe routine class A concedes. B directly acknowledges possible user losses and compares them with delayed transactions and unavailable attention. A repeatedly calls safeguards retrospective despite B’s explicit pre-payment enforcement, and continues invoking irreversible bookings despite refundable-only restrictions. A’s clipped turns also modestly weaken execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s answer that delegation removes repetition, not initial boundary-setting, undermines A’s precision-versus-convenience dilemma. Exceptions failing closed do not eliminate the benefit of ordinary compliant transactions proceeding automatically.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won a high-level exchange by cleanly resolving Side B's central precision-versus-convenience dilemma and building the stronger comparative case for permissive delegation. While Side B landed real pressure with its matching-versus-judging distinction and terms-of-service challenge, Side A showed that durable policies lift the burden of repetition while prompting or failing closed only at the boundary when exceptions arise. Side A also neutralized B's ex post and irreversibility claims through pre-settlement rule checks and refundable-only booking rules, honestly conceded initial user loss risk while showing that confirmation taps equally create a platform "you approved" defense, and avoided the two word-limit clips that cost Side B in Opening and Rebuttal 2.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 ([A4.1]-[A4.4]) broke Side B's "pick a poison" fork by distinguishing one-time boundary-setting from repetitive confirmation, and paired a candid concession on terms-of-service loss allocation with the sharp turn that clicking confirm gives platforms an even cleaner "you approved" shield while leaving offline harms unaddressed.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the motion’s actual permission central: bounded, revocable, user-selected delegation rather than compulsory spending. Side B had strong lines about judgment versus matching and liability, but overclaimed that no meaningful routine class remains, and its clipped turns were an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s “rules too precise/too vague” dilemma: precise rules are class-level instructions, not per-item decisions, and anything outside them should fail closed or prompt, preserving consent without requiring constant taps.
