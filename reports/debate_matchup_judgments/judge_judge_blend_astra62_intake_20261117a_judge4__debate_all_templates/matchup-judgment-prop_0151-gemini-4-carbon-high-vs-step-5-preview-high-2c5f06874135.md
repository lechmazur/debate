### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Both sides ran clean, mechanism-level cases with no fake sourcing. CON's best live route was the "hard case" (a celebrity's first retaliatory quote-post outruns any threshold) plus the closing dilemma (if organic networks suffice, throttling is unnecessary; if not, it's a heckler's veto). PRO blunted both: it answered the hard case by showing the small critic can use the shields themselves (detach handle, auto-hold the loyalist surge), and it answered the dilemma with the organic-human-endorsement vs. industrial-stranger-recruitment distinction, arguing brakes only remove automated mob recruitment while leaving callouts live and shareable. PRO also cleanly separated inbox-level shields from feed-level brakes, deflating CON's weaponization story, and rebutted the "on demand" suppression claim by noting brakes trip automatically on structural spikes. CON never fully neutralized PRO's asymmetry weighing: criticism survives a speed bump; targets don't survive a stampede. PRO's direct A1/A2 answers and disciplined, evolving clash gave it the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (B4): the speed-limit answer to the neutrality question plus the demonstration that shields only protect an overwhelmed inbox without touching the critic's public post, which neutralized CON's weaponization route and set up the decisive closing weighing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): A wins by distinguishing reduced algorithmic escalation from suppression of public criticism, while defending user-controlled shields that B conceded were harmless in isolation. B raised a legitimate discoverability cost for fast-moving callouts, which A sometimes understated. However, B repeatedly assumed an unsupported connection between inbox protections, celebrity complaints, and reach throttling. A answered the pressure questions more directly and better explained why partial, temporary protection improves on overwhelmed manual blocking.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest rebuttal exposed B’s conflation of inbox protection with suppressed criticism. B conceded that isolated shields do not reduce critics’ reach, then asserted rather than established their necessary connection to throttling. A’s closing reinforced the distinction: automatic thresholds are not activated merely by a celebrity declaring harassment.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A built the cleaner path: friction pauses algorithmic stranger-recruitment without deleting posts, and personal shields filter a target's inbox rather than suppressing critics. ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued this cleanly and the core clash narrowed to PRO's "disclosure vs. cascade" distinction versus CON's claim that they are mechanically inseparable. PRO (Side B) framed the moral stakes memorably and answered pressure questions directly, but its central distinction rested on the claim that evidence-bearing exposes are "legible" and survive throttling. CON (Side A) landed the sharpest unanswered pressure: outrage aggregators clear the proposed filter effortlessly by attaching timestamped links and inflammatory framing, while ordinary bystanders whose rapid repetitive quoting is the actual mechanism of accountability get caught by the cascade brake—and viral scale, not the existence of a lone expose, is what moves sponsors and studios. CON also exposed a real inconsistency between B2.6 ("source and counter-clip travel together") and B4.3's retreat to mere metadata, forcing a concession. PRO's response that inbox shields are mere "cleanup" was partially blunted by CON's pre-set non-follower filters and anti-raid modes. PRO's closing mostly reasserted the disclosure/cascade split rather than resolving the asymmetry, so CON holds a narrow but real edge on the live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3's asymmetry argument: aggregators pass the cascade filter with a timestamped link and dramatic framing no algorithm can distinguish from genuine "issue context" without content judgment, while ordinary users' dossier-free outrage triggers the brake—killing the public surge that forces accountability. PRO never dismantled this; the closing only restated the distinction.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won the central implementation clash: A never established how its cascade filters distinguish harassment from legitimate collective scrutiny. B explained why disclosures need subsequent amplification and why mechanical source links let outrage farmers evade A's safeguards. A answered the pressure questions formally, but narrowed its proposal without resolving those objections. Its closing repeated the contested disclosure/cascade distinction and mischaracterized preventive notification filters as cleanup. B occasionally overstated existing protections, but offered the stronger comparative case against A's remedies. A's nonexistent reference IDs were an additional execution flaw.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest response was that the “redundant swarm” is precisely how a small creator's disclosure reaches sponsors and wider audiences. Coupled with the point that clip farmers can attach source metadata, this exposed both collateral suppression and easy evasion in A's proposed distinction.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): CON wins by collapsing PRO's disclosure-versus-cascade distinction: the velocity, outsider, and repetition signals PRO would throttle are exactly how accountability gains leverage. CON also shows PRO's filter inverts, letting sophisticated clip farmers pass with a timestamp while burying ordinary users' outrage, and that pre-set shields address inbox harm without suppressing public speech. PRO's closing repeats the distinction without answering that inversion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2: outrage aggregators clear the timestamp/context check effortlessly while dossier-less bystanders trigger the cascade brake, so throttling spares commercial farmers and shields powerful targets.
