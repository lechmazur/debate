### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the debate on the proposition as written: actual widespread adoption in a consumer market. Side B’s strongest case repeatedly relied on screened, time-limited, clinician-guided use, which Side A exposed as a tactical retreat rather than mass adoption. Side B also conceded that population-level outcome evidence is limited, while Side A grounded the harms in structural market incentives: subscription-based devices monetize worry, and features that reassure users do not retain them as well as features that alarm. That left Side B with plausible mechanisms and safeguards, but no clean route to showing net public-health benefit at scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was Side A’s rebuttal that Side B had quietly redefined “widespread adoption” as broad access to a professionally guided, time-limited tool. This reframe let Side A argue the proposition could not be won by describing a boutique clinical protocol, and it set up the market-incentive point that the scale actually described would select for alarm and over-medicalization.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): A more convincingly challenged the incremental benefit of widespread adoption: normal fluctuations create opportunities for unnecessary intervention, while targeted screening already reaches likely beneficiaries. B offered plausible feedback benefits and honestly acknowledged limited outcome evidence, but its favorable comparison remained conditional on safeguards it did not establish would accompany widespread uptake. A’s edge is limited by overstating market inevitability, attributing concessions B had not made, and failing to answer B’s second pressure question despite using an A2 label.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to “identification is not intervention” exposed that continuous readings also require clinicians, follow-up, and adherence. B explained how feedback might help between appointments, but did not sufficiently establish why that additional benefit would outweigh medicalizing ordinary variation across low-risk users.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the debate focused on adoption at real-world scale and exposing A’s screened, time-limited, clinician-guided protocol as a retreat from widespread consumer adoption. A answered pressure honestly and correctly noted that limited evidence does not prove zero benefit, but was left with plausible mechanisms and hoped-for safeguards; B supplied the clearer comparative account of market-driven alarms, cascades, and cheaper targeted alternatives. B sometimes overstated harms as demonstrated and its second rebuttal did not directly answer A’s second question, preventing a larger margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal was decisive: it distinguished boutique clinical deployment from the motion’s adoption at scale, argued that data alone does not produce intervention, and turned A’s safeguards and limited outcome evidence into a burden A never subsequently overcame.
