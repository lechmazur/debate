### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a clear, plausible causal story: engagement ranking gives conflict a second, unearned turn. It recruits strangers who would otherwise have seen something benign, and over time it teaches authors that cruelty, and even objections to cruelty, buy them a larger audience. PRO adapted well across rounds. It conceded the prolific-poster cost honestly, explained why stranger recruitment is additive rather than a replacement, and tied the debate to the production of insults, not just their distribution. CON's opening points were credible: prolific posters, migration to other channels, and the distinction between amplification and toxicity. However, CON increasingly relied on a bare "unproven" burden claim. In Rebuttal 2 it misdescribed its own hypothetical (sixteen versus twelve) as "PRO's own numbers." Its target-burden answer in B4.3 was muddled and arguably conceded PRO's point that each stranger is a fresh account to block. B4.5 also granted that ranking extends quarrels the target cannot control. CON's reply that followers also reward cruelty is fair, but it never neutralized PRO's marginal-bonus mechanism. PRO therefore leaves the debate with the clearer live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 (A4.3–A4.4) was the most decisive exchange. PRO argued that ranking displaces another post in the stranger's feed, not a follower's reply, so recruited attackers are additional rather than substitutes. It added that the followers' quarrel is what gets the stranger invited. CON's response misread this as a concession that PRO "cannot explain why followers go quiet." CON then leaned on a hypothetical it had supplied itself, which PRO's closing correctly exposed.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) won a technically clean, high-level exchange. Both sides avoided fake sourcing and engaged the counterfactual seriously, but A built the more concrete causal story: engagement ranking supplies fresh participants to pile-ons and pays authors a bonus audience for cruelty, while chronology only rewards frequency. A's decisive move was the additionality/displacement argument (A4.3, reinforced in the closing): promoting an attack into a stranger's feed displaces a bus timetable, not the follower's reply, so recruited hostility is added rather than substituted — a point B conceded in passing ("ranking displaces content; it does not silence anyone") without neutralizing. A also handled B's pressure questions honestly, conceding the 16-vs-12 hypothetical on its own terms while correctly showing it was a question's premise, not evidence, and explaining why it begged the key question of why willing followers go quiet under ranking. B's strongest route was burden-shifting: PRO shows changed distribution, not reduced total toxicity. That framing was defensible and consistently pressed, but B leaned repeatedly on the hypothetical sixteen-replies figure as if it carried evidentiary weight, dismissed target burden as "not toxicity" (a narrow reading of the motion), and offered mostly speculative counterfactuals (prolific posters, migrating abuse) rather than a mechanism that offset A's recruitment story. A's closing crystallized the production-side incentive ("some insults never get written") while B's closing largely restated the burden claim. Clear but competitive win for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's "replacement vs. addition" pressure (A4.3): the slot a promoted attack fills in a stranger's feed costs a neutral post, not a follower's reply, so ranking's recruits are additional hostility — B's B4.1 reply ("ranking displaces content; it does not silence anyone") effectively conceded the mechanism and never recovered the comparison.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A kept the more persuasive live route: ranked feeds can recruit additional marginal participants by displacing benign content rather than existing hostile replies, and that extra reach changes both target experience and future author incentives. Side B effectively pressed A on net toxicity and landed a useful numerical hypothetical, but never fully answered the additionality point or showed why chronology would likely produce equal or greater total harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure question and follow-up on whether stranger recruitment merely replaces follower replies was decisive: A argued that ranking displaces a neutral post, not a follower’s hostile reply, making added attackers plausibly net-new toxicity; B’s “replacement possibility” response never fully neutralized that.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO began with the familiar and plausible mechanism: engagement ranking rewards outrage, and chronology removes that reward. It never developed that mechanism beyond assertion. Its opening also contained a slip that stated the reverse of its thesis. CON built a more original and steadily developed counter-mechanism. Chronology gives a standing advantage to persistent accusers in the local networks that matter most to a target, while ranking at least lets skip signals count as a "vote." CON then tightened that case round by round. PRO's response tried to redefine toxicity narrowly as slurs and abuse, which contradicted its own visibility-based definition in A1.5. CON exploited this cleanly with "pick one standard." CON's concession that angry shares survive chronology was turned to its advantage: posting happens under both defaults, but prominence does not. PRO's A1 conceded that an angry reply does not predict that a stranger will engage. That weakened its own amplification story. PRO's closing then shifted the burden onto CON rather than proving the net comparison the motion requires. CON's closing crystallized the tradeoff more memorably and specifically.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.1–B2.4 did the most damage. It exposed PRO's inconsistent definition of toxicity: stranger exposure counted as toxic, yet repeated local denunciation was dismissed as mere clutter. It also showed that chronology removes the boost from angry replies but preserves the boost from angry shares. That undercut PRO's claim that a clock confines quarrels to people who chose them.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A presented the intuitive engagement-amplification mechanism cleanly but leaned heavily on burden-shifting ("CON must establish... and has not") despite PRO carrying the comparative burden of the motion. Side B consistently broke the symmetries A relied on: [B2.1] exposed A's inconsistent toxicity standard (strangers seeing one comment counts, but neighbors seeing a dozen denunciations is "clutter"), [B2.5] reframed relevance filtering as commercial self-interest rather than idealism, and [B4.2]'s "posting is common; prominence is not" decisively dismantled A's claim that repeated denunciations are harm common to both defaults. B's referee example was vivid, original, and tied abstract persistence incentives to concrete reputational harm among the audience that matters most. Both sides answered pressure questions directly with proper A1/A2 format and avoided fake sourcing. B made the debate easier to decide by winning the key clashes rather than repeating; A's later speeches circled the burden argument without new weighing. B wins clear but competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, especially [B4.2]-[B4.3]: "Posting is common; prominence is not," plus the neighbor-who-skips example showing that a skip is a vote under ranking but not even a vote under chronology. This directly shattered A's core defensive claim that repeated local denunciations are harm shared by both defaults, and B's closing [B5.1] sealed the symmetry point memorably.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B wins by making the decisive clash about salience, not mere presence. A’s algorithmic-outrage mechanism was plausible, but it defined toxicity too narrowly, mishandled burdens, and never outweighed B’s concrete referee case. B’s posting-versus-prominence and conditional-versus-standing advantage weighing directly neutralized A’s common-harm reply and gave the cleaner reason to vote CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 answer that posting is common but prominence is not: under chronology, five fresh denunciations can still dominate the first screen even if a ranked feed could deprioritize them after skips, breaking A’s claim that local harassment is just shared harm.
