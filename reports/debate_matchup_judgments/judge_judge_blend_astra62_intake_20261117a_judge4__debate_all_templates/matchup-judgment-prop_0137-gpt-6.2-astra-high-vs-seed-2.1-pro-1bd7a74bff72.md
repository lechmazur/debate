### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B wins on the clearer, more decisive route. B successfully distinguished between disclosing egg freezing coverage and giving it flagship status, which undercut A's main access/hostage objection. A repeatedly overstated the rule as a ban or impossible checklist, but B showed employers could still advertise coverage on a recruiting page and retain a recruiting reason to offer it. B's core argument—that the same employer sets the cost of waiting and the cost of starting early, so it should not earn broad women's-benefit credit for one while maintaining penalties for the other—was not neutralized by A's double-standard or mental-health analogies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that the decisive question is whom the extra flagship publicity is meant to persuade was the strongest exchange. It reframed the clash away from whether women can read fine print and toward whether an employer deserves broad credit from women beyond those who specifically need egg freezing, while still allowing covered candidates to find and use the benefit.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by staying tighter to the motion: withholding flagship women's branding, not the benefit or a plain statement of coverage. B showed that employee sophistication makes unearned credit for controlling family timing more objectionable when the employer itself sets the penalty for starting earlier, and contained A's chilling-effect claim as marginal rather than a reason most firms would drop coverage. A's access argument was serious but overstated the restriction as burial or a speech code and never fully showed why flagship status, rather than a clear posting, was essential.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, which answered both pressure questions directly and turned A's "workers aren't fooled" and mental-health analogy into reasons the flagship claim fails when the employer prices early childbearing.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A made the cleaner distinction between preserving access to egg freezing and reserving flagship credit for employers whose policies support both feasible timings. A directly answered the recruitment, paternalism, and incremental-benefit pressures, conceding marginal publicity costs without accepting B’s much larger access-loss claim. B offered a coherent visibility and incentives case, but repeatedly overstated the restriction as burying or removing coverage and mischaracterized A as assuming employees were easily misled. B’s closing also claimed the tradeoff was unanswered despite A’s direct responses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, then turned B’s mental-health analogy against flagship marketing where the employer itself maintains the conditions making the alternative costly.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A gave the sharper, more adaptive case: it separated credit for a specific benefit from credit for family-friendliness and pressed an overbreadth example that Side B never squarely answered. B’s cheap-praise/signaling argument was morally clear and well phrased, but it repeated the framing rather than resolving whether transparent terms could avoid that signal or why the rule should penalize honest, employee-driven recruitment hooks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s overbreadth example—a firm with generous leave and childcare but thin fertility treatment—was the decisive exchange: it showed the proposition’s condition could block an egg-freezing headline without tracking the misleading “delay or punish parents” rationale, and B never neutralized it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A wins by making the proposition look broader than the harm Side B actually established. A separated narrow credit for a transparently described employee benefit from praise for solving caregiving penalties, showed that a firm can already have strong leave and childcare while the rule still bars the headline, and argued that disclosure beside a prized pitch pressures employers more than simply dropping the hook. Side B kept a strong signaling and cheap-equity frame and answered the questions, but never fully closed those gaps or proved that flagship status must mean a full equity verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's comparison of dropping the headline versus stating weak leave and no childcare beside it, paired with the firm that already supports parents but lacks conceive-now fertility coverage, left B's "workaround for a punishing culture" claim without a clean factual anchor.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B built the stronger path to victory by distinguishing credit for a specific, employee-valued benefit from credit for being comprehensively family-friendly. Its transparency alternative preserved useful recruitment information, while its example of generous leave and childcare but weak fertility coverage exposed the proposition’s overbreadth: adding the missing support would not itself change promotion practices or the headline’s meaning. A answered the pressure questions directly and presented a coherent account of workplace coercion and cheap equity branding, but too often reasserted that flagship status necessarily sends a delay message rather than defeating B’s narrower, informational interpretation or showing that suppressing the headline would produce better support.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first and second rebuttals were decisive: “Losing a recruitment hook is not the same as gaining a benefit” challenged A’s incentive mechanism, and the generous-leave/thin-fertility-coverage example showed that satisfying the proposition’s checklist would not necessarily cure the cultural signaling A opposed.
