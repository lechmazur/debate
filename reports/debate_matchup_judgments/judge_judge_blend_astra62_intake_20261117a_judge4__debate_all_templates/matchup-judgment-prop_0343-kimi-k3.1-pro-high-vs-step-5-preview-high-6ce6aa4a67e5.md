### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins a sharp, competitive debate by building the much stronger comparative policy case, even with a minor execution cost from clipping the final phrase of its opening. Side A articulated a compelling philosophical distinction between originating an argument from scratch and auditing or defending machine-generated prose, pressing a smart late critique that heavy oral defenses act as an unscalable rescue for drafts that no longer prove reasoning. However, Side B dismantled two pillars of Side A's case: first, showing via concrete coursework examples that permitting AI-assisted drafting does not mean compulsory delegation of thesis invention while explicit carve-outs still protect foundational unaided writing; and second, proving that Side A's counterfactual of prohibition plus process drafts fails because covert AI use can fabricate outlines and false starts too, leaving bans reliant on unverifiable abstinence rather than direct demonstration of understanding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second pressure question [B3.2] and follow-through in [B4.4]-[B4.5] and [B5.4] exposed that Side A's alternative—relying on outlines and visible false starts under a ban [A2.4]—can itself be fabricated by AI. Side A was forced to concede in [A4.3] that AI can fake process artifacts under either rule, retreating to the hollow claim that an unaided draft shows what the student was required by rule to produce rather than what they actually did.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that disclosed assistance plus required student invention, revision, and defense can test reasoning directly, while a ban neither restores unaided practice nor reliably reveals process.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which specified assignments where students still generate the thesis and analysis, then showed that an oral defense either establishes command of the reasoning or it does not—regardless of who produced the first sentences.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by reframing assisted drafting as disclosed, testable demonstration rather than compulsory outsourcing of invention.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2: it answered A's questions with concrete coursework where students retain invention while AI assists exposition, and turned A's concession on oral defense into a contradiction—if defense can reveal unaided reasoning, draft origin alone cannot erase it.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger, cleaner path to victory on the proposition as written. Side A showed that a student who discloses AI drafting and submits an accurate, well-reasoned AI-generated essay satisfies every condition of the motion without practicing or demonstrating the ability to formulate an argument independently. Side A also trapped Side B in a tight dilemma: without universal oral defenses or staged reconstructions (which the motion does not require), dependence goes unaudited whenever AI output is accurate; with them across most coursework, instructors face a major assessment overhaul that implicitly concedes assisted drafts cannot certify competence. Side B kept the debate competitive through strong framing around hidden status-quo AI use ("auditable dependence versus concealed dependence") and cleaner technical execution, whereas Side A incurred minor execution costs from a nonexistent reference prefix ([P2.3–P2.4]) and a clipped final word in Rebuttal 2, but Side B's overreaches—equating generative drafting with rubrics and claiming universities do not certify thesis formulation—left Side A clearly ahead.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B4.3]–[B4.4], Side B tried to escape the charge that disclosure without mandatory oral defenses is merely an honor system by arguing that submissions fail whenever AI hallucinates a citation or produces a circular argument ("That is enforcement, not trust"). Side A decisively neutralized this in [A4.6] by pointing out that error-based penalties police only defective outputs: when AI generates polished, accurate reasoning, no penalty ever triggers even though the student outsourced the core intellectual work.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by judging the motion as written. Disclosure plus accountability for accuracy can be fully met by a polished, AI-originated draft the student checks and later explains, without showing the student can formulate the skill the course certifies. Side B never closed that gap: its oral defenses, staged drafts, and separate unaided exams were treated as necessary but are not required by the proposition, and its ban-versus-transparency frame sidestepped A's narrower alternative of targeted AI use. B stayed live on hidden use and practical governance, but A's rebuttals made the vote clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal: a correct disclosed draft satisfies the written rule even when AI supplied the central reasoning, error penalties never catch that case, and the extra assessments B invoked are not part of the motion.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by distinguishing generating reasoning from judging supplied reasoning, and by showing PRO's oral defenses, staged drafts, and unaided tests are extra safeguards not required by the motion. PRO's auditable-versus-hidden dependence frame never answered the polished, accurate submission that satisfies disclosure while outsourcing the assessed skill, nor CON's targeted-use middle ground.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: error penalties only catch defective outputs, so they never trigger in the central case of accurate AI reasoning the student did not generate.
