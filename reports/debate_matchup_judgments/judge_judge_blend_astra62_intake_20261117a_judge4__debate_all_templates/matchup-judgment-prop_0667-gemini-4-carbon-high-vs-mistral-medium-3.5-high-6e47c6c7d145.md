### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B wins because it showed that a meaningful cut in unauthorized work and the avoidance of large shortages or discrimination cannot be had together. Side A’s own concessions—that E-Verify cannot stop cash or off-system hiring, and that Arizona workers could relocate—let B turn the “arbitrage” story around and treat formal-payroll declines as an incomplete substitute for the motion. B also better explained pre-offer screening and error-driven mismatches, while A mostly restated that safeguards exist and markets adapt.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used A’s cash-payment concession and formal-sector retreat to argue that a national mandate widens the underground advantage, denies Arizona’s cross-state escape valve, and leaves discrimination largely untouched before appeals ever run.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A built the stronger path to victory by showing that B’s case proved, at most, reduced formal-payroll hiring rather than reduced unauthorized employment. A then connected the live alternatives into a persuasive dilemma: displacement into cash work, contracting, or identity fraud preserves unauthorized employment, while genuine worker removal risks concentrated shortages. A also effectively explained why post-offer checks and appeals cannot prevent quiet pre-screening. B asked useful questions but relied on assertions about wage adjustment, automation, audits, and legal safeguards without answering these mechanisms. Both openings were clipped, and A’s source-heavy opening was cumbersome, but A’s later speeches directly answered pressure and progressively sharpened the motion’s central tradeoff.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions directly, exposed B’s contradictory claim that nationwide verification removes off-payroll arbitrage while conceding it cannot police cash payments, and used B’s admissions about data errors and existing illegal profiling to undermine the discrimination defense.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won by turning Side A’s strongest evidence into a liability: Arizona-style formal payroll declines became evidence of displacement into off-the-books work rather than proof of meaningful reduction in unauthorized employment. B also exploited A’s concession that E-Verify cannot stop cash work outside the system, and pressed the discrimination burden more effectively by showing that post-offer checks do not stop pre-screening of accented or foreign-sounding applicants. A’s patchwork-arbitrage and standardization arguments were plausible, but A never adequately answered the underground-work, sectoral-shortage, and pre-screening pressures. B’s rebuttals and closing made the decisive clash clearer and gave the judge a cleaner reason to vote CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing used A’s own admissions against it: because A conceded that E-Verify cannot stop cash payments and framed success as reducing unauthorized employment “in the formal sector,” B argued that universal payroll checking pushes unauthorized work underground instead of meaningfully reducing it, while also maintaining that tentative mismatches create discrimination before appeals can operate.
