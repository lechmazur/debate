### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): A made the stronger comparative case by distinguishing marginal tightening from cheap initial repairs and exposing weaknesses in B’s promised capture rates. B never reconciled multi-day screening with intercepting hours-long releases, and repeated generic CCS delays without adequately addressing concentrated industrial sources with on-site storage. A also directly corrected B’s claim that CCS only prevents emissions from new sources. The margin is limited because A’s own assumptions about LDAR saturation, regulatory reach, and rapid CCS deployment were insufficiently supported.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal used B’s own screening intervals against its claim to catch the vast majority of short-lived releases. This exposed the unresolved tradeoff between inexpensive periodic screening and costly continuous monitoring, undermining B’s repeated low-cost, high-capture assurances.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B exposed PRO's central inconsistency between real-time continuous monitoring and cheap 3-7 day aerial screening, showed how equivalent billions drive LDAR into diminishing marginal costs, and defended fast high-purity industrial CCS with on-site state-permitted injection. A largely repeated the 60% cut and $10-20 claims without resolving detection lag, stripper-well thresholds, or CCS scale under a fixed budget.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and closing physics argument: PRO cannot simultaneously claim continuous-monitoring capture rates and flyover prices when hours-long bursts vent during multi-day revisit and dispatch gaps.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won by turning the debate on equivalent spending and near-term cost-effectiveness. It exposed a key tension in PRO’s case: claiming a cheap 60% methane cut while relying on continuous detection of unpredictable super-emitters, then retreating to periodic aerial screening that B argued could not reliably catch short-lived plumes. B also presented a clearer CCS alternative—high-purity industrial sources with on-site state-permitted injection—that answered PRO’s delay argument better than PRO answered B’s scale and deployment points. PRO’s methane advantage remained intuitive, but its cost claims and CCS rebuttals became too brittle and under-answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to PRO’s aerial/satellite pivot was decisive: it argued that 3–7 day revisits plus dispatch lag cannot reliably stop hours-long blowdowns, while cheap screening saturates and CCS-scale spending forces LDAR into much higher marginal costs. That directly undermined PRO’s core $10–20 per ton claim.
