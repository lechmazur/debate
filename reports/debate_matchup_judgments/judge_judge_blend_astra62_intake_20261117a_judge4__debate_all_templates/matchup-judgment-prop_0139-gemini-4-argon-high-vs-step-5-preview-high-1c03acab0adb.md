### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): B better defended the permissive motion with a plausible immediate benefit and an explanation of why boundary enforcement can be comparatively manageable. A effectively exposed staffing opportunity costs, but repeatedly treated wholesale guard withdrawal, dangerous gender imbalances, and abandonment of reform as inevitable. Those assumptions were insufficiently established to defeat allowing the option. B’s initial dismissal of victim-blaming and tendency to portray A as demanding perfection kept this competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s staffing challenge punctured B’s claim that the policy simply adds an option. B recovered by distinguishing flexible boundary checks from permanent guard reassignment and comparing visible access enforcement with policing harassment throughout a train. A asserted, rather than established, that only its worst-case staffing model could work.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A established a genuine immediate benefit and reasonably stressed that the motion permits, rather than mandates, the policy. Side B, however, built the stronger comparative case: most women would remain outside the refuge, enforcement resources are scarce on precisely the systems targeted, and an officially designated safe zone may worsen conditions and accountability elsewhere. B answered both pressure questions directly and consistently weighed these train-wide costs against A’s carriage-specific benefit. A’s responses kept the debate competitive, but relied heavily on treating B’s operational and political concerns as optional worst-case assumptions without fully demonstrating an effective, non-diversionary implementation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s exposure of A’s enforcement dilemma was decisive: A first defended concentrating scarce guards at designated doors, then retreated to roving checks. B explained why fixed guards divert protection while roving checks may fail to preserve exclusivity, and combined this with the capacity argument to show that relief for some riders could degrade safety for the majority left outside.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by turning Side B’s guard-concentration point into a decisive dilemma: either the women-only car is ineffective without dedicated guards, or it pulls scarce protection away from most women. A also made the failing-system context central, arguing that the policy enables political substitution and worsens the remaining cars. B’s immediate-relief framing was clear, but it never fully answered the majority-harm and capacity pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s first and second rebuttals used B’s own “concentrating scarce guards” claim to force a choice between an unenforceable refuge and stripped security elsewhere, giving A its strongest winning path.
