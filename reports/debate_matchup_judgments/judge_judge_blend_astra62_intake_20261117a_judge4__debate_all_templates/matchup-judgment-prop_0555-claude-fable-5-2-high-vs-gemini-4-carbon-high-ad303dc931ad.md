### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B ran a sophisticated CON case: reframing the pathway as a standing rule rewarding duration of violation, catching an apparent contradiction between A's "rich paper trail" and "invisible unvetted residents," and pressing the sharp dilemma that PRO's own moral language makes its cutoff unbelievable. B's rebuttals were among the sharpest in the debate. However, A answered both pressure questions directly and substantively, resolved the records contradiction plausibly (scattered local files prove residence but yield no biometrics until applicants come forward), and landed the decisive blow on B's own concession that undeportable families would earn a living "the same way they earn it now" — institutionalizing permanent underground labor. A also supplied a causal mechanism B never rebutted: moving settled workers onto legal payrolls plus prospective hiring enforcement shrinks the shadow labor market that actually pulls in new arrivals, which answers B's "the shadow refilled" regeneration argument. B's residual-equivalence claim was thus outweighed by scale and mechanism, and B's best remaining route (cutoff credibility) rests on an unverifiable prediction about PRO's future behavior. B additionally incurred execution costs with three clipped turns, including the endings of both rebuttals. A wins clear but competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1-A4.3): answering B's future-cohort dilemma with a direct "No" while turning it — CON's world also gets future arrivals but stacks them as permanently unvetted shadow generations — and exposing that CON's tolerance-plus-no-work-authorization combination creates "lifelong civic purgatory," a frame B's "the same way they earn it now" answer only confirmed.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B wins the practical comparison: legalization substantially reduces existing precarity, while A ultimately relies on residents continuing to work illegally. A’s strongest challenge—the pressure for future regularizations—exposes a genuine tension, but does not establish additional migration sufficient to outweigh those gains. Future arrivals also do not erase benefits to the legalized cohort. B directly answers the documentation challenge by distinguishing scattered residency records from centralized vetting. A’s repeated clipping weakens execution, but its decisive failure is treating any residual underclass as eliminating B’s comparative advantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing connects A’s answer, “the same way they earn it now,” to continued unlawful employment, then dismantles A’s claim that residual undocumented populations make both approaches equivalent. That exchange gives B the clearer reason to prefer its policy.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won by forcing CON to defend permanent illegal work for people CON admits will not be deported. B directly answered the cutoff and documentation traps, distinguished scattered residence records from actual vetting, and made scale—not the mere existence of a residual population—the decisive comparison. A's rolling-amnesty argument was sharp, but its clipped turns and evasive answer on lawful employment left B's purgatory critique standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 and closing use of A's answer that families would earn a living “the same way they earn it now,” turning CON's residual-population point into an admission of lifelong off-the-books work.
