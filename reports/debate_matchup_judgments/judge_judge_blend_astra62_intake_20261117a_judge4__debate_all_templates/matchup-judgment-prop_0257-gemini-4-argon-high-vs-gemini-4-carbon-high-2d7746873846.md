### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B wins a competitive debate by better connecting its remedies to the proposition: durable access, portability, and preservation rather than merely clearer labels or refunds. A exposed genuine difficulties enforcing resale restrictions on offline files, but overstated their consequences as legalized duplication, automatically halved sales, and inevitable subscriptions. B answered with legal restrictions, transfer caps, royalties, and the distinction between retaining existing copies and requiring continued hosting. A's targeted consumer protections narrowed the confiscation clash but left preservation and lock-in inadequately answered. Both sides made unsupported generalizations, but A's were more central to its claimed decisive impacts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest rebuttal distinguished ownership rights from perfect technical enforcement: retaining a copy after resale remains unlawful even when remote deletion is impossible. A's frictionless-resale comparison preserved a meaningful revenue concern, but did not establish its much stronger claim that ownership necessarily produces two-for-one giveaways and destroys standalone sales.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a live contradiction in Side A's ownership model: unlocked offline files plus transferable entitlements either recreate remote controls or legalize easy duplication that undercuts primary sales. Narrow anti-confiscation rules answered the worst abuses without mandating the full regime, and Side A's CD and DRM-free analogies never closed the friction and official-resale gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, showing that transferring only an account entitlement while the buyer keeps the complete offline file turns capped resale into two-for-one duplication.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won a close debate by exposing the central practical weakness in A’s model: unlocked offline files combined with official resale cannot reliably verify relinquishment, making duplication far easier than with physical media. B also offered narrower safeguards against confiscation. A’s lock-in and cultural-preservation case remained substantial, and B overstated claims such as resale necessarily halving sales, but A’s CD analogy never fully neutralized the frictionless storefront-resale objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: transferring only an account entitlement leaves a finished book, film, or game intact on the seller’s drive. B’s closing then sharpened why A’s CD comparison did not answer an official, low-friction digital resale button.
