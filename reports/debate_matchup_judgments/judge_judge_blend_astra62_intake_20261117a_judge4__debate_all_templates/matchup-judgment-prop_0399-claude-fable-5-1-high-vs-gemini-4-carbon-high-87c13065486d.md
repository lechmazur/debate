### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side A won a close, high-quality debate by holding the motion as written. A's core framing—"prioritize" governs the margin, not abolition—survived B's "margin is abolition" attack (A's rebuttal that rec demand is bounded by the number of children, leaving weekend and off-peak slots, went unanswered in kind). B's equity flip (subsidized vans, waivers, and academies as the only public bridge for low-income competitors) was the debate's most original argument and kept B live, and B eventually committed to a ranking (the twelve keep their block, the sixty flex on half-fields). But that commitment rested on the shaky claim that the flex costs rec kids nothing, and A's closing dilemma—if the flex is truly costless, the motion costs the travel team nothing either—exposed it. A also forced B into a "both tiers, ring-fenced" posture that reads as dodging the required ranking, and A's "their ladder stands on my floor" point turned B's own academy mechanism into support for the rec base. B's counter that recreation "bends" while competition "breaks" was asserted more than demonstrated. Both sides answered pressure questions directly and cleanly; A's answers were slightly more responsive (especially the honest concession that showcases are an unwinnable arms race), while B's "zero lose minutes" answer was glib. Slight edge to A on grounding discipline; B's equity reframe and closing line ("fund everyone's starting line and ration the finish line by wealth") earn orig/rhet parity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Decisive exchange: B2.2/B4.1 commit to the trade—rec kids "flex" on half-fields and shortened games so twelve rostered players keep a full block—against A4.5/A5.3, where A notes B never quantified the cost and then traps B in a dilemma: either the flex costs the sixty something (defending the few at the many's expense) or it costs nothing (proving the motion costs travel teams nothing). B's "recreation bends, competition breaks" asymmetry never fully escaped that trap.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): B won the central ranking clash. B consistently distinguished prioritizing broad access from abolishing travel teams, answered the abolition charge with bounded recreational demand and remaining off-peak capacity, and exposed A's recurring appeal to funding both tiers as an evasion of the motion. A's low-income pathway and recreation-bends-versus-competition-breaks arguments were strong, but they defended only a narrow exception and never justified placing pre-selected rosters ahead of unmet universal access.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's prioritize-versus-abolish rebuttal, reinforced in closing, was decisive: it forced A either to defend making sixty recreational players flex for twelve selected players or to retreat into balanced co-investment rather than contest the proposed ordering.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate anchored to the actual motion: when resources conflict, which tier gets priority? B framed “prioritize” as budget ordering rather than abolition, made the broad, no-tryout, no-fee tier the clearest public good, and forced A to defend selective subsidies for a small roster. B’s closing crystallized that clash cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure question about how many of the sixty rec players should lose access to protect twelve travel players, followed by B’s closing turn that A’s answer meant either the rec kids bear a cost for a select few or the trade does not exist.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides were strong and the debate narrowed honestly to one live clash: whether a modest public competitive tier (regional Saturday play) survives the "prioritize" tiebreaker in poor districts. A landed real damage with the regressivity dilemma (a two-job mother cannot do travel even with cheap fees) and the fee trap, and forced B to concede year-round play. But B's structural insight — that "prioritize" is inert in wealthy suburbs and only bites in the cash-strapped cities where private clubs cost the most — was the sharpest framing of the motion as written, and A's answers (tiered local divisions, pool rebuilds) were asserted more than proven against B's cohort arithmetic. B also handled A's pressure questions directly, conceded the year-round point cheaply by showing the motion defunds competition wholesale, and closed with a coherent fee-plus-subsidy answer to A's trap. B's clipped Rebuttal 2 and A's consistent regressivity pressure keep this razor-thin, but B made the debate easier to decide and won the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2: the pivot showing that PRO's own "tiebreaker" concession ([A2.5]) means the rule bites hardest exactly where private clubs are least affordable, converting A's scarcity framing into B's equity weapon ("who still has a ladder on Wednesday"). A's best counter was the A2 dilemma that subsidized travel still excludes the working poor it supposedly serves.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won the scarcity clash: prioritizing broad access is not a ban, and local upper divisions can preserve serious competition without subsidizing travel. B also showed that fee subsidies do not solve the time and transport barriers facing the two-job family A centered, while scarce fields and coaching dollars spent on a small roster displace many more beginners. A's poor-city ladder argument was powerful, but its concession against year-round public programs narrowed its case to inter-county travel for a handful of players, where B's build-the-base-first weighing prevailed. A's clipped second rebuttal was an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's opening-rebuttal dilemma, later sharpened in closing: if A's team is local and seasonal it fits within broad recreation, but if it is genuinely elite travel, subsidies still leave poor families facing travel time and costs while consuming resources needed by the pipeline that creates players in the first place.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the debate centered on the actual motion: when public resources are scarce, broad access should come before subsidizing elite travel and year-round circuits. It cleanly separated local competitive upper divisions from subsidized travel teams, answered the pressure questions directly with the required A1/A2 format, and made the tradeoff concrete for cash-strapped districts. CON had the more memorable rhetoric and a strong equity concern about private clubs, but its case narrowed too much to preserving one level of regional travel, relied on somewhat speculative fee/revenue claims, and suffered from the clipped rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 directly answered the one-field scarcity and poaching questions, distinguishing local upper divisions from inter-county travel and forcing CON into the difficult trade of justifying travel subsidies where basic access is already short.
