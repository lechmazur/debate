### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): The debate turned on whose counterfactual was more credible: CON's claim that Saddam's regime was in "terminal collapse" and would have produced an equally or more catastrophic implosion regardless, versus PRO's claim that specific occupation choices (de-Baathification, army disbandment, sectarian quotas, Iranian-friendly Baghdad government) made the actual outcome worse than plausible continued containment. PRO's case was better grounded in well-known, non-exotic historical mechanisms, while CON leaned on more speculative, less-verifiable specifics ("a third of territory," "multiple coup plots," "neutral observers agreed") that read as overclaimed precision. PRO also landed a sharp, decisive hit in Rebuttal 1 by isolating CON's "exactly the same" vacuum claim as an unsupported overclaim, forcing CON into a walk-back ("nobody is claiming identical outcomes") that undercut CON's own opening framing. PRO then leveraged CON's own pressure question ("might have staggered on contained for another decade") as evidence that even CON's framing concedes a milder counterfactual is plausible, shifting the burden decisively. CON's best counter to this trick—correctly noting that a question is not a concession—only appeared in the final closing, a full round after PRO first deployed it, and CON's Rebuttal 2 never engaged that specific framing when it had the chance. This late, un-rebuttable-by-PRO correction helped CON but could not fully undo the cumulative mechanistic and grounding advantage PRO built across the debate. On balance, PRO made the clearer, more specifically supported comparative case and handled the central clash with sharper, better-timed rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 (B2.3) isolating CON's "exactly the same" vacuum/outcome claim as the weakest, unsupported link in CON's case, followed by PRO's reuse of CON's own pressure question as a "concession" lever in Rebuttal 2/Closing (B4.1, B5.1), which CON only managed to rhetorically defuse in its final closing rather than in the intervening rebuttal.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): The motion is explicitly counterfactual ("than it otherwise would have been"), and the decisive clash was over whose baseline for "otherwise" was more defensible. CON consistently pressed this point, arguing that PRO's "twelve stable years" was a coercive pause after a crushing defeat, not evidence of a sustainable equilibrium, and backed this with concrete counter-evidence (1998 inspector expulsion, Oil-for-Food erosion, unresolved succession over a minority regime). PRO's rebuttals largely asserted "no evidence of imminent collapse" and shifted the burden back to CON, without ever constructing an affirmative case for why continued containment was the more plausible counterfactual rather than simply the observed status quo. PRO's concession that ISIS's leadership formed in US-run prison camps was rhetorically powerful but analytically risky: CON correctly exploited it to argue PRO was indicting occupation execution rather than the invasion itself, and PRO's rebuttal ("no good execution path ever existed") was asserted rather than demonstrated. The pressure questions were sharp on both sides, but CON's answers (A1/A2) directly engaged and reframed the key weighing, while PRO's closing mostly recapped its framing ("don't burn down a house") without re-engaging the execution-vs-invasion distinction or the baseline critique. CON's closing better crystallized why PRO's burden remained unmet.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 point that ISIS's roots in "US-run prison camps" and de-Baathification are occupation choices, not the invasion per se, forced PRO into defending "no alternative execution was possible" by bare assertion rather than demonstration — a gap CON kept exploiting through to the close.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by offering the stronger magnitude-sensitive comparison: an observed containment regime versus state collapse, ISIS, and expanded Iranian influence. B correctly challenged A’s counterfactual assumptions and identified plausible future shocks, but never showed that succession, sanctions erosion, or the Arab Spring would likely have produced instability comparable to the actual aftermath. B’s separation of the invasion from its occupation policies also imposed an overly narrow causal test. A’s unsupported absolutes kept the result close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned the baseline. B rightly noted that A could not contrast reality with “mere hypotheticals,” but A successfully pressed that twelve years of observed containment was stronger evidence than B’s speculative succession crisis, especially because B never matched the scale of the instability that followed.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): PRO established a clear causal chain from invasion to state collapse, power vacuum, ISIS, and Iranian expansion, anchored by twelve years of pre-war containment without comparable regional catastrophe. CON successfully complicated the baseline with counterfactual speculation about succession crises and sanctions erosion, but never provided concrete indicators that containment was failing or that an alternative trajectory would have produced instability on the scale of the actual post-2003 devastation. CON's distinction between invasion and occupation execution was theoretically interesting but ultimately unpersuasive because the motion encompasses the historical consequences of the intervention, and PRO showed that dismantling Iraq's state apparatus predictably produced the vacuum that followed. PRO's burden-of-proof framing and "house on fire" crystallization made the comparative choice clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2's narrowing of ISIS causation to contingent occupation choices (de-Baathification, prison camps) versus B4/B5's counter that dismantling a national army and state predictably produces such power vacuums regardless of execution.
