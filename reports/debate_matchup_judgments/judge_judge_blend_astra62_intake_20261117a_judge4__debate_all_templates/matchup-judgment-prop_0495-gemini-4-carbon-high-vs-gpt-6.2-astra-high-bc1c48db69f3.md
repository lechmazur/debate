### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate by exploiting Side A's necessary concessions. A made a strong collective-action and continuity case for public funding, but B showed that once public pay requires enforceable duties and eligibility vetting, it either fails at the hardest bottlenecks or risks state control through breach liability. A never fully resolved the tension between enforceable public accountability and maintaining neutral, global open-source trust. B's vendor-warranty and downstream-security alternative, while not flawless, remained a live and more practical route to securing critical systems.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive: it used A's admission that unverifiable or unvetted maintainers would be declined to show public payrolls collapse on the very bottlenecks they target, and it turned A's enforceable-duty answer into an unresolved "who judges safety" problem that threatened state control of shared code. A did not fully defuse that liability/independence tension afterward.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that infrastructure-comparable public pay cannot deliver infrastructure-comparable reliability without either hollow duties or state judgment over global release decisions. Side B built a strong shared-release public-goods case and answered questions directly, but its own concessions—decline unverifiable maintainers, do not seize keys, do not promise a timely safe fix—left the sharpest bottlenecks on Side A's downstream model. Side A also kept a live alternative: warranties, local maintenance, and neutral commercial pools reject unpaid labor without turning committers into publicly accountable staff. Side B never fully closed who judges "reasonable" release conduct or why that legal hazard would not deter the maintainers public services most need.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, using Side B's own answers: where maintainers cannot pass eligibility and handover rules, public payrolls are declined, and where they qualify, an enforceable duty to ship once a fix is "safe" either gives the state merge leverage or leaves taxpayers without a real guarantee.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing Side A's vendor-warranty model admits no obligation to fund the next shared release and leaves direct public users uncovered, while answering the control dilemma with enforceable testing, notice, and safe-release duties, confidential eligibility checks, and project-authorized handover without surrendering commit keys.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the claim that utilities can patch their own deployments: submitting a patch is only a request, while review, testing, and preparing the shared release remain unpaid maintainer work.
