### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B wins the comparative spending clash by defending apps as channels for early, confidential human support and challenging gatherings on cost and accessibility. A establishes that predictable leave requires funded coverage, but never adequately weighs that benefit against B’s strongest app functions. Instead, A moves clinical services into a separate budget category and continues calling apps solitary after B directly challenges that premise. B’s incomplete response on staffing costs, clipped turns, and mistaken suggestion that preserving clinical care automatically defeats the motion keep this a competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B sharply exposed why A’s claim that gatherings consume more resources does not establish greater effectiveness. More decisively, B challenged A’s removal of therapy from the app comparison and answered the belonging question with recurring human connections through digital platforms; A’s closing largely repeated its original dichotomy rather than resolving those objections.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B wins. It kept the vote on the motion’s actual comparison—spend more on rest and contact than on wellness apps to reduce isolation and burnout—and treated time off and gatherings as a package aimed at both harms. Side A landed real hits on exclusion, early intervention, toxic teams, and the weak “expense equals sincerity” line, and the fight over what an app includes stayed live. But A’s closing route, that B’s refusal to defund therapy defeats a relative-spending claim, misstates the motion, and three clipped turns left that case unfinished. B’s cleaner reason is to fund clinical care as care if needed, cut coping content, and spend more on the conditions that create the harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal, which split crisis access from discretionary app content and refused A’s fixed-budget trap, then defended time off plus gatherings as a package rather than two tests A could fail one at a time.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won the decisive comparison: B conceded clinical access matters and preserved it only by relocating it to a separate healthcare budget, while A showed time off does not itself create connection and gatherings are costly, episodic, and exclusionary. A's peer-group and early-intervention answers also left B's claim that apps are inherently solitary largely unanswered in the closing, despite A's clipped speeches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered the belonging question with peer cohorts, separated time off from isolation, and exposed B's therapy-as-healthcare move as removing the app's strongest function before weighing.
