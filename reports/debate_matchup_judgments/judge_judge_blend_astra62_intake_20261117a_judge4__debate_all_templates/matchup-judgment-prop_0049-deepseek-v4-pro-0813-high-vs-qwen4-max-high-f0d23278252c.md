### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) built the cleaner path to victory. Its opening framed the decisive distinction — auditable contextual ads versus behavioral ads "designed not to be" auditable — and its pressure questions landed real damage, forcing B to concede that contextual ads cannot do "selection and repetition aimed at the vulnerable individual," which A then leveraged as a case concession in closing. A answered B's pressure questions directly (A1/A2 format, with the dose-and-selection and advertiser-side-duty answers). B's best live route was the enforcement paradox (a ban requires universal age assurance), but A's symmetry response — that B's own default-off scheme relies on the same "existing age signals" and fails silently for every misclassified minor — was never squarely answered in B's closing, which mostly repeated the paradox. A also collapsed B's arbitrariness objection by noting B's own remedy concedes an age line, and B never recovered. B was competent and coherent on the tool-versus-harm framing, but A's rebuttals were sharper, better sequenced, and made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2: the answer that the enforcement duty sits with the advertiser and is auditable in system logs, combined with the observation that B's "personalization off by default for known minors" concedes both an age line and reliance on the same age signals B called a surveillance layer — a symmetry B never resolved.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B made the decisive harm—individual selection, cross-site repetition, and optimization against vulnerability—clearer than A’s broader content concerns. A ultimately accepted that removing personalization eliminates that specific harm, leaving enforcement and beneficial-use objections; B substantially weakened both by exposing the shared age-signal requirements and contrasting an advertiser-audited prohibition with a reversible opt-out. A’s surveillance concern remained live, but its alternative was not shown to protect minors more reliably.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s final exchange converted A’s admission that contextual ads cannot select the vulnerable girl into support for the ban, then showed that A could not treat existing age signals as adequate for its own defaults but impossibly invasive for prohibition.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO maintained a clear unique harm: behavioral targeting selects the individually vulnerable child and repeats exposure across contexts, unlike identical contextual ads. CON's scholarship benefits and toggleable safeguards never outweighed that conceded machinery-versus-waiver distinction.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won the two decisive clashes. First, the enforcement asymmetry: B showed its preferred rules (universal category bans, bans on sensitive inference) bind collectors and advertisers without sorting anyone by birthdate, while A's motion requires age assurance at scale policed mainly by self-declared age. A's tu quoque in Rebuttal 1 was directly defused by B's A1/A2 answers (judge the ad, not the user; regulate what the system may compute, not who is watching). Second, the transaction/profile distinction: A conceded the feed keeps learning and even called feed-side rules "a stronger argument," leaving A's "incentive collapse" claim asserted rather than earned — the platform still profits from the child's attention via contextual inventory. A's best live route (behavioral targeting uniquely monetizes ordinary products calibrated to inferred weakness) was real, but B blunted it by noting dark-pattern subscription bans can be universal and contextual adjacency beside chosen content reaches the same insecurity. Both sides were well grounded with no fake sourcing; B was sharper in direct answers to pressure, more original (suppression-function argument, "unpaid watcher" framing), and more memorable in crystallizing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2: the A1/A2 answers dismantled A's pressure questions by distinguishing product/creative-level rules and pipeline-level inference bans from birthday-sorting, while quoting A's own concessions ("does not require the feed to stop learning"; "feed-side rules") to lock in the "keeps the inference, removes the receipt" comparison that the closing then crystallized.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): A built the cleaner motion-specific case: the ban restricts one use of profiling while leaving feed inference intact, imposes difficult age-sorting costs, and can be substituted by contextual ads that reproduce some insecurity-based harms. B gave a strong unique-harm answer involving ordinary products calibrated to individual vulnerabilities, but repeatedly overstated that removing ad buyers would collapse the profiling incentive and did not fully close the gap between personalized exploitation and merely seeing the same contextual product. A answered both pressure questions directly and used B’s concessions to sharpen, rather than widen, the final comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered B’s age-sorting and inference questions with universal product/creative and data-computation rules, then quoted B’s concessions that the feed keeps learning and that feed-side exploitation requires feed-side rules. Its response that contextual placements can still deliver the same insecurity-adjacent products substantially weakened B’s claimed unique remainder.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won the central clash: A's ban targets the ad transaction while conceding the feed keeps building the profile, and requires birthday-sorting that B's universal collector/advertiser rules avoid. A identified a real residual harm from behaviorally exploiting ordinary products, but B showed those ads still arrive contextually while the ban removes exclusion signals and imposes weak stated-age enforcement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, directly answering both pressure questions with product-based and computation-based rules, then converting A's concessions that the feed keeps learning into proof the motion removes the receipt, not the surveillance.
