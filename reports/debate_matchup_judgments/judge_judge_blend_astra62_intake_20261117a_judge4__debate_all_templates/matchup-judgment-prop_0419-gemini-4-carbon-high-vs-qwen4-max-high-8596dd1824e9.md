### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): CON (A) wins because it framed the debate early and kept PRO on the defensive. Its central claim was that the motion regulates the channel rather than the outcome, and that a channel can be gamed. That claim stayed live throughout. A's enforcement-parity argument [A2.1] was strong. If regulators can police "fast," "human," and "not a sham agent," they can also police clocked credits and logged safety actions, and those measure what users actually want. A also exposed a real tension in PRO's economics [A2.3]: PRO said obstruction is profitable [B1.1] but also that paid staff make it expensive [B2.5]. A then cited human retention departments as proof that people can be the obstruction. A's pressure questions forced the most damaging moment for PRO. PRO answered that "agent" means "empowered" [B4.1], which let A argue the operative variable is authority. A could then say PRO is reading extra rules into the motion that it would need to enforce separately. PRO's 2 a.m. answer [B4.2] also became an operating-hours line that A could attack. PRO had genuine strengths: - Fraud prevents blanket automated approval. - Live dialogue can clarify messy facts. - Wait times and agent permissions are easier to audit than "justice." - Its Q2 put A's safety triage under real pressure. A answered each of these: - The chargeback analogy for auditability. - Free-text intake against PRO's "garbage in" point. - "Pause matching" as reversible triage. PRO's closing then partly misdescribed A's triage as merely unpairing two accounts, which ignored the pause. Overall, A narrowed the clash more cleanly and closed more memorably.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 and its follow-up in [A4.4]. A asked whether the first agent must be empowered to refund or cancel. PRO answered yes, and A turned that into a concession that authority, not humanity, is the real remedy. It reinforced this with the line "they need my enforcement to make their channel meaningful; I don't need their channel."
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won by reframing the clash around outcomes rather than channel. A never fully neutralized B's core point that a mandated human can still be a powerless, scripted, or stalling gatekeeper. Once A conceded agents must be empowered, B turned that into a decisive concession: authority, not mere humanity, is the operative fix. B also answered the safety dilemma with reversible interim triage and clocked review, and offered a cleaner enforcement story by auditing outcomes rather than speed of human pickup. A's live-dialogue and edge-case arguments were strong but became defensive against B's front-door/back-office distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's use of A4.1 was decisive: PRO's concession that agents must be empowered exposed that authority, not humanity, was the real fix, and B then showed outcome auditing is the fewer-step route.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): A made the cleaner comparative case: a human channel guarantees neither authority nor a remedy, while outcome-focused rules can protect users without that channel. A directly answered the strongest pressure with provisional credit, appealable review, free-text intake, and reversible safety triage. B effectively defended live clarification on edge cases, but increasingly depended on added assumptions about agent empowerment, enforcement, and operating coverage rather than proving the human-path mandate itself necessary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s distinction between humans in the decision chain and a human at the front door, followed by its direct A1/A2 answers, turned B’s strongest challenges into the decisive contrast: B guarantees a conversation, while A’s alternative mandates a reviewable decision.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) opened with the sharper frame. Its "defendant running the courthouse entrance" conflict-of-interest story, the narrow three-lane mandate, and stakes asymmetry were all clear and memorable. Its first rebuttal was also strong: a floor, not a ceiling, and a human denial as an accountable act. CON (A), however, adapted better over the later rounds. Its screenshot point undercut PRO's claim that a named human refusal gives card issuers or regulators extra leverage. Its pressure question forced PRO to concede that "nothing stops" a human from pasting "under review." CON then pressed that concession hard: a mandate satisfied by a human typing a non-answer inside a timer leaves stonewalling untouched while imposing real costs. PRO's retreat to callback deadlines for billing and cancellation also strained the "fast path" framing. PRO's "pick one" dilemma (bots resolve these lanes in milliseconds, or the queues flood) was clever. CON answered it plausibly: denied users still haggle, and line-jumpers click "safety" to skip bots. PRO's closing restated the access-rights frame well but did not repair the hollow-compliance problem. CON's closing line that "escalate past" invents a supervisor tier the mandate never requires went unanswered. Close overall. CON edges it on late-round rebuttal execution, though some of its cost claims ("several dollars per chat") leaned speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and closing combined PRO's admissions into a single objection. B4.1 conceded that a human can paste "under review," B4.5 allowed one-sentence brush-offs, and B5.2 admitted "a rep can paste a brush-off." Together these meant a bad-faith seller could fully comply with a check that only confirms a human entered the chat in time. That hollowed out PRO's accountability rationale, which PRO never fully restored.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins because A's core accountability rationale was undercut: a scripted human can paste a non-answer, and a timestamped bot log already gives chargeback or regulator routes. A's closing admission that a rep can paste a brush-off let B reframe the human path as compliance theater. B also better resolved the pressure questions by explaining why fast automated resolutions and human queues can coexist, and why standby labor costs fall hardest on low-margin firms. A's case was coherent, but it relied on escalation and substantive decision features the mandate itself does not require.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's turn against A's admission that a rep can paste "under review" was decisive: a named human adds little enforceability beyond a written log, and a bad-faith seller can fully comply with the fast-path rule while still stonewalling.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the gap between reaching a human and obtaining accountability. Its strongest unanswered point was that the mandate could be satisfied by a rep pasting “under review,” while neither a decision nor supervisor escalation is required; meanwhile, chat logs already provide evidence for chargebacks or regulators. A also directly answered both pressure questions and plausibly reconciled instant automated decisions with heavy demand from denied or misclassified users. B’s conflict-of-interest framing and emphasis on a customer-invokable, verifiable right were powerful, but its later speeches relied too heavily on escalation benefits the proposal does not guarantee and left the cost and queue objections less resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal decisively combined B’s concessions: human contact is easily measurable, but a human may paste a non-answer or one-sentence brush-off and fully comply. A’s closing sharpened this by noting that escalation past the rep is an extra tier the mandate never requires.
