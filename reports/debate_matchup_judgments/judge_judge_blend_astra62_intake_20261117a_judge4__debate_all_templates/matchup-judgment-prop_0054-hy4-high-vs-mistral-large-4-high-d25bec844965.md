### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins solidly, though not overwhelmingly. Side B opened with a vivid "same minutes, opposite outcomes" frame. Side A neutralized it in three ways: - It called the contrast a cherry-picked extreme. - It grounded its own case in duration-driven displacement: sleep, homework, reading, play. - It argued that quality labels such as "creative," "social," and "research" are easily stretched by children and by platforms, whereas a cap supplies a real stop rule. Side B's main live route was "judgment travels, caps don't." That route was damaged when its own answer to A's Q2 relied on adult attention shifting to bedtime and on vague self-monitoring. A pointed out that this endpoint disappears exactly where the cap disappears, at a friend's house or on a school device. B also never squarely answered the displacement point that "cooperation does not replenish sleep." It kept repeating "two hours of outrage" after A had already answered: shorten the cap and block that content. B conceded that caps are a useful backstop. A, in turn, framed quality as a choosing rule inside the cap. This made the comparison cleaner for A, which offered a coherent account of what advice should prioritize. A answered B's pressure questions directly, recasting the cap as rehearsal for stopping, and adapted well across rounds. B's later speeches largely restated its opening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3–A4.4 delivered the decisive rebuttal. A took B's own A2 answer, that "the relationship supplies the endpoint," and showed that this external stop disappears in the very settings where B claimed internalized judgment would protect the child. That collapsed B's central "traveling judgment" route, and B's closing never repaired it.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the strongest comparative case for simple caps as the primary guardrail. A conceded that context matters, but reframed it as a selecting rule inside scarcity: the cap protects sleep, homework, and family time, while quality categories are easily stretched by platforms and tired children. B's traveling-judgment argument was compelling in theory, but A exposed its practical weakness: the concrete stop mechanisms B offered rely on adult co-presence or abstract self-monitoring, and both vanish at a friend's house or on a school device. A's pressure questions forced B into soft answers like "notice fatigue" and "the relationship supplies the endpoint," which A then showed do not tell a 10:30 PM child to close the laptop. B never fully neutralized that live burden, so A's enforceable-limit-first model carried the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 turned B's strongest point against it: B claimed caps cannot travel, but A showed B's quality-based endpoint relies on a present parent or vague self-monitoring. Where the cap is absent, B's co-watching framework is also absent, leaving the child with stretchable vocabulary, not a workable stop rule.
