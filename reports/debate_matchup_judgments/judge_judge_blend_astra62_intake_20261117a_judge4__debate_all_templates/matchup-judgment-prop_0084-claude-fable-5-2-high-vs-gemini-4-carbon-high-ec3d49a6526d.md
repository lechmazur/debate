### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by forcing A off its opening mechanism and then weighing what remained. A had to abandon true deletion and real-name deterrence, retaining a re-identification token for bans and lawful process while shifting to face-matching. B showed that this both fails the original deterrence claim and creates the live harms: forced recognizable faces, pre-ban outing, biometric retention, and state access where lawful process is the threat. A's ban-stickiness point and zero-budget concession stayed alive, but they did not justify universal compulsion once optional verified filters captured much of that benefit without locking out people for whom verification itself is the danger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, building on its Q1: A4.1 and A4.3 cancel each other. No legal name forfeits name-deterrence, while one-face matching and lawful-process access require retained biometrics and outlaw faceless profiles, so compulsion unmasks the hunted before any ban can fire.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side A won narrowly. Side B offered the clearer affirmative mechanism for persistent bans and answered both pressure questions directly, but its case moved from retained real-name accountability to face checks and a separated token without fully resolving the resulting privacy and coercion costs. Side A exposed that shift, explained why optional verified-only filters capture substantial benefits without universal compulsion, and left B’s lawful-process concession especially damaging for users whose legal identity itself creates danger. A sometimes overstated whether verification forces public facial exposure, while B’s closing rhetoric was stronger, but A’s central objection to compulsion survived more intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1 exposed the tension between “check-and-discard” and durable accountability. B4.1 honestly conceded a retained link for bans and lawful process, but A4.4 and A5.3–5.4 turned that concession into an unanswered risk of biometric retention and state compelled unmasking.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by showing compulsion adds exposure without adding protection beyond optional verified-only filters: casual fakes are stopped either way, syndicates can reuse a real face until reported, and A's shift from legal-name deterrence to a retained face token concedes both the biometric/subpoena risk and the loss of its original accountability claim. A's persistent-ban argument was strong but was answered for users who choose verified filters.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2: one coerced face is not taxed on every failed scam attempt because unreported greetings do not burn it, while forcing face-matched profiles exposes fleeing or closeted users before any ban can act.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B won by narrowing what mandatory ID uniquely does and then weighing that residue against a cost PRO never closed. PRO had a strong dating-safety frame and real hits on revolving-door bans, scam-farm friction, and optional badges, but its privacy fix depended on a regenerable token, platform-held keys, and platforms refusing state demands. B showed that recognition implies identification, that compulsion or compromise defeats severance from chats, and that proof-of-human plus monitoring already capture much of the claimed gain while professionals buy the next mule. The conceded stickier ban on single-identity amateurs stayed live, yet B made it look too narrow to justify linking every honest user's legal identity to intimate life.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that a real mule passes live selfie checks, so PRO's backup is the same device and location monitoring it had called too slow, then B's later point that a compelable platform can still run the token lookup.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A won a close, high-quality debate by more clearly isolating what identity verification uniquely adds beyond bot screening and behavioral monitoring. A conceded the real benefit of stickier bans against single-identity offenders, but persuasively framed that as narrow compared with the universal identity-linkage risk. B mounted strong offense on repeat predators, off-app scams, and the weakness of optional trust badges, but its privacy safeguards relied too heavily on platform design and corporate resistance to state compulsion. A answered those points directly and closed with the cleaner comparative burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first rebuttal exposed B’s dependence on the same behavioral monitoring B had dismissed and challenged the allegedly irreversible token’s identifiability. After B answered with hardware isolation and platform resistance, A’s second rebuttal directly showed that compromising or compelling the platform still leaves the lookup problem, while proof of humanness addresses much of the bot rationale without government ID.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON won the uniqueness clash: the safety in PRO's case was repeatedly shown to come from behavioral monitoring and proof-of-humanness, which need no passport, while professionals use real mules. PRO's token defence never escaped B's point that a marker must identify a person to ban them and can be run by whoever compels the platform. That left PRO with a conceded but narrow gain—stickier bans on single-identity amateurs—against a permanent identity link for every honest, including vulnerable, user.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal was decisive: a mule passes liveness with their own ID, so PRO's fallback to device/location monitoring adopts CON's passport-free tool and contradicts the claim that such monitoring is too slow, shrinking the mandate's unique value.
