### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A's opening cost-asymmetry and failure-mode comparison were strong, but B materially neutralized the core route by showing code-built homes do not require wall-opening retrofits and by shifting the target to emissions rather than building-level self-sufficiency. B's density and affordability turn remained live: universal net-zero-ready can burden apartments, shaded infill, and marginal starter homes, leaving families in older leaky buildings. B also answered pressure more cleanly, distinguishing burnout-time equipment swaps from envelope retrofits and explaining why build-time mandates hit developer financing harder. A's "surrender" closing was rhetorically sharp but assumed the contested premise that building-level readiness is the right metric.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it accepted that code-built homes would stay short of building-level readiness and argued that skipping the costly last rung is a feature, not a deferred retrofit, while turning A's geometry point against density. That collapsed A's "pay twice later" story into a "pay for low-return over-engineering now" comparison.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by reframing readiness as building-level self-sufficiency rather than emissions reduction. Its baseline-code, diminishing-returns, grid-decarbonization, and housing-displacement arguments neutralize PRO's pay-twice and lock-in claims: modern homes need not reopen walls, while mandates can leave families in genuinely leaky older housing. PRO's cost-asymmetry case remained strong but its industry-absorption and apartment answers were more asserted than established.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it honestly accepted that code-built homes stay short of readiness, argued that this permanently skips a wasteful expense rather than deferring one, and distinguished small burnout-time equipment increments from envelope retrofit bills.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): A wins narrowly because its affordability/housing-supply and density objections stayed live. B framed the readiness tradeoff sharply and used A’s concession effectively, but never fully answered how a universal mandate avoids sidelining marginal starter homes or burdening apartments with limited solar potential. A answered pressure directly, kept the debate focused on emissions and cost-effectiveness, and gave the judge a cleaner reason to reject the mandate despite B’s stronger rhetoric. B’s clipped opening is a small execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answered B’s pressure by conceding code-built homes stay short of readiness but reframing that as the correct climate metric, while defending burnout-time equipment standards and targeted weatherization.
