### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by turning Side A's central objection against it. A's whole case rested on the threshold being "unadministrable" due to incommensurable values; B's pressure question (B3.1) exposed that A's own targeted-fee remedy requires the same weighing of congestion, emissions, and displacement costs. B's closing landed this decisively: if those calculations are administrable, so is the trigger. A's attempted rescue (objective marginal cost pricing vs. subjective value weighing) was strained, since pricing displacement in a fee still requires commensuration. B also answered A's capture question cleanly (existing institutions already make comparable capacity judgments; capture proves too much) and converted A's regional-displacement question into support for coordinated regional thresholds. B's "complements, not substitutes" framing effectively showed shore power, shuttles, and taxes don't reduce scale, while A's later speeches mostly repeated the vagueness and alternatives points with diminishing returns, and A's closing was recap-heavy. A's Amsterdam "nearly 90%" shore-power stat was mildly brittle precision. Both sides used proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B3.1/B5.1: B's question asking how CON calculates an internalizing fee without performing the very weighing CON called impossible, followed by the closing crystallization that administrable fees imply an administrable trigger. This collapsed A's core unadministrability objection and left A's case without a clean escape route.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): A built the stronger path to victory by defending the motion’s conditionality and showing that CON’s fees, taxes, and routing policies also require judgments about costs, capacity, and sufficiency. A’s central dilemma remained damaging: if targeted fixes reduce harms below revenue gains, no cap triggers; if they do not, CON never explains why arrivals should remain uncapped. B offered a coherent administrative-vagueness and collateral-damage case, but repeatedly asserted that shuttles and taxes solve crowding and displacement without adequately answering A’s scale objection. B’s regional-displacement question was strong, but A answered it directly with coordinated thresholds. B also relied on an unsupported, suspiciously precise Amsterdam emissions figure and became increasingly repetitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure questions exposed the tension between CON’s claim that resident harms and revenue are incommensurable and its reliance on fees intended to internalize those same costs. The follow-up answer on ordinary safeguards, reversibility, and capture applying equally to CON’s preferred policies further weakened B’s main route to victory.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the motion’s conditionality central and by exposing that Side B’s favored tools still require the same weighing B called unworkable.
