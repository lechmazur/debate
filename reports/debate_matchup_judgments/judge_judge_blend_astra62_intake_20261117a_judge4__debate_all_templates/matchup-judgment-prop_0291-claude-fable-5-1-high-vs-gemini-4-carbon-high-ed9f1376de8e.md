### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won on the central framing. A defended the uncontested benefits of parental controls and had a credible middle-ground point, but B persuasively reframed the motion around the "cannot easily bypass" clause: take it out and the useful parts survive, while keeping it mainly removes the escape route for the most vulnerable teens. A's answers preserved calls/search/school devices but never identified a mechanism preventing liability-averse developers from over-classifying sexual-health or LGBTQ resources once an age flag travels with the device. A's late "obviously" assertion was weaker than B's friction asymmetry point: impulse is deterred by weaker friction, while the motivated teen who most needs an exit pays the cost. Minor execution costs to both sides—A's nonexistent reference IDs and B's clipped rebuttal—did not overturn the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question and follow-up exposed A's gap: once the device flags a minor, no entity in A's design stops compliance lawyers from classifying sexual-health/LGBTQ content as mature, making over-classification unescapable and taking out A's "crisis clinics stay open" defense.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): A won the decisive narrowing of the debate. B’s case about reasonable parents, collapsing app settings, and the harms of confiscation or spyware remained substantial, but B never supplied a mechanism preventing age flags from causing overbroad restrictions. A’s discovery argument also exposed why search, hotlines, and friends’ devices do not fully protect a teen who does not yet know what to seek. B’s closing ultimately answered that burden with “obviously,” while A more cleanly isolated the distinctive risks of hardening the bypass clause.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal turned B’s lifeline list into an assumption of prior knowledge, then used B’s admission that lawyers define “mature” to leave the over-classification burden unanswered.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A isolated the decisive marginal question: what "cannot easily bypass" adds beyond chronological feeds, age tiers, and rules set once. Its friction argument answered the midnight-scrolling pressure—impulse is deterred by bypassable friction, while a motivated teen in danger can still climb—while exposing that B never identified who prevents liability-averse developers from classifying sexual-health or LGBTQ resources as mature. B's confiscation/spyware comparison and claim that search and hotlines remain open were strong, but did not prove that unbypassability, rather than friction and app-store enforcement, produces the benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: the operating system cannot distinguish impulse from extremity, discovery often precedes knowing what to search for, and B had effectively conceded that app lawyers—not parents—draw the maturity line.
