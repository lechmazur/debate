### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): A won the comparative policy clash. B established a compelling accountability case for integrated generators, but never adequately reconciled fault-based liability with payments despite reasonable precautions, or answered downstream hosts’ litigation exposure. A distinguished upload-time suppression from notice-triggered removal and explained why tiny revenue does not imply tiny compliance or defense costs. B’s pressure exposed genuine uncompensated creator losses, and A overstated how reliably viral infringement attracts notices. Nevertheless, A better explained why B’s proposed remedy could impose broader costs without reliably solving that enforcement gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A decisively answered B’s closing claim that “pennies” of revenue cannot coexist with ruinous liability: revenue, damages exposure, and litigation costs are different quantities. That response preserved A’s competition and over-blocking arguments against B’s attempted final contradiction.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B powerfully framed integrated platforms that generate, promote, and profit, but Side A kept the motion's breadth live and showed B's fault-based share still becomes costly strict exposure in practice. A answered the pre-notice pressure with existing builder and takedown liability, while its cost-asymmetry point—pennies of revenue versus thousands in litigation and review—survived B's closing trap. B never convincingly solved detection of unregistered work, fair-use judgment before publication, or small-host discovery costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's Rebuttal 2 and closing distinction between takedown and pre-notice liability: takedown leaves speech up until a specific notice with counter-notice, whereas shared liability forces upload-time blocking, and undiscovered variants pay creators nothing under either system.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): A won by forcing the decisive clash onto integrated platforms that generate, promote, and monetize infringing output, then using B’s own pressure answers to show a harsh outcome: pre-notice platform profits stay with the platform and residual loss falls on creators. B kept the debate close with strong scope, fair-use, over-blocking, and startup-cost arguments, and A’s repeated clipping is a real execution cost, but B never gave a clean concession that an actively generating and monetizing platform should owe anything before notice. That left A with the cleaner closing vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 was most decisive: it answered B’s strict-liability dilemma by distinguishing zero damages for reasonable passive hosts from above-zero liability for platforms that produced, promoted, and profited from the work, while turning B’s detection-difficulty claim into proof that takedown-only relief is inadequate for creators.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): A more convincingly challenged the justification for expanding liability beyond knowledge-based duties. B established a serious problem with harm occurring before notice, but its assurances about provenance, licensing, and rapid review did not adequately answer context-sensitive filtering or small-platform exposure. A also showed how supplied references could support enforcement without repeated URL-chasing. Neither side fully resolved implementation, but A made the incremental tradeoff clearer. A’s repeatedly clipped turns count against its execution without reversing the result.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing turned B’s proposed screening of “opted-in references” into a strong response to the whack-a-mole argument: rightsholders could identify works once rather than chase every copy. This weakened B’s claim that its preferred enforcement mechanisms required broader liability.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A isolated the motion's unique addition: liability before a platform knows of infringement. It answered the viral-revenue and re-upload pressures with knowledge-triggered revenue freezes, stay-down matching, and repeat-infringer rules, while Side B's upload-time triage relied on speculative registries and conceded wrongly removed creators get nothing. B's scale argument remained powerful but never cleanly justified pre-knowledge damages, especially after proposing opted-in reference screening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it showed B's strongest examples were solved once a work is identified, leaving only an indiscriminate pre-notice filter that cannot reliably distinguish infringement from parody, licence, or human work.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by narrowing the debate to what the motion uniquely adds: liability before the platform has knowledge. It forced Side A to defend upload-time distinction between infringement, parody, and licensed work, exposed the speculative nature of registries/provenance systems, and highlighted that wrongly removed creators would not be compensated. Side A’s scale harm and incentive case were persuasive, but its closing reliance on opted-in reference screening helped B argue that the effective remedies fit within a knowledge-based regime rather than requiring the broader liability the motion proposes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal and closing turned Side A’s proposed filters, registries, and opted-in references into evidence that workable responses are knowledge-triggered, leaving the motion as pre-knowledge insurer liability with over-removal risks.
