### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): PRO opened with a memorable frame: counterfeiting the evidentiary commons and the liar's dividend. It held a clean principle throughout, that forged political evidence should be a wrong in itself. CON adapted better, though, and kept turning PRO's own concessions against it. CON's turns worked in three stages: - **Verification gap.** CON used it to show that a ban cannot act in real time. - **Ex post retreat.** Once PRO fell back on "ex post" enforcement, CON argued that viral fakes still outrun the law before Election Day. - **Safeguard thresholds.** PRO's protections against suppressing leaks ("obvious indicia," "forensic inconsistency") meant truly hyper-realistic fakes would escape anyway. CON also showed that PRO's own recklessness markers (source, metadata, refusal to label) describe genuine whistleblowers and deadpan satire too. That made the chilling cost concrete rather than speculative. PRO's best live point was that CON leaves knowing fabrication lawful outside defamation. CON answered it adequately: fabricating a bribe tape is already defamation. PRO did not press the gaps in defamation law beyond asserting there is "no distinct offense." PRO's closing leaned on "the norm matters." CON framed that as a concession that the ban trades real speech costs for a symbolic benefit. On balance, CON left PRO's efficacy claim more damaged than PRO left CON's chilling claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.3/A5.2 turn was decisive. PRO's answers to the pressure questions required "obvious indicia" or "forensic inconsistency" before liability or unmasking. CON showed this means truly hyper-realistic fakes, the motion's own target, evade the ban, while only clumsy fakes that journalists could already debunk get caught. This undercut PRO's core rationale from B1.1.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing a live dilemma: PRO's safeguards requiring obvious indicia or forensic inconsistency leave truly hyper-realistic, offshore and anonymous fakes untouched, while its recklessness factors—source, metadata and refusal to label—penalize whistleblowers and deadpan satire and push platforms to suppress unverified authentic leaks. A also answers the fabrication pressure by showing knowingly fake bribe tapes are already defamation and a ban acts no faster ex post. PRO's closing retreat to the value of a norm never restores the promised trust benefit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: PRO must either act without reliable verification and chill raw leaks, or wait for forensic proof that seamless deepfakes by definition lack, making the ban simultaneously overbroad and ineffective.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won by turning the debate on enforcement and chilling effects. A gave a strong principled case that forged political evidence should be illegal, but B showed that the safeguards PRO needed to protect leaks and whistleblowers would either leave hyper-realistic election-eve fakes online or push platforms to suppress unverified authentic speech. B’s pressure questions and later rebuttals made that tradeoff concrete, while A’s norm-based answer did not fully remove the real-time chill.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO built a concrete model and defended it under pressure. Its statutory test covers synthetic depiction of a real political figure, materially false content, and a reasonable-viewer standard. Contested tapes stay up during review, the challenger bears a forensic burden, and the ban also works upstream by requiring AI tools to block political cloning. That let PRO directly neutralize CON's strongest turn: that a ban empowers incumbents to suppress real scandal tapes. PRO's timing argument stayed live throughout. Intent-based fraud and defamation law cannot act before an election-eve hoax lands. CON's answer shifted from "after the fact, with warrants and adversarial proof" to "enjoined immediately," and PRO's closing exploited that tension cleanly. CON's best move was the parity argument: PRO's reasonable-viewer test also requires contextual judgment, so the ban does not escape the satire-versus-fraud problem. Its closing line that "a recording does not announce its own context" was sharp. However, CON never fully explained how its targeted alternative beats election-eve hoaxes faster than PRO's regime. CON's "permanent licensing regime" framing also leaned on hyperbole. PRO's mechanism details were occasionally overconfident, such as "automated checks take minutes," but stayed within plausible policy design. Overall PRO made the decision easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came in the Pressure Questions round. CON's Q2 asked who bears the burden of proof and whether the tape stays up. PRO answered that the tape stays up, the challenger must present credible forensic proof, and abusive claims face penalties. That defused CON's incumbent-capture turn. PRO then used CON's shift from "after the fact" to "enjoined immediately" to show that CON's alternative cannot solve the election-eve timing problem.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by proving CON's fraud-law alternative cannot address irreversible election-eve deception in time, while answering capture concerns with a stays-up rule, challenger burden, and forensic threshold. CON exposed real vagueness and chilling costs, but its shift from after-the-fact enforcement to immediate injunctions weakened its comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing was decisive: CON first demanded warrants and proof of intent and harm, then claimed hoaxes could be enjoined immediately, leaving no workable way to stop a last-minute fabricated confession before ballots are cast.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): PRO made the decisive clash clearer: election-eve deepfakes can cause irreversible harm, and intent-based fraud law is too slow to answer that. A also handled pressure better by supplying a workable statutory test, keeping real tapes up during review, and placing the burden on challengers to show synthetic fabrication. CON’s prior-restraint and chilling concerns were real and well framed, but its targeted-fraud alternative never fully matched PRO on timing and appeared to shift late toward emergency relief.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 directly answered B’s pressure questions with concrete enforcement mechanics, then used those mechanics to contrast facial synthetic-media review with slow intent-based fraud litigation, making PRO’s timing advantage decisive.
