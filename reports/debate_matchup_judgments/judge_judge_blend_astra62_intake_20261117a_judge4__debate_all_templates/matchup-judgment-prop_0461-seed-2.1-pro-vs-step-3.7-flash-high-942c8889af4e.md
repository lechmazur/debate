### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A won by owning the comparative weighing throughout: the documented status-quo accountability gap (whistleblower-leaked Meta teen harm research) versus B's residual-risk fears, with concrete guardrails (no raw data leaving platform infrastructure, MPC, joint liability, compensation pool, third-party review of access denials) that blunted each of B's live routes. B's sharpest lines—cross-border unenforceability of researcher liability, irreversibility of re-identification harm, and IRB capture—were answered directly in A's well-formatted A1/A2 rebuttal, and B never squarely weighed its privacy risks against the concrete societal harms A documented, instead repeatedly labeling the benefit "speculative" without engaging the Meta example. B also offered no alternative mechanism for the accountability gap, falling back on defending concentrated platform control, which A exploited given platforms' financial incentives to suppress findings. B's rebuttals were sharper and better grounded (avoiding A's occasional overclaims like "statistically impossible" re-identification), and its pressure questions were pointed, but B's case ultimately rested on worst-case hypotheticals that A's design answers partially neutralized, while B left A's core weighing largely intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 cross-border liability strike (B2.1) was the most damaging exchange: it exposed that A's one-line "strict liability" fix was functionally unenforceable across jurisdictions, forcing A to build the compensation pool and joint-liability apparatus in Rebuttal 2—A's answer was responsive but relied on stipulated mandate design, letting B's closing frame the whole regime as making vulnerable users bear uncompensable risk for unproven benefit.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side B wins by making the added, irreversible privacy risk the decisive burden. B repeatedly showed strict liability, IRB gatekeeping, and compensation do not solve cross-border enforcement or undo exposure for vulnerable users, while A shifted from probabilistic safeguards to overconfident claims of mathematical impossibility and never proved the research benefit would be reliable rather than sensationalized. A's accountability-gap case remained strong but was answered by B's bounded-versus-distributed risk comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal most decisively answered A's pressure responses: compensation cannot undo an abuser or employer learning sensitive activity, joint liability fails against unenforceable bad-faith actors, and the appeal panel does not cure inconsistent IRB review.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO wins narrowly by making the central clash clearer: an accountability gap under platform self-control versus residual but mitigated privacy risk. PRO answered pressure questions more directly with guardrails, compensation, and appeal mechanisms, while CON’s privacy and gatekeeping objections remained plausible but less decisive. PRO’s overconfident privacy claims were discounted, but its comparative case still edged out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 directly answered the privacy-threshold and incumbent-gatekeeping pressures with no-raw-data access, a compensation pool, and third-party review of denials; CON kept residual risk alive but did not fully neutralize the comparative accountability case.
