### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by turning PRO's own concessions into a decisive clash. A1 established the irreversibility asymmetry (defenses adjustable, cleared neighborhoods permanent) and identified real coastal assets. B2.2 narrowed retreat to scattered edge buyouts while keeping ports and hospitals defended, and A2.1 immediately exploited this as conceding that defense of dense assets remains the priority—directly undercutting the motion's "rather than prioritize" framing. A4.4 posed a genuine dilemma PRO never resolved: full voluntariness and maintained services mean permanent risk reduction is not guaranteed; a binding timetable means coercion. PRO's closing leaned on political capture, but A5.1 reversed it cleanly—the state too captured to deny luxury subsidies is somehow capable of sustaining replacement-value buyouts, full services, and resident timeline control. PRO's answers (A1/A2 format respected) were responsive but ultimately converted retreat into a supplement while calling it a "lead default," and its idealized program claims carried some brittle confidence. CON gave clearer thresholds, cleaner weighing, and a sharper reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4's dilemma (voluntariness vs. guaranteed risk reduction) combined with A5.1's capture reversal—using PRO's own "pre-flood full replacement value" promise to show retreat demands more, not less, state capacity than simply writing defense eligibility rules. PRO never answered either.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A won the central clash over the motion as written. A also answered the threshold and luxury-capture pressures directly, then turned both: capture would undermine PRO's generous, service-preserving buyouts even more, and full voluntariness cannot guarantee permanent risk reduction without eventual coercion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its threshold answer neutralized B's fiscal-lock-in pressure, while its coercion dilemma—either holdouts remain exposed or the timetable eventually coerces departure—undercut PRO's claim to permanent yet voluntary safety.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by building the cleaner decisive clash: defenses preserve adaptable options while retreat is irreversible, and it answered PRO’s political-economy attack with a strong capacity symmetry point. B also gave a clear threshold for when retreat should occur, making its position less rigid and more administrable. Side A made a compelling case about lock-in, equity, and reallocating resources, but did not fully resolve the voluntariness/holdout dilemma or explain why retreat politics would be less captured than defense politics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing response to Side A’s pressure questions: B conceded retreat only at physical infeasibility or negative net public value, then turned PRO’s capture argument by saying the same state capacity needed for fair retreat could instead enforce targeted defenses and deny luxury seawalls.
