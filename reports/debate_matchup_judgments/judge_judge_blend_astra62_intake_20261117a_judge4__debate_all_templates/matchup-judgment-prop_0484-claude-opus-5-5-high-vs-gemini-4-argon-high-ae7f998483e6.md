A made the debate easier to decide on the motion as written.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger path to victory. Its pragmatic case — votes cannot bind sovereign emitters, walkouts would follow, and moral authority is island states' real weapon — was pressed consistently and sharpened each round. A's rebuttals exploited two genuine concessions: B's CBAM answer (B4.1) showed enforcement leverage exists without the motion, and B's "baseline geography" answer (B4.2) quietly detached the metric from actual risk, which A correctly flagged as a static entitlement that abandons the motion as written. B's best live route — that the current system is failing and consensus lets polluters greenwash — survived but never resolved the core enforcement problem: B never showed why defection from a forum outvoted by tiny states costs more than staying. A's closing crystallized the decisive clash cleanly: legitimacy forfeited by a "rigged count" evaporates, and the atmosphere counts tons, not votes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5-A4.6: Side A's dissection of B's two pressure answers — noting that Europe's carbon tariffs were built unilaterally without weighted votes, and that freezing weight at "baseline physical geography" means the formula no longer measures existential risk at all — converted B's own concessions into the decisive reasons to reject the motion.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the European-tariff defense most directly damaged A’s enforcement mechanism: Europe’s independent trade leverage already exists, and A did not establish why weighted votes would make Europe enforce island-backed mandates. This exposed the missing bridge between winning procedural authority and achieving emissions reductions.
