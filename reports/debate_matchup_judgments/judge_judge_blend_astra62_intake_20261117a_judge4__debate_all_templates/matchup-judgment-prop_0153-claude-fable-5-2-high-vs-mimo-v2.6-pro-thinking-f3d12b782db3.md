### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued at a high level, but PRO (B) handled the decisive pressure better. CON's best weapon — the "screen can't do the work twice" dilemma — was blunted by PRO's closing denial of the binary: most women are neither desperate nor indifferent, and the nurse-with-a-house-deposit example made the middle case concrete. CON's rejoinder ("say the number out loud") reasserted the scale worry but only damages PRO's auxiliary demand-side argument, not the core comparison of regulated paid surrogacy versus prohibition. PRO also answered the contract pressure cleanly by splitting medical authority (no remedy, correctly) from parentage (pre-birth order), and turned CON's "marginal recruit" answer into a devastating framing: she keeps every pressure and loses the option, and nothing in CON's world pays her rent. CON's strongest moments — the consent-by-economic-pressure mechanism and the scale trap — were real but ultimately met by PRO's inducement-versus-coercion distinction and the note that unpaid surrogacy shares every pregnancy-specific harm with fewer protections. PRO's consistent comparative weighing ("which policy leaves a woman better off") made the debate easier to decide. CON's "my protection is modest, theirs is impossible" was memorable but rested on the dilemma PRO had already partially dismantled.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The B5.2–B5.4 sequence in PRO's closing, converting CON's "marginal recruit" into an admission that the ban protects no one — she keeps her debts and loses the option — combined with the nurse-with-a-house-deposit example that broke CON's strict/porous screen dichotomy.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by showing CON permits the same pregnancy, handover, and medical risks unpaid, making payment—not surrogacy—the supposed harm. PRO's distinction between bodily autonomy and legal parentage answers the contract dilemma, while the screened, financially stable surrogate motivated by a house deposit breaks CON's strict-or-porous screen dilemma. CON's recruitment-from-the-bottom mechanism remains plausible but never explains why denying pay protects the marginal woman or the surrogate hired abroad.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's rebuttal separating medical decisions, which remain unenforceable against the surrogate, from parentage, payment, and escrow, which a pre-birth order can secure—dissolving A's claim that the contract must be either coercive or fictional.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A won narrowly by making the debate about which regime leaves actual women better protected. It turned CON’s altruistic alternative into a concession by stressing that the same bodily and handover issues exist without pay, then offered a concrete regulatory architecture: financial screening, escrow, independent counsel, medical autonomy, and pre-birth parentage. B’s market-recruitment and screen-dilemma arguments were sharp, but they did not fully prove that a well-screened paid system would be worse than an unpaid or prohibition regime that still leaves demand, debt, and offshore surrogacy in place.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 screen dilemma—strict screening starves supply, loose screening admits economically pressured women—was the sharpest exchange; A’s stable-nurse/house-deposit answer kept its route alive, though the scale question remained contested.
