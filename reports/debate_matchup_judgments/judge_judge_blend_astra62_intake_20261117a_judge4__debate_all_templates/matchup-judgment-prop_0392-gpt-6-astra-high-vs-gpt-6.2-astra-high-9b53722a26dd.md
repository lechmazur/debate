### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A won a high-level exchange by proving that binding repair orders and meaningful fines for repeated failure perform complementary jobs, while neutralizing Side B's resource-diversion harms. Side A demonstrated that under compulsory remediation alone, delaying repairs or gambling on rushed releases preserves the benefit of delay—the provider merely pays later for the work it skipped, while unrecorded excluded users (like the midnight job applicant) suffer irreversible losses that post-hoc restoration cannot fix. Side A defused Side B's third-party supplier and capacity critiques by targeting upstream vendors where they control the code, exempting providers meeting realistic milestones while supplying interim access, and ring-fencing independently costed repair budgets with deferred collection where necessary. Finally, Side A exposed the gap in Side B's alternative of ongoing testing orders backed only by contempt for deliberate disobedience: procedural checklists allow compliance without guaranteeing that modified features actually work, whereas outcome-based fines align the provider's private knowledge of its changes with functional access.
