### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won the decisive exchanges. PRO's case rested heavily on a speculative adherence advantage, but CON showed that GLP-1s mainly produce an energy deficit and lean-mass loss, not a direct performance boost. PRO then conceded that the energy deficit costs power, leaving only the weaker claim that the drug makes weight loss less uncomfortable. CON's reply that "ease is not capacity" undercut that route. More importantly, CON trapped PRO on the policy question: the proposed declaration/TUE-style middle ground either collapsed into existing monitoring or became prohibition in fact, and PRO never produced a clear third option. PRO's detection reversal also hurt its credibility. While both sides had clipped speeches, B's comparative case was cleaner and better resolved the live clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's dilemma on "stricter scrutiny is not restriction" was decisive: it forced PRO's proposed declaration/medical-justification measure into either existing disclosure or a TUE-style prohibition, and PRO never recovered. B's follow-up that "ease is not capacity" after PRO conceded the deficit costs power also deflated the core advantage claim.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won both the institutional and physiological clashes while maintaining far stronger factual and citation discipline. Institutionally, B trapped A in a dilemma A never escaped: data-gathering without punishment already happens under anti-doping monitoring, whereas A's invocations of TUEs and medically justified use are prohibitions in disguise. Physiologically, B cleanly distinguished acute dehydration weight-cutting from genuine mass loss, contrasted EPO's direct oxygen-carrying boost with calorie-deficit power loss (which A conceded in A4.2), and showed why easier dieting compliance alone is not extra physical capacity on race day. B also caught A's factual error and 180-degree flip on semaglutide's clearance speed, whereas A misattributed claims to B and cited a nonexistent reference ID (C1.2-C1.3).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After B2.1-B2.3 and Q1 (B3.1) challenged A to name a concrete measure between existing monitoring and prohibition, A4.1 proposed mandatory declaration where use remains "permitted once declared and medically justified." In B4.3-B4.4, B quoted A's exact phrasing to show that barring medically unjustified use is simply a prohibition and TUE system under another name, while mere disclosure without restriction adds nothing to standard medication declarations.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side A won by giving the clearer live route: a concrete, non-ban form of stricter scrutiny (mandatory declaration plus targeted out-of-competition testing) and a precautionary epistemic frame that scrutiny is how evidence gets generated when a plausible advantage is unresolved. Side B’s physiological case and monitoring-status point were strong, but its status-quo-or-ban dilemma was too binary and did not fully neutralize A’s adherence/power-to-weight risk or the need for sport-specific targeted scrutiny. A’s early detection inconsistency and clipped turns kept the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Q1 demanded a measure that was neither existing monitoring nor prohibition; A’s Rebuttal 2 answer—declaration plus targeted out-of-competition testing—provided a workable middle ground and kept PRO’s evidence-gap route alive despite B’s binary counterattack.
