- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins a high-level debate by building a coherent cost-benefit framework and systematically dismantling Side A's aerospace learning-curve defense. Side A wrote with sharp rhetorical punch and rightly pushed back on Side B's overly specific pre-flight contract example in [B4.1], which Side B cleanly corrected in [B5.2]. However, Side A's closing synthesis relied on two flawed moves: treating "identical exhaust carries identical weight" as proof that emissions cannot separate sightseeing from drought monitoring (ignoring what we get in return for bearing the exhaust), and claiming that because an equally extravagant wedding also faces ethical scrutiny under global poverty, space tourism falls outside the motion (importing an unwritten uniqueness requirement while ignoring that rocket tourism combines acute upper-atmospheric externalities with massive marginal opportunity cost).

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that Side B's case either generalizes to all affluent discretionary spending or applies an asymmetric bar that counts tourist-flight costs fully while discounting price-mediated benefits that Side B's own drought example needs. Those concessions were never neutralized, so the motion-specific verdict did not survive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttals turned Side B's wedding-scrutiny and identical-exhaust concessions into a standing asymmetry: emissions could not single out tourism, and the charity test could not either, leaving only an evidentiary veto Side B had disowned.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B made the decisive clash clearer. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B wins a clear but competitive debate by anchoring the motion's exact burden—that "hard to justify" means steep, currently unmet justificatory demands rather than categorical impossibility—and turning Side A's own ethical conditions into proof of the proposition. Side A fought brilliantly on philosophical principle, neutralizing Side B's private-wealth and propulsion-engineer opportunity-cost claims, refuting subsistence double-counting in [A5.1], and catching Side B's slight overstatement of [A4.1] in [B5.1]. However, Side A's defense remained written in the conditional tense: it conceded that uncapped, unpriced operators failing climate limits should not fly, that stratospheric rocket exhaust requires a wide safety margin, and that buyers should decline tickets sold on a brochure alone absent credible upper-bound disclosures—while also requiring passengers to fulfill demanding poverty duties and forgo years of ordinary travel. Because real-world space tourism lacks those caps, prices, and verified disclosures while planning thousands of flights, Side A only salvaged in-principle permissibility for an idealized traveler rather than showing space tourism today is not ethically hard to justify.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's critique across [B2.1]-[B2.2], [B4.2], and [B4.7]—exposing that Side A defended a hypothetical industry "written in the conditional tense" and contrasting aviation's imperfect carbon ledgers with a suborbital ticket's "brochure"—was decisive because it forced Side A in [A4.1] and [A5.2] to concede that buyers should decline brochure-only tickets lacking credible upper-bound exhaust estimates, effectively proving that actual space tourism fails Side A's own standard.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B more convincingly established that space tourism is hard to justify under present conditions. It kept the burden on actual justification, showed that Side A's fair-allowance and fair-share escapes remain conditional, unaudited, and incomplete as to high-altitude harm and scarce talent, and closed by turning Side A's own "bound the costs or decline" rule against the industry as it exists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal and closing, which treated Side A's "refuse if costs cannot be bounded" standard as decisive once no tester, ledger, or produced estimate was shown to exist.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner test: meet a finite fair-share duty on poverty and stay within a conservatively estimated fair climate allowance. A's engineering and public-resource opportunity-cost claims were never tied to a concrete displaced project.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and closing distinction between no enforcer and no possible estimate, plus the double-counting answer: subsistence sets the fair share but cannot then be charged again against every discretionary trip.
