- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making legal fit the decisive issue: broker-dealer and securities-exchange duties attach to securities transactions, not merely to generic matching, custody, and spread-taking. CON also defused PRO's strongest enforcement point by showing a tailored regime could include licensing, capital, segregation, examination, and sanctions. Though A's Rebuttal 2 was clipped, the surviving text still answered the required A1/A2 points.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.1/A4.3 was decisive: it turned PRO's own pressure question back by showing no broker-dealer obligation becomes legally relevant without a securities transaction, and that tailored regulation can carry the same continuous oversight and enforcement tools PRO claimed were unique to registration.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins a clear but competitive debate by building the stronger comparative case on the proposition as written and executing cleanly across all five turns. Side B established that dominant crypto platforms combine order matching, custody, market-making, order routing, and leverage, and that lacking a central clearinghouse makes the platform itself the counterparty of last resort—rendering broker-dealer and exchange capital, segregation, conflict, and examination rules urgent relative to a lightly supervised category. Side A (CON) kept the round genuinely competitive with sharp critiques of securities-specific legal friction (particularly issuer disclosures and backdoor token classification) and Side B's shift from platform count to volume and losses when defining "most." However, under Side B's pressure on proof-of-reserves snapshots and continuous oversight, Side A quietly abandoned the motion's explicit counterfactual ("a distinct lightly supervised asset category") and proposed a bespoke regime with identical heavy supervisory, capital, examination, and enforcement powers ([A4.3], [A5.3]), validating Side B's core premise while leaving Side B with the practical advantage of an already-working regime over promised future rules. The erroneous P2.2 reference and the clipped second rebuttal cost Side B some execution quality, but not the core comparative advantage.
