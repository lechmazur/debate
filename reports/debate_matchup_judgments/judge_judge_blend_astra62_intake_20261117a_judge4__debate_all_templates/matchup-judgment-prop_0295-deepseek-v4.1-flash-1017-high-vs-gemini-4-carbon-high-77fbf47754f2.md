### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) won the decisive clash. Side B's strongest route was the "scoreboard/earmark leverage" dilemma: either refusal ends the duty (making "incomplete" meaningless) or it doesn't (donor priorities govern). Side A broke this dilemma cleanly in A4.3 and A5.3 with the waivable-debt/polluter-trust analogy — a duty to tender payment that the creditor can waive is still a duty, and marking it unpaid does real work by preventing wrongdoers from claiming moral closure at the loading dock. Side A also forced a key concession: under pressure, Side B admitted an unpaid debt for contamination and records exists (B4.1), reducing CON's case to a definitional firewall ("restitution is complete at shipping; the debt is separate") that never explained why victims should bear detoxification and identification costs in the interim. Side A's locally governed endowment model neutralized the trusteeship attack, which Side B effectively conceded gives no formal veto (B4.3). Side B's residual earmark critique was answered by redefining the three funding categories as repairs for the specific colonial harms (contamination, erasure, extraction) rather than a donor agenda. Both sides argued the motion as written, used plausible background knowledge without fake sourcing, and followed format; Side A simply resolved more of the live pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The pivotal exchange was B4.4–B4.5's dilemma ("if refusal ends the duty, the 'unfinished' label does no work") against A5.3's answer that a mandatorily tendered, creditor-waivable payment is a debt, not voluntary aid — it stops wrongdoers from walking away unpaid. That answer preserved PRO's completion test while eliminating CON's sovereignty objection, deciding the debate.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B better distinguished an obligation to fund repair from a right to supervise recipients. Its locally governed funding and explicit right of refusal answered A’s sovereignty objection, while contamination and lost provenance explained why shipment alone leaves substantive harms unresolved. A offered a credible unrestricted-compensation alternative, but repeatedly assumed donor control that B’s model expressly excluded and never established why heritage-directed funding necessarily creates that control.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest answer was that a creditor’s ability to waive payment does not make the debtor’s obligation voluntary. This directly defeated A’s repeated claim that allowing recipients to refuse funding emptied the proposition of meaning, while preserving B’s distinction between unconditional ownership and the former power’s repair obligations.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A won by holding the motion to its actual completion test. Both sides agreed toxic, undocumented returns create real debts and that a truly local trust need not give foreigners a veto. Side B’s honest answers—that refusal ends the duty and locals redefine every category—then left “incomplete” doing little work except marking whether an earmarked package was tendered. Side A converted that concession into a clean dilemma: either refusal completes restitution, in which case the label is donor disappointment, or it does not, in which case recipient control is qualified. Unrestricted reparations plus unconditional title, custody, and records remained the clearer rule. Side B’s walk-away critique stayed live but never reconciled the earmark with sovereignty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal and closing turned Side B’s concession that refusal ends the duty into a dilemma: the “unfinished” label either signals donor disappointment or lets donor priorities govern.
