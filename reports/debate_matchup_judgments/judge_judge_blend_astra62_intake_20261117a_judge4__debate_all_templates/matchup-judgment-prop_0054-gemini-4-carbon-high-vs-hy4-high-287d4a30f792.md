- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp and adapted well, but PRO built the cleaner path to victory on the motion as written. PRO pressed this consistently. Later, CON conceded that severe harms require "no bedroom devices, no stranger chat." That concession let PRO argue that the protections that matter most come from quality and context, which is exactly what the motion claims. CON's strongest line was opportunity cost and failure-mode asymmetry: "good context" can bless six hours, while a cap bounds the dose. CON pressed it well, and PRO's answer, that natural stopping points exist and a nearby parent will notice, was the softest part of PRO's case. CON answered the cross-device counting question competently with the recreational-token budget. However, CON never fully escaped the charge that a cap only bounds toxic exposure rather than preventing it. CON's "add safeguards to a cap" framing also quietly conceded that context rules carry much of the protective weight. On balance, PRO made the priority question easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's turn in A4.4 and A5.3 was the most decisive exchange. PRO took CON's "hard stops smuggled" charge and its admission that severe harms need bedroom and stranger-chat bans, and recast both as concessions that shaping what kids use and where devices live does the core protective work. That undercut CON's claim that the cap should come first.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won the exhausted-parent comparison. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): This was a close, high-quality debate, and CON edges it. PRO built a strong mechanism case: a cap is false precision that treats a grandparent call and a midnight pile-on as equivalent, and a child can stay under a limit while being harmed. PRO also landed a real hit by showing that CON's carve-outs need a judge. CON, however, did the more damaging comparative work. Its first rebuttal reframed PRO's own triage tools: bedroom charging is a time cutoff, and asking what media displaced is a duration test. That made PRO's "downstream fence" look like it borrows the cap's logic. CON's pressure question then forced PRO to clarify its boundary. PRO's answer, a household timetable built around protected goods, was reasonable but vulnerable. CON exploited it directly: unscheduled boredom and movement have no calendar slot, so without a leading cap, apps fill every free hour. CON also capitalized on PRO's admission that context checks cannot catch silent shifts in real time. PRO's best late turn was strong: if CON relies on mood and conversation after logging off, that is context work. CON pre-empted it, though, by casting the cap as the precondition for that conversation and arguing that pairing a cap with check-ins is easy while restoring a demoted boundary is hard. Both sides stayed grounded with no fake citations. CON's execution-and-displacement story was slightly harder to dislodge by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.1/B2.2 argument, that PRO's own defaults (bedroom charging, displacement checks) are really time-based limits, reframed PRO's "map first, fence later" as conceding that clock time protects offline childhood. It set up CON's later win on the unscheduled-boredom point. PRO's strongest counter was A5.1, which noted that CON's reliance on after-session conversation is itself context-first work.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B argued the priority question more convincingly: leading with caps provides an enforceable backstop against displacement and unseen exposure while retaining contextual check-ins. A offered credible safeguards, but repeatedly treated cap-first advice as cap-only and did not adequately resolve B's comparison between preset limits and recurring qualitative negotiation. B overstated the claim that unstructured offline time cannot be protected without daily caps, keeping this a competitive rather than decisive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B distinguished narrow, preset exemptions from ongoing judgments about leisure quality, substantially answering A's “timer needs a judge” objection. B then challenged A's compliant-but-distressed-child example directly: cap-first advice still permits check-ins, while context-first defaults also miss concealed harms. This shifted the comparison toward enforceable exposure limits rather than perfect detection.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): B wins narrowly because it kept the debate centered on what advice actually catches: caps can bound minutes but cannot identify, interpret, or repair social and emotional harms. A’s practicality and the unscheduled-boredom point were strong, but B’s triage defaults and closing failure-scenario comparison gave the cleaner reason to favor quality and context as the primary focus.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Q2 forced A to say hidden harm is revealed through a child’s mood and conversation after logging off; B then used that concession to show the timer creates space but context supplies the actual guidance.
