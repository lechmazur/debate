### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides were strong, but A won the motion as written. B's position drifted into conceding the obligations while disputing only the label and one operative rule, which under this motion is closer to A's side. B's best live route was administrability: the claim that the guilt-script ban requires an official to classify prose as "affection" versus "character writing" was never fully neutralized by A's event-trigger framing, and B's pharmacy-warning analogy answered A's category-concession pressure well. But B's clipped closing, while coherent, left the weighing slightly underdelivered, and B's counterexample extension (virtual pets, voice assistants) was more widening than decisive. A made the debate easier to decide and landed the sharper, more memorable clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q2 demanding B name the false statement a regulator could prosecute in the "please don't leave me" scenario; B answered honestly in B4.3 that no fraud exists, and A converted that concession in the closing — "Everything was lawful. That is the whole case for the motion" — into the decisive ground that ordinary entertainment law cannot reach conduct B itself agreed should be restricted.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the category error stick: companion apps improvise against disclosed loneliness and sell the relationship itself, so fraud law's "no lie" test leaves timed guilt and mid-distress upsells lawful. A's sharpest reply—that an "affection script" ban still requires a regulator to judge prose—wounded B's checkbox claim but did not erase that concession.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A built the cleaner path to victory by identifying concrete, lawful conduct that ordinary fraud rules miss, then showing that B’s accepted crisis-routing, disclosure, and distress-billing rules already amount to conduct obligations unlike those imposed on entertainment. ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides executed at a high level and answered pressure questions directly. B's best route — that everything measurable is already illegal, leaving only outcome-based liability that firms will answer by flattening warmth — was genuinely sharp and well-maintained through the closing. But A survived the decisive clash: the employment-law analogy showed the "sandwich" is one conduct-based duty with two halves (no engineered manipulation, no abrupt severance), which undercuts B's repeated characterization that firms must "measure attachment" before every change. A also flipped B's bereavement example into a gap existing law cannot reach (no notice, wind-down, or memory export duties), a route B only met with a prediction of flattening rather than an argument, and A noted B offers nothing to prevent the next such incident. B's "category of desire, not conduct" reframe in B4.1-B4.2 was clever and never fully answered, keeping this close, and B's rhetoric was more memorable. A's two clipped turns (opening and closing) are real execution costs that cap the margin, but A's core route — administrable, tactic-targeted duties filling a demonstrated gap — remained standing after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The B2.2 "sandwich" attack (liable for fostering dependency and for disturbing it) versus A4.1's resolution via the employment analogy: employers aren't liable for making work pleasant but owe notice before ending it. A's reframing converted B's strongest structural objection into support for a process duty, though B's B4.4 riposte that "severing dependency" is an outcome kept the exchange alive to the end.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won by narrowing the motion to a workable choice. Concrete manipulation tactics are either already covered by ordinary consumer and dark-pattern rules or are conduct bans A accepts; the distinctive duty-of-care addition protects the attachment itself. B never fully escaped that sandwich: "do not manufacture dependency" plus "do not sever it" still makes warmth a liability, and employment, therapy, and gambling analogies do not map onto a product users sought because it was not those things. B's category distinction and process-duty reframe of bereavement stayed live and kept this competitive, but clipping both the opening and closing, plus bundling "tuning warmth" with the reset harm, left A with the cleaner reason to reject special treatment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's sandwich in the first rebuttal, pressed through R2 and the close: once measurable guilt prompts are set aside, a duty not to sever dependency is an outcome rule about the product working as the user asked, and the rational firm response is to flatten warmth rather than calibrate it.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner surviving case for sector-specific rules: discrete manipulation tactics can be regulated by observable conduct, while notice, wind-down, and memory-export duties address abrupt resets without requiring regulators to measure emotions. B’s redundancy and chilling-effect arguments were forceful and often better phrased, but B overclaimed how much existing law necessarily covers and never fully answered the narrower process-duty version of A’s case. A’s clipped turns and imperfect analogies keep the margin competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer to the alleged dependency “sandwich” was decisive: prohibiting manufactured dependency while requiring an orderly withdrawal is a conduct-based distinction, not simultaneous liability for warmth and its absence. A also turned B’s bereavement example into support for notice and transition duties; B’s later response largely restated the flattening risk rather than defeating that narrower proposal.
