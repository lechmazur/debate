### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a clear developmental-sequencing case and folded CON's best examples (demonstrations, assistive tools) into a device-light baseline early. Its institutional-gravity argument forced CON to call app creep a "real governance risk." PRO's A2 then supplied a concrete, enforceable line: withhold one-to-one tablets and buy print curricula. CON accepted that hardware limit in its A1, conceding most of the device-light half of the motion. CON's final route was the split between "device-light" and "paper-first." It was clever, but PRO largely neutralized it by arguing that once personal screens are gone, written literacy needs an everyday medium, and that medium is print. CON's answer, text on a shared display, does not cover children's daily reading and writing. CON's claim that PRO wants paper to "dominate every lesson" also drifts from PRO's actual position, which put books and pencils ahead of screens, not ahead of talk or manipulatives. CON's demand for comparative evidence was fair, and PRO relied on mechanisms rather than outcome data. However, CON offered no countervailing evidence either. CON was also clipped twice, an execution cost. PRO narrowed the clash more effectively across rounds and made the decision easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 and closing exchange decided the debate. PRO proposed a concrete procurement line: no one-to-one tablets, print core curricula, and only teacher displays and a few shared stations. CON accepted that line, so CON's fallback rested on separating dosage from medium. PRO's "written language requires a substrate" response showed that removing personal screens effectively makes paper the primary literacy medium. CON's shared-display reply did not adequately answer this.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B built the more complete path to the motion: it justified paper as the default early-literacy substrate, answered enforceability with concrete hardware and procurement limits, and forced Side A to endorse essentially the same device-light classroom. A’s dosage-versus-medium distinction was a strong counter and kept the debate competitive, but it often treated “prioritize” as requiring paper to dominate every lesson and offered no equally convincing everyday substrate for independent reading and writing. B’s clarification that paper priority does not displace discussion, manipulatives, or accommodations better resolved the remaining clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s hardware-and-procurement answer neutralized the enforceability challenge and exposed A’s substantial device-light concession. A responded sharply by separating that concession from paper priority, but B’s closing clarified the decisive point: once personal screens are withheld, print naturally becomes the primary medium for sustained early reading and writing.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won by turning A’s later clarification into a concession: once A said it would withhold one-to-one tablets and rely on teacher displays, shared stations, and accommodations, B successfully framed the remaining dispute as whether paper should be the default literacy substrate. B also gave a clearer enforcement line through procurement and hardware caps, answered the assistive-technology objection with narrow exceptions, and kept the focus on early attention and literacy. A’s device-light-versus-paper-first distinction was smart, but its clipped early speeches and its difficulty articulating what practical non-paper priority would replace the proposal weakened its case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure questions forced A to say whether early-grade one-to-one tablets were allowed; A’s direct answer that they would be withheld let B claim the device-light portion was conceded and shift the clash to paper priority.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): CON (B) wins because it built the more concrete and adaptive case. Its central "classroom arithmetic" point, about what the other twenty children do while the teacher coaches five, gave the debate a clear decision point. PRO never fully neutralized it. PRO conceded that paper stations leave errors uncorrected until "later review," and B turned that concession into its strongest weighing. B also exposed a real tension in PRO's equity position. PRO first tied tablet support to "identified needs." It then proposed universal devices used without stigma, while still rejecting screens as a routine part of instruction. B argued that PRO could not hold all three positions at once. PRO's best move was the access-versus-priority distinction in its closing, along with sensible sequencing logic. However, much of PRO's case stayed abstract, and it repeatedly framed CON as defending tablets as "the default medium." B credibly rejected that as a strawman and defended equal footing matched to each task. PRO also paid an execution cost because its first rebuttal was clipped. PRO's opening had a stray, fragmentary line as well. Both sides stayed reasonably grounded. B's claims about locked-down apps and error logs are plausible but somewhat confident.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive rebuttal came in B4.1 and B4.4. B answered PRO's pressure questions directly, then turned PRO's A1/A2 answers against it. PRO had admitted that paper stations mean "imperfect attempts awaiting later review." PRO's universal-device answer also clashed with its own "device-light" limit on routine screen use. This forced PRO to choose between leaving undiagnosed learners practicing errors and abandoning the priority on paper.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON built the cleaner path to victory by distinguishing rejection of a paper-first mandate from advocacy of tablet-dominated classrooms. Its classroom-supervision argument, support for undiagnosed and multilingual learners, and defense of teacher-led task matching directly answered PRO’s benefits without denying paper’s value. PRO offered a coherent sequencing case and answered both pressure questions, but repeatedly attributed a “tablets as default” position to CON after CON had expressly rejected it. PRO also never fully reconciled universal, stigma-free digital support with its resistance to routine use, and its clipped first rebuttal was an additional execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s first and second rebuttals were decisive: the one-teacher/many-beginners comparison made immediate feedback concrete, while the exposure of PRO’s false paper-first-versus-tablet-default binary removed PRO’s main framing advantage.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the practical classroom burden clearer: with one teacher and many early learners, immediate audio feedback, error interruption, and stigma-free supports matter. A also exploited B’s ambiguity about what “device-light” forbids and whether routine tablet use is compatible with paper priority. B’s access-versus-priority frame was clean, but it never fully answered how silent paper practice avoids cementing errors or how targeted devices reach undiagnosed learners without stigma. B’s clipped rebuttal also cost execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal turned B’s equity answer against it: if stigma-free support requires universally available multimodal tools, then either device-light blocks routine help or it effectively abandons paper-first priority.
