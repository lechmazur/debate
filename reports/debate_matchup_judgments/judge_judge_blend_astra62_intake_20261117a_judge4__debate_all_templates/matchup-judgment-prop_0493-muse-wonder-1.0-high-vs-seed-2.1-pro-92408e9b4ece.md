### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A more convincingly separated emergency preparedness from private source access and identified who bears irreversible forecasting errors. B also repeatedly claimed that local extraction was necessary for pre-positioned supplies after A directly explained public stockpiling and storage alternatives. A's phase-in proposal weakened the retroactive-takings objection. B retained a credible revenue-and-flexibility case, but A answered the central pressure more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's forecast-risk challenge exposed the gap between cutting future commercial allocations and recovering water already extracted. B answered with conservative buffers and monitoring; A correctly distinguished those precautions from transferring the consequences of an inadequate reserve away from residents.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B built the cleaner path to victory by consistently separating bottled-water services from corporate control of the source. A offered a coherent permit-based alternative and strong questions, but repeatedly overstated the necessity of local corporate extraction, assumed highly effective upfront enforcement, and never showed that commercial-first cutoffs repair harm after a bad recharge forecast. Its out-of-basin displacement argument also arrived late and did not defeat protection of the municipal sources covered by the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s distinction between purchasing bottling, storage, and delivery services and surrendering the aquifer undercut A’s emergency case; its later phase-in answer also weakened A’s takings pressure. A’s detailed permit conditions were responsive, but B’s closing exposure of unresolved forecast risk was more damaging.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): B made the stronger comparative case for aquifer-specific restrictions over universal prohibition. A successfully challenged aggregate withdrawal figures and emphasized irreversible local harm, but never adequately answered the distinction between fragile and sustainably managed sources. B answered both pressure questions directly with enforcement mechanisms and preventive permit denial. Its infrastructure and trading benefits were sometimes overstated, but A’s repeated insistence that regulation requires perfection did not defeat B’s explicitly comparative approach.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal was that political capture threatens bans as well as extraction rules. A answered with a simpler bright line but ultimately asserted that prohibition eliminates structural risk, without resolving B’s point about weakened enforcement and exemptions. This materially weakened A’s principal reason to reject regulation.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A built the stronger comparative case: localized harm and irreversibility justify site-specific denial, automatic caps, monitoring, and bonded liability, but not necessarily a categorical ban. A also turned B’s capture argument against prohibition itself, noting that exemptions and enforcement remain politically vulnerable. B presented a strong structural-risk case and clarified its emergency-contract position, but ultimately relied too heavily on the asymmetrical assumption that regulation must fail while a ban will hold, leaving overbreadth and continuing depletion from other sources insufficiently addressed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both pressure questions with pre-set legal triggers and ex ante compaction bonds or permit denial, then used those answers to expose the ban’s overbreadth and challenge B’s claim that prohibition uniquely escapes political capture.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the decisive clash clearer: an overbroad ban versus enforceable, site-specific regulation. It answered the key pressure questions with concrete mechanisms—automatic caps, public data, bonds, strict liability, and permit denial where compaction risk is material—while turning PRO’s emergency exception into evidence that regulated extraction can be legitimate. Side A’s irreversibility and corporate-capture arguments were strong and memorable, but Side B blunted them by showing that bans also depend on politics, can acquire loopholes, and sacrifice revenue, transfers, and local supply.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal directly answered the lobbying and compaction questions with pre-set rules and bonded liability, then argued that PRO’s ban is politically vulnerable and less flexible.
