### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B established a recurring non-currency advantage: shared ordering and open participation without unilateral operator control. A's member-owned utility was a serious alternative, but A did not adequately resolve B's exclusion and hold-up objections. B also answered the fork pressure and correctly distinguished applications sharing one chain from interoperability between separate chains. A's currency-boundary concession widened B's live case, while its closing repeated an insufficiently defended equation between external legal enforcement and blockchain redundancy. B overstated how automatically these advantages defeat “nearly every,” keeping the margin competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest rebuttal distinguished authority over one's own records from root authority over everyone: signed corrections and court remedies can remain without a unilateral reversal switch. This directly undermined A's closing claim that removing the administrator necessarily removes accountability and correction.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B won by replacing the rival-server caricature with a chartered, answerable utility and then showing that expensive write-ordering without a remedy does not beat that incumbent for nearly every non-currency use. Side A kept real pressure on hold-up, borders, and open access, but treated a definitional concession as a collapse of "nearly every," and its fork answer did not restore the exit rights it had claimed. B's closing test—who can be made to fix a wrong—stayed live because A's own remedy still sat with courts, issuers, and statutes rather than consensus.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer that replication under one suable authority is not the Byzantine consortium it had rejected, capped by the close that statutory recognition is the charter's magic, not the chain's.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B more convincingly showed that ordering writes is the easy part of coordination, while correction, entitlement, and enforcement require answerable authority. Its neutral-utility alternative answered the root-access objection, its authority-versus-replication distinction neutralized Side A's consortium gotcha, and granting tickets and shares did not concede that chains beat chartered registries for them. Side A identified real openness and hold-up concerns, but did not establish that permissionless consensus is the binding constraint across nearly every proposed application.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: centralized meant one accountable authority rather than one machine, and legal authority behind registered transfers could just as readily designate a member-owned database, undercutting Side A's claim that atomic digital-native settlement proves blockchain superiority.
