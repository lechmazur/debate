### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by isolating the motion's two distinctive costs: a mandatory on-request trigger and open-ended judicial balancing of a river's needs. A largely conceded that guardians, standing, and restoration funding could be achieved by ordinary statutes, so the debate turned on those unique features. B showed they force one community's request to restructure governance across a shared basin, then push flood defenses and grid projects into endless litigation. A's proportionality answer made the delay problem worse rather than resolving it. A also had minor nonexistent reference slips, but B's structural comparison was the decisive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it took A's admission that personhood conflicts would be resolved by courts applying proportionality and turned that into open-ended litigation over what a river "needs," while separating personhood's mandatory trigger from the bounded statutory protections B had already conceded could work.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON isolated the motion's unique costs: a mandatory request trigger imposed basin-wide and open-ended judicial balancing. PRO answered the veto charge with proportionality, but that conceded CON's delay argument, while CON showed standing, guardians, restoration funds, and even holistic sediment and wetland rules could be delivered through bounded statutes without personhood. PRO's mandatory-trigger defence never resolved downstream communities being bound without requesting it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal distinguished bounded minimum-flow and consent cases from litigation over a river's open-ended rights to flow and regenerate, turning PRO's proportionality and mandatory-trigger concessions into a decisive speed and democratic-legitimacy advantage.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by narrowing the debate to the motion’s unique features: mandatory personhood on request and open-ended judicial balancing. B argued convincingly that the attractive benefits PRO emphasized could be delivered by ordinary bounded statutes, while the motion’s special triggers risk unrepresentative basin-wide control and costly delay. Side A answered the pressure questions directly, but its concessions that governments may not refuse requests and courts would balance conflicts reinforced B’s central harm. A also had some sloppy reference glitches.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal turned A’s pressure answers into a decisive comparison: bounded statutory rules produce faster, predictable cases, while personhood’s proportionality standard invites prolonged litigation over what a river needs, especially for urgent flood defenses.
