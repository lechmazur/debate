### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON (Side A) won by keeping the debate focused on what "substantial control" means at a mass grave, which is also a crime scene and evidence site. CON proposed a concrete alternative: independent, reviewable authority with enforceable duties to descendants. It pressed two burdens PRO never discharged. First, CON asked what stops a community's "final say" from filtering evidence. PRO's answer rested on good motives. It then retreated to the claim that control "does not mean a veto." CON exploited this cleanly: either PRO keeps the final say, and evidence can be filtered, or it drops the final say and effectively concedes CON's consultation model. Second, CON asked who defines membership, minority appeals, and representation for victims with no identifiable descendants. PRO deferred these questions to unspecified "transparent criteria" inside the community. CON noted these still need an outside verifier and appeal forum. CON answered PRO's pressure questions directly, giving concrete answers on mixed appointment and on refusing to invent a case. It also reframed the evidentiary burden: safeguards for crime scenes are justified before any failure occurs. PRO's strongest line was the record of state capture of memorials. CON partly neutralized it by framing its model as exposing capture rather than presuming neutral authority. PRO's assertion about Rwandan community-led commissions was loosely grounded. PRO's case leaned on asserting moral authority rather than defending it, and its clipped closing was an added execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5: CON showed that PRO's Rebuttal 2 claim that control "does not mean a veto" abandoned its earlier "final say" and "pen's orientation" language. This forced a dilemma: keeping final authority allows evidence filtering, while dropping it concedes CON's model of independent process with consultation. PRO's closing never resolved this.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won by keeping a clear, stable line through the whole debate. It separated the factual and forensic record, which stays independent and non-negotiable, from dignitary choices about bodies, images, names and framing, which belong with descendants. This answered CON's two main threats, evidence suppression and Srebrenica, without giving up the motion's core. "Substantial" control was never presented as absolute control. PRO answered both pressure questions directly. It named the adjudicator for euphemism and set out constituency and review rules for capture. It then turned the capture objection back on CON's alternative. CON (A) opened with a reasonable set of risks: internal division, forensic obstruction and elite capture. It never engaged PRO's dignity/fact distinction. It also leaned on the brittle claim that the motion "contains no" courts, which treats "substantial control" as absolute veto power. CON's second rebuttal badly self-destructed. Its A1 never identified what power in the motion blocks legally required exhumation. Its A2 conceded that overseers are appointed by ministries and museums that have whitewashed atrocities, and even said "descendant control, though imperfect, centers the injured community." It also mislabeled its own model as PRO's, and its closing repeated that mislabeling. CON's Rwanda and Bosnia examples were plausible but were asserted repeatedly rather than developed. PRO was the more coherent, responsive and decision-ready side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 and the follow-up in B4.3 asked why "shared governance with mandatory independent oversight" is less capture-prone than descendant authority. CON's A4.3 answer effectively conceded that ministry- and museum-appointed overseers have whitewashed atrocities and that descendant control centers the injured community.
