### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): A won a close, high-quality clash by keeping the motion’s narrow text decisive: “otherwise qualified” and “solely,” plus coverage of assistance beyond bureaucracy-heavy vouchers. A repeatedly forced B onto the pure case—an applicant with covered rent and no concrete administrative incompatibility—and B’s best replies (inseparability, pretext risk, no comparable twin, chilling effects) largely treated that case as fiction or converted it into enforcement costs. Those are serious practical worries, but they never fully justified preserving categorical refusal where no independent objection remains, nor did they answer A’s Q2 about assistance without voucher features. B was sharp on property and litigation realities, yet A gave the cleaner comparative reason to affirm: genuine burdens still survive the rule; open blanket exclusion does not.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first-rebuttal scissors (genuine incompatibilities already survive; CON cannot save categorical refusal by citing them) and A’s later A1 burden/consistency answer, which undercut B’s “solely is a trap / prove a negative” pressure without erasing B’s cost concern.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's narrowing words live — otherwise qualified and solely — and won the overbreadth clash. CON's strongest point about inspections and third-party risk explains some voucher cases but cannot justify categorical rejection of all lawful assistance, especially non-voucher aid with no such features. A answered litigation-cost pressure by showing ordinary pretext law handles neutral reasons, while B never solved the decisive case: a fully covered, otherwise qualified applicant rejected with no concrete incompatibility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 dismantling of the "different contract" claim — showing assistance need not change counterparty and that CON's burden arguments fail for 'other lawful public assistance' — left CON defending blanket exclusion beyond demonstrated necessity.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully arguing that the practical enforcement of the word "solely" creates an unworkable litigation trap for landlords, as the bureaucratic terms of a voucher are inseparable from the voucher itself. Side A's point that there is no "comparable arrangement" to test consistency against effectively neutralized Side B's main defense, making Side A's consequentialist warnings about risk-avoidance and market exit more persuasive than Side B's theoretical text-based arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1), where they dismantled Side B's "consistent application" defense by pointing out that a voucher is a sui generis contract with no non-assistance twin for comparison, rendering the consistency check practically empty.
