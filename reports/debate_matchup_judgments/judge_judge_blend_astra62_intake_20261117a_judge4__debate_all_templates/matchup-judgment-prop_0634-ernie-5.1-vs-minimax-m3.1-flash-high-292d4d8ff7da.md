### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won by keeping the decisive clash on burden allocation: a consenting somatic patient versus unconsenting future descendants. PRO showed a targeted reproductive ban leaves research and oversight legal, so CON's strongest claim that prohibition drives the science underground became less decisive. CON's embryo-selection pressure was sharp, but PRO answered it with a meaningful natural-vs-novel genome distinction and by accepting that current regulation may be inconsistent without justifying an unvalidated heritable intervention. CON's regulation alternative never explained why first-in-human and multigenerational risk should fall on children rather than requiring proof first.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answers were the turning point: it turned embryo selection into a distinction between choosing an existing genome and introducing a new unvalidated sequence, and it neutralized the underground argument by noting that no law prevents every breach but permission is not therefore justified.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A provides the stronger comparative case: research can remain supervised while reproductive use is prohibited, and lawful embryo selection does not establish the safety of editing. B repeatedly asserts that prohibition necessarily eliminates oversight without demonstrating that clinic-level enforcement cannot reduce use. Its inheritance claims and equation of selection with genomic alteration also weaken its grounding. A's largely self-directed pressure questions, overstatement that descendants receive no benefit, and incomplete response concerning families without selection options limit the margin, but B never adequately resolves the safety burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest response distinguishes imperfect enforcement from ineffective enforcement: some illicit activity does not establish that permitting reproductive editing improves safety. Combined with continued lawful research, this directly undermines B's central claim that a ban necessarily replaces accountable science with secrecy.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because it kept the motion's actual choice—ban reproductive germline use while leaving research and somatic care available—clearer than Side A's underground-and-families case. B's live route held: nonconsenting descendants should not bear unvalidated, lineage-wide risk, and illegal use elsewhere is not a reason to authorize it here. A's selection-equals-editing argument and the claim that lab creation is indistinguishable from implantation were category errors that B answered directly and that A never repaired. A's pressure on the evidentiary bar exposed a real tension, but B still gave the cleaner vote by allocating uncertainty away from the first children.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second-rebuttal distinction that embryo selection chooses among naturally occurring genomes while editing introduces a new, unvalidated sequence, paired with the point that evasion abroad does not justify local permission to experiment on descendants.
