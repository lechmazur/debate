### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO won because it turned CON's own safeguards into evidence for the cap. CON conceded that law must block mostly offset-based claims and confine offsets to genuinely residual emissions, which is close to the proposition CON was opposing. PRO then exposed the gap: without a legal cap or quantitative boundary, "feasible internal abatement first" rewards delay and lets heavy emitters treat unabated process emissions as residual. PRO also answered the hardest pressure directly: unabated cement cannot honestly claim net-zero today, and value-chain methane capture is not made cosmetic because the firm's own smokestack still has to be cleaned up. CON's atmospheric-equivalence argument was sharp but depended on verifiable additionality and permanence at scale, and PRO showed why that reliance is exactly the problem a cap addresses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer on value-chain methane arithmetic was decisive: capturing methane at a supplier reduces a 200-ton footprint to 100, while buying an identical external offset leaves the buyer's 100 smokestack tons untouched but still earns the same net-zero badge. It neutralized CON's best "contract boundary" attack and connected it to CON's concession that mostly offset claims should be blocked.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): A better distinguished eligibility for a net-zero claim from the value of funding external mitigation, and directly answered the cement and supplier-boundary questions. B’s cost-effectiveness argument remained significant, especially for durable removals, but its counterfactual accounting did not adequately answer the distinction between avoiding emissions and balancing ongoing emissions. A also exposed an unresolved inconsistency in B’s proposed alternative, making its cap the more coherent claims standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing turned B’s pressure answer against it: B said its rules block mostly offset-based claims, yet defended net-zero eligibility for largely unabated cement producers. Calling their emissions currently “residual” did not reconcile those positions or answer A’s concern that feasibility-based exemptions reward delayed investment.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive clash about what a net-zero claim may honestly mean: ongoing fossil emissions cannot be mostly cancelled by external avoidance credits. B answered pressure questions directly, gave a clear value-chain accounting example, and exposed vagueness in A’s non-quota residual rule. A’s cost-effectiveness and identical-tons arguments were strong, but they never fully removed the burden that unlimited offsets can let firms keep emitting while claiming net-zero.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 answer to the supplier-methane question was decisive: it distinguished in-value-chain capture that lowers a firm’s footprint from external offsets that let a firm declare zero while its own smokestack emissions remain, blunting A’s boundary-arbitrariness attack.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A controlled the decisive turn: B’s own molecule test, once external tons are verifiably real, cuts toward regulating credit quality and disclosure rather than a quantitative cap. B never convincingly explained why verified removals are acceptable at 10% but unusable at 50%, and its fallback that hard-to-abate firms should retreat to honest partial claims conceded A’s core harm—uncompensated emissions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s treatment of B’s partial-claim fallback was most decisive: it showed a cap deters hard-to-abate firms from funding count-eligible external mitigation, leaving the majority of their emissions unpriced and in the sky, while B merely hoped they might fund removals anyway.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B more convincingly distinguished credit integrity from credit quantity, and a voluntary claim restriction from an emissions mandate. Its disclosure alternative addressed A’s labeling concern, while corporate retreat exposed the missing link between a cap and actual internal cuts. A conceded genuine engineered removals and potentially uncompensated emissions, then largely reasserted that net-zero must mean business transformation. B’s optimistic verification assumptions limit the margin, but A never adequately justified the small-share ceiling against these objections.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s ten-percent-versus-fifty-percent challenge undermined A’s internal-molecules/external-stories distinction. When A conceded genuine removals but predicted larger offset shares would rely on dubious credits, B directly answered that its proposed quality rules would prohibit that substitution. Combined with A’s concession that firms might abandon compensation, this gave B the cleaner comparative case.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by making the decisive clash about what “net-zero” should certify: business transformation versus purchased accounting. Its pressure question forced CON to endorse 100% external mitigation as sufficient, which PRO then turned into the memorable “receipt, not transformation” argument. CON’s molecule test and lost-finance objection were strong, but its no-cap alternative was less clear, especially under the motion’s requirement that most reductions occur in-value-chain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Q1 forced Side A to defend a firm with zero internal cuts claiming net-zero through external credits; B’s closing converted that concession into the decisive claim that CON had reduced net-zero to a purchased label.
