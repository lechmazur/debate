### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): A won because it kept the comparison on the median program rather than the ideal one. PRO’s near-zero-cost rewilding case survived B’s main counterattack: B asserted that most abandoned land is seed-dead or degraded where succession stalls, but never proved that, while PRO used broad, ordinary historical examples of spontaneous reforestation. A also exposed that B’s winning comparator kept expanding into costly, well-designed mixed-species restoration without showing that most real programs look like that.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A’s rebuttal that B had substituted an ideal “well-designed ecological restoration” for the median planting program, combined with the seed-bank pressure question: if most abandoned hectares were seed-dead, the broad spontaneous reforestation of Europe and North America would not have happened.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won solidly by anchoring the debate to the motion's two decisive qualifiers: "per dollar" and "most active reforestation programs." Side B supported spontaneous regeneration with continent-scale empirical examples across both named regions (New England, Iberia, the Balkans, and post-Soviet Eastern Europe) while showing how Side A's unpriced list of silvicultural interventions inflates per-hectare costs far beyond what median planting schemes deliver. Side A mounted smart mid-debate pressure on arrested bracken succession and the intervention costs of trophic rewilding, but failed to substantiate its claim that most European and North American abandoned farmland lacks nearby seed sources, dodged whether most real-world planting budgets actually fund mixed-species stands with maintained openings, and stumbled in closing by conflating trophic rewilding with active reforestation and garbling its "retreat" attack in [A5.4].
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A exposed a real tension in [A2.5] and [A3.2]—arguing that the fencing, grazers, and beavers needed to keep rewilded mosaics fire-resilient are active line items that erase Side B's "near-zero" cost claim—Side B neutralized the attack in [B4.2] by distinguishing landscape-scale fixed costs from per-hectare recurring costs: a perimeter fence or beaver pair is paid once and amortized across a whole valley for decades, whereas seedlings, tubes, weeding, and replanting multiply with every hectare. Side A never answered that scaling math.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the debate on the motion's per-dollar, most-programs comparison: near-zero recurring costs, continent-scale spontaneous reforestation, and mosaic biodiversity. CON's collapsed-numerator argument was strong but depended on the unsupported claim that most abandoned land is seed-dead and stalled, while its winning comparator drifted toward well-designed mixed planting rather than the median program. PRO also answered the intervention-cost pressure with a credible one-time landscape-cost versus per-hectare recurring-cost distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and closing on CON's retreat: CON conceded spontaneous forests and resilient mosaics, then defended only mixed, maintained planting on disconnected parcels, shrinking its case below the motion's 'most' threshold.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): CON wins because PRO’s core efficiency case depended on near-zero-cost succession, but CON exposed that where succession works the gains are not attributable to the program, and where succession stalls PRO’s own remedies—seed trees, exclosures, invasive control—are costly active interventions. PRO then conceded a bad site may still be scrub at year twenty, which CON turned into a decisive dilemma: easy sites earn no per-dollar return, hard sites cost enough and still underperform planting. CON also answered the deadwood/soil complexity point by explaining that planted mixed native stands self-thin and produce those pools sooner once canopy closes. PRO’s portfolio reframe was rhetorically sharp but did not overcome the attribution and stalled-site problems.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s strongest exchange was exposing that PRO’s “low-cost nudges” reintroduce real line items and that rewilding becomes either free and non-additional or reliable but no longer cheap; this was capped by using PRO’s own admission that a bad site may still be scrub at year twenty.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins solidly by locking Side B (PRO) into a structural additionality dilemma and winning nearly every downstream clash. On sites where succession works naturally, Side A convincingly argued that merely monitoring gratis regrowth generates zero attributable return per dollar, and Side B never established how near-zero spend causes additional protection rather than counting the baseline. On stalled sites, Side A forced Side B to shift from "free" succession to active interventions (cutting invasives, seed trees, exclosures, grazing) and extracted a damaging concession that bad sites may still be scrub at year twenty—while showing that mixed native planting closes canopy sooner and begins generating litter, roots, and self-thinning deadwood decades earlier. Side A also won the European meadow biodiversity clash outright after Side B mischaracterized where abandonment occurs and dropped the point after Turn 3.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's multi-turn rebuttal sequence ([A2.1-A2.2], [A3.1-A3.2], [A4.2-A4.6]) tracking Side B's retreat from "for free" succession to paid interventions (invasive cutting, seed trees, temporary deer exclosures), using Pressure Questions to force Side B's honest admission that "by year twenty a bad site may still be scrub" [B4.3], and exposing the implausibility of covering deer exclosures for "low tens of dollars per hectare."
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive accounting and comparison clash: rewilding is either free but unattributable and unreliable, or assisted, costly, and slower. PRO's portfolio answer helped, but its concession that stalled sites may still be scrub at year twenty, plus an implausibly cheap exclosure cost, left active mixed planting with the cleaner per-dollar payoff where money actually changes outcomes. CON also sustained the European meadow biodiversity reversal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, turned PRO's year-twenty scrub concession against the carbon claim, and argued deadwood, roots, and soil carbon follow sooner once planted trees actually exist.
