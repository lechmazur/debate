### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won clearly on the central economic and institutional clashes, even after accounting for a real execution cost from getting clipped at the end of three speeches. Side A also effectively showed that replacing tips with a house-administered service-charge pool trades workers' direct legal claim on tips for management-written formulas and manager-evaluated bonuses, while trapping Side B on why individual restaurants adopting the model lose top staff and diners unless competitors are compelled to follow.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal on marginal labor costs and shift cutting ([A2.8]-[A2.9], pressed again in [A4.2] and [A5.1]-[A5.2]): answering Side B's claim that shift cuts are independent of wage structure ([B2.2]), Side A showed that an extra tipped server costs the house little while a high base-wage hour in an empty room is the first expense cut—neutralizing Side B's core "dead Tuesday" advantage, which Side B left unanswered through the closing.

PRO’s retreat to industry rules and extra-motion contracts did not answer unilateral feasibility. Clipping hurt CON’s execution, but the visible clash still favors rejecting the switch.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal: it answered the Tuesday question honestly, then showed a service charge is equal at best on a dead night, worse on Saturday, and PRO’s contractual pool is still the management-controlled horn with hours that shrink when sales do.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): CON won a clear but competitive debate by shifting the comparison from tipping’s genuine flaws to whether the replacement reliably improves workers’ outcomes. However, A’s responses about lost hours, management control, reduced Saturday upside, and the need for industry-wide coordination left B’s practical worker-welfare advantage substantially asserted rather than demonstrated. A’s repeated clipping and B’s sharp pressure questions keep the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal conceded Tuesday revenue equivalence but distinguished shift retention from meaningful pay, then exposed the remaining slow-week revenue, hours, and management-control problems in B’s pooled-wage design.
