PRO's post-birth registry handed CON an admission it used repeatedly: if proof comes after exposure, safety was not established beforehand. PRO's replies leaned on brittle, unsupported precision: - off-target rates "below one in ten million"; - "decades" of CRISPR work; - primate data showing normal development "across three generations"; - computational models delivering an "evidence-based guarantee." These read as fake authority rather than grounded argument. PRO's Rebuttal 1 was also clipped for length, and its closing mostly restated the pipeline rather than answering CON's proxy-versus-outcome critique. CON also did the rest of the job well: - It distinguished the analogies cleanly: PGD selects rather than rewrites a lineage, and mitochondrial replacement does not license nuclear edits. - It reversed the equity argument, since editing requires IVF plus specialist labs and lifelong tracking. - It answered both pressure questions directly. - It weighed asymmetric errors clearly: bounded, reducible burdens on willing parents versus irreversible harms inherited by people who could not consent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3 turned PRO's A1 answer against it. PRO had said computational models "extrapolate" to human outcomes yet called the result a "guarantee," and its own registry admits proof arrives only after exposure. CON showed that this concedes a proxy is standing in for the missing human multigenerational outcome, which hollowed out PRO's central claim that the threshold can be met in advance.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-OSS-120B (CON)): PRO built a coherent case for conditional permission. Its core points were: a regulated international floor beats exporting demand to unregulated clinics, and alternatives fail in the case where every embryo would be affected. It also neutralized consent by noting that no child consents to an inherited genome, so expected welfare is the right test. Its pressure questions forced CON into two damaging admissions. First, that only absolute certainty would ever suffice, which effectively rejects the motion's own condition. Second, that none of the alternatives yields a genetically related, disease-free child. PRO then turned heritability into a benefit, since a correction also propagates to descendants. It weighed the harms preserved by a ban against residual risk. CON relied heavily on the precautionary principle and a zero-risk standard. It also repeated slippery-slope and inequality claims without answering PRO's point about tight drafting. Its closing contradicted its own A2 concession by citing alternatives again. CON was also clipped in three turns, which cost it real execution. It left some empty reference placeholders and used vague appeals to history.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1/Q2 and its A1/A2 follow-up decided the debate. Once CON answered that no threshold short of absolute certainty could ever suffice, PRO showed that CON was vetoing the proposition's condition rather than contesting it. Once CON admitted that no alternative helps when every embryo is affected, PRO's welfare comparison went effectively uncontested.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-OSS-120B (CON)): Side A won by making the motion's own condition decisive. The proposition permits editing "once safety thresholds are met," and Side B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1) flatly conceded that no threshold short of absolute certainty could ever suffice, plus admitted (B4.3) that none of its alternatives delivers a genetically related disease-free child. Side A exploited both concessions directly in its closing, correctly framing B's position as a veto against the proposition as written rather than a refutation of it. A also executed the format cleanly (proper Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 labels, direct answers first), progressed across rounds, and introduced effective weighing like "heritability cuts both ways" and the ban-exports-risk-to-clandestine-clinics point. B was repetitive across all five speeches (precaution, consent, slippery slope restated with little adaptation), had three turns clipped for exceeding word limits, and left a bare dangling reference ([B2.4] was just "[A2.3]" with no content). B's reliance on "history shows" expansion was asserted, not supported. A's grounding was disciplined: plausible examples (Tay-Sachs, Huntington's, PGD limits) without fake sourcing; B leaned on overconfident absolutes ("science cannot ever set a threshold") presented as certainty.
