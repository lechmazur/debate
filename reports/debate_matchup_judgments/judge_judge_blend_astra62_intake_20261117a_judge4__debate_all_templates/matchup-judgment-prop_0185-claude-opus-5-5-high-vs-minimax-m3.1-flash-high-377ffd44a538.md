  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's decisive exchange was its pressure question and A1: a statutory supermajority requirement can be repealed by a simple majority, so CON's alternative binds only until it binds. CON's admission that the statutory model was weaker made that rebuttal stick.

Once Side A conceded pure discretion fails and advocated statutory triggers, correction accounts, and supermajority rules, Side B exposed that ordinary statutes cannot legally entrench supermajorities against simple-majority repeal and historically get rewritten whenever they bind, as with Gramm-Rudman and PAYGO waivers. ### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by turning Pro's own design into a liability: emergency exceptions still require certification or a recorded supermajority, creating minority veto points in the hardest crises. A also defused Pro's central stabilizer argument by showing automatic stabilizers already operate under legislative discretion, so the exception only prevents the rule from doing harm. B's durability argument was substantially weakened by A's point that entrenchment prevents honest revision without stopping off-budget evasion. Pro offered a coherent fairness and deficit-bias case, but A made the comparative tradeoff clearer and answered the key pressure more decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's best exchange was its response to B's pressure answer: B introduced council certification plus a supermajority override, and A turned that into a March 2020 minority-veto scenario, then closed by arguing the easy case needed no constitution and the hard case was made worse by entrenchment.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won every phase of the debate through sharper comparative framing, stronger real-world grounding, and superior risk weighing. Side A forced Side B to concede that ordinary statutes can supply every safeguard in its design (audits, sunsets, capital carve-outs) except protection against simple-majority revision, and then showed that blocking open revision encourages opaque fiscal relabelling while risking paralysis in unscripted crises like March 2020 or rapid rearmament. Side B also committed an unforced error in Pressure Question Q2 by accidentally arguing CON's position against judicial control, and its fallback emergency mechanism (fiscal council certification or a legislative supermajority override) allowed Side A to show how a partisan minority could hold urgent crisis relief hostage. Side A sealed the decision in closing by showing that automatic stabilizers already operate without a constitution, making entrenchment redundant in routine downturns and dangerous in severe shocks, whereas peacetime deficit bias is slow, visible, and reversible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B2.1]-[B2.3], Side B tried to answer Side A's unscripted-crisis critique by expanding its promised "narrow triggers" into broad constitutional emergency clauses covering wars, disasters, and lagging data. In [A2.1]-[A2.5], Side A turned that expansion into a decisive dilemma—narrow triggers fail in novel shocks, while broad triggers hand operational fiscal judgment to courts—and dismantled Side B's reading of Germany by pointing out that Germany's debt brake already contained an emergency clause and judicial enforcement of its limits is precisely what triggered the budget crisis.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Con wins the central dilemma: narrow exceptions miss unlabelled shocks, while broad exceptions recreate discretion under councils, courts, or supermajorities. Pro concedes statutes can supply the safeguards, leaving entrenchment as durability against correction and honest revision. Con's final weighing is decisive: automatic stabilizers already operate under discretion, so the rule adds nothing in easy recessions and risks blocking the simple-majority response needed in severe crises, whereas deficit bias is slower and reversible.
