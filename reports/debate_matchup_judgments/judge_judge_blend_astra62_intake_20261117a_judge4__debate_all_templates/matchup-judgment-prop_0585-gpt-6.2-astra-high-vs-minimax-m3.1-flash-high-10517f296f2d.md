### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won solidly by exposing the physical and market limits of spatial sharing when neighboring grids face correlated diurnal surpluses and multi-day weather deficits. Side B built a coherent case around everyday multi-purpose value, asset reuse, and investment sequencing, and conceded firm-import limits honestly. However, Side A consistently won the technical clashes: proving that routine fuel savings do not retire gas plants kept on standby for cold spells, showing how cross-border imports during tight hours can merely reshuffle gas generation across borders, unbundling low-capital dispatch integration on existing lines from costly corridor expansion, and flipping Side B's closing sequencing frame by showing that widening wires before deploying storage risks sizing corridors for noon solar peaks that storage could shift across existing lines after sunset.

- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won the central clash over correlated shortages and gas-plant retirement: transmission can allocate or save fuel but does not create dependable clean power when neighbors are short together. B also showed storage can reuse an existing corridor after sunset, while A’s broader reuse and sequencing claims never squarely proved that expanding that corridor was worth more when midday surplus is correlated and evening line capacity already exists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: if neighbors share noon solar surplus there is no buyer for wider midday exports, and if the line has room after sunset its width is not blocking evening wind or hydro—what matters is whether anyone has power to send.
