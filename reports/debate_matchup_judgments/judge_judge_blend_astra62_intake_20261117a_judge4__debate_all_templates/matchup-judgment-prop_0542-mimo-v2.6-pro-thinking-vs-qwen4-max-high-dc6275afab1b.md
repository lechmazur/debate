### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): CON wins by isolating what the motion uniquely requires: more detention, not merely the use of risk tools. PRO's best material compared structured scores with judicial instinct. That argument was strong on substituting scores for instinct. CON granted it and showed it does not reach expansion, since equal accuracy means each extra detention buys the same uncertain guess at a certain cost. CON reinforced this point with three further arguments: - **Selection bias:** validation data comes only from people the system already released, so the tools are untested on the newly detained group. - **Cohort inference:** counsel can challenge a miscounted prior, but cannot challenge a prediction drawn from people who resemble the defendant. - **Correctability:** a released defendant who threatens someone can be rearrested, while time in jail and a coerced plea cannot be undone. PRO's answers to the pressure questions hurt it. It accepted a flat cap on detention and called that a gain, which let CON argue that every benefit PRO claimed survives without the motion. PRO's justification for the extra detention ("people who go on to commit murder") was persuasively labeled as assuming the disputed premise. PRO's double-standard framing and its claim that individualized proof is a mirage were rhetorically effective. CON's closing still answered the mirage claim cleanly: individualized proof means evidence about this defendant, not certainty about the future. Both sides avoided fabricated precision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.6, answering PRO's acceptance of a flat cap: "Everything they praise... survives without this motion. What the motion adds, uniquely, is more people in cells." Together with the earlier point in A2.1–A2.2 that substitution and expansion are different votes, this reframed the debate around the increment. PRO never adequately justified that increment.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won on the sharpest and most motion-true clash: the substitution vs. expansion distinction. CON consistently argued that PRO's advertised benefits — measurement, contestability, auditability — justify swapping a hunch for a score, not jailing more people, and PRO's own flat-cap concession in Rebuttal 2 ("a gain for my position") handed CON the decisive weapon, exploited fully in A4.6 and the closing. PRO never squarely answered the selection-effect argument that tools are untested on the marginal population the expansion newly sweeps in, and PRO's limiting principle ("catastrophic harm") was effectively exposed as the reason there is no limit. PRO's best material — measured error vs. hidden error, and the closing reframe that individualized proof is impossible for any method — was real, but it mostly defends the substitution half of the case, and CON's burden-of-proof and correctability asymmetry (release is revocable, detention is not) held. Pressure questions from A forced the costly concession; A's A1/A2 answers were direct and clean. Both sides were disciplined, concrete, and free of fake sourcing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.6 turn: PRO endorsed a flat detention cap and called it a gain, and CON converted that concession into the core voting issue — every virtue PRO advertised arrives without the motion, so the only thing the proposition adds is more people in cells, justified by restating the disputed premise.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): A won by keeping the debate on the motion’s actual increment: broader detention, not merely replacing judicial intuition with auditable tools. A’s false-positive, marginal-validation, and detention-harm arguments left B needing specific benefits from expansion. B’s measured-error-versus-hidden-error frame was strong, and B directly answered both pressure questions, but the flat-cap answer made substitution’s benefits available without expansion while the added benefit remained asserted rather than demonstrated. A also answered pressure more cleanly through the state’s burden and the asymmetry between revocable release and irreversible detention. B’s closing weakened its position by recasting individualized evidence as an impossible demand for certainty and treating A’s arguendo accuracy concession as an outright grant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s substitution-versus-expansion distinction in Rebuttal 1, sharpened in Rebuttal 2 after B accepted a flat cap: measurement and contestability could justify better tool use without justifying additional cells, while “catastrophic harm” supplied no concrete limiting principle for the increment.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) wins by keeping the comparison tight. Its frame was "calibrated prediction versus guesswork," not "prediction versus freedom," and CON never fully escaped it. CON (A) opened strongly. The base-rate point, biased inputs, anchoring and laundering, and the net-widening clause all landed. CON also scored a real hit by exposing the one-way appellate ratchet. PRO handled pressure honestly and directly: - It owned "net-widening as written" without retreating to a substitution motion. - It conceded the ratchet and repaired it with bidirectional review. - It turned that repair into an argument: review against a low score requires a score to exist. CON's A1 answer weakened its own position. It claimed hearings can audit which detained defendants reoffended, which is not possible because detained defendants cannot reoffend. PRO exploited this cleanly in its closing. CON's closing attempted a clever turn: detention makes false positives unverifiable. But that problem applies equally to CON's discretionary hearings. CON also failed to answer the "shared ruler across courtrooms" point. CON relied heavily on repeated slogans ("measurable injustice is still injustice," "the flag is not the fact"). Its argument that the safeguards are "outside the motion" was reasonably answered by PRO's line that conditions are part of what allowing a tool means. Two clipped turns add an execution cost for CON. CON's liberty and base-rate concerns remain live, but PRO gave the clearer reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing response to CON's A1 claim decided the measurement clash. CON had said hearings already let courts "audit which detained defendants reoffended." PRO answered that detention erases the outcome it would be checked against, so CON's false positives are uncountable without a common scale. That left CON's "harms you can count" frame without support.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B (PRO) won a close, high-level debate. B's core reframe — every pretrial system already predicts; the choice is calibrated instruments versus unreviewable intuition — survived the whole exchange and forced A to argue legibility-versus-justification rather than prediction-versus-freedom. B handled pressure honestly: conceded the base-rate problem, conceded and dropped the one-way appellate ratchet A exposed, then turned that concession into an argument (bidirectional review requires a score to depart from). B's closing "shared ruler" point — that detained defendants cannot reoffend, so CON's proposed audit of dangerousness hearings is structurally impossible — sharply neutralized A's answer to Q1 and left CON defending an error rate it cannot count. A's strongest live route (the motion pre-authorizes net-widening, and B admitted "net-widening as written") was real, but B had pre-positioned the answer in the opening: the increase should land at the violent tail, and CON's world detains the same people chosen worse. A's closings on "legibility is not justification" were philosophically sharp but B's comparison — reversible harms versus irreversible ones, measurable mistakes versus invisible ones — did more weighing. A also incurred small execution costs from two clipped turns, while B's speeches were complete. Marginal edge to B on concessions handled, pressure questions answered, and decisive clash crystallized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2: answering A's one-way ratchet exposure with "You caught a real asymmetry, so I'll drop it," extending review in both directions, and then flipping it — a judge detaining on instinct has no score to depart from, so nothing triggers review at all — plus the closing demonstration that CON's proposed audit of detention outcomes is impossible because detention erases the outcome it would be checked against.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger comparative case: even conceding base-rate false positives and increased detention, validated scores could improve targeting and make judicial errors more testable than unaided discretion. A's closing challenge to B's audit claims kept the contest competitive.

A's earlier exposure of release-only appellate review was also damaging, but B directly conceded and repaired that asymmetry.
