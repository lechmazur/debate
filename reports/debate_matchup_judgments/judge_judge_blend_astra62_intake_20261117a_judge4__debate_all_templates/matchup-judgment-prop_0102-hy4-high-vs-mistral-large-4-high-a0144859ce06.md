### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A wins the central distinction between causal contribution and primacy. B supplied plausible mechanisms and intervention arguments, but repeatedly treated short-term symptom improvement as proof of population-level primacy, eventually asserting crisis reversal without adequate support. A directly challenged that inference and the applicability of college rollout evidence to the teen crisis. A weakened its case by demanding necessity and sufficiency—more than the motion requires—but B still did not establish the comparative importance its affirmative claim needed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest response was that holding offline conditions fixed establishes an app’s causal effect, not its importance relative to those conditions. B answered that the app was the variable moving outcomes, which defended causality but left the ranking challenge unresolved.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by holding PRO to the motion's ranking burden: showing social media can cause or maintain distress does not show it is primary over offline pressures. A's distinction between a removable trigger and the load-bearing ecology survived B's deactivation, timing, and rollout arguments, while B repeatedly equated any reversible causal effect with primacy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its class-ranking-app and fever-lever examples showed that removing a maintaining cue can improve symptoms without proving that cue created the underlying competition or crisis, directly defeating B's claim that only a primary driver could reverse the trend.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the debate focused on the motion’s key word: primary. It conceded that social media can harm teens, but consistently argued that deactivation effects, timing, and dose-response show modifiability or amplification rather than rank-order primacy over deeper offline pressures. A had strong causal evidence and clear mechanisms, but repeatedly slid from showing a lever to proving the primary driver, and some study-like claims felt overconfident. B answered pressure questions directly, avoided brittle precision, and closed with the cleaner voting line: relief is not primacy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s deactivation counterfactual was decisive: within-person improvement shows that the app is a removable trigger or maintaining cue, not that it created the underlying wound, and A never fully proved that short-term relief ranks platforms above fixed offline causes.
