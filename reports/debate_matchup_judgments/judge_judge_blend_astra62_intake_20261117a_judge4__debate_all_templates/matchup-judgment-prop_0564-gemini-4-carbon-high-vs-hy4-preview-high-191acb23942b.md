### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger case by defending immediate benefits to existing riders rather than relying exclusively on driver conversion. A repeatedly imposed that narrower success criterion despite the motion and never established that replacement taxes would outweigh a regular low-income commuter’s fare savings. A’s fare-modernization alternative remained credible, and B overstated the certainty of progressive tax incidence. Nevertheless, B answered the central pressure more directly, defended universal access against underdeveloped targeted discounts, and better explained why modest initial gains need not defeat the policy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B exposed A’s incorrect redefinition of ridership as driver switching and challenged why filling already-running buses requires additional crews. A conceded the empty-seat point but substituted an unquantified crowding scenario. This materially weakened its attempted dilemma between negligible benefits and expensive induced demand.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the more complete route to affirming the motion: immediate benefits for existing riders, simpler boarding, and durable funding do not depend on substantial initial driver conversion, while permanence supports longer-term household changes. A landed strong objections about household tax incidence and fare modernization, but too often equated ridership with drivers switching and treated congestion relief as the decisive benefit. That left A’s “no gains or costly surge” dilemma vulnerable to B’s existing-rider case. Neither side fully proved its tax-incidence arithmetic, keeping the result competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal directly answered both pressure questions and exposed A’s narrow motorist-centered metric: existing riders already provide congestion and labor-market benefits, while total boardings cannot simply be redefined as former drivers. A’s share-of-the-tax-base versus household-budget response was sharp, but did not eliminate B’s independent equity and access case.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won narrowly by making the decisive clash about more than immediate driver conversion. It kept the equity and existing-rider externality case alive, answered the congestion pressure by defending current mode share, and made fares look like a regressive toll on mandatory commutes. A made strong points about off-board modernization, speculative car-shedding, and tax incidence, but never fully neutralized PRO’s core reason to fund transit broadly even with modest initial ridership gains.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing answered A’s “no congestion relief if few drivers switch” pressure by arguing motorists already benefit from existing riders staying on transit, while exposing A’s claim that fare-burdened riders save little because they ration trips.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A wins the central comparison: use finite tax revenue to replace fares or retain net fare income and improve service. B establishes genuine access and administrative benefits, but never convincingly shows why these outweigh foregone service investment under A’s targeted-relief alternative. A answers the funding pressure more directly, although its sweeping claims about crowding and eliminating poverty policing are overstated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s decisive response to B’s “spends scarce dollars twice” argument distinguishes collection expenses from net revenue. Eliminating collection costs does not fund greater frequency when it also eliminates substantially larger receipts. B’s closing repeats the farebox-as-waste framing without repairing that comparison.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A built the stronger comparative case: permanently replacing fare revenue consumes finite tax capacity without adding service, while contactless boarding, civil enforcement, fare caps, and targeted discounts capture many of Side B’s benefits at lower cost. Side B offered a coherent access-and-dignity case and effectively challenged regressive funding and off-peak capacity assumptions, but never squarely overcame the net-revenue and opportunity-cost objection. Its claim that free boardings are not new spending especially failed to answer who replaces the lost revenue.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, distinguished fare-capping from benefit-roll verification, and exposed Side B’s unresolved fiscal trade-off by arguing that taxes spent replacing fares cannot simultaneously buy the vehicles and frequency Side B endorsed.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the opportunity-cost clash decisive: finite broad taxes spent replacing fare revenue cannot also buy more service, especially in major rail systems where net fare revenue is substantial. Side B’s public-infrastructure frame, equity appeal, and rhetoric were strong, but they never fully answered the major-city revenue loss, peak-crowding pressure, or the cheaper targeted alternatives A offered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal answered the pressure questions directly and turned Side B’s concession that taxes should buy vehicles and frequency into the decisive “cannot spend the same tax dollar twice” weighing.
