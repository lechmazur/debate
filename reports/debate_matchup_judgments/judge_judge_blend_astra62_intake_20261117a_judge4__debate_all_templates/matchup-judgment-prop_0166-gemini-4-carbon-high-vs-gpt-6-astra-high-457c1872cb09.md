### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): CON wins. PRO built an appealing case that de-extinction forces integrated biotechnology and fills empty keystone niches, but CON's comparative answer survived more cleanly: governments can fund ambitious genetic rescue directly, so the additional reconstruction gamble needs its own justification. PRO never decisively showed that only an extinct flagship can deliver multi-trait platforms, and its later acceptance that some living populations could be lost once biobanked became a major vulnerability. CON showed that frozen cells are not functioning populations or secure habitats, and treating revival as insurance assumes the technology succeeds. The risk asymmetry—research can be scaled or redirected, but a lost population cannot be restored by cancelling a failed trial—gave CON the stronger reason to oppose diversion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's attack on PRO's biobanking triage was decisive: once PRO accepted losing some endangered populations if their tissues were banked, CON showed this substitutes a contingent reconstruction project for a living population and quietly assumes the revival technology will work.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Both sides were disciplined and responsive, but Side A (CON) held the decisive asymmetry through every round: a lost population cannot be restored by cancelling a failed trial, while research can be scaled and redirected. Side B's best pressure — that CON's "targeted biotech plus protection" alternative also diverts money and thus invents free money — was real, but A's A4.1 answer (fund research around living species and scale it rather than sacrifice its beneficiaries) was coherent and never fully broken. B's rebuttals increasingly leaned on contingent assertions: that only an extinct flagship recruits elite engineers, and that biobanked cells plus revival platforms insure present losses. A's A4.6 retort — that this "assumes the success of the very technology being funded" — landed cleanly and B's closing never neutralized it, instead repeating that losses stay permanent without de-extinction, which A directly answered (funding revival does not make losses reversible). A also consistently met the burden structure the motion implies for PRO, forcing B to justify the additional reconstruction gamble rather than borrow the benefits of conservation biotechnology generally. B's institutional "moonshot recruitment" argument was original and plausibly true but remained an assertion A effectively characterized as grant design rather than necessity, and A's weighing on irreversibility survived to the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's "free money" question (B3.1) was the sharpest PRO moment, exposing a genuine tension in A's alternative, but A's A4.1/A4.2 answer held: the issue is the additional reconstruction expense, not that research costs money. The truly decisive exchange was A's A4.6 against B's B4.2 concession that biobanked "climate-trapped" populations could be allowed to slip — A showed this treats unproven revival technology as insurance for losses it has not yet demonstrated it can reverse, and B's closing never recovered from that.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the motion's actual trade-off in focus: why fund full extinct reconstruction when it diverts from living protection. A showed that much of the claimed biotech benefit is grant-designable without that extra cost, and that banking cells cannot insure against losses of functioning populations. B's niche and moonshot points were real but did not neutralize the circular insurance argument or the unjustified additional expense.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal, which answered the budget pressure by scaling living-species research rather than inventing free money, then exposed that tissue banking assumes the unproven revival it is supposed to insure.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by building the sharper comparative case: irreversible losses to living endangered species outweigh a deferrable, highly uncertain project that cannot truly resurrect extinct species and lacks proof of unique ecological advantage. Side A successfully collapsed PRO's staged-research defense by showing that meaningful ecological testing still requires risky endangered-elephant surrogacy and generations of breeding, while living substitutes can be field-tested now. PRO's long-term capability argument was genuine but too speculative to overcome the asymmetry between permanent extinction and postponable research.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive: it reversed PRO's own comparative-trials concession by showing that trials would first require diverting funds and risking endangered surrogate pregnancies, meaning staging caps fiscal losses but not irreversible biological ones.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a high-quality exchange. A's case rested on the asymmetry of irreversibility—delaying de-extinction forfeits little (samples stay preserved) while delaying protection is permanently fatal—which survived B's counter that "wait" becomes indefinite exclusion, because A's answer (fund only after core protection is covered) is a defensible priority rather than an evasion, and A's closing converted B's own concessions into the verdict. A also exposed the circularity of B's "distinctive return" (the unique benefit of revival research is enabling revival) and landed the sharpest rebuttals: "cells in a dish graze zero tundra" and the reversal showing B's proposed comparative trials could test living substitutes without diversion while testing hybrids requires risky surrogate pregnancies first. B performed admirably—biting the bullet on experimental pregnancies in A1, conceding no proven superiority of proxies, and defending staged funding as "buying evidence"—but these honest concessions left B's expected-value case thinner after A's "staging caps fiscal losses, not irreversible biological ones" response. Both sides answered pressure questions directly with proper A1/A2 format; the decisive difference was that A made the final weighing cleaner and blunted B's live routes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal (A2.1–A2.6) exposing B's contradiction between promising restored ecosystems and retreating to "contained work," combined with the irreversibility asymmetry ("delaying de-extinction loses nothing permanent; delaying protection is permanently fatal"), was the debate's turning point; B's A1 concession in Rebuttal 2 (accepting controlled experimental pregnancies) partially repaired it but validated the pressure.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A more convincingly showed that the motion’s required diversion fails an expected-benefit test. Side B conceded that hybrids have no established ecological advantage and ultimately accepted risky surrogate pathways, while Side A showed that living substitutes and direct genetic rescue can be pursued first and that staging cannot refund irreversible extinctions. Side B’s scarcity-and-veto frame remained live, but it was too abstract to justify cannibalizing endangered-species protection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it turned B’s call for comparative trials and staged “evidence-buying” into a cost-first gamble requiring experimental pregnancies and permanent losses before any hybrid could be shown superior to living substitutes.
