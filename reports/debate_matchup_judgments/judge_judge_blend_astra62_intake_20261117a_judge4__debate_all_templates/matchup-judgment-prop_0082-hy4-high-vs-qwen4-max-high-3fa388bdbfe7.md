### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp, and the debate narrowed well. CON built a coherent case: disclosure fixes concealment, caps subtract options, firms cannot be trusted to define the harm, and uncertainty should favor reversible, user-controlled tools. PRO edges ahead for three reasons. First, the motion is conditional. It asks for limits only where the features undermine match quality and trust. That forced CON into defending these mechanics even where harm is shown, which is a harder position. Second, PRO's framing landed. A ranked, rationed feed already exists, so there is no permission-preserving status quo. CON's distinction between ordering a feed and subtracting options was clever but did not fully dislodge this. Third, CON's concessions accumulated. It accepted product-honored user caps. On the blur wall, CON's closing answer was to "remove the blur," which is itself a limit on a paid visibility feature. That partly validates PRO's claim that labels alone cannot reach manufactured ambiguity. CON's strongest residual point was "who decides." It also landed a fair critique that PRO's thin-market exemption creates discretion for the same untrusted firm. PRO's answer, that a trigger can be written tightly, was thin but adequate. PRO's Q1, exposing CON's asymmetric evidentiary standard, also drew only a mechanism-based reply. That leaves a real but narrow edge to PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing response to the blur wall: a truthful "pay to see" label leaves the manufactured ambiguity intact, so CON's concession that concealment is the harm demands a limit on the feature. CON's reply, "remove the blur," effectively concedes a limit on paid visibility, which tipped the core clash toward PRO.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by keeping the remedy comparison precise. A established plausible harms and effectively challenged the idea of a neutral status quo, but moved among disclosure, unblurring, user-set controls, and compulsory caps without establishing that rationing visibility improves matches. B answered both pressure rounds directly, restored the reciprocal-exposure case for boosts, showed why deferral can be a real loss, and defended less restrictive, reversible alternatives. A’s conditional framing and argument that B’s remedies sometimes amount to limits kept the contest close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the “same twelve faces” argument with reciprocal visibility, accepted only user-set limits while distinguishing them from platform rationing, and exposed A’s movement between information remedies and compulsory ceilings.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO wins by exploiting the motion's conditional wording and reframing the status quo as already rationed for revenue, leaving CON to defend harms even when proven. CON's who-decides and disclosure alternatives land, but its acceptance of removing blurred paywalls and user-honored caps narrows its objection to who sets compulsory limits rather than defeating limits themselves.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's closing on the blurred 'who liked you' wall: a truthful 'pay to see' label still leaves manufactured ambiguity as the product, undercutting A's claim that disclosure alone solves the trust harm.
