### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B built the cleaner path to victory. Side A offered a morally vivid but categorical rule: a name given as an honor remains an honor, so retention is state endorsement. Side B successfully undermined that rule by distinguishing what the state asks of citizens: a holiday compels celebration, while a street sign or diploma primarily functions as an address or institutional identifier. B's strongest material was the integrated-family example, where retaining a slaveholder-named school could recognize the descendants who defeated segregation rather than the original honoree. A did not squarely neutralize this; it dismissed those families as prioritizing personal attachment, which mischaracterized B's point and made A's answer feel evasive. B also answered pressure more directly by arguing that context cannot change the original dedication but can change the present public judgment. That distinction was more responsive and more faithful to the motion's "primarily honor" language than A's insistence that original purpose permanently controls meaning.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it distinguished compulsory encounter from compulsory tribute and contrasted a street that rescinds a dedication but keeps an address with one that still lays a wreath to the dictator. This directly answered A's pressure question and exposed the weakness in A's claim that original dedication alone establishes present veneration.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won solidly by reframing historical continuity around the living communities who conquered exclusionary institutions rather than nostalgia for oppressors, while cleanly distinguishing purely celebratory observances like dictator holidays from functional civic institutions like schools and streets. Side B (PRO) presented an intuitive moral case and applied real pressure on whether Franco streets differ from Franco holidays, keeping the debate competitive where A's reclamation model fits integrated schools more naturally than dictator-named streets. However, Side B staked its entire case on the rigid premise that an originally honorific name permanently constitutes state veneration—going so far as to insist the state must overrule both integrating Black families and current pupils who want to keep a school's name on their diplomas, while dismissing their claim as "nostalgia." Side A dismantled this absolutism by showing that formally rescinding a dedication changes the state's public judgment, that museums cannot preserve visible continuity across generations of diplomas, and that treating original intent as immutable grants founders permanent authority over an institution's meaning.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure question [A3.2] and follow-up in [A4.5-A4.7], capped by [A5.1]: after Side B argued in [B4.3] that the state must overrule integrating families and current pupils rather than indulge "some citizens' nostalgia," Side A showed this confused praising a slaveholder with recognizing the families who defeated his exclusionary policy, and then dismantled B's closing Hitler-statue analogy in two sentences ("A statue puts a man on display.
