### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won by giving the cleaner operational test of what "structural" should mean. A's core inference—boys lag within every subgroup, so sex must be structural—was effectively answered by B's persistence-is-not-salience move: a pattern surviving controls does not itself establish magnitude, mechanism, or a different policy. B also used the math subject reversal, the already male-tilted spending, and the balanced-literacy explanation to show the real defect may be universal reading instruction rather than maleness. A never squarely resolved the subject flip or showed a dollar the male label uniquely moves, and its "thirty years failed" argument was undercut once B reframed those years as the wrong universal instruction, not universal instruction as such.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's persistence-vs-salience rebuttal was decisive: the relative-age analogy showed that a variable can survive controls yet still not deserve structural priority, and the math subject flip plus balanced-literacy point neutralized A's strongest claim that the gap is a condition of maleness rather than a literacy and behavior problem concentrated in particular schools.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won solidly by showing that a variable earns major structural policy status not merely by persisting statistically, but by having dominant magnitude and changing what schools should actually do. Side B built a credible opening case around cross-national gaps, reactive special-education triage, and within-subgroup persistence. However, Side A systematically dismantled B's policy conclusion: A showed that B's proposed upstream fixes (movement, varied instruction, mentorship) are universal good teaching that equally benefit struggling girls in the same classrooms, exposed the real opportunity cost and lowered-expectation risks of spending scarce training hours on male developmental framing rather than systematic reading instruction, neutralized subgroup persistence with the relative-age analogy, and repeatedly pressed the fact that the gender gap narrows or reverses in mathematics—a fatal anomaly for B's claim that schools are structurally mismatched to boys, which Side B dropped completely across the final rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In response to Side B's sharpest pressure question [B3.1]—asking what could disqualify sex from structural attention when boys lag within every demographic control—Side A answered in [A4.1] that "persistence is not salience," pointing out that birth-month relative-age effects also survive every control across cohorts yet nobody reorganizes education policy around birth month because poverty dwarfs the residual and the teaching remedy is identical. Paired with Side A's unanswered point that the gap reverses in math, this cleanly severed Side B's central link between statistical persistence and gender-targeted structural policy.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B more cleanly showed that a persistent male gap does not justify organizing policy around sex. B conceded the pattern but pressed salience, opportunity cost, subject reversal, and the fact that Side A's remedies were universal good teaching. Side A never fully answered the math flip or why labeling changes a scarce training hour rather than merely renaming existing fixes, and its claim that decades of universal instruction failed was undercut by B's distinction between bad methods and the untried remedy. The live route favored treating the gap as real but not a major sex-based structural priority.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal: the thirty-year failure was bad literacy instruction, not proof that universal fixes fail, and a structural condition of maleness should not reverse in math.
