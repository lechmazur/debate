### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins. Its core mechanism held up across the debate: ranked feeds actively expand a post's reach to strangers selected for predicted reactivity, and that expansion rewards inflammatory framing. Chronology confines a post to people who chose to follow the poster. PRO also dismantled CON's main line that chronology means "no levers." Bans, rate limits, reply filters and brigading detection do not need an engagement model, so chronology removes the amplifier rather than moderation. Its distinction between opt-in follower reach and platform-selected expansionary reach answered CON's Q1 cleanly. On the toggle argument, PRO showed that a user switching their own feed to "latest" leaves platform distribution unchanged, which defused CON's best late point. CON had real strengths: - the follower-graph and troll point; - historical confounds such as mobile adoption and polarization; - a sharper A2 answer about pre-distribution reach control. But CON leaned heavily on the hypothetical "ranking tuned well" against what actually shipped. Its closing dilemma also misfires: if toxicity is amplification-driven, chronology simply removes the stranger-facing amplification that ranking would need to throttle. CON also repeated stock lines ("rearranging the furniture," "no intervention point") without much development. PRO paid an execution cost, with three turns clipped for exceeding the word limit. That cost is not enough to offset its clearer comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over levers. CON claimed that chronology has "no intervention point." PRO answered with B2.1 and Q2: no toxicity lever, whether bans, rate limits, reply filters or brigading detection, requires engagement ranking, so chronology strips only the amplifier. CON's later reframing to "pre-distribution reach control" conceded that the lever exists only to throttle an amplification that chronology does not create.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): B won by keeping the motion on actual defaults: engagement ranking expands hostile posts to reactive strangers and subsidizes inflammatory writing, while chronology only reaches opt-in followers. A's "ranking can be tuned" and "no brakes" lines stayed live but compared the real product to a hypothetical safety ranker, and the latest-toggle point was answered—individual time-order does not change platform-wide distribution incentives. Clipped B turns narrowed the margin; they did not erase the cleaner causal path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to the latest-toggle: opt-in chronology changes one user's view, not what the default ranker distributes to everyone else or what creators are paid to write.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): B won the comparative mechanism: ranked defaults allocate expanding reach by predicted reaction, subsidizing outrage, while chronology bounds reach to opt-in followers. B repeatedly exposed A's reliance on a hypothetically well-tuned ranker versus the engagement-optimized default that actually shipped, and answered the follower-graph and toggle objections with bounded versus expansionary distribution and individual-view versus system-wide incentives. A's pre-distribution throttle was a strong counter, but remained a potential use outweighed by the shipped incentive structure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure on 'can be tuned well' versus what shipped, reinforced in Rebuttal 2 and the closing distinction between an individual chronological toggle and a chronological default, most decisively damaged A's main route.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO wins a close debate. Its causal story is clear and holds up through the exchange: engagement ranking routes conflict to strangers who never opted in, while a chronological feed caps reach at the follow graph. CON applied the sharpest pressure through its counterfactual dilemma. It asked whether the motion covers feed order alone or the whole recommendation stack, and it argued that discovery surfaces would have existed anyway. That landed real damage. Even so, PRO's answer is defensible as a reading of the motion as written: the ranked feed is where recommendation became the default surface. CON's main comparative harms were also weaker than they sounded. Mention and reply raids happen under either feed ordering, so they do not favor CON. PRO's B4.3 caught this, noting that if ranking mostly reorders the follow graph, it cannot be the missing brake on mentions. CON's "patchy brake beats none" reply was reasonable, but it never showed that the demotion lever reliably cuts toxicity rather than amplifying it. CON's volume-bias point stays bounded by the opt-in ceiling. CON also lost some execution credit: its second rebuttal was clipped, and its closing cited nonexistent IDs (P4.1, P5.2) while partly misdescribing PRO's position as switching counterfactuals. PRO's line "does not need to slow the inbox" sounded concessive, which kept the margin thin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over the moderation lever. CON's A2.4 claimed ranking is the only non-removal brake on pile-ons. PRO's B4.3 turned CON's own Q1 against it: if ranked feeds mostly reorder the follow graph, then ranking does not touch mentions and replies, which stay chronological in both worlds. The only place ranking changes visibility is amplification, where it optimizes for engagement. That made CON's inbox harm non-comparative, and CON's later "a patchy brake beats none" reply did not restore it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the comparison on the motion's actual default-product difference: an engagement-ranked feed turns outrage into a distribution strategy, while a chronological default keeps the main surface an opt-in follow graph and leaves discovery secondary. CON's alternatives—mention raids and a lost demotion brake—were materially damaged: mention channels stay chronological under both designs, and an engagement optimizer will not reliably demote the conflict that performs best. CON's two-counterfactual attack was sharp but slightly misread PRO's consistent claim, so the residual doubt about side discovery modules keeps the win competitive rather than dominant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal, especially the point that if ranking mainly reorders the follow graph it cannot be the missing brake on mentions and raids, while where ranking does change visibility its objective is engagement, so demotion is the exception and amplification the rule.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO's bounded opt-in versus stranger-recruitment comparison survived the exchange: chronological reach remains tied to followers, while engagement ranking exports conflict and rewards provocation. CON's scope challenge was strong, but PRO plausibly tied it to the default product, and CON's demotion brake was undercut by the engagement incentive to amplify conflict. CON's clipped second rebuttal also weakened its finish.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's B4.3 folding CON's case: if ranking mainly reorders the follow graph it cannot brake mentions and raids, while where it does change visibility its engagement objective favors amplification over demotion.
