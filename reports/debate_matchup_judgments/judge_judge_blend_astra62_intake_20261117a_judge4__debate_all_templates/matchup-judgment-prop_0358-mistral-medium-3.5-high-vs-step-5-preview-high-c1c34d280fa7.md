### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO began with the stronger core mechanism. Its link between speed and survival, and its point that penalties cannot stop the first crash by an ordinary driver, give it a real route to victory. However, PRO's later speeches mostly repeated the same London, Portland and survival figures, which are presented with somewhat brittle precision. It also did little to adapt as the clash developed. PRO overclaimed that Portland showed "no displacement," and CON exposed this cleanly: an unmeasured offset is not a measured absence. PRO's answer on texting was reasonable, since design lowers lethality whatever the driver's intent, but its second rebuttal was short and its closing was a recap. CON built a distinct counter-frame: a high-risk minority produces the fatal tail, and penalties act as an incapacitation filter. CON answered both pressure questions directly and honestly, conceding the clean first-crash case. It also sharpened the decisive comparison memorably with "Geometry asks; penalties remove." CON's case has weaknesses. Its claim that fatalities concentrate in a reckless minority is unsupported. Its shift from "post-crash" to "post-violation" penalties stretches the motion. Its concession in B2.7 that "the city needs both" softens its stance on the "more than" comparison. Overall, CON engaged and adapted better, while PRO's main argument survived only in repeated rather than developed form. That gives CON a clear but competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over displacement and the Portland evidence (A2.5 vs B2.6/B4.2). PRO asserted that Portland showed no displacement. CON replied that treated-corridor results never measured adjacent side streets, so an unmeasured offset is not a measured absence. PRO's A2 answer simply repeated the claim, which undercut PRO's evidentiary credibility.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won solidly through superior rebuttal engagement, progression across rounds, and epistemic discipline. While Side B opened with a strong kinetic-energy mechanism and pivoted neatly when pressed on distracted texting, B largely repeated the same three statistics across four speeches and left A's core arguments unanswered. - **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): B retained the stronger path to the motion: lower speeds and redesign address both crash occurrence and lethality before a death, applying across ordinary errors and deliberate risk-taking. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): PRO built a clear causal case: impact speed determines whether a crash is survivable, design and lower defaults reduce that speed on every trip, and penalties cannot reach ordinary error or inattention. PRO adapted across rounds. It reframed CON's Sweden and London examples as paired systems in which enforcement amplified safer streets rather than replacing them. It also answered both pressure questions directly with A1/A2, though its claim that defaults alone reduce speeds without enforcement was somewhat thin. CON's best point was real: limits and design may need enforcement to work, and design cannot stop a drunk driver's decision. However, CON leaned repeatedly on an unverified and overconfident claim that Sweden's sharpest declines followed penalties. It also quietly shifted from "harsher penalties" to "automated enforcement," which is a different lever from the one the motion compares. Its rebuttal and closing speeches largely repeated the opening without engaging PRO's point that the most common crashes come from inattention rather than deliberate choice. CON's answer to PRO's causation question simply restated its earlier assertion. Net result: PRO made the debate easier to decide and kept its central route to victory intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2.2/A4.5 turn on Sweden and London was the most decisive exchange. PRO argued that both cases paired enforcement with lower-speed zones and engineered streets, making enforcement an amplifier rather than the source of survivability. Q2 then pressed CON for evidence that penalties caused the declines. CON answered only by repeating its assertion, which left its main empirical support unproven.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built a far stronger, more progressive case grounded in crash physics and realistic driver behavior. Side B explained why lower default speed limits and self-enforcing street geometry reduce impact energy and forgive ordinary distraction, misjudgment, and unfamiliarity that threat-based deterrence cannot prevent. Side A (CON) quietly conflated harsher post-crash penalties with pre-crash automated camera enforcement, repeated overconfident assertions about Sweden and London nearly verbatim across four speeches instead of answering Q2, and saw its best pressure points on compliance and phased construction cleanly answered in Side B's second rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's turn of Side A's Sweden and London examples across [B2.2], [B3.2], and [B4.5]: Side B showed that Vision Zero and London's 20 mph zones relied on lower speed limits and engineered streets, making enforcement at best an amplifier of a speed rule rather than a substitute for physical survivability, and exposed Side A's inability in [A4.2] to offer anything beyond circular repetition when asked to separate penalties from design.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger comparative case: lower speeds and forgiving design reduce both crash likelihood and lethality even when drivers err, while A largely relied on deterrence claims that did not isolate harsher post-crash penalties from general enforcement. B directly answered compliance and phased-redesign pressure; A mostly repeated unsupported Sweden/London assertions and never adequately addressed ordinary mistakes or impact survivability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal decisively answered both pressure questions by explaining self-enforcing design, citywide default limits during phased reconstruction, and why enforcement amplifies rather than replaces engineered survivability.
