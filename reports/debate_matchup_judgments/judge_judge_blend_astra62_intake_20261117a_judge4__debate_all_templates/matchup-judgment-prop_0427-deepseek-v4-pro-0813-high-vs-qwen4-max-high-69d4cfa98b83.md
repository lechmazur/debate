### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO wins a competitive debate. Both sides avoided fake precision and argued from plausible mechanisms. CON's best route stayed live to the end: clubs are voluntary, so the most isolated teenagers may never walk in, while a classroom reaches everyone. PRO did not fully neutralize that point, but it softened it materially. Its answers were that funding buys personal recruitment, transport, stipends and non-competitive crew roles, and that much of what looks like avoidance is really logistics. PRO's decisive move was exposing how far CON had to stretch "literacy campaigns alone" to stay competitive. CON's version became a recurring, adult-led, coached school curriculum. PRO then posed a dilemma: either this is sustained public investment in PRO's mechanism, or CON is back to thin posters and assemblies and loses the dose comparison. CON's reply that the curriculum could run on existing staff budgets was partial and plausible. But it implicitly conceded that recurring, adult-led practice is the active ingredient, and PRO capitalized on that concession. PRO's closing turn landed hard: "she is already there... and still lonely." It reframed CON's compulsory-attendance advantage as proof that proximity alone does not fix loneliness. CON's closing restated the entry argument competently but did not answer the obligation and counter-audience point. PRO framed the stakes more memorably and adapted better across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 dilemma was the most decisive exchange. CON's "well-run campaign" needed recurring, coached, adult-led practice, which PRO argued is sustained public funding under a literacy label. CON countered that existing school staff could deliver it. That answer only partly escaped the trap and left PRO's mechanism point standing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): B wins a competitive debate by defending recurring school-day literacy against A's artificially thin campaign comparison. A compellingly distinguishes belonging from mere attendance, and recruitment plus meaningful roles partially answer exclusion. But B supplies an intervention beyond attendance—supervised bystander training—and maintains comparative pressure over reaching club-avoidant teenagers. A never adequately answers that stronger alternative, instead repeatedly treating any sustained, publicly funded instruction as a concession to clubs, which the proposition does not support.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal directly dismantles A's funding dilemma: existing school staff can deliver repeated literacy instruction without that becoming youth-club funding. This neutralizes A's dose advantage while preserving B's voluntary-entry objection. A's closing distinction between reach and belonging is sharp, but does not fully answer that combined comparison.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won clearly in a high-level debate by proving that compulsory reach without a strong causal mechanism does not beat sustained, task-based belonging. Side B smartly steelmanned literacy campaigns as recurring school-day advisory training and pressed a real limitation in Side A's case: voluntary clubs still require opt-in, which deeply avoidant teens may resist. However, Side A substantially narrowed that gap by showing what public funding actually buys—buses, stipends for working teens, personal adult recruitment, and non-cut backstage roles like sound or set crew—while exposing fatal gaps in Side B's alternative. On loneliness, stopping online pile-ons does not create friendships, and students are already physically present in compulsory classrooms while remaining isolated. On violence, funded clubs fill high-risk after-school hours and build offline status ladders and real allies, whereas classroom scripts rely on angry teens heeding advice or peers spending social capital unsupervised on midnight feeds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's compulsory-classroom defense across Q2 [A3.2] and Closing [A5.1]-[A5.4], paired with [A2.2] and [A4.1]-[A4.2]: when Side B answered in [B4.3] that the classroom is safer because the isolated girl "is already there," Side A turned that concession against CON—showing she is already in class every day and still lonely, and that only load-bearing shared tasks (like running cables on a crew), not taught bystander scripts, turn proximity into people who notice when she disappears.
