### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): A better isolated the proposition’s actual tradeoff: clearer drafting is common ground, but sacrificing substantive boundaries requires justification. Its distinction between safe-harbor guidance and comprehensive statutory rules directly answered B’s strongest pressure. B established meaningful accessibility benefits, but inadequately answered why shifting lost precision into interpretation would help citizens facing better-funded opponents. Its closing largely compared readable statutes against inaccessible law rather than A’s combined alternative of precise statutes and protected guidance. A overstated the certainty and costlessness precision provides, keeping this a competitive rather than dominant win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange combined two responses: readable statutory wording does not prevent adverse interpretations within its loosened boundaries, and “litigation has two sides.” This directly turned B’s claim that sophisticated parties could recover lost precision into a disadvantage for the vulnerable. B’s reply that unequal litigation already exists did not resolve that incremental risk.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that the required sacrifice of precision creates gray areas the resourced exploit against the unrepresented, while official guides with one-way safe harbors already supply quotable notice and exact statutes supply mechanical protection at zero litigation cost. PRO's moral case and plain-versus-vague distinction were strong, but concessions that courts, regulations, and affordable litigation would recover lost precision, plus a narrowing of "non-lawyers" for technical statutes, were turned into evidence that the motion recreates false notice and shifts outcomes toward wealthier adversaries. CON answered the pressure questions more damagingly and left the cleaner comparative reason not to mandate the sacrifice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4's flip of A4.7: precision "recovered" by sophisticated parties through litigation they can afford is recovered against tenants, borrowers, and the public, so lost exactness is not a cost borne only by the powerful.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won the central tradeoff: precision is lost at the litigated margins, where vagueness and regulatory gloss are exploited through litigation the weak cannot afford. PRO's readable-text legitimacy case remained powerful, but CON's guide-plus-safe-harbor alternative preserved notice without making a simplified summary the binding, exploitable law, and PRO's retreat to compliance officers weakened its accessibility claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: the safe harbor as a one-way shield versus a statute as a two-way sword answered PRO's strongest question, while turning PRO's claim that sophisticated parties can recover precision through litigation into proof they recover it against unrepresented citizens.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): A narrowly wins the precision trade-off. B effectively defended everyday accessibility and exposed the private-law gaps in official summaries. But A more convincingly distinguished explaining settled law plainly from replacing its settled meanings. B’s assurances that lost precision would produce only narrow boundary disputes did not adequately answer the consequences for criminal liability or the discretion created by ordinary-meaning interpretation. A’s alternative remained imperfect, but its account of the additional uncertainty was stronger than B’s justification for accepting it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A turned B’s jury-instruction example against it: translating an established legal term into ordinary words does not require abandoning its established meaning. B’s response that juries already exercise judgment did not establish that discarding precedent would leave uncertainty or discretion unchanged.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing the motion’s admitted precision sacrifice and showing it shifts contested meanings to later unelected decision-makers, undercutting PRO’s own notice and legislative-control goals. PRO made a strong accessibility and capture case, but CON better neutralized the rewrites, summaries critique, and enforcement story while offering a lower-cost route to comprehension.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal that PRO’s 30-day and certified-mail examples are already plain and precise, so the mandate only bites where precision is lost and hard cases migrate to judges and juries after the fact.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): CON won the central tradeoff: where plain language is already precise the mandate adds nothing, and where it sacrifices precision PRO shifted contested lines to judges, juries, and post-hoc interpretation. PRO effectively challenged unread precedent and misleading summaries, but its reliance on everyday judgment and lenity left the fair-notice and test-case objections largely intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal that PRO's “recklessly” rewrite merely creates new disputes over “obvious” and “serious,” making early defendants test cases, most decisively damaged PRO's notice argument.
