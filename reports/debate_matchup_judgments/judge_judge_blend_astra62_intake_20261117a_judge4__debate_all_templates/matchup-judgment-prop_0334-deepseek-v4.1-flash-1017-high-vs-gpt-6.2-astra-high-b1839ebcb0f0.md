### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO built a clean frame: notes govern contestable political speech, and takedowns are a narrow exception for verifiable, imminent harms. PRO stayed consistent and answered both pressure questions directly. CON, however, progressively defused PRO's main offense, which turned out to be the bigger move. PRO's martyrdom, opacity and irreversibility harms all attach to removing contested content. CON never proposed doing that. By Rebuttal 2, CON had made the comparison explicit with its rule: "if either finding is uncertain, leave it up." Under both policies the contested clip stays visible, so the real marginal value of expanding notes is only some extra explanation, which speakers and readers can already supply. CON also reframed "bigger part" in terms of institutional commitment rather than removal counts. On that measure, detection that cannot wait for contributors or traffic spikes is a large standing job, even if it seldom ends in removal. PRO's reply, "fund the emergency channel; keep it an exception," largely conceded the resource priority without contesting that framing. PRO's own expected-harm-and-reversibility metric also cut toward CON's lost-vote example. PRO's closing point that CON's leave-it-up rule concedes the contested terrain to notes was CON's best target to answer. CON answered it well: leaving a post up is a rule against suppression, not a mandate to annotate. PRO kept the better-intuited description of most political content. CON nonetheless made the decisive comparison clearer and more resistant to rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.6 turned PRO's own expected-harm test against it. Because contested clips stay up under CON's rule anyway, expanding notes saves no speech from suppression and only adds explanation. By contrast, no one outside the platform can stop obsolete voting instructions from reaching the next voter. This neutralized PRO's central martyrdom and irreversibility harms. PRO's closing attempt to recast "leave it up" as a concession was answered cleanly in CON's closing.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by exposing A’s false dichotomy between annotation and suppression: under B’s policy, contestable clips remain up anyway, so notes’ reversibility does not itself prove they deserve the larger governing role. B then gave the clearer priority argument—proactive detection requires substantial institutional capacity even when removals are rare, and readers cannot substitute for timely platform action on verified voting instructions. A answered the pressure questions competently but largely relabeled that capacity an exception without defeating the resource-based comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it separated “leave it up” from “annotate it,” explained why a small number of removals can still require a large proactive-enforcement commitment, and showed that A’s reversibility advantage did not apply to the disputed clips B would also leave visible.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by narrowing the debate to verified, time-sensitive political harms and showing that notes cannot repair irreparable losses such as missed voting opportunities. A also blunted B’s core opacity concern by conceding that uncertain contested speech should stay up, then argued that proactive detection still requires a substantial institutional role. B’s context-first case was strong, but its “emergency exception” response never fully answered why that exception should remain smaller than notes when the stakes are highest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal cleanly answered B’s pressure by defining a narrow verification standard for removal, then turned B’s conceded emergency monitoring into a resource-heavy, non-delegable task that notes cannot replace.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): PRO wins because it framed the dispute early and held that frame. Both sides agree that fake polling notices and similar emergencies come down, so the real contest is the ordinary middle of political claims. PRO's two pressure questions did the most damage. On the clipped video, CON admitted that no reliable ex ante test exists. That concession undercut its own "remove the suspicious clip" model, and PRO answered it sharply: restoring an accurate clip after the vote repairs nothing. On the disproved cost claim, CON proposed deleting the post and then quoting it in the platform's correction. PRO's closing turned this neatly: if voters can safely see the quoted words beside the ledger, they can see the original post beside it. CON had real strengths. It pressed timing well and fairly called PRO's visibility threshold "an autopsy," a metric moderators cannot apply in the moment. Its recurrence and network argument was also legitimate, and it drew a concession that account sanctions handle coordinated networks. But that concession concerns behavior enforcement rather than the governance of ordinary political content. CON also expanded removal to every "clear falsehood" without answering PRO's concern that this puts platforms in charge of deciding which campaign claims voters may see. PRO made the cleaner, more decidable comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over the clipped video. PRO asked what test separates a misleading clip from an accurate one before the full footage is available. CON answered that no reliable test exists and proposed provisional removal with restoration later. PRO had already marked "suspicious" as the decisive word: CON's speed advantage comes from acting before anyone knows whether the edit misleads. It then showed that restoration after an election cannot return what voters lost. Together these turned CON's speed advantage into a liability.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): A won a very close debate by keeping the comparison on ordinary political-content governance while accommodating removals for emergencies and coordinated networks. B pressed delay, reach, and recurrence effectively, but its reversible-takedown model never fully answered the irreversible cost of suppressing an accurate clip before an election, nor showed why exceptional abuse should make takedowns the larger overall component. A’s claim-and-evidence model also preserved public accountability more convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response that restoration cannot recover lost pre-election exposure, followed by the observation that B’s correction would itself quote the removed claim, sharply exposed the costs and inconsistency of removal-first governance.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B narrowly wins by keeping the focus on ordinary political content: after both sides agree emergencies and coordinated networks can be removed or sanctioned, B better shows that side-by-side context preserves accountability and avoids irreversibly suppressing uncertain but accurate political speech. A’s timing and recurrence pressure is real, but B’s concessions and “put the correction where the audience is” answer leave the cleaner default for the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s clipped-video and cost-claim exchange: removing an uncertain clip cannot restore lost election-time speech, and if a correction can safely quote the claim, it can also sit beside the original post.
