### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won by turning PRO's own concessions into a decisive dilemma. PRO admitted insurance prices only property/liability losses, not the sleep, allergy, and safety harms that motivate the debate (B4.1), and admitted landlords may not deny covered pets on building-wide concerns (B4.2). That left the neighbor externality unpriced, uncompensated, and reachable only through enforcement that runs after the harm. CON's "hollow or real" framing then boxed PRO in: either insurer underwriting and discretion still decide everything (window dressing, and insurer breed/size tables are themselves categorical, blunting PRO's "individualization" claim), or the motion genuinely strips the ex ante tool neighbors rely on. PRO's best counter — proportionality, that a ban sacrifices the quiet insured cat for the rare nuisance — was genuinely strong and sustained, and its pressure questions were pointed and answered. But PRO's closing claim that "even CON's landlord discretion must wait for evidence" misfires, since a ban default is precisely ex ante action. CON answered both pressure questions directly with A1/A2 labels; its Rebuttal 2 was clipped but only at the final sentence, a minor execution cost. Overall CON made the debate easier to decide and left the central burden — that the "priced risk" narrative fails for the harms that matter — squarely on PRO's side of the table.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.2: CON's "blanket ban with a private intermediary" reframe, exposing that PRO's insurer-underwriting escape hatch reintroduces the very categorical sorting PRO condemned, while PRO's B4.2 concession that landlords cannot deny on building-wide concerns sealed the externality gap.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B built the stronger path to victory by showing that the motion’s financial mechanism covers repairs while leaving neighbor nuisance, allergens, and safety dependent on largely retrospective enforcement. Side A answered the pressure questions directly and made a credible proportionality case, but never fully neutralized the loss of ex ante discretion, especially for harms that behavior rules cannot prevent. B’s clipped second rebuttal and its occasional overstatement of A’s silence reduce the margin, but its central objection remained live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal was decisive: it turned A’s underwriting concession into a transparency and categorical-screening problem, then showed that A’s individualized enforcement requires neighbors to suffer harm before supplying the evidence needed for removal.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON won the central clash: PRO conceded insurance prices repairs, not the neighbor harms from barking, allergens, and safety, leaving only slow post-hoc enforcement.
