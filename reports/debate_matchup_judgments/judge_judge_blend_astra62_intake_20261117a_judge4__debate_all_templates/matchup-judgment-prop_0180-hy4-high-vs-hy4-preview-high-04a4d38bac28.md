### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Both sides were competent and well-matched, but the decisive clash was baseline/additionality, and A resolved it more cleanly. B's strongest route was that rewilding on seed-rich abandoned land is non-additional and that hard land eventually requires planting's tools. A's symmetry rebuttal (A4.3, A5.1) landed hard: if background recovery is the counterfactual, planting on the same land can claim only the speed premium, so debiting rewilding for that recovery while crediting plantations is double counting. B's response—pivoting to targeted planting on stalled, invasive-locked sites—narrowed the motion, since the proposition compares rewilding to "most" active programs, and A plausibly noted that hard sites are the exception, not the benchmark. A also turned B's open-habitat biodiversity point against reforestation (which closes habitat by design), and B's reply that gap maintenance is active management met A's retort that CON was importing the successional mosaic at active-program prices. Pressure questions were sharp on both sides; both answered A1/A2 in format, though A's A1 was partly burden-shifting. B's framing and closing were punchier rhetorically, but A did the stronger comparative weighing on the live issues, making the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.3) and closing (A5.1): the "asymmetric baseline / double counting" argument—B's non-additionality debit applied only to rewilding while planting claims the whole future tree—was the exchange that most damaged B's live route to victory, and B never fully neutralized it beyond narrowing the comparator to hard-site targeted planting.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built a cleaner, more durable path to victory on the per-dollar comparison. Side B showed that land tenure, monitoring, and targeted invasive or drainage fixes are shared costs, whereas active reforestation adds nursery stock, site prep, crews, guards, and replanting—leaving far fewer hectares restored per dollar while overlooking soil and wetland carbon pools. Side A mounted a sophisticated counter-case around additionality and stalled, invasive-locked fields, but Side B decisively exposed the asymmetric baseline behind that move: if seed-rich fields recover on their own, active planting on ordinary land can claim only a costly speed premium rather than the whole tree, and stalled sites do not define "most" abandoned land. Side B also caught Side A's biodiversity contradiction in attacking scrub closure while advocating faster-closing tree canopies or expensive paid gap maintenance that further erodes per-dollar returns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: When Side A pressed its core additionality trap in [A2.2] and [A3.2]—arguing that protecting seed-rich fields buys carbon that passive abandonment would store anyway—Side B answered in [B4.3] and [B5.1] that autonomous recovery is a shared counterfactual baseline: planting cannot deduct natural recovery from rewilding while claiming the whole planted tree rather than just its expensive speed premium, whereas rewilding cheaply secures tenure against recultivation and removes bottlenecks across many more hectares.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive clash the symmetric counterfactual: if abandoned land would recover anyway, active planting can only claim a speed premium while still bearing seedlings, site preparation, watering, replanting, and gap-management costs. ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were competent and grounded, but B progressively narrowed the dispute to the decisive question: the motion is a comparative quotient claim ("more carbon and biodiversity per dollar than most programs"), and A offered a plausible cost-structure mechanism rather than a demonstrated outcome. B's accounting-asymmetry argument was the sharpest thread of the debate: planting failures are visible invoices while rewilding's stalled succession, retreatment, and low-biomass grazing become "cost structure," and avoided peat emissions get booked as sequestration. B also exposed the circularity of A's "habitat niches per dollar" biodiversity metric and correctly handled A's best pressure question (that B's best-practice comparator is itself unproven) by shifting the burden back onto A's positive "most programs" claim. A landed real points—cost leverage, beaver-dam analogs, the realism of the modal stem-counting program—but several key claims ("stalled patches need re-treatment less often," "most programs are standardized stem-counting") stayed assertion-level, and A's closing reply that B's evidentiary bar was one B could not clear failed because B never bore the burden of a counter-quotient. B made the debate easier to decide and answered more of the live pressure directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (B4.1-B4.4): B conceded no defensible "share" could be stated but flipped A's pressure question into a burden argument, then dismantled A's "area, not stems" carbon claim ("treated area is not delivered carbon") and the circular biodiversity denominator—directly neutralizing A's two strongest routes to victory.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins a competitive, high-level debate by dismantling the carbon-sequestration half of the conjunctive proposition and exposing systematic asymmetries in Side B's (PRO's) cost and comparator framing. While Side B built a persuasive ecological case for biodiversity leverage—especially via beaver-dam analogs, habitat mosaics, and the structural uniformity of young even-aged plantings—it never bridged the gap from lower per-hectare spending to higher carbon sequestered per dollar on ordinary mineral farmland. Side A cleanly showed that tillage has already ceased on abandoned land, that halting peat drainage is avoided emissions rather than sequestration on atypical hydrology, and that open grazed mosaics or fescue-stalled fields keep woody biomass low ("treated area is not delivered carbon"). Side A also persuasively exposed Side B's double standard of granting rewilding a full active toolkit (hydrology work, invasive control, native seed, fire, grazers) while reducing "most" reforestation programs to row-plantation caricatures, and caught Side B in the closing rounds misstating Side A's explicit inclusion of soil carbon.

After Side B claimed rewilding wins immediately on carbon by halting tillage and rewetting drained peat, Side A pointed out in [A2.2] that tillage has already stopped on abandoned cropland and that halting peat oxidation is avoided loss on special hydrology, not sequestration on ordinary mineral fields.
