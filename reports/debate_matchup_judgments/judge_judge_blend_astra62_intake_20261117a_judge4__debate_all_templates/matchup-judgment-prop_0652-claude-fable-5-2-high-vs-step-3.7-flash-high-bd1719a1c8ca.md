### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won the decisive clash by forcing Side B to admit that platforms would pay even without notice or knowledge. That concession made B's proposal a form of strict outcome-based liability, not the conditional accountability B claimed. A then used B's own phrase that AI infringement is "invisible at scale" to show the rule demands payment for what no one could foresee. A also exposed B's false equivalence between copying unprotected style and copyright infringement, and offered a more plausible incentive story: platforms facing damages for unpredictable future rulings will over-remove lawful content. B's fairness/profit argument was coherent but never recovered from the strict-liability admission, and its detection proposal depended on reference matching that B itself said fails for AI outputs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure question extracted B's admission that liability attaches without prior notice, and A then turned it into the central choice between knowledge-based liability and paying for verdicts no platform could predict.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by turning Side B's own concessions into the decision rule. A's pressure question forced B to admit liability attaches "even without prior notice" — strict liability — and A hammered that concession across Rebuttal 2 and Closing. A also exploited B's "invisible at scale" framing (if rights-holders can't spot it, hosts can't either) and B's own example of infringing "style," which is unprotected. B's tiered-review answer was undermined by A's point that existing matching systems rely on rights-holder reference files, the method B admitted fails for AI output. B kept the profit-follows-liability fairness frame alive but never resolved the central contradiction between invisible infringement and a workable detection duty. Both sides had clipped turns, a minor shared execution cost; A's clipped closings still completed the argumentative arc.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Question Q1 ("does a platform with no notice still pay?") plus A's Rebuttal 2, which welded B's "yes, liability attaches even without prior notice" [B4.1] to B's "invisible at scale" concession [B5.1] into a single decisive dilemma: the motion requires platforms to pay for what nobody could see.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by converting Side B's own admissions into a clear choice between knowledge-based liability and outcome-based strict liability. A showed that non-hash AI outputs, private licenses, and fair-use judgments make ex ante detection unreliable, so damages without notice rationally produce deletion rather than careful review. B never neutralized that incentive problem, and its claim that copying unprotected style is infringement further weakened its legal grounding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2, which answered the questions directly, pinned B to "liability attaches even without prior notice," and showed why tiered review still makes deletion the cheap bet.
