### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B more convincingly compared imperfect containment with the invasion’s mechanisms of regional destabilization. A raised genuine risks of eventual collapse and effectively questioned whether a fractured Iraq could block Iran, but never established that its predicted “two Syrias” alternative was likely or worse overall. Its closing also misrepresented B’s “even if” argument as an admission. B answered the containment pressure directly and better weighed demonstrated destabilization against speculative future dangers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B decisively challenged A’s claim that post-withdrawal chaos demonstrated the dangers of non-invasion: that Iraq already contained the institutional wreckage and militant networks generated by invasion. Temporary American restraint therefore did not establish that invading improved the overall counterfactual.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B built the cleaner causal comparison: state dismantling forged AQI/ISIS from disbanded forces and removed Iraq as a barrier to Iran. Side A's Syria counterfactual was inventive, but B repeatedly showed Syria was partly downstream of Iraq's collapse and that even a possible 2011 Iraqi crisis would avoid eight prior years of occupation, civil war, and cross-border contagion. A's attacks made the counterfactual plausible, not equally or more destabilizing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal decisively reversed A's Syria control case by arguing ISIS and Iraqi militias exported Iraq's collapse into Syria, while its second rebuttal preserved that edge with the even-if-2011 weighing.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won because it kept the debate tied to the motion’s counterfactual burden: it showed a plausible path where pre-2003 containment was ugly but comparatively stable, and where the invasion directly produced state collapse, jihadist fusion, and Iranian leverage. Side A’s Syria counterfactual was sharp and original, but B’s “even if Iraq later fractured” fallback blunted it: even granting a later collapse, the invasion added eight years of occupation, an incubated militant architecture, and earlier Iranian capture of Baghdad. A was rhetorically stronger and landed several good hits, but its alternative relied more on speculative future collapse, while B’s causal story remained more concrete and less fully neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B’s response to A’s claim that U.S. troops restrained spillover until 2011: B argued post-2011 Iraq was not an untouched baseline but invasion-created wreckage, preserving PRO’s core causal link between the invasion and later instability.
