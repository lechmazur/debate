### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B wins on the cleaner comparative path. B resolved the central “same paycheck” tension by distinguishing an already-sheltered family’s urgent near-term rent need from a marginal filer’s much smaller additional incentive after a multi-year payoff was already available. A’s memorable counterpunch was strong, but its fraud case overclassified merely weak or legally insufficient claims as fraudulent, and its aggregate-cost arithmetic assumed a long shelter stay for induced filers that B exposed as assumed rather than shown.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal that a truthful but legally insufficient claim is not fraud, and that treating every induced filer as two years of prohibitive shelter costs assumed the point, most damaged A’s core route.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won a high-quality exchange. CON's core symmetry mechanism — "money does not know who is holding it" — forced PRO to defend a paycheck that is decisive for a shelter family yet trivial for a marginal filer, and CON's reframing of the marginal filer as someone deterred by the no-income waiting period (an entry cost, not a total-payoff calculation) survived PRO's best counter. CON also landed the burden framing (the motion requires both halves), exploited PRO's A4.6 concession that induced filers exist, and turned PRO's own confound rebuttal against the 1990s history ("the system only worked when authorization was detached from filing"). PRO scored real points: the pressure question on what makes a truthful-but-insufficient claim fraudulent was sharp, and the closing exposed that CON's six-to-one arithmetic assumes an induced newcomer needs two full years of shelter despite being drawn by the chance to work — a hit CON never answered. But PRO's fiscal route alone cannot carry the motion; CON's fraud/inducement route remained the stronger live path, and CON needed only one. Slight rhetorical edge to B, whose closing crystallized the clash memorably.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 [B2.1-B2.2]: the observation that PRO cannot have a paycheck that is life-changing for the shelter family yet trivial for the marginal filer, combined with the turn that if "most of the payoff is already available," PRO's savings are small too. PRO's A4.1 answer (saved shelter nights are not available under the longer wait) partially repaired this, but B's entry-cost reframe in B2.3 kept the pressure alive through the closing.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by separating the certain benefit to families already in shelter from the narrow marginal group for whom four extra months, atop years of possible work, would tip them into deception. That threshold argument answered Side A's paradox, and B also exposed A's shelter arithmetic for assuming induced filers remain dependent for the whole backlog despite gaining work. A repeated the paradox and stretched fraud to cover truthful but legally weak claims without overcoming those points.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 and closing on the marginal-threshold point and the six-to-one arithmetic: the savings are earlier rent for an existing stock, while A's offset assumes both many threshold fraudsters and two years of shelter dependence for people supposedly drawn by earning rent.
