### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B built a much cleaner comparative path to victory across every round. First, Side B separated building arrival from compulsory instruction, showing that an 8:00-shift parent needs supervised drop-off rather than an entire student body sitting in first period, and candidly conceded that riders on an unmovable early bus may gain no sleep while proving that an early bell needlessly penalizes every walker, flexible-ride student, and movable-route rider too. Second, Side B explained why local discretion and trials fail: bus savings and using early classes as de facto supervision show up in school budgets while lost adolescent sleep does not, a structural incentive point Side A never neutralized. Finally, Side A undermined its own critique of unscientific cutoffs and unfunded mandates by proposing a binding, exemption-free 8:00 a.m. floor, then mischaracterized Side B's opening premise that 8:30 is a policy judgment informed by biology as a fatal late concession.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A proposed a "meaningful floor on extreme starts" in [A2.6], Side B's pressure questions ([B3.1]-[B3.2]) forced Side A in [A4.1]-[A4.2] to endorse an illustrative binding 8:00 a.m. floor with no financial exemptions. Side B then turned that answer in [B4.5] and [B5.1]-[B5.2], showing that Side A's alternative relies on the exact same "judgment informed by biology" Side A claimed invalidated 8:30 while still forcing students with an hour morning routine to wake at 7:00 a.m.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A better defended the motion as written: a binding 8:30 floor is a policy judgment informed by the adolescent sleep shift, needed to stop schools from setting an unreclaimable early alarm for everyone, while supervision and funding can protect parent schedules without making early instruction the default. Side B rightly stressed unequal benefits and real costs, but its 8:00 alternative and “concession” attack did not show why rejecting this floor better respects the science or solves the budget incentive to keep early bells.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and closing half-hour clash: an 8:00 start still steals sleep from students who could leave later and does not restore sleep to early-bus riders, while an adult on duty—not an early class—meets the parent’s actual need.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner path to victory by separating early arrival from compulsory instruction, candidly identifying which students would gain sleep, and framing 8:30 as a science-informed policy floor rather than a biological constant. B pressed the universal mandate’s costs effectively, but repeatedly treated A’s acknowledgment of policy judgment as fatal even though B’s own 8:00 floor required the same judgment. A’s final comparison—that supervision and transport can be funded or redesigned, while sleep lost to compulsory instruction cannot be returned—left the stronger answer to the motion’s explicit trade-off.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s pressure questions forced B to defend its alternative floor against the same arbitrariness and hardship objections. B answered directly, but A’s Rebuttal 2 then answered both sleep-mechanism and funding questions honestly and narrowed the clash to whether the extra half-hour justified separable logistical costs.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won solidly by conceding adolescent sleep biology upfront and severing a rigid 8:30 bell mandate from actual wake times. Side A showed that for fixed-shift families using supervised early drop-off or riding constrained bus routes, moving the bell can fail to buy extra morning sleep while delaying dismissal and breaking afternoon sibling care. Whenever Side B raised institutional objections—such as exhausted students having to petition school boards or officials making hollow claims—Side A neutralized them with automatic public wake-time verification, while respecting the motion's premise that genuine sleep gains justify harder schedules. The Pressure Questions exchange sealed the outcome: Side A forced damaging concessions that the floor neither guarantees later pickups ([B4.1]) nor is needed to spare students from filing complaints ([B4.2]), whereas Side B mistakenly aimed both of its own questions ([B3.1]-[B3.2]) against the rationale for an 8:30 minimum.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's logistical assurances across [A1.2], [A2.3], and [A3.1]-[A4.4]: after turning [B1.3]'s supervised early arrival into a zero-sleep, lost-sibling-care bind and exposing the clock-arithmetic impossibility of [B2.3]'s claim to start later without delaying dismissal or cutting minutes, Side A forced [B4.1] to concede that an 8:30 floor guarantees only a later class rather than a later pickup—then used that concession in [A4.4] to defeat [B4.3]'s unequal-resources argument because a bus-short district at 8:15 remains just as short of buses at 8:30.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing the live clash onto cases where an earlier bell moves pickups and secures the same sleep, attendance, and safety gains while preserving afternoon care. PRO conceded the floor does not itself guarantee later pickups and never supplied a principled reason to ban an evidence-equivalent 8:15 timetable. CON’s measurement-and-reopen alternative also answered the hollow-claims worry without requiring a student complaint, leaving PRO’s rigidity underjustified against the motion’s own “even when” costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s equal-gains comparison: if 8:15 moves the same pickups and preserves sibling care while outcomes match, the mandate forces families to surrender care for no added sleep protection, and PRO never neutralized that margin.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner path to victory by comparing feasible timetables and counting only the incremental benefit of 8:30 over an effective 8:15 redesign. B directly answered both pressure questions, proposed outcome-based district review without relying on family complaints, and exposed A’s concession that the floor guarantees a later class rather than a later pickup. A’s closing largely repeated institutional-duty claims without overcoming that comparison.
