### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO built a coherent comparative case on three grounds. First, cash makes citizens' benefit depend on the timing of commodity prices. Second, households cannot insure public services such as hospital payrolls through a slump. Third, children born after the resource runs out have a claim that private saving does not protect. PRO also seriously damaged CON's signature accountability argument. Identical cheques cannot reveal royalties understated before the division, and CON conceded this in A1. A missed fund deposit is a transaction, not a market return, which answered CON's line that "a ten percent fall and a ten percent theft look identical." PRO also neutralized CON's "just borrow" alternative by noting that borrowing is hardest exactly when the pledged revenue collapses. CON's strongest route was the weak-institutions frame. It exploited PRO's candid concessions well: "nothing guarantees that ward" and "where withdrawal rules hold." CON's closing line, that cash loses a cushion it never promised, was effective. However, CON's residual accountability claim, that a shortfall shows up as a smaller cheque, slid back toward the very point it had just conceded. CON also never fully answered why forfeiting the cushion outright is better than risking a raid on it. PRO's line "under immediate distribution, giving up that cushion is the policy" landed that point. CON also misreferenced IDs (P1.5, P2.3) and leaned on asserted costs without much development. PRO wins clearly but not by a wide margin, because the institutional-fragility objection retained real force.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over accountability. CON claimed cash puts "an auditor in every household" and that fund theft hides in market swings. PRO's Q1 forced CON to admit that comparing cheques cannot detect upstream understatement. PRO's A4.4 then showed that a missing deposit cannot be explained away by market moves. Together these hollowed out CON's main comparative advantage and left CON relying mainly on the institutional-fragility frame.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): B won a close but clear debate by re-anchoring the clash on institutional capacity. A made a strong principled case for stabilization and public services, and A's pressure on understated royalties weakened B's "auditor in every household" claim. But B conceded only the upstream disclosure point while preserving the downstream advantage: cash reaches identifiable hands under weak institutions, whereas a fund becomes a discretionary promise routed through the same treasury. A's admission that the fund cannot guarantee the ward's budget, and that it wins mainly where withdrawal rules hold, gave B a decisive comparative lever: in the very developing-country context the motion names, the fund's advantage is conditional while cash's direct transfer is more robust.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's use of A's no-guarantee concession: B showed that a fund does not pay nurses directly and only works where withdrawal rules and honest budgeting hold, which undercut A's central public-service argument and made the institutional weakness point dispositive.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A wins on the central comparison: household saving cannot insure shared services or give children born after depletion a claim, while a fund at least preserves fiscal capacity in a slump. B powerfully exposed weak-institution and fungibility risks, but conceded cash cannot reveal upstream understatement or guarantee a hospital payroll either, leaving A's smoothing and intergenerational route live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's Rebuttal 2 distinguishing a fund deposit from investment returns and household saving from insuring a staffed public service, which blunted B's market-masking and self-smoothing arguments.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): A built a clean, conventional case resting on intergenerational equity, commodity-price stabilization, and irreversibility. Those points stayed partly live through the end, helped by B's honest concessions that smoothing is the fund's strongest claim and that post-depletion budgets face a tighter constraint. B won the more decisive exchanges. - **Reframing consumption:** B recast household spending by credit-constrained families as investment, which undercut A's framing of dividends as mere "current consumption." - **Enforcement asymmetry:** B separated a checkable statutory dividend from discretionary relief that can be lawfully steered elsewhere. - **Pooled finance:** B noted that roads and grids need pooled revenue, not necessarily a sovereign wealth fund. This blunted A's scale argument. - **Option value:** B turned A's option-value claim by arguing that delay costs families irreversible opportunities too. A had real answers. Its point that governance risk cuts both ways was its best, and its closing claim that the windfall is the only capital available to build a nonresource economy was strong. However, A's Rebuttal 2 was clipped. It also claimed its questions "still stand unanswered" before B had its turn to answer them, and B then answered both directly. A's close is clean but repeats the irreversibility point B had already contested. B's progression was tighter and its framing more original and specific to developing countries. Close, but a real edge to B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The option-value clash decided it. A argued that a fund preserves flexible timing while distribution is irreversible. B answered that timing is adjustable for the treasury but not for a child facing a schooling deadline, so "the lost option runs both ways." A's closing reply, that a fund could pay her fees directly, was met by B's point that such spending drains the buffer and depends on official discretion she cannot legally enforce.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B wins a close but clear debate by reframing the windfall as an immediate, enforceable household entitlement in weak-governance developing countries. A's fund case on stabilization, intergenerational equity, and public capital stayed live, but B answered the hardest pressure with plausible mechanisms: roads and schools are financed from ordinary taxes, not the dividend, and tax/reallocation constraints are not the same as permanent loss. B also turned A's option-value argument against it: fund flexibility protects the treasury's timing, not a family's time-sensitive needs. A's clipped second rebuttal was a real execution cost, and its fallback that fund spending can mimic dividend relief weakened under B's enforcement distinction between a checkable statutory payment and discretionary relief.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's distinction that a denied statutory dividend gives an individual a missing-payment claim, while legally steered relief leaves an audit but no remedy, and that a fund can write next year's cheque but cannot buy back a child's lost school year, was the most decisive exchange.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly because stabilization and post-depletion management remain its live advantages: B conceded a dividend is not bust insurance and that post-boom budgets face a tighter constraint, while offering no pooled replacement asset. B's enforceable-entitlement and household-capital arguments seriously limit A's governance and appraisal claims, but do not solve aggregate volatility, public goods at scale, or wealth after extraction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The irreversibility exchange was decisive: B's claim that a lost school year is also irreversible sharply narrowed A's asymmetry, but B still could not explain how distributed cash recreates a stabilization buffer or pooled capital once reserves end.
