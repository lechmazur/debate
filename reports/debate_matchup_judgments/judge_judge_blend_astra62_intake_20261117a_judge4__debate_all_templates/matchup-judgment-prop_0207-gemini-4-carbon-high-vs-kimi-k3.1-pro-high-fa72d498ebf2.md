### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): PRO won because it kept the debate on comparative fiscal and protection outcomes: excluding affluent and smuggled fuel consumption funds broad cash, and phasing cuts to verified receipt answers CON’s implementation worries without making the motion an overnight shock. CON pressed a sharp savings/conditionality objection, but its best argument often treated any readiness condition as defeat rather than part of a phased reform. PRO’s “approximate cash beats an exact share of an empty pump” reframing was not neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON’s savings/trilemma pressure against PRO’s A1/A2 answers: PRO showed savings could come from cutting high-end and smuggled consumption and offered provisional walk-in cash, turning CON’s strongest objection into a comparative case for phased cash.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined and clash-centered, but Side A consistently converted B's abstract feasibility doubts into concrete comparative choices. A answered the pressure questions directly (A1/A2 format) with workable remedies—bridge financing, statutory walk-in desks, flat rural supplements—while B's answers were more deflationary. B's best line, the trilemma that generous, indexed, broad transfers recreate an uncapped liability, was reasonably defused by A's point that indexing a fixed household basket costs far less than subsidizing every liter for elites and smugglers. B's endgame rested on the lawyerly claim that A's "verified receipt before cuts" condition defeats the motion as written; A's rebuttal that "phase out" inherently commands sequenced transition is the more natural reading, and B never explained why indefinite "readiness" deferral beats reform before fiscal collapse forces brutal uncompensated cuts. B also suffered a real execution cost: the opening was clipped mid-sentence for exceeding the word limit. A's closing crystallized the weighing memorably ("approximate cash in a family's hand beats an exact share of an empty pump"), while B's closing mostly repeated the conditional-reform frame without new resolution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B2.3's trilemma (broad, generous, indexed transfers pull in opposite directions and recreate uncapped shock liability) against A4.1's answer that savings come from cutting top-end consumption and that a fixed indexed household basket is far cheaper than per-liter absorption of an oil shock. A's answer held, and B4.6's "savings remain an assertion" did not dislodge the plausible mechanism.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): PRO maintained the decisive fiscal comparison: subsidizing every litre for affluent drivers and smugglers costs more than indexed cash for a fixed household basket, so CON's affordability objection applies even more strongly to the status quo. ### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the debate on whether protection follows need or fuel consumption. B raised serious concerns about cascading inflation and targeting capacity, but A showed that smoothing, margin caps, and luxury levies are separable tools compatible with reform rather than defenses of the universal per-litre discount. A also answered implementation pressure more directly with phased, broad categorical enrolment, while B's crawling bands mostly delayed sustained pass-through rather than absorbed it. Despite A's clipped first rebuttal, the comparative path to targeted support for vulnerable households was cleaner after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's sustained-cost pressure: B's crawling bands only delay pass-through, and A then showed smoothing, margin caps, and luxury levies are compatible with targeted transfers, leaving B unable to defend the consumption-linked universal subsidy itself.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side A won the decisive framing clash: every tool B proposed (margin caps, smoothing funds, luxury-vehicle levies, crawling bands) is separable and compatible with targeted transfers, so the only thing B uniquely defended was the consumption-linked universal discount, which leaks to the affluent and delivers only unguaranteed pass-through to the carless poor. A answered B's sharpest pressure—administrative infeasibility in informal economies—with broad categorical enrolment, sequencing, and slower phase-ins where capacity is weak, and exposed the fiscal-contradiction attack as manufactured. B's counters ("abandons millions," "zero exclusion error," "levies recover leakage cleanly") leaned on overclaiming that A repeatedly punctured, and B never escaped the charge that its controls either pass costs through (no insulation) or suppress them (fiscal gap or shortages). A's clipped Rebuttal 1 and minor evasions on overnight shocks cost it some polish, but its closing crystallized the vote cleanly while B's relied on restating capacity skepticism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5) was the decisive move: demonstrating that B's entire toolkit—smoothing, margin caps, levies—can accompany PRO's reform, reducing B's unique case to the regressive universal discount itself, and forcing B into the retreat of defending "permanent broad price stability" with no financing story for sustained cost suppression.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side A wins by forcing a dilemma B never escapes: controls that cover costs must pass sustained increases through, while controls that suppress them shift the bill to the Treasury or create shortages. A also shows margin caps, smoothing, and luxury levies are separable from the universal per-litre discount. B lands strong pressure on informal-economy exclusion, timing lags, and stipend erosion, but A answers with phased enrolment, broad categories, and protection per scarce dollar for carless poor households.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's pressure on B's smoothing fund: once surpluses are exhausted, a sustained shock requires pass-through, public losses, or shortages; B's crawling-band answer mainly concedes gradual pass-through.
