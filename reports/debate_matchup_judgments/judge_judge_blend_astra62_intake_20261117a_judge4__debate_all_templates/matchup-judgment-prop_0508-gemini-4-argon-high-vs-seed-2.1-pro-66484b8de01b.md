### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B wins by making deliverability the live test and then owning it. A had a coherent diminishing-returns case, but its own examples and the claim that licensing is incumbent protectionism yet has no entrenched lobby were squarely damaged. Once A conceded outcome-equivalence and public-interest safeguards, B showed those tools hand the pen back to autonomous boards, that governments will not enforce recognition with bloc-wide retaliation, and that tariff cuts remain self-executing under central executive control. A’s closing mostly restated the lobby stalemate and did not neutralize that implementation gap. B’s nonexistent closing citations are a small execution cost, not enough to erase the clearer path to a no vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal exposed A’s lobby contradiction and demolished the electrician, plumber, and food-seller examples; the second rebuttal then turned A’s equivalence concession into a blank check for local boards and showed why market-access threats will not bind them.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): CON won the feasibility and enforceability clash. PRO showed remaining tariffs are politically protected, but CON's equivalence dilemma was never escaped: recognition either excludes lower-income members, accepts weaker enforcement, or lets autonomous boards use safeguards to recreate exams and audits. CON also persuasively contrasted self-executing tariff cuts controlled by executives with mutual-recognition pacts vulnerable to sub-national sabotage, making tariff priority the more deliverable use of negotiating capital.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 exposed PRO's lobby contradiction and turned the equivalence concession into an exclude-or-dilute-or-recreate-barriers trap, which A then sharpened in Rebuttal 2 against PRO's treaty-leverage answer.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the decisive clash about deliverability and enforcement. It convincingly contrasted tariff cuts as centralized, phased, and automatically enforceable at customs with mutual recognition as vulnerable to autonomous licensing boards, subjective safeguards, and development-gap problems. Side A had a strong political-economy critique of remaining tariff peaks, but left too much pressure unanswered on how recognition deals would bind subnational gatekeepers without recreating the barriers they target. B’s closing reference to nonexistent IDs was an execution cost, but not enough to overturn its clearer route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal directly answered the pressure questions by arguing national executives can credibly phase and trade tariff commitments, while cannot easily compel independent professional boards, making safeguards likely to recreate the very barriers PRO promised to remove.
