- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that Side A established a case for reform and reduced captivity, not the categorical loss of ethical justification. This exposed a gap in A’s necessity argument: proving that education serves humans, or that alternatives exist, does not by itself show that welfare-compatible display is unethical.

B’s best line (display need not add restriction; packages may not survive separation) stayed live and competitive, but the clipped opening and weaker proof that public cages remain required left A’s route clearer after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s R2 A1/A2: captivity can be justified for care/breeding without licensing public display; harmlessness is not permission when less-restrictive digital/habitat alternatives already supply the human ends.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side B made that implementation gap decisive through concrete conservation examples and strong risk weighing: an untested transition risks irreversible species loss, whereas zoos can be incrementally reformed.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s pressure question about an independently successful non-display replacement produced Side A’s candid “No.” B then used that concession effectively: A’s proposed funding mix remained a plausible blueprint rather than a demonstrated substitute for the existing conservation infrastructure.
