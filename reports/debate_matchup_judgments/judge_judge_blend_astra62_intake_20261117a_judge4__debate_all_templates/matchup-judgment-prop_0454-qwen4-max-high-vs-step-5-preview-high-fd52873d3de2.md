### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing PRO into a fiscal bind. The trigger's off-switch was the decisive issue: PRO ultimately conceded it would not cut checks while unemployment remained high, and since unemployment peaks after recovery begins, CON successfully turned the stabilizer into a standing claim that keeps borrowing into the expansion. CON also scored heavily on the cap debate by showing PRO's asserted cap was merely the pre-existing transfer architecture, not a financing limit added by the motion. PRO's delay-and-scarring case was real but weakened by CON's spring 2020 example and by the fact that existing unemployment insurance and short-time-work schemes already provide automatic speed without the contested expansion. PRO's denominator argument was rhetorically strong but insufficiently answered the supply-constraint inflation and foreign-currency borrowing risks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pincer on the off-switch was the most decisive exchange: if the trigger contracts while unemployment is high it recreates the scarring cutoff PRO condemns, and if it does not, the ratchet is real. PRO's answer accepted continued borrowing during high unemployment, which CON then converted into a lasting debt-service problem keyed to lagging unemployment.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): B wins narrowly through its scarring mechanism and direct explanation of automatic tapering. A established serious affordability concerns and challenged B’s exaggerated contrast between automatic relief and inevitable delay. However, A repeatedly treated the proposition as requiring uncapped borrowing and treated continued support during a weak labor-market recovery as evidence of permanence. Neither followed from B’s proposed rule. B’s response to foreign-currency financing stress remained insufficient: predictable commitments are not necessarily affordable. That surviving objection keeps the result close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s unemployment-based taper answered A’s claim that expansion is automatic but contraction requires a vote. Output recovering before employment does not establish an irreversible spending ratchet. A’s strongest surviving response was that bounded eligibility and predictable payments do not establish that a stressed treasury can finance them.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won by narrowing the motion to what it actually adds: a debt-financed trigger with no independent size or stop decision, not transfers as such. A separated speed from scale, trapped B on the off-switch, and showed B’s supposed cap was only the pre-existing system both sides already accept. B’s scarring and debt-ratio case stayed alive, but it never neutralized supply shocks, foreign-currency borrowers, or the fact that under the proposal more borrowing is the default.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s off-switch pincer, then A’s second rebuttal: B chose not to stop while unemployment stayed high and defined the ceiling as the architecture A already endorsed, leaving the motion as uncapped generosity on a lagging statistic.
