### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Both sides were strong, disciplined, and avoided fake sourcing. B's central move — treating A's A4.1 admission that approval can follow despite strong opposition as a damning concession — landed rhetorically but is weak as a blow, since that concession simply restates the motion; A defended it with a concrete procedural model (burden of justification, independent appeal, enforceable mitigation) that B's "decree" framing struggled to absorb. B's delay/backlash claim stayed asserted while A grounded the cost of waiting in a plausible curtailment mechanism. B also introduced a local-representative rejection power late in Rebuttal 2 that functionally neared the veto A had criticized, and A's closing [A5.4-5.5] cleanly turned it. B's strongest material — the distributional premium argument — was met squarely by A's mirror-image point that forcing burial shifts the premium onto other households, leaving the clash roughly even on that front but tilted overall by A's unanswered asymmetry and cleaner answers to pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 / Closing seized on A4.1 ("approve it despite strong opposition") as proof of the decree model, and Side A's Closing [A5.1-5.5] delivered the decisive counter: the valley's council speaks for one of three affected constituencies, and residents' identified harms can still stop a project — they just may not alone judge whether stopping it does greater harm elsewhere.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side A won clearly by building a disciplined, practical model and winning every major comparative clash. A also cleanly refuted B's claim that storage, market tweaks, or interconnectors can solve curtailment bottlenecks without siting new lines, a point B dropped entirely. Most decisively, A exposed the contradiction in B's claim to oppose a local veto while resisting approval whenever strong local opposition persists, showing that letting a transit valley's council reject a justified corridor allows one electorate to decide for upstream communities willing to host wind and downstream households paying for gas. B's warnings about litigation and political backlash kept the round competitive, but B never explained how empowering local councils to reject projects outright delivers infrastructure better than a regionally accountable process with merits review.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side B repeatedly insisted it did not favor a local veto ([B1.3], [B2.7]), Side A's first pressure question ([A3.1]) forced B to define what "surviving strong local objection" meant when residents do not change their minds.
