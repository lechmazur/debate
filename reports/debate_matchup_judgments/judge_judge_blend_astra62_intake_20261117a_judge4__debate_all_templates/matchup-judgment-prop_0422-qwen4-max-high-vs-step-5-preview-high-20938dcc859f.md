B made the debate easier to decide and owned the normative core of the motion; A's economic case was solid but never dismantled B's manufactured-default framing.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the instrument-design clash. A immediately spotted that PRO's B2.3 concession ("does not promise a five-seat adjacent run on a sold-out flight") narrowed the motion until its price clause either demands the impossible or duplicates an allocation duty. A's pressure question — name one family the fee ban seats that a duty wouldn't — forced B into the "toll booth" scenario, which A dismantled: on a half-empty flight adjacency exists so the duty already requires free allocation, and charging to comply is a breach, not a loophole. A's half-empty/full comparison in A4.4 gave the cleanest decision framework of the debate. B's equity and safety framing was genuinely strong rhetorically ("safety tax," "monetized anxiety"), and B answered pressure questions with proper A1/A2 structure, but B never resolved where the held-inventory cost lands, retreating to "carriers choose to punish families," which A rightly called out as denying that firms recover costs. Both sides argued the motion as written; A simply made the price clause look like it does no independent work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.4: A's answer to the toll-booth pressure — showing the manufactured-gap example is exactly where the allocation duty already works, then running the half-empty/full comparison showing PRO's ban delivers either nothing or a cheaper separated seat while A's duty delivers rebooking — was the decisive exchange.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): A narrowly wins despite weak answers on inventory costs. Its cost-shifting objection consequently also burdens its own policy. B delivered sharper enforcement rebuttals, but repeatedly overstated A’s rejection of a five-seat block as conceding that children could remain separated on full services. - **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by turning PRO's sold-out concession into a decisive comparison: where adjacency exists, an enforceable allocation duty already seats the child free; where it does not, the fee ban only makes separation cheaper while A's rebooking duty actually seats them together.
