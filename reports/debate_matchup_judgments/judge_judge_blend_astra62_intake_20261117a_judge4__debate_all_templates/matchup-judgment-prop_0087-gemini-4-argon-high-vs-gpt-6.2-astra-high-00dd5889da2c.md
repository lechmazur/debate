### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides were competent and disciplined, but Side A kept a live route to victory that Side B never fully closed. A's core distinction — that a product which delivers a loss, offers an immediate way to undo it, and makes hesitation costly creates a harm mechanism distinct from total-exposure arithmetic — survived B's attacks. B's strongest line, the "affordable regret" reframe (once uniform daily caps exist, speed rules only police sub-ceiling spending), was squarely rebutted by A5.1: a daily limit is a maximum, not consent to lose it all tonight, and caps don't answer "do I want another bet after this loss?" A also answered B's escalation pressure well (sequential fresh losses with expiring offers vs. concurrent pending bets; raising a stake is an extra decision). B's offshore argument was structurally clever (speed rules frustrate every player from the first wager, caps bind only near harm) but rested on unproven magnitudes that A reasonably contested. A's layered position — keep financial safeguards everywhere, add breathing room where the operator sells its absence — was coherent and motion-faithful, while B's case increasingly depended on assuming its own preferred policy was already adopted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's "affordable regret" framing in B4.3 versus A's closing rebuttal in A5.1 that a bettor who sets a daily loss limit has said "no more than this," not "I'm content to lose this much tonight." A's reply that caps and pauses answer different questions (how much vs. whether to continue after a fresh loss) neutralized B's best weighing move and preserved the distinct-harm claim at the heart of the motion.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained a distinct mechanism: rapid settlement creates a fresh loss, an immediate chance to undo it, and a deadline for deciding. CON's uniform caps were absorbed as a complementary ceiling rather than a substitute, especially through PRO's distinction between a maximum a bettor can afford and what they would choose to lose that evening. PRO also consistently answered substitution and offshore claims by noting that raising stakes or leaving licensed sites requires an additional, more conscious step—the same friction logic supporting pauses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 distinction between several pending bets and several sequentially settled losses, each followed by an offer to recover; it directly answered CON's claim that abundant fixtures and concurrent markets erase the speed distinction.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the motion central: it showed a distinct harm profile in rapid settlement and immediate repeat opportunities, and explained why uniform financial caps do not fully address the after-loss decision point. Side A’s uniform-regulation and offshore-risk counters were strong, but B’s concession-and-add structure, plus the clear “pause versus deadline” frame, made the proposition easier to accept.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 directly answered the pressure questions by conceding that caps are still needed while arguing that intervals protect the moment after a loss, when fast products otherwise turn hesitation into a missed chance to stop.
