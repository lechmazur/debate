### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B won by turning A's strongest concession into a timing and insolvency problem. A convincingly showed the bond cannot physically revive a dead satellite and raised real capital/correlation concerns, but B narrowed the clash to the living-spacecraft failure-to-dispose case and showed that licensing rules plus a flat levy become unenforceable or sunk exactly when end-of-life risk peaks. A's wealth-filter attack rested partly on the ambiguous phrase "class revenue capacity," while B clarified class- and risk-based pricing. B also gave clearer answers to both pressure questions: a bond schedule that avoids the false binary and a remediation fund that can sign removal tenders. A's alternative was plausible but did not fully solve insolvency or create the same conditional end-of-life incentive, so B's refundable-bond mechanism was the cleaner route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal and closing effectively converted A's public-fund concession into a comparison: a per-launch levy is sunk before retirement, while a deorbit bond keeps recoverable value alive until the spacecraft is safely retired, directly undercutting A's "zero capital cost" alternative.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won a closely fought, high-quality debate. B's core frame — a bond moves money, not spacecraft, so its reach is limited to a living satellite with an unwilling owner — survived the whole exchange and was sharpened each round. B's strongest materials: the "every patch is a licensing rule" observation, the correlated-risk argument that no surety writes thin-premium catastrophic exposure (which A's staged-tranche answer never really decorrelated), and the decisive catch that A's own pricing schedule used "class revenue capacity" — a wealth filter A then quietly dropped in closing, which B flagged. But A's sizing schedule and tender mechanism arrived late and introduced new vulnerabilities, and A's closing denial of wealth-based pricing sat uneasily beside its own Rebuttal 2. B's counter-advocacy (retire-on-redundancy-loss rules plus a per-launch levy funding a public remediation fund) was consistent from opening to close and absorbed A's best pressure. B made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2: answering A's pressure questions, B conceded the pooled cleanup fund, proposed funding it by levy instead of collateral, then caught A's "class revenue capacity" pricing as an admitted wealth filter and dismantled the staged-tranche decorrelation claim — the exchange that decided the sizing clash.
