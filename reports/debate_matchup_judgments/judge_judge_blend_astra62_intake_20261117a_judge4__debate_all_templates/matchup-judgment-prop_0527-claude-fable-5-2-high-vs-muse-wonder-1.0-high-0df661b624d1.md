### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B wins a competitive but clear debate. A built a serious case that a capped, centrally purchased high unit price can be stewardship-compatible, but B more decisively showed that the high per-course price leaves a marginal profit incentive and that A's safeguards either switch the price off or move rationing to the central gatekeeper. A's only named job for the high price—verified delivery—was answered by attaching delivery conditions to a flat availability fee. B also used A's own opening diagnosis against it: making revenue rise with each treated patient is the volume-linked flaw A initially condemned. A's final hypothetical that only a high-price deal may be signable was reasonably countered by B's point that permission itself weakens the buyer's leverage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal of A's "one indispensable job" was decisive: verified delivery can be secured through a delivery condition on an availability fee, so the high unit price is not needed and instead revives the volume incentive A diagnosed.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing a central contradiction in Side A's case: Side A diagnosed volume-linked revenue as the antibiotic market's fatal flaw, yet defended high unit prices that keep marginal revenue tied to volume. Side B cleanly distinguished ex post average cost from behavioral marginal price, showed that Side A's contract patches work by neutralizing the unit price, and demonstrated that delivery verification can be built into a delinked availability fee without per-course pricing. Crucially, Side B also answered Side A's "permission" framing by explaining that permitting high unit prices surrenders buyer leverage and crowds out delinked deals—a point Side A left unanswered in closing. Side A forced a concession on ward-level rationing and had cleaner length discipline than Side B, which incurred minor execution costs from three clipped endings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B2.2]-[B2.6] and [B4.3]-[B4.5], Side B dismantled Side A's claim that flat fees and high unit prices are equivalent accounting labels, separating average cost per patient from marginal unit price and showing that per-course payment either starves developers in quiet years or revives volume incentives, whereas quality-assured delivery can simply be a condition of a delinked contract.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins narrowly by keeping the motion’s permission framing central: a capped, centrally controlled high-price commitment can be preferable to risking no new antibiotic, and permitting it does not mandate abandoning delinkage. Side A’s delinkage critique was technically sharp, especially the marginal-price versus average-cost distinction, but B’s pragmatic insurance frame and bounded-exposure safeguards gave the cleaner route for the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The pressure-question exchange over what job a high unit price still does: A forced B to name one, and B’s performance-contingent delivery answer gave PRO a live closing lane, even though A’s delivery-condition rebuttal kept the clash very close.
