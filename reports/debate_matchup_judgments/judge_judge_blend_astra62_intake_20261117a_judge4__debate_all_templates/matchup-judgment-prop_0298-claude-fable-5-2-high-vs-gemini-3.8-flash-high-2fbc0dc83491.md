- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by turning CON's central objection: the state already holds these records, so state custody and political-capture risks exist either way. CON's own key example, Freedmen's Bureau indexing, depended on state preservation and nonprofit/volunteer labor, effectively conceding the market failure PRO identified. Despite some clipped turns, PRO's core arguments and answers remained intact.

It then turned CON's Freedmen's Bureau example against the market case: the records sat unindexed for fifty years until nonprofit and volunteer labor intervened, undermining the claim that private subscription capital drives recovery.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON built the cleaner path to victory. PRO's opening promised "fully open and searchable" public custody, but under pressure it retreated to redactions, mediation, tribal co-management, and community repositories — which CON correctly showed is closer to CON's plural ecosystem than to the motion's state-run digitization and access. CON's distinction between the state as funder/referee and the state as custodian was decisive and never squarely neutralized. PRO's strongest live point was market failure for unprofitable records, but CON answered it adequately with volunteer/nonprofit indexing, targeted grants with open-license conditions, and the point that a paywall is a price problem with a cheap fix. The DNA concession was also damaging: PRO admitted no genetic registry, so for the hardest cases the breakthrough tool stays private. PRO's closing repeated the credit-card framing rather than resolving the custody clash, while CON's closing crystallized the keys question and the retreats. CON wins clearly but competitively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 1 exposed PRO's retreat from "fully open and searchable" to "redactions, time-release thresholds, and mediated access," then showed PRO's revised model of treasury-funded community repositories is CON's own plural ecosystem — turning PRO's central case into a concession.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) controlled the decisive clash. Its funder-vs-custodian distinction absorbed Side B's best weapon (the "you conceded public funding" frame) while tracking and punishing B's serial retreats: "fully open and searchable" became redaction and mediation, the "public commons" became community repositories, and "no genetic registry is proposed" conceded the hardest cases to private tools. A answered both pressure questions directly (A1/A2) with the consent-and-exit asymmetry that B never squarely resolved. B's counter — the motion only forbids leaving recovery "mostly to private markets" — was live but undercut by B's repeated reduction of A's plural ecosystem (volunteers, churches, tribal archives, nonprofits) to "shareholder profit" after A had explicitly disclaimed that, plus the strong A answer that vouchers plus open-licence grants fix thin incentive where records are unprofitable. A loses some credit for three clipped speeches, but its shown material was consistently sharper and more adaptive. B was coherent and disciplined but repetitive on the paywall line.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5-A4.7): exposing that B's "no genetic registry" answer and "tribal co-management" retreat meant B had effectively adopted A's model — a treasury that pays while non-state actors hold and govern — crystallized by the closing line "I can accept compensation from the party that wronged me without handing it my keys."
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by pinning the motion to government custody and digitization rather than mere funding, then showing that Side B's privacy guardrails, tribal co-management, community repositories, and refusal of a genetic registry collapsed into the subsidized plural ecosystem A already offered. Market gaps were answerable by vouchers, open licences, volunteers, and targeted grants; distrust of the historical perpetrator as permanent key-holder was not. Clipping hurt A, but the visible clash still favored CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttals exposing B's retreat from a fully open public commons to mediated access and treasury-funded community holding, plus B's concession that no government genetic registry is proposed, so the hardest recoveries still depend on the private ecosystem the motion would displace.
