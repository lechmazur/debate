### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won by staying on the motion: keep dedicated controls for core functions rather than route them mainly through touchscreens. B showed a spring is not magically necessary and that glass can be given ridges and textures, but that answered a stricter hardware-purity claim. A turned those alternatives back: a safety-equivalent strip still freezes a dedicated place, so updates stop helping exactly where the screen matches a button, and B never showed that removing a well-placed defrost or hazard control makes the driver safer once voice and automation are already available.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal and closing absorbed B's capacitive and ridged-strip alternatives: texture can locate a control, but pre-activation discrimination and wrong-reach recovery still favor a dedicated switch, and a strip good enough to match defrost has already committed frozen panel space—the icon can move while the ridge stays put.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A built the clearer comparative case: voice, automation, and software updates can coexist with buttons, while tactile identification and recovery from a mistaken reach remain valuable during the residual manual emergency. B’s performance-standard critique was strong and exposed that mechanical springs are not intrinsically necessary, but B repeatedly treated the motion as a rigid hardware mandate and never established a meaningful safety gain from removal. A’s closing also turned B’s best alternative against its flexibility and panel-space advantages: a fixed, textured, dedicated control must sacrifice much of both to match a button.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The capacitive-control exchange was decisive. A directly conceded that an equivalent dedicated nonmechanical control could work, but separated that concession from routing functions mainly through a touchscreen. B supplied fixed ridges and textures as an answer, after which A showed that this alternative must also reserve fixed physical space and loses the easy relocatability B had touted.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A wins by equalizing voice, automation, and updates across both cars, then isolating the only disputed difference: a tactile, fixed control lets a driver identify and correct a reach before activating it when those systems fail.
