- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built a much cleaner and more convincing path to victory, despite incurring minor execution costs from end-of-turn word-limit clips in Turns 1 and 3. PRO decisively won the pressure exchange and comparative weighing, showing that base fares are also shifting live quotes, that CON's pre-payment countermodel still leaves rankings distorted by incomplete prices, and that a small, neutral, labelled approximation error beats a large, unlabelled error systematically skewed in the seller's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B4.1]-[B4.6], Side B directly neutralized Side A's pressure questions and burger analogy: B showed that base fares already fluctuate with live inventory just like lowest-available adjacent seat quotes, turned forced fare-class upgrades to seat a child into proof that the comparison works, dismantled the "fries cost extra" analogy because parents cannot skip sitting beside a six-year-old, and weighed small, neutral edge-case variance against the status quo's massive, self-serving omission.

A’s clipped turns were a minor execution cost.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger comparative case by proving that no uniform default reflects "the full practical cost" across diverse families and by winning the error-cost weighing when defaults prove sticky. Forced in Pressure Questions to define its basket, Side B specified one checked bag per passenger, paid adjacent seats, and no loyalty or card waivers—a formula Side A showed misranks options for huge shares of ordinary travelers, including families with toddlers sharing one or two suitcases, carry-on travelers, families with teens, and cardholders. Side A also won the decisive clash on what happens when rushed users never click: under Side A's bare-fare floor, bag and seat fees still surface before payment, whereas under Side B's padded default, families either buy unneeded bundles or choose a pricier all-in carrier and never realize they overpaid. Side A further showed that marking third-party listings "incomplete" when live seat-map data is missing unfairly demotes budget carriers. Side B argued cleanly and avoided Side A's repeated word-cap clipping at the end of all four substantive turns, an execution cost that narrows the margin, but Side A's core rebuttals were complete and decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's turn of Side B's behavioral anchoring and default-basket defense across [A2.4-A2.5], [A4.4], and [A5.1-A5.4]: Side A used Side B's own premise that rushed parents are anchored by initial sort orders—plus Side B's admission in [B4.2] that non-clicking users "buy a displayed bundle"—to show that a mandated four-bag default also pre-sorts the screen on a false assumption before users un-tick it, creating silent overpayment that never announces itself at checkout.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): A won narrowly by preserving the central distinction between a visible, purchasable, adjustable inclusion and an omitted fee that distorts the initial ranking. B posed the sharper objections about arbitrary baskets and sticky defaults, especially for families who never adjust them, but its checkout walk-away response addressed payment surprise more directly than comparison steering. A’s burden-shifting case therefore remained slightly more complete; B’s repeated clipping also cost some execution completeness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sticky-default pressure was the strongest attack, but A’s visible-inclusion versus hidden-omission answer, crystallized by “Beside is not inside the comparison,” best preserved its route to victory.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by building the cleaner live route: it defined the mandate as a labeled, adjustable family basket, answered variation and data-gap concerns with plausible mechanisms, and kept the focus on how base fares omit ordinary family costs before comparison.
