### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won by exposing a fatal flaw in A's verification mechanism: standardized data still comes from the untrusted platform, so it cannot verify the very underpayment or algorithmic bias A targets. A's flagship example of checking premium-tier streams against subscriber status actually requires independent ledger access, which B showed is an audit, not a formatted data share. A also conceded cross-platform comparability by saying creators do not need to compare platforms, weakening the rationale for "standardized" data. A's privacy threshold of 50 with national roll-up then withdrew the regional granularity promised to niche artists. B offered a concrete alternative—statutory, cheap, pooled audits—that directly addressed the verification need. A's closing was largely recap and did not resolve those concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's decisive turn was that A's own example of verifying premium-tier streams actually requires an independent auditor, because the platform asserts both the tier and the subscriber status. This converted A's strongest verification claim into support for B's audit-based alternative.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) controlled the decisive clashes throughout. The dilemma over "standardized" — either a mere file format preserving platform discretion, or shared definitions reaching into royalty accounting — was never resolved; PRO effectively conceded cross-platform comparability by arguing creators only need to verify each platform against its own rules. CON's verification argument (standardized self-reporting from an untrusted source cannot detect fraud it also authors) went unanswered: PRO's flagship premium-tier example was shown to require exactly the audit access PRO dismissed. CON also answered PRO's best pressure question (audit affordability) with concrete mechanisms — statutory cost-shifting, pooled audits via collecting societies, log retention — while PRO's aggregation-threshold answer conceded that the niche regional artists from its own opening get rolled up to national totals they already had. PRO's closing was largely recap with a fair but underdeveloped proactive-vs-reactive weighing point. CON's speeches progressed, narrowed the dispute, and crystallized cleanly ("a witness who cannot be cross-examined"; "fund the audits").
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4–A4.5: CON's demonstration that PRO's flagship premium-tier verification example requires cross-checking one platform-asserted column against another — something only an inside-the-ledger auditor can do — turning PRO's own answer into a description of CON's alternative.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won the decisive clashes: it forced a dilemma on “standardized” — either a mere format for still-unverified platform numbers or a redefinition of streams and tiers that reaches royalty math — and showed standardized self-reporting cannot verify its own source.
