### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined and clash-centered, but B did more to damage A's route to victory. B's core reduction argument—that "reputational fear" is the forecast of real losses (bank exits, network fines, advertiser flight), so the motion's "solely" qualifier either forbids nothing or compels carriage—put A on the defensive all round. A's A1 concession that identified bank pressure is "concrete commercial risk" outside the motion invited B's devastating follow-up that networks price categories, not creators, so the exemption swallows the rule. A's answers were honest and direct (proper A1/A2 compliance, real distinction between identified exposure and vague anticipated scandal), but each answer opened a further concession B exploited: "genuine route to be found, downloaded, and paid for" became mandated discoverability, and the blunt closing "the firm absorbs it, that is the price of doing business" let B reframe the whole motion as liability assignment plus stripped remedy. A's closing also slightly misattributed the indispensability/utility-regulation point, which was B's critique of A's own premise. B crystallized better ("Lawful is not the same as owed; the right to sell is not a right to be carried") and made the debate easier to decide. A held ground on targeted-controls alternatives and the infrastructure framing, keeping this competitive but clearly B's round.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1–B4.5, where B catalogued A's accumulating concessions—bank pressure allowed, age-gating allowed, search suppression allowed until it works—and argued that because counterparties price categories rather than individuals, PRO's carve-out licenses nearly every real refusal while forbidding only its honest description; B5.1 then weaponized A5.5's "the firm absorbs it" into the closing liability-assignment frame.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B offered the stronger comparison between creators’ access interests and intermediaries’ commercial exposure. Its pressure questions exposed uncertainty in A’s distinction between reputational forecasts and concrete commercial risk. A answered directly and retained a plausible infrastructure-based case, but did not adequately justify imposing the remaining costs on platforms. Its closing weakened that case by denying compelled association while explicitly assigning losses to firms, and by misrepresenting B’s utility-regulation argument. B overstated that the bank-risk exemption swallowed virtually every actual refusal and sometimes ignored that exemption when attacking A, keeping this a clear but competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing directly juxtaposed A’s claim of “no compelled association at all” with its admission that “the firm absorbs” the loss. This exposed an unresolved tradeoff: A could defend an access obligation, but could not convincingly portray that obligation as preserving unrestricted voluntary association.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the motion's key distinction collapse. Once Side B exempted a real bank or network threat as concrete risk, Side A showed that category-level pricing makes that exemption swallow nearly every actual refusal, while Side B's demand for a genuine paid, discoverable route plus "the firm absorbs the loss" is compelled underwriting, not voluntary association. Side B's access framing stayed live, but it never repaired that contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal and closing used B4.1 and B5.5 against Side B: the bank-threat carve-out licenses real categorical refusals, and assigning the residual loss to the firm is compulsion by another name.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) controlled the decisive clash from the start by conceding all measurable risks (chargebacks, fraud, moderation) and isolating "solely reputational" denial as the undefended residue. A's pressure questions were surgical: when B could not name a rail a lawful creator could actually use, and could not specify evidence separating "stakeholder consequences" from "vague disgust," A converted those silences into the case. B's best moment was the closing reframe that documented churn and partner withdrawal are genuine reputational risks even absent creator misconduct — a real counter to A's dichotomy — but A had already pre-empted this by arguing that documented consequences are precisely A's evidence-based rule, making B's evidence list an endorsement of "deny on evidence, never on nerves." B's "compulsory product mix" framing was persistent but never escaped A's age-gating/unlisted-distribution answer. B also incurred execution costs: both Rebuttal 2 and the Closing were clipped for exceeding the word limit, cutting off B's final weighing mid-argument. A's speeches progressed, narrowed the dispute, and ended with the cleanest reason to vote: recoverable platform error versus unrecoverable creator harm. B was competent and coherent but kept re-running the same lawfulness-versus-carriage reframe rather than resolving A's carve-out contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2, answering B's pressure questions with A1/A2 labels and then turning B's silence on the evidentiary threshold into the centerpiece: "vague disgust wearing a spreadsheet" and the observation that B's carve-out "permits everything it claims to forbid." B’s repeated legal-entitlement framing, weaker pressure questions, and clipped turns keep the result close.

This exposed the central weakness in A’s otherwise effective “deny on evidence, never on nerves” framing.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by keeping the motion’s “solely” and “fear” intact while Side A kept smuggling measurable costs back into a residual reputational case it had already said vague disgust cannot justify.
