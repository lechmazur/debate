### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A wins through more responsive comparison: transmission's deployment delays versus storage's earlier emissions benefits, and temporal shifting versus simultaneous imports. B retained a credible system-wide efficiency case, but did not adequately weigh lifetime benefits against the waiting period and repeatedly characterized storage as isolated and idle after direct challenges. A's sweeping weather claims, incomplete answer on rare-event storage sizing, and three clipped turns keep this competitive rather than decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A directly answered B's congestion question: storage can charge on the surplus side of an existing line and discharge when capacity becomes available. This materially weakened B's repeated claim that stranded renewables necessarily require new transmission before storage can help.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive exchange by making deployability the decision rule for “primary investment.” A had a coherent network theory—wires unlock many generators, markets share reserves, and storage cannot manufacture electrons—but conceded the decade lead time and that the binding fix is regulatory rather than capital. B used that concession, plus the same-moment versus earlier-surplus asymmetry and storage sited on the stranded side of congestion, to keep a live path that a transmission-first dollar may not be spendable when reliability and near-term decarbonization are tested. A’s daily-displacement and market-integration case stayed partly standing, and B’s three clipped turns narrowed the edge, but B answered the sharpest pressure more cleanly and gave the easier vote against the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s reply that a battery can sit where curtailed energy is already trapped, combined with the later use of A’s “reforms are not capital” line, most damaged A’s claim that the primary dollar should go to wires.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A built the sharper path to victory by distinguishing deployable storage investment from transmission’s regulatory delays and by framing the reliability asymmetry cleanly: wires require simultaneous surplus elsewhere, while storage can use earlier surplus. A also directly answered B’s congestion pressure by locating storage beside stranded generation. B retained a credible system-scale, high-utilization, cumulative-emissions case, but too often repeated the network-versus-node framing without neutralizing A’s timing and deliverability objections. A’s repeatedly clipped speeches prevent a larger margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest sequence exposed that planning and cost-allocation reforms are not themselves transmission capital, then answered B’s pressure question by explaining how source-side storage can charge from congested clean output and export when line capacity becomes available.
