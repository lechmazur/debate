### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): PRO opened with a vivid "scheduling as upsell" story. CON then controlled most of the clash. Its "a showtime lives in a room" and seat-neutral reshuffle arguments forced PRO to prove that reassigning slots adds access rather than moving the shortage around. CON also caught real tension in PRO's rebuttal: standard shows selling out versus "filler title or an empty house." In Rebuttal 2, CON further showed that PRO's demand-based trigger had drifted from counting screenings to counting sellouts. PRO answered this reasonably on distribution: a cheaper seat is a different good for a budget family, and that bears directly on the motion's concern with standard-price screenings. PRO's "fair share" stayed unquantified, though. CON's A2 concession that near-zero standard prime time would be dominance was a real opening for PRO, since the motion is conditional. PRO never exploited it in the closing and leaned instead on CON's "cheaper seats" admission. CON's closing line, "cheaper is not more," framed the remaining dispute more cleanly. This is a narrow but real CON edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A2 was the decisive exchange. It turned PRO's threshold answer against PRO: the sellout trigger contains no dominance clause, fires whenever cheap supply runs out first, and never fires in PRO's own example where premium sells out too. That reframed PRO's complaint as one about capacity rather than format dominance.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): B narrowly wins on the proposition’s distinction between total capacity and affordable access. A’s repeated objection that changing schedules adds no chairs does not establish that budget-constrained viewers gain nothing from additional standard-price opportunities. Its claimed cross-subsidy remained insufficiently developed to outweigh that access benefit. A nevertheless landed substantial pressure: B’s sellout-based trigger does not necessarily establish premium dominance, and B overstated cheaper seats as guaranteed additional admissions. Those weaknesses limit the margin, but do not defeat B’s narrower case against relegating standard screenings to unusable times.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s distinction between willingness to buy premium tickets and the exclusion of viewers unable to afford them directly undermined A’s reliance on premium sellouts. A’s strongest response exposed the overbreadth of B’s sellout trigger, but left that affordability distinction standing.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A won the central clash over whether reallocating a prime slot creates access. Its seat-scarcity and cross-subsidy arguments survived, while Side B's demand-based trigger was exposed as firing under ordinary scarcity and not when premium shows genuinely dominate and sell out. B made a strong price-access case, but never resolved A's typical-multiplex numbers or showed that conversion adds admissions for ordinary viewers rather than reshuffling a fixed shortage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: conceding cheaper seats but denying new admissions, then showing B's sellout trigger contains no dominance test and cannot fire in B's own premium-sellout scenario.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO wins because it set the decisive terms of the clash and then held them under pressure. Its core mechanism, a "price hike laundered through scheduling," was clear from the opening. Its "menu talking" rebuttal exposed a real circularity in CON's revealed-preference defense. When CON pressed for a number, PRO answered directly with an observable floor: two standard-price evening showings of the top title, every day of opening week. That neutralized CON's administrability attack. PRO's questions then forced CON into two answers that helped PRO: - **Grid conversion:** CON conceded that adding a standard showing means converting a premium slot. - **"Too few" test:** CON's definition required standard showings to exist and sell out. PRO's closing turned this effectively by showing the test goes quiet exactly when all standard showings are deleted. CON had real points: - the cross-subsidy and damage-relocation argument; - the redundant-or-binding dilemma; - the general administrability worry. However, CON never quantified what the floor would cost. PRO's fire-exit analogy defused the dilemma. CON also leaned on an unsupported assertion that standard auditoriums outnumber premium ones and described it as a PRO concession, which PRO never made. CON's argument against regulation drifted somewhat from the motion as written, and its clipped closing carries an execution cost. Overall, PRO's case was easier to decide on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO's Q2 asking what observation would count as "too few," followed by CON's answer and PRO's closing reply. CON's test required standard showings to exist and sell out. PRO showed that complete premium takeover, the exact harm the motion describes, would therefore never trigger CON's test, turning CON's own metric against it.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B better defended meaningful standard-price access rather than merely the possibility of attending later. Its constrained-menu argument undermined A’s reliance on premium sales, and it answered the demand for a concrete scheduling floor. A convincingly explained how strong premium demand could coexist with thin overall margins, but did not establish that lost screenings would outweigh restored evening access. B’s overstated closing attack on A’s threshold limits the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal was that premium sales cannot establish format preference when convenient standard options have been removed. A’s conversion response addressed empty premium screenings, but left unresolved the case where premium sells well while budget-constrained customers remain excluded.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B exposed the circularity in CON's demand test, turned the finite scheduling grid into clear exclusion at usable hours, and answered pressure with a countable two-evening-showing floor. CON's cross-subsidy and damage-relocation arguments remained competitive, but B's administrable test and fire-exit response made the case easier to apply and decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing showed CON's proposed proof of “too few”—standard shows selling out—requires standard showings to exist, so deleting them entirely would make the alleged violation undetectable.
