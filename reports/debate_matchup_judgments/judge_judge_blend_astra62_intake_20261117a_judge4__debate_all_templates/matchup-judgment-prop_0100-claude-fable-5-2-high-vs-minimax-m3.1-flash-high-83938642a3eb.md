### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) won on sharper clash control and better weighing. A consistently framed the decision rule — "can the thing called central actually be built in most countries, in time?" — and extracted damaging concessions: B admitted new reactors cannot replace coal this decade, that new projects can be uneconomic, and that the near-term case is fleet preservation. A's Q1 about the ~160 reactor-less countries forced B into defending institution-building "within a generation," which A persuasively reweighed as the entire decarbonization window. B's Q2 ("why is nuclear a necessary central strategy rather than a useful option for particular grids?") was a significant own-goal that A capitalized on repeatedly. B did land real substantive ground — the sufficiency gap (no grid has fully eliminated fossil), the geographic limits of hydro/geothermal/imports, and the 2030s firmness argument — and B4.3 was B's best moment, showing why the firm component might specifically need nuclear. But B's framing drifted toward "nuclear as one portfolio option," which is A's ground, and B's clipped closing added an execution cost. Both sides argued the motion as written with grounded, plausible claims and no fake sourcing; A simply made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.6): answering B's own pressure question by declaring "a useful option for particular grids" to be the proposition itself, then folding in B's accumulated concessions (uneconomic, cannot replace coal this decade, fleet-preservation focus) to show nothing buildable or timely remains for the reactor-less majority. B's attempted recovery in B4.4 ("conceding nuclear is optional concedes the motion") only deepened the confusion rather than repairing it.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A anchored the debate cleanly in the motion's exact burdens—whether nuclear is necessary as a "central" strategy in "most countries." Side A demonstrated that roughly 160 countries lack nuclear institutions, showed that capital-heavy reactors are poorly matched to occasional renewable lulls, and exploited Side B's own concessions that new builds can be "uneconomic," cannot displace coal this decade, and take "within a generation" to establish from scratch. While Side B raised credible grid-adequacy points regarding multi-day wind droughts and industrial winter peaks, it never bridged the gap from needing firm resources to needing nuclear at the center in a majority of nations, and it further damaged its case with a role-reversed Q2 [B3.2], a backwards reading of the motion in [B4.4], and a clipped closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A pressed Side B across [A2.2] and [A3.1] on what a "central" nuclear strategy actually means for the ~160 countries with no existing reactors to preserve, given Side B's admission that new builds can be "uneconomic." When Side B answered in [B4.1] that those nations can build reactors, regulators, fuel cycles, and waste programs "within a generation," Side A delivered the knockout in [A5.2]-[A5.3]: a generation is the entire decarbonization window, proving nuclear arrives too late and too expensively to be the centerpiece for most countries.
