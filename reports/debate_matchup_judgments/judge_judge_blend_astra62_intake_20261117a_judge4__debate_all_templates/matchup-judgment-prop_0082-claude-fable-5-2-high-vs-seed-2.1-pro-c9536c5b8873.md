### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A controlled the clash from the rebuttals onward. A's strongest route — the motion is conditional ("only when features undermine match quality and trust"), so B's examples of happy wide-pool users and willing boost buyers sit outside the proposition — was established early and never neutralized. A also landed two clean traps: B's own admission that firms can't self-enforce because limits "cut directly into their revenue" proved A's incentive-misalignment story, and B's endorsement of "clearly labeled paid visibility" conceded a limit on boosts, which A exploited in the closing. B's best material — the unmeasurability of "match quality," the hoarding-of-scarce-swipes mechanism, and the sharp "paternalism-tuned algorithm" reversal — was genuinely good, but A answered the measurement objection with the user's-own-stated-goal standard and the "dead match serves no one" line, which B never squarely rebutted (B4.1's broad-search reply conflates deliberate breadth with non-responding matches). B's fallback that labeling is "a narrow disclosure rule, not a hard cap" read as ad hoc redefinition. A's clipped turns (1, 3, 9) are minor execution costs since the visible substance was substantively complete, while B's turn structure was clean. B's closing mostly recapitulated rather than resolving the concession problem, leaving A with the clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / Closing concession trap: B4.1's "No, of course an app should adjust a feature that clearly fails its own users" was seized by A as a direct concession of the proposition, and B's only escape — redefining the motion as mandatory cross-platform caps — relied on language A convincingly showed was not in the motion text.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by pinning the motion’s actual conditional—“apps should limit features when they undermine quality and trust”—and showing Side A mostly attacked unstated blanket bans, uniform caps, and regulators. B extracted and used A’s concession that failing features should be adjusted, answered the measurement and suspicion pressures with user-stated goals and checkable public rules, and left A defending implementation worries rather than keeping harmful mechanics. Repeated clipping hurt B’s execution, but the visible case still gave the cleaner vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 and closing turned A’s “of course an app should adjust a feature that clearly fails its own users” into the proposition itself, while showing that A’s praise of labeling already accepts a limit and that incentive conflict was conceded.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A won by anchoring the debate to the motion’s conditional wording and making Side B defend features only after they have been shown to undermine match quality and trust. A’s user-goal standard, opacity-plus-checkout trust argument, and use of B’s own concessions gave it the cleaner live route. B raised real implementation and preference-diversity concerns, but too often attacked blanket bans or cross-platform rules not clearly required by the motion, and its fallback to transparency and competition was weakened by A’s incentive-conflict pressure. A’s repeated clipping is a modest execution cost, but it did not erase its substantive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal and closing turned Side B’s admission that apps should adjust clearly failing features into near-acceptance of the proposition, while also treating B’s support for labeled paid visibility as itself a limit, leaving B mostly with implementation worries.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) built the stronger path to victory despite three clipped turns. CON's core attack—that the motion's conditional "when they undermine match quality" standard is unworkable and, once PRO named a metric (share of matches leading to sustained conversation), that the remedy games the metric by shrinking the denominator—was the decisive live route that PRO never neutralized. CON also exposed that PRO's guardrail against reselling capped swipes is not in the proposition, and turned PRO's own pressure question about labeling into a dilemma (either opacity explains the cynicism, or only a ban cures it). PRO executed well format-wise, answered pressure directly with A1/A2 structure, and offered a plausible collective-action-trap answer to the "self-defeating vs. profitable" contradiction, but its core harms (decision fatigue, cynicism-driven misdirected swipes) stayed asserted rather than shown, and its late-added rulemaking apparatus widened the motion. CON's asymmetry framing ("a nudged user can still slow down; a capped user cannot") and the thin-pool volume concession gave the cleaner reason to vote. The repeated clipping of B's opening and both rebuttals is a real execution cost that keeps this from being a solid win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal: taking PRO's proposed quality metric and showing that capping swipes raises the ratio by reducing matches—"the test rewards scarcity, and the proposed remedy is scarcity"—then tying it to PRO's own admission that thin-pool users need volume on big apps.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A made the motion harder to adopt as written. “When they undermine” never got a non-gameable trigger: Side B’s conversation-share metric rewards fewer matches, and the rulemaking and anti-resale safeguards were added outside the proposition. Side B also conceded thin-pool users need volume, then proposed throttling the only apps that supply it. Design power and short-term incentives stayed live, but Side B’s closing restated a false contradiction instead of answering those pressures.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal: the proposed quality ratio rises by shrinking matches, thin-pool users need the volume a cap removes, and the ban on reselling capped swipes is not in the motion.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly because it gave the more workable account of the conditional motion: concrete platform metrics, a baseline/oversight answer, and an anti-resale guardrail, while explaining why design-shaped engagement can harm match quality and trust. B’s scarcity/denominator attack and thin-pool point were sharp, but repeated clipping and its reliance on labels/user choice left the trust harm and race-to-bottom pressure less fully answered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 directly answering B’s metric/who-decides pressure with concrete measures and guardrails was the key exchange; B’s denominator-gaming reply was incisive but did not completely defeat A’s multi-metric standard.
