### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger comparative case: local verification, existing-registry recovery, and physical-ID fallbacks directly challenged A's claims of inevitable surveillance and total lockout. B also explained why today's duplicated, reusable identity data is an important risk baseline. A raised genuine concerns about revocation delays and market-driven exclusion, but repeatedly asserted catastrophic inevitability without overcoming B's answers. B's clipped, authority-heavy opening and overstated guarantees limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's offline-verification versus instant-revocation challenge exposed an overstatement. However, B answered with bulk revocation lists and then weighed their update delays against the enduring usefulness of stolen static identifiers. A never established why imperfectly immediate revocation eliminates fraud benefits or necessarily requires individual tracking.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B wins the baseline comparison despite a clipped opening: today's static identifiers are copied widely, unrevocable, and overshared, while device-held, selectively disclosed, revocable credentials reduce that exposure. B also answers the recovery and surveillance pressures with existing registries, physical fallback, and bulk local revocation checks. A's market-exclusion and real-time-revocation tradeoffs are strong, but B shows the status quo already produces automated exclusion and permanent breach harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: bulk-downloaded revocation lists and local verification directly neutralized A's claimed phone-home contradiction, while recovery through pre-existing registries plus physical IDs answered the honeypot and lockout charges.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the comparative baseline clearer: today’s fragmented systems already spread static, unrevocable identifiers and exclude people lacking documents. A directly answered pressure on revocation and recovery with locally checked bulk revocation lists, device-bound keys, physical-ID fallback, and existing civil registries, while B’s “architectural impossibility” and honeypot claims remained overbroad after those answers. B’s market-coercion and digital-divide points stayed live, but A gave more concrete mitigation and a stronger net-benefit story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s pressure question and Rebuttal 2 response on revocation: bulk public revocation lists allow status checks without individualized “phone home” logs, blunting B’s core surveillance critique while preserving fraud mitigation.
