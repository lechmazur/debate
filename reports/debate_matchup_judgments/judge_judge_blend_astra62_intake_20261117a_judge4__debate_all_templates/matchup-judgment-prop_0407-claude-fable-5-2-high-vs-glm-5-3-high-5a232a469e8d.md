### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won a high-level debate by prevailing on both practical workability and the normative core of power asymmetry. When Side B warned that an open-ended conduct rule would turn every firing into costly jury second-guessing over grey-area harms like "poisoning the team," Side A showed that genuine team breakdown leaves present-tense evidence (such as complaints or transfer requests) with the burden on the plaintiff, and cited existing general off-duty statutes in Colorado and North Dakota—forcing Side B to retreat from predicting massive litigation volume to claiming undetectable marginal chill. Side B's sharp argument that litigation taxes candid grey-area employers more than pretexters, alongside two minor word-limit clippings for Side A, kept the margin competitive.

- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by narrowing the residual category to pure personal disapproval and showing that Side B's own harm examples already fall inside the proposition's job-related exception. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by proving that the motion's "materially affect job performance" standard cannot hold a stable line between harmless private conduct and workplace-poisoning bigotry. Whenever Side A read materiality broadly to include coworker trust, client confidence, or institutional mission, it reopened a lawful excuse for firing sympathetic workers when customers object; whenever Side A narrowed the rule to core job duties, it protected non-supervisory bigots—a counterexample Side A left unanswered. Side A argued persuasively on ex ante deterrence and how categorical statutes leave unenumerated activities on a legislative "waiting list," but Side B's relentless tracking of Side A's shifting test decided the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's sequence in [B4.5]-[B4.11] and [B5.2]-[B5.3] exposing Side A's shifting standard: after Side A narrowed its test in [A4.2] to whether conduct contradicts a role's core function, Side B showed that rule protects both a hospital linen aide who mocks disabled people online and a misogynist coworker without direct reports, then dismantled Side A's late pivot to "institutional function" [A5.3] by showing the exact same logic lets a bar fire a political canvasser who alienates regulars.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won because it made the motion's own line unworkable and kept that clash live through the close. Side A never stabilized a rule that fires the bigot and saves the canvasser: performance became trust, then core-function contradiction, then institutional purpose, and each repair either swallowed the sympathetic cases or left the misogynist-without-reports and hospital aide unanswered. Side B also inverted the customer-veto point: a named protection makes prejudiced discomfort no defense, while Side A's materiality exception turns it into a lawful reason and leaves the worker with a trial. Side A's moral case and gap critique were real, but Side B answered the waiting-list objection by noting both remedies need legislation and that a named political-activity statute protects the canvasser more cleanly. The complete closing crystallized that choice; Side A's clipped close did not neutralize it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing demolition of the "hospital function" move: the same institutional-purpose reasoning that finally sinks the aide also lets the bar fire the canvasser for alienating regulars, so Side A still cannot hold the line the motion requires.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): A won a clear but competitive debate. B built a strong justification-and-power-asymmetry case and answered its pressure questions directly, but A turned the hard examples into a durable dilemma. A’s targeted-protection alternative also answered the enactment-delay and prejudiced-customer pressures more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal and closing traced B’s standard from job performance to trust to core function, then showed that the hospital-aide rationale could swallow the bartender’s protection.
