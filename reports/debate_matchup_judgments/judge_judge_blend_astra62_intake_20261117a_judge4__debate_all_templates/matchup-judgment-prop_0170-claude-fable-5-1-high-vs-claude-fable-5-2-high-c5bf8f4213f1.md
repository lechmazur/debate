### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won the definitional battleground on "primarily." B's clever escape in B4.1 — that allocation is a rule, not capital, so the motion's capital must flow to desalination — was directly refuted in A4.6, which pointed out that lined canals, drip systems, meters, treatment plants, and reuse networks are billion-dollar infrastructure, not legislation. B never answered that. B also accumulated damaging concessions: farms (most of the water) are fixed by demand management, leaking cities get "pipes first," and inland regions are beyond desalination's reach. A's closing crystallized the clash cleanly: B proved desalination is necessary, never that it is primary, and the ratio (efficiency) determines how much expensive seawater a region must buy. B's strongest surviving route — desalination as the only new input, with reuse merely multiplying existing supply — was well-argued and vivid ("cannot multiply zero"), but A's drought-year farm transfers and pre-built, right-sized supplement blunted its decisiveness. Both sides showed excellent epistemic discipline with plausible hedged examples (Israel, Cape Town, Perth, Australia) and no fake sourcing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A3.2/B4.3/A4.6: A's pressure question forced B to concede "pipes first" for leaking cities, and when B reframed demand management as legislation rather than capital investment, A4.6 demolished that by listing the billions in efficiency infrastructure, locking in the concession that most water problems are solved by B's own admission with A's tools.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B more convincingly distinguishes necessary desalination from primary investment. Its agricultural-demand, infrastructure-cost, and plant-sizing arguments undermine A's claim that conservation needs little capital and soon exhausts its usefulness. Accepting prebuilt supplemental desalination also answers A's emergency-backup challenge. A sharply exposes reuse's dependence on incoming water, but does not establish why that limitation warrants directing most investment toward desalination across the regions covered by the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's “I built that plant too” neutralizes A's portrayal of conservation-first policy as having no drought backup. Its closing then grants A's strongest point—that desalination adds independent supply—while showing that necessity does not establish primacy. This resolves the central clash more effectively than A's repeated exhaustion scenario.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B won the central primary-versus-supplement clash. A powerfully argued that only desalination creates new, rain-independent water, but conceded farms are fixed by allocation and leaking cities need pipes first. B converted those concessions into a sizing argument: conservation and reuse determine the shortfall and therefore how much expensive desalination is needed. B also answered the Cape Town and reuse pressure directly, while A's claim that allocation is merely a cheap rule was undercut by the capital cost of canals, meters, drip systems, and reuse infrastructure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered both questions, treated wastewater and farm savings as drought-resilient supply, and reframed A's slow-and-expensive argument as proof desalination is insurance sized by efficiency, not the primary strategy.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a close, high-quality debate. A (CON) landed real hits: the "primarily" definitional fight exposed B's capital-share metric as one where the dearest water wins automatically, and the Sahel/inland-India burden forced B into a partial concession ("the motion bites less"). But B's core route survived every exchange: all of A's portfolio — reuse, storage, reallocation, disciplined demand — is a bet on rain that already fell, while desalination is the only rainfall-uncorrelated source. The decisive turn came when A conceded a coastal plant "sized for the deficit" [A4.2] and B's closing converted that into "large-scale desalination under a more modest name" [B5.3], showing both plans ultimately rest on B's supply. A's closing was also clipped mid-sentence, an execution cost that cut off the final crystallization. B answered both pressure questions cleanly (brackish groundwater, coastal cities freeing inland sources) and never let the worst-year metric slip. A's strongest answer — rivers fail because agriculture drained them, so honest pricing preserves a multi-year buffer — was good but only slowed B's "buffer is a countdown" frame rather than breaking it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing: B reframed A's entire portfolio as "four bets on the same weather" [B4.6], then in the closing turned A's own concession of a deficit-sized coastal plant [A4.2] into proof that "a plant sized for that honestly, and built years ahead... is large-scale desalination under a more modest name" [B5.3]. A's "rigged scale" counter [A4.3] was A's best moment of the debate and kept the "primarily" clash alive, but the clipped closing left it under-resolved.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B wins on the breadth of the investment prescription. A correctly distinguished primary spending from doing efficiency first, weakening B's repeated sequencing objection. But B's agricultural-demand and inland-delivery challenges remained live: A chiefly justified desalination for already-efficient coastal systems facing exhausted buffers, not its general investment priority. B's overstated assurances about stored water and its clipped closing keep this a competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's geographic pressure forced A to acknowledge that the motion “bites less” inland; invoking brackish groundwater did not establish scalable, affordable coverage. A's “four bets on the same weather” effectively exposed the limits of storage and reuse, but demonstrated desalination's complementary value more clearly than its claim to primary investment.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won the live clash over “primarily”: A conceded efficiency comes first, redefined primacy as bulk capital, and admitted the motion “bites less” inland. B showed cheap agricultural and demand savings remain large, persistent, geographically broader, and that desalination works best as a marginal coastal layer after discipline. A’s rainfall-independent worst-year argument was strong, but B absorbed it with buffers, reuse, and a deficit-sized plant without conceding primary investment.
