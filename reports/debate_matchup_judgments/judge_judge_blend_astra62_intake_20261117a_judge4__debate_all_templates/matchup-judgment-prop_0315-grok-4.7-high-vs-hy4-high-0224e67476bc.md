### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined, avoided fake precision, and engaged closely, but PRO built the cleaner path to victory. PRO's central claim was that legitimacy for a title-deciding review depends on the angle used, the evidence considered, and how the standard was applied. CON's alternative of season-long rulebook comparison cannot reveal those facts for a single decisive call. CON's answer to Q1 asserted that the published rule plus the broadcast call is enough. That claim was strained for judgment-laden thresholds like "clear and obvious," where the basis of the decision is exactly what is in dispute. CON's best material was genuinely sharp: the chilling effect on candid deliberation, weaponization of the official's own voice, and "reconciliation is authorship." Yet PRO answered each of these. Raw audio is not authored after the fact and serves as a check on the explanation. Hesitation can show proper use of a certainty threshold. Partisans already weaponize freeze-frames. CON never fully explained why a noisier but checkable record is worse than an uncheckable one at the highest stakes. PRO also used its pressure questions more effectively and gave more direct A1/A2 answers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 and A4.3 landed hardest. They pressed CON's "independent comparison of calls to the rulebook" alternative and showed that outcome auditing flags inconsistency but cannot reveal the interpretation actually used in a specific major review. That left CON defending pattern correction as a substitute for justifying the decisive call. CON's strongest counter was B4.5, which argued that reconciling hesitation with the note is authorship rather than inspection. It was clever but only partially blunted PRO's point that the unedited audio sits alongside the explanation as an independent check.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): A made checkability, rather than universal fan acceptance, the persuasive standard. It explained why comparing outcomes with the rulebook cannot fully reveal the evidence and interpretation behind a particular review. B raised credible risks to candor and referee safety, which A did not eliminate, but relied too heavily on treating public misunderstanding as inevitable and explanations as inherently unauditable. A answered the central pressure more directly and sharpened the comparison across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A challenged B's raw-deliberation-versus-polished-narrative dilemma by showing how audio, explanations, and published standards can constrain one another. B's response that reconciliation is merely authorship did not establish why an imperfect, challengeable record is no better than withholding the decision's basis.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A wins because its core distinction survives: season-long outcome comparison can detect drift, but cannot justify a single title-deciding review where legality depends on the angle, evidence, and threshold actually applied. A also answers the chilling and clip-weaponization claims by reframing hesitation as potentially disciplined use of the standard and by making the whole contemporaneous record, not silence, the check on a later explanation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's Rebuttal 2 and closing turn on B's alternative: independent rulebook comparison flags inconsistent outcomes without revealing the interpretation used in the decisive call, leaving B's accountability route unable to answer the motion's central legitimacy problem.
