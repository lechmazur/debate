### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by turning the debate from cost-per-mission into whether reallocated dollars would survive the political system. While PRO framed current crewed spending as guaranteed waste, CON’s fixed-cost and budget-anchor argument made the proposal look like an untried gamble. PRO’s closing repeated the spending/lobbying distinction, but CON defused it as evasion and gave the cleaner comparative reason: more science per dollar matters less if fewer dollars remain.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON’s exploitation of PRO’s admitted lack of any precedent for a successful crewed-to-science budget shift, paired with the post-Apollo example where money left rather than migrated to probes.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): This was a high-quality, closely fought debate. Side A (CON) built a genuinely strong structural case: budgets are politically anchored by astronauts, the fixed-cost line kills rather than trims a crewed program, and Hubble repair plus the commercial-launch ecosystem show crewed dividends. But Side B (PRO) never lost control of the motion's stated metric, rebutted cleanly at every turn, and won the key meta-clash over whose burden it was. PRO's sharpest moves: turning CON's fixed-cost point into "that is my case in a sentence" [B2.7], drawing the crucial distinction between post-Apollo topline cuts and a deliberate reallocation ("absence of a trial is not evidence of failure" [B4.3]), and delivering the cleanest weighing of the debate in [B4.7] — certain, recurring billions spent on life support versus a speculative political penalty.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON's pressure question demanding one precedent of crewed cuts producing science gains [A3.2]. PRO's answer — admitting no precedent exists but reframing it as "no reallocation experiment that failed" and noting science thrived as the junior partner [B4.3-B4.4] — blunted CON's strongest historical argument, and PRO's closing then weaponized CON's own concession that crewed Mars sample return would cost "hundreds of billions" and take decades, reducing CON's case to "crewed flight is worth funding because it attracts funding."
