- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO, Side B, built an intuitive causal story. That carried real weight on whether development worsened. The motion, however, also requires that the harm be greater than employers and workers usually admit. CON, Side A, attacked that clause relentlessly and effectively: - Return-to-office mandates justified by junior mentorship are themselves employer admissions. - Onboarding overhauls and coaching requests in pulse surveys show that workers and firms openly name the problem. PRO's reply was to distinguish surface acknowledgment from confession of deep damage. CON then turned this into a credible unfalsifiability charge (A4.4): every form of admission gets relabeled as a "band-aid" or a "compromise." PRO's contradiction argument (B4.3), that retreats and overhauls prove the damage, cut both ways, because it conceded that firms visibly treat the problem as serious. CON also contested the harm itself: the "office as camouflage" reframe and written critique as judgment-building. PRO punctured part of this by mocking the mid-call side-chat scenario as fantasy multitasking, which was a fair hit. Even so, CON kept the cleaner route to victory on the admission clause. PRO also cited nonexistent reference IDs ([C1.3], [C2.3], [P1.3], [P1.4]), a small execution and grounding cost.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B wins a competitive debate by building the more realistic causal story and pressing it consistently. The office made micro-coaching nearly effortless. Remote work turns coaching into a deliberate chore. Busy managers therefore default to trusted veterans and give juniors cleanly scoped work. A had the stronger angle on the "usually admit" clause, pointing out that executives loudly invoke mentorship and that B's "mutual denial" framing risks being unfalsifiable. B, however, answered A's pressure question concretely: honest admission means accepting real trade-offs, such as funding coaching or synchronizing senior and junior attendance. That kept B's admission claim alive. On the harm itself, A increasingly relied on idealized "mature remote workflows": five juniors silently shadowing negotiations, staying on the line after calls, shaping strategy drafts "by rule." These describe what good remote practice could look like, not what typically happens. B's closing exploited this, arguing that A's model depends on the same busy, unincentivized managers both sides had already described. A's attack on the romanticized office was real but mostly showed that offices were imperfect, not that remote work is no worse. A also overclaimed a "collapse" of B's case from a routine point about busy managers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came in B4.3–B4.4 and B5.1. A argued in A3.2 that the bottleneck was unincentivized management rather than remote work. B turned this around: managers were always busy, and offices worked anyway because coaching cost them almost nothing. Remote work raises that cost, so unincentivized managers stop coaching. This undercut A's later reliance on deliberate "intentionality" and private huddles. A's reply about structural defaults "by rule" asserted ideal practice without showing it typically happens.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Both sides clashed directly and consistently. Side A's best route was the "admission" prong: junior-development anxiety is loudly discussed, not hidden, and A's unfalsifiability charge against B's "mutual denial" was sharp. B defused it by defining honest admission as owning a real trade-off (cutting output to fund coaching, synchronizing hybrid days) rather than mere talk. On the causal clash, A's positive case leaned on idealized "mature remote workflows" with rubrics, rule-based shadowing, and strategy memos, while B's friction thesis (questions cost status, coaching becomes a deliberate chore, archives sanitize candor, managers route stretch work to trusted veterans) stayed grounded in mundane, plausible behavior. B's pressure Q1 exposed a real tension—private candid huddles reintroduce the favoritism A condemned—and B's closing crystallized the sharpest contradiction: A concedes managers are unincentivized yet relies on deliberate intentionality. A's structural-defaults answer was coherent but asserted rather than demonstrated. Narrow edge to B on grounding and late-stage weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B3.1/Q1 forcing A to defend private unrecorded huddles, followed by B5.1's "clicking an invite takes seconds; remembering to include an unseen screen name is the real barrier," which put A's intentionality answer under sustained, precise pressure across the final two speeches.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B won narrowly. Side A seriously challenged the under-admission claim and offered plausible remote alternatives, but its case increasingly relied on mature workflows, systematic shadowing, and candid private coaching operating as intended. B kept the comparison anchored in ordinary managerial incentives: remote work makes coaching deliberate, archives sanitize judgment, and routine output conceals lost sponsorship. B also answered both pressure questions concretely, while its own questions exposed the tension in A's scalable-archives-plus-private-huddles model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it addressed the falsifiability and management-versus-remote challenges directly, then showed why private huddles can restore exclusion while sacrificing the scalability A claimed.
