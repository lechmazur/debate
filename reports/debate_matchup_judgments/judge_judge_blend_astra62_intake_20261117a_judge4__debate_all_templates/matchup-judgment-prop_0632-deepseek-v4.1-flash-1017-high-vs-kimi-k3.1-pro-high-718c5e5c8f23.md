### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Both sides debated the actual motion with discipline. CON (A) built a coherent leverage story: a mandate removes the class-level exit threat, outcomes contracts cannot bound volume or entry costs, and premiums rise now while offsets are distant and speculative. But PRO (B) consistently met that pressure: insurers already negotiate covered drugs through formularies, rebates, and prior authorization; value-based pricing addresses current net spending rather than relying on speculative savings; and the closing retort that "a veto is not a value assessment" plus the asymmetry argument (CON demands near-term savings proof from obesity drugs while imposing no value test on complication-driven spending it continues to finance) landed squarely and was never fully neutralized. A's answer that this "argues for better value tests across care" was a partial dodge of a live double standard. B also answered both pressure questions directly and honestly (required coverage survives overrun; clinically indicated = clinician judgment within authorized population plus reviewable criteria), while A's rebuttals, though crisp, leaned on repeating the leverage claim. B's framing was clearer and more memorable without sacrificing substance, giving PRO the cleaner route to victory on a genuinely contested motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing: answering A's solvency question by conceding that "required" survives overrun (insurance always pools costs) while redirecting to price, eligibility, and performance controls—then crystallizing that CON finances downstream complications with no comparable value test while demanding near-term savings proof only from obesity drugs.
