- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): B offered the stronger comparison: controlled access and tailored safeguards can preserve heritage without broad institutional immunity. Nevertheless, A did not establish why its exemption outperformed the narrower alternative. B's overstated legal assurances and unsupported catalog-scale claims keep the win competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B answered the inaccessible-archive objection with authenticated research access, then addressed liability fears through reasonable-search standards and damage caps. B’s controlled-access and targeted-safe-harbor alternative left a cleaner reason to reject a blanket exemption.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s rebuttal that preservation is not worldwide publishing, anchored by the Internet Archive’s in-print book dispute rather than Geocities, then pressed in Rebuttal 2: A’s commercial-work carveout concedes that enforcement must remain.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: after B limited the exemption to works not sold, licensed, or actively stewarded, A argued that this conceded the need for traditional enforcement and left self-appointed libraries to police the boundary, reinforcing the point with the Internet Archive’s in-print book dispute.
