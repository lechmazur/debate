- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A won a competitive, well-argued debate by establishing the clearer economic and investigative case for treating shared rent-setting algorithms as functional coordination rather than unilateral software use. ### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won a clear but competitive debate. B's central strategy—forcing PRO to narrow the trigger from "shared algorithmic use" to "pooled confidential competitor data, aligned recommendations, and suppressed discounting," then showing that narrowed version is exactly what evidence-led enforcement already reaches—was executed consistently across all five speeches and never squarely neutralized. A's best route was the "loophole/timing" argument (colluders routing coordination through vendors to delay oversight), but B answered it directly: anonymization labels are claims to test, early investigation doesn't require a confession, and the motion adds presumption rather than reach. A answered both pressure questions with the required A1/A2 format but effectively conceded B's framing each time. B also handled A's two pressure questions cleanly (harm identifies what to examine, not who agreed; labels are not legal shields). A retained a live argument on scrutiny thresholds and delivered strong rhetoric ("free pass for collusion by code"), keeping the margin modest, but B made the debate easier to decide and landed the cleaner comparative case ("the algorithm can be the smoking gun; sharing it is not smoke"). Both sides showed good epistemic discipline—plausible mechanisms and hypotheticals without fake citations or brittle precision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.4-B4.7]: after A's A1 answer defined the trigger as pooled confidential data, aligned recommendations, and suppressed discounting, B exposed this as a retreat from the categorical motion, argued those features already justify early scrutiny without the "as if" presumption, and crystallized the choice as "targeted inquiry versus technological association"—the decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won solidly by exposing a decisive mismatch between the categorical motion and Side A's narrower case.
