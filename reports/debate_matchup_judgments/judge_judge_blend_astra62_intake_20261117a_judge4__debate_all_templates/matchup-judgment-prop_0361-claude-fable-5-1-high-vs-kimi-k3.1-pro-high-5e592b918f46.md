### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): A won narrowly by defending a coherent presumption rather than an absolute rule and by showing that sequential release can capture theatrical revenue, publicity, rentals, and eventual streaming value. B's access case was strong, but largely established a temporary cost. Its central empty-or-binding dilemma overstated A's concession, while its claim that streaming captures the whole dollar never adequately resolved the incremental subscriber-value and churn problem. B's repeated clipping also imposed a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's response that a flexible presumption can still constrain decisions was decisive: under uncertainty, subscriber growth alone does not justify irreversibly forfeiting theatrical and rental value. B memorably called this mere good analysis, but did not establish that studios already make the comprehensive comparison or that early streaming's marginal subscriber value exceeds sequenced revenues.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by trapping Side A in a clean structural dilemma: if the window allows for case-by-case exceptions based on profitability, it ceases to be a meaningful rule and merely becomes a plea for accurate accounting. Side A conceded that the window yields to comprehensive analysis, allowing Side B to effectively neutralize the affirmative burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly exploited Side A's answer to Q1, showing that a rule which only forbids studios from deliberately ignoring ticket revenue is entirely empty.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A wins decisively by trapping Side B on the definition and function of the "meaningful window." Through sharp pressure questions and rebuttals, A forced B to concede that the window yields if a studio's comprehensive analysis shows a film earns more on streaming. A effectively argued this leaves the window as either harmful (binding against rational analysis) or empty (merely demanding "good analysis" rather than a specific release rule). A also won the access clash by forcing B to admit studios have no lever to make theatrical viewing cheaper than an existing streaming subscription, leaving the access harm uncontested. B's arguments on cultural impact and revenue were solid, but B could not escape A's structural dismantling of the motion's mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it capitalized on Side B's pressure question answer to show that B's "presumption" either forbids only irrational decisions (making it empty) or overrides studio analysis (making it harmful), perfectly crystallizing the clash.
