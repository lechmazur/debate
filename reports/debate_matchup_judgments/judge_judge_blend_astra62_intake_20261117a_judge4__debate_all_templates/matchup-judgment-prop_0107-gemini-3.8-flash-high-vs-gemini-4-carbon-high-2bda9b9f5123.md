### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) wins. PRO built its best route around the claim that the no-treatment baseline is not neutral, because endogenous puberty causes permanent, unwanted changes. That point has real force and CON never fully neutralized it. But PRO leaned on "proven, durable" mental-health benefit and "remarkably low" regret without defending either claim once CON pressed on them. CON dismantled several of PRO's supports in turn: - **The oncology analogy:** CON separated treating objective disease from altering a healthy body. - **Progression as proof of accurate screening:** CON showed this reasoning is circular. - **Fertility preservation:** CON showed it is inconsistent with blocking puberty early. - **PRO's own concession:** CON exploited PRO's admission that hormones don't resolve co-occurring psychiatric conditions, which weakened the causal story for mental-health gains. CON also answered the pressure questions directly. On Q2, CON said it withholds hormones, not care, and offered intensive psychotherapy and psychiatric treatment as an alternative. PRO's closing then misdescribed that answer as withholding "medical care" altogether. It mostly restated the opening rather than engaging the fertility and circularity points. Overall, CON made the weighing clearer: certain, irreversible developmental loss against confounded relief. PRO left key rebuttals unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.3 and A5.3 rebuttal was decisive. PRO's appeal to gamete cryopreservation (B4.2) contradicts PRO's own case for blocking puberty before voice deepening or breast development (B4.1), since no mature gametes exist at that stage. This turned PRO's answer to the consent and fertility question into a self-contradiction that PRO never repaired.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the comparative harms and causation clearer. A used B's concession that hormones do not resolve co-occurring psychiatric conditions to undercut the claimed mental-health benefit, showed screening cannot establish lifelong identity, and exposed a sharp contradiction in B's fertility-preservation answer. B's strongest charge that A denies all relief was answered convincingly with psychiatric crisis care and adult transition, leaving B leaning on a repeated baseline argument already rebutted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's rebuttal of B's harm management: B offered gamete cryopreservation, but A showed that intervening early enough to block puberty means no mature gametes exist yet, while progression to cross-sex hormones sacrifices adult sexual function and fertility.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): A wins the comparative weighing, not the claim that natural puberty is harmless. It distinguishes regret from lasting physical harm and materially undermines B’s monitoring and fertility-preservation response. B identifies meaningful costs of waiting but repeatedly asserts proven benefit without resolving confounding and long-term-harm objections. A’s own unsupported categorical claims about sterility and natural resolution limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3 challenges B4.2’s gamete-freezing answer: early suppression can precede mature gametes, so counseling and monitoring do not themselves establish fertility preservation. This exposes a concrete gap in B’s characterization of harms as manageable, and B’s closing does not resolve it.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) wins a clear but competitive debate. It anchored the motion as a comparison between treatment and the untreated condition, then adapted well across rounds. It exposed CON's evidentiary double standard: CON rejects observational studies of medical care while offering no comparative evidence that psychotherapy alone resolves persistent pubertal dysphoria. PRO also challenged CON's claim that sterility and sexual dysfunction are universal certainties by distinguishing early-Tanner blocking from later-puberty initiation. PRO answered both pressure questions directly with A1:/A2:. It conceded probabilistic screening and reframed that as the ordinary standard of medicine. It gave a plausible, non-precise argument from hormone continuation as an upper bound on regret, though that remained largely assertion. CON (A) had a real live route: weak long-term evidence for durable mental-health benefit, combined with irreversible physical costs. Its "probabilistic screening" pressure landed somewhat. However, CON became repetitive and execution suffered in several ways: - It kept asserting "guaranteed" impairment without engaging PRO's later-puberty point. - It cited nonexistent reference IDs ([P1.4, P2.5]). - It largely sidestepped Q1 by shifting the burden rather than addressing whether untreated dysphoria worsens. - Its closing mostly restated earlier claims. Because the motion asks for a net comparison, PRO's sharper weighing and its handling of the pressure round gave it the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over the comparative burden. PRO's Q1 asked what long-term comparative evidence supports psychotherapy as a safe alternative. CON answered only that non-invasive care needs no proof because it causes no physical harm. That answer left PRO's claim unanswered: screened youth already receive therapy, yet their distress worsens as puberty advances. As a result, the cost of withholding care went largely uncontested in CON's weighing.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO won by reframing non-treatment as its own irreversible harm and exposing CON's double standard on evidence. CON's precautionary case was strong, but it leaned on overstated certainty that screened care uniformly causes permanent impairment; PRO plausibly narrowed that with later-puberty care and restored bone accrual. CON also sidestepped PRO's pointed question about evidence for psychotherapy as a resolving alternative, while PRO answered both pressure questions directly. The decisive comparison became likely relief for the screened persisting majority versus rare regret and manageable risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 directly answered hidden-regret pressure with continuation records and then turned the evidence burden back on CON's unsupported claim that exploratory psychotherapy resolves persistent pubertal dysphoria, a point CON never squarely neutralized.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B better defended the motion’s comparative standard: withholding treatment also carries potentially irreversible costs, and probabilistic screening is not automatically disqualifying. A repeatedly substituted the natural-versus-medical distinction and universalized physical harms for weighing both pathways. A’s objections about confounding, attrition, and long-term evidence remained important, however. B overstated screening’s reliability and treated prescription continuation as an unjustifiably strong proxy for absence of regret. Those weaknesses limit the margin, but A’s failure to defend its supposedly safe alternative leaves B ahead.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure question about psychotherapy exposed a central evasion: A answered that therapy cannot cause sterilization, not whether waiting safely addresses persistent dysphoria. Conversely, A effectively rebutted B’s blinding defense by explaining that confounding, attrition, and missing comparisons—not merely observational design—undermine causal conclusions.
