- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because its central structure survived the exchange and CON's did not fully answer it. PRO argued that life without parole already provides incapacitation and permanent exclusion. On that view, execution adds only irreversible error risk and a possible martyrdom payoff. CON forced PRO to defend proportionality, but CON's own route kept shrinking toward symbolism and "finality." PRO named this cleanly: once confinement secures safety, killing is expression purchased with a life. CON's best moment was the concrete special-master safeguard in A2. CON also made several execution errors: - It attributed its own "accumulate decades" and "very long timeout" framing to PRO, calling the latter "CON's" euphemism. - It claimed PRO "admits" lifers produce manifestos, which PRO never said. - Its closing line that "the terrorist already had the spectacular death" was muddled. - Its own mention of "accidental release" invited PRO's simple reply that this calls for secure confinement, not execution. PRO's second rebuttal was short. It nonetheless answered both pressure questions directly. Its war distinction (imminent threat vs. a neutralized captive) was a clean answer to the coherence challenge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 demanded a safeguard against executing an innocent person in a classified-evidence terrorism case. CON answered with a special-master review panel, a concrete reply. PRO's closing then pointed out that procedures and classified material can still be wrong and that no appellate innovation can "un-kill" an innocent person. That kept irreversibility live as the decisive asymmetry against CON's "refine, don't abolish" line.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won the decisive clash: once incapacitation was conceded to be fully served by life without parole, CON's case reduced to expressive "finality," which A repeatedly framed as symbolism purchased with a life. Both sides were rhetorically strong and disciplined, but A made the debate easier to decide on the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answer to the war-combatant question (A4.2), distinguishing imminent-threat killing under armed conflict from killing an already-neutralized captive, combined with the closing crystallization that B's remaining reasons for execution—finality and expression—are not protection but ceremony.
