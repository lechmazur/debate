### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides were disciplined, well-grounded, and stayed on the motion as written. The decisive clash was A's detection-gap case: the teenager who welcomes an exploitative adult's attention shows no outward signal, so B's suspicion-first rule depends on the endangered teen recognizing and reporting the danger. B's answers (noticing dread, the gaming example) all conceded the same limit — contact conducted entirely in private may remain invisible — and B explicitly admitted "periodic inspection has an advantage in that case," then only argued timing, which A correctly noted reduces but does not cancel the added opportunity. B's best line, the chilled confidence a friend never sends, was met with a fair comparative weighing from A (confidentiality can also preserve dangerous secrecy between children who misunderstand risk), and B's closing asymmetry argument (contingent catches vs. guaranteed chilling) was good but did not decisively outweigh the structural detection gap. B's "exit test is not an entry test" was the sharpest single move of the debate, but A's A5.1 response — that a default permits case-by-case maturity assessment without demanding a warning sign — held. B also conceded always-on location's distinctive benefit for unforeseen ordinary-afternoon emergencies. B's rhetoric and framing were superior, but A did the stronger argumentative work on the live routes and answered pressure more squarely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's closing "exit test is not an entry test" turn against A's exit criteria was the most damaging exchange, forcing A to explain why maturity facts can justify turning monitoring off but not staying off at entry; A's answer held, but the pressure was real. Conversely, A's sustained exploitation of B's concession that "periodic inspection has an advantage" in the welcomed-manipulation case did the most to secure the win.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both debaters were disciplined, responsive, and epistemically honest, with Side A mounting a formidable case around flattery-based grooming where a teenager welcomes manipulation and shows no outward distress. Side B nevertheless won clearly for two reasons. First, on private-message monitoring, Side B trapped Side A's retreat to periodic spot-checking: reading less often lowers the odds of catching hidden manipulation in time while preserving the full ex-ante chilling effect on peers, since friends cannot know which messages an adult will inspect and confidential handling cannot protect a plea for help that is never sent. Second, on location tracking, Side B cleanly separated finite, purpose-bound precautions—such as sharing location on an unfamiliar trip or while learning a bus route—from an always-on default, then turned Side A's maturity-based exit criteria by showing those same functional facts can serve as an entry test rather than forcing teenagers to earn unobserved life out of a blanket rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: When Side A defended message monitoring via "restrained, periodic review" and "confidential handling," Side B first showed that without a warning sign routine protection requires routine reading, and then exposed a fatal asymmetry in spot-checking: infrequent checks dilute the chance of catching welcomed manipulation in time without reassuring a friend beforehand that his confidence will stay private, since "confidential handling does nothing if the boy never sends it."
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won the comparative weighing: it separated location from messages, showed temporary or concern-based use captures most conceded safety benefits, and exposed that periodic reading retains the friend-deterrence cost while only sometimes catching harm. Side A established a real hidden-grooming detection gap, but mostly asserted that an extra chance justified routine intrusion despite conceding its costs and imperfect detection.
