### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but CON's "collapse dilemma" — that independent verification either duplicates adequate case-specific checking (doing no extra work) or arbitrarily excludes well-tested output for lacking a paid credential (doing harm) — was never fully defused by PRO. PRO's main response, that independence matters because a sponsoring witness has an interest in the outcome, was undercut by CON's unanswered point that a separately retained expert is typically hired by the same advocating party and thus no more neutral, with oath, disclosure, and cross-examination doing the real work either way. CON also sustained a concrete equity argument (poor defendants priced out of a second expert layer while the state buys compliance) that PRO's "cost cuts both ways" reply never specifically rebutted, since CON's regime does not require a separately retained certifier, only case-specific proof via existing witnesses. PRO scored real points on reconstructions/gap-filling being unverifiable even by experts, but CON conceded and absorbed this by noting existing foundational rules already exclude such evidence, leaving the presumption doing little independent work. The clipped B4 speech is a minor execution cost. Overall CON made the clash easier to resolve and left fewer live, unanswered threats.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal 1 "collapse" argument (A2.2–A2.4), sharpened again in Rebuttal 2 (A4.2–A4.4) via the "could is not must" / exhaustive-witness-vs-light-expert-sample example, was the most decisive exchange; PRO's closing reasserted the independence rationale but never answered why a party-retained "independent" expert is meaningfully more neutral than a cross-examined sponsoring witness.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully narrowing the debate to the practical delta between the two worlds: the strict requirement of an "independent human expert." Side A effectively cornered Side B into conceding that the expert's methods (sampling, source comparison) would be the same as existing evidentiary requirements, allowing Side A to frame the motion as a redundant "paid signature" that prices out poor defendants without actually improving reliability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 masterfully capitalized on Side B's A1 concession, pointing out that if the verification method is identical to existing practices, the presumption shrinks to a costly mandate on who performs the check rather than how it is tested.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side B wins because it successfully exposed the practical and structural flaws in Side A's proposed rule, particularly the resource disparity between prosecution and defense. While Side A argued principled points about independence and the burden of proof, Side B convincingly demonstrated that requiring a separately retained human expert acts as a financial tollbooth that disproportionately excludes exculpatory defense evidence. Side B also effectively argued that existing evidentiary gatekeeping already demands case-specific proof of fidelity, making the categorical presumption redundant for checkable evidence and useless for unverifiable reconstructions. Side A's response to the funding issue—suggesting the remedy is better defense funding rather than adjusting the evidence rule—was a weak practical answer in the context of the motion as written. Side B's framing of "judging the proof vs. judging the pedigree" crystallized the clash memorably and decisively, securing a clear victory despite Side A's strong theoretical defense of independent verification.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side A's shrinking rule by showing that if verification just means sampling, the independent expert requirement is merely a costly credential check, and if the underlying checking is exhaustive, excluding it for the wrong job title does not make the trial safer.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly without fake sourcing, but A built the more decisive path: it conceded the legitimate reliability worries (unconfirmable reconstructions, silent omissions) while showing the motion's specific mechanism—mandatory independent human expert verification as a categorical presumption—is both overinclusive and underinclusive. A's strongest hits were (1) the indigent-defendant burden point, which B never adequately rebutted beyond the weak "usually the prosecution" line that A directly punctured by noting defendants also offer AI-assisted evidence; and (2) the logical gap that expert endorsement is neither necessary (stipulations, disclosed source comparison can suffice) nor sufficient (an expert can rubber-stamp fluent error, and cannot certify sourceless detail regardless) for reliability. B's pressure questions were sharp and exposed a real tension (checkable-in-principle vs. actually-checked), but A's answers in Rebuttal 2 and closing converged the dispute into "who performs the check," and A's proportional-scrutiny framework absorbed that concern without needing the categorical rule on the ballot. B's closing restated the timing argument well but didn't resolve the necessity/sufficiency critique or the resource-burden asymmetry. A did suffer an execution cost from being clipped twice for exceeding word limits (opening and Rebuttal 1), slightly denting its polish, but the core logic survived into later speeches and the clash was still resolved in A's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A4.4–A4.8 response, which used B's own pressure-question concessions (unconfirmable reconstructions stay out; checks must actually occur) to show the remaining dispute is not whether reliability must be shown but whether a categorical expert-mandate is the only way to show it—paired with the unanswered indigent-defendant burden point.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating the need for evidence validation from the motion's strict mandate for an 'independent human expert.' Side A effectively used Side B's own concessions—that an expert cannot validate unconfirmable reconstructions and they must remain excluded—to prove that the expert requirement acts merely as a costly toll booth rather than a unique guarantor of truth.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive, demonstrating that both sides agreed unconfirmable evidence should stay out and verifiable evidence should get in, thereby narrowing the clash to whether a mandatory independent expert is the only way to achieve this (which Side A showed was unnecessarily costly for defendants).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully demonstrating that the proposition's specific mechanism—an "independent human expert"—is an overbroad ritual that fails to guarantee truth while actively harming resource-poor defendants who cannot afford such experts for their own exculpatory evidence. Side B effectively neutralizes Side A's strongest rhetorical frame ("checkable is not checked") by conceding that actual checks must occur before admission, but proving that adversarial stipulations, source comparisons, and proportional scrutiny can achieve this without the rigid, costly mandate of the proposition. Side A argued beautifully and framed the stakes well, but could not adequately defend the necessity of the "expert" requirement for uncontested or easily verifiable AI outputs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they absorb Side A's "checkable vs checked" distinction by agreeing that checks must happen pre-admission, but sharply distinguish between "actual reliability" and "one prescribed reviewer," exposing the proposition as a costly formality.
