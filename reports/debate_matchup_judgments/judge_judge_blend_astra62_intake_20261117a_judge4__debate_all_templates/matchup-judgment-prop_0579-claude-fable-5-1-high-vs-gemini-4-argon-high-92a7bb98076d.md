### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides executed at a high level, but PRO (A) won the decisive clashes. A's core comparative frame — judge the digital ID against today's replayable, irrevocable static secrets, not a utopia — was applied consistently and survived every attack. B's strongest lines were sharp: the option-becomes-mandate dilemma, the enrollment-anchors-on-compromised-records point, and the verifier-side "clean join key" ledger argument. But A neutralized the key harms: no central biometric database (device-local storage, passport-chip re-provisioning), the Estonia example was turned into a revocation success story, and the closing showed the state already holds subpoena power over the same records today, so the credential adds no new surveillance capacity while stripping commercial hoarders of raw documents. B's dilemma was reinforced by the Germany eID example, yet B never answered A's France counter-example of an optional system delivering access, and A correctly noted the motion requires improvement, not transformation. B's recovery-code-as-static-secret jujitsu was clever but narrow. A answered pressure questions directly with required A1/A2 format and gave cleaner weighing across rounds; B's residual case leaned on speculative trajectory claims. Clear but competitive win for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The B2.7/B4.5-B5.3 dilemma chain was B's best work: if the ID stays truly optional with paper fallbacks, access gains are marginal (Germany's languishing eID); if it spreads, paper withers and failures become total (India, Estonia). A's counter — France's decade-long optional system with legally guaranteed paper, plus the point that the motion only requires net improvement — blunted but did not fully erase it, leaving it the sharpest unresolved thread in an otherwise A-leaning debate.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by locking Side A into a dilemma it never escaped: large fraud and access gains require the credential to spread until the “option” behaves like a mandate, while a truly voluntary system with robust paper fallbacks delivers only marginal relief. B converted A’s France/Germany examples and recovery methods into evidence for that tradeoff, and more cleanly weighed recoverable, partial fraud against a trusted root, cleaner state join key, and harder-to-revoke failure modes. A’s status-quo comparison stayed coherent but did not show the claimed net benefit survives that clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it treated A’s recovery code and post-office paths as a renewed static-secret and weak-enrollment problem, then used A’s own country examples to show voluntariness and transformative reach cannot be claimed together.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won the status-quo comparison: static tax and license numbers are already universal, remotely replayable, linkable, and unrevocable, while a device-bound credential with selective disclosure, local storage, and revocable certificates reduces data held by verifiers. B's mandate, central biometric honeypot, and surveillance-index risks were forcefully argued but remained claims of inevitability against A's specified optional, non-central design and targeted access gains for people failed by credit-bureau checks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing reversal of B's master-key attack: breached static identifiers already function as an unrevocable master key usable remotely across portals, whereas credential misuse requires defeating a chip or in-person binding and the certificate can be revoked; A similarly showed state subpoena access to identity records already exists today.
