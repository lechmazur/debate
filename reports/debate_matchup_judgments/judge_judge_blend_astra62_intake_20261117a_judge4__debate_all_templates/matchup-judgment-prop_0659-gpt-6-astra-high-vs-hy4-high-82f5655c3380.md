### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won narrowly. Side B gave the ban a memorable upstream-selection rationale, but A more effectively dismantled the claim that emotion recognition must be either redundant or prejudicial: an imperfect cue could locate conduct for review without itself becoming employment evidence. A also answered both pressure questions directly with selection logs, disclosure, individual challenges, and testing against alternative review methods. B often reiterated that such selection is inherently “suspicion” and that workers must defend their inner lives without fully answering A’s employer-burden and conduct-only safeguards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal separated selection quality from the truth of an emotion inference and explained why different scrutiny is not automatically wrongful scrutiny. This left a bounded, testable use alive that B’s categorical prohibition argument never quite excluded.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by establishing that conditional authorization already excludes systems that cannot demonstrate job-relevant selection value, contestability, and non-discriminatory scrutiny, while a ban additionally excludes even a system meeting those tests. Side B powerfully identifies upstream selection and unverifiable affect as harms, but its redundant-or-discriminatory dilemma never eliminates A's third possibility: validated triage that locates independently assessable conduct more efficiently. A's burden-shifting answers also leave the worker contesting selection and conduct rather than proving an inner state.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: distinguishing reviewer-blinding from worker/auditor disclosure, treating the flag's causal role as inspectable rather than an unprovable counterfactual, and validating selection against conduct and random review rather than the truth of an emotion label.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive clash clear: whether safeguards can cure the upstream use of an inferred emotional state as the gate to scrutiny. Side A offered a careful conditional-permission model and plausibly limited it to conduct-based findings, but Side B consistently showed that even a validated, label-blinded search function still singles workers out on an uncontestable inner-state guess. That left A’s best-case exception morally and administratively vulnerable, while B’s ban argument remained intact as the cleaner protection for contestable employment evidence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s Rebuttal 2 answers to the pressure questions were decisive: it accepted the hypothetical of no unequal aggregate scrutiny but argued the individual upstream singling-out still remains unanswerable, turning A’s efficiency case into demeanor-based suspicion rather than a neutral triage tool.
