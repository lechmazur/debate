### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B wins by separating transparency from a pickup default. A convincingly showed current apps steer toward the high-fee path and that unnoticed fees can be worse than missed pickups, but B offered a sharper alternative: require an explicit two-button choice with price and time, no carried default. That solves hidden fees without presuming the customer wants to walk. A tried to absorb that screen as the motion, but B exposed the key gap: a mandatory choice is not a default to pickup. The decisive distinction was that A showed why the courier should not be selected on the customer's behalf, not why the walk should be.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's two-button screen rebuttal equalized the pre-payment comparison, answered A's hidden-fee asymmetry, and turned A's own "affirmative act before charge" principle into an argument for mandatory choice rather than a pickup default.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A razor-thin edge to CON. Both sides were disciplined and grounded. PRO's best live routes were the error-asymmetry argument (missed pickup is loud and fixable; unnoticed fees are silent and repeated) and the incentive-drift claim that "neutral" interfaces erode back toward margin. But A's two-button screen ("Pickup: $12" beside "Delivery: $25," each with its time) largely neutralized the asymmetry by putting price and mode on the same salient control, making inattention safe since no order proceeds without a choice. A answered B's sharp Q2 pressure honestly and concretely (the mixed-mode reorderer), and A's closing crystallized the decisive gap: PRO showed why the courier shouldn't be preselected on the customer's behalf, not why the walk should be. B's counter-move — declaring CON's screen "is the motion" — was rhetorically clever but quietly swapped "default to cheaper" for "don't presume expensive," a reframe A directly undercut with the no-tie/no-order distinction. B's un-answered incentive-drift point keeps this close, but A left the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The B3.2 pressure question ("if 'ask when unclear' never fires, aren't you defending the default you disown?") was the sharpest single blow, and A's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 — the two-button reorder screen with no carried-over courier — was the decisive response, later sharpened by A5.1's point that on that screen no default exists and no order proceeds without an affirmative choice.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B more convincingly defended the proposition as written by showing that defaulting to pickup or lower-fee options more often still presumes a valuation of the customer’s time the app does not know, while equally visible explicit choice stops profit-driven steering without that presumption. Side A landed real pressure on hidden fees, incentives, and asymmetric errors, but increasingly treated “do not preselect the courier” as victory for a motion that affirmatively defaults the cheaper path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing distinction that two priced buttons create no tie to break and require an affirmative choice either way, whereas a pickup default can commit an inattentive user to the trip—showing A justified not selecting the courier on the customer’s behalf, not selecting the walk.
