### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side A won because it turned PRO's own concession into a live regulatory alternative. Once PRO admitted that a tank can be humane when justified, overseen, and enriched, A showed those are exactly the variables a licence can require. PRO's strongest remaining argument was that industrial scale removes individual attention, but A converted that into a dilemma: if the standards are too costly then no farms would be licensed, making a blanket ban unnecessary; if farms can meet them, a global ban would destroy the humane operation. A's wild-capture substitution argument also remained under-answered: PRO said nets could also be regulated, but never showed how moving decks at sea are easier to audit than fixed tanks. This gave A the cleaner comparative path to vote against the ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 seized PRO's admission that not every tank is inhumane and reframed it as the end of the motion: if a tank is defensible when justified, overseen, and ended humanely, those are licence conditions, not reasons for a global prohibition.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a gripping, high-level debate by systematically breaking PRO's core claim that cephalopod cognition makes confinement inherently indefensible. Turning PRO's own EU and UK regulatory precedent and pressing the example of public aquaria, CON forced PRO to concede that tanks can be humane and to retreat from biological impossibility to keeper labor costs at scale. CON then closed cleanly by showing that strict licensing either prevents economically unviable cruel farms from operating or permits genuinely humane ones, whereas a blanket ban leaves demand to uninspected wild-capture fisheries at sea.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side B declared in [B2.7] that "no rule can supply a reef" and argued in [B4.1] that lab tanks are only defensible because of scientific benefit and early endpoints, Side A countered in [A2.1-A2.2] and [A4.2] with licensed public aquaria—which confine octopuses for life without early endpoints merely for display—forcing Side B to pivot to staffing costs in [B5.1] and allowing Side A to seal the debate with its licensing-conditions dilemma in [A5.1-A5.5].
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): A presented a vivid, coherent cruelty case, especially through the crowding-versus-isolation bind, and answered its pressure questions directly. A asserted that individual care cannot survive scale but never squarely answered B’s dilemma that economically prohibitive standards would simply produce no farms, while a compliant farm would undermine the need for a blanket ban. B somewhat overstated A’s concession as literally ending the motion, so the win remains competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal and closing converted A’s laboratory-and-aquarium concession into a licensing test, then sharpened it into the unanswered dilemma: strict standards either prevent cruel farms from operating or permit a demonstrably humane farm, making prohibition unnecessary in either case.
