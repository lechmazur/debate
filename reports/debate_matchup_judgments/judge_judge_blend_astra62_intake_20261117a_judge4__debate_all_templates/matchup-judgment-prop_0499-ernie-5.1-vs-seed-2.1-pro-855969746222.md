### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): CON wins clearly. Its central argument is that the unmapped seabed is a survey problem best handled by uncrewed systems, and that crewed missions make sense in space mainly because of signal lag that does not exist undersea. This mechanism was established early and extended consistently, and PRO never squarely neutralized it. PRO's case for crewed presence rested on the 1977 Alvin dive and on general claims about "serendipitous, interpretive" science. When CON asked for a recent discovery that actually required humans, PRO effectively dodged. PRO's budget answer of $2–5B also undercut the "comparable to space" framing and let CON argue that the money buys only a few prestige dives. PRO's Q1 on tether range and hadal depths was decent pressure, and CON answered it directly with surface support vessels and autonomous vehicles. PRO's closing largely restated its romance-of-discovery theme without addressing the latency asymmetry. Both sides used some brittle precision. CON claimed "90 cents of every dollar" and full mapping "within a decade," while PRO claimed "more on one telescope than all crewed research." These claims cost both sides somewhat, but they do not change the outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.3 turned PRO's latency point against it. Crewed presence is justified for Mars because of a lag of tens of minutes, but fiber tethers on the seabed give near-instant control. This reframed the space comparison itself as a reason against the motion, and PRO never answered it.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): A better distinguished supporting ocean science from funding crewed exploration at space-comparable levels. B never convincingly established why scientists must be physically underwater to deliver adaptive judgment, nor why its proposed budget followed from the funding imbalance. A also answered the monitoring-and-deterrence pressure more directly. However, A’s unsupported cost ratios, handful-of-dives claim, and sweeping global-mapping promises substantially weaken its grounding and limit the winning margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s reminder that remotely operated vehicles retain human scientists on surface ships undercut B’s repeated contrast between onboard scientists and pre-programmed algorithms. Asked for a modern discovery demonstrating a uniquely crewed advantage, B supplied another assertion about serendipity rather than resolving that central distinction.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): B won by separating the motion’s laudable ocean-science goals from its specific demand for space-comparable funding of crewed missions. Its scalability, safety, opportunity-cost, and real-time remote-operation arguments directly undercut A’s mapping and human-judgment case. A offered memorable rhetoric about discovery but repeatedly asserted, rather than demonstrated, unique modern crewed value and retreated to a $2–5 billion proposal without clearly defending comparability. B’s numerical claims were sometimes too precise, limiting its grounding score.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s signal-lag contrast decisively broke A’s space analogy, while B’s demand for a recent discovery requiring crewed presence exposed A’s evasive DEEPSEA CHALLENGER answer. B also directly answered both pressure questions, especially by explaining how persistent monitoring enables legal and diplomatic deterrence.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): CON wins by tying the motion to its two specific demands: crewed exploration, and funding comparable to space programs. CON then showed that PRO never justified either one. CON separated bathymetric mapping, which robots and sonar already handle, from targeted on-site science. That separation pushed PRO toward conceding that the "80 percent unmapped" figure is mostly a robotic problem. The decisive point was CON's ROV and telepresence argument. Expert pilots on surface ships provide the adaptive, real-time judgment PRO had used as its central reason for putting humans at depth. PRO never squarely answered this. Its closing only asserted that at-depth human judgment is still needed. PRO also shifted to "robots and people integrated," which weakens the specifically crewed case. PRO's spin-off claims stayed general and were fairly called speculative. Its answer on a measurable outcome, ground-truthing faults and vents before mining begins, was decent but did not show why a crewed fleet at space-program scale is needed rather than more ROVs. CON loses some credit for overstating PRO's concessions, since PRO never conceded that ROVs match human judgment. CON's answer to the benchmark question was also somewhat thin. Even so, CON gave the cleaner reason to vote and kept the burden on the motion's specific funding level.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A1 in Rebuttal 2 was the decisive exchange. CON argued that ROVs piloted by scientists on surface ships already deliver the adaptive sampling PRO credited to human crews, without the cost or lethal risk. This cut the core link between PRO's claimed benefits and the "crewed" part of the motion, and PRO answered it only with a bare assertion in its closing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): A wins by separating increased ocean investment from space-comparable funding for crewed exploration. B’s marginal-return and integrated-program arguments support sustained research, but never establish why scientists must descend or why comparable funding is necessary. A’s ROV response undermines B’s claimed monopoly on adaptive sampling, while its budget challenge remains unanswered. A also handles pressure more directly; B’s closing misrepresents increased robotic investment as continued neglect. The margin is limited by A’s conflation of crewed spaceflight with broader space benefits, treatment of “comparable” as exact parity, and incorrect claim that B conceded the ROV point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B’s demand for adaptive, context-rich sampling versus A’s explanation that surface scientists can direct ROVs. This preserves human judgment without requiring humans at depth. B’s closing returns to flagship commitment rather than explaining why that substitute cannot deliver the claimed benefits.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): B kept the debate anchored to the motion’s actual demand for space-comparable funding and showed that A’s benefits supported expanded, targeted ocean science rather than that spending level. A offered energetic claims about marginal returns and integrated exploration, but never demonstrated why the required adaptive work demanded human crews or why parity was the appropriate scale. A’s closing also overstated what B had left undisputed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: surface-piloted ROVs supplied the expert adaptive judgment A reserved for crews, while rejecting the proposition’s specified benchmark did not require B to invent an alternative budget. This undercut both A’s necessity argument and its burden-shifting.
