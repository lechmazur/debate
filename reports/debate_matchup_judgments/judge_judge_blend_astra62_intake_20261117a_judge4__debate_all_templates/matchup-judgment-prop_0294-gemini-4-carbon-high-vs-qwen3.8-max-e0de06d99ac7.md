### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Both sides were disciplined and clashed directly. CON had the sharper conceptual frame: narrative emphasis is a normative civic judgment, and reviewers become unaccountable gatekeepers. Its point that "foregrounding contradiction" is itself an emphasis landed well. PRO, however, built the more complete comparative case. It conceded that no system is neutral, then made institutional incentives decisive: politicians are rewarded for flattering myths, while scholars gain standing by overturning consensus with evidence. PRO's Q1 on minority protection exposed a real weakness. CON's answer ("no design guarantees justice") listed courts, the press, and rival jurisdictions, but these were generic and weakly tied to textbook content. CON's A2 also partly conceded PRO's ground by accepting an "evidentiary floor" and "professional consultation." PRO answered the gatekeeper question concretely with cross-institutional nominations, staggered terms, published dissents, and multiple approved texts, which blunted the "monopoly" framing. That framing also somewhat overstated a motion that only asks for stronger review rather than leaving narratives "mainly" to politics. CON's corrigibility claim, that expert dissent is dismissed as ignorance, stayed largely asserted rather than demonstrated. PRO's mechanism, by which electoral arithmetic rewards distortion, stayed live through the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The pressure-question exchange on minority protection was the most decisive. PRO's Q1 asked what protects Indigenous and religious minorities when majorities reward sanitizing. CON's answer, that no design guarantees justice and that courts and the press can help, conceded the incentive problem without neutralizing it. PRO's closing then argued that judges do not police lopsided chapters and that sanitized history shapes the future electorate. CON's best counter was B4.4, arguing that "major omission" and "foreground contradiction" are themselves interpretive power, but it did not overcome PRO's incentive comparison.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): A wins the institutional comparison rather than proving scholarly neutrality. Competing textbooks, published dissents, and transparent review substantially answer B’s monopoly objection while preserving stronger evidentiary constraints. B correctly establishes that emphasis involves normative judgment, and A overstates its safeguards against capture. But B never adequately explains why political accountability corrects distortions that voters actively reward. Its repeated claim that citizens cannot challenge reviewers also fails to engage A’s transparency mechanisms. A better supports stronger review as the proposition actually requires, without needing to establish expert infallibility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s challenge to “vote them out” was decisive: exposing sanitization does not produce correction when the governing majority wants sanitization. B’s appeal to elections, publicity, and courts did not establish a reliable answer, and A’s closing directly crystallized that weakness.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner comparative case: politicians are rewarded for flattering majorities and sanitizing war and colonialism, while diverse scholarly panels are rewarded for challenging claims with evidence. CON's emphasis and gatekeeping objections were serious, but PRO answered them with multiple approved textbooks, published dissent, and district choice. CON's reliance on elections, courts, and publicity was weakened by its concession that hostile majorities may reward erasure, leaving PRO's corrigibility argument stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's closing turning CON's corrigibility test against it: if voters reward sanitized history, exposure and elections entrench rather than correct distortion, whereas adversarial scholarly review at least ties revision to evidence.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): This was a close, high-quality debate. PRO built a compelling moral case. Its strongest point was that political control lets majorities erase victims' histories through tone and proportion, and that its Q1 and Q2 exposed CON's lack of any mechanical limit. CON, however, framed the decisive clash more effectively from the opening onward. It argued that the real dispute is selection and moral framing, not fabricated facts, and that scholarly review therefore relocates value judgments to unremovable reviewers rather than removing politics from textbooks. PRO's A1 conceded "final authority" over "minimum framing" and "tone." CON exploited that concession sharply, showing that PRO's floor either imposes a contested verdict or collapses back into politics. CON answered PRO's Q2 directly by saying inclusion is a moral right, not a matter of vote share, and it kept removability and recourse as a clean weighing mechanism. PRO's remedies, such as plural panels, public comment and expedited revision, were reasonable. Still, CON plausibly cast them as toothless if the panel retains final say. PRO's "only after distortion reaches children" point was good but came late and was only partially developed. CON leaned somewhat on a generalized claim that academics lagged behind activists, but both sides used similar levels of general assertion. Overall, CON made the debate slightly easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.3–B4.4 response to PRO's A1/A2 was the most decisive exchange. CON seized on PRO's admission that panels hold final authority over proportion and tone. It argued that these are "the entire civic verdict," that "competing interpretations" still require the panel to decide which views are legitimate, and that public comment offers no recourse against an unremovable body. This turned PRO's guardrail framing into an overreach.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): A established a credible case against political minimization of atrocities, but B exposed the unresolved scope of A’s scholarly veto: “minimum framing” involves the very value judgments under dispute. A’s transparency, plural panels, and revision procedures did not sufficiently explain how outsiders could correct scholarly capture. B won the accountability comparison and more effectively developed its pressure across rounds, although its claim that panel errors would become permanent was overstated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s pressure questions elicited A’s concession of final authority over minimum framing. B then argued that requiring competing interpretations still leaves reviewers deciding which interpretations qualify, directly challenging A’s claim to offer only an evidentiary floor rather than transfer contested narrative authority.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): B wins because it turns CON's framing concession against it: dilution through proportion and tone can erase without omitting facts. B gives scholarly review a concrete evidentiary floor, plural panels, dissent, and revision triggered without winning elections, while A ultimately offers only elections and publicity as limits on majorities distorting atrocities.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B's pressure on an enforceable limit. A answered that exposure and electoral replacement check abuse, effectively conceding there is no barrier before distortion reaches students or unless victims build political leverage.
