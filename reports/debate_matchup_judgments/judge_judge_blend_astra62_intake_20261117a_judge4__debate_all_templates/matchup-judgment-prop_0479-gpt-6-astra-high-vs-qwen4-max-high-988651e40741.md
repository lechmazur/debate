### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by narrowing the dispute to whether markets deserve formal standing, not whether they can forecast or be subsidized. The bank-rescue counterfactual showed a gradeable price need not answer which intervention works, and B showed repeated repricing of one outcome does not create independent reliability. A’s strongest move was that B accepted subsidies, scoring, and outside entry; but B answered with open competitions, timestamped trial scoring, and ministerial responses to published challenges, showing accountability is available without making a market price the default. A’s closing restated hierarchy rather than resolving causal relevance and manipulation problems.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s reply on order flow was decisive: concentration or persistence may flag suspicion, but officials cannot distinguish informed pricing from strategic pressure before the decision, and “settles correctly” comes too late to guide policy.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by separating A’s attractive accountability reforms from the proposition’s specific requirement of formal market standing. A offered the more memorable case about hierarchical aggregation and selective burial, but B consistently showed that publication, open forecasting, scoring, and mandatory ministerial responses can provide those benefits without privileging a price. A never fully answered the causal-relevance objection or established why market prices deserve the default position.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it exposed the tension in A’s pilot-participation claim, showed that order flow detects suspicion rather than manipulation or truth, and explained why daily repricing does not create independent demonstrations of reliability.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): B won by consistently separating useful forecasting and accountability reforms from the proposition's specific demand for formal standing for subsidized market prices. B showed prices do not supply causal guidance, strategic traders may rationally absorb losses, and timestamped, independently scored expert or competition forecasts can be tested and publicly answered without becoming the default benchmark. A powerfully attacked hierarchy and discretion, but never closed the gap between needing answerable numbers and privileging a trading price.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: prizes and retainers answer recruitment without market standing, scoring against live outcomes answers the track-record challenge, and daily repricing of one outcome is not repeated validation or causal evidence.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): CON built the stronger path to victory by holding PRO to the motion’s shift away from relying mainly on expert panels. PRO’s case narrowed to cost and delivery inputs, and its admission that voided conditional contracts never count as forecasting successes let CON argue the market’s performance record is built from cases where it changed little. The authorless-price accountability problem also remained live: PRO shifted accountability to the minister, but did not fully answer why prices moved. PRO had strong replies about panels sharing the missing counterfactual and about cost/delivery being consequential, but those replies left the case as a scoped supplement rather than a justification for displacing expert predictive primacy overall.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s cancellation/validation turn was the most decisive: after PRO admitted voided conditional contracts cannot count as successes, CON showed that a market’s influence often operates through cancellations, so its scoring record is weakest precisely where the market mattered most.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): A answered both pressure questions directly and defended a plausible performance-based model, but its case increasingly narrowed to settleable cost and schedule inputs with mandatory explanation. B kept the motion’s displacement burden in view and pressed the stronger comparative concerns: informed participation may be conflicted, prices lack inspectable reasoning, and cancellations distort the validation record. A showed these were limitations rather than total knockouts, keeping the debate competitive, but B more clearly explained why they support supplementation rather than reduced reliance mainly on experts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal turned A’s voided-contract concession into a selection problem: the performance record would be strongest where markets changed nothing and silent where their influence caused cancellation. A’s closing partly blunted this by noting that projects can change while still proceeding, but did not fully restore the case for broader predictive primacy.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B wins because it kept the motion's burden in view: replacing mainly expert reliance, not merely publishing a useful forecast. Its informed-versus-clean dilemma, refusal-to-transfer point on milestone accuracy, and cross-examination objection survived A's safeguards. A's strongest wedge—that outperforming markets deserve weight on cost and delivery—was effectively answered as a scoped supplement, especially after A conceded markets do not gain authority over causal policy questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: voided contracts cannot build the performance record meant to justify influence, leaving that record drawn from cases where the market changed nothing, while its anonymity argument answered A's supposedly independent-trader setting.
