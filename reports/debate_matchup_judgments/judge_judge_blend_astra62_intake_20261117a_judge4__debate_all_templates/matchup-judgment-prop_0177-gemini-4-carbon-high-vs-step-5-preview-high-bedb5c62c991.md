### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by turning the timeline itself into the decisive clash. The compound motion requires routine deployment within fifteen years, and B's dilemma—staged gates that wait for multi-year durability are just phased trials renamed (yielding a niche therapy), while anything faster scales unfinished grafts on short-term markers—was never squarely answered. A's "regulated learning compounds; the clock runs only when patients are treated" was a clever reframe but supplied no arithmetic showing sequential cohorts plus breeding cycles reach routine scale by the fifteen-year mark. A's biosecurity reversal (registries beat fragmented compassionate use) was its best moment and initially damaged B, but B recovered by noting phased trials already enforce sentinel herds and lot tracing, and that monitored hosts returning home cannot be mass-quarantined after a missed latent virus. A's pressure Q2 on CRISPR-era speed was answered cleanly: gene editing accelerates building a prototype pig, not multi-year human survival readouts. Both sides were well-grounded, used plausible background knowledge without fake citations, followed format, and landed memorable lines, but B made the debate easier to decide and controlled the weighing on the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3: the contradiction trap. B seized on A's staged-pathway concession and forced the fork—either the gates genuinely wait for multi-year endpoints (making it phased trials, defeating "routine within fifteen years") or expansion runs on short-term markers (subjecting fragile dialysis patients to early graft loss and sensitization). A's closing (A5.3) restated the gates without resolving the timing math, leaving B's dilemma standing as the debate's decisive exchange.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won by maintaining the relevant comparison: disciplined trials versus accelerated rollout, not acceleration versus paralysis. B's staged-access answer usefully identified dialysis-failing patients, but never sufficiently connected conditional expansion to routine care within fifteen years or established its advantage over ordinary trials. A more effectively distinguished experimental success from durable, scalable treatment. The margin remains competitive because A overstated the inevitability of its timeline constraints and presented speculative spillover risks too categorically.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest rebuttal was that standard phased trials already provide screening, registries, and comparative evidence, undermining B's claim that broad deployment is necessary for structured learning. B eventually conceded those trial safeguards but did not adequately establish why accelerated approval uniquely enables safe scaling.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won by forcing a fork the motion cannot survive. If staged gates truly wait for multi-year durability, biosecurity, and the gene-edit combination needed before sterile herds can scale, accelerated pathways collapse into ordinary phased trials and cannot make pig organs a routine solution to the shortage within fifteen years. If they expand faster on short-term markers, they risk early graft loss, sensitization, and community spillover from imperfect pathogen assays. Side A framed urgency and infrastructure well and rightly noted that dialysis is not a safe baseline, but never showed how regulated learning compounds into population-scale durable supply inside the biological clock.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal and closing crystallized the decisive fork: waiting for multi-year gates merely renames phased trials and yields niche rescue, while skipping that clock scales unfinished grafts and multiplies uncontainable risk.
