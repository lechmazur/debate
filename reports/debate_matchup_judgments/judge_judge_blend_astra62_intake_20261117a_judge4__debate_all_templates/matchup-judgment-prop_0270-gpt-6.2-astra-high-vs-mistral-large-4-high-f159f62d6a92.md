### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): A (CON) won by exposing the limits of B's core-goals frame with a concrete live counterexample: a child can sleep well, play freely, and tolerate boredom while still being harmed by a nonconsensual picture circulating in a group chat. B conceded the school must address the forwarding, but A showed that duty remains urgent regardless of the child's offline life, so sleep and play cannot supply the needed digital guidance. A also won the bedroom exchange by defending a less restrictive, app-specific mute that preserves a valued parent call over B's blanket phone removal. B's whack-a-mole concern stayed live, but A reframed targeted guidance as asking what needs to stop and who can stop it, not auditing every app.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's second rebuttal on the picture circulation: moving homework restores the child's choice to read the chat, but gives her no choice about being its subject, so no improvement in sleep, boredom, or free play makes the next forward acceptable. This turned B's own concession that schools must address the conduct into a reason why the core-goals frame is insufficient.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won the decisive clash. Its opening scenario—a child harmed by a specific group chat regardless of sleep, boredom, or play quality—was a live route PRO never neutralized. When pressed on what family offline life would change the school's duty, Side A could only reassert resilience and vulnerability, and Side B's "Notice who gets assigned the work" rebuttal (B4.3) exposed that the displacement frame quietly shifts responsibility toward the victim's capacities. Side B also flipped the bedroom example effectively: a one-time alert mute versus a nightly phone rule, and Side A's patches ("schedule it before bed," "use a landline") assumed facts not in evidence. Side B's "tomorrow's new messenger is not tomorrow's battle unless it causes trouble" blunted the whack-a-mole frame, and the closing ("Someone else pressed send") crystallized the burden memorably. Side A argued cleanly and stayed disciplined, with a solid sustainability story, but its answers to the bullying case amounted to conceding the conduct fix while retreating to foundation-building, which Side B showed was orthogonal to the harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal (B4.2–B4.4): conceding that moving homework restores the child's choice about reading the chat but not about being its subject, then asking what answer about the family's offline life would change the school's duty—this squarely dismantled Side A's resilience-based answer to the bullying example.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by showing that the proposed displacement goals can be fully met while concrete harms—circulating pictures, or alerts that can be muted without dropping a needed call—continue, and that those harms turn on others’ conduct or a narrow setting rather than the child’s sleep, boredom, or play. Side B’s scalability and “foundation first” case stayed coherent but never neutralized that mismatch of remedy to harm or the risk of shifting institutional duty onto the child’s resilience. A’s later speeches kept the “rather than” burden live and answered the pressure questions more directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal and closing: once homework is moved, the picture still circulates without consent, and no answer about the child’s offline life changes the school’s duty to stop the forwarders—while a blanket bedroom rule imposes costs the specific mute does not.
