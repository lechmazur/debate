### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A wins on the conditionality clash. PRO’s strongest case relied on safe-haven financing, timely recovery, and strong multipliers, but it never showed expansion is safe merely because debt is high. CON converted PRO’s own carve-out for market-access collapse into a concession that financing conditions can override the motion, then extended that point to ordinary high-debt cases where risk premia respond to deficit paths. A’s rebuttal that PRO held financing costs fixed at safe-issuer levels was decisive because it made PRO’s arithmetic conditional on the very assumption in dispute. PRO’s closing defaulted to weighing common versus rare mistakes, but did not repair the textual and economic hole.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s exploitation of PRO’s safe-issuer examples and carve-out: PRO conceded a financing-constraint exception, and CON showed that expansion works only when borrowing is already safe, not whenever debt is elevated. That turned PRO’s own examples into support for CON’s conditionality argument.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): This was a tightly matched, well-executed exchange. Side A (CON) ran a disciplined two-pronged attack: a semantic trap on "even if" (PRO's carve-outs for total market collapse supposedly concede the condition) and a substantive claim that spreads widen before scarring shows in forecasts. Both were pressed consistently and crystallized memorably. However, Side B (PRO) answered the pressure honestly and directly with proper A1/A2 structure, reframed the carve-out as a trivial tail case where no policy rule works, and—crucially—kept the debate on the motion's most natural reading: elevated debt alone should not trigger cuts to stabilizers. CON itself conceded in A4.1 that in ordinary high-debt downturns where markets still fund the sovereign, expansion beats slash-and-cut, which under PRO's reading of the motion is nearly the whole game. PRO's eurozone reinterpretation (crisis caused by premature slashing and no central bank backstop, not by initial stabilizer expansion) and its safe-issuer borrowing-cost examples held up, and its closing weighing of common self-defeating austerity against rare tail risk gave the cleaner reason to vote PRO. CON's residual route—the unconditionality reading plus the spread-widening zone—keeps this razor-thin rather than clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A4.3 ("markets do not price counterfactual scarring; they price debt service capacity on the horizon they can observe") was the sharpest substantive blow, exposing an asymmetry PRO never fully answered. But Side B's B2.1 reversal of the eurozone evidence—arguing Greece, Portugal, and Spain suffered from premature stabilizer cuts and a missing central bank backstop rather than from initial automatic expansion—did the most to neutralize CON's core empirical example and sustain PRO's path to victory.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B won by making the debate about the motion as written. Side A had the stronger general macroeconomic case for stabilizers in ordinary high-debt downturns, but it narrowed the motion to common cases and carved out active fiscal collapse, which Side B repeatedly turned into a concession that financing constraints can override expansion. Side B also landed the sharper comparative point that stabilizer outlays may reverse while the debt stock and interest obligations do not, and that elevated debt can affect spreads before scarring appears in forecasts. Side A’s answers were plausible but less responsive to the textual burden and occasionally overconfident, especially on interest arithmetic.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s rebuttals after pressure questions were decisive: they used Side A’s active-collapse carve-out to show the proposition was no longer being defended unconditionally, and paired that with the stock-versus-flow point that temporary stabilizer spending still adds permanent debt service from an elevated base.
