### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins clearly. Its strongest early move was showing that CON's preferred model already contains the motion's substance: rights that bind everyone and enabled language acquisition. That narrowed the real clash to the word "expected," and PRO defended that word with a concrete causal story. Expectation, on PRO's account, turns an offered service into a standard with enrolment, employer release and continuing funding. PRO also turned the Switzerland example against CON, since each Swiss locality still has a common public language. It then gave a workable boundary test for "core" norms: a rule counts only if it could bind a native-born family without objection. Finally, it pinned CON on the twenty-year-decliner scenario. CON answered that scenario honestly but conceded the outcome was "less satisfying," which PRO exploited well in closing ("dependence with better paperwork"). CON's best point was that "expected" may add pressure rather than any separate institutional delivery, and PRO's "two budget lines" reply was somewhat asserted rather than demonstrated. However, CON spent much of the debate attacking "cultural conformity," "uniformity" and "private assimilation." Those terms go beyond the motion and the policy PRO actually defended, so CON's resistance often answered a punitive policy PRO never proposed. CON's "already illegal for everyone" point had some force, but PRO reframed it convincingly: legal rights are only fully usable when a person can invoke them directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The key exchange came in Rebuttal 2. CON admitted that a newcomer who declines classes for twenty years stays permanently interpreted, a "less satisfying" result. PRO's closing then turned this into the debate's defining image: one model ends with her speaking for herself, the other with an interpreter, "still accurate, still needed." This undercut CON's claim that expectation adds nothing beyond access.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B built the stronger comparative case by separating equal access and public participation from cultural conformity. A relied heavily on the claim that expectation generates funding and independence, but B showed equal-access duties can support classes, interpretation, and access without making fluency a test of belonging. B also answered A’s sharpest pressure directly: a person declining classes remains a rights-holder served by qualified interpretation, which is accessibility rather than permanent dependence. A’s closing was rhetorically powerful but targeted a punitive withdrawal of services that B never endorsed. B better neutralized the causal and definitional routes to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 was decisive: it turned A’s “what does encouragement deliver?” challenge by arguing expectation adds pressure and conditional belonging rather than new institutional delivery, and it answered the 20-year question with professional interpretation as rights-preserving access.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): CON won by narrowing the debate to the marginal effect of making integration non-optional, then showing that PRO could not identify a benefit that funded classes and ordinary need do not already supply. - **Fork and unbundling:** The opening fork was "no consequence means no addition; a consequence means it falls on the weakest." CON also pointed out that the civic norms are already binding on everyone, which left the language condition as the only new demand. Together these shaped the whole exchange. - **PRO's escape route:** PRO's answer was humane exemptions. CON effectively turned this into "the expectation binds only where you've waived it." - **The increment exposed:** CON's pressure question forced PRO to name a concrete consequence, which was deferral of permanent status. CON then argued this was the entire increment and that it misfires, because refusal and inability look identical on file. - **PRO's strengths:** PRO's answers were honest and reasonably moderate. The functional-competence standard and "interpretation as bridge, not destination" were decent. - **PRO's weaknesses:** PRO never offered a strong independent mechanism, whether expressive, signaling, or behavioral, for why the expectation itself raises acquisition. Its closing leaned on abstract reciprocity and "direction of travel." PRO's own Q1 was also muddled, attributing a "non-optional" expectation to CON. CON's weighing ("direction is free; the deferral is not") gave the cleaner ballot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 took PRO's A1 answer (deferring permanent status for deliberate refusers) and recast it as the motion's sole increment. CON argued it teaches no one any language, and that because discretion cannot distinguish refusal from inability, it lands on the illiterate and the overworked. This neutralized PRO's attempt to give "not optional" real content.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B won by exposing the central weakness in PRO's case: either the expectation has no real consequence, or its residual consequence is withholding permanent status. PRO's exemptions already removed the people most likely to struggle, but B showed the remaining enforcement would fall on borderline cases where inability and refusal are hard to distinguish. B also turned PRO's own admission into a comparative harm argument, arguing that funded classes, workplace need, and ordinary contact already produce integration without adding a probationary sanction. PRO never showed a clear marginal benefit that outweighed that cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it took PRO's admission that the consequence is deferred permanent status and converted it into a harm ledger, arguing it buys no additional fluency while penalizing the very people PRO had exempted.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won solidly by isolating and dismantling the exact marginal effect of making integration non-optional. Side B showed early that core legal norms already bind everyone equally and that everyday practical need plus funded classes already motivate newcomers who are able to learn. Pressing the tension between Side A's claim to be "neither punitive nor coercive" and a non-optional mandate, Side B forced Side A to exempt vulnerable groups and pin its enforcement mechanism on deferring permanent status for working-age adults who deliberately refuse instruction. Side B decisively won the resulting comparison: conditionality adds no extra fluency beyond workplace and school necessity, while requiring officials to separate deliberate refusal from inability inevitably traps overworked, unschooled, or traumatized newcomers in prolonged legal precarity. Side A made a sensible point that Geneva still relies on a local public language and framed interpretation well as a transitional bridge, but it never proved conditionality's marginal benefit and squandered its Pressure Questions turn by misdirecting its questions against its own side's position.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In Turn 6, Side B's pressure questions forced Side A to identify who remained bound after its exemptions and what sanction applied, prompting Side A in [A4.1] to specify deferred permanent status for working-age "deliberate refusers." Side B's follow-up in [B4.1]-[B4.2] and [B5.1]-[B5.2] was the decisive blow of the debate, showing that deferral buys zero language acquisition while inevitably landing on the illiterate, traumatized, and double-shift workers because inability and refusal look identical in an administrative file.
