### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued at a high level with clean A1/A2 compliance and real progression. Side B's strongest route—custody transfer as the bright line, community delay as authority rather than leverage—was principled and directly responsive. But Side A repeatedly landed the more damaging comparative moves: exposing that B's own framework (interim custodian, optional testing, "no irreversible burial follows" on refusal) requires years of waiting anyway, so "limbo is just my rule with the knowledge deleted"; and that B quietly redefined "return promptly" as a transfer of institutional control rather than the ancestor actually going home. B's detention/sovereign-timeline distinction answered some of this, but never fully resolved why a council-controlled unburied ancestor satisfies "return promptly" as written. Side A's crystallization of the mis-affiliation asymmetry (remediable delay vs. permanent wrong burial, or no burial at all) survived the exchange as a live route, while B's final weighing relied more on assertion than on resolving A's strongest pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 ([A4.5]-[A4.7]) was decisive: it caught B conceding that "promptly" is satisfied when control changes hands even if the body sits unburied for years, then framed the whole debate as a choice between two kinds of waiting—one permitted to learn whose relative this is, and one forbidden to. B's "custody is not the fee for making a request" [B5.5] was the best counter, but the "limbo with the knowledge deleted" frame better captured the motion as written.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by making Side A's retreat the decisive clash. Once affiliation is unknown, a categorical ban on study-based delay either risks a permanent wrong return or, as Side A's own council model conceded, leaves the ancestor in unresolved custody. Side B showed that calling a transfer of control "prompt return" still does not get the body to the right people, while the identification work Side A sidelined is often what makes return possible. Side A's consent-versus-leverage principle stayed forceful, but it never closed who receives the body or why an interested hold is worse than a claimant process allowed to learn.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal: the joint council, permanent standing, and ban on irreversible burial produced limbo rather than return, so the proposal became "my rule with the knowledge deleted."
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO's custody-versus-reburial distinction survived the exchange: prompt transfer to rights-holders ends institutional leverage while still allowing consent-gated affiliation study. CON powerfully exposed contested-provenance and limbo risks, but PRO avoided irreversible misburial through joint custody and never justified why uncertainty should preserve the original possessor's veto.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: contested remains go to a jointly chosen rights-holder custodian, testing may be requested but is not the price of release, and no irreversible burial follows, directly blunting CON's wrong-burial and first-filer objections.
