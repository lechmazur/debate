- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side A built and sustained the stronger path to victory. B's counter that chance is part of the product was coherent but defensive and arrived too late to neutralize the framing. B's distinction between household-level tools and platform-scale identification was fair, but A correctly noted opt-in tools never reach children of inattentive parents. B's line-drawing pressure was sharp and forced A to concede card packs are covered, but A absorbed it by arguing that is the goal, not over-breadth. Both sides were grounded, used no fake sourcing, and followed format; A's clipped opening and rebuttal are minor execution costs that did not obscure the substance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's closing built on B's [B4.2] admission: B claimed aggregate moderate spending from packs because teens "will not buy" a rare item at its transparent expected cost, and A crystallized this as proof that the revenue model depends on children not seeing the price — the single strongest reason for default protection offered by either side.
