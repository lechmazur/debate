### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won clearly. It built a coherent case early: a monument is primary evidence of propaganda, museum display can strip it of authority, and destruction is irreversible. It then sharpened that case through the exchanges. The Klan robes and Nazi art analogy forced CON to argue that statues are uniquely untamable, and that distinction was never convincingly justified. The reversibility question was decisive. CON's A2 answer effectively conceded that destruction "eliminates the option" while relocation "permits contest and withdrawal," which reads as an argument for the motion. CON's strongest material was that context is fragile and that museum framing carries no guarantee. It also argued that the motion overclaims universality. However, CON's narrowed "conditional destruction" position left it unable to show why destruction beats correctable curation. Its closing conceded the loss is "real" and was clipped before completing its final weighing. PRO's closing used CON's own concessions effectively, without introducing new material.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 asked why irreversible destruction is safer than a museum display that can always be reformed. CON's A2 answer conceded that destruction is irrevocable and that relocation preserves future options. PRO then turned this in B4.4–B4.5 and B5.2 into the decisive risk-asymmetry weighing that CON never recovered from.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): A establishes the stronger comparative case: relocation ends compulsory civic honor while retaining material evidence and opportunities to revise interpretation. B correctly challenges the equation of destruction with historical amnesia and identifies plausible risks of renewed veneration. However, B never adequately weighs those risks against preservation’s benefits, repeatedly substitutes a universal guarantee for the motion’s comparative claim, and expressly concedes preservation’s option value. A’s concrete curation mechanisms and closing exploitation of that concession make the choice clearer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing directly exploits B4.2’s admission that destruction eliminates future choices while relocation permits contest and withdrawal. B’s response acknowledges that loss but merely restates the possibility of veneration, leaving A’s reversibility advantage insufficiently answered.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A built the cleaner comparative case: removal ends compulsory civic honor, while museum preservation retains physical evidence of propaganda in a voluntary, critical setting. Side B ably stressed that curation is contingent and affected communities bear the risk, but never convincingly explained why grounded monuments are uniquely less curatable than other domination artifacts. B's concessions that relocation is often preferable and that destruction irreversibly eliminates future options left A's reversible-preservation argument standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's use of B4.2 in closing: B acknowledged destruction is irrevocable and removes future choices while relocation permits contest and revision, converting B's irreversibility answer into strong support for A's asymmetry-of-risk argument.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) wins a clear but competitive debate. It built a coherent alternative of documented destruction: archives, scans, preserved fragments and repurposed bronze. It argued that this keeps the historical record while removing the monument's intimidating aura. CON then set a sharp dilemma. Storage educates no one, while display costs space and money and risks glorification. PRO's answers to that dilemma created openings. PRO conceded that storage alone fails to educate. Its proposals of rotation and digital access invited CON's strong reply: heavy statues cannot easily be rotated, and leaning on digital access concedes CON's core point that records carry the educational value. PRO's best route was irreversibility and option value, the idea that destruction forecloses future reinterpretation. That point stayed live but was never fully weighed against CON's zero-sum resource argument. PRO's objecthood claim also leaned toward the "encounter with an object made to command space," which CON reframed effectively as propaganda still doing its work. CON had weaknesses. It overclaimed that scans capture weathered material, and its "pilgrimage sites" worry was speculative. Even so, it set the terms of the clash more decisively and pressed it more consistently.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's warehouse-or-gallery trap ([A2.3]–[A2.5]), followed by [A4.3]–[A4.4]. That sequence turned PRO's concession that storage fails to educate, and its reliance on digital access, into evidence for CON's documentation-over-objecthood position.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B better established why destroying a monument need not erase its history and made preservation's opportunity costs central. A defended contextualization and future options, but insufficiently explained why intact originals add enough value beyond documentation and fragments to justify their continuing costs. B's overclaims about preserving every historical fact and museum display necessarily perpetuating reverence prevent a wider win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B exposed the tension between A's storage fallback and its visitor-education rationale. A honestly conceded that storage alone does not educate and proposed rotation and loans, but B effectively pressed why those remedies still require costly handling, conservation, and exhibition rather than resolving the resource tradeoff.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B wins by forcing a dilemma A never escaped: storage defeats the promised public education, while display preserves the monument's intimidating aura at finite museum cost to victims' artifacts. B's scans-plus-fragments alternative plausibly preserves historical information without preserving the intact instrument of domination, outweighing A's irreversibility and objecthood claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2, which turned A's reliance on storage, rotation, and digital access into a concession that physical preservation is either unseen, impractical for massive monuments, or replaceable by documentation.
