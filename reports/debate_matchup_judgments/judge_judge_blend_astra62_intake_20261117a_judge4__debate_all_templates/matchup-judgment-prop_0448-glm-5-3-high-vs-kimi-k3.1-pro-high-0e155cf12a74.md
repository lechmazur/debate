### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but PRO's recurring symmetry argument—that every objection CON raised (capping, rationing, remote access, political dependency, pretext abuse) applies equally to the resettlement/evacuation/national schemes CON endorsed as alternatives—was never fully neutralized. CON's main counter, that those alternatives make "narrower promises," is largely a rhetorical distinction about what is claimed rather than a substantive difference in what the policies deliver, and CON's closing did not meaningfully rebuild against this point. CON did land real structural hits (the distribution/responsibility problem after approval, the risk that overseas refusal becomes a pretext to harden territorial deterrence), but PRO answered the pretext point by noting deterrence already exists without the visa and offered a non-preclusion safeguard, and PRO's closing correctly exposed CON's shift from "this instrument is flawed" to "CON's own preferred instruments are equally flawed but somehow exempt." That symmetry move, combined with clean A1/A2 compliance in the pressure-question rebuttal, gives PRO the cleaner final path to the resolution. CON's case is strong and competitive but ultimately rests on an overclaim/underclaim distinction that doesn't fully escape PRO's reductio. PRO's case, however, suffered a real execution cost: it was clipped for exceeding word limits in three separate turns (opening, rebuttal 1, rebuttal 2), versus CON's one clipped turn, which tempers the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing move in [B5.2]-[B5.3], turning CON's endorsed alternatives (resettlement, evacuation, national schemes) into equally "selective, capped, remotely processed, and dependent on political commitments," which CON's final speech never fully answered beyond restating that those schemes "promise less."
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won because the proposition requires a beneficial unified channel, not proof that it would eliminate smuggling or solve every distribution dispute. Side A effectively exposed B’s systemic overclaims, accessibility limits, and unresolved allocation problem, but never showed that these defects outweighed the concrete benefit of safe admission for at least some applicants. Its preferred alternatives shared many constraints and were not shown to preclude adding the proposed channel. B’s safeguards also reduced the externalization concern, though its repeated clipping and occasional overstatement kept the debate competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest response was that caps, remote screening, and political dependence also characterize Side A’s preferred resettlement and evacuation schemes. Side A answered that those programs make narrower promises, but that limited the force of B’s rhetoric without establishing why an additional unified visa should be rejected.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around a practical comparison rather than an absolute standard. Side A effectively proved that Side B's proposal would face severe political constraints and wouldn't perfectly solve irregular migration, but Side B successfully trapped Side A by pointing out that Side A's preferred alternatives (resettlement, national schemes) suffer from the exact same limitations (caps, remote processing, political pledges). Side B demonstrated that an imperfect unified visa is still a net improvement over the status quo.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first pressure question and its payoff in Closing exposed Side A's reliance on alternatives that shared the exact same flaws as the proposition, forcing Side A into a weak distinction based on "narrower promises."

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON built a cumulative case around three concrete mechanisms (magnet effect, stranded-applicant problem, EU architecture failure) and sharpened them in rebuttal into a decisive asymmetry: benefits from the visa are capped by approval rate, while costs from refusal/waiting are uncapped, since refused applicants retain every incentive to pursue smuggling routes anyway. CON's pressure questions (demand for a processing-capacity number and a specific member-state binding mechanism) exposed real gaps, and PRO's answers conceded both points ("no defensible single number exists," "no state should be bound unilaterally... common EU legislation can establish") without closing them. PRO's case rested on a strong moral frame and the valid point that approved applicants would stop using smugglers, but PRO never rebutted the asymmetry mechanism itself, instead calling it an "assumption" without supplying a counter-mechanism. PRO's Rebuttal 1 was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, a real execution cost that lost potential responses. CON's closing crystallized the clash cleanly around the asymmetry and the unanswered specifics; PRO's closing restated the moral stakes without resolving the capped-benefit/uncapped-cost tension. This gives CON a clear, competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Questions (A3.1/A3.2) demanding a concrete processing cap and a specific member-state binding mechanism, which PRO could only answer by disclaiming precision and gesturing at future legislation ([B4.1]-[B4.3]), directly feeding CON's "design is not evidence" and asymmetry framing in Rebuttal 2 and Closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a concrete, conceded benefit: approved applicants would substitute lawful travel for dangerous crossings. Side A’s memorable “capped subtraction, uncapped addition” framing never sufficiently proved that applications, waits, or refusals would translate into enough new boat journeys to outweigh those avoided. B also answered the access objection by retaining vulnerability-based resettlement and reasonably treated political difficulty as a design challenge rather than a refutation of the policy. A exposed genuine capacity and implementation risks, and B’s clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost, but A repeatedly converted possible backlogs and political resistance into certain net harm without enough grounding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response in B4.4 was decisive: A conceded that approved applicants would avoid smugglers, while its claim of higher total crossings required an unproven leap from additional or rejected applicants to additional dangerous journeys exceeding those prevented.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully establishing the 'induction plus rationing' mechanism, showing that a visa would create uncapped new demand while only offering capped approvals. Side A effectively weaponized Side B's reliance on ideal legislative design, pressing them on political feasibility and capacity limits to win the pragmatic clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively answered Side B's pressure questions by contrasting 'rationed subtraction' (capped approvals) with 'uncapped addition' (unlimited new applicants drawn to transit hubs).
