### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner path to victory by keeping the debate on the proposition's core condition: whether a person can complete the transaction without making the body the credential. PRO successfully clarified that "convenient" does not mean equal speed, but does mean an ordinary, adjacent, fee-free, peak-usable non-biometric route. This defused CON's contradiction charge and turned CON's queue, market-exit, and staff-override answers into evidence that the fallback was not always present. CON raised real overbreadth, cost, and queue-relief concerns, but PRO's burden reversal and point that governance/hash security protects data rather than the refusal itself were more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that "go next door is exit, not payment" and that a re-salted hash protects a database, not the refusal to give a finger, most sharply exposed why CON's market-choice and security alternatives did not satisfy the proposition's always-present non-biometric condition.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B gave the clearer and more consistent decision rule: a fallback may be somewhat slower, but must remain ordinary and usable when access matters. Its distinction between payment and exit, notice, or post-failure overrides directly answered CON’s main alternatives. Side A raised formidable overbreadth and queue-relief objections, especially for competitive cafes and crowded stations, keeping the debate close; however, parts of its case relied on overstated claims that convenient means equal speed or that supplemented formats would necessarily be banned, and its template-security argument did not answer exclusion or refusal at the transaction itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first and second rebuttals broke the alleged equal-speed contradiction and explained why “go next door,” signage, and staff overrides are not convenient non-biometric payment. A’s queue-math response was the strongest counterpressure, but did not fully close that gap.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side A built the cleaner decision rule: convenient means an ordinary, usable non-biometric route at the moment of payment, not equal speed. That answered CON's contradiction charge, exposed exit, notice, and staff overrides as substitutes for actually paying, and showed governance protects enrollees rather than preserving refusal. CON's space, cost, and competitive-shop objections remained forceful but relied on replacing the proposition with narrower transit-only safeguards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its peak-usability distinction allowed slower card lanes while treating predictable train-missing queues as enrollment by exhaustion, directly dissolving CON's alleged contradiction.
