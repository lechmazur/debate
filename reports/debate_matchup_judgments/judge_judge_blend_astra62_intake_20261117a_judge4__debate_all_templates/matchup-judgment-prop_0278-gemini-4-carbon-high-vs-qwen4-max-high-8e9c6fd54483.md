### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO wins on the comparative test it set early, which CON accepted: least-bad means beating a named rival. CON named a strong rival, cantonal consociational federation, and pressed the settler problem effectively. Its points on settler scale, Jerusalem and highland security were PRO's most serious vulnerabilities. However, PRO's B2.4 point was never neutralised. Federation requires Israel to accept Palestinian parity at the centre of its own state, which is a larger concession than territorial withdrawal. PRO also showed that federation's protections remain licensed by the army that holds the single perimeter. Under pressure questioning, CON answered by adopting PRO's own external guarantors, joint policing and buyouts. It then added parity vetoes and a requirement that armed factions disarm before holding office. PRO's closing exploited this cleanly: CON's plan becomes PRO's plan with extra trust demands attached. PRO also answered its own pressure questions honestly. It conceded that deep-ridge evacuations and a divided Jerusalem would be required, and it turned the enforcement question back onto federation. CON's closing landed a fair hit: a demilitarised Palestine also implies disarmament. Its line about budgets freezing while cities burn was memorable. Even so, it did not repair the core asymmetry over which plan asks more of Israeli sovereignty. The result is a clear but competitive PRO edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came in PRO's rebuttal 2 and closing (B4.2–B4.6 and B5.1–B5.3), responding to CON's A4.1–A4.2. PRO showed that an army breaching a border creates a named, priceable violation. Under federation, by contrast, Israel's army is lawfully present everywhere, so a broken lock is only a domestic dispute. PRO then showed that CON's answers borrowed partition's guarantors and added parity and disarmament preconditions. Together these inverted CON's claim that federation is the lower-trust path.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a high-quality, closely fought debate. B set a clear comparative frame in the opening ("least-bad" as a comparison of alternatives, "separation is the low-trust technology"), ran the alternatives efficiently, and then consistently narrowed the clash to the sharpest question: whose hardest step is smaller? B's rebuttals were the debate's best work — repossessing Northern Ireland ("it works because partition already happened"), reframing Gaza as separation without a treaty, and delivering the decisive turn that CON's "one overarching authority" is Israel's army, making federation "licensed self-rule with a cantonal flag." B answered both pressure questions directly and honestly (conceding the settler evacuation is painful but noting Israel has done it before), then crystallized superbly: CON's credible version of federation borrowed PRO's guarantors, buyouts, and policing while adding harder conditions, and "a shared sovereign cannot be unshared." Side A fought well: the geography rebuttal to the Sinai analogy, the Dayton counter-turn, and the closing catch that PRO's "no reconciliation needed" contradicts its demilitarised-Palestine requirement were genuinely damaging, and "when power-sharing stumbles, budgets freeze; when partition fails, cities burn" was the debate's best single line. But A repeatedly had to invent new machinery (joint federal police, constitutional courts, concurrent majorities) under pressure, and never fully escaped B's core dilemma that its plan asks the distrusted sovereign to voluntarily lock itself into permanent parity. B made the debate easier to decide and controlled the weighing throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (Turn 7): answering the monitors question with "monitors make crossing legible and expensive" and "partition converts unchecked discretion into an attributable breach," then narrowing the whole debate to "both plans need Israeli consent; mine asks for a withdrawal Israel has actually performed; theirs asks Israel to dissolve sovereignty" — this directly dismantled A's live route and set up the winning closing.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B won a close, high-quality debate by keeping the motion comparative and supplying the cleaner achievability test: a difficult but precedented withdrawal and guaranteed border versus Israel voluntarily accepting permanent binational parity. A seriously damaged B on settlers, highlands security, and demilitarisation, but its federation ultimately required the same external enforcement plus additional consent, disarmament, and policing burdens. B’s repairable-failure comparison therefore survived more intact.

A’s direct joint-policing and parity-veto answers were resourceful, yet did not establish that these cumulative burdens were lighter than settler evacuation.
