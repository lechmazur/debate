### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Both sides were strong and responsive, but CON won the decisive clashes. CON's core mechanism — that a firm facing parity demands will delete targeted discounts and keep the inflated uniform baseline — was turned cleverly against PRO's own "ledger" argument ("PRO has described my harm and called it a remedy"), and PRO's competition-based answer conceded that the mandate is redundant where rivals exist, completing CON's pincer: unnecessary where competition works, harmful where buyers are captive. CON's financial-aid/negotiated-car analogy rebutted PRO's structural claim that beneficial pricing survives disclosure, and PRO never distinguished it; CON's "log out, ask, try a rival — available today" dilemma in B4.1 also went unanswered. PRO scored real points by forcing CON's concession of a regulator-disclosure duty and framing the "confession" in closing, but CON held the more faithful reading of the motion (PRO's own opening framed disclosure as shopper notice, making the later two-level pivot look like a retreat), and CON's final "a discount that cannot survive parity is not a fraud; it is a discount" blunted PRO's central frame. PRO's office-supply and captive-buyer points landed, but not enough to outweigh CON's cleaner final weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.2, where CON inverted PRO's inflated-baseline argument — if list price is inflated by targeting, forced uniformity keeps the inflated number and deletes the discount — converting PRO's strongest mechanism into CON's harm. PRO's A4.3 competition answer partially defused it, but B4.3–B4.4 completed the pincer and it was never fully undone.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): A wins through the motion’s disclosure alternative. B convincingly challenged A’s claim that transparency cannot destroy beneficial discounts, particularly through its cannibalization argument. But B repeatedly treated disclosure as requiring price parity or revealing every customer’s deal. A established a narrower route: mandatory regulator-facing disclosure enables oversight without necessarily producing those harms. B expressly accepted a duty to supply models, then asserted rather than demonstrated that this fell outside the proposition. That unresolved concession gives A the stronger overall path, despite weaknesses in its claims about inflated baselines and discount funding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first pressure question exposed that B’s preferred audit regime could itself satisfy “disclosed.” A then distinguished standing disclosure duties from occasional investigations. B accepted mandatory model access but never adequately answered why that disclosure would not support the motion.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B wins a high-level, competitive debate by better isolating the marginal effect of the motion's presumption against secret customer-specific pricing. Side A built a compelling opening and scored strongly in Turn 3 by showing that geographic markups and insurance price optimization could not be policed without visibility. However, Side B smartly conceded regulatory model access and testing rights while showing that shopper-facing notice reveals nothing about systemic ZIP-code proxies and instead triggers inframarginal parity demands that wipe out deep targeted discounts—backing the mechanism with unrefuted analogies to financial aid and negotiated car prices. Side A's late bid to count confidential regulatory filing as satisfying the motion clashed with its own core case against keeping full-price customers in the dark, while Side B closed with an unanswered pincer showing uniformity is redundant where competition works and harmful to poor buyers where markets are captive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A argued in [A2.3] that personalization inflates list prices to fund discounts, Side B used [B2.2] and Q2 [B3.2] to force Side A to explain which uniform price survives, then turned Side A's answer [A4.3]-[A4.4] into a decisive pincer in [B4.3]-[B4.5]: competition already pulls prices down where rivals exist, uniformity locks everyone into the inflated baseline where buyers are captive, and replacing targeted offers with universal coupon rules flattens deep discounts into shallow ones.
