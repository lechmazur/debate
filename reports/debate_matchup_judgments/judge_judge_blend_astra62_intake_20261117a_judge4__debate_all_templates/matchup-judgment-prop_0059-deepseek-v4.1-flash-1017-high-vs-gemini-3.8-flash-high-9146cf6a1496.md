### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and stayed on-topic, but PRO (B) controlled the decisive measurement question of the debate: whether aggregate success stories (apps as "leading source of marriages") actually tell us anything about the median user's experience. B's lottery/scratch-card and toll-bridge analogies repeatedly forced CON to confront the composition fallacy of treating visible winners as proof of typical outcomes, and CON's answers in Rebuttal 2 and Closing kept reasserting the same aggregate statistic ("apps are the leading source of marriages, therefore odds are higher") without ever supplying an actual median-level mechanism distinct from "more access exists." CON's baseline argument (apps vs. isolation) was strong and never fully neutralized, and its Q2 pressure question exposed that PRO's "worse for most" claim lacks hard evidence of counterfactual harm — PRO's answer reframed rather than resolved this. But on balance, PRO's structural account (queues, pay-to-be-seen visibility auctions, context-stripped swiping, frictionless ghosting) gave a clearer causal story for *why* access without quality filtering could leave most users worse off, and CON never landed a rebuttal that closed that specific gap. That unresolved asymmetry is the deciding factor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 "three substitutions" (baseline, aggregate-vs-median, match-vs-mutual-intent) combined with the Pressure Q1 asking what measurable outcome would ever falsify CON's position — CON's answer doubled down on the same aggregate marriage statistic rather than supplying a genuine median-user mechanism, leaving that critique live through the closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): B won by insisting on the relevant real-world comparison and showing that expanded discovery, mutual interest, and basic filtering enable relationships that otherwise may never begin. A offered strong mechanisms of fatigue, superficiality, and misaligned incentives, but never established that these costs make most users worse off relative to available alternatives. Both sides overclaimed median outcomes without firm support, but A's admission that no alternative currently matches the model's scale strengthened B's case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to the toll-bridge analogy was decisive: congestion and tolls can be harmful, but a flawed bridge may still improve formation compared with being unable to cross. This exposed A's failure to prove that most users would have a better path without the dominant model.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A successfully distinguished between the existence of a path to relationship formation (aggregate marriages) and the median user's experience within the dominant model's specific design (endless queues, visibility auctions). Side A's toll bridge and lottery analogies effectively dismantled Side B's reliance on a "grim baseline" and aggregate success stories. Side B struggled to defend the median user's experience against Side A's structural critiques of the swipe-and-subscribe mechanism, often conflating mere introductions with actual relationship formation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they deployed the toll bridge analogy to explain how a system can be the primary route to a destination while still being structurally worse for most users due to extractive design, perfectly neutralizing Side B's "stranded on the riverbank" argument.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) controlled the decisive comparative frame from the opening—"worse than what?"—and held PRO to the motion's outcome-based burden throughout. PRO (B) built a vivid and original mechanism story (engagement-optimized apps monetizing singlehood, disposability eroding patience), but repeatedly conflated process frustration (ghosting, burnout, choice overload) with the required outcome claim that most users end up with fewer or worse relationships than the realistic alternative. A's pressure questions forced B to state a falsification standard (A4.2/B4.2: proportion converting to lasting relationships), and A then correctly noted in Rebuttal 2 and Closing that B never met that standard, only asserted churn. B's own pressure questions were reasonable but A's answers (market competition/churn selecting against pure-frustration platforms, organic dating also permitting silent cheap exits) adequately blunted them. B's closing mostly restated the mechanism story without closing the evidentiary gap A had exposed, while A's closing crisply crystallized the unresolved burden ("a tax, not a deficit"). This made A's path to victory cleaner and more resolved by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.5–A4.7), which used B's own stated falsification criterion from the Pressure Questions answers to show PRO had only asserted outcomes rather than demonstrated them, directly undercutting PRO's central mechanism-to-outcome leap.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side B won by enforcing the motion’s comparative burden: apps must be judged against realistic, often sparse adult dating alternatives rather than an idealized organic community. Side A offered plausible mechanisms of burnout, disposability, and choice overload, but repeatedly inferred worse relationship outcomes for most users without establishing that net result. B also effectively separated platform incentives from actual outcomes and showed how wider pools and cheap screening can remain beneficial despite an unpleasant process. A’s repeated nonexistent C-reference IDs were an additional execution flaw.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used A’s own proposed falsification standard against A, noting that A had supplied no comparative evidence of fewer or less durable partnerships. It also answered the incentive critique by distinguishing a motive story from an outcome and grounding the comparison in thin post-college social pools.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully establishing the realistic counterfactual for most adults (a thin, non-replenishing social pool) and showing that Side A's harms (burnout, ghosting, choice overload) are costs of a larger search space rather than proof of a net decrease in relationship formation. Side A's reliance on an idealized "third places" baseline weakened their comparative case, allowing Side B to secure the decisive weighing mechanism that a frustrating process can still be the best available route to a relationship compared to the alternative of staying single in a sparse market.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of Side A's "organic social ecosystem" baseline in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2, effectively showing that for most post-college adults, the alternative to apps is not a rich village but a sparse, accidental pool, making the app's expanded search space a net positive despite the psychological friction.
