### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by holding the motion's burden. PRO had to show a missing cognitive ability and an outsized role, but by rebuttal 2 PRO conceded that information-seeking need not be a separate faculty and reframed the case as a generic anti-veto procedural rule. CON showed that any threshold on any measure excludes unseen evidence, so PRO's complaint was about scarcity rather than IQ-style testing specifically. PRO's strongest point—that tests supply information but do not measure seeking it—was substantially neutralized by CON's dilemma: reliable measures of information-seeking would track general reasoning, while unreliable ones would reward exposure. CON also absorbed the plausible improvement by allowing supplementation by formula. PRO remained competitive but never rebuilt a narrowness case specific to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's decisive turn exploited PRO's concession that no separate information-seeking faculty was established: once PRO admitted that, the remaining case became a generic cutoff complaint that would survive swapping the IQ test for grades or portfolios.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B won the motion as written. A made a sharp case that supplied-item puzzles do not directly show who knows what to ask, and that a screening veto can silence other evidence. But B kept the burden on “too narrow a band,” showed that A never established a missed cognitive ability, and converted A’s concession that no separate faculty was needed into a scarcity complaint that would apply to any threshold. B’s incremental-validity rule, cross-domain prediction, and universal-screening comparison gave the cleaner reason to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: after A admitted nothing established information-seeking as a distinct ability, B treated the remainder as experience or noise, showed a composite still has a cutoff, and argued that one afternoon task should not override the steadier measure.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B prevailed by separating predictive breadth from cognitive breadth: strong average prediction does not show that supplying all relevant information captures reasoning about what information to seek. A’s reliability, equity, and scarcity case was substantial, but it repeatedly treated B’s denial of a separate mental faculty as abandonment of the omitted-ability claim. B’s final distinction between weighting a score heavily and letting it preclude other evidence preserved a clear route to affirming the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal used the prompting and missing-control examples to show how equal test performance can conceal materially different reasoning, while directly accepting incremental-validity weighting rather than demanding equal weight. A’s scarcity and cutoff objections defended efficient screening but did not fully neutralize that distinction.
