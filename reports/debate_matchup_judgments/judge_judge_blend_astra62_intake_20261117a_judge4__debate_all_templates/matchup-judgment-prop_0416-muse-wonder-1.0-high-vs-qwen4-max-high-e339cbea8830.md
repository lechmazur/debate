### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): In an exceptionally sharp debate, Side A (CON) wins by proving that clinic-level egg-freezing outcomes cannot yet supply the "realistic age-based success rates" the proposition mandates. Side B (PRO) landed strong hits on metric-switching, format asymmetry, and the risk that conditional disclosure lets non-numeric slogans like "extend your fertility" escape scrutiny and encourages clinics to drop statistics. However, Side A neutralized the worst status-quo abuses by requiring prominent same-unit denominators whenever numbers are cited and banning "insurance" guarantees, while dismantling Side B's positive mechanism. Once Side B specified standardized clinic rates "per woman who froze" and "too few returns to estimate" for smaller clinics, Side A showed that right-censoring (most cohorts have not returned), self-selected returnees, older lab techniques, and unmeasured ovarian reserve create false comparability, penalize newer clinics, and risk deterring high-reserve older women with a state-backed population average before consultation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure question [A3.1] and follow-up in [A4.2-A4.3] against [B4.1]: after forcing Side B off vague qualitative warnings ("live births were uncommon") onto standardized clinic rates "per woman who froze" with "too few returns to estimate" for small programs, Side A showed that uniform labels cannot cure a right-censored denominator where most patients have not returned and older cohorts reflect outdated lab methods—turning prominence into false comparability and an unfair barrier against newer clinics.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that Side A’s conditional-disclosure alternative exempts numberless reassurance slogans that set the harmful optimistic anchor and even incentivizes clinics to drop statistics. Side B refined a workable mandate—standardized bands, per-starter denominators, intervals, and prominent “too few returns”—that presents realistic uncertainty without demanding false certainty. Side A’s data-quality and false-anchor objections were serious and well grounded, but they did not neutralize why honest prominent limitations outperform a regime that leaves the core misleading framing untouched.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s closing crystallizing that “extend your fertility” triggers nothing under Side A’s rule, so clinics can delete numbers, keep the unbounded promise, and punish the clinics that try to be informative.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger path by tying misleading advertising to irreversible delay, then answering concerns about unstable data with standardized bands, denominators, intervals, returnee counts, and explicit statements when estimates are impossible. A offered a serious critique of censored cohorts, false comparability, and mandated anchoring, but its conditional-disclosure alternative left a crucial gap: nonnumeric reassurance such as “extend your fertility” could remain prominent without age-based context. B identified that gap decisively, and A’s closing repeated its anchor objection rather than answering it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing response to A’s conditional-disclosure proposal was decisive: numeric claims would trigger obligations while vague reassurance could escape them, encouraging clinics to remove statistics but retain the promise. A never squarely repaired that loophole.
