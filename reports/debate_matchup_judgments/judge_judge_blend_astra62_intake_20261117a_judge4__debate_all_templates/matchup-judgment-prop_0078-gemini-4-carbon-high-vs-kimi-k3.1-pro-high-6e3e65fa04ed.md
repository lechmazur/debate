### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) wins because it held the motion as written while Side B (CON) had to concede most of its substance. Under pressure questioning, CON accepted several measures: naturalization language requirements, refusal of cultural exemptions in schools, agencies speaking to women directly, and social challenges to lawful sexism and shunning. Those are expectations, not optional integration, and PRO's closing made that point cleanly. CON argued sharply that "nonoptional" adds only stigma and a movable "core." The "fluent bigot" and "mother using an interpreter" examples were vivid. But CON increasingly fought an inflated version of the motion, one about "diminished belonging" and "inner identity" that PRO had explicitly disclaimed. PRO met the movable-core worry by tying core norms to constitutional rights. It met the stigma worry by treating waivers and translation as bridges within a shared standard. PRO also showed what the expectation adds: a naturalization standard for able adults and a stated destination that institutions work toward. CON's best remaining point was that PRO never showed how declaring an expectation speeds learning. That point is real, but it does not defeat a normative motion about what should be expected.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO's Q2 together with CON's A2 and PRO's closing. PRO asked whether lawful patriarchal pressure and shunning should be challenged or accepted as "voluntary" culture. CON answered that society should challenge them, a stance PRO then showed amounts to a nonoptional civic expectation. That collapsed CON's earlier framing of cultural integration as voluntary.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): A better defended the proposition as written: supported expectations of public language and civic conduct, not compulsory inner conformity or revocable belonging. A directly answered pressure about waivers, equal membership, and limits on core norms. B raised meaningful concerns about stigma and practical barriers, while A overstated how inevitably optional integration produces dependency. Nevertheless, B accepted flexible naturalization requirements and social criticism of lawful coercion, substantially overlapping with A’s model. B’s repeated objections that expectations cannot guarantee fluency or liberal convictions imposed a stronger burden than the motion required.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s closing effectively used B’s pressure answers: accepting language requirements and challenging lawful coercive practices undermined B’s rejection of shared public expectations. B’s distinction between institutional conduct and certified inner liberalism did not defeat A’s expressly public-practice standard.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): B forced PRO to narrow the motion to waivers, post-naturalization equality, and institutional policies CON also supports, leaving 'nonoptional' as stigma without a mechanism to produce fluency or liberal norms. A's unscripted-life agency argument was strong, but B answered it with rights, access, and participation during learning, while preserving the two-tier objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 concession trap: waived citizens gain membership without fluency and naturalized citizens keep it despite illiberal views, so PRO's expectation reduces to encouragement plus shared institutional rules wrapped in moral suspicion.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined and avoided fake precision. B edged it on rebuttal execution. B's fork in B2.2 held for the rest of the debate: either a non-optional expectation has institutional teeth and harms the vulnerable, or it has none and adds only stigma. A answered the pressure questions honestly and directly in A4.1 and A4.2, which deserves credit. Those answers, however, conceded a great deal: citizenship without fluency, permanent translation, protected enclave life, and no policing of private beliefs. B then tracked that narrowing precisely. It showed that civic norms had moved from shaping parental attitudes and neighborly deliberation (A2.3) to mere lawful public conduct (A4.2, A4.5). B argued that this reduces the civic half of the motion to ordinary law. That left only a "duty to try" on language. A's best defense was the literacy-goal analogy and the "capability versus managed dependence" frame. B blunted the analogy by distinguishing goals that bind institutions from blame placed on adults. B also noted that both sides fund classes, so A's destination framing adds little practical capability. B's "suspicion" impact was somewhat speculative, and A kept a coherent reading of the motion as written. That makes the edge clear but competitive rather than decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3–B4.4: After A's A1/A2 answers, B showed that civic norms had collapsed into anti-coercion law A already conceded binds everyone. B then isolated the remaining language "duty to try" as adding blame without adding capacity. This turned A's honest concessions into evidence that "non-optional integration" had been hollowed out.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins a competitive debate by better comparing the actual alternatives. A successfully established that expectations need not entail penalties, but repeatedly attributed independence to its approach even though B also supported accessible classes and language learning. B challenged what the additional duty accomplishes when incentives and support already exist, while explaining how social judgment could stigmatize people facing invisible constraints. A's independence-versus-dependence framing never adequately answered that voluntary-learning alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest response was that declaring learning non-optional neither gives an exhausted worker more time nor supplies additional instruction. Combined with its defense of voluntary classes, this undercut A's claim that rejecting a civic duty necessarily means accepting permanent dependence.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won the central fork: once A conceded no fluency test, no penalties, permanent translation, protected enclave life and private beliefs, its civic norms collapsed into ordinary law obedience and its language expectation into symbolic 'falling short.' A showed brokers create dependence, but never showed how a non-optional label adds capability beyond the funded classes and translation both sides support, while B credibly weighed the resulting stigma for elderly, traumatized, and exhausted newcomers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 fork, sharpened in Rebuttal 2: an expectation with teeth harms the vulnerable, while one without teeth adds only official stigma, since all practical protections reduce to law enforcement and voluntary support.
