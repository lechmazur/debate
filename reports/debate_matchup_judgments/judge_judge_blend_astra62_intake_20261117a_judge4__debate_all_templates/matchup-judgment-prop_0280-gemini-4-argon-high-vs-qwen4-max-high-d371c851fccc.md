### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): CON wins because its opening reframe shaped the rest of the debate. It argued that the expensive items PRO attacked (vendor markups, destination events, matching attire) are recent commercial add-ons, not tradition. It added that a settled traditional form helps couples cap spending, and that obligation runs to people rather than to vendors. PRO's best response was that today's families treat a formal catered reception as the inherited script. That point had real force, but CON turned it by showing that every affordable escape PRO named (courthouse, elopement, dinner for twelve) is itself a recognized traditional form. CON also caught PRO treating the guest list as modern performativity in its opening and as tradition's floor in rebuttal. PRO's pressure questions did expose a genuine tension: how can a couple elope and still owe the traveling aunt a celebration? CON answered directly and plausibly: tradition discharges that duty in scaled forms, such as a later reception, an announcement, or an afternoon cake-and-punch event. PRO's closing leaned on a claimed contradiction that CON had already addressed. It also asserted rather than argued that eloping simply means escaping tradition. CON's point that guilt comes from relatives' expectations, which survive the motion, while the couple loses its "this is a real wedding" defense, went largely unanswered. PRO's own concession that rejecting the script "does not mean owing loved ones nothing" further narrowed the gap between the motion and CON's position. PRO stayed competent and relatable, but CON narrowed the clash more effectively and framed the stakes more memorably.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.1–B2.3 was the most damaging exchange. PRO had offered the courthouse, elopement, and dinner for twelve as liberation from tradition; CON showed these are themselves traditional forms. That recast PRO's remedy as "have a smaller traditional wedding" rather than "feel little obligation," and PRO never fully recovered from the move.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A built the cleaner path by separating commercial wedding excess from genuine traditional obligation. Its decisive turn was that B’s own affordable remedies—courthouse, elopement, dinner for twelve—are traditional forms, so B’s escape is inside tradition rather than against it. A also gave a stronger comparison: obligation to people caps spending and supplies the social script that resists vendor upselling, while dissolving obligation leaves couples with endless preferences and lingering family guilt. B pressed a real contradiction about hosting extended family versus eloping, but A resolved it by arguing the obligation runs to people, not a seating chart, and can be discharged in scaled traditional forms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was A’s rebuttal that elopement, courthouse weddings, and small dinners are themselves traditional forms. This reframed B’s best examples as support for A’s case, and B’s later contradiction charge never fully overcame A’s reply that the duty is to people, not to a fixed headcount or rented venue.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won by showing that the motion is self-defeating. Traditional expectations, properly understood, are the cheap recognizable forms Side A itself relies on, and a shared standard supplies the defense against upsells and family pressure that dissolving obligation would erase. Side A’s real-world colonization point and attempted contradiction never fully neutralized that comparison: flexible, budgeted hospitality still discharges a duty to people, while “little obligation” leaves relatives’ expectations intact and the couple without a closing sentence.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal, which answered both pressure questions by separating duty to people from any single seating chart, showed that cost floors are chosen rather than inherited, and then turned A’s own concession—that loved ones are still owed something—into proof that the format can bend while the obligation remains the couple’s shield.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides were strong and disciplined, but PRO controlled the frame more effectively. CON's opening made a sharp point: drone videographers and destination weekends are recent upgrades rather than tradition, and the meaningful core of a wedding is traditional. PRO answered well on three fronts. It argued that CON's own remedy, scaling to one's means, is the motion in practice. It showed that the traditional guest list, not vendor upsells, drives the bill, because the old cheap version depended on free communal labor ("kept the bill and lost the discount"). Its pressure questions then forced CON to admit that the duty bends to budget and to parental contribution. PRO turned those concessions into a clean thesis: an obligation conditional on budget, consent, and reciprocity is "little obligation." CON's best counter was a real hit. In A1/A2, PRO said couples owe nothing even when a rite is free or the cousins are affordable, which arguably cuts PRO loose from the motion's "because expensive" causal language. PRO's "boat" comparison also landed poorly. PRO answered in its closing that expense revealed the norm's character rather than created the absence of duty. That reply is plausible, though not airtight. PRO also offered a memorable asymmetric-harm weighing: a repairable awkward conversation versus debt that follows the couple home. Overall, PRO's case was easier to vote for, but CON's motion-logic turn keeps the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2.1–A2.3 was the most decisive exchange. It turned CON's "scale to your means" remedy into an endorsement of the motion. It also located the cost driver in the traditional guest list itself, which undercut CON's split between tradition and upsell. CON's strongest reply was B4.3/B5.3, which exploited PRO's admission that duty is absent even when the rite is free, but this came too late to fully reclaim the frame.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A won because Side B ultimately overextended into claiming no obligation even for free or easily affordable traditional rites, which weakened the motion's expensive/performative rationale and let Side A reframe it as atomized individualism. A's distinction between commercial luxury and modest family-centered tradition stayed live, answered pressure more sustainably, and defused B's wedding-debt harm by showing it attaches to vendor excess rather than traditional obligation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal 2 turned B's admission that even free rites and affordable family hosting create no duty into a decisive attack: it made B's case no longer about expensive/performative wedding culture, but about rejecting family obligations altogether.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the motion's causal test intact: expense and spectacle justify rejecting luxury upsells, not the duty to gather kin and honor elders, which can be met modestly. Side A stayed competitive with the guest-list cost point and sharper lines, but under pressure admitted couples owe nothing even when rites are free and hospitality is easily affordable. That concession turned commercial bloat into an alibi and left B with the cleaner reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: after answering that the non-negotiable core is family witnessing and elder honor within one's means, it used A's own replies—that cheapness creates no duty and that affordable cousins may still be excluded—to show the "because expensive and performative" premise was doing no work.
