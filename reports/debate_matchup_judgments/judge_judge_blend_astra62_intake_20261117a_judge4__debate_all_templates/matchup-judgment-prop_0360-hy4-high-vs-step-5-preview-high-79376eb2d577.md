### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO won because it reframed the debate around scarce enforcement and expected risk, not guilt. CON’s strongest principle was act-not-badge, but it leaned heavily on perfect compliance and treated after-the-fact cameras as sufficient. PRO showed that the same driver lapse becomes louder, quicker, and more repeatable in a high-capability car, so the external cost is concentrated before the individual violation is traced. The technical-class criterion partly answered CON’s visibility/pressure objection, and the truck/emissions comparison made class-based verification feel grounded rather than purely punitive. CON’s universal enforcement alternative avoided unfairness but was broader, later, and did not address the first harm as cleanly. This made the before-vs-after comparison decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s second rebuttal was the key exchange: it answered the pressure questions by distinguishing a technical registration class from silhouette policing and turned CON’s sound-camera remedy into a concession that cameras solve traceability, not concentration, and operate only after the harm has already escaped.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won by proving that urban noise and acceleration externalities track conduct and vehicle modification rather than luxury/performance status, while exposing an unresolved operational contradiction in Side A's enforcement model. Side A built a clever affirmative mechanism—arguing that torque, launch software, valved exhausts, and insulated cabins amplify expected harm under ordinary driver lapses—but could not justify looser urban decibel tolerances for equally loud hatchbacks or explain how officers could intervene "before the burst" without treating lawful ownership as grounds for suspicionless stops. Side B showed that universal thresholds enforced via patrols, acoustic cameras, and penalties scaled to the measured violation deter ex ante and capture concentrated spikes without badge profiling.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B4.3] (pressing the trap set in [B3.1]-[B3.2]), Side B dismantled Side A's "registration/model list" criterion [A4.1] with a clean fork: a registration list is invisible at the roadside—pushing officers back onto silhouette/price profiling or pretextual stops unlike universal periodic emissions checks—or else it waits for "observed hard starts or noise," collapsing into Side B's conduct rule and erasing Side A's flagship "before versus after" advantage.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by offering a cleaner alternative that captures the harm without class suspicion: enforce universal noise and launch rules with penalties scaled to the measured violation. PRO showed louder lapses can concentrate costs, but never escaped CON's dilemma that its criterion is either invisible at the roadside and becomes silhouette/price profiling, or based on observed noise and hard starts and collapses into conduct enforcement. CON also answered the before-versus-after frame with deterrence and an event-triggered camera versus suspicionless class check distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: the registration-list criterion is either unusable without profiling or collapses into observed conduct, and a violation-triggered camera is not equivalent to presuming noncompliance from the machine owned.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by converting PRO's own concession that enforcement fires only when concentrated capacity is actually used into a case for conduct-based triggers. CON showed that measured decibel, maneuver, time, location, and repeat-offense rules already capture concentrated harm wherever it appears, while a luxury/performance class filter overreaches on quiet vehicles and underreaches on modified cheap offenders. PRO's prospective-capacity argument was plausible but never demonstrated a uniquely avoided harm once severity and recurrence are priced directly; CON's equal-measured-spike response neutralized the core class increment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON's rebuttal that once general enforcement scales to measured severity and repeat offenses, a class-specific increment adds no avoided decibel and merely charges capacity twice; design-vs-alteration is not an externality distinction.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B won solidly by showing that external costs are concentrated in measurable conduct—decibel spikes, standing launches, late-night timing, and repeat offenses—rather than in a luxury or performance badge. Side B cleanly absorbed all of Side A's prospective deterrence tools (steep severity scaling, repeat-event escalation, and post-violation inspections) into a class-neutral regime that also catches modified hatchbacks and loud motorcycles without overincluding quiet luxury EVs or gently driven performance cars.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B4.3]-[B4.4] and [B5.1]-[B5.2], Side B leveraged Side A's own concession that enforcement "fires only when concentrated capacity is actually used" [A5.4] into a decisive fork: if enforcement waits for spikes and recurrence, conduct-scaled rules deter repeat blasts by any vehicle without a class filter; if thresholds drop before any spike occurs, the rule punishes quiet luxury and gentle performance cars without harm.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won the central comparison: concentrated harm lies in the measured late-night blast or launch, not the badge. A showed severity-, time-, and repeat-scaled conduct rules already fall hardest on vehicles capable of extreme spikes, while PRO never proved its class increment prevents any additional harm or that luxury/performance vehicles actually dominate the severe tail. PRO's conduct-triggered concession left the class filter with no independent work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: PRO's own test using per-trip rates, clustering, peak levels, and repeatability consists of conduct and context variables, so it justifies escalating against any repeat offender rather than imposing separate class thresholds.
