Side B offered the more plausible preventive mechanism: lower impact speeds and street conditions that reduce both conflicts and the lethality of ordinary mistakes. Side A's immediacy and network-coverage objections created pressure, but B answered that lower defaults are network-wide, redesign can be phased, and passing a harsher law does not itself change behavior tonight. The clipped opening is a minor execution cost, not enough to offset B's stronger causal and comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's sharpest rebuttal was that “applies everywhere” does not mean “protects everywhere”: post-crash penalties still require drivers to anticipate an unlikely crash before they can deter, whereas lower speeds and redesign operate before impact. This exposed the unsupported behavioral step underlying Side A's claims of immediate universal protection.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A won because it kept the live comparison on priority under the motion—conditions that reduce exposure before impact versus harsher punishment after a crash—and repeatedly blocked B’s main route to victory: collapsing ordinary compliance tools into “harsher post-crash penalties.” A’s physics/forgiving-error story and the applies-everywhere ≠ protects-everywhere weigh made the decisive clash easier to decide. B’s tempo and citywide-reach lines were rhetorically sharp and remained live pressure, but they never repaired the weak pre-impact deterrence chain once A framed ordinary, unanticipated mistakes as a large share of the risk. B also spent too much of the exchange re-asserting that signs need sanctions without showing why *more severe after-death sentences* beat lower defaults plus redesign on deaths prevented.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A1/A2 answers in Rebuttal 2 (and the preceding A2.1 distinction): ordinary pre-crash enforcement and design can support lower limits without making harsher post-crash penalties the priority, and universal legal scope still fails before the first death.
