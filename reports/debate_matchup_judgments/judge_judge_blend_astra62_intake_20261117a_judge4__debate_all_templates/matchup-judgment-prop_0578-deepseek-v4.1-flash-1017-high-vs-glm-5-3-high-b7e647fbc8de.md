### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side A won by offering the stronger comparative mechanism: added capacity reduces docket pressure and remote access can prevent defaults, preserve representation, and mitigate delay-driven evidentiary loss. Its irreversibility weighing also undercounted the irreversible harms caused by prolonged delay.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s like-for-like response on remote hearings was decisive: for detained respondents, the realistic alternative may be remote counsel versus no counsel, not video versus an ideal in-person hearing. Coupled with the point that backlog pressure itself produces rushed, thin-record decisions, this weakened Side B’s equation of speed with error.

Expanded staffing directly increases minutes and flexibility per case, and remote hearings reduce distance barriers, which turns CON's own safeguard list into what the motion purchases. CON's strongest substitution critique was answered with docket arithmetic and symmetrical irreversibility (witness loss), leaving PRO with a cleaner net-error comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.3 docket-arithmetic answer to B's Q1: staffing IS the safeguard that creates stable calendars, technician-run interpreters, and counsel ability to appear, neutralizing the claim that the motion adds only throughput.

Furthermore, Side A won the crucial weighing clash by distinguishing between revisable compliance failures (in-absentia orders) and irreversible fairness failures (wrongful removals), effectively neutralizing Side B's strong arguments about the irreversible harms of delay.
