### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B made a strong case that recovering passive, unregistered patients at the narrow authorization bottleneck would measurably increase transplants and relieve supportive families of having to invent consent. However, the proposition required achieving those gains without increasing ethical violations or family distress, and Side A decisively won both harm prongs. Side A stripped system confounders (coordinators, registries, campaigns) out of the default comparison, distinguished recovering unrecorded expressed wishes from presuming consent on pure silence, and established two asymmetries Side B never overcame: ethically, leaving a body untouched absent permission forfeits organs without violating bodily integrity, whereas harvesting on silence risks invasive unconsented use when objections are unrecorded or hesitant; practically, requiring grieving, uncertain, or marginalized families to actively halt an official medical plan turns grief into confrontation or pressured compliance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A's pressure questions [A3.1]-[A3.2] forced Side B to admit in [B4.1]-[B4.3] that gains come from proceeding absent contrary evidence and to argue that unknowable objections mirror opt-in's lost organs, Side A's [A4.4]-[A4.5] dismantled that equivalence—showing that bodily integrity asymmetrically requires permission before invasive use and that relying on non-objection at the bedside risks compliance under pressure—while [A4.1]-[A4.2] cleanly escaped Side B's trap by limiting opt-in recovery to expressed wishes.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): A narrowly won. B presented a plausible causal mechanism—recovering passive supporters at the authorization stage—and effectively showed that opt-in also interprets silence. However, A preserved the stronger challenge to the proposition’s no-harm requirement: an unrecorded objection can still make procurement an ethical violation, while shifting grieving families from granting permission to stopping an official plan can add distress. B’s uncertainty argument did not fully erase the asymmetry between non-use and bodily use without permission. A sometimes overstated its family-stop dilemma, keeping the contest close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s response to B’s unknowable-objection argument was decisive: opt-in may lose organs from willing donors, but presumed consent can newly authorize bodily use against an unrecorded wish. That distinction survived B’s otherwise effective claim that both systems must interpret silence.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): A's fork and error-asymmetry survived: if the family stop is real, gains depend on grieving relatives not objecting under an official presumption; if it is weakened, violations and distress rise. B showed a plausible authorization-bottleneck gain, but never neutralized A's distinction between mistakenly not using organs and mistakenly using a body without permission, nor proved objection is gentler than authorization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: answering with affirmative-evidence recovery, then exposing B's 'absence of contrary evidence' as replacing authorization and arguing opt-in errs toward non-use while presumed consent errs toward bodily use.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by locking Side B into an inescapable structural dilemma across all three burdens of the proposition. While Side B offered a plausible "soft middle" argument—that presumed consent relieves grieving families from making an affirmative choice while stopping at credible objections—Side A systematically dismantled it. First, Side A showed that cross-country opt-out gains confound the legal default with bundled coordinator staffing and public campaigns that can equally assist paralyzed families under opt-in, leaving actual transplant-rate gains for most patients unisolated. Second, after pinning Side B down into conceding that any unverified family objection stops procurement, Side A demonstrated that marginal gains must come from shocked or hesitant families remaining silent against an announced state plan to remove organs—creating new distress for reluctant families. Finally, Side B never answered Side A's point that administrative silence among people facing language, literacy, or housing barriers is not personal consent, making bodily procurement without assent an ethical violation in itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's follow-through from Q2 [A3.2] to Rebuttal 2 [A4.3-A4.4] and Closing [A5.1]: after Side B conceded in [B4.3] that a daughter's unverified verbal objection stops procurement, Side A showed this "soft middle" shrinks the donor gain down to families who stay silent in shock while still forcing hesitant relatives to actively challenge an announced organ removal.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A built the stronger path to victory by keeping the proposition’s triple burden together: actual transplant gains, no added ethical violations, and no added family distress. Its soft-versus-hard dilemma was somewhat repetitive, but it remained live because Side B largely asserted higher transplant activity and reduced distress without fully isolating the default’s effect or answering the burdens placed on confused, grieving, or marginalized families. Side B gave direct, plausible answers about the large middle of willing but unregistered donors and honored objections, yet did not completely close the gap between legal eligibility and the promised outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange followed the daughter’s unwritten objection. B answered directly that procurement must stop without written proof, but A used that concession to narrow the alleged gain to non-objecting silence while separately pressing that eligible donors are legal starting points, not demonstrated transplants. This kept both the ethical and empirical burdens unresolved.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON wins the conjunctive burden: PRO asserts a soft middle that honors objections and yields more donors, but never isolates measurable transplants for most patients from bundled campaigns and the medical pipeline, nor shows silence in shock equals assent without added burden.
