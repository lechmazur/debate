### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins. A turned PRO's own concessions into its main route to victory. The first was the comma-fixer: if the student "chooses every word of the repair," then an AI that only asks questions also preserves voice. The second was PRO's "freed teacher time," which A argued actually strengthens CON's model. A also kept pressing a clear "main, not only" distinction that PRO never fully neutralized. PRO's A1 answer, that drafts two through four "need no external reader," was pedagogically defensible but let A frame PRO's alternative as scarcity rather than human primacy. PRO's strongest live point was the contrast between verifiable mastery tasks and contested interpretive meaning, and it fairly exposed A's overclaim that internal consistency is "as checkable as atom counts." However, PRO mostly asserted that AI would prune bold ideas and never showed that the harm comes from primacy rather than from how the tool is designed. A abandoned its weak opening claim that flawed factoring is invisible, which costs it a little. Overall, A narrowed the clash more effectively and made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A4.5–A4.6 against B4.2. PRO defended its permitted surface-mechanics AI by saying the student chooses every word of the repair. A replied that the same safeguard applies to an AI that asks evidence questions without rewriting. That left PRO's flattening harm tied to one design choice rather than to AI being the main source of feedback, and PRO's closing reasserted the harm without separating the two.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a sharp, well-executed exchange. A's core reframes held throughout: "main means volume, not sole reader," the flattening harm attaches to design (rewriting vs. questioning) rather than primacy, and B's own concessions — permitting a comma-fixer and defending voice via "the teenager chooses every word" — were turned against B's boundary. A's most damaging catch was B prescribing that drafts two through four "need no external reader," which A framed devastatingly as the very silence B denied. B landed real counters: conservation of mass as an anchor no feedback loop can corrupt, and the claim that even questioning-mode AI at high volume trains students to prune until objections stop. But B's central philosophical claim — that machines reward safe convention in interpretive work — stayed asserted rather than grounded, while A offered a concrete counter-design (a model that asks "what supports this claim?" and stops) that B only partially neutralized. Both sides followed format cleanly; A's progression narrowed the dispute more decisively and made the choice easier to make.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.5–A4.6: A's exploitation of B's comma-fixer concession — "if student agency protects voice at the comma, it protects voice at the thesis" — forced B to defend the volume-based harm in B5.3, the decisive late clash, which B answered only by assertion.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side A better held the motion's core distinction: mastery drills have verifiable ground truth, while writing feedback on contested meaning does not. Side B pressed hard on volume, silence, and design, but never showed that internal-consistency checks or student word-choice neutralize the comprehension gap or the bias of high-volume machine skepticism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's reply that unaided passes between a conference and peer workshop build self-judgment, paired with the closing point that repeated machine questions on Macbeth-style claims still favor safe convention over bold subtext.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Both sides were sharp, but PRO (B) built the cleaner decision rule. That rule is the error-visibility asymmetry: in mastery practice a bad hint meets an answer key, while in writing a bad judgment is invisible to the novice and becomes her standard. PRO tied this directly to the motion's split and to "main source." CON's (A) strongest line was the bottleneck arithmetic. It argued that whoever speaks at most of the "twenty small decisions" is the main source, and that barring AI leaves those decisions uncoached. That pressure was real. PRO's answer (most decisions were always the student's; trade volume for a reader who means it) was only partially satisfying. CON also scored with the peer-response tension and with the closing turn on PRO's own "final read arrives after the standard is installed." However, CON's safeguards leaned heavily on idealized specifications: "locked-down platform," "refuses easy validation," a rubric-bound Socratic tutor. PRO correctly labeled these specifications rather than descriptions, and CON never showed who audits them. CON's "two checks" (the writer's intent plus the teacher's final read) was dismantled in PRO's closing. Intent cannot evaluate whether a critique is right, and the final read comes too late. PRO's distinction between a peer's genuine confusion and a model's manufactured, retractable confusion also answered the peer-response contradiction effectively. On balance, PRO's frame is more coherent and better answers the key pressure, though CON kept the arithmetic point live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 resolved CON's charge that PRO endorsed peer response while denying that students can audit critique. PRO argued that it trusts a classmate's confusion rather than her verdict: "I lost your argument here" cannot be false the way "paragraph three contradicts your thesis" can. PRO then drew the contrast between witnessed classroom peer sessions and unwitnessed midnight AI sessions. Together these turned CON's contradiction charge into support for PRO's asymmetry frame.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Both sides were strong, well-grounded, and format-compliant, with excellent A1/A2 answers to pressure. Side B (PRO) won the decisive clashes: the error-visibility asymmetry (mastery mistakes meet an answer key; bad writing judgments become the student's standard) survived A's two-checks inversion because B's closing showed intent can only catch misreadings, not wrong-but-fluent critiques, and the teacher's final read arrives after the standard is installed. B answered the arithmetic dilemma honestly (accept it, trade volume for a reader who means it) and defused A's peer-response contradiction with the elegant "trust her confusion, not its imitation" distinction. Side A's classroom arithmetic and the locked-down-tutor defense were genuinely good, but A's case ultimately rested on an idealized Socratic tutor that B credibly framed as a specification nobody audits, and A's rationing attack was partially answered by B's claim that uncoached decisions are still made against a human-taught standard. B made the debate easier to decide with sharper pressure questions and the memorable closing crystallization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5-B4.7: Side B's observation that both of A's defenses "assume the novice can supervise the thing that is supervising her," followed by the answer-key asymmetry, was the debate's turning point — and B's closing ("intent... names the problem and calls it the solution") decisively closed A's two-checks escape route.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): A wins by making the motion's split principled: mastery errors announce themselves, while unauditable writing judgments become the student's standard. A answered the 19-of-20 arithmetic by choosing less volume over an unreliable default voice, and then showed B's intent-plus-final-grade backstop arrives too late and assumes the novice can catch the tutor. B's classroom-bottleneck case stayed live and forced a real concession, but it never got past stipulated good tutors or the difference between genuine reader confusion and manufactured critique.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal and close on the audit problem: triage and "two checks" still require a novice to detect or flag the very misjudgment she is being taught, while a late grade cannot un-teach twenty nightly decisions.
