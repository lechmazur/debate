### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B (CON) wins because it shifted the debate from local control to marginal "additional" yield. PRO conceded the inland return-flow problem and later conceded that a superior import should be funded first. B turned that concession into the decision rule: compare projects on reliably added dry-year supply rather than give reuse a prior claim. PRO's later appeal to controllability did not answer the uncommitted-effluent constraint and risked double-counting local control. A's clipped closing also compounded the failure to resolve that clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's reply to PRO's concession was decisive: once PRO agreed a superior import should be funded first, B showed the supposed exception is actually the whole comparison rule, and "genuine additional supply" is only a threshold for considering reuse, not a reason to prioritize it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won by converting the motion’s priority claim into a comparison Side A conceded. After return flows, delivery risk, outages, and flexibility are counted as drought protection per dollar, local control does not justify funding reuse first. Side A’s “exception” collapsed into that decision rule, and the clipped closing misstated the concession instead of restoring a lexical priority.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal: once Side A admitted a superior import should be funded first, Side B showed that comparing before committing makes the exception the rule, and that a further preference for local control double-counts advantages already inside the shortage comparison.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by reframing the debate around reliably additional water after downstream obligations, then consistently applying that test to both options. B answered pressure honestly and defended local control, but its concession that a superior import should be funded first left “prioritize reuse” without a clear decision rule. A also answered B’s adaptability point by noting that treatment capacity can expand while legally available effluent cannot; B’s clipped closing further weakened its final response.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal exposed the central contradiction in B’s case: if reuse is funded before comparison, the import exception comes too late; if both are compared first, funding the better project is the decision rule, not an exception to reuse priority.
