### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built genuinely strong cases: A (CON) landed vivid, specific harms (irreversible data tied to legal identity, exclusion of closeted/trans/undocumented users, badges creating false reassurance) and a sharp rebuttal that B's own duplicate/device/payment signals already do the fraud-catching work, making universal ID an unnecessary add-on. But B (PRO) kept a cleaner throughline tied directly to the motion as written—framing the dispute as who should bear the burden of proof, and using the Q1 pressure question to expose that A's "optional verification" fallback still lets scammers simply opt out while leaving honest unverified users (including the vulnerable people A champions) indistinguishable from fraud. A's quarantine fix (A4.1) partially answered this but wasn't reasserted or sharpened in A's final closing, while B's closing ("warning label on a loaded gun," "accountable pseudonymity vs. disposable anonymity") directly crystallized the decision rule and was not squarely rebutted in the last speech. That gives B the slightly more resolved and memorable path to victory, though the margin is not large given A's strong, well-grounded privacy case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Q1 ("if verification is optional, what stops a scammer from staying unverified and contacting victims anyway?") forced A into a patchwork quarantine answer that A never fully re-leveraged in its closing, letting B's final "loaded gun" framing stand largely unanswered.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): B won the decisive comparison: PRO’s own safety upside (ban linking, duplicate detection, fraud signals) can run on phone/device/behavior tools without universal legal-ID collection, while the mandate irreversibly fuses profiles to name/face/documents the motion never protects with deletion, vendor limits, or subpoena shields. A kept restating accountable-pseudonymity design promises and “optional lets scammers opt out,” but never neutralized false-confidence from badges, exclusion of transitional/undocumented users, or that adaptable fraud can buy the check. B made the live vote clearer: require everyone to pay permanent identity risk for a day-one document match that still needs the same non-ID tools CON already accepts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4 turning A’s “accountability layer” concession: if duplicate/device/payment checks do the linking, universal legal ID is extra sensitive data on a tool that works without the mandate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): CON showed the mandate imposes irreversible privacy costs on vulnerable users while delivering only partial safety gains, since determined fraudsters can bypass checks and PRO's own accountability goals can be met through targeted behavioral and device signals. CON's "design promise" critique exposed that PRO's privacy protections are not required by the motion, and the quarantine mechanism for optional verification effectively deflated PRO's core objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal demonstrating that behavioral and device checks can deliver accountability and repeat-offender linking without universal legal identity, and that unverified accounts can be quarantined rather than treated equally, blunted PRO's central argument against optional verification.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and engaged the actual clash, but CON built the more durable case. CON's throughline—honeypot risk, a "false badge" that reassures victims, and concrete exclusion of DV survivors, closeted/trans, and undocumented users—was never fully neutralized; PRO's hash/token fix addressed on-platform storage but not the processor-side link or the subpoena/insider-abuse risk CON kept raising.
