### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B built the cleaner route to victory. Its core line survived: mandatory task-specific safety checks and hazard alerts can be distinguished from compulsory general productivity coaching. B answered both pressure questions well, especially by showing that a right to override cannot restore attention already interrupted by an ill-suited prompt. A's strongest turn—that B already conceded always-on monitoring and mid-call interruptions—failed to collapse B's case because B justified those interruptions by bounded, specific hazards rather than open-ended productivity goals. Most damagingly, B's question about which ordinary deadline would not justify all-shift direction drew from A the sweeping answer that any legitimate operational deadline does, weakening A's limiting principle.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's handling of the pressure questions was decisive: it turned A's broad "any legitimate deadline" answer into evidence that A had no principled stop against an employer occupying attention all shift, and it rebutted the override alternative by noting that discipline protection cannot restore the nurse's lost train of thought.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A built the more durable distinction between compulsory, hazard-specific checks and compulsory general productivity coaching, while directly addressing interruption, defensive conformity, and less restrictive alternatives. Side B applied serious pressure by showing that pace and safety can overlap, but its claim that A’s concessions made the line impossible was largely asserted; A’s scope-of-required-function test remained workable, and B’s acceptance of coaching for any legitimate operational deadline left its position unusually broad.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal directly answered both pressure questions: a mandatory alert must be bounded by a particular hazard, while an override right cannot restore attention already consumed or prevent conformity to employer-backed advice. B’s reply memorably exposed the tension between permitted alerts and banned pacing prompts, but A’s closing explained why a specified hazard, unlike an ordinary deadline, can justify the interruption.
