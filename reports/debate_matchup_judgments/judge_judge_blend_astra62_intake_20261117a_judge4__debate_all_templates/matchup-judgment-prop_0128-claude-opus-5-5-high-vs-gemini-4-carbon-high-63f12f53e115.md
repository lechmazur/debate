### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the key distinction sharp: an employer can still prefer or weigh a degree without making it a mandatory cutoff. CON showed degrees have predictive value, but that only proved they are useful, not that automatic exclusion is justified. PRO's cash-or-card analogy and auto-scored first-cut answer neutralized CON's strongest turns on "degree or equivalent" and screening volume, and PRO effectively answered the entry-level signal concern by noting the degree remains visible and can still be credited. CON's "necessary" reductio and employer-autonomy argument were live but overbroad and did not overcome PRO's cleaner asymmetry: ending mandates forfeits the ability to value diplomas but stops rejecting qualified non-degree applicants sight unseen.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal that "degree or equivalent" is not a degree requirement—like accepting cash or card does not require cash—and that degrees need not carry zero information to justify ending mandatory cutoffs was the most decisive exchange. It converted CON's concession attack into a reason for PRO while answering the screening-volume objection with fairer first cuts.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B kept the decisive distinction clear: a degree can provide useful information without justifying an automatic requirement where degree completion is unnecessary for performance. B directly answered both pressure questions, explained that graduates retain their credential’s signaling value, and offered plausible alternatives for other applicants. Side A raised strong entry-level and screening-scale concerns, but repeatedly shifted the motion into a ban on all degree requirements or an attack on employer discretion. Its claim that B had to prove degrees carry no useful information was squarely and effectively refuted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: the cash-or-card analogy dismantled A’s “degree or equivalent” gotcha, while the second-language example separated predictive value from necessity. B then answered A’s strongest volume argument with a practical first-cut alternative rather than ignoring it.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO sustained the key distinction between valuing a degree as one signal and mandating it as an automatic cutoff. CON's screening-at-scale and entry-level persistence points were strong, but PRO answered that graduates still get credit, degrees alone do not reduce hundreds to three, and direct skill checks can make the first cut. CON's concession that sensible firms make veteran exceptions weakened its defense of strict cutoffs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: employers deciding for themselves, 'degree or equivalent' as cash-or-card rather than a cash requirement, and the fluency analogy showed a signal can be useful without justifying a lockout.
