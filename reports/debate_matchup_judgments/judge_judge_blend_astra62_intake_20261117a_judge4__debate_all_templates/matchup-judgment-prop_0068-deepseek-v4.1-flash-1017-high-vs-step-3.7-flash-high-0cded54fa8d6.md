### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully proving the mandate was unworkable and would either result in over-labeling or missed enforcement.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 was exceptional, particularly B4.2, which pointed out that A's mechanism for detecting micro-targeting relied on ad metadata, making A's primary example of a non-ad anti-vax post impossible to enforce under A's own framework.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on operational category and useful transparency. A’s harm story—especially non-deceptive, individualized calibration eroding informed choice—was real and better than a pure “ban speech” straw man, but A never supplied a clean, observable line for “AI-optimized persuasion campaigns.” Later narrowing (substantial modification, coordination, individual behavioral data) still required judgment calls platforms cannot read like payment contracts, so B’s recurring dilemma held: broad enough to chill legitimate micro-outreach, or narrow enough to miss the cases A cared about. B also weighed alternatives better: deception, fraud, data-misuse, and paid-ad disclosure target conduct users can act on; a technique badge mainly adds stigma and migration pressure. A answered pressure more completely on format, but B’s answers more damagingly showed the label neither stops the measles-style case nor spares borderline advocacy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sustained dilemma after A’s narrowed test: either platforms must still infer degree/intent on ordinary posts, or the rule brands legitimate AI-assisted micro-outreach while worst actors route around major platforms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a persistent, unresolved dilemma in PRO's case: the mandate either sweeps in legitimate grassroots advocacy or misses the covert campaigns it aims to catch. Meanwhile, CON's alternative of targeting deception, fraud, data misuse, and paid political disclosure offered a clearer, more enforceable path, and PRO never fully showed why a technique label would help users more than restricting the underlying conduct and data exploitation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 sequence framing the mandate as collapsing into either unworkable boilerplate or arbitrary enforcement, and using the tenants' group and church outreach examples to prove that PRO's "observable test" could not cleanly separate manipulation from legitimate tailored speech.
