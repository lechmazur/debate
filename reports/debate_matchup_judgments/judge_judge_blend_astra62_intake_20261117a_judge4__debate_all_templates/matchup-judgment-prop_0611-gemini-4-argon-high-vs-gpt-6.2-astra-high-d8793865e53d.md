### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO wins a competitive debate by holding one consistent mechanism and defending it under pressure. That mechanism is that permanence changes the political default. Rotations force recurring decisions to extend or replace forces, and Moscow can pressure those decisions. A permanent posture means coercion has to extract an explicit withdrawal. PRO also accepted the motion's premise that escalation risk is real and argued that the risk is worth bearing. PRO's sharpest move was the opening line of its first rebuttal. Rotating troops sit just as close to Russian forces as permanent ones, so CON's first-strike and proximity argument cuts against CON's own preferred alternative. PRO's pressure question then separated unit basing from families and primary depots. That damaged CON's main vulnerability claims, which leaned on dependents and forward logistics hubs. CON's "administrative label" reframe in its second rebuttal was rhetorically effective, and its budget point was only partly answered. However, CON's reframe depended on defining permanent basing as necessarily including families and depots. That reads as a definitional escape more than a refutation. CON's closing argument about ammunition convoys was largely pre-empted by PRO's point that sending supplies does not require renewing a unit's assignment. CON also cited nonexistent reference IDs (P4.5, P5.1, P5.2, P5.3), a minor execution cost. Both sides answered the pressure questions directly with A1/A2 labels.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's first rebuttal (A2.1) turned CON's vulnerability argument back on CON: a missile does not distinguish rotating from permanent troops, so CON's proximity and first-strike risks apply equally to the rotations CON relies on. PRO's later separation of unit basing from families and depots (A3.2 and A4.5) forced CON into a definitional fallback. CON's "administrative label" counter in its second rebuttal was its strongest response, but it rested on that definition rather than on refuting PRO's mechanism.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by forcing Side B's permanent-basing case into a dilemma: either it places families, depots, and heavy sustainment forward and creates inviting first-strike targets, or it keeps those in the rear and becomes dependent on the very reinforcement and resupply decisions Moscow could coerce. B's strongest point was that permanence changes the political default, but A showed that personnel still cycle through and that a timid government could just as easily stall flights, supplies, or stand-down orders. That reduced B's central distinction to an administrative label while preserving A's escalation and resource-misallocation concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's rebuttal that a permanent forward unit with rear depots would still need continuous resupply, so a government afraid to send replacement troops would also be afraid to send ammunition convoys, was the most decisive. It turned B's own mitigation into a live vulnerability and undercut the claim that permanent basing meaningfully escapes political coercion.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B made a sharp political-default case: delaying a rotation is easier than ordering a permanent unit home. Side A ultimately won by exposing a dilemma: real permanence imports families, depots, fixed targets and misallocated resources, while B's stripped-down version still relies on personnel handovers and rear resupply that Russia can threaten just as it can threaten rotations. That left permanence looking largely like an administrative label insufficient to justify prioritization and added escalation risk.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dilemma was decisive: either permanent basing brings its sustainment tail forward and creates first-strike targets, or it leaves that tail behind and remains dependent on the same coercible reinforcement decisions as continuous rotations.
