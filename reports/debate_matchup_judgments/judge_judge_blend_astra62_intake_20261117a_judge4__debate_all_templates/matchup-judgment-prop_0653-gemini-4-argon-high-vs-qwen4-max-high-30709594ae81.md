### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A's principled case against worker prophecies was vivid and often sharp, but B gave the more durable comparative answer. B showed that a total ban drives the same forecasting into unaudited human judgment, while algorithmic decision trails and training records make abuse more discoverable. The decisive damage was to A's measurement-versus-prediction line: a two-factor overtime rule is allowed, but a richer burnout model is banned, leaving the proposed rule looking arbitrary. B also gave responsive answers to the pressure questions, while A's help-can-use-measurements answer did not fully recover from the threshold problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 attack on A's threshold-vs-score distinction was the most decisive: if flagging six night shifts and thirty overtime hours is permitted, but an AI model combining shift irregularity, skill mismatch, and patient acuity is banned, the ban's line looks arbitrary. A never fully restored that distinction.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won a close but clear debate. B's case rested on a sharp principle — the injury is the inference itself, not just downstream misuse — and B defended the key lines of that principle all the way through. B's pressure Q1 (name one legitimate use of a unionization score) was genuinely damaging: A's stay-interview answer was countered cleanly in B's closing by noting stay interviews follow complaints actually filed, "nobody needed the prophecy." B also handled A's strongest point — the audit trail — with the counterfactual argument that the harm erases its own control group, and B answered both pressure questions directly with the required A1/A2 structure, even conceding gracefully on the "announcement" point while turning it. A performed well: the ward-average rebuttal, the cross-referencing of training records in A2, and the "threshold is a clumsy prediction" attack on B's line were all responsive and created real pressure. But A never fully resolved the unionization edge case and the self-fulfilling prophecy argument survived the exchange, while B's closing crystallized the rule-about-conditions versus verdict-about-a-person distinction in a way that made the motion easier to decide. B was also marginally sharper rhetorically ("bias by the seat," "certificate of accuracy," "ships as a feature") without sacrificing substance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's pressure Q1 demanding a legitimate use for an individual unionization score was the decisive exchange. A's rebuttal-2 answer (proactive stay interviews and grievance fixing) was plausible, but B's closing neutralized it: stay interviews follow complaints the worker actually filed, so the prophecy adds nothing — showing that "regulate, don't ban" leaves the unionization score with zero permitted uses, which anchored B's broader claim that the inference itself is the harm.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): A made the stronger case against the blanket ban by defending individualized support under restrictions on harmful uses and separating union profiling from productivity and retention forecasting. B compellingly explained scalable, self-fulfilling harms, but its closing confused validating predictions with auditing discriminatory decisions. A better resolved that central comparison, although its claims that audits readily expose wrongdoing were overstated, keeping the debate competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A answered the missing-denial-record challenge with records of actual training recipients. Its closing then explained that detecting score-linked exclusion does not require knowing whether the worker would otherwise have stayed. B's missing-counterfactual objection therefore did not defeat A's proposed safeguard.
