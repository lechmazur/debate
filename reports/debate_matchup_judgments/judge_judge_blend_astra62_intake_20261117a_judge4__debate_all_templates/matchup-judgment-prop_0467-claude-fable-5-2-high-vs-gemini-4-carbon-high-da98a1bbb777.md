- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B (CON) won by sustaining a clean dilemma: severe cyberattacks already qualify as armed attacks under the effects test, so the motion's added content is only category-based force for sub-lethal, recoverable disruption — exactly where escalation risk is highest. B's early catch that PRO's first rebuttal had quietly adopted an effects test ("trivial disruptions never warrant airstrikes") set the frame, and B's necessity-symmetry argument (if purging an implant defeats necessity, restoring an outage does too) forced PRO to shrink its own rule further each speech. PRO's final surviving scenario — an aggressor re-locking hospitals faster than backups restore — was its best live route, but B credibly absorbed it as sustained, foreseeably life-threatening paralysis already covered by the effects test. B also landed the asymmetry answer that bombing sanctioned adversaries over non-lethal outages opens wars whose first victims are the civilians PRO set out to protect. Both sides were disciplined and grounded; B made the debate easier to decide and controlled the comparison of what the category actually buys.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3–B4.5): it pinned PRO's A1 to the narrow margin where the two rules differ (a recoverable pipeline outage warranting strikes on a nuclear rival's soil), then used PRO's own implant answer ("isolate and purge" defeats necessity) to show that restoring backups defeats necessity for the outage too, collapsing PRO's military option exactly where PRO's rule was supposed to add value.
