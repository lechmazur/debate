### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by establishing reporting as a complementary information infrastructure rather than a complete remedy. A directly answered the impossibility of proving no abuse, explained what process auditing can test, and showed how standardized publication creates prospective maps, comparable gaps, and evidence that strengthens enforcement. B powerfully exposed the danger of caveated compliance and A's reliance on additional sanctions, but too often framed reports and direct accountability as substitutes. Since the motion does not exclude liability, bans, or targeted traceability, B did not fully neutralize the reports' incremental public-accountability value or prove that their costs and false assurance outweighed it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned unmapped tiers. B argued that a compliant disclosure of ignorance merely launders opacity; A answered that standardized disclosure dates and identifies the gap, makes claims auditable, and supplies evidence for later enforcement. A's addition of substantive penalties stretched beyond the motion, but B's response did not eliminate the independent value of making those gaps public and comparable.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that the mandate creates an ineffective compliance artifact rather than a remedy. Side A effectively used Side B's concession that firms could report deeper tiers as "unmapped" to show that the report would merely record ignorance and act as a liability shield, while direct enforcement tools do the actual work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal (A4.1 and A4.3) effectively neutralized Side B's claim that the report is the only way to proactively map supply chains, showing that customs requirements and strict liability achieve this without the corporate essay.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully reframing the "unmapped" loophole from a shield for corporate ignorance into an auditable admission of negligence. While Side B effectively challenged the practical limits of supply chain audits, Side A's argument that prospective mapping is a prerequisite for Side B's own preferred enforcement tools (liability, bans) proved decisive. Side A's closing crystallized the clash perfectly, demonstrating that remedy without a standardized map is merely reactive guesswork.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it answered the pressure questions by explaining that a persistent "unmapped" disclosure is a sanctioned control failure, turning the firm's admitted ignorance into an enforceable liability rather than a legal shield.
