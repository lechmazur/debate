- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won on the strength of a consistent, well-defended line: "the people are the obligation, the price tag is not." B's opening distinction — that the expensive performative items PRO attacked are recent inventions, while tradition's core is nearly free — put PRO on the back foot, since PRO's own potluck example was itself a traditional wedding in miniature. PRO's best rebuttal was the "sleight of hand" charge: B shrinks tradition to a cheap checklist when cost is raised, then inflates it to a full room when eloping is discussed. This was a genuine hit, but B parried it in Rebuttal 2 and Closing by arguing that trimming a custom (hosting rules, guest caps) to protect a person is fidelity to obligation, not abandonment of it. B also answered both pressure questions cleanly (forty chosen people over a quota; falsehood versus inconvenience is a real moral distinction), while PRO's answer to B's Q1 — that a grandfather "is not owed a wedding seat" — confirmed B's framing that the motion licenses dropping people, not just prices. B's closing inversion ("you say no to the ballroom precisely because you owe your people more than a show") directly neutralized PRO's guilt-pressure argument. Both sides were grounded and disciplined, with no fake sourcing, but B made the debate easier to decide and held the sharper comparative framing throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.6-B4.9): after PRO admitted the strained grandfather "is not owed a wedding seat," B seized the admission to reframe the whole motion as being about who can be dropped once tiresome, capped by "Both models save the money.
