### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it built a coherent opt-in model and kept defending that model through every round. Riders see both pickup times and can still book an ordinary car. Women who would otherwise log off add driver supply. The benefit is counted in trips that would otherwise be walked or never taken. PRO adapted as the debate went on. It conceded where a concession was warranted, such as diversion costs and the lack of a measured fraud rate, and proposed limiting the option where ordinary service suffers. It then reframed the measurement problem: the woman who walks home never appears in complaint data. CON had real objections in matching gaps and verification fraud. However, CON leaned on the claim that the feature "strands" women at peak need, and that collapsed once PRO showed the ordinary queue stays available. CON then half-conceded the point in A1 but kept implying riders wait unsheltered for drivers who never arrive. CON's honeypot argument was asserted as decisive without any reason to think its magnitude outweighs the benefit. CON also made execution slips. A2.1 attributes CON's own example to "CON" while purporting to quote PRO. A5.1 says PRO made a concession that PRO had actually attributed to CON. CON's closing largely repeated earlier points rather than resolving the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over the empty-queue question. PRO's Q1 and B4.4 established that an empty preferred queue means no gain on that request, not a newly stranded rider, because ordinary dispatch remains available. This neutralized CON's central claim that the feature "vanishes when needed most," a claim CON had built on its 2 AM example. CON's A4.1 effectively conceded the point while trying to preserve the rhetoric.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A won because it kept the comparison anchored to the woman who otherwise walks home or avoids the trip, and it handled the two strongest CON attacks cleanly. B raised real concerns about verification and structural matching, but it never established that targeted fraud outweighs the benefit of genuine matches, and its empty-queue point showed lost benefit rather than net harm. A's answer that universal safeguards remain while an optional preference adds pre-pickup control was the better path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal two was decisive: it reframed an empty preferred queue as no gain rather than a new injury and showed that pointing to a possible gamed check does not prove riders are safer without genuine women-only matches.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO reframed the tradeoff as a trusted ride versus walking alone, not merely fast versus slow, while preserving ordinary dispatch, showing both waits, and limiting rollout where diversion hurts. PRO also showed why reactive monitoring cannot prevent frightening in-car pressure before pickup.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 decisively answered both pressure questions honestly, distinguished no gain from new harm, and explained that tracking a normal route cannot detect verbal pressure or give the rider control before entering the car.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by keeping the burden on the motion's actual tradeoff: whether the safety gains outweigh the matching costs once both are judged under the same dispatch rule. Its two-curb example showed that a single willing woman driver cannot serve two waiting women, so the inefficiency is recurring and has safety costs of its own rather than being mere lost time. PRO answered mainly with a list of design fixes, including fallback, detour caps, and honest ETAs. CON showed that each fix either drops the guarantee the option promises or pushes the wait onto someone else. PRO's economic claim that "even a fraction" of new riders is decisive was never backed by a reason to expect net gains, and CON's pressure question exposed this. PRO's answer offered only a way to test for gains, which CON correctly noted is not evidence that the test would come out positive. PRO's reply that "choice itself" is the extra protection on fallback trips was thin, and CON fairly conceded the layered safeguards while showing they are not unique to the option. PRO's closing restated "total loss" stakes and misread CON's final point as a concession rather than engaging the double-counting charge. CON's arguments were measured, original, and progressively narrowed the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's charge in A4.5 and A5.4 that PRO "prices the service as though dispatch protects the nearest pickup and values it as though dispatch honors the preference." This exposed that PRO's design fixes and PRO's benefit claims rely on incompatible dispatch choices, and PRO's closing never resolved that.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner path on the central scarcity tradeoff. A plausibly showed that a women-only option could improve trust and unlock demand, and that some matching costs can be managed. But B repeatedly exposed the unresolved tension: when a female driver is scarce, honoring one woman's safety preference can impose a longer, riskier curb wait on another woman, and caps/fallback either remove the promised reassurance or do not avoid that externality. B also made the net-benefit question live by noting that women who would have ridden anyway do not generate new trips, while adverse spillovers must still be weighed. A's measurement answer showed how one might test net gain, but did not resolve why the test would come out positive. B's final same-driver-at-two-curbs argument framed that contradiction decisively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was the scarcity dilemma: when one woman driver is wanted at two curbs, PRO cannot simultaneously claim the preferred match makes the first woman safer while invoking caps as though the second woman keeps a prompt pickup. That forced PRO to graze over the externality it had called merely a delay.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by forcing the scarcity comparison PRO never resolves: one nearby woman driver cannot both honor a preference and preserve another woman's prompt, safe pickup. CON also shows fallback, caps, and measurement proposals either surrender the promised match or merely describe a test rather than establish net benefit. PRO establishes real trust value, but repeatedly asserts that bounded design and even a fraction of new rides suffice without weighing the second rider's curb exposure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2: PRO prices the service as though dispatch protects the nearest pickup while valuing it as though dispatch honors the preference, exposing that the same driver is being counted at both curbs.
