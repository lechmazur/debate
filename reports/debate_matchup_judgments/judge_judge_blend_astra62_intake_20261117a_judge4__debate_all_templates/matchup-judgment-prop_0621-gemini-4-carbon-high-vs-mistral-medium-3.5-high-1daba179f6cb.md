CON's A2 answer, citing procurement audit clauses, undercut its own market-exit argument. CON's closing largely repeated the delay claim. It never engaged the one-packet-per-version answer. It also leaned on vague, unverified Wisconsin litigation claims. CON's grounding was weaker overall, given the named vendors with spending claims and the unsupported coaching anecdote. PRO's answers to the pressure questions were direct and well-labeled. CON's answers were thin and partly self-defeating.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and closing turned CON's own concessions against it. CON had admitted that tools can be "failing catastrophically for specific groups" and that Daubert covers only trial admissibility. PRO combined these into a showing that CON leaves no safeguard at charging, bail, or sentencing. PRO then argued that CON's "procurement contracts with audit clauses" concedes vendors will accept audits rather than flee the market.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner path to victory. B's closing largely repeated delay and gaming without resolving the central clash.

A argued that offices cannot build their own validated tools, so the realistic counterfactual is unrecorded human instinct. That instinct leaves no score trail. A also noted that COMPAS disparities were exposed through outcome testing, without source code. PRO (B) accepted "none at all" but never explained what replaces the tools. B left the trained-weights critique of source-code disclosure unanswered. B's protective-order answer undercut its own public-accountability claim.
