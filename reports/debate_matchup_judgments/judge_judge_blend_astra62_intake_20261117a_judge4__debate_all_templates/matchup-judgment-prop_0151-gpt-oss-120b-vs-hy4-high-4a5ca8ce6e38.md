### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO)): CON (A) wins clearly. PRO staked its case on velocity-based burst throttling and then never showed that such a classifier could separate abuse from urgent collective response. That was the burden CON pinned down in the opening and pressed in every later round. PRO compounded the problem in several ways. Two speeches were clipped. It leaned on brittle fake precision: "10–15% dip," ">90% true-positive rates in pilot systems," and a "1–2%" ceiling that was asserted rather than achieved. Its own pressure questions conceded CON's framing that classifiers may be unable to distinguish dogpiles from rallies and that no real-time appeal exists, which handed CON easy answers. CON answered both rounds of questions directly. It exposed the circularity of PRO's "harassment-impact index," since suppression improves those numbers by construction. It also offered a coherent alternative: conduct-based triggers rather than speed-based ones. CON's closing tied this into a clean dilemma. If the classifier can find abuse, act on the abuse; if it cannot, the brake falls on ambiguous speech. PRO's closing largely restated its assertions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A4.5 and A5.3 against B4.1–B4.3. CON argued that PRO's error budget, 30-second undo, and impact index all presuppose a working classifier. It added that the impact metrics improve by construction once visible bursts are suppressed while harassment migrates to DMs and screenshots. This neutralized PRO's only attempt to meet the false-positive and measurement burdens.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B delivered a dominant performance, systematically dismantling Side A's reliance on automated burst-throttling while defending targeted, conduct-based enforcement. Side B proved that high-velocity circulation is indistinguishable from urgent public counterspeech, context-sharing, and mutual aid, meaning generalized velocity brakes suppress defenders alongside attackers and invite bad-faith threshold gaming. Meanwhile, Side A suffered severe execution and epistemic costs: two clipped speeches, nonexistent reference IDs (C1.1–C1.5), invented statistics (a 10–15% platform-wide watch-time drop and >90% pilot true-positive rates conflated with false-positive ceilings), a self-sabotaging Pressure Questions turn where Side A mistakenly asked questions attacking its own proposal, and an unresponsive closing that contradicted its earlier reactive-threshold model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side B's pressure questions [B3.1–B3.2] pinned PRO down on false-positive rates and off-public migration, Side A proposed a 1–2% error ceiling, a 30-second undo window, and a composite harassment-impact index [A4.1–A4.3]. Side B demolished all three in [B4.1–B4.5], showing that announcing an error budget does not create an accurate classifier, true-positive boasts do not bound false positives, a delayed undo cannot reconvene a missed audience during a crisis, and visible post-throttle metrics improve by construction while abuse migrates to DMs and screenshots.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-OSS-120B (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) built a clear causal story: dogpiles are a distribution event, so the remedy belongs at the distribution layer. It proposed specific, targeted friction measures and consistently weighed small delays against concentrated harm. CON (A) opened with a coherent user-responsibility frame, but it had real problems. It relied on fabricated precision, including a Twitter "ten-second quote cooldown in 2022" and claims of "thousands of dollars lost per month." Three of its speeches were clipped for exceeding the word limit. Most damaging, A's first rebuttal effectively argued PRO's side. It said public amplification is the engine of dogpiles and that friction "removes the cheap, algorithmic boost." A's own pressure question then conceded that victims cannot react before notifications saturate. B exploited both concessions and exposed the internal contradiction between "too late to matter" and "indiscriminately throttles everything." B also answered overbreadth by pointing to target-shaped trigger signals. A's later speeches asserted that the dilemma proves the proposition untenable but never engaged B's narrower trigger design. A's closing even described distribution-slowing as "the only viable intervention" before concluding against it. B's path to victory stayed clean throughout, while A's collapsed under its own concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3–B4.6) turned A's own words against it. A's pressure question admitted that victims cannot react before notifications saturate, which concedes that user-level tools are mismatched to the harm. A2.4 admitted that public amplification is the multiplier. B paired these with the "inert and omnipotent" contradiction, leaving CON without a coherent route.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-OSS-120B (CON)): Side A wins dominantly through superior mechanism design, epistemic discipline, and surgical exploitation of Side B's severe self-contradictions.
