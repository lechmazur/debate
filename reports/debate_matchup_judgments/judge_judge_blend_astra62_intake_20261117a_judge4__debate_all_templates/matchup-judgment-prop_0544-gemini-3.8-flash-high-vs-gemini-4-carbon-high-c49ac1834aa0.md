### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won the comparative test by neutralizing CON's core structural ceiling and substitution arguments. CON's strongest claim—that retail handguns are already barred under 21 and only long-gun sales are affected—was directly answered by PRO's point that private handgun sales to 18-20-year-olds remain legal in many states and would be closed by the policy. CON's surge argument depended on the implausible assumption that law-abiding young adults and their older peers would broadly engage in straw purchases and street buys; PRO's deterrence and traceability response, plus the observation that existing handgun age limits have not produced a massive black-market wave, was cleaner and more persuasive. PRO also landed the decisive behavioral reframe: the same impulsivity CON invoked as volatility actually explains why friction works, because impulsive crises are urgent and short-lived, not patient enough for illicit sourcing or method substitution. CON's hanging-substitution point was asserted but not defended against PRO's setup/time and gun-access arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's reframing of CON's "contradiction" into "impulsivity is why friction works," combined with PRO's answer on why household firearm access raises suicide mortality despite available hanging substitutes, was the most decisive exchange. It turned CON's strongest attack into a point for PRO and exposed the weakness in CON's substitution math.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger comparative case: purchase restrictions can interrupt impulsive violence without every blocked purchase becoming an illicit acquisition. A raised legitimate enforcement and displacement concerns, but repeatedly assumed a vast trafficking surge and never repaired its long-gun-only ceiling after B identified private handgun sales. A also continued conflating purchases with unpaid transfers. B answered pressure more directly and adapted better across rounds. Its categorical claims about seller deterrence and firearm tracing limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s correction that the policy covers private handgun purchases undermined A’s central estimate of negligible benefits. Its later distinction between purchases and unpaid gifts also weakened A’s automatic-reclassification argument. A’s closing repeated both disputed premises rather than resolving these objections.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO wins by isolating a middle group—clean-record, impulsive 18-to-20-year-olds who can buy legally but lack illicit networks—and showing friction can stop them without one-for-one illegal substitution. PRO also exposes CON's tensions between youths already buying illegally versus a new trafficking surge, and between independent adults needing defense versus suicides supposedly using parents' guns. CON's scale and reclassification arguments are strong, but PRO's unanswered point that the existing federal retail handgun ban has not driven millions of independent young adults to street markets undercuts the predicted surge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered the dilemma by distinguishing entrenched offenders from newly legal impulsive buyers, argued traceability deters private and straw sales rather than merely relabeling them, and used the existing handgun age ban to challenge CON's claimed illicit-market surge.
