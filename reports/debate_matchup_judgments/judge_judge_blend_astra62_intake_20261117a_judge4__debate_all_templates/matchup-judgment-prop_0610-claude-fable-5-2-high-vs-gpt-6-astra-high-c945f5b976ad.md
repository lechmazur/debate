### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO kept a live, proposition-aligned path: broad coverage prevents imported downstream industrial goods from escaping the carbon cost borne by EU manufacturers. CON’s critique that complex-goods defaults become flat tariffs and that retaliation is costly was strong, but PRO answered by treating defaults as fallbacks with correction and by showing CON’s narrower materials-only alternative already imposes many of the same input-cost harms. Retaliation was framed as a cost to manage and weigh rather than a veto, which matches the motion’s “even if” language. CON exposed real tensions but never fully neutralized the marginal downstream-leakage benefit. B wins a close but clear debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was B’s handling of the default-tariff and retaliation pressure: B conceded that an uncorrected default gives no firm-level decarbonization reward and that severe net harm could pause rollout, then compared that against CON’s own materials-only alternative—so those costs could not all count against broad coverage.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): A won by keeping the decision focused on the incremental case for covering most industrial goods now: complex-product defaults weaken firm-level decarbonization incentives, shipment reshuffling limits near-term climate gains, and retaliation compounds exporters’ existing costs. B gave a strong downstream-leakage answer and handled both pressure questions honestly, but never fully showed why those benefits outweigh the stipulated costs. A’s repeated clipping was an execution cost, keeping the margin competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s gearbox/default-value exchange was decisive: B conceded that an uncorrected default gives neither clean nor dirty producers a firm-specific reward, and A used that concession to distinguish protecting European factories from pricing carbon effectively.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A isolated the decisive incremental question: what extending from measurable materials to most complex goods buys versus its retaliation and measurement costs. A forced and exploited PRO concessions that defaults give no firm-specific decarbonization reward and that reshuffling limits foreign leverage, then answered pressure by supporting extension where carbon is genuinely measurable. PRO's downstream-loophole argument was strong, but A narrowed it to a small embedded-carbon leak and contrasted it with retaliation on exporters' full product margins. PRO's willingness to pause if costs outweigh benefits further weakened its 'even if retaliation' burden without supplying the missing weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it turned PRO's admissions on uncorrected defaults and pausing for excessive disruption into proof that broad coverage functions as a flat tariff under a cost-benefit test PRO never actually performed.
