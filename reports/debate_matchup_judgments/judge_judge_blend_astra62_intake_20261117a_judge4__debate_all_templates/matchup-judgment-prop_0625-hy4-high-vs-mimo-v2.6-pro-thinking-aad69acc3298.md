### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): This was a close, high-quality exchange, and A wins narrowly. CON's substantive lines were sharp: - the "temporary means indefinite" trap; - the claim that thresholds need field data; - the bad-actor turn, where a moratorium binds only the compliant and hands the first-mover precedent to unilateralists; - a clever dilemma built on PRO's "binding needn't be universal." PRO answered each of these with a coherent, consistent frame. The real deficit is legitimacy and authority over a shared medium, not data. PRO's other answers held up: - Law cannot prevent every violation, but it converts a holdout's test into a breach rather than a precedent. - The off-ramp is an institutional event, not a data threshold. - "Same numbers; opposite legal meaning" defused CON's best turn on the A4.3 concession about provisional thresholds. CON's turns did land partially. The point "you cannot compensate a drought with a label" stings. Even so, CON never fully answered the question of who outside the jurisdiction can object, halt, or be compensated under domestic review. CON also carried real execution costs: - Both its opening and its second rebuttal were clipped, so the final weighing in Rebuttal 2 was lost. - It cited nonexistent reference IDs ([P2.5, C1.7]). PRO's execution was cleaner, and its A1/A2 answers were direct. The net result is a thin but real PRO edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.4 turned PRO's A4.3 concession: the treaty body would also start from provisional thresholds, so the moratorium just delays the same guesswork. PRO answered in A5.2 that provisional thresholds set inside a binding framework are law's judgment, while the same numbers set before it are self-claimed permission. "Same numbers; opposite legal meaning" neutralized CON's strongest attempted turn and re-centered the debate on legitimacy.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a high-level debate by maintaining tighter internal consistency, neutralizing Side A's core epistemic critique, and framing the decisive choice around cross-border authority and precedent. Side A landed real pressure on non-universal treaties and adverse selection against compliant researchers, but undermined its central "rules drafted in the dark" argument when forced to concede that domestic trials also start with conservative provisional protocols before field data exist. Side B persuasively distinguished multilateral rules, outsider objection rights, and collective non-recognition from unilateral domestic self-permitting, while executing cleanly against Side A's two clipped turns and nonexistent reference IDs in [A2.6].
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Q2 [B3.2] and closing synthesis [B5.1-B5.2]: by asking what stop conditions govern Side A's preferred pre-treaty trials before "defensible numbers" exist, Side B forced Side A [A4.3] to concede that conservative provisional procedures suffice—allowing Side B to show that both paths start with provisional thresholds, with only a binding framework supplying cross-border objection rights, liability, and legitimacy ("Same numbers; opposite legal meaning").
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO sustained a clean authority-first case: outdoor tests on a shared atmosphere need consent, objection, monitoring, liability, and shutdown rules before precedent is set. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Both sides were sharp. CON wins narrowly but clearly because it neutralized more of PRO's live routes than PRO neutralized of CON's. PRO had two strong lines. The first was lock-in: experiments build hardware, staff and sponsors. The second was a memorable burden asymmetry: a moratorium defaults to "no," while a permit regime defaults to "yes." PRO also landed a good A1 point that domestic gates give a downstream monsoon state no standing. CON, however, did several things PRO could not fully undo: - **Motive test:** It exposed PRO's measurement-versus-demonstration carve-out as a motive test. PRO's A2 answer ("released to alter radiative properties as a demonstration of control") effectively conceded that purpose is the dividing line. - **Verification gap:** Its argument that binding rules need practical experience of verification went largely unrebutted. PRO kept answering it with consent and authority, which CON had already separated out as deployment-level questions. - **Interim gates:** It turned PRO's "substantive core" concession around. If the core gates can exist before a treaty, the reason to wait weakens. - **Lock-in:** It offered concrete design responses: sunset permits, fresh authorization for any scale-up, and open data. PRO's two clipped rebuttals were also a real execution cost, cutting off its own weighing. PRO's closing was rhetorically strong, but it largely restated lock-in and the burden asymmetry rather than resolving the verification clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A2 in Rebuttal 2 was decisive. It showed that PRO's moratorium must classify "measurement" versus "demonstration of control" by reading purpose. That is the very self-certification problem PRO had pinned on CON. CON then contrasted this with objective limits filed before launch and audited afterward, which reversed PRO's Q2 pressure.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a competitive, high-level debate through sharper institutional analysis, cleaner answers under pressure, and superior execution. Side B (PRO) mounted a compelling political-economy case that governance bottlenecks are political rather than technical and that early experiments create constituencies that lock in continuation. However, Side A directly answered lock-in with specific interim domestic gates (conservative physical caps, sunset permits, fresh authorization for scale-up, pre-launch public registries, and open data) while showing that a treaty-gated pause risks an indefinite freeze that leaves holdout or classified programs with the only field records and deprives vulnerable states of verification practice. Side A also dismantled Side B's "atmospheric measurement" exemption as a self-certifying motive test, exposed Side B's conflation of tiny capped releases with transboundary monsoon harm requiring downstream causation proof, and stayed cleanly within word limits while both of Side B's rebuttals were clipped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side B tried to save ordinary science by exempting balloon instrument tests and tracer plumes as "atmospheric measurement" rather than banned experiments advancing intervention [B2.3, B4.3], Side A showed [A2.3-A2.4, A4.3] that in a pre-treaty world without an international adjudicator, a purpose test invites unilateral relabeling, whereas objective physical limits (quantity, altitude, duration, material, dispersion) with mandatory pre-launch disclosure govern what enters the sky regardless of stated motive.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A wins by showing the moratorium undermines its own goal: binding rules need objective, verifiable records from bounded experiments, while PRO's measurement-versus-demonstration exemption relies on an unstable motive test. A also credibly distinguishes workable domestic interim gates from the separate international treaty the motion waits for, and turns the burden argument by noting a pause creates no registry or inspectable record. B's lock-in and cross-border standing points remain strong, but two clipped rebuttals left them less fully developed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it answered both pressure questions directly, exposed the purpose-based classification in PRO's bright line, and reversed the asymmetry by arguing transparent capped tests create evidence vulnerable states can contest, whereas a moratorium leaves only secret actors with records.
