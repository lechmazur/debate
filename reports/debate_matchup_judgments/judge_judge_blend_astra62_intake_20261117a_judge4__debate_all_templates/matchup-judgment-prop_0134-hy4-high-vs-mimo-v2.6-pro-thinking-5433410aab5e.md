### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): PRO wins a clear but competitive debate because it built and defended a coherent causal story. Its case had three parts: whole-home conversion removes homes, it raises the outside option for landlords, and it drives nonrenewals in fixed supply. PRO then used exemptions to narrow the harm side to professional whole-home operations. CON pressed real points: - the exemption "trap," - the claim that returned units go to the highest bidder, and - the claim that hotel substitution leaks neighborhood spending. These forced PRO to concede that substitution is "not all, and not zero." However, CON's thin-slice claim stayed largely asserted. CON never met PRO's demand to say when the nightly option stops setting the margin. PRO's answers had more structural force: - "Benefit and harm scale together" neutralized the quantification demand. - Reversing the exemption trap meant spared hosts cannot also be counted as harms. - The "restriction acts before the auction" point directly answered the highest-bidder line. CON also carries execution costs. Its opening was clipped before its conclusion. Its closing claimed a "quantified loss" that it never quantified, which overstates its own ledger. CON's incidence framing was rhetorically sharp, but it did not displace PRO's prevention-of-displacement mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1/A4.4 exchange decided the round. PRO argued that the returned-stock benefit and the conceded harms scale together, so CON's demand for a percentage cuts both ways. PRO paired this with the reversal that sympathetic hosts spared by exemptions cannot also be counted as losses. CON's "scales together is not scales equally" retort was clever but rested on an unsupported claim about relative magnitude.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides were disciplined, clash-focused, and free of fake precision. B landed real damage with the exemption trap [B2.3] and the incidence argument that returned units go to the highest bidder [B4.3], and its closing "unmeasured counterfactual vs. quantified cash" framing was crisp. But A consistently answered the live pressure: it reversed the exemption trap by noting that spared hosts cannot be counted as harms, matched B's demand for magnitudes with the reciprocal "benefit and harm scale together" point [A4.1], and its closing timing reframe — that the restriction acts before the auction, preventing the nonrenewal rather than reallocating after it — directly dissolved B's central incidence story. A's pressure questions were sharper (the threshold question exposed that B's "thin slice" claim carries no magnitude), and A's weighing of durable, recurring housing relief against partial and substitutable losses gave the cleaner decision rule. B's opening was also clipped, a minor execution cost. A narrow but real win on argumentative resolution, despite B's superior one-liners.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing timing reframe [A5.1-A5.2] answering B's "outbid at market rent" incidence claim [B4.3]: by shifting the analysis to preventing the nonrenewal before the auction occurs, A neutralized B's strongest remaining route to victory; B's reply that the protected set shrinks under exemptions recycled the trap A had already reversed.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): A built the cleaner comparative path: targeted whole-home restrictions can prevent conversions and nonrenewals, preserve year-round households and spending, spare sympathetic home-sharing, and allow some visitor spending to shift. Together with A’s point that exempted hosts cannot simultaneously be counted as victims, this substantially blunted B’s incidence-based case.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): This was a close, well-matched debate on substance, and CON wins narrowly. PRO had sharp moments: - The "dilemma" turn: if the policy is too narrow to cut rents, it is also too narrow to hurt incomes much. - The "queue competition" mechanism for how returned units ease rents. - The closing line "same event, same certainty," which was the most memorable reframe of the debate. However, PRO's opening, first rebuttal and second rebuttal were all clipped for exceeding the word limit. That is a real execution cost: key conclusions were lost each time. CON was cleaner and more consistent. It held a single comparative frame throughout: the income loss is immediate, concentrated and certain once bookings stop, while the housing gain is conditional, delayed and diluted across a wider market. It answered PRO's Q1 directly, explaining why a narrow cash flow can be fully eliminated while a broad rent price barely shifts. It also pressed a strong point PRO never fully closed: many returned units do not become attainable leases for vulnerable tenants. Owner move-in, luxury relets and renovation end nightly use without guaranteeing relief. PRO landed a fair reply that the diffuse rent harm aggregates across every renter in the neighborhood. But neither side proved actual magnitudes. Under the "more than" burden, CON's certainty asymmetry, together with its "one block outward" displacement point, gives the slightly cleaner route to victory. PRO's repeated clipping tips the call.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over whether the magnitudes diverge. PRO asked why a rule could cut incomes heavily while barely moving rents. CON answered in its second rebuttal that a banned listing fully zeroes out a narrow cash flow, while the same unit is only a small, conditional change in a broad rent market and may go to owner use, family use, luxury tenancy or renovation. PRO's closing "same event, same certainty" was a strong counter. CON's reply landed: the vanished booking is certain, but the housing dividend is only an inference from it. That left PRO's magnitude claim under-supported.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side A (PRO) built the stronger path to victory. A's core mechanism — per-night yield outbidding monthly rent, converting homes to tourist use, shrinking resident supply — was never seriously disrupted, and A's queue-competition answer (A4.1) gave a coherent reason why every alternative use of a returned unit still removes a bidder and relieves displacement pressure. B's best counter, the denominator asymmetry (B2.1/B4.1: rents measured metro-wide, income concentrated in one neighborhood), was a real and intelligent response that partially defused A's opening dilemma. But B never established any magnitude for the income/visitor-spending harm, relying instead on certainty, immediacy, and "mays" (may sell to investors, may move one block outward). A's closing "same event, same certainty" crystallized the weighing: the diffuse, permanent rent harm on every renter versus a narrow, visible booking-income line. B's "unequal certainty" riposte was sharp but leaned on the weakest links (luxury submarkets, renovation withdrawal) without net-magnitude support. Execution costs: A had three clipped turns and mischaracterized B as "never answering" the magnitude-divergence question that B had addressed twice; B's speeches were cleaner on format. Net: A won the mechanism and the weighing; B won timing and framing but left its harm side under-supplied. Clear but competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's one-for-one dilemma (each returned unit is exactly one removed booking source) against B's denominator answer (B2.1/B4.1: a rule can zero out a narrow cash flow while barely shifting a metro rent price). B defused the charge of contradiction, but A's queue-competition rebuttal (A4.1) and closing "same event, same certainty" (A5.1-5.2) re-established that the housing-side certainty is symmetric, while B's B4.3 "smuggled premise" counter relied on strained submarket arguments and never quantified its own harm.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won by exposing the central gap in A’s case: ending a booking is certain, but converting that event into an additional attainable home, reduced queue pressure, and rent relief for vulnerable tenants requires further assumptions. B answered both pressure questions directly and consistently distinguished concentrated neighborhood losses from metro-wide housing effects. A’s “same event, same certainty” framing was memorable and initially effective, but became repetitive and did not overcome B’s owner-use, luxury-tenancy, and renovation objections or establish that the benefits exceeded the harms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it explained why magnitudes can diverge and then directly attacked A’s queue-competition premise, showing that one removed booking source is not necessarily one additional household housed or one unit of displacement relief.
