### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins on the interim-risk asymmetry, which CON never neutralized. A wrongly suspended account can be restored, while a doxxed person cannot be made unfindable. PRO answered CON's flagship tenants'-group example early. That example involved public filings and a business address, not locating data. When CON's pressure question swapped in a home address, PRO's A1 drew a usable test: did this account help make a person findable or unable to speak? PRO also tied that test to the account's own acts, which undercut CON's "guilt by association" framing. CON's strongest moves were real: - It argued that PRO's added qualifiers narrowed the motion. - It stressed timing costs for wrongly suspended journalists. - In Rebuttal 2, it offered a functional-freeze alternative (hash-matching, disabling tags, replies and report privileges). The freeze alternative backfired. PRO's closing plausibly cast it as "suspension in pieces," conceding that account-level restrictions are justified before any explicit threat. CON's reply, that a vector-tied freeze is not suspension, was rhetorically sharp but thin. CON also never squarely said whether an account that posts a nonconsensual home address should be immediately suspended, the core case of the motion, beyond "sanction the poster." Both sides were well grounded and vivid. PRO made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO's closing [B5.1–B5.2] responding to CON's A2 functional freeze [A4.3]. PRO reframed CON's targeted freeze of tags, replies and reports as a concession that account-level restrictions are warranted before any threat. It added that a failing freeze leaves the account still pushing the map. CON's closing countered with "the messenger is not the weapon," but did not explain why a near-total tool freeze differs in principle from suspension.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): B built the clearer path to victory by consistently locating suspension in an account’s own participation in exposure or coordinated silencing, then weighing temporary, appealable speech loss against the irreversibility of becoming findable. A offered a serious overbreadth and lost-news-cycle objection and a credible targeted-freeze alternative, but too often treated B’s conduct-based trigger as mere crowd reaction. B answered both pressure questions directly and better explained why post removal and throttling could leave an active coordinating node operational.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first and second rebuttals decisively narrowed A’s tenants’ analogy: public-record advocacy is not residential exposure, and the relevant trigger is the account’s own tags, reuploads, reports, and locating details. A’s functional-freeze response sharpened the remaining dispute but did not fully neutralize B’s interim-risk comparison.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): A won by separating actual doxxing content, which can be removed and sanctioned, from protected tagging, linking, and complaints whose claimed harm depends on crowd reaction. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON wins a competitive debate by forcing the clash onto the gap between detection and disposition, then holding that ground. PRO's strongest assets were the irreversibility asymmetry and its first pressure question, which pointed out that CON's own content-level tools depend on the same detection signals CON distrusts. CON answered that question cleanly. Lower-confidence signals can justify bounded, repairable containment but not whole-account disablement, because the size of the penalty changes the burden of proof. CON also pressed a point PRO never resolved: why archives and contacts must be cut off when outbound posting, DMs, and uploads could be frozen instead. PRO's replies were "the account is the distribution engine" and "not harsher in kind, only effective." Neither explains why read access must go. CON's charge that PRO's narrow definitions ("to enable harm," "aimed at silencing") are intent conclusions rather than observable triggers stayed live. PRO's "campaign architecture" answer was decent but leaned on "no ordinary civic purpose," which CON rightly called a post-context conclusion. PRO's closing framed CON's "articulable danger marker" as a disguised magic-word test, which was a sharp line. However, CON's examples of markers did not require any phrase, so the attack only partly landed. CON's outbound-freeze proposal does concede much of the immediacy PRO wanted. PRO never exploited that concession cleanly, which kept CON's distinction intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.1–A4.5 sequence was decisive. "A beep can check a bag, not arrest its owner" neutralized PRO's Q1 consistency trap. CON then proposed freezing outbound activity while preserving read access, which turned PRO's "account is the engine" claim into an overbreadth problem that PRO never answered.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A won a close, high-quality debate by cleanly separating detection from disposition: low-confidence suspicion could justify immediate containment without yet justifying loss of an entire account. A directly answered both pressure questions with a graduated proof burden and concrete danger markers, then used outbound-only restrictions with preserved read access to undercut B’s claim that only full suspension could stop retransmission. B’s irreversible-harm versus reversible-hold asymmetry remained forceful, but B never adequately explained why archives and contacts had to become inaccessible and too readily mischaracterized A’s conduct-based markers as a magic-word test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: the bag-check/arrest analogy answered B’s reliability challenge, and “shutting the spigot without padlocking the house” exposed the unresolved gap between stopping distribution and disabling the whole account. B’s closing repeated the asymmetry without repairing that gap.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by separating detection from disposition: low-confidence coordination signals can justify rapid content quarantine and outbound limits, but not an immediate whole-account freeze. A answered B's implied-threat pressure with articulable danger markers and exposed that B never justified cutting read access to archives and contacts to stop distribution, defeating the necessity and asymmetry claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its bag-check versus arrest analogy and outbound-freeze alternative neutralized B's claim that trusting detection for takedowns logically requires trusting it for suspension.
