### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by separating genuine endorsement from the method used to produce its audio. It showed that the proposed label is overinclusive for approved, accountable endorsements and underinclusive for false testimonials recorded in a real voice. Side A compellingly framed synthetic speech as exploiting parasocial trust, but relied on unsupported claims about actual harms and audience behavior, overstated the rule as costless, and never established why an AI-production label reliably prevents the supplement harms it invoked. B also answered A’s pressure questions directly and offered a more precise alternative focused on false claims, fabricated experience, and unauthorized impersonation. B’s clipped opening is an execution cost, but not enough to erase its stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered that a prerecorded, scripted ad is not literally direct speech in the moment, then showed that an AI label neither establishes whether an endorsement is truthful nor stops the same fraud through a human recording. This directly weakened A’s central prevention claim.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive clash on fit between harm and remedy. PRO argued voice authenticity is the trust transaction and disclosure is trivial. CON showed sponsorship disclosure targets incentive bias, while authorized voice cloning targets production method, and that the rule is overinclusive (marks honest, consensual, accessibility/translation uses as suspect) and underinclusive (a disclosed clone can still deliver a fake supplement testimonial, and a human-recorded fake escapes). PRO never answered that prevention gap, only asserted vocal authenticity drives trust. B answered A’s deception-feeling pressure directly: feeling ≠ representation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Pressure Q2 into B2.4/B4.3-4: demonstrating the AI-voiced label does not prevent the invoked supplement harm and burdens honest creators while missing identical fraud via real voice – exposing overinclusive/underinclusive flaw.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively separating the medium of the ad from its message. B effectively demonstrates that the proposed mandate is both under-inclusive (failing to stop human-recorded fraudsters making false claims) and over-inclusive (stigmatizing honest creators using AI for accessibility, translation, or minor repairs). While Side A builds a strong intuitive case around parasocial trust and the "feeling" of direct address, B's logical dismantling of the policy's preventive mechanism and its sharp handling of edge cases make for a more rigorous and convincing policy argument. B's framing that "a feeling is not a representation" neatly neutralizes A's core premise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's argument that the mandate targets the instrument rather than the deception: a dishonest creator can disclose the AI clone and still make a false testimonial, while a human-recorded host can make the exact same false testimonial without triggering the rule at all, proving the label does not actually prevent the harm Side A claims.
