### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins clearly but competitively. PRO built a coherent causal story: delays are mostly administrative dead time, and withholding credentials creates the exploitable second tier. PRO also showed that labor-market testing does nothing for family, humanitarian, or points-stream arrivals, and that a rural hospital passing even the toughest vacancy test still cannot use an uncredentialed doctor. CON's strongest move was the "stolen premise" charge: PRO's own sequencing by high-need fields looks like labor-market testing. PRO answered it directly in A1 by separating macro rollout planning from tighter case-by-case vacancy policing. That left CON having to redefine "tighter testing" as consulting shortage lists, which PRO credibly framed as baseline planning rather than the tightening the motion contemplates. CON also shifted from "speed cannot avoid trading off rigor" to endorsing "fast, accurate credential processes," and PRO exploited that concession. CON scored real points on caps failing to match inflows to specific gaps and on wage pressure from oversupply. However, the "200 candidates chasing 40 roles" figure was illustrative rather than grounded. CON also treated already-resident immigrants as a "separate problem," which PRO turned into an argument about how scarce regulator capacity should be spent. On balance, PRO handled the decisive pressure better and gave the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over CON's claim that PRO's "sequence high-need fields first" is labor-market testing. In B4.1, PRO distinguished macro-level infrastructure sequencing from stricter per-vacancy recruitment hurdles. PRO then forced CON in A4.1 to retreat to a softened definition of testing that no longer clearly supports "tighter" testing as the priority.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A wins a clear but competitive debate by keeping the motion as a priority between two instruments. A extracted B’s concessions that speed need not sacrifice rigor and that already-present family and humanitarian immigrants deserve prompt reviews, then used the binding-bottleneck test: tighter vacancy checks can only confirm an empty chair, while credential capacity is what fills it. B’s stolen-premise charge was the best attack, but it depended on collapsing macro sequencing into “tighter labor-market testing,” a redefinition A answered directly and B never fully repaired.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it separated infrastructure sequencing by existing shortage data from case-by-case recruitment hurdles, then showed that even a cleared labor test cannot employ an uncredentialed professional.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): PRO won the definitional and bottleneck clash: sequencing infrastructure by existing macro shortage data is not the same as tightening case-by-case employer tests, and CON's concessions that fast, accurate reviews are possible and desirable for residents undercut its rigor and prioritization case. CON's wage-suppression and stolen-premise arguments were strong, but PRO answered that withholding credentials merely pushes already-present immigrants into low-wage competition while a passed labor test still leaves an uncredentialed doctor unable to work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 distinguishing macro sequencing from micro-level recruitment hurdles, then showing CON had conceded fast credentialing need not sacrifice rigor.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): CON (A) wins clearly but competitively. A built three live routes: wage floors, safety vetting, and rural distribution. It then adapted each route to PRO's pressure. PRO's strongest attack was that testing ties workers to single employers and is easily gamed. A neutralized it by recasting the gaming problem as the case for tighter audits. It also specified mobility across verified-shortage employers. PRO never engaged that design and repeated the "single-employer bondage" line in its closing, so its central harm claim went stale. PRO's answer to the rural question conceded that "nothing forces workers to take rural roles." It also asserted that prevailing-wage rules apply regardless of sponsorship, which A plausibly corrected. That left PRO without a mechanism for its rural and wage claims. On safety, PRO's "faster is not weaker" framing was reasonable. However, its A2 relied on "globally accredited programs" and on counting foreign experience toward hands-on requirements. A turned this into evidence that a speed mandate erodes safeguards. PRO also leaned on loosely asserted claims: decades of high vacancy rates, "thousands" of pandemic providers, and no documented safety spikes. A's mechanisms were more internally coherent. A's mobility-within-verified-shortages design is somewhat convenient, and PRO's point that compliance costs favor large urban employers had merit. Still, A answered more pressure directly and gave the cleaner sequencing frame: verify demand first.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3 was the most decisive exchange. It seized on PRO's admission that "nothing forces workers to take rural roles." It then corrected PRO's claim that prevailing wages apply outside sponsorship. Together these collapsed PRO's story that portable fast-tracked licenses fill rural gaps without depressing urban professional pay. PRO's closing never answered either point.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the priority choice clearer: verify real, fairly paid demand before expanding credentialed supply. B separated tighter testing from single-employer lock-in, used A's own "nothing forces rural roles" concession, and exposed the shaky claim that professional prevailing wages apply without labor-market testing. A never updated that attack and left the wage-floor and geographic-mismatch problems live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, which answered both pressure questions and then turned A's rural concession and prevailing-wage claim into the decisive comparison on sequence, pay, and safety.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): Side A built the cleaner comparative path by decoupling labor-market testing from single-employer sponsorship, preserving mobility across verified vacancies, and framing the issue as sequence: verify fairly paid demand before expanding supply. It turned B's rural and wage claims with the portable-domestic-worker analogy and the point that professional prevailing wages exist through testing, not general minimum-wage law. B's waste framing was vivid, but it repeatedly reasserted employer-tying and safety-through-speed claims without squarely answering those distinctions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive, using B's concession that 'Nothing forces workers to take rural roles' and correcting B's prevailing-wage claim to show fast portable credentials would likely reproduce urban crowding and wage pressure while rural posts remain empty.
