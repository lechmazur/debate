### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won by turning PRO's core mechanism against it. PRO argued sectoral bargaining follows the work while wage hikes follow only the payslip, but B showed that network liability, coverage, and lead-firm responsibility are legal rules, not features of bargaining itself. Once those rules exist, they can make a repeated statutory minimum follow the same chain. PRO's later reliance on a transitional statutory floor weakened its comparative case and allowed B to frame the public wage rate as the immediate, broad floor while sectoral packages add classification and scope disputes before any standard applies. A's points about scheduling and waiting time were relevant, but B more cleanly resolved the decisive question of which approach gives vulnerable workers an enforceable minimum first.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive rebuttal was that bargaining itself binds no brand; network liability is a legal rule, not a bargain. If those legal rules can be created for sectoral bargaining, they can equally attach a simple repeated wage hike to the same chain. This directly undermined PRO's follow-the-work distinction and forced PRO into a transitional-floor concession that B then exploited.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): A wins by separating legal coverage from the standard-setting mechanism. With equivalent coverage and liability rules, B’s claimed unique reach largely disappears. B establishes a genuine advantage on scheduling and paid availability, but does not adequately weigh those benefits against A’s representation, scope-dispute, and implementation objections. A also directly corrects B’s conflation of shell employment with legal exclusion. B’s closing repeats that vulnerable premise instead of resolving it, leaving A with the cleaner comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest rebuttal was that bargaining requires legal boundaries that statutory wage floors can also use. B acknowledged those shared prerequisites and pivoted to a richer package, preserving a benefit but failing to establish superior reach or sufficiently answer the additional institutional costs.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing the decisive comparison: coverage in fragmented chains comes from statutes defining payers, lead-firm liability, and covered work, not from bargaining itself. Once those rules exist, repeated hikes can follow the same chain, while bargaining still waits on representatives, scope, and agreement. A's richer-package point stayed live, but the concession that a transitional statutory floor must hold the line on Monday left bargaining as a later supplement, which is B's position rather than a win for the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that bargaining consumes boundaries rather than solving them: if network liability can bind a shell or brand, the same statute can attach a public rate, so a richer board package adds contestable classifications before a minimum exists, not greater reach.
