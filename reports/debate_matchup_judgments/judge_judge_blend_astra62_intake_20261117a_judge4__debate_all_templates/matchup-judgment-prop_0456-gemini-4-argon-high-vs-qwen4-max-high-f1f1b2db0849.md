### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B wins on a cleaner comparative frame. B argued that compulsion uniquely buys two things: the end of refusal and a price ceiling. B then showed that the benefits PRO wanted (creator splits, audits, standing) are available without compulsion. PRO's best material was the transaction-cost problem and its point that artists cannot collectively withhold copyrights they don't own. Both were live, but B absorbed them. B noted that non-ownership means PRO's scheme pays the label anyway, and that session players' leverage comes from union labor contracts. PRO relied increasingly on statute features absent from the motion, and its claim that "the guarantee lives in the term itself" was effectively exposed as definition doing legislative work. PRO also cited a nonexistent reference ID [C2.3]. B's closing dilemma was decisive: if no catalog is indispensable, the compulsory fee is evadable too. That left PRO's "universal inclusion" claim largely unsupported. PRO had a plausible reply available, namely that lower clearance costs would encourage broad ingestion, but never developed it clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to A1 turned PRO's own bypass argument against it. A compulsory license sets a price only if a studio ingests a work. So the shift to cheaper, overseas, or public-domain corpora survives PRO's statute untouched. "Universal inclusion" therefore collapses, and the substitutability premise cuts against PRO's fee as much as against refusal.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B wins by keeping the clash on the motion as written. A repeatedly needed to add unprovided creator splits, audits, and public rate hearings to solve the payee problem, while B showed compulsion alone mainly ends refusal and caps price. The decisive turn was B's dilemma: if no catalog is indispensable and studios can train on other corpora, the compulsory fee is evadable; if refusal does carry leverage, the scheme confiscates it. A's universal-inclusion answer did not survive because a compulsory license attaches only to works actually ingested. B's closing crystallized the choice as a negotiable no versus a fixed maybe.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal that A's universal-inclusion claim collapses under its own substitutability premise: if studios can bypass any particular work, the fee attaches only to what they choose, so the scheme does not guarantee payment to the independents A says it protects.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A wins the clearer marginal comparison: creator-payment protections can accompany negotiated licensing, whereas compulsory access specifically removes refusal and constrains bargaining. B offered substantial transaction-cost benefits and sharp pressure against holdout leverage, but overstated conditional royalties as universal inclusion and direct creator payments as inherent in compulsory licensing. A’s demand that every safeguard appear in the proposition was overly literal, and bypass remaining possible does not erase B’s efficiency benefits. Nevertheless, A better exposed the gap between B’s compensation promise and its mechanism, while preserving a meaningful consent-based objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest response was that compulsory licensing does not compel ingestion. This turned B’s argument about studios bypassing withholding creators against its promise of universal compensation: bypass remains possible under either regime. B’s closing recovered the transaction-cost argument but did not adequately reconcile that limitation with its earlier guarantee.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO wins because it set the comparison, "substitution with a paycheck versus substitution without one," and kept CON defending inside it. PRO's strongest moves: - **Turning CON's fallback.** It used CON's own suggestion, training on public domain and opt-in works, to argue that a dissenter's veto removes her from the payroll without stopping the competitor. - **Turning the analogy.** It recast substitutability as the classic justification for compulsory licensing, via the cover-song example. - **Pressing the style gap.** It stressed that copyright does not protect style, so withholding files does not prevent mimicry. PRO's pressure questions exposed a real tension in CON's case: cleared archives are "adequate" versus "indispensable fuel." PRO also answered CON's questions directly with A1:/A2:, covering the training manifest, per-work floors, and a recurring levy base. CON's best material was real and kept the debate competitive: - the pennies-per-work and self-dealing distribution critique; - the claim that guardrail clauses depend on a right to refuse; - a clever closing turn: if studios truly didn't need her work, they wouldn't need a compulsory license. But CON's weaknesses outweighed this: - **The quality-spectrum answer only partly resolved the tension.** It conceded that baseline substitutes exist without CON. - **The syndicate-leverage story was left exposed.** PRO's point that only organized, large players get that leverage went largely unanswered. - **The closing cited nonexistent reference IDs.** References like P5.4, C4.2, and P4.3 do not exist, which is an execution and grounding cost. Overall, PRO's case was more coherent and better at absorbing pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO's A3.1/A4.5, pressing the contradiction between CON's claims that studios can "settle for public domain/opt-in archives" and that contemporary work is "indispensable fuel." PRO paired this with the point that copyright does not protect style. CON's spectrum-of-quality and collective-withholding reply partly recovered, but in doing so it conceded that baseline substitutes arrive regardless. That let PRO's closing frame withholding as a shield that doesn't stop the blow.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A built the cleaner path: it turned B's consent fallback into a fatal dilemma, showing that a creator's veto can leave her replaced from other/public-domain training while forfeiting payment. B's collective-syndicate and guardrail replies helped, but A showed they favor organized/major players and cannot bind rival trainers. B's pricing objection was also reduced to a revisable rate-design problem, not a reason to reject the gate. B's closing mislabeled references, a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal that the dissenting creator's veto 'only removes her from the payroll' while the substitute arrives anyway was the most decisive exchange; B never fully neutralized the paycheck-vs-zero framing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B wins a competitive debate through stronger answers on recurring compensation and allocation. A’s quality-spectrum distinction genuinely answered the alleged contradiction between usable substitutes and valuable contemporary work, preserving its bargaining-leverage case. But A never adequately established that collective withholding would deliver its promised protection, or that adjustable royalties necessarily produce negligible compensation. B better demonstrated the limits of individual refusal, although its payment-versus-zero framing overstated the comparison. A’s closing also weakened its execution with nonexistent reference IDs and the unsupported claim that demand for licensing proves indispensability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A sharply distinguished royalties to songwriters from competition between recordings. B nevertheless answered the economic objection with a recurring model-revenue levy: compulsory licensing need not mean one ingestion payment followed by uncompensated commercial exploitation. A’s subsequent dilution argument did not establish why recurring revenue and allocation safeguards could not improve that comparison.
