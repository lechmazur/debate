### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B wins because the decisive clash became whether compulsion adds preservation value beyond legal permission and active capture. A made a strong case for deposit as default, but B showed that a deposited client for online or delisted software often does not contain the work, and that for endangered works the mandate either guesses too early or binds no one too late. B's release-day capture answer directly neutralized A's strongest "archives begging for files" pressure, and B used A's own concessions—declining to compel server-side code and falling back on archival capture of surviving copies—to show the mandate lapses where risk is highest. A's timing and significance machinery became complicated and less decisive than B's straightforward permission-plus-collection model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was answering A's pressure by saying archives should capture at release, not at delisting, and then tracing A's admission that the mandate would not compel server code. That turned PRO's core claim into a redundant permission reform with a compliance burden.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): B wins the comparative question: what does compulsory deposit add beyond preservation exemptions and funded, proactive collection? A establishes cultural value and genuine documentation benefits, but insufficiently resolves selective designation’s timing problem or answers official release-time capture. Its closing largely mischaracterizes B’s proactive archive as passive fan rescue. B overstates deposit’s redundancy and misreads A’s conditional server-code answer; repeated clipping also costs execution. Those weaknesses limit an otherwise clear, competitive win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s answer that archives can capture authenticated builds before delisting undercuts A’s central claim that permission leaves archives begging after disappearance. A repeats the disappearance risk rather than showing why its selective mandate captures vulnerable works more reliably than proactive collection.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): A built the stronger comparative case: its dilemma between inadequate client-side deposit and coercive server-side surrender, together with the early-versus-late significance problem, forced B to narrow the mandate substantially. A’s repeated clipping was an execution cost, yet its visible arguments and closing synthesis remained unusually sharp.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal traced B’s answers item by item: no compelled server code, storefront deposit yielding only the distributed build, and orphaned works falling back on archival capture.
