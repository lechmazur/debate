### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): PRO wins because it built a coherent enforcement argument and CON never answered it. PRO argued that AI outputs can look original on their face, so judging only the final work cannot separate human authorship from one-click generation. CON conceded that prompt-only outputs get no copyright. It then relied on "the creator's account" to enforce that line, which PRO credibly framed as an honor system that rewards dishonest claimants. PRO handled CON's objections directly. It answered the burden objection by tying "substantial" to the familiar idea/expression line. It answered the double-standard charge by explaining why earlier tools like cameras never needed process records: they could not invent expression on their own. It also turned CON's own songwriter and brainstorming examples back on CON. CON's case was largely repetition across rounds: the system already works, documentation is bureaucratic, corporations benefit, innovation is chilled. It never explained how examiners actually detect undisclosed AI generation. Its market-flooding answer leaned on originality, which PRO had shown novel AI outputs can pass. CON's first rebuttal and closing were thin, and later speeches added little new comparison. CON did land some fair points. The threshold for "substantial" is vague, and non-digital processes may leave no trail. But PRO neutralized both reasonably well.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3–A4.4) exposed a contradiction in CON's position. CON praised the Copyright Office for rejecting purely AI outputs while also insisting that evaluators look only at the final work, even though one-click outputs appear original on their face. CON's answer in B4.1, relying on "the creator's account," effectively conceded that its system depends on self-reporting. PRO then pressed that concession decisively in its closing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): A wins by isolating documentation as the decisive clash once both sides accept excluding purely machine-authored expression. A defines control through authorship, arrangement, and material reshaping, then answers compliance concerns with ordinary working records. B repeatedly invokes existing registration denials without establishing how they resolve undisclosed AI generation, and continues attacking exhaustive recordkeeping that A expressly disclaims. A overstates documentation’s costlessness and the threat to independently created works, limiting the margin, but offers the clearer and more responsive comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s distinction between facial originality and human authorship defeats B’s repeated claim that originality requirements already solve the problem. B eventually relies on the creator’s account; A directly challenges that safeguard as self-reporting. B’s closing repeats that the current system works rather than resolving this evidentiary gap.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): B built the stronger path to victory by exposing the central weakness in A’s case: A endorsed denying protection to prompt-only outputs but offered only final-work inspection and the creator’s account to enforce that boundary. B showed why superficially original machine outputs defeat inspection and why self-reporting rewards concealment. B also directly answered both pressure questions with a workable authorship threshold and a tool-specific justification for documentation. A’s burden and innovation concerns were legitimate but increasingly repetitive and never squarely resolved that enforcement dilemma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A1/A2 directly, then combined A’s reliance on final-work originality with its concession that prompt-only outputs lack authorship to demonstrate an unenforced honor system. A’s subsequent response largely restated current law rather than repairing that gap.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins clearly. B's camera and splatter-painting analogies showed that copyright has always protected human direction and selection without requiring control over every detail of the final expression. A's attempt to distinguish the splatter painter in A2.3 credited "method and selection," and B turned that concession against A. A's own answer in A4.1 then cut against its earlier claim in A2.7 that saved prompts and refinements would suffice, because it said that dozens of prompts plus deliberate selection earn zero protection. That exposed the double standard B had been pressing. B's "copyright is not a patent" point (B1.6, B2.3) answered A's enclosure and thicket harm, which was A's main impact, and A never responded to it. A simply repeated "algorithmic enclosure." B also built a strong perverse-incentive argument: a documentation mandate rewards people who hide AI use, while the "disclosed or suspected" trigger lets copycats burden conventional artists. A's later speeches were thin. Its pressure questions were minimal, its Rebuttal 2 was short and repetitive, and its closing restated the human-authorship principle without engaging B's responses. A's grounding was reasonable in citing the human-authorship doctrine, but it lost the application of that doctrine to the motion's "substantial control" threshold.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange ran from B2.1 through B4.3. B took A's own concession that the splatter painter earns copyright through "method and selection" and showed that it applies equally to choosing models, constraints, and iterative prompts. B then pressed with Q1 until A admitted in A4.1 that prompting plus selection earns nothing, which exposed the harsher-than-any-other-medium double standard. B's unanswered "copyright is not a patent" point also neutralized A's enclosure impact.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins on the authorship threshold and documentation burden. A inadequately explains why iterative prompting differs categorically from other tool-mediated creative choices, and never resolves the incentives to conceal AI use while large firms automate compliance. B directly answers both pressure questions and develops these comparisons; A’s closing mostly repeats enclosure concerns. B overstates copyright’s limits by claiming it prohibits only verbatim copying, leaving its public-domain rebuttal incomplete, but its stronger comparative argument still prevails.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s attack on documentation being triggered when AI is “disclosed or suspected” undermines A’s claim of a minimal, neutral safeguard: concealment receives favorable treatment, while honest creators face additional burdens. A never repairs this asymmetry.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built the stronger comparative case: the photography and splatter-painting analogies exposed PRO’s harsher, AI-specific control standard, while the compliance-log and dishonest-nondisclosure arguments gave the documentation requirement concrete regressive costs. Side B offered a coherent human-authorship principle but repeatedly relied on an underanswered enclosure claim; A’s point that copyright does not prevent independent creation remained standing. B’s closing largely recapped rather than resolving these clashes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal turned B’s own answers against PRO: denying protection after dozens of prompts and deliberate selection sharpened the double standard, and the “disclosed or suspected” documentation trigger created both an honesty penalty and a route for bad-faith challenges to conventional creators.
