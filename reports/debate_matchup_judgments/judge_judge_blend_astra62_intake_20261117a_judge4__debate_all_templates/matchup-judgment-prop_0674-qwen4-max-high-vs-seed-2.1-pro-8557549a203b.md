### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins clearly. Its central route is deterrence by denial: aid makes the next invasion costly, while a settlement fixed at the current line resembles earlier pauses that left Russian gains intact. That route survived the exchange largely intact. CON (Side A) relied on a hypothetical settlement with full Russian withdrawal, binding guarantees, and enforcement backed by "collective Western leverage." PRO's pressure questions exposed the gap directly. What leverage remains without aid? Who fights when force caps are breached? CON did answer in A1/A2 format, but its answer reduced to sanctions relief and sanctions snapback. PRO turned that cleanly: the same economic deterrence was already threatened before February 2022 and failed. CON's strongest line was the risk of escalation toward an irreversible great-power war, and its roulette metaphor was memorable. PRO neutralized much of this. It recast the question as one of probabilities, pointed to the constraints that have held through each escalation step, and noted that CON's own peacekeepers and inspectors create a tripwire of their own. CON's later speeches mostly repeated the escalation and bloc-formation points without adapting to PRO's attrition arithmetic or to PRO's argument that the settlement offers relief first and enforcement later. CON's assumption of full withdrawal was also never made credible.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing answered CON's A2 claim that any breach would trigger "immediate, coordinated cost reimposition." PRO identified this as deterrence by punishment, already tried and failed when sanctions were threatened before the February 2022 invasion. It then contrasted that with deterrence by denial, which needs no one's permission. Its line "a cost reimposed after the border is crossed isn't deterrence, it's the invoice" collapsed CON's enforcement mechanism.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): A won by making the live comparison easier to decide: denial that consumes the instruments of further aggression versus a settlement whose enforcement collapsed into sanctions and monitors. B kept a real tail-risk case and answered both pressure questions, but never showed why Russia would trade ground for neutrality or why economic snapback would deter after Moscow already absorbed severe costs for territory. A's probability standard matched the motion, and the peacekeeper tripwire symmetry plus the production-minus-attrition point left B's alternative looking like Minsk with better branding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's crystallization that B's "cost reimposition" is the same punishment threat already priced in before the full-scale invasion: a bill after the border is crossed, not deterrence by denial.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (CON)): Side A built the cleaner comparative path: aid as deterrence-by-denial and attrition versus a settlement whose enforcement collapsed under pressure. B's sanctions-relief and snapback leverage never answered why Moscow would accept full withdrawal or be deterred by costs it already absorbed in 2022, while A effectively weighed speculative escalation against the observed Minsk-to-2022 failure and noted B's peacekeepers create their own tripwire.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's closing/Rebuttal 2 attack on B's enforcement: peacekeepers who will not fight plus sanctions reimposed after a breach is Minsk-style punishment after the fact, crystallized as 'a cost reimposed after the border is crossed isn't deterrence—it's the invoice.'

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO)): CON (A) wins clearly. A reframed the motion early in a way that stays faithful to it: an immediate settlement need not mean cutting aid, so the real comparison is continued war versus an armed, guaranteed peace. PRO kept treating a settlement as abandonment and never squarely answered that reframe. A then turned PRO's own examples. Minsk failed because its guarantees were demilitarized, and PRO's account of Korea concedes that the tripwire presence was the decisive variable. The pressure questions were the turning point. A asked PRO for falsifiable markers, and PRO named three: cancelled Belarus offensives, an end to grid strikes, and dropped demilitarization demands. A then argued that by those same markers aid has not yet delivered deterrence. A also exposed an internal tension: aid cannot both grind Russia down and leave it strong enough to overrun Narva after a pause, because a pause freezes attrition rather than reversing it. PRO's later speeches largely repeated the signaling and Minsk points, and its closing did not answer the falsification turn or the contradiction. PRO's signaling argument stayed partly live, since pushing a settlement could teach Moscow that coercion pays, but A blunted it with the "what follows the signature" response.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turned PRO's own deterrence markers against it. A argued that the Belarus axis went quiet before the aid surge, while grid strikes and demilitarization demands have continued. A paired this with the point that a pause freezes attrition rather than refunding it. Together these undercut PRO's falsifiability answer and its Narva threat story, and PRO never recovered from either.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B won by forcing the real comparison—sustained war versus an armed, guaranteed freeze—and then holding Side A to it. B turned A’s Korea concession, own falsification markers, and double claim that Russia is both ground down and still able to overrun NATO into unanswered contradictions. A’s Minsk signal argument stayed live, but the closing mostly restated it instead of repairing those pressures, so B left the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal applied A’s three deterrence markers and showed they had not been met, then landed the line that a pause freezes attrition and does not refund it.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.1 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side B reframed settlement as an armed, guaranteed peace, won the Korea comparison, and showed sustained aid consumes the stockpiles and manpower needed to deter at the next border. Side A's signalling argument was strong but never escaped B's resource and credibility weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 applied Side A's own falsification markers—continued grid strikes and demilitarization demands—to show aid had not produced the promised deterrent shift, and exposed the contradiction between Russia being ground down yet still able to overrun NATO after a pause.
