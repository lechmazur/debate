### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won by converting A's own administrability concessions into a decisive squeeze. B's pressure questions (deal documents vs. informal introductions; what advance rule covers general-purpose AI) forced A to concede that reputation alone clears, general-purpose AI software clears, and a board seat clears even when introductions follow closing. B then crystallized the "perverse selection" problem: where substitutes exist, screening just pushes founders to unaligned syndicates or state patrons at real strategic cost; where a screened fund's contact is unique, parties can sequence the introduction post-closing and clear anyway. A's best counter — that pre-signing assurance cannot be sequenced because the founder needs it before giving up the board seat — was genuinely strong, but B's closing answered it squarely: the same bargain can be sealed with an unrecorded conversation, leaving none of the documented proof A's own standard requires to block. A defended honestly and narrowed to a modest, coherent fallback ("need not stop a rival to have reason not to hasten it"), but too much of the original case had been ceded for that to carry the motion. Both sides argued with plausible mechanisms and no fake sourcing; B simply made the debate easier to decide by tracking A's concessions and weighing them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing: the "perverse selection" argument (B4.3/B5.1-B5.2) — showing that A's concessions let informed parties sequence introductions post-closing or seal assurance without a paper trail — directly dismantled A's core mechanism while using A's own concessions as the lever.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): A won by turning B’s own narrowing concessions into the decisive comparison. B preserved an appealing “do not hasten a documented contribution” principle, but never showed that mandatory review would reliably catch unique pre-closing assurances rather than drive founders toward substitutes while sacrificing visibility. A’s sequencing, paper-trail, and state-patronage replies left B’s residual catch too thin to justify the strategic cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used B’s admissions that board seats, reputation, and general-purpose AI clear, then showed parties can assure founders orally or after closing while screening still imposes patron-substitution costs.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): Side B wins by turning A's administrability concessions into a dilemma: board seats, reputation, and general-purpose AI clear, while decisive help can be sequenced post-closing or left unrecorded and substitutes can supply cash and hiring.
