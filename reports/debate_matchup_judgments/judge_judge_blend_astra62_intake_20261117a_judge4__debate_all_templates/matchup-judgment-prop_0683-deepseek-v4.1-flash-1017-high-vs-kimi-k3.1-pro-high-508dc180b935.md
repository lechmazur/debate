- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): PRO built a strong case on the harms of unexplained bans: lost livelihoods and networks, and correction tools that are useless without reasons. CON, however, found and pressed the decisive point of the motion as written: the word "before." From Rebuttal 1 onward, CON set up a dilemma. Either the account stays live during pre-suspension review and bad actors get an operating window, or the platform freezes outreach, monetization, and distribution, which is effectively suspension. That makes the "before" safeguard formal rather than real. PRO's answer was to distinguish protective controls from exclusion, preserving read-only access, history, and community. This was reasonable but partly undercut PRO's own opening stakes. If customers and audiences already lose access during the freeze, the irreversibility argument weakens. CON's closing exploited this cleanly. CON shifted ground over the debate. It opened by favoring voluntary transparency and later accepted required post-suspension notice and review. PRO noted this concession but did not fully punish it. PRO's opening was also clipped, a minor execution cost. Overall, CON made the final clash narrower and easier to decide on the motion's actual wording, earning a narrow but real edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's response to A1 and its closing, which turned PRO's "technically accessible"/"read-only access" answer into a dilemma: a pre-suspension appeal is safe only if the account is neutralized, at which point it is already effectively suspended. PRO's protective-controls distinction did not fully escape this.
