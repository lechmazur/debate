### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): CON wins a competitive debate on its sharper causal discipline and clearer burden framing. Its central distinction held up through the later rounds: a GLP-1 drug makes eating less easier, but it does not lower the fuel requirement. Weigh-ins test mass, not tolerance for hunger. Detecting that an athlete used a drug is not the same as showing the drug distorted competition. PRO raised real pressure. Its strongest moment was the A1 analogy: beta-blockers and blood doping are banned even though psychology and altitude camps reach similar ends. That partly answers CON's "same benefit as a meal plan" point. CON's reply about archer steadiness covered only half of the analogy and left the EPO comparison mostly unanswered. However, PRO's case leaned heavily on asserted mechanisms rather than demonstrated ones: - micro-dosing protocols that preserve lean tissue; - medicated athletes completing the same workouts while better rested; - the rival rider being assumed two kilograms heavier. CON repeatedly exposed these as assumptions. Its framing that "the margin was put there by assumption" landed. PRO also cited nonexistent reference IDs ([C2.1, C2.3]), which is a grounding cost. The motion's word "may" helps PRO. But CON reasonably separated investigating a plausible benefit, which it endorsed, from audits that shift the burden onto treated athletes. That left PRO's demand for restrictions less justified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came in CON's B2.1–B2.3 and B4.4. CON reframed the cyclist and wrestler comparison as a rival pursuing the same target weight, and showed that PRO had bundled several outcomes into a single appetite effect: dropping a division, sustaining training, and keeping power. This forced PRO to rely on asserted contrasts between deep sleep and "relentless starvation signals" rather than demonstrated performance differences.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the cleaner path to victory. B extracted a key concession in A's A4.1 ("It is an advantage") and then supplied the decisive principled distinction A lacked: sport bans pharmacological shortcuts even when the outcome could be pursued by natural means (sports psychologist vs beta-blocker, altitude camp vs blood doping), which directly dismantled A's "the weigh-in doesn't measure hunger tolerance" framing. B's closing line that a finish line doesn't measure November recovery either crystallized why the "contested skill" test fails. A's best route—demanding evidence that appetite suppression actually preserves power at race weight—was real but ultimately resolved into "needs testing," a retreat to research-first that B answered with the selection-bias point and the coercion/arms-race cost of inaction. A's burden argument (audits pressure treated athletes before a problem is defined) was A's strongest surviving point, but it never neutralized B's precedent-based case. Minor cost for B: garbled reference IDs [C2.1, C2.3] in B4.3, though the underlying claims match the record. Both sides followed format properly with clean Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 structure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's B4.1 answer to A's dietitian-vs-drug pressure question: the psychologist/beta-blocker and altitude/blood-doping pairings showed that fairness in anti-doping turns on the means (synthetic pharmacology overriding biological barriers), not on whether the measured outcome differs—breaking A's core "same weight, same test" defense, then reinforced by A's own concession that the drug delivers an advantage.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): A won because it tied the motion’s “may create” threshold to a plausible anti-doping precaution: GLP-1s could ease weight cuts and recovery, covert use could evade ordinary checks, and scrutiny itself is how regulators gather data and deter coercion. B’s epistemic pressure was sharp, but its research-only alternative left secret use and live competitions less protected, and its proof burden sat uneasily with a scrutiny motion. A’s stray C-reference was a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answers distinguishing synthetic pharmacology from meal-plan optimization and exposing selection bias in voluntary athlete studies blunted B’s main fairness and research objections.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO wins because it tied its case to the motion's low bar ("may create") and kept the remedy modest: confidential surveys, protection for medical use, and no sanctions. Its strongest point was that a weight class is a threshold rather than a sliding scale. If a drug changes who reliably clears the cutoff, it reshapes the field and puts pressure on teammates, even if the user loses some strength. CON never fully neutralized this. CON's physiological case was vivid and often plausible. However, it overreached into universal certainties: - "guaranteeing a catastrophic energy crash" - "no mystical subset" - "paralyzes digestion" - the claim that lost mass "cannot" fall proportionally more than power These claims assume severe restriction in every case. PRO directly contested that assumption by framing use as a smaller, sustained deficit. CON's attack on the surveys ("cannot establish causation") was answered in advance and again in closing. PRO separated the standard for opening an inquiry from the standard for sanctioning an athlete, which also blunts CON's late false-positive argument. CON's selection-trials answer (A2) was reasonable, but it did not address PRO's point that a user can still win the wrestle-off and so alter the field. PRO's endurance argument stayed thinner. Even so, the weight-class line and the coercion line remained live routes to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.5 and A5.1 argued that the weigh-in "decides who gets to start" and is a hard cutoff, not a sliding scale. This reframed CON's "strength loss" rebuttal as insufficient. CON's reply in B4.3, that access only matters if muscle is preserved, was its best counter. It still relied on assuming large tissue loss in every case, which PRO had already contested.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by matching the motion's low bar. The proposition asks whether GLP-1s *may* create unfair advantages and *should face scrutiny* — not whether the advantage is proven. B's threshold argument (the weigh-in decides who gets to start; the value of the drug is reliable access to a bracket, not retained strength against one's former self) survived A's physiological assault because A needed muscle loss to fully erase the value of changing opponents, and A never established that. A's strongest material — gastric emptying lowering the fueling ceiling on climbs — was genuinely damaging and B answered it only weakly ("need not limit power"), so A clearly won the endurance sub-clash. But B's rebuttal-2 move distinguishing the standard for opening an inquiry from the standard for sanctioning an athlete, plus the point that CON's own welfare framing still describes a problem federations should be asking about in confidence, gave B a cleaner route to the ballot. A's counterproductivity case (surveys manufacture hype, causation-blind false positives) was plausible but speculative and never grounded beyond assertion, while B's survey proposal was deliberately modest and fit the motion as written. A's physiology was at times overconfident ("guaranteeing a catastrophic energy crash," "lowers that ceiling for everyone"), slightly undercutting its grounding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5 in Side B's second rebuttal: the cutoff is not a sliding scale — a small strength loss costs a point in a bout, but a small miss on the scale costs entry altogether. This reframing forced A to treat the weigh-in as the finish line (A4.3), which B then dismantled in closing (B5.1) by noting the weigh-in merely decides who gets to start, crystallizing that CON needed the strength loss to erase the entire value of facing a different field — a burden CON never discharged.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won by keeping the proposition’s “may create unfair advantages” burden realistic and by separating low-cost scrutiny from sanctioning. Its weight-class access argument survived because it only needed a plausible edge for some athletes, while Side B’s physiological case was vivid but often overconfident and too absolute. A also handled the pressure questions more cleanly and made the survey-versus-punishment distinction the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was most decisive: it answered B’s pressure by proposing survey questions tied to competitive outcomes, and it reframed the weigh-in as a binary entry threshold rather than a simple performance tradeoff.
