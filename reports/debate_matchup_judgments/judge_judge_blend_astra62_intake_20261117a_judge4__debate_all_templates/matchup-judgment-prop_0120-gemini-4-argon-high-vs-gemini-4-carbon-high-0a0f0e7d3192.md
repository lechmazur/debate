### Judge Notes

- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A wins narrowly. B exposed genuine problems with annual private-asset valuations and deferred liabilities, but did not defeat A’s case for taxing readily valued liquid fortunes annually. A explained why corporate taxes do not resolve household-level tax asymmetry and why collecting revenue before death matters. B’s claimed equivalence between capped deferred wealth charges and capital-gains taxation was not established, while its warnings about destructive share sales did insufficient comparative work against A’s revenue and compounding arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B sharply showed that falling future valuations do not erase liabilities accrued at a temporary peak, forcing A to introduce a lookback cap. However, A’s closing distinction between narrow illiquidity deferrals and annual collection from liquid portfolios prevented that objection from defeating the entire proposal.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A preserved a decisive segmentation: liquid publicly traded fortunes are easily valued and can be taxed annually, while deferral with a lookback cap contains the illiquidity problem for private assets. Side B strongly damaged private-asset valuation, but its loan, consumption, and death taxes still leave the bulk of unspent accumulation untouched for decades, and corporate taxes did not answer the personal fairness asymmetry with workers and homeowners.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's claim that deferral plus a lookback cap collapses into holding-period realization taxation was the sharpest attack, but Side A's closing contained it by framing deferral as a narrow illiquid-asset safety valve while annual cash taxation remains the rule for liquid fortunes.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger path by keeping the debate centered on the fairness gap: workers and homeowners pay annually while ultra-wealthy households can let unrealized fortunes compound and borrow modestly against them. B also gave a workable design: high thresholds, annual valuation for liquid public assets, and deferral with interest plus a lookback cap for genuinely illiquid holdings. Side A’s best move was showing that deferral plus lookback resembles a realization-based holding-period tax, but B blunted that by treating it as a narrow safety valve and returning to the core problem of decades of untaxed personal accumulation. A’s corporate-tax and realization arguments were clear, but they did not fully answer why reformed income and consumption taxes should leave enormous household net worth untouched for so long.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over deferral and phantom gains: A pressured B on whether a crashed valuation would still owe accrued annual taxes, and B answered with a lookback cap while insisting the tax still accrues annually. A reframed that as realization in disguise, but B’s later emphasis on liquid public fortunes kept the annual-tax advantage alive.
