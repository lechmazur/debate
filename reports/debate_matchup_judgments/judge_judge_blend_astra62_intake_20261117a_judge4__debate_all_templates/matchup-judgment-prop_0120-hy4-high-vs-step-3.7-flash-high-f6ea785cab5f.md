### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins. It built a clear structural case: income and consumption taxes need a taxable event, so large unrealized fortunes can wait indefinitely while wages and purchases are taxed on a fixed calendar. PRO then set up the decisive comparison of "bounded friction versus structural exemption" and sustained it in every round. Its Q1 forced the key concession. In A1, CON admitted that no targeted reform reaches yearly unrealized gains without some form of valuation. That undercut CON's own "fix existing taxes" alternative, and PRO used the admission well in closing. PRO also answered both of CON's pressure questions directly with A1:/A2: labels, covering contestable valuation, look-through, and exit settlement. CON had a few points with real bite: liquidity for illiquid holders, and the claim that interest on deferred tax compounds. Neither was developed beyond assertion. CON's turns also carried several execution and grounding costs. Two turns were clipped for length. CON repeated a brittle statistic about French emigration as "proven" across three speeches. The farmer example rested on an assumed 2% rate and a low exemption, and PRO exposed that. The debt-arbitrage argument was muddled, since borrowing to spend lowers net worth only by spending it. CON's closing largely recapped earlier points without engaging the concession PRO highlighted.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 and its follow-up decided the debate. Q1 asked which targeted reform reaches yearly unrealized share gains without annual valuation. CON's A1 conceded that none can, then retreated to taxing realized gains through step-up reform. PRO's closing turned this into a dilemma: CON either rebuilds the same valuation machinery under another label, or accepts that the largest accumulations go untaxed until the owner chooses to sell, die, or spend.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the central clash: CON conceded no targeted income or consumption reform reaches yearly unrealized gains without valuation, then offered only realized-gain fixes, leaving PRO's structural-exemption and owner-controlled timing argument intact.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's closing decisively used B's Rebuttal 2 admission that unrealized gains cannot be reached without valuation, forcing CON into either recreating wealth-tax machinery or accepting delayed, owner-chosen taxation.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by making the central clash decisive: it forced Side A to admit that targeted income/consumption reforms cannot reach yearly unrealized gains without some form of valuation, then argued that the same valuation machinery is better applied to a clean annual net-worth base. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won solidly by turning PRO's own design fixes against it. Early on, CON offered a concrete alternative: tax gains at sale, gift, death, and loan-financed spending. That reframed PRO's "permanent exemption" story as a fixable flaw in flow taxes rather than a reason for annual balance-sheet levies. CON then identified PRO's deferral provision as a key concession. If illiquid assets pay only at sale or death, the tax no longer yields stable annual or recession revenue. CON used this to build a clear trilemma: deferral sacrifices revenue, current collection brings back forced sales, and anti-avoidance rules rebuild the flow-tax system. PRO's answers deepened the problem rather than solving it: - **Borrowing:** PRO's A2 claimed a wealth tax catches loan-financed consumption, yet admitted that such borrowing lowers net worth and therefore lowers the tax. CON exploited this directly. - **Closing:** PRO misdescribed CON as "admitting" a permanent borrowing loophole, which CON had explicitly addressed. PRO also kept repeating "stable recession revenue" without answering the deferral contradiction. CON slightly overstated the motion as requiring full "replacement," when "rather than rely almost entirely" would allow a supplemental wealth tax. PRO never contested this framing, however, so it did not cost CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was CON's A2 in its second rebuttal [A4.3]. CON quoted PRO's own line that borrowing for spending reduces "net worth and thus their tax liability," showing that a wealth tax rewards the yacht loan unless it adds loan-use tracing, which is itself a flow tax. This converted PRO's strongest dynastic-loophole attack into evidence for CON's position.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B won by collapsing PRO's promises into a trade-off: deferral avoids forced sales but surrenders annual stable cash, while current collection restores valuation and liquidity fights. B also showed repaired flow taxes answer the stepped-up-basis and borrowing loopholes, and PRO's own borrowing answer conceded debt reduces net-worth tax liability. A's closing misdescribed that answer rather than resolving it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was B's Rebuttal 2 on borrowing: it quoted A's admission that personal borrowing reduces net worth and tax liability, proving the wealth tax alone rewards loan-financed consumption unless it adds the flow-tax tracing B advocated.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by turning PRO’s own design fixes into decisive concessions: deferral for illiquid assets undermines the promise of stable annual revenue, while debt-financed consumption reduces net worth and therefore weakens the wealth tax unless PRO adds loan-use tracing, which is effectively a flow tax. A consistently framed the proposition as requiring a workable annual replacement for income and consumption taxes, and showed that PRO’s repaired version quietly rebuilds those flow-tax tools. B had strong fairness rhetoric and a plausible anti-avoidance answer, but never squarely neutralized the borrowing contradiction and overclaimed in closing that CON admitted a permanent loophole.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer to PRO’s borrowing question was decisive: PRO said borrowing for personal spending lowers net worth and thus tax liability, and A used that to show the wealth tax would reward yacht-style borrowing unless it adopted flow-tax tracing.
