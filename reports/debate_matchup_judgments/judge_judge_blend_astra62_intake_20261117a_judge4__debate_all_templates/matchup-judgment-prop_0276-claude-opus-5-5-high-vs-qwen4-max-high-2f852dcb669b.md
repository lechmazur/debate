### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): This was an unusually tight, well-matched debate that narrowed cleanly to a single question: which default better serves passengers — automatic payment with appeal, or universal notification plus a one-click claim where the airline bears the burden of proving an exemption. Side A's strongest live route was behavioral: complaint processes suppress claims, so "owed money should not depend on the willingness to ask." Side B blunted this by progressively stripping away every concrete friction A attacked (forms, receipts, ignorance, deadlines), leaving only the initiation barrier, and then absorbing it into a one-click design. Meanwhile Side B consistently damaged A's case: B2.1 exposed A's appeal patch as a rebuilt complaint process, B3.1's pressure question forced A into the "lawfully rested crew" counterfactual that B then showed only the airline could answer, and B4.6-4.7 stacked A's own judgment layers (root cause, mitigation, reasonable staffing) into a persuasive case that automation hides rather than removes airline judgment. A's counter-crystallization in A4.4-4.5 ("airline judgment that pays by default against airline judgment that pays nothing") was excellent and kept A fully in the fight, and A5.2-5.3 ("the asking is the obstacle") landed a real residual point B never fully answered beyond minimization. But B answered A's pressure questions more decisively (cause-neutral trigger dissolving the defendant's-ledger inconsistency) and B's closing honestly framed the final weighing. Slight edge to B on rebuttal execution and pressure-question handling; the core value dispute itself remained genuinely contestable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1: Side B's identification that A's "require the cause code be disclosed with a right to appeal" concession rebuilds a complaint process — with the passenger now overturning a verdict instead of filing a claim — was the debate's sharpest reversal. Side A's best counter was A4.4-4.5, reframing the choice as airline judgment that pays by default versus airline judgment that pays nothing, but B's A2/B4 answers (one click, airline must prove exemption before a reviewer) largely restored B's starting-position advantage.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): B wins a competitive debate by better separating automatic payment in clear cases from review of disputed exemptions. Disclosure, airline-held burdens and preserved appeals substantially weaken A's claim that automation necessarily entrenches hidden judgments. A's cause-neutral notification and one-click review were meaningful answers, and B's closing unfairly suggested airline codes still screened that notification. Nevertheless, A never sufficiently justified requiring every eligible passenger to initiate a claim rather than paying clear entitlements automatically. B also effectively answered the standardized-settlement objection and the fifteen-minute hold example.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest rebuttal was that an appeal mechanism can supplement automatic payment rather than defeat its purpose. A's observation that appeals remain complaints did not establish why everyone should have to claim, including passengers whose entitlement the airline already recognizes.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): CON wins by neutralizing PRO's friction case with universal cause-neutral notification, one-click claims, and an airline burden to prove exemptions, while preserving the decisive objection that automatic payment lets the airline's initial weather/controllable code silently decide mixed-cause cases. PRO shows some passengers still won't click, but never shows that loss outweighs miscoding under a pay-by-default system.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2: answering Q1 with a cause-neutral notification trigger and Q2 with a burden-shifted one-click claim, then showing PRO's reasonableness, root-cause, and mitigation tests are counterfactuals only the airline can initially code.
