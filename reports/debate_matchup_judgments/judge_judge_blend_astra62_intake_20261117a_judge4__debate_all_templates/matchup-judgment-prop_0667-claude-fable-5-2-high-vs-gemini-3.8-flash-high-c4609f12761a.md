### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won the core clash. B's dilemma — every unit of enforcement bite is a unit of shortage, every unit of evasion is a unit of failure — survived the whole debate. A's sharpest attack (the "contradiction" between easy bypass and catastrophic shortage) was cleanly resolved in B's Rebuttal 2: both things happen in different sectors, distributed across the economy. B's borrowed-identity rebuttal [B2.8] against photo-matching was never answered, and B's point that pre-offer discrimination (the Hernández non-callback) leaves no audit trail neutralized A's "auditable digital trail" defense. B also effectively showed that A's own answer (workers exit, employers raise wages and buy equipment) concedes a multi-year labor gap, which the motion's "no large shortages" bar cannot absorb. A countered with a reframed bar ("structural collapse" vs. "rebalancing") that B correctly flagged as moving the goalposts. A's execution costs include nonexistent reference IDs [C1.6, C2.7], while B suffered repeated clipping on four turns — a real cost, but the visible content was substantively stronger and more responsive throughout. B's pressure questions exposed burdens A never squarely met; A's contradiction question was sharp but was answered directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1-B4.9] was decisive: it resolved A's contradiction charge by showing evasion and shortage coexist in different sectors, reasserted the unanswered borrowed-identity problem, and dismantled A's audit-trail discrimination defense by noting that pre-offer screening generates no record at all.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A built the decisive dilemma: where E-Verify bites it creates shortages, where it is evaded through borrowed identities or off-books work it does not reduce unauthorized employment. A turned B's Georgia migration claim into a nationwide problem, showed exit from formal work is not a reduction, and exposed that quiet non-callbacks leave no audit trail. B mainly asserted wage, H-2A, and mechanization adaptation over years without answering those links or restoring the motion's 'large shortages' bar.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 resolving the alleged contradiction: evasion and shortages occur in different sectors, so every unit of bite is pain and every unit of evasion is failure, while a pre-offer screen-out generates no E-Verify record to audit.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B won by keeping the debate focused on the motion's actual tests. It showed that PRO often defined success as excluding unauthorized workers from formal W-2 jobs, not necessarily reducing unauthorized employment, especially if workers moved to cash work or used matching identities. B also made the labor-shortage risk more concrete by stressing seasonal and high-turnover sectors where adaptation takes longer than the mandate bites. PRO's discrimination safeguards were weaker against quiet pre-offer screening. B's repeated clipping and some vague evaluation language reduced the margin, but its core path remained stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal defused PRO's contradiction charge by locating evasion in informal sectors and shortages in formal sectors, then used PRO's "exit formal employment" concession to argue that unauthorized employment was not actually reduced.
