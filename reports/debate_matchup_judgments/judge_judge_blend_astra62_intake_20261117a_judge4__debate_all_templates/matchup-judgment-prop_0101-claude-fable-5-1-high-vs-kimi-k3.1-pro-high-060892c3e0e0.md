### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by showing that a ban creates stronger incentives for children to misstate their age, while mandatory youth design rules encourage honest identification and preserve protections. A defended imperfect enforcement competently, but choosing light assurance left its universal-default claim vulnerable and suspension plausibly pushed evaders back into adult-classified accounts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to A's age-detection symmetry was decisive: the same inference tools operate as a cooperative safety net under design duties but as an adversarial gate under a ban. B then turned A's safeguards-before-suspension answer by arguing that suspension removes the protection and incentivizes a fresh lie.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by structurally outmaneuvering Side B on the issue of evasion and incentives. Side A effectively argued that a ban maximizes lying (exposing children to unmitigated adult algorithms), while mandatory design duties minimize lying (allowing children to retain access honestly while being protected). Side B's defense of light age assurance left them vulnerable to this trap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal cleanly exploited Side B's answer to the pressure questions, pointing out that Side B's protocol of applying youth protections and then suspending detected children simply undoes the protection and sends them back to a fresh lie.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively capturing the central clash on enforcement and evasion. By forcing Side A to choose light assurance in the Pressure Questions round, Side B sets up a devastating trap in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing: under light assurance, the ban incentivizes lying, pushing the child into the unregulated adult internet, and Side A's proposed solution of applying youth protections then suspending the account merely applies Side B's regime temporarily before exiling the child. Side B's crystallization of this mechanic is highly persuasive and neutralizes Side A's structural arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it exploits Side A's concession on light assurance to show that the ban's enforcement mechanism either fails to stop the child or temporarily applies Side B's design protections before suspending the account and stripping those protections away.
