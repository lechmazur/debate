### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by narrowing the clash to a precise, defensible line. It distinguishes stopping future releases from stopping the descendants of mosquitoes already released, and it makes neighbor consent the decisive concern. CON also explicitly disclaims any demand for ecological certainty, and it gives a workable pathway: confined tests plus field observation of existing mosquito movement, then independent review, with a different site chosen if evidence falls short. That answer substantially defuses PRO's central "self-sealing standard" attack. PRO has the stronger stakes rhetoric, including the death toll, the status-quo-as-catastrophe framing, and the bed net and insecticide comparisons. However, its later speeches lean on claims CON had already answered. PRO's closing also misdescribes A4.5 as a concession when it was a critique of PRO's own inconsistency, and it inflates CON's position into "unanimous regional consent" when CON limited consent to plausibly exposed jurisdictions. PRO's A2 answer, that the host may proceed despite a neighbor's objection, gave CON a clean, memorable closing line. CON's position does come close to conditional permission rather than outright blocking. Even so, it contests what "tightly regulated" must mean, and PRO never showed that CON's conditions amount to impossible certainty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.6 is the most decisive exchange. CON answers the self-sealing question by pointing to field observation of wild mosquito movement and to choosing a different site, which shows the standard is not a permanent "no." It then seizes on PRO's admission that the host "may proceed" over a neighbor's refusal: "do not call consultation consent." This reframes the debate around consent to an irreversible exposure, and PRO's closing never adequately addresses that point.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON built the cleaner path. Its distinction between enclosed trials and an open release was not neutralized, and it answered the self-sealing charge by offering a concrete evidentiary route: cage data plus observations of existing mosquito movement, assessed site by site before permission. PRO’s strongest consent answer backfired when it conceded a host could proceed despite a reasonably exposed neighbor’s refusal; CON turned this into a live, unresolved objection that experimental exposure could be imposed without consent. PRO’s closing overstated CON as allowing no trial ever, weakening its final comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON’s response to PRO’s boundedness/consent framing: once mosquitoes leave the enclosure, a permit cannot stop their descendants, and B’s admission that the host may proceed despite neighbor objection meant consultation was not consent for an irreversible experimental exposure.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO)): CON won by splitting confined outdoor testing from open release, answering the self-sealing charge with cage data plus observation of existing mosquito movement, and exploiting PRO's concession that a host may proceed despite a plausibly exposed neighbor's refusal. Its reversible-delay versus irreversible-exposure weighing survived PRO's mortality framing, which counted unproven trial benefits too directly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it directly answered both pressure questions, offered a non-self-sealing evidentiary pathway, distinguished direct cross-border exposure from indirect policy spillovers, and turned PRO's neighbor-consent answer into the debate's central concession.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins a competitive debate on its case-by-case framework. It argued that irreversibility raises the burden of proof for each proposal rather than settling the question for every proposal. Its core distinction held up through the end: reviewers need evidence that consequences are acceptable across plausible outcomes, not proof of which outcome will occur. PRO also answered both pressure questions directly. Its strongest answer was that reversible threshold-limited releases cannot show whether a self-propagating drive's protection would persist. That neutralized CON's "alternatives can answer the efficacy questions" route, and CON never really engaged that point again. CON (Side A) had the punchier rhetoric and a genuinely strong irreversibility framing. Its claim that "if the question can only be answered by doing the irreversible thing, that is the case for not doing it" was memorable. Its point that the list of at-risk communities is itself an output of the unverified model was also good. However, CON repeatedly overclaimed concessions. It treated PRO's "no community must accept that design" as abandoning the motion, when PRO plainly meant communities retain a right to refuse. It also cast PRO's refuse-if-recall-is-needed condition as a hidden admission, which overstated what PRO said. CON's second rebuttal was clipped for length, an execution cost. CON's delivery-gap argument was largely dropped after PRO's comparison about nets not covering bites before bedtime.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive exchange. PRO argued that a reversible intervention showing that reducing a local vector reduces biting cannot establish whether a persistent drive's protection lasts once vector replacement is considered. That turned CON's own replacement objection against its "use reversible alternatives instead" route, and CON only restated its position in response.
- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): B wins a close but clear debate by preserving the motion's actual distinction: rejecting a demand for long-term ecological certainty while keeping a stringent ex ante safety standard. A made a strong irreversibility and consent case, but B answered it by showing reviewers can identify plausibly exposed communities and refuse releases when they cannot, without needing to certify the drive's exact range. B also turned A's own replacement-ecology objection against the proposed reversible alternatives, since threshold-limited releases cannot test whether a self-propagating drive's protection would last. A's closing overread B's "no community must accept" as abandoning the motion and treated inability to certify outcomes as equivalent to inability to form a safety case, which B had already distinguished. A's clipped second rebuttal was a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's use of A's replacement-ecology point against threshold-limited releases was the most decisive: if one vector replaces another, a reversible release cannot establish that a self-propagating drive would deliver lasting protection, undermining A's safer-alternative route.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO)): PRO won by distinguishing safety that must be acceptable across plausible outcomes before release from efficacy that only a field trial can test. That answered CON's irreversibility and consent pressures without denying them: unidentifiable exposure or recall-dependent safety means refuse that proposal, not impose a permanent blanket veto. CON powerfully stressed unbounded spread and irreversible error, but overstated PRO's concessions about community refusal and reversible alternatives, and never showed the regulated-permission category must be empty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: reviewers can use contained-population behavior, recorded mosquito distribution, and prior replacement patterns to judge plausible exposure without certifying an exact range, while a reversible intervention cannot answer whether this intervention's protection persists.
