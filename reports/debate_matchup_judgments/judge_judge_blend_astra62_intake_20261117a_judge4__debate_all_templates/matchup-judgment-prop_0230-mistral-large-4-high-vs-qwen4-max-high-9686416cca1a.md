### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A built the cleaner path to victory. A repeatedly turned B's strongest examples—Estonia and Ukraine—into evidence for mandatory service, showing that refresher reserves and mobilized manpower depend on an intake obligation. A's key move was narrowing the dispute to "obligation versus offer": B never supplied a convincing source for a voluntary reserve pool that could fill at crisis speed. B's coercion and opportunity-cost attacks were real, but A answered them with transparent criteria, civilian options, graduated enforcement, and the sharper time asymmetry: a generation never trained cannot be bought back in a crisis. B's final insistence that only universal induction counts as mandatory national service was a definitional narrowing that selective conscription systems plainly undercut. The debate remained competitive, but A's comparisons and pressure questions carried the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was A's use of Estonia: B cited Estonia as proof that readiness comes from repeated reserve training, but A showed that Estonia's pool is created by conscription and then refreshed. That turned B's own evidence into a live contradiction B never fully resolved.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won the decisive exchanges. A opened by citing Estonia as proof that readiness comes from voluntary-style reserve training, and B correctly turned this: Estonia's reserve is generated by conscription, and its selective intake is itself an expansion model the motion explicitly allows ("reintroduce or expand"). B's pressure question exposing that A had spent three speeches attacking a universal draft nobody proposed was the sharpest strategic moment, and A's definitional retreat ("selective isn't really mandatory") never fully answered it, since Norway/Sweden-style systems are legally mandatory with selective intake. B also repeatedly pressed the unresolved gap in A's case — where a voluntary reserve's trained pool comes from before its first refresher — and A's answer ("paid recruitment, built over years") conceded the time asymmetry B then crystallized: obligations fill when the state needs them; offers fill when convenient. B handled A's fairness and enforcement pressure directly with proper A1/A2 format, and the "conscription by unemployment" reframe plus the recoverable-money-versus-untrained-generation weighing gave clean comparative reasons to vote PRO. A was polished and the definitional pivot was clever, but both of its flagship examples (Estonia, Ukraine) ended up working for B, and its closing was largely recap with a less convincing inversion of B's asymmetry.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 turn on Estonia [B2.1-B2.2]: "You cannot refresh a pool that was never filled" seized A's own flagship example and converted it into proof of conscription's necessity, then A's forced pivot to "Estonia is selective, not universal" was met by B's Q1 pointing out the motion says reintroduce or expand — the definitional trap A never escaped.
