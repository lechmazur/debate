- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Side A won the decisive clash on minutes. B conceded ranking raises time spent (and even offered "opens another app" as an alternative), conceded in A1 that neither sleep displacement nor comparison dose is immune to minutes, yet rested its defense on a "fixed attention budget" it never grounded. But B needed either net-new-minutes denial with support or equal-dose proof, and its closing response ("a fact about an app, not a fourteen-year-old's night") was an assertion, not a neutralization. Both sides showed excellent epistemic discipline — cautious hedging, no fake citations. A's progression, synthesis of B's concessions, and final line ("Watching in order to retain is.") made the debate easier to decide for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.6-A4.8/A5: A's "union of harms" argument — the ranked feed doesn't remove the classmate, it ranks her, then adds the strangers — forced B into the "fixed slots" defense, which A then collapsed in closing by pairing B's concession that ranking raises time spent with B's concession that no pathway is immune to minutes. B's B4.5 fixed-slots counter and B4.3 explore-exploit answer were the best resistance in the debate, but they rested on the unproven fixed budget.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won a close debate by tying the ranking objective—retain the viewer, never reach “caught up,” and amplify whatever holds them—to a higher dose of comparison, rabbit-holing, and sleep loss than a chronological feed. - **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by anchoring the motion as a strict comparative claim ("swapping the ranker for a timestamp") and showing that A's evidence — internal body-image research, deactivation studies — lacked the comparator the proposition requires, while the nearest direct tests (chronological-feed swaps) came back flat. A fought back impressively: the pressure question on training signals, the single-channel behavior argument ("the flinch is read in one direction only"), and the recruitment-vs-availability asymmetry kept the mechanism clash alive, and A answered both pressure questions honestly.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure question demanding the training signal behind "personalization is how the feed learns to stop" was the sharpest exchange: B answered directly with retention, session length, and churn mechanics (B4.1), and A's closing rejoinder that rumination looks identical to enjoyment in a behavioral signal was the best moment of the debate — but B's earlier B2.1 ("infinite supply is a property of a platform full of fifteen-second clips, not of the sort order") did more structural damage by breaking A's opening frame.
