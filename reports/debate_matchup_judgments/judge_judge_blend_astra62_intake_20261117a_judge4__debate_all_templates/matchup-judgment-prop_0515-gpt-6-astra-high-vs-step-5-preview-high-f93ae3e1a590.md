- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both sides argued with discipline and quickly narrowed the debate to one question: should curricular continuation carry a lighter evidentiary burden than departure? PRO had the stronger opening frame. Its "classroom lottery" equity story and its point about novices needing a default under scarce time were real advantages, and PRO's best argument is that some tiebreaker is needed when evidence is mixed. CON, however, did more damage in the exchange. It reframed the default as "also a bet." It pressed PRO's "plan to return to the sequence" until PRO softened return into "no code prerequisite left unaddressed" and allowed a prescribed sequence to be replaced. CON then argued that this protection does not require structure to outrank classroom judgment. PRO's closing asymmetry, that "persistence risks inefficiency" while departure risks a lost year, was asserted rather than defended. CON's closing exposed it: continuing instruction that misses a child's barrier also loses learning time. PRO's line that CON lets a progression be abandoned "without evidentiary cost" also misdescribed CON, which required evidence either way. PRO kept a live route through its point that symmetry leaves no default under uncertainty, so this is close. Even so, CON neutralized more of PRO's pressure than the reverse.
