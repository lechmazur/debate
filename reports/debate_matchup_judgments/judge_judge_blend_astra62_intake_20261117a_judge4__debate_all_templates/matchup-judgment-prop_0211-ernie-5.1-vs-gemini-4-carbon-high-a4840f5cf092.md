### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing and pressing two unresolved tensions in the PRO case. First, B justified the mandate on incomplete long-term outcome data [B1.1] but then defended a 12-month weight-loss checkpoint [B2.3]; A flagged the retreat, and B's rescue—staged multi-year payments [B4.1]—was effectively dismantled by A's point that monthly chronic prescriptions across insurer churn, pregnancies, and drug switching make multi-year attribution unworkable [A4.3]. Second, A forced a dilemma on spending caps—ration access or demand free chronic supply [A3.2]—and B's answer of "flexible caps with annual review" [B4.4] quietly abandoned the original "hard ceiling" [B1.2], which A explicitly called out as collapsing into the ordinary formulary pricing CON already endorses [A4.4]. B's pressure Q1 also mischaracterized A's position (A never argued for blocking coverage), and A corrected this cleanly [A4.1]. B's strongest remaining thread—that upfront discounts never verify real-world disease prevention—was met with a coherent alternative: cost-effectiveness review prices uncertainty day one and contracts reprice as evidence matures [A5.2]. B's rhetoric was punchy ("public money follows proof"), but A did more comparative weighing, answered every pressure directly, and made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal [A4.3]-[A4.4] was decisive: it showed that B's staged-payment rescue fails for chronic therapy with patient churn, and that B's "flexible caps" abandon the original "hard ceiling," collapsing the PRO mechanism into the standard discounting CON already supports—turning B's two escapes into concessions.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B won the central “only when” clash: A established reasons to control spending, but not why these two contractual mechanisms must be prerequisites for broad reimbursement. B also answered both pressure questions directly and challenged A’s proposed safeguards through tracking difficulties, patient-selection incentives, and the tension between hard caps and continued access.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest rebuttal was that calling discounts “complements” never answers why sufficiently favorable discount-only deals must be rejected. Its follow-up exposed how A’s flexible-cap proposal weakened the promised hard ceiling, while staged outcome payments retained the long-term tracking problems already raised.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won on the literal 'only when' gate: it showed payers could secure immediate value through cost-effectiveness review, formulary competition, and volume-tiered discounts, while B's outcome test retreated from long-term endpoints to a 12-month surrogate and its hard cap softened into renegotiated discounts.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was A's Rebuttal 2: B's staged five-year payments were shown unworkable amid insurer churn, and its 'flexible cap' either abandoned the hard ceiling for A's own discount model or forced rationing and midstream switching.

### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the stronger path to victory. B's affordability-vs-cost-effectiveness distinction and the "bargaining chip" argument went essentially unanswered, and B decisively exploited A's own concessions: A4.1 admitted that conditioning coverage "sharpens" the formulary-exclusion threat, and A2.2 admitted firms price to maximize revenue — both of which B turned against A's veto/monopsony arguments. B answered pressure questions directly (A1:/A2: format, clean mechanisms: refill-based rebates, back-end reconciliations under caps). A landed real hits — the surrogate-endpoint critique and the sharp observation that a spending cap does not claw back dropout waste — but B's "two distinct tools" response held. A's closing pivoted heavily to the veto/delay framing B had already blunted, and A never explained how HTA plus discounts manages system-wide affordability when millions qualify at once. B also showed better epistemic discipline: plausible mechanisms, no loose sourcing, whereas A's oncology reference-pricing claim and "legally fraught" point were under-supported and dropped. A's toll-booth rhetoric was memorable but rested on a premise B had already neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4: B catches A abandoning the development-cost justification and openly admitting firms charge specialty prices across a massive population "to maximize total revenue," then shows that A's simultaneous claim of precondition-free monopsony leverage is self-defeating — leverage requires the credible power to say no, which unconditional broad coverage surrenders. This was the decisive exchange that collapsed A's veto argument and was reinforced again in B's closing.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A made the stronger comparative case: individual cost-effectiveness does not establish population-wide affordability, and spending caps survive B's objections to outcome tracking. B correctly challenged A's tendency to equate ordinary reimbursement with an open checkbook, but never adequately explained how its alternative handles aggregate exposure or avoids the bargaining delays it criticized. A sharpened these clashes through direct answers and effective use of B's concessions. A's categorical claims about manufacturer participation and zero prevention after dropout limit the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing effectively paired B's admission that caps protect aggregate budgets with its concession that conditional coverage strengthens bargaining leverage. Criticizing caps for not also recovering dropout payments did not defeat the motion's either/or structure, while B's repeated manufacturer-veto claim remained insufficiently comparative.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side B built the clearer fiscal-risk case and repeatedly answered Side A's objections: outcome deals can use persistence and sustained metabolic measures with rebates after access, while caps need no clinical tracking. B also exposed A's contradiction between revenue-maximizing manufacturers and monopsony leverage without a credible refusal, and showed tiered formularies ration patients rather than control total spending. A's veto and delay claims were repeated but never overcame the rival-competition and guaranteed-volume mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it corrected A's misattributed withdrawal warning, explained formulary exclusion and competition as anti-withdrawal mechanisms, and turned A's own revenue-maximization and leverage claims against unconditional coverage. Side A's later concession that conditioning sharpens exclusion pressure further strengthened B.
