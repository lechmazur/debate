### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it turned CON's own procedural alternative, firm deadlines and limits on serial lawsuits, into a concession that those measures necessarily reduce some review or litigation. CON could not sustain a clean 'safeguards intact' position and instead demanded a stronger causal guarantee than the status quo ever faces. PRO's asymmetry between irreversible delay emissions and more localized, partly mitigable harms under retained substantive floors was clearer after the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal 1 concession turn was the decisive exchange: CON had endorsed firm deadlines and limits on serial lawsuits, which PRO showed are themselves reduced review and litigation pathways. That put CON on the defensive for the rest of the debate.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the much clearer and more complete path to victory across every major clash. Side A scored real points by arguing that statutory floors are not self-enforcing without site-specific fact-finding and that built transmission lines permanently fragment habitat rather than being easily rerouted. However, Side B decisively neutralized Side A's two main routes to victory. First, Side B trapped Side A's procedural counterplan in a dilemma: either firm deadlines and limits on serial suits cut off unresolved local challenges (conceding the motion's core trade-off), or allowing supplemental review whenever litigants allege potential statutory violations ([A4.1]) preserves the delay mechanism. Meanwhile, when pressed in Q1 to define "significant" streamlining, Side B owned concrete, realistic reforms in [B4.1]-[B4.2] while preserving substantive legal floors. Second, Side B dismantled Side A's causation defense by showing that interconnection queues and grid bottlenecks are downstream of unpermitted wires and that financing and equipment orders wait on permit certainty. Side A ultimately retreated to an asymmetric burden of proof that demanded causal certainty from permitting reform while excusing a decade of fossil-fuel emissions during status-quo delays.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Across [B2.6]-[B2.10] and [B4.4]-[B4.5], Side B dismantled Side A's claim that non-permitting constraints (transmission bottlenecks, interconnection queues, financing, equipment, and crews) undermine the causal value of faster permits—showing first that missing wires and backed-up queues are downstream symptoms of unpermitted lines, and second that financing and transformer orders happen in sequence after permitting because capital prices in the risk of decade-long litigation. Side A's reply that this showed "sequencing, not causation" failed to blunt the mechanism.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by matching the motion as written and giving the cleaner vote. It specified significant streamlining—one lead review, programmatic zones, shorter consolidated court windows, and no local veto of regional lines—while keeping substantive floors, and showed that Side B’s own deadlines and limits on serial suits already accept reduced pathways. Side B landed real hits: permits are not sufficient, the Texas example shows sequencing rather than proven displacement, and some habitat harms are permanent. Those kept it competitive, but B never fully escaped demanding proof from building and not from delay, and “speed with nothing given up” stayed less operational than A’s repairable-versus-irreversible error frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first rebuttal trapped B: firm deadlines and limits on serial suits either have teeth and reduce review, or they are empty. B’s later split—narrow supplemental analysis and no automatic extension—was the best answer, but still left delay available whenever a legal violation is alleged, which A then weighed against unrecoverable emissions.

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won the marginal comparison. PRO's only concrete reduced pathway was a local rehearing under broader local standards, which CON convincingly showed was not merely duplicative and removed substantive community protection. PRO's refutation of CON's remedy fork was strong but did not resolve the core cost-benefit gap; A's repeated clipped turns were a real execution cost, but the visible line remained cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Most decisive was CON's rebuttal 2 turning PRO's own admission that the removed local rehearing applied "broader local legal standards" and saved only months into proof that the marginal gain was small and the lost pathway was non-duplicative.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won clearly by isolating the motion's controversial margin—actual cuts to local environmental review and court access—from non-sacrificial streamlining like agency staffing, concurrent reviews, and proactive corridor planning. Side A showed how record-based judicial deference makes procedural claims indispensable for catching omitted harms, forcing Side B to walk back restrictions on procedural litigation and narrow its concrete cut to local rehearings yielding modest, non-uniform time savings.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [A4.3]-[A4.6], Side A turned both of Side B's concrete Turn 7 answers against the motion: showing that abolishing local rehearings under "broader local legal standards" [B4.1] deletes substantive local habitat and water protections rather than mere duplication, while keeping "failure to consider" claims [B4.2] preserves the exact procedural litigation pathway Side B had argued for narrowing.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON kept the motion’s hard clause—reducing review and litigation, not just uncontroversial staffing and concurrency—at the center and forced PRO to name a concrete cut. PRO’s scale and “no stopping rule” replies never showed why elimination, rather than a deadline on the same hearing, was required for significant speed, especially once both sides treated interconnection and agency capacity as the larger delays. Irreversible local harms therefore outweighed a modest, non-uniform gain.
