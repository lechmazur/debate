### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): B wins because A’s core attack depended on a strained reading of the motion: that presumptive release would eliminate all long-term custody and impose an arbitrary cap. B repeatedly showed the motion bans indefinite detention, not identified detention, and preserves individualized, evidence-based custody for flight risk or danger. A never grounded the supposed fixed limit, and its high-risk example fit the narrow exception B identified. B’s pressure questions, especially Q1 on where the motion sets a time cap, exposed that weakness, and A’s later answer was more assertion than answer. The public-safety concern was real but B adequately addressed it through criminal law, renewed detention grounds, and the costs of open-ended administrative custody.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal and pressure-question exchange were the decisive turn: A treated the violent noncitizen as proof the motion forced release, but B showed such a case was exactly where evidence-based, time-limited detention could remain, and A never located any actual arbitrary limit in the proposition.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): B won by defending the motion as written: presumptive release with an evidence burden, not automatic release of proven risks. B repeatedly showed that flight risk, concealed identity, refusal to engage, and danger remain grounds for custody, while indefinite hold-because-removal-is-hard is the practice being banned. A pressed a real hard case but never located the “arbitrary cap,” treated B’s opening exceptions as a post-hoc add-on, and leaned on unsupported “sharply higher,” “near-zero,” and “near-perfect” claims. B answered both pressure questions directly; A largely restated the misread.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal was decisive: the violent unrepatriable case is the narrow category where justified custody remains, and absconding or reoffending is a fresh ground for detention rather than proof that open-ended confinement was required.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the proposition’s practical meaning clearer: presumptive release does not require releasing proven high-risk people, but shifts the default to individualized, reviewable justification for detention. Side B kept a live hard-case pressure, but too often treated the motion as if it necessarily imposed an arbitrary release mandate and relied on unsupported claims about compliance rates. A’s rebuttals answered the central pressure more directly, preserved a role for detention where danger, flight risk, or noncooperation is shown, and framed the decisive contrast as proof versus bureaucratic delay. B’s public-safety framing was forceful, but it never proved that the proposed replacement must release someone who remains demonstrably dangerous or non-compliant.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s response to B’s violent non-citizen example was decisive: A treated that case as exactly where individualized, evidence-based detention may continue, while B could not show that the motion imposes a fixed cap forcing release despite ongoing risk.
