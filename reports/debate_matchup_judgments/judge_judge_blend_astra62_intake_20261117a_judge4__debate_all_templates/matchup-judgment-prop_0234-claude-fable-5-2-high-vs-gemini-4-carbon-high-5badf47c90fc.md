### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): A wins because its distortion costs stayed live and better answered the clash: public role-level metrics invite title inflation, churn-inflated promotion rates, padded bands, and safe proxies for subjective merit. B's later concession that bands need not show distributions weakened its pay-equity claim, and its closing mainly reframed A's harms as fear of hard questions rather than resolving them. B had a strong "imperfect information beats none" line, so the edge is clear but competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's promotion-scorecard rebuttal was decisive: pairing bands does not stop title inflation because adjacent bands overlap even in honest hierarchies, and a burnout shop backfilling exits can post a higher promotion rate than a high-retention team. That undercut B's core informational-benefit claim and remained unresolved.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A increasingly relied on “some information beats none” without adequately weighing misleading signals and behavioral distortions. Its closing also mischaracterized B's explicit reasons for rejecting additional metrics as opposition to worker scrutiny itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's observation that legitimate promotion bands also overlap directly defeated A's claim that paired disclosures expose title inflation. Its “boundary, not a distribution” challenge also forced A to narrow its pay-equity promise to the more modest benefit of knowing one's position within a range.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by keeping the comparison focused on workers' current near-blindness versus imperfect but usable disclosure. ### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by persistently pressing the self-authoring problem. PRO gave a strong opening and useful cross-checks, but A exposed the central tension: bands must be rigid enough to block side deals yet elastic enough to retain outliers, and promotion rates can be gamed with employer-built rungs. A's closing frame, "who writes the numbers?", cleanly turned PRO's concessions about invented titles, market-only band changes, and lost stars into a reason why the costs are certain while the information benefit remains unreliable. PRO defended secrecy's harms well, but A more decisively resolved the clash in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive rebuttal was A4.2-A4.6: A used PRO's own retention solutions against it, showing that stretching a public band becomes a side deal with a press release, while openly promoting high performers makes retention-driven title bumps indistinguishable from real advancement, contaminating the very signal PRO relies on.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): A won the comparison between imperfect disclosure and continued information asymmetry. B raised credible gaming and retention risks, but never established that informative bands must be binding or that employer-defined promotion rates are worse than undisclosed progression and recruiter promises. A answered the strongest pressure: public ceilings can change without automatic raises for everyone, and retention-driven advancement still conveys value when accompanied by meaningful pay growth. A overstated how reliably scrutiny prevents favoritism, but more convincingly weighed accountability against exceptional retention costs. B’s alternatives conceded the promotion-information gap, while its closing asserted rather than established that the mandate’s benefits disappear. Its clipped turns were a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s ceiling-expansion question forced B to concede that retaining one star does not mechanically require raising everyone’s salary. B recovered by identifying scrutiny as a behavioral cost, but A’s closing directly weighed that cost: paying comparable peers fairly or explaining differences is a central benefit of disclosure, not automatically a reason against it.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Carbon (high) (PRO)): CON's wide-versus-binding band dilemma and employer-authored promotion metric survived the exchange. PRO answered directly and plausibly on paired disclosure, but never fully reconciled needing bands elastic for stars yet rigid against favors, or promotions as both retention tools and honest signals. CON's closing cleanly turned PRO's concessions—stars may walk, titles may be invented, bands move only with the market—into a certain cost for an unreliable benefit, despite repeated clipping hurting CON's execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it conceded no automatic raises, then showed B's own scrutiny mechanism deters ceiling lifts, and that a retention promotion with a real raise passes B's pay-band cross-check without proving genuine advancement.
