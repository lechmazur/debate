### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won by framing the proposal as an optional, reversible, feature-specific control and showing that search, subscriptions, direct links, and catalogues could remain available. Side A identified a serious vulnerable-teen concern, but repeatedly assumed recommendation systems would reliably surface lifesaving support and that delay controls would cause broad age fraud. Those claims remained speculative, while Side B directly explained why one possible misuse should not deny controls to all families, especially younger children. Side B's clipped first rebuttal was an execution cost, but not enough to offset its clearer comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest response rejected Side A's equation of disabling personalized feeds with severing all discovery: it preserved multiple access routes, emphasized that recommenders are unreliable emergency services, and turned optionality against Side A's worst-case-parent framing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively exposing the flaws in Side B's worst-case scenarios, noting that Side B treated an optional parental control as a compulsory shutdown. Side A successfully countered the "lifeline" argument by reminding the judge that algorithms are optimized for engagement, not emergency support, and neutralized the age-fraud argument by pointing out it shouldn't dictate policy for younger children.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 in Turn 7, which conceded that distressed teens might struggle to search, but masterfully flipped this to demonstrate why the tool must be an individualized parental option rather than relying on a commercially driven algorithm.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion as optional, reversible, child-specific control over the mechanism that selects what comes next. CON's strongest path — vulnerable teens need passive discovery and delay drives age fraud — was sharply pressured by A3 and largely defused in A4: search/subscriptions remain, an engagement-optimized feed is not a reliable emergency service, and a supportive parent can leave it on. CON never answered that parents can already isolate children and that timers/filters don't address recommendation. Despite the clipped R1, PRO's weighing on who should have final say was clearer and more responsive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.4 answering the lifeline pressure: optional control means the worst-case parent hypothetical does not veto every family, and non-personalized alternatives need not be a harmful popularity contest.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that granular parental controls are a proportional improvement over the current choice between deleting an app and accepting its feed. Side A raised serious autonomy, abuse, and implementation concerns, but its safer-platform alternative did not establish why regulation and parental brakes cannot coexist. B's age-calibration concession preserved the motion's modest comparative claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest response was that an imperfect recommendation block can still narrow exposure, while existing parental authority is already broader and blunter. This undercut A's porosity objection and reframed the proposal as proportional harm reduction rather than absolute parental control.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully forcing Side A to heavily qualify its stance. By pressing Side A on whether a 17-year-old could appeal a parental block, Side B baited Side A into adopting teen appeals and statutory regulations. Side B then decisively framed this concession as proof that child safety actually relies on institutional safeguards, binding platform duties, and youth agency rather than raw parental power.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Turn 8 (B's Rebuttal 2) decisively capitalized on A's answers to the Pressure Questions, pointing out that A's newly proposed statutory regime and teen appeal mechanisms were exactly the kinds of limits on parental power that B had been advocating for all along.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): PRO kept a live route: current choice is all-or-nothing delete vs accept invisible engagement feed, and a granular block/delay narrows attack surface as defense-in-depth while regulation lags. CON's strongest clashes - poor boundary [B1.2] and teen autonomy [B1.4][B2.4] - were mitigated when PRO defined the switch as passive feed/autoplay/suggestions [A4.1] and conceded a 17-year-old appeal [A4.2]. CON still showed a default-protection advantage, but did not establish that parents should NOT have more power; its alternative of binding duties does not preclude the modest extra switch, and its surveillance dilemma was less grounded than PRO's proportionality point.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.4 - PRO's proportionality reply that CON's abusive/controlling-parent objection proves too much because it would also invalidate existing powers to delete apps and restrict devices parents already hold.
