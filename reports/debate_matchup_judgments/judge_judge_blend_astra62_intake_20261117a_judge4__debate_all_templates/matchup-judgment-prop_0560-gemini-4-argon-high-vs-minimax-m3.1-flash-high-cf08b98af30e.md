### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B wins. A built a reasonable value-per-dollar case, but B made the decisive clash about which metric and which patients count.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 turned A's admission that prevention may add net costs against A's crowd-out claim, isolating the trade as comfortable terminal months versus diffuse preventive gains and reinforcing both the moral-asymmetry and innovation arguments.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) won a competitive debate. A's speeches progressed, crystallized the clash, and made the debate easier to decide; A's execution costs were minimal while B's closing failure was real.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive sequence was A's "twin concession" attack (A4.4) — that B admitted both denying terminal patients comfortable months and that prevention adds net cost — met by B's honest pivot in B4.1/B4.2 (priority, not savings; same per-dollar threshold for both). B survived on logic, but A's closing catch of B5.4's misquotation of A's own description, combined with the unanswered hindsight problem on innovation, gave A the cleaner path to victory.
