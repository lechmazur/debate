B’s own answers—that no identity-building mega-event could be named without that rooted culture, and that the spectacle is only a temporary amplifier—confirmed rather than neutralized the ranking. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won a solid victory by pairing sharp comparative framing with concrete historical grounding that Side A never matched. While Side A built a thoughtful conceptual case around resident authorship, decentralized facilitation, and normal-case participation, it stayed entirely abstract—never substantiating mega-event failures or offering an independent city example of its own. Side B persuasively argued that place identity requires citywide unity and external salience (flipping Side A's substitutability claim onto ubiquitous neighborhood food and art markets) and showed through Barcelona, Glasgow, and Edinburgh that prestige showcases often build the physical and civic stage upon which recurring local culture thrives.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: After Side A conceded in [A2.2] that Barcelona and Glasgow were "legacies catalyzed by an event," Side B used [B3.1] to ask for one city recognized beyond its region through creator-led festivals without an upstream showcase. When Side A named Edinburgh in [A4.1] while admitting it was "not a laboratory-pure" case, Side B capitalized in [B4.1] by showing that Side A's own flagship example began as a deliberate post-war showcase, cementing B's root-versus-branch framing.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the catalyst-versus-downstream clash decisive and never letting Side A reverse it. B turned A's own admissions—that Barcelona and Glasgow were event-catalyzed legacies, that Edinburgh was not a pure counterexample, and that spectacle should not be wholly replaced—into a direct case that the motion requires preferring the upstream instrument. A's ownership and ordinary-effects theory remained attractive, but A never neutralized the missing counterexample, the coordination-is-authorship pressure, or the retreat from "prefer" to balance.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's first rebuttal, which converted A's "legacies catalyzed by an event" concession into the branch-over-root frame, then sharpened it in the second rebuttal by treating "not to replace all spectacle" as a retreat from the motion's required preference.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = MiniMax M3.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): B won by making identity relational and turning the mega-event into the upstream catalyst for the recurring culture A prizes. A defended local ownership and ordinary, durable participation, but never cleanly explained how fragmented recurring events gain citywide or external salience without the central coordination B exposed as mega-event logic. A's closing weighing on rare ceilings versus ordinary effects helped, yet B's root-versus-branch comparison remained the cleaner live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question demanding one externally recognized city built by recurring festivals alone was decisive: A offered Edinburgh while conceding it was not pure, allowing B to repeatedly present Edinburgh, Barcelona, and Glasgow as prestige showcase first and recurring identity downstream.
