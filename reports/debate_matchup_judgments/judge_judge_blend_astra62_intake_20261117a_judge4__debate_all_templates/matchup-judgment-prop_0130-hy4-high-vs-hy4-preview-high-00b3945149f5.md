### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it built the cleaner decision rule and kept it consistent across the debate. Its core claim was that care creates duties, not title, so preservation governs how and when an artifact is returned, not whether. PRO then sharpened this into the distinction between an adequate receiving institution and the best possible one. That distinction directly answered the motion's "even when" clause. CON's strongest moment was A2.1. It argued that PRO's title-first model, with physical movement "only when safe," amounts to foreign custody under a new name. That pressure was real. PRO answered it squarely in A1 by specifying that the object physically moves once an adequacy floor is met, even if the old vault has finer equipment. CON's own answers then blurred its position. In A4.1 it said transfer becomes automatic once an adequacy floor is met, which is essentially PRO's mechanism. In A4.3 and A4.6 it instead required that transfer be "no longer riskier" than staying in the superior vault. PRO's closing exposed this slide between adequate and best. It also showed how a "while superior, it stays" rule turns a marginal equipment advantage into indefinite possession by the beneficiary of the taking. CON's closing line, "safe is not better," tracks the motion's wording well. However, it relies on asserting that an adequate facility is meaningfully riskier, without showing that this risk outweighs the continuing dispossession PRO emphasized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was the second round of pressure questions and answers. PRO's A1 drew a sharp line between "better" and "only safe," backed by a concrete adequacy floor and expiring, reviewable emergency custody. CON's reply adopted much of that panel-and-floor framework but also insisted on comparative superiority, which let PRO argue in its closing that CON's rule makes foreign custody the default for as long as the richer museum stays "better."
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): B wins by distinguishing adequate preservation from the best available preservation. Its eventual physical-return requirement, with narrow emergency delays, answers A's strongest objection that title transfer alone is not return. A meaningfully answers the unilateral-veto criticism through independent review, but still requires transfer to be no riskier than foreign custody. It never adequately explains why even marginal conservation advantages outweigh restitution and restored authority. Instead, A repeatedly treats comparatively worse preservation as impending destruction. B supplies the stronger decision rule while addressing both ownership and material survival.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal directly answers A's pressure: “Better” is not “only safe.” The adequacy floor permits actual return despite superior foreign facilities. A's subsequent insistence on equal safety clarifies the remaining disagreement but does not establish why every additional risk should defeat restitution.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): B won a close, high-quality debate by drawing the cleaner distinction between adequate safety and comparatively superior preservation. Its second rebuttal directly answered the physical-custody and decision-trigger challenges with title transfer, funded readiness, independent review, expiring emergency custody, and automatic physical return.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: “better” was separated from “only safe,” answering A’s strongest custody objection while preserving a narrow emergency exception. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides converged on similar practical mechanisms: neutral panels, funded capacity, shared custody, and eventual return. The decision therefore turns on who better fit the motion's "even when" clause. PRO opened strongly. The burglar analogy, the missing limiting principle, and the cultural-context point all landed. PRO also scored real hits when it argued that CON's safeguards concede the destination is home, and that possessor-controlled review becomes a permanent loan. CON, however, adapted more effectively: - It accepted independent review. - It answered Q2 directly by listing what claims are too weak to block return, such as finer cases, larger audiences, and marginal humidity advantages. - It then used PRO's own "binding transfer date" and "physical transfer waits on safety" to build a clean dilemma: ship amid serious risk, or wait until the risk is gone, which is CON's condition. PRO's escape was to redefine return as transfer of title now with the artifact moving later. CON exploited this well: the motion says return artifacts, and "a deed is not restitution." PRO's closing doubled down that "only title is restitution," which reads as a substitution rather than a resolution. It also still claimed that under CON "the same institution defines serious risk," even though CON had explicitly committed to joint, independent review. That is a slight misdescription. PRO's distinction between mere comparative superiority and an actual failure mechanism was valuable. But CON had already adopted that same distinction, which blunted it. The result is a clear but competitive CON edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [A4.5]/[A4.7] exploited PRO's A2 answer. Once PRO said physical transfer waits on safety, CON argued the binding date either forces shipment amid serious risk or waits for capacity, which is CON's own conditional rule. CON added that title-only return leaves the artifact abroad, so it is not the return the motion demands. PRO's closing never escaped this dilemma and fell back on redefining restitution as title alone.
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): A wins by answering the preservation-veto objection with independent review, transparent evidence, and an object-specific threshold for irreversible harm. B's ownership-versus-logistics distinction was meaningful, but its eventual assertion that only title constitutes restitution weakened its connection to returning artifacts to their countries of origin. B also closed by attributing museum-controlled review to A despite A's explicit safeguards. A overstated the motion somewhat by treating it as requiring immediate return, which limits the margin, but handled the decisive pressure more convincingly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A exposed the unresolved tension between B's binding transfer date and its promise that physical transfer would wait for safety. Transferring title did not resolve that physical-return question, while A directly answered B's concerns about captured reviewers and weak preservation claims.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing the central tension in Side B’s case: B ultimately conceded that physical transfer must wait until preservation risks are resolved, then redefined return as an immediate transfer of title. A persuasively argued that this substitutes paperwork for returning artifacts to their countries of origin and thereby abandons the motion’s “even when” requirement. A also directly answered B’s pressure about captured museum assessments with joint appointment, open records, claimant-triggered review, and a threshold of likely irreversible damage. B’s ownership and cultural-context arguments were forceful, but never escaped the resulting dilemma between preservation-conditioned delay and risking the artifact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal and closing turned B’s “title today, artifact later” position into the decisive dilemma: a binding date either forces unsafe shipment or waits on preservation, while title-only return leaves the artifact abroad. B’s closing assertion that only title constitutes restitution did not answer that objection.
