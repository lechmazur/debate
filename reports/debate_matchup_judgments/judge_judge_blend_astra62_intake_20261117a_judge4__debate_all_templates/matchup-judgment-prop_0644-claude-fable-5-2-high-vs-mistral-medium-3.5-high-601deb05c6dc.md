### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive clash. B's core argument — that sunsets schedule fear rather than cure it, producing either rubber stamps or minority-driven accidents — was built almost entirely out of PRO's own concessions ("political risks in revoking," "the political cost of obstruction ensures renewal") and was never neutralized. A's pivotal error was redefining a decade of Patriot Act renewals as "the sunsets working," which B exploited with the unfalsifiability point: a mechanism that counts as success whether powers end or continue is not being tested. A's answers to B's pressure questions were weak — the A2 answer directly conceded the rubber stamp, and A's New York/California examples were late, thinly specified, and effectively rebutted as affirmative termination or executive self-termination. B answered A's pressure questions directly and cleanly with the asymmetry between a majority-stalled lapse and a majority-governed termination. B's two clipped turns are a minor execution cost, but the surviving content was surgical, well-sourced to the transcript, and progressed the debate each round. A's closing mostly repeated the circuit-breaker frame without resolving the falsifiability or rubber-stamp pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.5-B4.6]: quoting A's own line that "the political cost of obstruction ensures renewal" to show that PRO had conceded the rubber stamp — if fear guarantees renewal in every real crisis, the circuit breaker never trips when it matters, and if it trips, a minority, not a majority, caused it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): CON won by turning PRO's own premise—that legislators fear the political cost of ending emergency powers—against the sunset mechanism itself. CON cleanly answered the gridlock flip, dismantled the Patriot Act “success” redefinition and the late COVID examples, and left PRO unable to show the circuit breaker trips when fear is highest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal and closing, which held PRO to the concession that political cost “ensures renewal,” so the breaker cannot trip in a real crisis and, when it does, it is obstruction rather than deliberation.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.2 (high) (CON)): Side B made the decisive clash about whether sunset votes actually constrain fear-driven legislatures, turning PRO's Patriot Act example into evidence of rubber-stamp renewal and forcing PRO into the damaging concession that political pressure ensures renewal.
