### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO built the stronger net-deterrence case: permanent forward presence did not need to stop a Russian corps on its own, only to deny a quick fait accompli and make NATO commitment harder to reverse. CON's fixed-target, families, and hostage critique was sharp and kept this close, but PRO reabsorbed it as the source of credible commitment and exposed that CON's alternative also relies on reinforcement after an initial grab. CON's dispersed prepositioning and pre-delegated authority were plausible but more politically revocable, while CON's admission that nothing stops a corps under either posture left it relying on reversal after occupation, the very risk PRO centered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO pressing CON on what stops Russia from taking Vilnius before reinforcements, then converting CON's "nothing stops a corps" answer into an admission that CON relies on deterrence by reversal rather than denial.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): Both sides executed cleanly, honored the Q1/Q2 and A1/A2 formats, and used plausible background knowledge without fake sourcing. The decisive clash was whether permanent basing actually delivers the deterrence-by-denial that justifies accepting escalation risk. PRO's opening story (forces that "close the window" on a fait accompli) was seriously degraded by CON's structural point that the weeks-long surge is needed under both postures, so permanence "pays twice and buys the same surge." PRO's fallback to signaling and commitment theory was coherent and at times brilliant ("the signal and the target are the same thing"), but CON effectively flagged that as a confession that the risk was never priced, and caught PRO's inconsistency on families (sold as irrevocable signal in the opening, demoted to "a dependents policy" once the missile-strike scenario arrived). PRO's best remaining route — that CON's dispersed depots and reception bases are also fixed targets, and that crisis surges are the most escalatory move — was answered squarely by CON's dispersion/choice distinction and pre-delegated authority. PRO's "can be unsigned tomorrow" response kept that route barely alive but never showed the favorable probability comparison its own breaking point demanded. CON maintained cleaner burden discipline on capability per unit of risk and made the debate easier to decide, earning a narrow win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B2.3–B2.4 ("the surge happens under both postures; PRO pays twice and buys the same surge") structurally undermined PRO's core causal story, and the B4.5 catch — PRO banking families as proof of commitment in the opening, then disowning them as "a dependents policy" when the first-hour strike arrived — was the sharpest single exchange, crystallized by "you cannot bank the signal and disown the target."
- **GPT-6 Astra (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): B narrowly won the comparison between permanent basing and reinforcement-focused readiness. A never adequately established that its forward brigade could buy the time its reinforcement plan required, while B distinguished useful forward infrastructure from prioritizing permanent garrisons. A's warning about reversing an occupation under nuclear threats remained powerful and insufficiently answered. However, A's closing assurance that permanence makes a quick grab unwinnable exceeded its demonstrated case, leaving B's capability-per-risk objection stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest rebuttal was that A still required a crisis surge: permanent basing therefore did not eliminate the mobilization risk A attributed to B. This materially weakened A's clearest attempt to turn the escalation objection against its opponent.

### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): CON kept the motion's burden in focus: PRO needed to show that prioritizing permanent basing buys meaningful denial worth the accepted escalation risk. PRO's natural experiment actually conceded that current tripwire presence plus Article 5 already deters, and its denial promise collapsed into a handful of brigades with a self-imposed ceiling against long-range strike. CON turned that into a live dilemma: either the package is too small to deny Russian mass, or effective denial requires escalation PRO disclaims. PRO never resolved that contradiction, while CON's flexible reinforcement alternative preserved deterrence without locking scarce brigades into fixed targets.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's strongest exchange was the closing dilemma built from PRO's own answers: PRO promised denial but offered only brigade-scale forces and barred massed long-range strike, while also claiming permanence merely changes where existing brigades sleep. CON showed this means either permanence adds little deterrence or real denial crosses PRO's own escalation red line.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued at a high level, but Side B held a cleaner path to victory across the whole exchange. B's central burden — that the motion demands *prioritizing* permanent basing and accepting extra escalation risk, so PRO must show a marginal gain over existing deterrence — was established in the opening, enforced through pressure questions ("how many brigades does real denial take?"), and crystallized in the closing dilemma: the affordable version of PRO's denial is a heavier tripwire the Baltics already have, while the effective version requires deep fires that cross PRO's own escalation ceiling. A's closing reframes ("where they sleep," "a brigade in Lithuania is a border") landed well on the flexibility point but never fully answered whether a handful of brigades without deep fires can actually deliver "fails at the border" denial. A was more rhetorically vivid and its tripwire-versus-denial and risk-comparison framing were strong, but B's discipline, burden control, and the unresolved sufficiency dilemma give CON the narrow edge on the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4/B5.2: B caught that PRO's self-drawn ceiling against massed long-range strike excludes exactly the deep fires needed for genuine denial, turning PRO's own answer to pressure into a contradiction — either the brigades add little or they breach PRO's escalation line.
