### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): CON was the sharper debater for much of the round. Its opening named concrete bottlenecks: tritium breeding, materials qualification, and ITER's overruns. Its first rebuttal turned PRO's "reform the instrument" line into "defending my budget with their adjective" and recast the research option as "a subscription." Those were strong points. But the pressure round forced CON to define a floor. That floor protected plasma labs, the irradiation facility, the blanket program, and the university pipeline at roughly today's non-ITER level. CON also conceded that the test blanket hardware is indispensable. Together, that is a substantial, ongoing public commitment to fusion research, which is very close to what the motion affirms. PRO answered both questions directly. It gave an order-of-magnitude level and defined what would count as excessive. It then crystallized cleanly that the remaining dispute is about procurement discipline, not about whether the research deserves money at scale. CON's closing argued that PRO's reading makes the motion unfalsifiable. That was clever but not fully fair, since PRO did state limits. CON's other closing move, reading "even though" as requiring unconditional money ahead of results, felt like a late narrowing of the motion. PRO never squarely priced the odds of success, which keeps this competitive. Still, on the motion as written, PRO holds the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange came through PRO's Q2 and the answers in Rebuttal 2. CON was asked for a funding floor and committed to protecting the existing scientific core plus the blanket and irradiation programs. PRO's closing then leveraged that answer: CON's own floor amounts to continued large public investment, leaving only a disagreement over ITER and overrun discipline. That neutralized CON's earlier and stronger "adjective" and "subscription" attacks.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO)): Side A better preserved a live distinction between protecting a non-ITER scientific core and underwriting megaproject slips, private first-of-a-kind costs, and front-loaded checks. Side B’s late win condition—that A had already conceded “large” continued investment—depended on folding ITER-scale spending into a floor A had expressly carved out, and on recasting the motion as funding-versus-starvation. A answered the facility and capacity questions directly, then used B’s own gating, non-duplication, and near-term constraints to show that “even though decades away” was no longer doing independent work. That left the cleaner vote against expansive public commitment ahead of serial results.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s second rebuttal, which conceded the test-blanket hardware, named the cheaper surrounding information purchases, fixed the floor at non-ITER core spending, and turned B’s “routine portfolio” and “excessive” definitions into A’s own cap.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (PRO); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON)): CON won by forcing PRO to qualify "large" into milestone-gated, non-duplicative funding subordinate to nearer-term decarbonization, while never pricing fusion's probability or delivered cost. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Both sides were sharp and well disciplined, but PRO built the cleaner path to victory. CON's best weapon was hedge timing: fusion cannot pay in the 2030s–2040s crunch. PRO answered it directly. It argued that the windless winter of 2060 is also a real state. It also noted that fission's Western costs have risen and that seasonal storage is unbuilt. CON's closing then recast PRO's scenario as only the 2030s and 2040s, which PRO never limited itself to. CON's early "rounding error vs. large" contradiction was clever. PRO defused it with the absolute-versus-relative distinction and then named a concrete figure. CON's own threshold, about a billion a year as the line between research and a "national bet," looked arbitrary. PRO also turned CON's concession effectively. CON granted that materials qualification and tritium breeding need public funding. PRO showed those require costly dedicated facilities and sustained staff, which brings "large" investment back within the motion. CON did score real points: - The solar-in-1975 distinction, since solar was already delivering power to loads. - The opportunity-cost pressure on ten billion a year. - The "it becomes ITER again" retort. Still, PRO's option-asymmetry framing, that money can be revoted but forfeited capacity cannot, went largely unanswered beyond CON's assertion that skills transfer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1/A4.2 answer in Rebuttal 2 decided the debate. PRO named an order of magnitude and argued that CON's conceded "narrow" program needs a high-flux neutron source and in-environment blanket tests, a large investment under a modest label. That reframed CON's best concession-trap into support for the motion, and CON's billion-dollar line did not fully recover from it.
- **Grok 4.7 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because the motion already grants that commercial fusion may be decades away and asks whether large public funding should continue anyway. B kept that rule clearer: neutron-damage work, tritium breeding, and specialist capacity are public goods private capital will not carry, and cutting them is harder to reverse than a budget line. A's timing attack was the sharpest live hit—fusion does not cover the near-term winter B first invoked—but it raised the bar above the motion, and reclassifying the needed facilities as a sub-billion stipend did not hold. Absolute-versus-relative cost was not the contradiction A claimed, and B named an order of magnitude under pressure. The edge stays competitive because B never fully priced the multi-decade opportunity cost or closed the solar-analogy gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, arguing that neutron qualification and tritium breeding inside a fusion environment are sustained public programs rather than the narrow stipend A conceded, did the most to keep the proposition standing. A's point that a hedge must pay inside the crisis window was rhetorically sharper but fit the motion's decades-away premise less tightly.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side A resolved the scale contradiction by naming single-digit billions per major program, about ten billion globally: large absolutely yet small against trillion-dollar energy spending. More decisively, A captured B's concession that neutron-damage and tritium-breeding work needs public support, arguing plausibly that such facilities and test environments are themselves sustained large investment. A also re-anchored the hedge to the post-2050 grid rather than B's 2030s-40s window, making the acknowledged decades-long delay an option-value case rather than a fatal timing flaw. B's opportunity-cost and no-net-electricity pressure remained strong, but its billion-dollar cutoff looked arbitrary.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: its direct A1/A2 answers supplied the missing magnitude and turned B's narrow materials-and-tritium concession into evidence for the large public program the motion requires.
