### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): Both sides engaged the sleep-science premise seriously, but CON (A) built the more precise and adaptive case for how the motion should be judged: it distinguished "harder logistics" from "demonstrated harm" (unsafe transport, lost attendance, disrupted caregiving) and pressed PRO on whether the mandate still binds when it provably hurts the students it claims to protect. PRO's (B) answer in A1 ("Yes, the mandate compels it... reflects administrative reluctance") conceded the unconditional reading but opened a critical vulnerability: it made the policy functionally unfalsifiable, since any harmful outcome is simply redefined as insufficient logistical effort rather than evidence against the rule. CON exploited this cleanly in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, turning PRO's own answer into the clinching argument. PRO's younger-sibling-safety rebuttal (earlier circadian rhythms make earlier waking "fine") also never squarely answered CON's point about dark roadside waits and disrupted childcare from inverted bus tiers. PRO's case stayed forceful and well-organized throughout, with vivid biology-vs-institutions framing, but it largely repeated the same sleep-science and "institutions must adapt" framing across rounds rather than directly neutralizing CON's sharpest pressure point. That left CON with the clearer, more defensible path: supporting a strong presumption for later starts while rejecting an unconditional rule that persists even after proven harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Pressure Question (A3.1) asking whether the mandate still compels after demonstrated attendance/safety harm, and PRO's concessive "Yes" answer in A1 (B4.1), which CON then converted into the unfalsifiability critique in Rebuttal 2 and Closing — the single most decisive exchange of the debate.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by distinguishing strong evidence for later starts from justification for an unconditional national floor. Side A established substantial sleep and health benefits, but repeatedly assumed logistical harms would always be solved through adaptation. Its explicit concession that the mandate should remain even after demonstrated attendance or safe-transport harm left B with the cleaner welfare comparison. B supported later starts where beneficial without letting one clock time override contrary outcomes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B decisively exploited A4.1: when A said the mandate still applies despite demonstrated attendance or transport harm, B showed that A had made the policy unfalsifiable by relabeling every failure as administrative reluctance. A never supplied a limiting principle or directly resolved that counterexample.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by holding the motion’s hard “even when” burden: sleep science supports later starts as a strong presumption, not an unconditional clock that still binds after demonstrated attendance or transport harm. PRO’s A1 yes-and-reframe (harm equals mere administrative reluctance) made the rule unfalsifiable and left hard-family/access cases unanswered. Across rounds CON kept the clash on total student welfare versus rigid override, while PRO largely restated biology-vs-logistics without neutralizing that concession.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R2/closing on PRO’s [B4.1] “yes, still compel” answer: treating transport/attendance harm as proof of reluctance makes the mandate unfalsifiable.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.8 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a vivid, concrete case about bus logistics, elementary pre-dawn risk, and working-class teen economics, but repeatedly treated worst-case implementation failures (duplicate fleets, forced pre-dawn elementary starts, total bedtime-creep neutralization) as logical necessities of an 8:30 floor rather than policy choices, a framing PRO consistently and correctly challenged as conflating "difficult" with "impossible." The decisive pressure exchange was PRO's Q1 asking what principle stops "logistics" from excusing any amount of sleep loss (6:30, 7:00, etc.); CON's answer ("holistic student welfare and democratic accountability... communities set start times") never actually supplied a limiting principle, effectively conceding that its own logic has no floor. PRO, by contrast, directly used the required A1:/A2: format, maintained the distinction between the mandate's actual requirements and CON's assumed failure modes, and offered a coherent alternative (targeted support/redesign rather than universal early starts) to CON's strongest point about working teens. CON's practical grounding was strong and rhetorically effective, but PRO's tighter logical discipline and sharper handling of the pressure round gave it the cleaner path to the resolution as written. PRO's opening being clipped is a real but minor execution cost that didn't end up mattering much since its core arguments were restated and defended in later rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Q1 ("what stops logistics from excusing any amount of sleep loss?") and CON's vague, principle-free answer in Rebuttal 2 was the turning point: it exposed that CON's case, taken to its logical end, has no floor against ever-earlier start times, which PRO then used to reframe the entire debate as cost-shifting onto adolescents versus adaptable adult systems.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange challenged B's claim that later dismissal necessarily erases every sleep gain: B's examples applied most directly to a vulnerable subset, yet B generalized them to all students. B's phantom-buses response exposed weakness in A's implementation detail, but did not prove its own forced dilemma was exhaustive.

B’s pressure on the missing limiting principle and non-universal bedtime-creep claim landed, and B answered A’s A1/A2 burdens more cleanly (redesign/funding/mitigation rather than pre-dawn kids or program cuts). A remained competitively sharp on working-class scaffolding, yet never fully converted implementation friction into a reason to reject the motion as written.
