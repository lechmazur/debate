### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): B wins on the decisive weighing question. A built a plausible affirmative case from deadheading, mode shift, and dense-core concentration, but B exposed the gap between added bookings and net added delay. B's strongest move was showing that rising mileage does not prove transit/walking switchers dominate, because ride-hail replacing a private car can still add empty-approach miles. A tried to repair this with surveys, but B correctly noted that "a third or more" from transit/walking/forgone travel does not establish that car replacement was not the majority. B also reframed Manhattan and San Francisco as credible local harm, not proof across major cities overall, and answered A's "name one improved city" pressure with a valid counterfactual point: no observed improvement is not the same as no avoided delay. A's closing leaned on "as" being confused with "because," but B directly articulated and defended that distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to the mileage pressure question was decisive: even if ride-hail caused the entire mileage increase, empty-approach miles can rise for trips that replace private cars, so mileage growth does not force the conclusion that transit and walking switchers dominated.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): Side B won a competitive debate through sharper counterfactual analysis, cleaner responsiveness under pressure, and stronger epistemic discipline. Side A built a forceful baseline using deadheading, transit substitution surveys, and findings from Manhattan and central San Francisco, which Side B candidly conceded showed local harm. However, Side B systematically dismantled every bridge Side A used to extend those local core slowdowns to major cities overall: exposing the surge-pricing fallacy, showing that displaced downtown parking searches and taxi cruising relieve nonlinear delay on the exact same saturated blocks, proving that total vehicle-miles can rise even when car-replacement reduces bottleneck congestion, and explaining why demanding a growing city where raw traffic sped up confuses correlation ("as") with counterfactual causation ("because"). By contrast, Side A slid unwarrantedly from "a third or more" to "car-replacement is not the majority," misdescribed Side B's arguments twice, repeated its refuted mileage inference through Closing, and cited nonexistent reference IDs ([P3.1, C4.1]).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In Pressure Questions [B3.1] and Rebuttal 2 [B4.2-B4.3], Side B dismantled Side A's twin claims that rising urban vehicle-miles proved transit switchers dominated and that CON lacked a mechanism for savings on congested streets. Side B demonstrated that replacing a motorist's short, disruptive parking search on a saturated downtown block with a longer empty approach on open streets increases total mileage while reducing bottleneck delay, and caught Side A inflating its "a third or more" mode-shift figure into "not the majority" while repeating the refuted mileage claim.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (CON)): PRO sustained a convergent case: deadheading, substantial transit/walking/forgone-trip substitution, and attributed core slowdowns create pure added traffic where delay is costliest. CON exposed real gaps between mileage, pickups, and net delay, but its offsetting savings remained hypothetical and unquantified, and it could not name any city where ride-hail measurably improved congestion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 pairing rider surveys with rising vehicle-miles answered B's challenge that mileage alone cannot identify mode shift, preserving the pure-addition route despite B's strong parking-search counter on saturated streets.

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): A won the decisive comparison: “worse overall” is a claim about net delay versus the counterfactual, not about whether ride-hail was the largest cause.
