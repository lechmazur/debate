### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON built a clean frame early, "people, not places," and kept narrowing the debate around it. CON seized PRO's concession that counter-subsidies "slowed decline, not reversed it" and turned it into a burden: what does economic viability mean, and when would PRO admit transfers only delay the inevitable? PRO's answer was to redefine viability as service maintenance and enough population to function. That is defensible given the motion's "do more" wording, but CON exploited it effectively as a lowered bar. PRO's best line was that CON's own broadband and transport prescriptions are place-based. CON answered it with the purpose distinction ("same cable, a bridge out vs a fence in"), which at least neutralized the charge. PRO's attacks had specific weaknesses: - **Democratic dilution:** CON's overrepresentation rebuttal was largely unrebutted beyond assertion. - **Food and energy security:** CON's point that land-based production does not require dense towns blunted the security argument. - **Misdescribed concession:** PRO also misread CON's position, claiming CON "conceded" metro-skewed policy in A2.6 when A2.6 argued the opposite. PRO's closing relied on rhetoric ("surrender dressed as realism," "no off switch") more than on resolving the viability burden. CON's progression was tighter and easier to vote on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange began with CON's A2.1–A2.2 use of PRO's "slowed decline, not reversed it" concession to set a viability burden, which CON pressed in Q1. PRO's A1 redefined viability as maintaining services. CON's A4.5–A4.6 then framed that answer as lowering the bar from economic viability to "not collapsing." PRO never fully recovered from this exchange.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B won a close, well-matched debate by keeping one decisive clash alive across every speech: Side A conceded counter-subsidies "slowed decline, not reversed it," and the motion's own words demand "economically viable," not slower attrition. B pressed this with a clear standard demand, answered A's pressure questions crisply (universal services follow residents; purpose distinguishes infrastructure spending), and produced memorable framing ("a ward, not an economy," "same cable, a bridge out for us, a fence in for them"). Side A mounted a real counter with the "rigged standard" argument — metros also depend on continuous federal support — and the "no off switch" charge against CON's externalities management, but never resolved the viability definition to B's satisfaction and offered no concrete example of place-based investment achieving viability, only slowed decline. Both sides followed format and showed disciplined grounding; B's tighter burden-setting and sharper execution give a clear but competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 [B4.1-B4.10], directly answering A's pressure questions and then landing the "that is maintenance, not economic viability" reframing of A's own viability definition [A4.1], which B crystallized in the closing as "every patient in hospice is thriving."
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (CON)): Side B controlled the central burden of the motion by separating aid to rural people from subsidies to uneconomic places and showing—through Italian, Japanese, and EU regional programs—that place-based transfers fail to overcome agglomeration economics. Once Side A conceded that past transfers merely slowed decline rather than reversing it [A2.3] and redefined viability as keeping schools and hospitals open [A4.1], Side B convincingly exposed Side A's case as permanent service maintenance rather than true economic viability. Side A generated real pressure regarding residual infrastructure costs in depopulated counties [A3.1] and local labor for land-based industries [A4.4], but weakened its late speeches by leaving Side B's agricultural and energy subsidy rebuttal unanswered and misdescribing Side B's positions on urban housing, metro policy skew, and place-based grants [A2.6, A4.3, A5.2].
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B seized on Side A's concession in [A2.3] that counter-subsidies only slowed decline, used Q1 [B3.1] to force Side A to define observable viability, and then dismantled Side A's [A4.1] answer in [B4.5]-[B4.6] by showing that funding hospitals and schools via ongoing transfers is baseline service maintenance for a dependent ward, not an economically viable town.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the clearer causal story: rural decline as a feedback loop, prevention distinguished from post-collapse salvage, and dispersed capacity as resilience insurance for functions the whole economy relies on. PRO adapted across rounds, narrowing the dispute to a choice between maintenance before collapse and transition after hollowing out. CON (Side A) made a legitimate fiscal opportunity-cost case and asked a strong pressure question about who picks winners. PRO answered it adequately, if somewhat generally, with formula-based viability criteria. CON then kept attacking "keep every county seat alive," a position PRO had repeatedly disclaimed. CON's claim that metro externalities are "already internalised" was exposed, and its recovery, arguing that metro subsidy need justifies further concentration, was strained. CON's automation and rotating-crew answer was decent but did not fully neutralize the physical-labor and resilience point. CON also leaned on unsupported assertions such as interventions having "repeatedly proven fleeting" and the "evidence is clear" fiscal return. PRO's answer to the evidence question was partly a reframe, judging success by delay or avoided closure rather than reversal, which leaves some burden unmet. Both sides cited nonexistent reference IDs, so neither gains an edge there. Overall PRO had the stronger live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1/Q2 pressure, together with B4.4, was the most decisive exchange. It challenged CON's claim that congestion costs are "internalised" by pointing to metros' ongoing housing and transit subsidies. It also showed that "automated monitoring" can watch a grid but not repair it. CON's reply that metro subsidies prove its own point was clever but left the core fiscal ledger looking assumed rather than demonstrated, and PRO capitalized on this in its closing.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A (PRO) won on engagement and clash resolution. A answered B's pressure questions directly with the required A1/A2 format, giving a concrete, formula-based answer to the "who picks winners" challenge and reframing the Youngstown dispute as a circularity problem (judging prevention by the failure of post-collapse salvage). A's pressure questions exposed real burdens—uninternalized metro externalities and the physical-labor limits of "automated monitoring"—and the closing crystallized a clean maintenance-vs-transition comparison. B's answers to those pressures were partially effective (rural co-ops, telemedicine, mechanized agriculture were good grounding), but B's A1 flip ("metro subsidies prove our point") was strained, B's central fiscal-calculus claim was asserted repeatedly rather than developed, and B's later speeches recycled the lottery/Youngstown framing instead of engaging A's criteria answer. Both sides used bogus reference IDs ([C2.3, P2.4] from A; [C1.4, C4.5] from B), a minor shared cost. B was competent but never decisively neutralized A's preventive-investment or resilience routes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's exposure of the circularity in B's Youngstown evidence (A2.4, extended in A4.3) combined with B's inability to show preventive interventions fail, plus A's pointed Q2 on who physically repairs grids and harvests food during shocks, which forced B into the rotating-crews/automation answer that A then blunted in closing.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Step 5 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won solidly through superior adaptation, sharper rebuttals, and a much cleaner comparative closing. Side B offered a coherent urban-economics alternative centered on agglomeration returns and person-based mobility, and asked tough pressure questions on town selection and outmigration. However, Side A directly answered those burdens in Rebuttal 2—defining formula-based catchment criteria for a viability floor and distinguishing threshold maintenance from post-collapse salvage—while Side B ignored those answers entirely and reverted to strawmanning in Closing ("a town of a few hundred"). Side A also exposed Side B's overclaim that metros already internalize congestion and housing costs, and convincingly showed that distributed rural functions cannot be sustained if resident communities are hollowed out.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's sustained takedown of Side B's "production without habitation" claim ([B2.2] to [A3.2] to [B4.2] to [A5.1]): after Side A's pressure question exposed that "automated monitoring" cannot physically repair grids, harvest food, or provide elder care during a shock, Side B pivoted to rotating utility crews and telemedicine, which Side A cleanly neutralized as fair-weather supplements rather than substitutes when storms or corridor closures hit.
