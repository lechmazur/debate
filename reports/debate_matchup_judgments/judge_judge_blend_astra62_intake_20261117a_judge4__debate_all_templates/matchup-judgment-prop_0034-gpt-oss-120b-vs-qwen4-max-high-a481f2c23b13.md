### Judge Notes

- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing a live internal contradiction in Side B's case: B repeatedly defended the motion only by importing protections not in its text — a fair-use expressive carve-out, "informed, specific, revocable" consent, and an asserted labor-law specificity doctrine — while the motion says simply "affirmative consent." A's pressure questions forced B to choose between a parody veto and conceding the bright line, and B's A1 answered "No" but then conceded fair use "would still allow expressive use without a licence," effectively collapsing the gate B promised. B's closing claim that dubbing and documentaries "rely on original recordings, not AI-generated clones" was factually weak (a synthesized dub is exactly an AI-generated clone) and A hammered it. B's Q1 also misdescribed A's argument. B's strongest material — the platform-liability gate shifting responsibility to distributors, and the evidentiary hook — was never fully neutralized, but A's response that provenance is not truth and that the willing rather than the deceptive are filtered did real damage. A's alternative (punish deception and unauthorized endorsement precisely) gave a clean comparative endpoint.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1–A4.4: A showed that every case A named was synthetic, so B's AI/original distinction cut against B, and that B's fair-use concession meant the "bright line" was now a clip-by-clit permission regime — the exact nuance B said platforms cannot handle. A then showed B was defending a rewritten motion ("informed, specific, revocable consent, backed by a labor-law specificity doctrine. None of that appears in the motion"), which was the decisive blow.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Qwen 4 Max (high) (CON); B = GPT-OSS-120B (PRO)): Side A built the stronger case against the motion as written, showing that commercial distribution includes monetized satire, documentaries, and synthetic dubbing, and that consent does not itself prevent deception or guarantee workers bargaining power.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal decisively answered both pressure questions: it exposed Side B’s synthetic-parody answer as a concession that the supposed bright line depends on case-by-case fair-use litigation, then showed that Side B had to invent additional consent and labor-law safeguards to answer the worker-coercion objection.
