### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides were disciplined and well grounded, with no fake precision. PRO built a clean externality case and engaged the motion's "even when" clause directly. It answered CON's fairness objection with protections that operate before charging begins. In Rebuttal 2, it specified a pre-launch credit rule with published criteria and independent appeals, which answered CON's Q2 concretely. CON's legitimacy argument was sophisticated. Its strongest version was that strong opposition signals the preconditions are missing: alternatives, working relief, and trust. PRO's Q1 then forced a revealing concession. CON admitted that opposition still blocks the fee even after those preconditions are met. That broke the evidentiary link CON had leaned on and left a pure consent requirement. PRO fairly framed this as the veto CON had denied defending. CON kept one partially live route that PRO underanswered: bus lanes, curb rules, and freight and parking measures can attack delay without a general fee. CON's point that officials "judge their own permission" also had some bite. On balance, PRO made the decisive clash clearer and resolved it in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was PRO's Q1 in [B3.1], asking whether CON would still block pricing once its own listed conditions were met. CON answered in [A4.1] that it would. PRO's closing in [B5.1] turned that answer into its central point: CON's objection survives even when its practical demands are satisfied, so opposition becomes a self-justifying veto.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side A won a clear, competitive debate by exposing the circularity in Side B's legitimacy standard and winning the comparative weighing of status-quo costs. Side B mounted a sophisticated institutional critique regarding constrained travelers and the classification friction of targeted exemptions, but Side A convincingly showed that shifting marginal flexible trips helps essential drivers and bus riders alike while proposing pre-billing credits and independent appeals to manage hardship. Decisively, Side A forced Side B to concede in [B4.1] that strong opposition blocks pricing even after viable transit alternatives, working relief, and transparent spending are established—demonstrating that Side B's framework grants motorists a substantive veto while ignoring the unconsented delays and pollution already imposed on everyone else.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Opus 5.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO)): This was a close, high-quality exchange, and CON wins narrowly. **PRO's strengths.** PRO built a coherent framework: - roads as a commons; - intensity of opposition is not the same as representativeness; - pricing protects and funds transit. PRO also produced memorable lines, such as "a charge can be rebated; missed shifts cannot." It answered the hardship pressure honestly and directly with A1/A2. **What PRO conceded.** Those answers carried costs. PRO agreed that unprotected low-paid drivers must be covered before any charge. It also agreed that if traffic management alone suffices, no charge is needed. **How CON used the concessions.** CON converted these concessions into its main line: an unequal evidentiary standard. Under that standard, pricing gets credit for a plausible mechanism, while CON's alternatives must show observed success. CON also corrected PRO's baseline repeatedly. Its alternative was active bus priority, enforcement, and better connections, not "doing nothing." **Where PRO fell short.** PRO's closing still leaned heavily on the "unpriced queue" framing. Its two counters to the alternative route were real but underdeveloped: - "unresolved uncertainty cannot protect the status quo"; - "free demand presses back into lanes." CON had already said enforced bus lanes stop general traffic from consuming them. PRO never really engaged that point. **Why CON edges it.** CON conceded that opposition is no veto, which weakens its hold on the "even when drivers oppose" part of the motion. Even so, it shifted the remaining burden to justifying the fee itself, and PRO did not fully discharge that burden by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.5/A5.2 exposed an asymmetry in PRO's own A2 answer. PRO demanded observed proof that traffic management works, while defending pricing on mechanism and promised safeguards. PRO's closing reply, that uncertainty cannot protect the status quo, only partly neutralized this point.
- **Gemini 4 Carbon (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Both debaters showed strong epistemic discipline and crisp prose, with Side A building a compelling commons-and-externalities case and landing a sharp turn that fees can be rebated while missed shifts and ambulance delays cannot ([A2.1]-[A2.2]). However, Side B won the back half of the debate decisively. Using surgical Pressure Questions ([B3.1]-[B3.2]), Side B drew two critical concessions: that low-paid workers lacking usable transit must be protected before charging ([A4.1]), and that no fee is needed if bus priority and traffic management keep queues down ([A4.3], [A5.2]). Side B then dismantled Side A's vehicle-count trap ([B4.3]), refuted Side A's repeated mischaracterization of active non-pricing management as "doing nothing" ([B4.6], [B5.1]), and exposed an unanswered double standard in which PRO demanded empirical proof from traffic management while granting congestion pricing a presumption based on theoretical mechanisms and proposed safeguards ([B4.5], [B5.2]-[B5.5]).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In [B4.3]-[B4.6] (answering [A4.3]-[A4.6]), Side B dismantled Side A's claim that assessing workable alternatives before charging ran into CON's own critique of raw vehicle counts and meant "doing nothing." Side B pointed out that route coverage, operating hours, and pilot bus speeds test alternatives directly without a toll or raw car counts, while flipping Side A's [A4.3] concession to show PRO required observed proof from active street management yet granted pricing an unearned presumption.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): A won narrowly by making the status quo a non-neutral baseline and converting CON’s fairness pressure into pre-launch exemptions, rebates, and appeals. That let PRO defend pricing as corrigible while portraying CON’s sequence as delaying relief from real congestion harms. B remained strong on equity and exposed PRO’s evidence asymmetry, but never fully neutralized the core PRO claim that dense central districts need demand management, not only better street management, when opposition is strong.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was A’s rebuttal after pressure questions: A directly answered B’s low-worker question by accepting pre-charge protection, then turned B’s evidence demand by arguing that CON’s pre-proof requirement risks preserving the hidden costs of the unpriced queue indefinitely.
