### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the cleaner path to victory. The opening correctly framed Japan and South Korea as import-dependent island economies needing firm, low-carbon baseload power, and PRO defended restart and expansion without abandoning either half. CON’s concentration-risk and timeline attacks were strong and made the debate close, but PRO neutralized the timeline pressure by naming reactors connectable before 2035 and exposed a critical gap in CON’s alternative: distributed renewables cannot supply 24/7 industrial power without fossil backup. CON’s best line—that fuel beside an offline reactor is a warehouse—was sharp, but it did not outweigh the asymmetry PRO drew between manageable domestic engineering and unavoidable weather/import dependence. PRO also answered pressure questions directly, while CON partly reframed the blockade question rather than supplying firm zero-emission capacity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s decisive exchange was turning CON’s own security framing around: short-duration batteries and distributed wind/solar still leave imported coal or gas as the backup when calm weather settles, whereas multi-year uranium stockpiles insulate normal peacetime fuel shocks. CON’s “warehouse, not a grid” reply was strong, but it did not fill the 24/7 firm-power gap PRO exposed.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A won the decisive clashes on concentrated fleet-wide failure and on expansion timing. It showed stockpiled fuel does not help offline reactors, that new coastal output still faces congestion, and that B's pre-2035 reactors were long-delayed pre-existing projects rather than results of new expansion. B's intermittency and LNG-stockpile arguments remained strong, but were outweighed by A's comparison of failures lasting days versus fleet outages lasting years.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 was most decisive: it answered the near-demand question with Yellow Sea generation, turned Saeul against B on transmission congestion, and reframed B's named reactors as proof that nothing newly approved arrives before the mid-2030s.
- **Qwen 3.8 Max 0902** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): B won because it tied restarts to immediate emissions and energy-security gains, gave plausible answers on fuel stockpiles, dry-cask waste, and near-term reactors, and kept pressure on CON’s firm-power alternative. A’s concentration-risk, delay, and grid arguments were strong and memorable, but they never fully neutralized PRO’s core claim that restarted, stocked reactors reduce dependence on volatile fossil imports better than an under-specified renewables mix.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal flipped A’s Fukushima point by arguing that idling reactors caused the fossil-import shock PRO seeks to avoid, and B’s later named-reactor answer kept expansion alive even though A successfully cast those projects as legacy rather than fresh expansion.
