- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): A wins because CON's detection-first framing carried the comparison: warning time is the binding constraint, and PRO's own fallback was a ground-stored bus awaiting a payload chosen after recon, which undercuts the need for a permanently deployed kinetic fleet. CON defused the contradiction charge by distinguishing common long-lead components from target-specific payloads and repeatedly weighed risk per dollar toward surveys, with the 'telescopes create time; parked spacecraft spend it' closer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's [A4.1]-[A4.2]: it answered the contradiction pressure by separating common parts from recon-dependent choices and turned PRO's nuclear-capable ground-stored bus into a slower, less 'deployed' version of CON's own readiness model.
- **GLM-5.3 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): B won by converting the debate into a clean risk-per-dollar comparison and then showing that A's own answers collapsed into B's model. A's strongest route was the "assembly gap" — integrated flight-ready spacecraft save two years that unassembled parts lose. But under pressure, A's own A1 answer (a ground-stored bus awaiting a nuclear payload chosen after recon) is functionally the research-led readiness model B advocated: finished designs plus pre-ordered long-lead parts, completed once the target is characterized. B landed this directly ("that is my model with a grander label") and paired it with the weighing that detection buys decades of warning across all threat slices while standby hardware buys at most the two-year gap for a narrow band of threats. A's counter that surveys can't guarantee long warning (eccentric objects, long-period comets) was blunted by B noting comets are exactly where generic impactors are weakest, forcing A's own turn to nuclear payloads. A scored real points early — the "true warning time equals calendar minus build time" reframe and the contradiction charge on parallel builds — but B answered the contradiction cleanly in B4.1 and narrowed the dispute on B's terms. Both sides showed good epistemic discipline with plausible background knowledge and no fake citations. B's closings were sharper and more crystallizing, ending with the memorable "telescopes create time; parked spacecraft only spend it."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.2: B's observation that A's pressure-question answer — a ground-stored bus waiting for a payload chosen after recon — "is my model with a grander label" was the decisive blow, because it converted A's entire deployed-infrastructure case into a relabeling of the research-led readiness B defended.

The margin remains competitive because B overstated nuclear readiness, timing certainty, and its claim that dedicated funding displaces no survey spending.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest reply was that switching to nuclear does not solve delivery readiness: the payload still needs a tested spacecraft bus.
