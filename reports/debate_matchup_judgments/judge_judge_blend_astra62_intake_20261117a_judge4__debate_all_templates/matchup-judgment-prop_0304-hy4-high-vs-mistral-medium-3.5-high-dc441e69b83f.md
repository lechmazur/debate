### Judge Notes

- **DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): B built the cleaner path to victory. A's core empirical point about wealthy overrepresentation was real, but B successfully reframed it as composition rather than causation and made the marginal counterfactual central: a wealthy reject usually keeps class position, while a low-income admit gains high-leverage access. More decisively, B exploited A's own admission that no feasible reform could falsify the inheritance label. That made A's policy frame unfalsifiable and unable to supply benchmarks, breach standards, or enforcement mechanisms. B also reclaimed A's proposed reforms—legacy bans, aid, socioeconomic conditions—as public-benefit duties generated by the fairness framework, not the inheritance label. A offered urgency but little policy machinery, and its closing largely repeated the failed-fairness claim rather than resolving the enforceability clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 turned A's "No feasible reform can falsify the inheritance-by-design claim" into the decisive liability: a label that survives every change in rules and composition cannot diagnose a breach, set a benchmark, or tell policy what to do. B's surgery/tumor and unenforced-speed-limit analogies made the rebuttal concrete and damaging.
- **Kimi K3.1 Pro (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A built the stronger comparative case by distinguishing unequal intake from institutional causation, using a per-seat counterfactual, and showing that enforceable fairness duties better support both sides’ proposed reforms. B’s selection-and-socialization case remained plausible, and A never fully proved that high-leverage mobility numerically outweighs reproduction, but B mostly repeated descriptive claims and asserted that only the inheritance label creates urgency. B’s concession that no feasible reform could falsify that label seriously damaged its causal and policy route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer to B’s concession that “no feasible reform can falsify” inheritance-by-design was decisive: A exposed the claim as unfalsifiable, then showed that legacy bans, funding conditions, and diversity benchmarks require an auditable public-duty standard rather than merely an inheritance label.
- **Muse Wonder 1.0 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by distinguishing composition from marginal causal effect and by showing reproduction is contingent on severable admissions rules, not institutional essence. Side A largely repeated overrepresentation claims without answering that a denied affluent applicant usually retains class position while an aided low-income admit gains transformative access. B also showed A's radical remedies presuppose enforceable fairness duties rather than the inheritance label.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's falsifiability pressure question was decisive: A conceded no feasible reform could falsify inheritance-by-design, and B effectively reframed that concession in Rebuttal 2 and the closing as proof the label is unfalsifiable, unenforceable, and inferior to auditable public-benefit duties.
