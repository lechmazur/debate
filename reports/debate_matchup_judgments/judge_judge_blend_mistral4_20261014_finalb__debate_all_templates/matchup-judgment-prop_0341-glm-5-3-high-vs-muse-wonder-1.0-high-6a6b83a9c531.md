### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side A won by grounding the comparison in the motion’s express privacy-preserving condition and showing a plausible mechanism: blind proof of age, data minimization, and meaningful friction against easy child access. Side B identified real risks involving enrollment, breaches, device sharing, and mission creep, but too often attacked a noncompliant centralized implementation rather than the stipulated policy. Its late alternatives only partly addressed gambling and did not comparably solve direct access to pornography. A therefore established that imperfect protection can still justify a limited adult burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.3 was decisive: A answered B’s procurement challenge by noting that privacy preservation is part of the proposition, then exposed B’s tendency to require near-perfect effectiveness while treating any conceivable privacy failure as disqualifying. B’s response preserved an objection to compelled identity infrastructure but did not defeat A’s compliant decentralized model.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively answering B's pressure questions with concrete, non-intrusive alternatives (payment rails and device-level controls) that Side B completely ignored in its closing. Side A also successfully framed B's defense as reliant on an idealized, unbuildable technical architecture.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 perfectly answered B's Pressure Question 2 by offering targeted alternatives, neutralizing B's core accusation that A was defending the click-through status quo.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): CON won by showing that PRO's case rested on an idealized architectural white paper rather than the real mandate the motion authorizes, and by introducing targeted alternatives (payment-rail enforcement, default device controls) that protect children without universal adult enrollment. PRO's closing never engaged these alternatives, instead mischaracterizing CON as defending the status-quo click-through. CON maintained a consistent framework: certain, population-scale intrusion versus speculative protection, with less restrictive means available. The decisive clash was whether "privacy-preserving" is a guaranteed property of a real mandate; CON demonstrated it is an aspiration that leaves compulsory enrollment intact, while PRO never proved that infrastructure is necessary compared to CON's targeted friction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4's introduction of concrete alternatives (payment rails, device-level controls) and reversal of the perfection double-standard, which PRO's closing left unanswered.
