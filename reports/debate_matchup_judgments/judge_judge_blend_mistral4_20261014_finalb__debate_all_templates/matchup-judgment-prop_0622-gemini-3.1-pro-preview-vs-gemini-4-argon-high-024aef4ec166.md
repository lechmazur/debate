### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. B persuasively identified infrastructure bottlenecks, innovation risk, and global learning benefits, but never fully answered A’s economy-wide comparison: subsidies selectively cheapen favored technologies without consistently penalizing emissions across existing plants, industrial processes, and low-cost efficiency opportunities. A also won the distributional clash because equal dividends offered a concrete broad benefit, while B’s claim that subsidies fund themselves through jobs and growth did not adequately answer who bears their fiscal cost. A’s repeated malformed reference IDs were an execution flaw, but not enough to erase its stronger comparative mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange concerned B’s claim that carbon pricing must be immediately astronomical. A answered that long-lived investments respond today to a predictable rising price path over the asset’s lifetime. B repeated that gradual pricing was too slow, but did not establish why expected future liabilities would fail to redirect current capital.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the decisive clash on subsidies' two failures—no direct penalty on ongoing pollution and regressive public financing of corporate handouts—while showing equal dividends deliver net gains to working families and a predictable rising carbon path still mobilizes long-horizon private capital. B landed real supply-side, valley-of-death, and learning-curve points but never fully neutralized free pollution or the distribution problem, so A's path remained cleaner to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2: progressive dividends leave renters ahead while factory subsidies give them nothing, paired with the continuing free-pollution failure of cheap solar.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): B built the cleaner path: pricing pollution penalizes existing paid-off coal/gas and all industrial emissions where subsidies only cheapen selected green products, an escalating predictable price pulls 30-year private capital without instant shock, and equal dividends make low/middle-income net winners from day one while CON's subsidy funding via taxes/inflation is regressive and narrowly targeted. B answered pressure on shock and renter equity more directly; A never solved the no-penalty problem or funding resentment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 A2 escalating-price answer plus the P1-Q1 point that subsidies leave pollution free - even cheap solar doesn't retire paid-off fossil plants - decisively undercut CON's valley-of-death / cheaper-than-marginal-cost claim.
