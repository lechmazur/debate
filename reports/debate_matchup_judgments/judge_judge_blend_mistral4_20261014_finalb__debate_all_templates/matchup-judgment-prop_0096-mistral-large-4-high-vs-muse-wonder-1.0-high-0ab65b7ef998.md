### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): The debate turned on whether the post-2023 "AI rider" inside standard contracts is functionally equivalent to opt-in. B pressed this directly with Q1 in Pressure Questions, asking whether a performer who refuses the AI clause can still be hired. A's answer (A4.1) was circular and never squarely said yes or no, effectively conceding the coercion point by evasion. B, by contrast, answered A's pressure questions cleanly (A1/A2 format followed precisely), drawing a crisp distinction between licensing a performance (translation, human dubbing) and licensing a reusable biometric model for future reuse (sequels, games, rewritten lines), and explained the "dignitary injury" as fabricated public performance rather than mere discomfort. A's strongest material — accessibility delays, forty-language paralysis, archival documentaries — was substantially defused once B showed human dubbing and subtitles continue unchanged under opt-in, leaving A's catastrophizing largely answered. A's closing was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, an execution cost that blunted its final synthesis, while B's closing crystallized the central separability test cleanly. Overall B built the more coherent, pressure-tested case and left fewer live routes for A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to A's own pressure question on sequels/opt-in scope (B4.1), combined with A's evasive non-answer to B's Q1 about whether refusing the AI rider costs the job (A4.1), was the decisive exchange: it exposed that A's "consent mechanism" could not clearly survive the separability test that B had set up as the core of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by effectively trapping Side B into conceding the core premise of the resolution. By forcing Side B to define their "better boilerplate" as a system where actors give a "separate affirmative yes" and can refuse cloning without losing the job, Side A successfully demonstrated that Side B was just arguing for opt-in under a different name. Side B also contradicted itself by conceding that opt-in is a single upfront 'yes', but later repeating the debunked claim that it would cause '40 holdouts for 40 languages'.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Q1 and Rebuttal 2 (A4.3) decisively reframed Side B's defense of the 2023 union contracts, proving that if a performer can say no to cloning and keep the job, the system is functionally opt-in, collapsing Side B's entire position.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B maintained a cleaner route to victory by grounding the debate in a crisp, memorable test: a performer must be able to refuse voice cloning and still be hired, or the "consent" is merely a condition of employment. Side A never squarely met this burden—its answers wavered between saying the AI rider was strikable (which would be opt-in by another name) and saying an internal "specific yes" within non-strikable boilerplate was sufficient, without showing that current industry practice actually allows performers to refuse the clause without consequence. A’s closing was clipped, leaving its final crystallization unfinished, while B’s closing elegantly sharpened the stakes. A’s derivative-use and paralysis arguments had force, but B neutralized the permission-minefield fear by showing human dubbing remains available, and B’s biometric-dignity framing gave the audience a concrete reason to prefer separable opt-in over bundled riders.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response to Side A’s pressure questions in Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where B cleanly distinguished sequels and new uses as requiring fresh opt-ins, clarified that opt-in is not a project veto but a boundary on reanimating a performer’s voice beyond what was sold, and then turned the exchange back to expose that bundled boilerplate is only meaningful if the performer can strike the rider without losing the job.
