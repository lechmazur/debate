### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built sophisticated, well-structured cases, but the pressure-question exchange proved pivotal. PRO forced CON to admit that no World Cup has ever been relocated for rights failures, which directly undercut CON's "leverage" mechanism. CON tried to recast this as "daily leverage" via reputational stakes, but PRO's closing ("scrutiny without consequence is not leverage... it is observation the host can price in") exploited that concession more decisively than CON could repair it. PRO also answered CON's "unfalsifiable sportswashing" charge with a genuinely falsifiable test (enforceable rights that survive after the cameras leave), neutralizing one of CON's sharpest attacks. CON's Qatar precedent and "decade-long tenancy" framing were strong and kept the debate close, and its rhetorical framing (witness stand, silence vs. engagement) was vivid, but it leaned on unestablished specifics (sponsor exit clauses, auditor access) that were never shown to exist in the actual deal, weakening its grounding at the critical moment. On balance PRO narrowed the clash more effectively and left CON's central leverage claim more exposed by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question forcing CON to concede FIFA would not actually relocate the tournament, followed by PRO's closing line that "scrutiny without consequence is not leverage," which CON's "daily pressure" reframe never fully answered.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by focusing the debate on the actual, unconditional award rather than engagement in principle. Its admission that FIFA would not relocate the tournament even after severe continuing abuses substantially weakened the claim that Saudi Arabia had something decisive to lose. Side B offered the cleaner comparison: pressure while the prize remained conditional versus scrutiny after FIFA and commercial partners became invested in protecting the event.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest exchange was its response that prestige cuts both ways: once FIFA, sponsors, and broadcasters invest in 2034, they gain incentives to minimize scandal and celebrate cosmetic progress. Side A’s admission that FIFA would not move the tournament reinforced rather than answered that critique.
