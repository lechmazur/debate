### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): The debate turned on whether PRO's "conditional patience" is distinguishable from CON's feared "quiet acceptance" that unanchors expectations. PRO's Q2 pressure question ("what would establish causing a recession now is less damaging than gradual restraint?") was the most incisive of the debate, and CON's answer in Rebuttal 2 largely restated priors (non-linear costs, "cannot await proof beyond doubt") without supplying the comparative proof PRO demanded. PRO's closing crystallized this cleanly: CON's test for accepting recession is evidence of non-convergence, which is just PRO's policy rule, not a case for the "recession premium." CON's rebuttals were vivid and well-argued on anchoring psychology, but never closed the loop on why anticipatory recession beats anticipatory tightening-without-recession. PRO maintained the tighter logical structure and answered pressure more directly, narrowly winning the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 pressure question and CON's Rebuttal 2 response—CON conceded reversing entrenched expectations "may be costly" but never showed why causing recession preemptively is better than tightening further only if drift is confirmed, which is the crux PRO kept returning to.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully establishing a concrete causal mechanism for why "patience" inherently fails: annual contract renewals based on lived inflation mechanically entrench higher prices, regardless of long-term faith in a central bank's target.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal (A4.1 and A4.4) decisively dismantled Side B's "conditional path" by showing that economic actors setting next year's prices at 3% mechanically sustains inflation, and that waiting for clear evidence of a stall ensures the cure will be vastly more expensive.

Side A effectively argued that tolerating above-target inflation for "several years" mechanically embeds those higher rates into wage and price contracts, destroying the central bank's anchor through lived experience rather than official announcements. Side A's answers to the pressure questions and subsequent rebuttals sharply exposed that Side B's safeguards—waiting for expectations to visibly drift—would only trigger after the inflationary damage was already locked into the economy. This made Side A's case for proactive, even if painful, stabilization much stronger and more logically cohesive.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued competently with plausible mechanisms and no fabricated sourcing, but B built a more coherent and consistently defended case: recession scarring is irreversible, supply-driven disinflation works without demand destruction, policy lags counsel patience, and the distributional comparison (lost paycheck vs. slower real-wage catch-up) was pressed harder and answered A's reversal attempt. Critically, in the pressure-question exchange A conceded it would not tighten "regardless" and would wait for a forecast of persistent inflation despite falling headline numbers and anchored expectations — essentially endorsing B's conditional-tolerance framework. B's closing explicitly seized on this concession ("once that case is conceded, the case for engineering a recession collapses"), sharpening the decisive clash in its favor. A's closing reasserted uncertainty about forecasts but never fully escaped the trap that its own stated threshold converges toward B's position, leaving B with the cleaner, more decisive path to the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) answers, later leveraged in Closing B, where B used A's own concession ("I would not tighten regardless," conditional on forecasted persistence) to argue that under the scenario both sides agree matters most — falling inflation, anchored expectations, ongoing restrictive policy — A's case for engineered recession has no remaining footing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins a very close, high-level debate by systematically dismantling Side B's "asymmetry" argument. However, Side A decisively countered that due to policy lags and embedded inflation, waiting preserves the tool but increases the price of using it, meaning delay risks an even larger recession later.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response in Rebuttal 2 to B's asymmetry argument: "Waiting preserves the tool, not the price of using it." This brilliantly weaponized B's own points about policy lags against B's wait-and-see strategy.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a compelling asymmetry in the costs: the irreversible scarring of a deliberate recession versus the manageable, monitored risk of delayed disinflation. Side B effectively neutralized Side A's main concern about embedded inflation by clarifying they would tighten if progress stalled, leaving Side A to rely on the speculative risk that waiting makes the eventual cure more expensive. Side B's rhetorical framing of buying the calendar at the cost of permanent job losses was highly persuasive and decisively answered the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they answered Side A's pressure questions by clarifying that conditional tolerance includes clear triggers for further tightening if inflation stalls, effectively collapsing Side A's argument that tolerance means waiting until a crisis is undeniable.
