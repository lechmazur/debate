### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A opened with a clean, memorable "shadow bank" framing and the bank-run/contagion story, but the case stalled under pressure. B's key structural distinction — that fully-backed, short-duration T-bill reserves held in segregated custody are not the same as fractional-reserve maturity transformation — was never squarely answered; A kept asserting "holding bonds is maturity transformation" without engaging the liquidity/duration distinction B kept pressing. B also landed a sharp, specific counter-example (a premier stablecoin's peg break being caused by the failure of an already bank-regulated custodian bank), which directly undercuts A's claim that bank-style rules are "proactive" and preventive. In the pressure round, A's answers (endorsing MMF-style redemption gates and conceding that non-compliant decentralized protocols "must be prohibited") handed B exactly the ammunition it needed, and B's second rebuttal and closing converted those concessions into a tight, three-part "here's what voting for A actually gets you" indictment (bank fragility without the bailout backstop A had praised, redemption gates that accelerate runs, and a ban on protocols B says proved resilient in real crashes). A's closing mostly re-asserted the original thesis rather than meeting these specific charges, which cost it on progression and final crystallization. B's case was not flawless (some claims about decentralized stablecoin resilience are asserted rather than proven), but it adapted better round over round and ended with the clearer, more damaging comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's use of A's own pressure-question answers (endorsing redemption gates and banning non-compliant decentralized protocols, while earlier praising bailout-style backstops) to construct the "fatal contradiction" charge in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive exchange — A never directly neutralized it in closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash clearer: bank/MMF rules are a poor fit that import maturity-mismatch fragility, gates, and moral-hazard problems A cannot cleanly escape. A kept a coherent “economic function over packaging” line and pressured the payment-app and off-chain-portfolio points well, but B’s answers and later weighing used A’s concessions on gates, backstops, and banning autonomous protocols more effectively, turning the proposition’s specific comparison against A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3–B4.5 converting A’s A1/A2 answers (retreat from bailouts while endorsing gates and prohibiting resilient over-collateralized protocols) into a live contradiction against A’s earlier “guardrails” claim.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B won the functional-risk comparison. A framed stablecoins as shadow banks prone to runs and contagion, but B neutralized that with two live counters: the flagship de-peg was caused by a regulated bank's maturity-mismatch failure, not by T-bills, and importing bank/MMF tools creates worse incentives via taxpayer backstops and redemption gates that trigger pre-emptive runs. A's Rebuttal 2 concessions — endorsing gates [A4.1] and prohibiting autonomous over-collateralized protocols [A4.2] — let B cleanly flip the systemic-risk claim and show software + fraud law + continuous attestations addresses fully-backed models without legacy fragility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.3-B4.5 turning A4.1/A4.2 into a contradiction: A praised deposit insurance/liquidity facilities as essential then disavowed bailouts, embraced maturity mismatch as inherent while celebrating gates that freeze payments, and admitted banning resilient over-collateralized code that held its peg.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A built a tighter, more coherent case around economic function (par-redemption promise = run risk) and consistently exploited a genuine contradiction in B's position: every time B tried to show stablecoins are "just software," B smuggled in financial-regulatory tools (bankruptcy-remote trusts, 100% reserve mandates, regulated custodian banks, money-transmitter licenses) that are not features of ordinary software regulation. A's closing crystallized this cleanly by distinguishing "transparency" (mere disclosure) from "prudential restriction" (actual asset-composition limits), a distinction B never squarely answered. B's strongest counter—that literal MMF/bank regulation would impose securities-law broker-dealer onboarding on peer-to-peer transfers—was a real pressure point, but A defused it by clarifying the motion asks for regulation "more like" banks/MMFs in principle (asset quality, capital, audits at the issuer/mint-redeem level), not a wholesale legal transplant, which is a fair reading of the proposition as worded. Both sides handled the Pressure Questions format correctly with labeled A1/A2 answers, but A's answers did more actual work advancing its case, while B's answers (oracle-locked custody, contract-law-enforced reserves) relied on somewhat hand-wavy technical assurances that were never fully substantiated against A's fire-sale/run scenario.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1–A2.2 (showing B's "no fractional lending" distinction fails to separate stablecoins from money-market funds, and that B's own trust/100%-reserve proposals concede the need for financial-style prudential rules), reinforced in A5.2–A5.4 where A exposes that B's "truth-in-advertising" solution only mandates disclosure, not actual safe-asset composition, a point B's closing never rebutted.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the motion's comparative frame live: function over form. A showed fiat-backed stablecoins match the MMF economic model (par promise + reserve portfolio + run risk), forced B to smuggle in non-software tools (100% mandates, trusts, custodians, licenses), and answered pressure by separating issuer-level prudential rules from full securities onboarding. B's utility and category-error points were real but repeatedly diluted by conceding financial guardrails while denying the need for bank/MMF-grade rules; A's direct pressure replies and contradiction weighing decided the clash cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 and later pressure answers on MMF parity + the impossibility of pure software law forcing safe liquid reserves (while B's fraud/ToS and oracle replies left the asset-quality and fire-sale gaps open).
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO won the functional comparison: MMFs also hold 100% reserves without fractional lending, so CON's bank-only distinction fails. PRO showed trust law and API transparency alone cannot mandate safe liquid assets or stop runs, exposing that CON smuggles in financial guardrails while claiming software rules suffice. CON's friction argument was neutralized by PRO's "more like" reading limiting prudential rules to issuer balance sheet, leaving P2P settlement intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's first rebuttal collapsing CON's fractional-reserve defense by invoking money-market funds, and showing blockchains prove tokens not off-chain reserves – leaving CON's safety story dependent on the very financial regulation it rejects.
