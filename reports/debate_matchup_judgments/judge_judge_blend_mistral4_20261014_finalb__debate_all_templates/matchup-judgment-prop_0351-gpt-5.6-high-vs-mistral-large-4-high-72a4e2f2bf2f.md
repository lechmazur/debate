### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): The decisive clash was whether loud discussion of remote-junior-career risk counts as "admission" under the motion. CON's strongest move was reframing the debate around this empirical point ("damage is not hidden, it is invented"), but PRO answered it cleanly and sustained the answer across rounds: salience (mandates, anxious commentary) is not the same as accurate calibration, since firms still measure remote success mainly through output/retention while leaving mentoring, sponsorship, and tacit judgment unmeasured. CON's rebuttal to this ("spending resources is pricing in the risk") never squarely engaged the calibration-vs-salience distinction PRO kept sharpening, and CON's pressure-question charge of "unfalsifiability" was a fair jab but left unresolved in CON's own follow-up, which likewise offered no outcome data. PRO's "documentation vs invisibility" line (recorded threads preserve what was chosen to be shared, not the meeting never attended or question never asked) was never directly rebutted by CON in Rebuttal 2, and PRO's distinction between recruitment access and in-firm progression similarly went unanswered. CON's opening reframe and rhetorical closers were strong, but the case stopped developing after Rebuttal 1, essentially repeating "discussion equals admission" through to the close, while PRO's framework visibly sharpened each round. Both sides suffered a clipped turn, roughly offsetting that cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response in Rebuttal 1/2 distinguishing "documentation as storage" from "development as unrealized interaction" (A2.5, A4.4) directly neutralized CON's central "digital artifacts beat tacit learning" argument, and CON never found an answer to the "never invited to the meeting" point.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by demonstrating that workplace anxiety and return-to-office mandates do not equal accurate calibration of remote work’s cumulative developmental cost. B’s equation of discussion with admission remained analytically crude, and B never overcame A’s distinction between explicit digital artifacts and the unarticulated, ambient interactions that build junior judgment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answer to B’s pressure questions distinguishing rhetorical awareness from operational calibration, paired with the observation that recorded meetings and searchable threads preserve only what someone deliberately shared and cannot replace the meeting a junior was never invited to, the misconception a manager never noticed, or the stretch assignment that never materialized.

While Side B argued that widespread HR anxiety and return-to-office mandates proved the harm was fully "admitted," Side A brilliantly distinguished between merely acknowledging a risk's existence and accurately pricing its cumulative, counterfactual cost. Side A's framing of "measurement asymmetry"—that remote work's benefits are legible and immediate while its developmental harms are invisible—provided a compelling, original explanation for why the damage is systematically understated. Substantively, Side A's rebuttal that digital artifacts "preserve what was deliberately shared" but miss "what never became an interaction" decisively defeated Side B's claim that explicit documentation replaces ambient learning.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the "digital explicitness" defense in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, arguing that searchable threads and recorded meetings only preserve what juniors already know to ask for, thereby erasing the crucial ambient learning of unrecognized needs and uninvited opportunities.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A built a vivid, emotionally resonant narrative around "ambient apprenticeship" and a "developmental tax," but its core claim rested on an under-admission mechanism that B correctly pressured as structurally unfalsifiable: good performance/satisfaction is read as ignorance, while hypothetical future data is treated as vindication. A's answers to this pressure (A4.1-A4.2) named plausible metrics (promotion velocity, sponsorship density) but never actually demonstrated a lag, leaving the charge largely unresolved. B's most damaging move—"presence is not access," arguing that office osmosis is itself overclaimed since real development (context, responsibility, sponsorship) always requires a senior's deliberate choice in either setting—directly attacked A's central metaphor and was never fully neutralized; A kept restating the asymmetry rather than defeating this specific counter. B also offered a genuinely original equity angle (disability, caregiving, geography) and reframed "intentionality" from cost to advantage, sharpening the comparative stakes. B did suffer a real execution cost from two clipped speeches (opening and rebuttal 2), losing its concluding lines, which tempers the margin, but what was visible consistently did more direct, comparative damage to A's live route to victory than A did to B's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's "presence is not access" critique (B2.4, B4.1, B5.1) directly dismantled A's core "ambient apprenticeship" mechanism, and A's response never moved beyond reasserting the asymmetry rather than refuting the claim that office proximity without deliberate inclusion is just as empty as remote silence.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a durable causal mechanism: remote work eliminates the ambient apprenticeship baseline that even mediocre offices provide, replacing passive exposure with intentional inclusion that defaults to silence because busy seniors do not initiate and juniors do not know to ask. Side B landed sharp challenges—unfalsifiability, presence-is-not-access, and remote equity—but never squarely answered why the structural coordination tax does not produce net harm under the modal employer. Side A turned Side B’s own management argument against it, showing that remote raises the cost of every developmental substitute. Side A’s pressure questions exposed this gap, and its closing crystallized the default asymmetry effectively. Side B’s replies were plausible but more defensive, and clipped endings imposed a modest execution cost without resolving the live clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A in Rebuttal 1 (A2.5–A2.6) turning Side B’s concession that career development depends on structured mentorship, feedback, and stretch assignments into a demonstration that remote work raises coordination costs for each element, making the default outcome worse under typically mediocre management.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A built a highly cohesive and persuasive case around the "ambient apprenticeship" of the office versus the "coordination tax" of remote work. Side B offered strong epistemic critiques regarding unfalsifiability and the idealization of the office, but struggled to overcome A's asymmetry argument about mediocre management. A's rhetoric was also sharper and more memorable, and B suffered minor execution costs from clipped turns.
