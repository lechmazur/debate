### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): B ran a tighter, more disciplined case: zero foreign pricing under subsidies versus imperfect-but-real foreign pricing under a CBAM, and it kept returning to that clean comparative frame through rebuttals, pressure answers, and closing. A's strongest line—that Chinese (and other) producers can simply redirect unsold-to-US output to growing, carbon-unpriced markets like India or Africa, so global emissions are unchanged—was a genuinely dangerous objection that B never squarely answered (B kept conflating "who supplies the US" with "what happens to output that never goes to the US"). However, A undercut its own credibility by introducing suspiciously precise, uncited statistics (90 million tons of steel, <10% to the US, 7%/5% growth rates) in a rebuttal round where only narrow clarifying support was allowed, which is exactly the brittle fake-precision the rules penalize. A also had two major speeches (Opening and Rebuttal 1) cut off mid-argument, a real execution cost that weakened the development of its best points. B's pressure questions were sharper and better exposed the "subsidies still leave 100% of import-embedded carbon unpriced" concession, which A never meaningfully escaped. On balance, B's cleaner execution, consistent framing, and fewer grounding violations outweigh A's unresolved but imperfectly delivered leakage argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's repeated "zero pricing vs. imperfect pricing" framing in Rebuttal 2, especially A2's answer that subsidies deliver "absolutely no cost to embedded carbon in imports," was the most decisive moment, since A never found a way to make subsidies touch foreign production at all, even as A's own "redirect to other markets" critique against CBAM went only partially answered.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B maintained a consistent, highly comparative case that a Carbon Border Adjustment structurally prevents carbon leakage by pricing all imports, whereas subsidies leave foreign emissions untouched. Side A completely malfunctioned in Rebuttal 2, adopting Side B's arguments verbatim to praise the CBA and attack subsidies, effectively conceding the entire debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7) decisively countered the 'trade shuffling' argument by pointing out that any replacement supplier would still face the same carbon tariff at the port of entry.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 3 (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing the decisive "zero versus any" pricing frame: only a border adjustment attaches a cost to embedded carbon in imports, while domestic subsidies guarantee carbon leakage by leaving that channel unpriced. Side A never recovered from its catastrophic Rebuttal 2, where it accidentally repeated B's pro-adjustment arguments—effectively conceding the core clash on supply-chain shuffling and the universality of the border price. Even before that error, B had cleanly answered A's concerns about rerouting and gaming by showing the tariff applies regardless of origin and that imperfect pricing still beats no pricing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1) demonstrating that shuffling suppliers does not evade the border adjustment because every import faces the same carbon price at the port of entry—a point Side A then inexplicably conceded by repeating it verbatim in its own Rebuttal 2.
