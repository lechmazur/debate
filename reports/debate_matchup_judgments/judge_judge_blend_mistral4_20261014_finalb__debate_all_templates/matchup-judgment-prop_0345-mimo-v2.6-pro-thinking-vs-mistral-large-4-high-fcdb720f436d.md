### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the unresolved enforcement dilemma. If generic cultural language satisfies the duty, the reform is cosmetic; if reasons must pass substantive review, Side B creates a much broader adjudicative regime than existing discrimination law. Side B compellingly established worker harms and the value of transparency, but never showed how a written justification prevents harm before a slow challenge, and overstated the extent to which existing legal standards already supply the proposed universal duty. Side A’s two clipped turns count against execution but did not erase its stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange was its response to Side B’s arbiter answer: existing tribunals do not eliminate the new regime because current justification duties arise from defined disputes, while the proposal applies prospectively across every covered mandate and role. Side B’s embrace of courts confirmed enforcement costs without answering how review would be timely.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): Side B systematically dismantled Side A's case by trapping them in two fatal contradictions. First, B forced A to concede that courts and tribunals would be the arbiters of the new rule, which B successfully argued would recreate the exact same slow, unequal process A decried in the status quo. Second, B masterfully exploited A's own example of exempting a senior engineer while mandating a junior associate to prove that role-specific rules break interdependent, team-level functions like mentorship.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 flawlessly caught the flaw in Side A's mentor/mentee example, pointing out that exempting the senior employee from the mandate breaks the very mechanism of cultural transmission the junior employee is there to receive.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON won the central workability clash. Its font-change-or-lawsuit dilemma (if "cultural transmission" counts, rule is empty; if not, it bans real team-level reasons) went unanswered, and its veteran-as-source example showed PRO's role-specific differentiation breaks the transmission PRO concedes is real. PRO never solved who judges sufficiency, while CON explained why upfront universal duty expands existing law and replicates market's slowness. PRO's transparency fairness point survived but could not overcome that structural defect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 exposing that PRO must either accept "presence for cultural transmission" (making rule theater) or reject it (banning legitimate interdependent effects), plus the junior-needs-senior-present reversal that turns PRO's differentiation example into arbitrariness.
