- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Both sides built sophisticated, well-matched cases, but the debate turned on CON's answer to PRO's pressure question about what evidence/horizon would ever satisfy the "outweigh" trigger. CON's reply (persistent harm, flat audiences, sponsor exits despite optimized mitigation, "no plausible reversal") effectively set a near-impossible bar, and PRO's closing sharply exposed this as "a test for near-total failure, not whether the commitment is worthwhile"—a point CON never answered in its final speech. CON's self-fulfilling-prophecy argument was the most original and insightful idea in the debate, and PRO only partially blunted it (folding withdrawal costs into the calculus), but PRO's closing landed the more decisive, unrebutted blow by showing CON's operational standard effectively nullifies the motion's actual conditional in ordinary cases. That asymmetry in how the final pressure point was resolved tips a close, high-quality debate to PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing (B5.2), calling out CON's A1 answer to the pressure question as demanding near-total commercial collapse before scaling back—left unaddressed in CON's final speech—was the most damaging and decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): Side A successfully defended a conditional cost-benefit framework with a plausible stopping rule, while Side B never escaped the implication that it would tolerate virtually unlimited athlete and local fan burdens to preserve speculative future growth. A’s pressure question about returning fixtures home—eliminating overseas travel without requiring an empty week—exposed a gap in B’s fatigue mitigation story, and A’s closing crystallized B’s position as demanding near-total commercial collapse before allowing correction. B’s “self-fulfilling exit” critique was live and penetrating, but A absorbed it by insisting those withdrawal costs belong inside the comparison rather than exempting expansion from it. After the full exchange, A’s path to victory was cleaner: disciplined expansion with an exit condition is more coherent than expansion with an exit condition so high it is practically never triggered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over A’s Pressure Question Q2 and the “freed week”: A showed that scaling back an overseas trip could simply mean returning the fixture to its home venue, restoring local access and eliminating long-haul travel without demanding that athletes receive a blank week, while B’s response conceded the travel savings but reverted to treating every reduced trip as a total loss of international presence, overstating the strategic cost of selective pullback.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing a fatal structural flaw in the proposition's decision rule: comparing immediate, easily quantifiable costs (fatigue, displaced tickets) against delayed, continuity-dependent benefits (global brand expansion) creates a self-fulfilling prophecy. Side A's argument that a "probationary" or conditional international schedule actively suppresses the sponsor and fan investment needed to make it worthwhile was highly original and decisively dismantled Side B's cost-benefit framework. Side B defended the intuitive appeal of the motion competently and answered pressure questions directly, but could not overcome Side A's deeper critique of the weighing mechanism and market dynamics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Side B's "three-season pilot" proposal, arguing that placing international expansion on public probation guarantees the weak returns that would trigger a scale-back, effectively neutralizing Side B's attempt to balance short-term costs with long-term benefits.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with unusual sophistication, but PRO (B) ultimately controlled the decisive clash. CON's (A) central move—"redesign, not reduction," and the claim that overseas fan enjoyment is a benefit distinct from "brand expansion"—was clever, but B directly neutralized it by arguing that leagues' pursuit of overseas engagement IS the brand-expansion project, collapsing A's attempted carve-out, and by showing with concrete mechanisms (finite byes, zero-sum calendar slots, compounding travel length) that "redesign" has hard limits that eventually force reduction. A's rebuttal to that mechanism point ("consuming flexibility is not automatically distorting competition") stayed abstract and never answered the scarcity argument concretely. The pressure-question exchange was the turning point: B forced A to concede that scale-back is appropriate once residual costs exceed total benefits, and B's closing effectively used that concession to argue the remaining dispute was definitional bookkeeping already resolved in B's favor. A's closing reasserted the distinction without substantively refuting the merger argument. Both sides respected the format (clear A1/A2 answers), avoided fabricated evidence, and wrote strong closings, but B's case was more internally resistant to the opponent's strongest attack and left a cleaner, easier-to-adopt decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) answering the pressure questions by folding overseas fan access into "brand expansion" and highlighting the home/away asymmetry (counted loss vs. uncounted gain) most directly dismantled A's core distinguishing argument and was never fully recovered from in A's final speeches.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON)): Side A wins by showing that redesign faces hard institutional and calendar limits, persuasively framing existing overseas fan access as part of the league's brand-expansion calculus, and establishing that removing a promised home fixture is structurally more harmful than providing overseas access from a zero baseline. Side B’s semantic distinction between brand expansion and total benefits, while intellectually sharp, never overcame A’s practical and comparative case for proportionate scale-back.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answer to B’s pressure questions, where A exposed the asymmetry between adding overseas access versus subtracting a relied-upon home fixture, absorbed existing overseas fan welfare into the brand-expansion ledger, and forced out B’s concession that reduction is warranted once protections are exhausted.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B successfully defended the motion's specific condition by grounding the asymmetry of the harms (season-ticket holders losing a core fixture vs. overseas fans getting a rare event) and demonstrating the practical limits of schedule redesign. Side A relied heavily on a semantic distinction between "brand expansion" and "total benefits," which Side B effectively neutralized by contextualizing how leagues actually approach global markets and retaining audiences.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which dismantled Side A's symmetry argument by highlighting the baseline reliance of home supporters compared to the event-based nature of overseas games, and exposed the calendar limits of Side A's "redesign first" approach.
