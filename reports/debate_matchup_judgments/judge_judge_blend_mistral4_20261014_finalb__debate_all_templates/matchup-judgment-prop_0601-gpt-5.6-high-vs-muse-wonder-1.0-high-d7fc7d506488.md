### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-grounded cases without fake sourcing, and both handled the Q&A format correctly with explicit A1/A2 answers. The decisive edge goes to PRO because it stayed more tightly anchored to the motion's explicit "near-term" language, consistently framing the choice as certain, cumulative diesel-displacement beginning immediately versus rail's benefits that depend on a chain of unproven assumptions (financing, completion, driver switching, ridership, eventual land-use change). CON's strongest card—that rail captures the much larger prize of avoided car trips and that failing to act now forecloses corridors—was blunted rather than defeated: PRO's reply that corridor protection and targeted rail remain compatible with a bus-first main strategy, plus its point that rail projects are prone to cost overruns and delay in this very region, left CON's lock-in urgency looking more speculative than PRO's bus-displacement claims. CON never fully answered why "sequence is destiny" trumps the plain near-term reading of the motion beyond asserting trajectory matters, which read as a subtle broadening of the question rather than a clean rebuttal. PRO's closing crystallized this "reduction vs. promise of a reduction" distinction more sharply than CON's closing re-asserted trajectory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 answers) reframing the comparison from "bus vs. car" to "electric bus vs. diesel bus" and insisting the near-term case does not need a speculative car-switching forecast, combined with its reply that corridor protection and targeted rail can coexist with bus-first prioritization, most effectively defused CON's central lock-in and mode-shift arguments.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO won by keeping the motion’s near-term criterion decisive: certain, modular diesel cuts across the existing bus backbone beat CON’s longer chain of finish-the-line, attract-drivers, and reshape-development benefits. CON landed real pressure on mode-shift prize, dedicated-lane politics, and corridor lock-in, but repeatedly stretched “near-term” into multi-decade urban form and treated saturated mega-corridors as the national default. PRO answered the pressure questions cleanly, conceded targeted rail without surrendering priority, and closed on the cleaner cumulative-certainty comparison the ballot asks for.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s first rebuttal that CON swaps decades-long land-use transformation and a few megacity corridors for nationwide near-term decarbonization, paired with the diesel-bus-vs-diesel-bus A1 that forced CON’s carbon accounting onto the motion’s horizon.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): PRO successfully anchored the debate to the near-term timeframe, showing that bus electrification delivers immediate, cumulative emissions reductions across entire networks while CON's rail benefits remained speculative and long-term. PRO's pressure questions exposed CON's inability to square rail construction timelines with near-term decarbonization, and PRO's cleaner comparative framing—certainty versus promise—survived the full exchange intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4), where it directly answered CON's carbon-accounting challenge by exposing the asymmetry between certain diesel displacement and speculative future car avoidance, then crystallized that near-term strategy must count emissions avoided within the timeframe.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B built a tighter, more disciplined case around a clear decision metric (cumulative emissions avoided by 2035, certainty vs. speculation, modularity vs. lumpy delivery) and sustained it through every round, including a clean, correctly formatted A1/A2 response to pressure. A landed real damage — especially the point that if bus speed gains require dedicated lanes, road reallocation rather than electrification is doing the work, and that mode shift (not mere propulsion swap) is the real decarbonization lever — but A's opening was clipped before its strongest closing point landed, which is a real execution cost. B's concession that a shovel-ready rail project could "merit support" looked risky, but B recovered by reframing it as a burden-shifting test ("buses get first claim unless rail passes that test"), which is a coherent reading of "prioritize" rather than "exclude." A's closing leaned heavily on declaring this concession fatal without fully showing why a default-to-buses-with-exceptions standard fails to satisfy the motion. On balance B gave the cleaner, more resolvable path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to Q2 (A2 in Rebuttal 2), converting an apparent concession ("a funded, shovel-ready extension... can merit support") into an explicit burden-shifting test ("buses get first claim unless rail passes that test"), which preserved B's "main strategy" framing against A's strongest pressure point about rail sometimes winning project-by-project.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won a close debate by forcing the clash onto additionality, mode shift, and the meaning of a blanket hierarchy. PRO’s opening case on cumulative certainty, modular delivery, and existing ridership was strong and stayed live, but under pressure PRO conceded little standalone mode shift from batteries and that shovel-ready rail can deserve near-term priority under a tonnes-by-2035 test. CON used those concessions to show that PRO’s own standard collapses a region-wide “first claim” for buses into corridor-level comparison—which undercuts the proposition as written. CON also reframed “near-term” as maximizing avoided emissions within the horizon rather than rewarding whichever benefit starts first, and PRO never fully neutralized lock-in or car/motorcycle growth. PRO remained clearer on equity-of-current-networks and deployment speed, but CON’s later weighing made the decision rule easier to apply against a categorical priority. Opening clip on CON was a real execution ding, yet not decisive after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.4/A5.4: CON converting PRO’s “little mode shift” and rail-can-win admissions into a collapse of buses’ unexplained first claim.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A won by tying near-term decarbonization to cumulative, immediate emissions cuts from existing high-occupancy bus networks across entire cities, while Side B's counter-reliance on speculative mode shift from future rail lacked sufficient grounding in Latin American project timelines or ridership magnitudes. A's concession that shovel-ready rail extensions could beat buses on a tonnes-per-dollar test was successfully absorbed as a burden-shifting framework rather than a collapse of the motion, leaving A's equity and timing arguments about cleaning the air people breathe now comparatively decisive.
