### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A won a close, high-quality clash by keeping a cleaner hierarchy-with-exceptions story and making privacy the substantive stopping rule rather than a procedure that yields whenever danger is verified. B’s strongest weapons—precondition framing, harm asymmetry, and free-society practice (warrants, scanners, bank reporting)—forced real pressure, but B overreached with “yields completely,” then had to retreat into proportionality and operational uselessness. A answered the hardest Q2 burden directly (narrow monitoring yes; permanent indiscriminate no even at foreseeable cost) and used the closings’ “proportionate to what?” test to show that if independence can rule out effective security measures, privacy is ranking higher. B remained competitive and punchy, especially on irreversible death and translating privacy catastrophe into violence, but A more consistently decided the live comparison as the motion is written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A1/A2 answers in Rebuttal 2, especially distinguishing bounded emergency intrusion from permanent indiscriminate access and holding the line that privacy still forbids the latter at real security cost.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO)): Side B won by defending a coherent framework in which privacy governs the terms of security rather than submitting to it. B directly answered hard hypotheticals, explicitly rejecting permanent mass surveillance even at a foreseeable cost in lives. A’s case that privacy “yields completely” to substantiated threats was blunted by B’s distinction between bounded exceptions and a governing principle. A also relied on practical counters to the mass-surveillance hypothetical rather than meeting the principled burden, undermining the claim that security always outranks privacy when the two genuinely collide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 distinction between bounded exceptions and governing principles, which defused A’s central claim that privacy “yields completely” to security by showing that even authorized intrusions retain scope and duration limits set by privacy.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-6 Astra (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO maintained the live path that privacy defines a free society by supplying a stopping rule. It answered the emergency-mass screening push by distinguishing bounded exceptions (scanner, suspect search) from unlimited entitlement, and honestly accepted cost for permanent indiscriminate monitoring. CON's core that privacy yields completely when threat is verified and that rejection of total surveillance is just inefficiency never explained why an effective dragnet that would save lives should still be rejected - which is precisely the comparative test for "matters more" in a free society.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal that warrant/burning-home imposes privacy conditions on security, privacy continues to limit scope/duration after threat is verified, and proportionality must be measured against independence not just threat safety.
