### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): CON built a tighter, more sustained critique distinguishing loneliness (a subjective, causally ambiguous state with no diagnostic threshold) from classic public-health vectors (smoking, contaminated water) that have consent-violating externalities and standardized clinical protocols. PRO's analogies (hypertension, depression, smoking) were repeated across rounds but never fully answered CON's sharpest points: the bidirectional causation problem (illness/poverty causing isolation) and the absence of any diagnostic criteria for who counts as "pathologically" lonely. In the pressure-question exchange, CON's Q1/Q2 directly exposed this gap, and PRO's A1/A2 answered with assertions ("can be identified through validated measures," "independent causal pathway") rather than engaging the specific distinction CON drew between depression's standardized criteria and loneliness's lack thereof. CON's closing crystallized the clash cleanly around "does loneliness meet the threshold for public-health power," while PRO's closing mostly reasserted earlier analogies without rebutting CON's distinction between cost-as-evidence-of-harm and cost-as-mandate-for-coercive intervention. CON's consistent focus on autonomy, diagnostic rigor, and resource-opportunity-cost gave it the cleaner, more defensible path to the decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1), directly answering PRO's depression analogy by distinguishing depression's standardized diagnostic criteria and treatments from loneliness's lack of any consensus definition, undercut PRO's central comparative anchor that had been reused across multiple rounds.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively disproving Side B's comparisons to other public health priorities (like depression and hypertension). Side A successfully argues that without standardized diagnostic criteria or proven clinical interventions, turning loneliness into a public health mandate pathologizes normal human behavior and distracts from addressing the structural economic issues Side B themselves brought up.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response to Side B's depression comparison, establishing that depression is a clinical diagnosis with standardized criteria and proven protocols, whereas loneliness is an internal state lacking clinical boundaries.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a much stronger comparative framework, effectively distinguishing between objective public health vectors and subjective emotional states. A decisively dismantled B's analogies to smoking and depression by highlighting the lack of diagnostic criteria, proven interventions, and direct physical externalities for loneliness. A's rebuttal regarding civil society—that it has been starved by structural policies rather than inherently failing—was particularly sharp and neutralized B's core structural argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the depression analogy in Rebuttal 2, clearly distinguishing clinical diagnoses with standardized criteria and evidence-based treatments from vague emotional states, effectively neutralizing PRO's strongest comparative example.
