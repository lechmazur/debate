### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON built the more rigorous economic case and executed the sharper exchange. Its opening correctly reframed the debate around funding mechanics (fixed rights costs, cross-subsidy, perishability) rather than pure consumer-choice framing. Most decisively, CON caught a real internal contradiction in PRO's model—claiming stacked team/competition passes are both "alongside" the bundle (B1-era framing) and "cheaper" than it (A2.2 vs A2.4)—and pressed it relentlessly through Pressure Questions and into Rebuttal 2. PRO's attempted resolution (segmenting into a premium bundle tier vs. a cheap casual tier) implicitly conceded that superfans would downgrade, which fed directly into CON's strongest unanswered point: that PRO's own break-even arithmetic (nine new casual subscribers per downgrading superfan) requires durable, monthly, season-over-season retention from precisely the segment most likely to churn, and PRO answered this with assertion ("flakiness is a symptom, not immutable") rather than a demonstrated mechanism. CON's fragmentation example (league/cup/continental rights held separately) was also never neutralized—PRO's response effectively restated the fragmentation as a feature, which CON correctly flagged as relabeling rather than resolving. Both sides had turns clipped by the word-limit format note, but the lost material in each case was concluding flourish rather than core argument, so this roughly offsets. CON's closing was intact and crystallized the clash cleanly ("same bill with worse ergonomics," reliable pooled revenue vs. hope), while PRO's closing was clipped mid-point. Overall CON answered the decisive pressure more directly and left PRO's central mechanism more exposed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1-B2.2, where CON exposes the direct contradiction between PRO's claim that stacked passes "sit alongside" the bundle and the later claim that they are "cheaper" than it—this became the fulcrum of the debate and PRO never fully resolved it, instead conceding a downgrade dynamic that CON then exploited with the durability/churn argument.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON)): CON successfully exposed that PRO's market-expansion story rested on speculative volume from unreliable casual subscribers, while the downgrade risk to committed fans created a severe break-even burden (nine new casuals per lost superfan monthly). CON's fragmentation point—that team passes would splinter already-divided rights into worse ergonomics—survived PRO's rebuttal as a live cost. PRO's consumer-fairness case remained intuitive but never neutralized CON's funding-risk and retention challenges, leaving CON with the cleaner path to victory after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's initial rebuttal catching PRO's contradiction on whether superfans would pay more or less under unbundling ([B2.1]), converting it into the decisive break-even burden that dominated the rest of the debate.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.6 Pro (thinking) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the central economic clash. A exposed PRO's contradiction (superfan pays more vs bundle is a tax), turned PRO's own 9-to-1 break-even math into evidence of unsustainable reliance on churn-prone casuals against a fixed rights bill, and held the fragmentation harm that team seasons span split rights. PRO's market-expansion and piracy story remained plausible but never resolved whether cheap passes lose money through downgrades or fail to save money by costing more, leaving its route to revenue growth undercut.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 [A2.1-A2.4] isolating B2.2 vs B2.4 — if stacked passes cost less revenue collapses, if they cost more there is no consumer saving — and then using PRO's own 9-new-fans-per-downgrader arithmetic in Rebuttal 2 as a concession about durability and churn.
