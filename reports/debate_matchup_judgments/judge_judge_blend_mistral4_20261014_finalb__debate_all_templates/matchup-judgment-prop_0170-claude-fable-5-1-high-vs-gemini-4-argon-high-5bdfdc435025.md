### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): A won a clear but competitive debate by showing that desalination cannot plausibly be the primary regional strategy when agriculture dominates demand, desalinated water is too costly for staple crops, and many scarce basins are inland. B compellingly argued that conservation cannot create new water, but A directly answered that pressure through agricultural reallocation, recycling, and efficiency, while turning B's concession that desert grain must be curtailed into evidence that rationing necessarily does most of the work. B's clipped rebuttal and overbroad claim that desalination ends scarcity also weakened its execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.2-A4.3 most decisively answered B's repeated “first litre” challenge: A identified agricultural reallocation as the source for essential urban demand, then used B's concession about unsustainable desert grain to argue that quotas remain indispensable even under B's plan.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO)): CON wins the central arithmetic clash. B's "new water" adds supply for coastal cities, but A shows cities are a small fraction of basin use, most water-scarce people are inland with no sea access, and staple agriculture cannot afford desalinated water – a point B concedes in B4.2/B5.4. Transferring coastal cities off rivers frees only a sliver, while brackish desalination is finite and leaves concentrated brine with no ocean disposal (A pressure Q2 unanswered convincingly and B4 clipped). Once unsustainable desert farming must go anyway, reallocation + recycling + pipe repair covers municipal needs at far lower cost and works inland, making conservation/rationing the primary lever, not a coastal backstop.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1-A2.3 and A4.3: cities are a small share of basin use so moving them to desal cannot close deficit, farm water itself must be rationed, and inland brackish desal is finite with problematic brine – directly undercutting B's free-up-river and inland-brackish routes.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a decisive conceptual frame: conservation allocates a shrinking deficit, while desalination creates new supply. Side A effectively trapped Side B with pressure questions about where new water comes from once conservation hits its hard floor. Side B's answer (reallocating from agriculture) allowed A to paint B's case as permanent, tightening rationing. Side A also successfully defended the substitution mechanism (desalinating for cities frees natural water for farms). Side B made strong points about the economics of agricultural water and inland limitations, but ultimately could not overcome A's core argument that conservation alone cannot outpace a shrinking baseline.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where it dismantled Side B's answers to the pressure questions, exposing that "taking water from farms" is merely a transfer within a shrinking pie and that "earlier rationing" in Cape Town just delays the inevitable collapse.
