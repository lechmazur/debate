- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A built a cleaner, more disciplined case: a developmental-asymmetry frame (handwriting/attention windows close early, digital fluency can be learned quickly later), a clear burden ("show tablets outperform paper for young learners specifically"), and an equity reversal (school should supply the scarce resource—print—since affluent homes already supply screens). A exploited B's own concession that paper anchors "handwriting, sketching, and sustained reading" to expose tension in B's alternative, and used pressure questions effectively to force B to defend the "fail-on-paper-first" gatekeeping claim. B's case on accessibility and equity was genuinely strong and never fully neutralized (the claim that device-light forces disabled children to demonstrate failure before getting tools), and B's closing crystallized this vividly. But B's rebuttals largely restated the original accessibility worry rather than directly engaging A's specific counter that identification runs through teachers/screening independent of device default, and B never squarely answered the "thirty tablets vs. three" reframing of its own accessibility argument. A's final closing was clipped by the word-limit format note, which is a real execution cost and keeps the margin from being larger, but the substantive weighing had already been done in Rebuttal 2, so the clash was still resolved enough to favor A.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 reframing of B's accessibility argument ("defending one-to-one with an argument for one-in-twenty") combined with A4's point that tablets "don't diagnose anyone either," which B's Rebuttal 2 never directly refuted, was the most decisive exchange.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.3 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing the debate around asymmetric developmental windows and exposing Side B's accessibility argument as a justification for targeted exceptions rather than a universal default.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2 (A4.1) dismantling of the "bottleneck" argument by pointing out that tablets do not automatically diagnose students and that paper provides richer diagnostic signals for teachers.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by converting A's own concessions into the decisive weapon. A's opening built a plausible neurology/attention/equity case, but in Rebuttal 1 A introduced a "documented needs" carve-out for assistive tech that B immediately exploited: diagnosis lags behind need, so the carve-out leaves exactly the struggling, undiagnosed children PRO claims to protect without help, and it also concedes that devices work when purposeful. B's pressure question forced A to admit "not automatically" a device for the undiagnosed eight-year-old — a damaging admission A never recovered from. B also neutralized A's lone second example (paper math beats guess-and-check software) by showing it argued for regulating software design, not banning hardware, which is precisely B's "regulate, don't prohibit" frame. A's equity-reversal argument was answered cleanly (classroom devices vs. home broadband is a homework-policy distinction) and went unaddressed in A's final rebuttal and closing. B's closing crystallized the clash with a concrete, unanswered scenario (the waiting undiagnosed reader) while A's closing retreated to abstract "two kinds of childhood" framing without re-engaging B's sharpest point. Both sides had a speech clipped for length, roughly canceling as an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 (B2.1–B2.3) exposing that "documented needs" means diagnostic delay and stigma, later sharpened by the Q1 pressure question and A's damaging "not automatically" admission in A4.1 — this exchange was the turning point of the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by effectively turning Side A's "documented needs" carve-out into a devastating vulnerability. Side B successfully argued that relying on diagnostic paperwork leaves undiagnosed, struggling children waiting for tools that Side A already conceded are effective. Side B also cleanly neutralized Side A's distraction arguments by differentiating between software design and hardware utility.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal brilliantly capitalized on Side A's honest answer about the undiagnosed 8-year-old, framing the concession as proof that Side A's policy forces real children to fail while waiting for paperwork.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.3 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing the live clash onto accessibility timing and overgeneralization. A’s developmental handwriting/attention case stayed plausible, but the “documented needs” carve-out became a liability once A answered that the undiagnosed struggling child does not automatically get a device. B converted that into a concrete comparative: regulated classroom tools for every learner versus paper-default waiting for paperwork. A never rebuilt a clean reason why subtraction beats software control after conceding assistive value and after B neutralized the math example as a design flaw.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s R2/closing use of A’s “not automatically” answer: undiagnosed kids wait under PRO while CON gives same-day level-matched practice without stigma.
