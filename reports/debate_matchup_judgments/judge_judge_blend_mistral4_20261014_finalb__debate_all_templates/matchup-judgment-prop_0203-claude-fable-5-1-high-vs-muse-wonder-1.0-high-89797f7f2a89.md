### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate by converting PRO’s pivot from “scarce patient capital” into network/governance effects into a tool-mismatch problem: deal review severs formal equity while advice, recruitment, and introductions travel with people, and enforcement via third-country vehicles leaves the most determined capital less visible. CON’s certain-cost vs speculative-delay weighing (visibility loss, chill, focusing) stayed cleaner than PRO’s unquantified timing gains, despite PRO’s strong dilemma and export-control parallel.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON A2.1–A2.2: forcing the capital-to-networks shift and arguing screening targets the wrong object if relationships, not wire transfers, are the mechanism.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO successfully defended the mechanism that formal outbound investment transfers institutional know-how, governance, and network effects that state capital cannot replicate, and showed that screening narrows that compounding channel without needing to police every informal conversation. CON's strongest objections—evasion through third-country vehicles, loss of investor intelligence, and Huawei-style acceleration—were all answered with plausible enforcement distinctions and the observation that surviving sanctions at high cost is not evidence that help would have been harmless. CON's "certain costs versus speculative gains" framing was powerful, but PRO undermined it by demonstrating the concrete, non-speculative nature of institutional transfer and by exposing that CON could not simultaneously treat investor access as invaluable intelligence and strategically worthless when received by the rival. PRO's closing crystallized this asymmetry cleanly, while CON's closing, though sharp, leaned on an exaggerated reading of PRO's scope definition as a concession.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer (A4.1) distinguishing board seats, governance rights, and follow-on authority from casual advice or lunches, which directly neutralized CON's claim that screening cuts the formal shell while missing the substantive transfer.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A wins a narrow, highly competitive debate by successfully defending the core mechanism of outbound investment transfer (intangibles tied to equity and governance) and effectively weighing the strategic value of delay. While Side B introduced a strong, original argument about losing "ground-truth" intelligence (blindness) and made sharp points about definitional sprawl, Side A's rebuttal on evasion was decisive. Side A correctly identified that the strategic value lies in the expertise of compliant major funds, and if determined money routes through uncovered third-country vehicles, it loses that crucial institutional expertise, thus neutralizing Side B's evasion argument. Side A also effectively neutralized the Huawei example by distinguishing between surviving at a high cost versus thriving with Western acceleration.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 on evasion and compliance. By clarifying that the regulatory nexus catches the compliant major funds—which are the actual sources of valuable expertise—Side A effectively neutralized Side B's argument that determined money will simply route through third-country vehicles, pointing out that such evasive capital lacks the very institutional know-how the policy aims to restrict.
