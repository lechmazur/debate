### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more motion-faithful case by distinguishing "maintaining existing channels" (which both sides agree serves excluded groups) from "expanding" them, and arguing that marginal new investment should go where the next generation of citizens actually is. This distinction neutralized B's recurring universal-service objection, which kept treating any shift toward short-form as abandonment of infrastructure rather than a prioritization question. A also directly answered B's sharpest pressure question (why not hire creators as salaried staff) by proposing flat-fee/salaried co-production models that preserve editorial control without per-engagement incentives, and B never circled back to rebut that specific move. B's strongest material—depth requires sustained investigation, algorithms reward outrage and brevity, universal service demands reaching the digitally excluded—remained live and well-argued, but B's answer to A's key pressure question ("how does expanding channels youth don't watch build the familiarity needed for trust when the feed fails?") was largely circular, reasserting institutional authority without explaining how unencountered institutions earn recognition. A's closing crystallized this gap effectively. A's case loses some credit for a clipped Rebuttal 2 that was cut off before its full argument landed, which is a real execution cost, but the core comparative work was still visible and the clipped material appeared to restate rather than introduce decisive new content.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's Q2 (flat-fee/salaried creator partnerships avoiding per-engagement incentives) versus B's failure to re-engage that distinction in Rebuttal 2, leaving B's editorial-corruption argument only partially intact.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly distinguishing between maintaining existing channels and expanding them, neutralizing Side A's impacts regarding digitally excluded populations. Side B also presented a much stronger mechanism for how trust is built—through prior encounters—exposing the flaw in Side A's assumption that youth will instinctively seek out unfamiliar traditional channels during a crisis.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A2) cleanly dismantled Side A's pressure regarding creator incentives by explaining that partnerships can be flat-fee and leverage community trust without triggering algorithmic outrage.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): B won by keeping the decisive clash clear: unused depth on unwatched channels has no public-service effect on the next generation of funders and citizens, so marginal priority should go to encounter via short-form and controlled creator partnerships rather than further linear expansion. A landed real damage on budget cannibalization, the broken funnel, and the universal-mandate claim for the digitally excluded, but never fully solved how non-watching youth gain the familiarity A itself needs “when the feed fails.” B’s answers on commercial vs public-service saturation and non-engagement partnership models held; A’s closing largely restated mandate rather than closing that gap. B’s clipped R2 is a real execution cost and keeps the margin modest rather than large.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal (esp. B2.2/B2.3): reframed A’s depth attack as attacking replacement, not priority, and made encounter the precondition that renders unwatched channel depth empty for youth.
