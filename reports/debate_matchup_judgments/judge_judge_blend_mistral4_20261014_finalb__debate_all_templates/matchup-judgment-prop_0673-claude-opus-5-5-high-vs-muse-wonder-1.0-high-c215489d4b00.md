### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A wins a tightly contested and exceptionally high-quality debate. Side B exerted excellent pressure on A's seemingly contradictory claims about permits being too weak to guarantee self-sufficiency but strong enough to lure fraud. A resolved this decisively by distinguishing the ex-ante value of a work permit (a financeable promise for smugglers) from its ex-post reality (part-time work that still requires aid). A's framing that "if immediacy matters for savings, it matters for filing" successfully linked B's own premise to the mechanism for induced demand, proving why volume would swamp per-capita savings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 response (A1) distinguishing between the ex-ante value of a permit to secure smuggler financing versus its ex-post reality of only providing partial self-sufficiency, cleanly resolving B's contradiction trap.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate on the conjunction: the same immediacy that can trim current reliance also lowers the liquidity barrier to weak filings, so the clean trade fails once behavior changes. PRO landed a real dilemma and a strong zero-baseline savings floor, but repeatedly lacked magnitude on “materially,” lost ground on induced-claim accounting, and paid execution costs from repeated clipping. CON’s frozen-vs-created-population weighing and lottery distinction better resolved the live clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s lottery reply to B’s contradiction Q: ex ante lawful status/SSN lures filings even when ex post work often fails to replace assistance.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (CON)): PRO established a direct, near-certain mechanism: legal idleness guarantees public dependency, so earlier authorization necessarily reduces reliance for the existing population. CON’s fraud case relied on an under-quantified "liquidity gate" that was never shown to produce a material increase, and PRO successfully turned it by showing the deterrent operates only through the very public dependency the motion seeks to eliminate. PRO also won the key "materiality" burden by pressing CON twice for magnitude without receiving a concrete answer. CON’s systemic volume argument was plausible but too speculative to outweigh PRO’s floor of real savings.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Turn 7 dilemma exposing that CON’s fraud deterrent depends on enforcing months of taxpayer-funded dependency, undermining CON’s own anti-reliance position and making the motion’s tradeoff appear illusory on CON’s terms.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively framing the waiting period as a 'price screen' based on upfront liquidity rather than a merits test, neutralizing Side B's core defense. Side A brilliantly leveraged Side B's own concessions to show that reducing the barrier for honest applicants unavoidably lowers the entry cost for fraudulent ones.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Turn 8 argument that if four months constitute 'most of the forced-dependence window,' they also represent the bulk of the upfront cost for a fraudulent filer, dismantling Side B's 'different denominators' defense.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO)): CON won a competitive debate on burden and conjunction. PRO had a clean, concrete story on certain shelter savings for the already-waiting pool and a memorable “wait that screens nothing” line, but never discharged the no-material-fraud half beyond calling a surge speculative and treating four months as only a backlog fraction. CON’s decisive path was the price/liquidity reframe plus 1990s reform logic: if those months are “most of the forced-dependence window” for honest arrivals, they are also most of the upfront cost of filing for a work card. PRO’s different-denominators reply was clever but strained against that arrival-finance point. Format clipping on two CON rebuttals was a real cost and kept the margin from being larger, but the live clash still favored CON’s conjunction attack over PRO’s hope-plus-certainty split.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained contradiction (esp. A2.1–A2.3 / A4.1–A4.2 / A5.2–A5.3) turning PRO’s “most of the forced-dependence window” concession into proof that four earlier lawful months also lower the entry price of a fabricated claim; PRO’s denominator distinction never fully neutralized it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Wonder 1.0 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO won by establishing that the 60-180 day waiting period contains no actual merits screening, making it forced idleness rather than a meaningful fraud deterrent. This "screen that screens nothing" framing undercut CON's historical and price-screen arguments, while PRO's concrete assistance mechanism—converting shelter dependence to self-support for the existing applicant pool—remained largely uncontested. CON's volume-swamping argument stayed speculative and unquantified, and PRO's response that informal work already fills the arrival gap weakened CON's liquidity theory. Both sides were competitive, but PRO created the cleaner route to victory by narrowing the clash to the actual function of the waiting period.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4) directly answered CON's pressure questions with the "denominators differ" distinction—showing the same four months represent most of the assistance-dependence window for existing applicants but only a marginal addition to the payoff period for prospective fraudulent filers—while re-emphasizing that no credibility screening occurs between day 60 and day 180 regardless.
