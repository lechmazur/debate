### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A offered the cleaner motion-specific case: parents retain freedom to teach and model their values, but teachers should not have to enforce highly restrictive personal rules at the expense of instruction, common safety standards, and other children. Side B compellingly defended reasonable accommodations and deeply held ethics, but repeatedly shifted from the proposition's restrictive lifestyle rules to more sympathetic cases such as bullying prevention, modesty, and religious diets. It also never adequately answered A's finite-attention comparison or the risk of enforcing private social exclusions. Both sides used some erroneous reference labels, slightly reducing execution quality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure question forced B to concede that schools must reject discriminatory or educationally disruptive parental demands. A then used that concession effectively: the dispute became where schools should draw limits, not whether parental authority is absolute, and universal safety and conduct rules offered a clearer standard than B's vague appeal to reasonable boundaries.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO won the central clash on triage and burden. B insisted schools must never audit values, but then conceded in A1/A2 of Rebuttal 2 that civil-rights and disruption limits apply, validating PRO's standard: universal safety/conduct rules, not 30 bespoke lifestyles. PRO answered B's religious-vs-vegan discrimination pressure with equal non-enforcement (offer options, never force consumption, use blanket no-sharing for safety), while B could not justify parent-driven social boycotts without re-invoking teacher discretion it had attacked.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Pressure Q1 and Closing use of B's concession that schools must reject discriminatory/disruptive demands — turning B's no-triage stance into endorsement of PRO's neutral universal-rules standard.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B built a much stronger and more practical case, effectively distinguishing between a school overriding family values and merely declining to act as a proxy to police them. Side B successfully exposed the logistical impossibility of teachers enforcing bespoke micro-regulations for thirty students and dismantled Side A's defense of parental social boycotts. Side A's concession in the second rebuttal that schools can reject disruptive demands ultimately undermined their own case against drawing a line at lifestyle preferences, allowing Side B to crystallize the clash in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they cleanly answered the pressure questions by distinguishing between universal safety rules and individualized lifestyle audits, and then turned Side A's "in loco parentis" and "subtle bullying" arguments against them by showing how they would paralyze the classroom and enforce private social exclusions.
