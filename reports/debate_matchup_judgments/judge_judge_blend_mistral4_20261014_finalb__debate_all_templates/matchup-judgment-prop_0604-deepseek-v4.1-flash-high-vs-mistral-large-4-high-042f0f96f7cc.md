B never established why post-harm liability was comparatively adequate for irreversible harms.

CON landed real damage on cartelization, open weights, and the “only the willing apply” problem, and answered pressure with clear A1/A2 structure; but PRO’s pressure exposed the core hole in pure liability, and PRO’s rebuttal/closing crystallized that imperfect border control is still better than a postmortem. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that A's license targets a general artifact whose safety changes through fine-tuning, tools, and deployment context. A persuasively defended EU market leverage and source-level prevention, but never resolved whether licensing is an obsolete snapshot or an impractical continuous pre-clearance system. B also established that risk-based rules can impose ex-ante testing and audits, weakening A's repeated equation of risk regulation with purely post-harm litigation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's snapshot-versus-bottleneck response was decisive. A clarified that licensing applies to the provider rather than every downstream user, but this left downstream adaptations outside the gate while still requiring repeated reevaluation of evolving capabilities. B used that tension effectively in the final rebuttal and closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by keeping the decisive structural clash live: a mandatory license for frontier general-purpose models is either an obsolete snapshot or continuous pre-clearance of unbounded post-deployment uses. PRO’s strongest frame—ex-ante gate, source chokepoint, Brussels effect—never escaped that bind; when pressed, PRO conceded re-evaluation of evolving capabilities, which CON turned into the bottleneck. CON also better answered pressure on who the EU can actually reach (deployers and observed use versus relocatable labs), while PRO’s pharma/aviation analogies and dual bank/lab punishment claims stayed asserted more than solved. Rhetoric was close, but CON made the debate easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 asymmetry turn: risk-based can act on observed use/harm; licensing must pre-certify an unbounded general-purpose space, and PRO’s own “re-evaluation as capabilities evolve” collapses into continuous pre-clearance.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing the snapshot vs continuous-approval dilemma: PRO's gate must either freeze an obsolete snapshot or become the unbounded pre-clearance it denies, collapsing into the adaptive risk-based tools CON already offers. PRO's chokepoint/Brussels-effect case was strong but left compute-threshold proxy and offshore-deployer liability pressures under-answered, while CON's A1/A2 answers clarified leverage lies with EU deployers, not foreign labs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 A1 turned B's Q1 about tracking vs licensing: observing deployed use ≠ certifying unbounded future uses, so B's own "re-evaluation as capabilities evolve" concedes continuous pre-clearance and the bottleneck charge.
