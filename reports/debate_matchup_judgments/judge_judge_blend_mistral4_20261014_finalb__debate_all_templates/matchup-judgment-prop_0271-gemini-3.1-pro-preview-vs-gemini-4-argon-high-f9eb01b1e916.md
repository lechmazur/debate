### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON built a technical case that proved harder to dislodge: algorithmic ranking is also the mechanism that demotes legal-but-harmful "borderline" content, so stripping recommendation engines while keeping "search" and "basic abuse filters" leaves exactly the raw, unranked toxicity PRO warns about. PRO's answer to the pointed Q1 pressure question (that disabling the feed just lets kids retrieve "what they actually look for") never actually explained how unranked search avoids surfacing the same borderline material, and CON exploited that gap decisively in Rebuttal 2. Even more costly for PRO was its answer to Q2: to close the burner-browser workaround, PRO conceded parents must lock down mobile web browsers entirely — which directly undercuts PRO's own central framing that granular toggles create a "low-friction middle ground" that avoids all-or-nothing bans and rebellion. CON caught and hammered this self-contradiction through Rebuttal 2 and the closing, turning PRO's own answer into a weapon. PRO's casino/stopwatch metaphor and "restoring family power" framing were rhetorically vivid and the liability-shield response ("not mutually exclusive") was reasonable, but these never resolved the two structural holes CON kept pressing. CON's case was more internally consistent and adapted better across rounds, converging the debate onto points PRO could not cleanly answer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4.4-A4.5) turning PRO's own Pressure-Question answer about locking mobile browsers into proof that PRO's "low-friction middle ground" actually requires harsher restriction than simple time limits — a live contradiction PRO never recovered from.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): PRO won by keeping the decisive clash on the status-quo mismatch and parental authority: time limits do not dismantle engagement architecture, engagement can be distinguished from basic moderation, and a parental toggle is not mutually exclusive with safe-by-default rules. CON landed real damage on ranking inseparability and the browser-lockdown contradiction that weakened PRO’s “middle ground,” but never fully neutralized the core claim that families currently lack usable power against behavioral feeds. PRO’s cleaner weighing and answer to the liability-false-dichotomy pressure made the ballot easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s mutual-exclusivity answer (A3.2/A4.4) and CON’s later exploitation of the browser-lockdown concession (B4.4–B4.5) formed the sharpest exchange; the former preserved PRO’s live route.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON successfully undermined PRO's core distinction between engagement algorithms and safety algorithms, showing that disabling recommendation engines would strip away the ranking that demotes legal-but-harmful borderline content. CON also turned PRO's circumvention answer against them, demonstrating that preserving a "middle ground" requires draconian browser lockdowns that generate more resentment than existing time limits. Combined with the persistent liability-shield argument, CON built the cleaner comparative case that parental toggles create an illusion of control while letting platforms off the hook.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal ([A4.4]-[A4.5]), where they exposed that PRO's model collapses into a policing trap: the supposedly moderate "middle ground" of disabling feeds requires parents to lock down mobile web browsers, contradicting PRO's promise of low-friction compromise and proving that PRO's cure is more invasive than existing time limits.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but PRO (B) more effectively reframed the debate around a "middle-ground toggle" versus the status quo's all-or-nothing ban, and used that framing to blunt CON's strongest claim (that restrictions drive teens underground) by noting messaging/friend-links remain intact. PRO's car-lock analogy (B4.1) neutralized CON's "liability shield" argument, and CON never directly answered it in the final rebuttal or closing. Critically, on the pressure-question exchange about local context (eating-disorder relapse triggers), CON's answer (A4.1) effectively conceded that platform-level "safe by design" cannot handle such idiosyncratic cases and fell back on blunt device confiscation—an admission PRO sharply exploited in closing (B5.2–B5.3) to show CON's "universal baseline" solution collapses into the very all-or-nothing harshness PRO criticized. CON's own strong points (two-tier internet, overbearing-parent misuse, browser-bypass concession) were real and only partially answered by PRO, but CON's closing did not re-engage the car-lock rebuttal or press the unanswered local-context concession, leaving PRO's reframing as the last word and the clearer path to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q1 pressure question about eating-disorder relapse and local context, combined with CON's answer in A4.1 conceding that device confiscation (not platform design) is the real fallback, which PRO then used decisively in closing to collapse CON's "universal baseline" argument.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the motion as written (more parental block/delay power) and making the decisive local-context clash land: universal defaults cannot know a specific child’s ED or ADHD triggers, while a single feed toggle is simpler relief than today’s all-or-nothing bans. B’s strongest route (two-tiered internet + platform liability shield + workarounds) was real but never fully neutralized A’s both-and and middle-ground replies; B often reframed toward “universal by-design instead,” which softened pressure on the actual proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 and close: browser/peer-link access undercuts B’s “devastating social isolation” claim, while B’s device-restriction fallback for ED cases concedes parents still need power beyond platform defaults.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by more cleanly resolving the central clash: the real choice is not between perfect universal safety and parental toggles, but between an all-or-nothing ban that drives teens underground and a granular middle-ground that preserves social connection while defanging the compulsive personalized feed. Side B directly turned Side A's (CON) bypass argument by distinguishing logged-out, non-personalized browsing from the targeted behavioral slot machine, and exposed that CON's "safe-by-design" alternative could not handle local, individual vulnerabilities—forcing CON to admit that parents in acute situations should simply restrict device access entirely, which is the very coercive trap PRO set out to solve. Side B's pressure questions exposed real burdens that CON never squarely answered, particularly why adding a middle option increases tyrannical parental power when total confiscation is already available. Side A's systemic "two-tiered internet" objection remained rhetorically potent but lacked a clear causal link showing that a parental toggle would actively block universal platform reform, and Side B's "hostage" reframe successfully blunted it. Side B's progression was sharper, narrowing the debate to the comparative harm of all-or-nothing bans versus granular control and winning that comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.2–B4.3) turned CON's own Pressure Question concession—that teens could access culture through friends and browsers—into proof that disabling a personalized feed does not cause the devastating social isolation CON claimed. By distinguishing the compulsive, data-rich algorithmic slot machine from casual logged-out browsing, PRO collapsed CON's primary bypass/isolation argument and showed the toggle preserves peer connection while removing the uniquely harmful mechanism.
