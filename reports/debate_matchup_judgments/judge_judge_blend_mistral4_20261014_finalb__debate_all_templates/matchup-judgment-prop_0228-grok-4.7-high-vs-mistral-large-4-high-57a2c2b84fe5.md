### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): B built a sharper, more sustained causal mechanism: once idle login time carries payroll costs, platforms ration access via minimum-availability rules, converting casual multi-app flexibility into a smaller, permission-based formal core. A never squarely neutralized this dynamic, mostly reasserting the burden-of-proof framing without engaging the labor-market response B predicted. B also pressed effectively on the motion's own exemption test, showing that the paradigm courier (owns car, picks hours, bears fuel risk) still fails it, exposing the exemption as narrower than advertised — A's answer simply bit the bullet without defusing the "swallowing" charge. B further exploited the Europe/Asia heterogeneity point (enforcement capacity, informal economies) that A left largely unaddressed despite the motion explicitly spanning both regions, instead leaning on an unsupported blanket claim that part-time contracts already work "across European and Asian labor markets." B's pressure-question answers (A1/A2) were properly formatted and substantively engaged (institutional enforcement shifting proof, distinguishing "decline a shift" from "invent an hour"), while A's answers to B's harder questions were comparatively more conclusory. Both sides argued competently and closed by crystallizing the clash, but B's mechanism-level rebuttal and tighter exploitation of the exemption's internal tension gave it the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's repeated "fixed costs → rationed slots → permission, not exit" mechanism (B2.1-B2.2, reinforced in B4.3-B4.4), which A never directly dismantled, combined with B's pressure question exposing that A's own exemption test fails the paradigm multi-app courier it claims to protect.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): B won by distinguishing roster flexibility from the spontaneous ability to log in, reject individual jobs, and switch platforms, then explaining why employment costs would ration that freedom. B also offered agency enforcement, deactivation safeguards, and portable benefits as a targeted alternative. A asserted that flexible contracts preserve equivalent autonomy but never resolved B's fixed-cost mechanism, and its closing repeated the claim that couriers must enforce their own rights after B had directly answered it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer that part-time or zero-hours employment permits declining rostered work but does not preserve worker-initiated, job-by-job participation decisively undercut A's claim that employment can provide the same flexibility. Its agency-led enforcement response also neutralized A's strongest practical objection to targeted regulation.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A delivered a masterful deconstruction of the proposition's core premise, brilliantly distinguishing between the "permission" of part-time employment and the "right to invent" a schedule inherent in gig work. Side A's economic mechanism regarding the cost of idle option value effectively neutralized Side B's reliance on zero-hours contracts. Furthermore, Side A successfully trapped Side B on the narrowness of the freelancer exemption, proving it would swallow the very workers it aimed to protect. Side A's rhetoric was sharp, memorable, and deeply grounded in the realities of the gig economy.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they dismantled the equivalence between part-time contracts and gig flexibility by explaining how payroll costs on idle logins force platforms to ration access and demand minimum availability, destroying the casual multi-app flexibility workers rely on.
