- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won by keeping the root-cause comparison live: physical shortage vs rationing scarcity. CON’s immediacy/incumbent-security frame was coherent but never neutralized the lockout of movers/newcomers, condo conversion/screening harms, or finite high-earner bidding; PRO’s vacancy-chain, land-cost splitting, and closing screening-lottery turns made the decision cleaner.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B closing screening lottery (B5.2–B5.4): under caps, scarce vacant units still go to lowest-risk affluent applicants, so RC rations shortage rather than blocking outbidding.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because it successfully demonstrated that tenant protections and rent control merely ration existing scarcity, while supply reform resolves the underlying shortage. Side B's "screening lottery" argument—showing that rent-controlled units get allocated to high-credit, high-income applicants rather than the most vulnerable—exposed a fatal trade-off in Side A's case that was never adequately answered. While Side A made a compelling moral case for protecting incumbent tenants, it largely evaded the question of how newcomers and mobile families access housing under strict rent caps, resorting to the circular answer that preventing displacement eliminates the need for new leases. Side B's land-cost splitting mechanism and vacancy-chain logic provided a coherent causal model for how zoning reform generates affordability broadly and immediately, whereas Side A's reliance on "trickle-down" framing never fully overcame Side B's structural rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was Side B's handling of Side A's pressure questions in Rebuttal 2 (Turn 7), where B explained land-cost splitting and site redirection to show how upzoning produces modestly priced units, and argued that abundant supply forces landlords to compete via waived deposits and stable renewals rather than spikes. Combined with the closing "screening lottery" crystallization, this sequence revealed that rent controls ration scarce units to the most credentialed applicants while leaving vulnerable newcomers locked out.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side B built a more robust and logically cohesive case, effectively dismantling Side A's "time delay" and "luxury-only" arguments by explaining immediate vacancy chains and land-cost splitting. Crucially, Side A walked into a logical trap by claiming rent caps prevent affluent newcomers from outbidding working families; Side B expertly countered this with the "screening lottery" argument, demonstrating that non-price rationing under rent control disproportionately harms the vulnerable newcomers Side A claims to protect. Side B's closing crystallized this decisive clash perfectly, leaving Side A's incumbent-focused defense fundamentally incomplete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing explanation of the "screening lottery" (B5.2-B5.4), which decisively neutralized Side A's claim that rent caps protect vulnerable renters from affluent newcomers by exposing the non-price rationing mechanisms landlords inevitably use in a supply-frozen market.
