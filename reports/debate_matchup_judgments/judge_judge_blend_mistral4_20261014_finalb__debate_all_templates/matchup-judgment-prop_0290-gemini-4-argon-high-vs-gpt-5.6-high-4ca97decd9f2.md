### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by anchoring the debate in a clear asymmetry: biometric identifiers are bodily and permanent, so without a convenient alternative, participation becomes coerced rather than chosen. CON’s strongest challenge—that unmanned retail and express shops would close—was specific and damaging, but remained narrow. PRO successfully showed that most venues can offer simple off-ramps (tap cards, apps, PINs) without duplicating full ecosystems, and that “shop elsewhere” collapses in captive environments like transit stations and stadiums. CON never fully neutralized the voluntariness and resilience burdens; the concession that some kiosks must “adapt or not operate” proved too costly against PRO’s framing that efficiency gains cannot justify compulsory bodily enrollment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal weaponizing PRO’s admission that biometric-dependent kiosks must close or change format, turning it into a “leveling down” argument where the mandate eliminates services for everyone rather than protecting objectors.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON wins on scope and leveling-down. PRO's strongest points on captive settings and irreversibility survive, but CON forces a concession that defines the debate: B admits a kiosk that needs biometrics for theft control must "not operate in that form" [B4.4][B5.3] and that a separate nearby shop doesn't count as an alternative [B4.1]. A turns that into a consumer-welfare loss - the objector still forfeits the purchase when the kiosk closes at midnight, and willing users lose 24-hour access or express service [A4.5][A5.2]. A also answers pressure directly with voluntary exchange/membership analogy and competition plus targeted monopoly regulation, while B's two clipped turns incur execution cost and leave the universal "always" requirement overbroad. The comparative weighing favors narrow governance over blanket mandate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 / Closing exploitation of B's "abandon that format" concession - showing PRO's rule levels down by shutting the late-night kiosk and banning express biometric-only shops even when card vendors are yards away, turning protection into universal loss.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side A won by strictly adhering to the text of the motion and dismantling Side B's attempt to substitute "market competition between different shops" for a "convenient non-biometric way to pay" within the same payment system. Side A's rebuttals regarding how walk-out stores can use app/QR credentials linked to sensor tracking effectively neutralized Side B's strongest practical objection, and Side A's willingness to bite the bullet on unviable biometric-only kiosks was philosophically consistent and well-justified.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1 and A2), which sharply distinguished between parallel payment channels at the same merchant versus entirely different merchants, and explained how sensor-based walk-out stores can verify identity via app/QR without needing biometrics.
