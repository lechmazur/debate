- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent causal stories, but the decisive exchange was the Pressure Questions round. B's answer (graduated civil sanctions, outpatient/residential care, "medical detention") effectively redefined the backstop as clinical and civil rather than criminal — a mechanism fully compatible with decriminalization. A's closing seized on this, arguing B had conceded that what punitive enforcement uniquely adds is criminalization itself, not treatment leverage. B's final closing reasserted the voluntary-vs-coerced framing but never squarely repaired this self-undermining concession, leaving A's reframe as the last word standing on the central clash. B's elasticity/initiation argument about aggregate overdose deaths was a reasonable counter that A answered only by assertion, keeping that sub-issue closer, but it wasn't enough to offset the structural damage done by the backstop concession. Both sides executed the Q&A format correctly and avoided fake precision, but A's rebuttal sequencing and closing crystallization were sharper and more damaging.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but B pressed harder and left fewer loose threads. A's case leaned almost entirely on Portugal and a timing/stigma mechanism, repeated across rounds with modest refinement (health-first contact, civil leverage alternatives). B's Q1 ("if leverage is the active ingredient, why did deaths fall when criminal penalties were removed?") was answered reasonably by noting Portugal's sanctions still had coercive teeth and predated fentanyl, and B's key structural point—that Europe never faced the fentanyl-driven supply contamination North America did—was never squarely rebutted by A, leaving the Portugal analogy weakened for the modern context that matters most for the resolution. Conversely, A's sharpest pressure question (how can "leverage" work if addiction destroys rational agency?) was cleverly answered by B reframing coercion as physical interruption via custody rather than rational choice, defusing the contradiction. B also introduced a structural enforcement-paralysis argument (loss of probable cause once possession is legal, enabling dealers to hide behind personal-use thresholds) that A's "scales, cash, communications" response only partially addressed and never fully neutralized by closing. B's closing crystallized the clash more sharply ("you cannot intervene early if the person in crisis simply walks away"), while A's closing largely restated its timing framing without engaging the unresolved probable-cause and fentanyl-supply points. This gives B the cleaner, more resilient path through the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to A's Pressure Q2 (A2), reframing criminal "leverage" as physical interruption of the user from supply rather than rational deterrence, resolved what looked like a fatal contradiction in B's case and kept its coercion argument alive through the final rounds.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by narrowing Side B’s case to reduced fear and avoided records, then arguing that Good Samaritan protections and expunging drug-court diversion can capture those benefits while retaining leverage for treatment. Side B’s Portugal comparison remained meaningful, but it never adequately resolved A’s distinctions about treatment expansion, civil sanctions, synthetic opioids, or the weak crime mechanism when supply remains illegal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: after Side B conceded that decriminalization does not make supply safer or cheaper, A directly compared targeted protections and diversion against voluntary referrals, substantially weakening B’s routes on both overdoses and downstream crime.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully distinguishing the modern fentanyl crisis from the pre-fentanyl Portugal model, and by introducing highly effective mechanical arguments about enforcement (loss of probable cause to search for dealing evidence) and addiction (physical interruption of the consumption cycle). Side A also neatly solved Side B's main downstream crime argument regarding criminal records by pointing to drug courts that expunge records upon completion. This left Side B's case reliant on an overly optimistic view of voluntary treatment for severe synthetic opioid addiction, which Side A convincingly dismantled using the Oregon example and the realities of withdrawal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, specifically the dismantling of Side B's "intent to supply" enforcement argument by pointing out that legalizing possession strips police of the probable cause needed to stop and search suspects for scales and cash, alongside the clear explanation of how criminal leverage physically interrupts the consumption cycle to restore agency.
