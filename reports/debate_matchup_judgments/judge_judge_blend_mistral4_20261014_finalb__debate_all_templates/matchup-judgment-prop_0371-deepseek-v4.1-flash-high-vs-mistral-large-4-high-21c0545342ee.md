### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating the problem of transparency from the proposed solution of forced bundling. By championing upfront fee disclosure as an alternative, Side A neutralized Side B's core grievances about deception while proving that mandated inclusion would force light travelers to subsidize heavy packers. Side A also decisively won the clash over physical scarcity in overhead bins.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal cleanly dismantled Side B's 'shared cost' analogy by pointing out that crew and lavatories are fixed infrastructure every flight needs, whereas bag volume is a variable cost and physical scarcity.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON)): PRO won by cleanly neutralizing CON's two strongest objections. CON argued that a "reasonable" baseline would create unmanageable litigation and compliance costs across aircraft types; PRO answered by showing the rule could simply piggyback on each airline's already-published, aircraft-specific sizer, deleting the surprise fee without inventing new dimensions. CON never recovered from this answer, instead repeating the litigation fear without engaging PRO's tailored solution. On bin scarcity, PRO reframed the fee as a wallet filter that does not shrink bags or prevent overflow, while a sizer-plus-one-bag rule actually controls physical volume. CON's reliance on price-as-rationing was undermined by PRO's pointed examples of late-boarding premium passengers losing laptops to fee-paying leisure travelers. CON's clipped first rebuttal and closing that largely recycled earlier claims without sharpening the weighing left their core argument about cross-subsidies and ultra-low-cost carriers underdeveloped, while PRO crystallized the debate around an elegant standard: a ticket that cannot carry the ordinary bag most trips require is not a cheaper ticket, it is an incomplete one.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer that a "reasonable" baseline can adopt each airline's already-published aircraft-specific sizer, directly defusing CON's pressure question about litigation and compliance costs while preserving operational flexibility.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO won the definitional and transparency clash. CON's best pressure was undefinable 'reasonable' and bin scarcity needing a price signal, but PRO answered in R2 by tying baseline to airlines' own published per-aircraft sizers and showing fee rations by wallet not volume. CON's disclosure alternative still advertises a fare most travelers cannot use, and its R1 was clipped for length, leaving subsidy and choice claims without full weighing. B made the vote about honest base price vs bait sticker more clearly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4 A1-A2 - reframes 'reasonable' as existing airline sizer per aircraft and reframes rationing as sizer+one-bag rule, directly neutralizing CON's litigation and tragedy-of-commons arguments.

### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully separating the problem of deceptive pricing from the proposed solution of a mandated bundle. Side A provided a cleaner alternative (all-in price disclosure) and successfully pressured Side B on the practical effects of a mandate, specifically cross-subsidies and bin scarcity. Side B's case became internally contradictory when they tried to defend the mandate while simultaneously claiming airlines could still offer stripped-down personal-item fares.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal decisively dismantled B's claims about bin scarcity, pointing out that making carry-ons free for everyone would logically increase gate-checking rather than solve it, and highlighting B's contradiction regarding personal-item fares.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side B won by constantly reframing the dispute as a ban on legitimate price differentiation rather than a transparency fix, showing that mandatory all-in price display fully cures the comparison-shopping harm while preserving choice. Side A’s “shell game” framing was vivid but never overcame the economic logic that bin space is scarce and that bundling forces backpackers to subsidize rollaboards. A closing concession that stripped-down personal-item fares could still survive also softened the mandate toward B’s disclosure alternative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s sustained demonstration that all-in price disclosure fully solves the apples-to-oranges comparison problem, making the carry-on mandate an unnecessary prohibition of a pricing structure rather than a remedy for deception.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won the core transparency vs prohibition clash. PRO's harm was apples-to-oranges comparison, which CON showed is cured by mandatory all-in display without forcing a bundle. PRO never neutralized the cross-subsidy point that personal-item-only travelers would pay for bins they don't use, and the scarcity argument that bundling increases bin demand. A1's pointed questions exposed the "reasonable" vagueness and subsidy issue; B's clipped R2 and weak anxiety-cost reply left those live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1 / A4.2- A4.3: CON seizes PRO's concession that disclosure lets travelers compare $180+$70 vs $250 all-in, reframes itemization as capacity pricing, and shows including carry-ons doesn't create bin space but increases demand and gate-checking.
