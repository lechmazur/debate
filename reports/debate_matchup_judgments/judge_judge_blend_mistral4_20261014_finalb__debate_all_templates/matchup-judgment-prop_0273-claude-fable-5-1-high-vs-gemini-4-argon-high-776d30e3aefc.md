### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that personalized mandatory prompts can include each family's selected bags and seating without abolishing stripped-down fares. Side B sharply exposed defaults and changing seat inventory, but treated a live comparison quote as if the motion required an immutable legal guarantee. It also never adequately answered A's central market point that separate fee columns leave headline rankings favoring teaser fares and impose substantial comparison friction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was answering that a family can select zero bags, toggle seating, and receive a live total just as base fares are quoted live today. This substantially neutralized B's bundling and volatility objections while preserving A's case for ranking options by practical cost.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing that the mandated full practical cost would rest on a padded default and moving seat inventory, making it a legally certified fiction. Side A’s market-distortion harm stayed live, but Side B showed the cure was conceptually broken for check-in seating policies and that standardized fee columns could fix the friction without manufacturing a binding price platforms could not honor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s second rebuttal (Turn 8), which forced Side A to own the pre-ticked seating default and the updating seat quote, then drove home the unresolved trilemma for family seating at check-in: price it at $0 and vouch for a certainty never sold, or price a paid seat and demote a family-friendly carrier.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Side A built a highly rigorous and logically airtight case against the mechanics of the mandate. By focusing on the word "full" and the legal and technical implications of mandating a single upfront number, Side A trapped Side B into conceding a "padded" default and the volatility of live seat maps. Side A's handling of the check-in carrier dilemma was particularly decisive, showing that the mandate would either force platforms to legally vouch for a certainty the airline never sold, or unfairly punish family-friendly airlines. Side B's intuitive case against drip pricing was strong, but they failed to defend their proposed mechanism against Side A's sharp, targeted rebuttals and pressure questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the check-in carrier dilemma and the exposure of Side B's "pre-checked" default as a mandated padded assumption, which proved the mechanism would fabricate a legally binding "full" price rather than simply disclosing fees.
