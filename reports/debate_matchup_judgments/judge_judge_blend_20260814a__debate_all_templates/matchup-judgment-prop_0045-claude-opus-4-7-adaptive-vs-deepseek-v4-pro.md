### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B won a competitive debate by keeping the focus on the actual tools named in the motion: categorical, unstable MBTI/DISC-style instruments that gain workplace authority despite weak predictive validity. Side A had a strong counterframe about proportionate use and realistic replacement by gut-feel hiring, but it leaned too heavily on assuming bad counterfactual adoption and on rescuing the tests through continuous, responsible use that sat uneasily with its own high-D/high-S team-building examples. Side B better answered that move by arguing that abandoning the labels abandons the product employers actually use, and that honest conversations or structured job-related methods can replace the tests without the false scientific stamp.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's most decisive rebuttal was to Side A's dimensional-scoring escape: B argued that the defended team-building vocabulary and hiring use depend on discrete type labels, so shifting to continuous scores concedes the unreliability of the product as actually used.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing a fatal concession in Side B's defence: to rescue MBTI and DISC from well-known retest instability, B argued employers should discard the discrete-type outputs and rely on continuous dimension scores. Because the motion specifically targets these branded type-based instruments, B's rescue effectively conceded that the products actually sold to employers are too unreliable to use. A crystallized this as a confession rather than a defence, and paired it with the stronger claim that pseudoscientific labels carry false authority and stereotyping harms that unstructured alternatives lack. B's comparative counterfactual frame was strategically sharp, but A successfully shifted the burden: when an instrument is itself an active source of misclassification and legal risk, removing it is a net gain without needing to prove perfect universal adoption of replacements.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's continuous-scoring rescue in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, showing that abandoning discrete types to save stability abandons the very "shared vocabulary" product employers buy, turning B's empirical fix into a concession that the instruments as marketed are unfit for high-stakes use.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins by successfully anchoring the debate to the realistic counterfactual of a ban. While PRO delivers sharp psychometric critiques (test-retest instability, binary typing), CON consistently forces PRO to defend what actually replaces these tools in median workplaces. PRO's reliance on idealized work samples without proving adoption leaves a critical gap, and the claim that "gut feel is preferable to pseudoscience" fails to overcome CON's audit-trail and bias-detection arguments. CON's "proportionate use" frame neutralizes PRO's worst-case caricatures, and CON's direct, structured responses to pressure questions clarify the comparative stakes. PRO's case identifies real flaws but never proves prohibition yields better net outcomes than regulated, light-weight use.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 & 2 turn PRO's replacement and audit-trail arguments by forcing the realistic counterfactual (manager intuition vs. documented light input) and clarifying that continuous/proportionate use is already standard among responsible employers, effectively collapsing PRO's prohibition burden.
