### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by making the central harm more concrete: behavioral targeting of minors requires surveillance profiles and exploits developmental vulnerability, while contextual advertising and platform-side duties provide a workable alternative. Side B had real pressure on paywalls, enforcement, and displacement into influencer marketing, but it mostly asserted collapse and never gave a specific reform mechanism strong enough to beat prohibition. A's repeated clipping is a cost, but B's alternative remained too vague and its visible-versus-invisible framing was less persuasive than A's point that removing data-driven profiling directly reduces the distinctive harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was turning B's influencer-displacement argument: if replacement channels lack the surveillance data and algorithmic optimization that make behavioral targeting uniquely dangerous, then banning the profiling infrastructure is meaningful even if advertising does not disappear entirely.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B wins decisively by capitalizing on a major execution error and landing the strongest rebuttal of the debate. Side A completely failed to answer Side B's Pressure Questions, improperly using the A1/A2 tags to attack Side B's answers instead, which left A's "smart regulation" alternative entirely undefended. Furthermore, Side B brilliantly turned Side A's core disadvantage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing turned Side A's warning about "influencer marketing" into a victory for the PRO side, pointing out that stripping away the surveillance-data infrastructure is the entire point of the ban.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully framing the unique harm of algorithmic data exploitation and turning CON’s core displacement argument. While CON effectively highlighted economic risks to low-income families and the danger of driving ads to unregulated channels, PRO consistently weighed the psychological and privacy harms as paramount and demonstrated that stripping the surveillance infrastructure inherently reduces the danger of any displaced advertising. PRO also provided a more concrete enforcement mechanism and handled pressure questions with direct A1/A2 responses that narrowed the clash. CON’s alternative of “smarter regulation” remained vague on enforcement after PRO’s COPPA pressure. Despite PRO suffering clipping penalties across multiple rounds, the substantive weighing and argument progression favor PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing turn on CON’s displacement argument. PRO concedes revenue may shift to influencers but argues this is a net win because influencer marketing lacks the terabytes of personal data and algorithmic optimization that make behavioral targeting uniquely dangerous. This successfully neutralizes CON’s primary offensive and reframes the ban’s core benefit as dismantling the surveillance infrastructure itself.
