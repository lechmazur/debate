PRO had the better rhetorical packaging and more memorable metaphors, but CON built the stronger path through argument progression and direct rebuttal of the highest-stakes clash.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins on two decisive and compounding grounds. First, CON correctly anchored the entire debate in the motion's "rather than" language — a replacement frame, not an additive one — and PRO never escaped it. PRO kept retreating to "core doesn't mean only" and "app controls are tools, not outcomes," but the proposition explicitly says "rather than tell parents to fight device battles," and PRO's own concrete safety examples ("no private messaging with strangers") require exactly the app-level feature knowledge the motion claims to retire. CON landed this contradiction cleanly in Rebuttal 1 [A2.5] and pressed it every round. Second, PRO's A2 answer to Side A's pressure question — "no guidance can see every dangerous post, including CON's" — was genuinely evasive. It equalized a failure rather than defending why developmental framing should be the *core* response. CON's displacement argument is real, but CON correctly showed it coexists with content-specific harms (predatory contact, algorithmic radicalization, self-harm content) that sleep schedules and free play cannot detect or address. PRO's displacement framing is the stronger intuitive case, and the whack-a-mole critique lands rhetorically, but PRO never resolved the structural self-contradiction its own examples introduced. Two clipped speeches (Opening and Rebuttal 2) are additional execution costs. CON's closing cleanly crystallized the live clash; PRO's closing acknowledged CON's content examples justify targeted intervention "when a known danger appears," which effectively conceded that app-level management is unavoidable — confirming rather than rebutting CON's core argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 [A2.5]: "Once PRO admits that broad rules about messaging and contact are needed, it has conceded that managing platform-specific features is unavoidable." This caught PRO in a genuine contradiction — PRO's own concrete example ("no private messaging with strangers") requires knowing which apps have messaging and how to disable it, i.e., exactly the app-level work the proposition says to abandon. PRO's A1 response tried to reframe this as a "broad principle" that travels across platforms, but CON correctly pointed out that implementation still depends on platform-specific knowledge. This exchange defined the rest of the debate and PRO never fully neutralized it.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): CON wins because it squarely neutralized PRO's core route by exposing that the "rather than" clause and PRO's own examples require the very app-level management PRO claims to replace, while specific content harms remain unaddressed by developmental framing alone. PRO's feasibility argument is undercut by this concession and by the lack of any mechanism for platform-specific risks. Clash narrows cleanly in B's favor after Pressure Questions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.5 and B4.4 directly damage PRO's migration and durability claims by forcing the admission that broad rules still require platform knowledge, turning PRO's operational examples into liabilities.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins by successfully exploiting the motion's "rather than" framing and exposing an internal contradiction in PRO's case: PRO's own safety examples (e.g., blocking stranger messaging) require the platform-specific knowledge PRO claims to replace. PRO's clipping in two turns slightly hampered its progression, but the decisive factor was CON's cleaner handling of the core clash and pressure questions.
