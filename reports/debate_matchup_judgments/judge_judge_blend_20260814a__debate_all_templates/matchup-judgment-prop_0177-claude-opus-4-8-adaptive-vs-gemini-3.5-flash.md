### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by making the motion's dual burden decisive: PRO had to prove both routine use within fifteen years and accelerated pathways now. Side B gave a plausible optimism story around CRISPR, pig scalability, and adaptive regulation, but relied heavily on assertion that early proof-of-concept cases and primate survival would translate into routine durable human use. Side A more effectively distinguished proof of function from routine decade-scale transplant success, turned the heart-transplant analogy, and argued that existing investigational pathways already permit human data, so acceleration adds little except pressure to outrun the evidence. On safety, Side B answered PERVs better than unknown pathogens, but Side A kept the zoonotic-risk asymmetry alive and weighed it more clearly against bounded waitlist harms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was the catch-22 rebuttal: recent human xenografts already occurred under current pathways, so PRO's claim that accelerated regulation is necessary to generate human data was substantially weakened.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A wins by establishing a decisive, unresolved asymmetry: waitlist deaths are tragic but bounded while an adapted porcine pathogen is unbounded and irreversible. A directly neutralized B’s catch-22, historical analogy, and pathogen claims with concrete counters that B never answered, making the risk comparison clean and the motion untenable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.4–A2.6 and A4.4 on the uncatalogued pathogen and the failed catch-22 rebuttal, which exposed that B’s safety defense only covered known sequences.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by dismantling PRO's central regulatory and safety cases. CON showed that first-in-human xenotransplantation is already proceeding under existing investigational pathways, collapsing PRO's "catch-22" claim that acceleration is needed to generate human data. CON also turned PRO's heart-transplant analogy against him by noting the subsequent moratorium and the unscheduled arrival of cyclosporine, undermining the fifteen-year guarantee. Most decisively, CON exposed that PRO's pathogen defense—gene editing and metagenomic sequencing—only addresses known threats, and that surveillance missing a porcine virus in an actual recipient proves the uncatalogued risk remains uncontrolled. CON's clean comparative weighing—bounded waitlist tragedy versus unbounded, irreversible pandemic risk—gave the clearer reason to vote CON.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to PRO's pressure questions, showing that recent first-in-human pig organ transplants already occurred under existing regulatory frameworks. This collapsed PRO's central "catch-22" argument that accelerated pathways are necessary to obtain human data, establishing that the bottleneck is biological maturity rather than paperwork and that "accelerate now" can only mean compressing safety review faster than the evidence justifies.
