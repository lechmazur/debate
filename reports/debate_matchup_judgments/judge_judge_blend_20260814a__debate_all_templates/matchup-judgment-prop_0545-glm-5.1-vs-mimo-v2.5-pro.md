### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won on the debate's decisive clash: the proposition's specific causal claim that "shifting to presumed consent" would measurably increase transplant rates. CON's Spain stagnation argument (flat rates for a decade under presumed consent alone, gains only after 1989 infrastructure investment) was specific, plausible, and never neutralized. PRO's "both-and" concession in Rebuttal 1 admitted that the default cannot be isolated from infrastructure—but that concession is more damaging than PRO acknowledged, because the proposition asks precisely about the shift itself. CON then crystallized an asymmetry PRO never answered: infrastructure without presumed consent produces gains; presumed consent without infrastructure does not. That asymmetry is fatal to the specific proposition. On ethics, CON's "extraction vs. return" distinction and the "consultation about objections is not consultation about donation" reframe consistently outperformed PRO's analogies (tax withholding, jury duty), which CON correctly identified as non-parallel because they don't involve taking tissue from a permanently silent person for redistribution to strangers. PRO's strongest card—behavioral defaults—was genuinely good, but PRO never tied it to a clean causal story that survived CON's infrastructure critique. PRO's "impossible standard" defense acknowledged it could not isolate the default's effect, which ceded the core ground the proposition required. CON's closing sharpened all of this without introducing new material, maintaining a clean argumentative line throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 identification that PRO's "both-and" concession undermines the proposition. By accepting that infrastructure and default work together, PRO implicitly admitted the default alone doesn't drive gains—exactly what Spain's 1979–1989 stagnation demonstrates. PRO never recovered a clean causal story after this exchange, and CON kept pressing the asymmetry through the closing.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by making the causal burden sharper: the proposition was about the default shift, and A repeatedly used Spain and the infrastructure point to show B was proving at most a reform package, not presumed consent itself. B had a plausible defaults-and-inertia mechanism, but conceded the effect often cannot be isolated and leaned on broad correlations. A also gave the cleaner ethical rebuttal, distinguishing B's analogies and arguing that consultation about objections is not real authorization. B's opt-out and family-relief framing kept it competitive, but did not fully neutralize the consent and distress concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was turning B's "both-and" infrastructure answer into a concession: if presumed consent needs coordinators, funding, and campaigns to work, then B has not proven the default shift itself measurably raises transplant rates.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side B won decisively by isolating the core causal claim and dismantling it using Side A's own primary example (Spain). By pointing out that Spain's rates were flat under presumed consent until infrastructure was added, Side B forced Side A to concede that the default alone cannot be isolated, which Side B brilliantly weaponized in the final speeches. Side B also systematically defeated all of Side A's ethical analogies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing weaponized Side A's concession about causal isolation, noting that infrastructure without presumed consent works, but presumed consent without infrastructure (Spain 1979-1989) does not.
