### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more responsive case: proxy discrimination plus algorithmic opacity plus a weak existing "reasonable factor other than age" standard, then used the Pressure Questions round to sharpen scope (distinguishing transparent, regulated traditional age-rating from opaque algorithmic exclusion) and to bite the bullet on the residual-signal question rather than dodge it. Crucially, A's closing pressed an evidentiary catch-22 — an isolated rejected applicant cannot show the systemic statistical disparity needed to trigger the discovery tools B relies on — and B's closing never answered this, instead repeating the adverse-selection/cross-subsidization argument verbatim. B's economic mechanism (age as a genuine risk/tenure signal, market exit if age is blinded) was solid and grounded, and A's rebuttal to it was more assertion than mechanism, but this was a partial wash rather than a full answer gap. B also suffered a real execution cost from a clipped opening that cut off part of its constructive case. On balance, A answered pressure more directly, adapted its scope precisely, and landed the decisive unrebutted closing point, giving it a clear but not overwhelming edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing point (A5.2–A5.4) that a lone rejected applicant cannot produce the aggregate statistical disparity needed to trigger B's proposed discovery-based remedy (B4.2) was the most damaging exchange — B's case leaned heavily on "existing discovery tools already pierce the black box," and this went unanswered in B's final speech.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by identifying the enforcement catch-22 at the heart of Side B’s reliance on existing law: an excluded individual often lacks the data needed to establish the disparity required to obtain discovery. That gave proactive audits and stronger transparency rules a clear justification. Side B persuasively showed that blanket bans on residual age signals could reduce accuracy and harm insurance markets, but it overstated the necessity of all such proxies and never adequately answered how hidden systemic disparities are initially detected. Its opening was also clipped and one rebuttal used erroneous reference labels.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s closing response to Side B’s discovery argument was decisive: saying courts can compel algorithmic records after a disparity is shown does not explain how an isolated applicant, denied access to applicant pools and model features, can show that disparity in the first place.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (B) won the central clash on detectability and proxy laundering. CON (A) argued residual age signals improve accuracy and existing discovery suffices, but B's pressure Q1/Q2 exposed that direct skill measures make graduation-year proxies unnecessary and that ghosted applicants cannot trigger discovery without data. B's closing crystallized this catch-22, while A's clipped opening and reliance on cross-subsidy/exit fears never answered the black-box detection problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q2 on black-box invisibility → closing catch-22: if plaintiffs must show statistical disparity to get discovery but are ghosted by proprietary screening with no access to applicant pools or feature weights, CON's claim that ordinary tools already pierce the black box collapses.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON's core distinction—that age, unlike race or sex, is causally linked to actuarial risk and skill recency—went largely unanswered in substance. PRO's key mitigation (swap "near-perfect proxies" like medical-history length for "current biomarkers" or snapshot data) was directly and effectively attacked by CON's medical-reasoning rebuttal (two people with identical current labs can have very different multi-year mortality risk due to cumulative history), and PRO's closing never rebuilt that ground. CON's quota-incentive mechanism (litigation costs make hardcoded parity the rational corporate response) was asserted with plausible causal reasoning and never dismantled beyond conclusory assertions ("firms that use validated factors pass"). PRO's rebuttals were solid at knocking down the "outlaw actuarial science" strawman framing, but failed to close the deeper gap CON kept pressing: how do firms defend legitimate, age-correlated predictors without either accepting quotas or accepting crippling litigation risk. That unresolved tension is the decisive thread, and CON controlled it more consistently across rounds.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1/2 sequence exposing that PRO's own example ("medical-history length") is simultaneously called an illegal proxy and later replaced with "current biomarkers," which CON then shows is medically inadequate for capturing longitudinal mortality risk—a point PRO never fully answered in the closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the unresolved boundary between prohibited age proxies and legitimate time-based predictors. A established a plausible accountability gap and correctly resisted the quota straw man, but its proposed substitutes for longitudinal medical information did not adequately answer B’s risk-accuracy objection. B overstated quota inevitability, yet created the sharper practical pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s biomarker answer was decisive: it argued that current clinical snapshots cannot fully replace longitudinal information when predicting mortality. That directly weakened A’s claimed non-quota route for insurance, although B was less persuasive in extending the same logic categorically to hiring and lending.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins on the decisive actuarial distinction. PRO establishes a real accountability gap for black-box proxies but never solves the pressure that age directly causes risk and time-based experience. Its answer that current biomarkers beat medical-history length is implausible and was sharply countered by A's 70 vs 25 example. A sustains the Catch-22: under liability for uncorrected patterns any accurate model skews older, so firms face quota incentives, which PRO never refutes with a workable non-quota path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4's second rebuttal - exposing that snapshot labs cannot replace cumulative history and that identical competence signals will still trigger liability, cementing the quota inevitability.
