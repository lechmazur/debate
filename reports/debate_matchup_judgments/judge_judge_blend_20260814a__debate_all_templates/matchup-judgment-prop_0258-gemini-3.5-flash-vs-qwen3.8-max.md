- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by confronting the motion’s explicit tradeoff: when accessibility actually costs precision, broader wording reduces advance notice and transfers policy choices to judges, agencies, and prosecutors. Side A also offered a credible middle path through summaries, publication reforms, safe harbors, and reliance protections.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange showed that a readable standard such as “unreasonable pollution” may be easier to parse yet harder to apply prospectively than a technical threshold. This directly reversed Side B’s loophole argument: wealthy actors can litigate broad concepts like reasonableness and purpose, while precise lines better constrain enforcement discretion.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON won by locking the clash on the motion’s real zone—plainness when precision is sacrificed—and showing that readable generality widens enforcement discretion, worsens advance notice for ordinary parties, and helps sophisticated actors argue “reasonableness.” PRO had a strong democratic-consent frame and effective attacks on pure legalese and powerless summaries, but never neutralized CON’s alternative (guides, indexed definitions, safe harbors) or proved that required less-precise binding text beats constrained precise text. CON’s answers on litigation margins and estoppel/safe harbors closed the key pressure points more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R1/R2 turn that precise thresholds narrow margins while “reasonable”/principle drafting widens judicial-agency gap-filling and favors elite argument over lay notice.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): CON won the trade-off frame that the motion requires. PRO's democratic-access and regressive-tax case is powerful, but CON showed sacrificing precision widens the litigation margin, transfers hard choices to inspectors/judges, and hurts tenants/small businesses who need bright lines. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by separating plainness from vagueness and showing that formulas, standards, exceptions, and technical detail can remain while operative commands become readable. B also gave the stronger democratic weighing: summaries do not cure inaccessible binding law, and marginal precision is less valuable when citizens cannot discover their duties without specialists.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the blueprint analogy was decisive: passengers need not obey blueprints, whereas citizens must obey statutes. Combined with B’s explanation that ordinary grammar can coexist with schedules, formulas, and technical standards, this substantially weakened A’s central equation of accessibility with structural imprecision.

A’s core route (plain language equals elastic ambiguity that helps the wealthy and judges) was repeatedly damaged by B’s counters that technical opacity already creates loopholes, that plain text can still state operative duties, and that summaries cannot substitute for the controlling statute. A answered pressure but never neutralized the legitimacy burden; B adapted across rounds and left the cleaner reason to affirm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 answers (A1/A2) turning A’s loophole and agency-delegation pressure into evidence that opacity, not plainness, feeds specialist power.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO won the central trade-off: A showed precision != complexity, that opacity creates the loophole market CON fears, and that plain drafting can keep schedules/formulas while stating duties in ordinary grammar.
