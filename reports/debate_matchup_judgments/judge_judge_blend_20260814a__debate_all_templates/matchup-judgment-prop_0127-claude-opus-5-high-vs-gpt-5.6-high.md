### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON effectively neutralized PRO's donation mandate by identifying charity capacity as the binding constraint, forcing PRO to concede that the rule only requires a documented attempt. CON then dismantled the discounting mandate by showing how a regulator-defined window removes the unpredictability of voluntary markdowns, leading to cannibalized sales and selectively bare shelves.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 explanation that a mandated discount window converts an unpredictable private judgment into a public promise, causing shoppers to wait for the category and forcing stores to understock perishables.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON won by turning PRO’s own mechanism answers into the decisive clash. After PRO conceded recipient consent and last-resort disposal, CON’s donor-side dilemma (paperwork non-bite vs forced absorption) landed; when PRO then specified a regulator half-price final window, CON cleanly showed that converts voluntary unannounced markdowns into a planned category event, driving selective understocking of perishables and empty late shelves. PRO kept a live externality/timing case and fair dual-route replies, but never neutralized that adverse-incentive comparison or the cleaner alternatives (price landfill, fund cold chains). Clipping hurt both slightly; CON still made the easier vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 on B’s regulator-set half-price final window: published uniform schedule destroys markdown unpredictability and causes selective thinner ordering.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B wins the central dilemma: if disposal remains lawful after a genuine offer, the mandate is paperwork; if it bites, PRO's own answer in R2 turns it into a regulator-set floor (half-price final window) that is a published uniform price event. B shows why that matters - voluntary markdowns work because timing is unpredictable, but a statutory schedule lets shoppers wait for the category, prompting selective thin ordering on spoilage lines and hurting late shoppers. A never rehabilitates the marginal-loaf timing gain against that price-control cost, and the landfill-tax alternative addresses waste more broadly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 A2 unpacking category-waiting vs item-waiting: publishing a half-price final window destroys the unpredictability that made voluntary markdowns safe, so managers will order thinner on salad/fruit/meals and leave the bin empty because the shelf was.
