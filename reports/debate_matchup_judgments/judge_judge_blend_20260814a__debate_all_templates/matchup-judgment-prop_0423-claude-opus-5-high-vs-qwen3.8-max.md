### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B isolated the decisive issue: what geographic preference adds once both sides accept protected ballots, identity checks, and resale controls. A established a plausible host-community claim but never adequately connected an opt-in ticket ballot to compensation for burdened neighbors. A also conceded that shrinking the dynamic pool can raise remaining prices while inconsistently claiming the organizer, rather than outside buyers, bears that cost. B’s clipped rebuttal incurred a modest execution penalty, but its core analysis was complete and carried through a focused closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to A’s second pressure answer was decisive: it showed that identity safeguards make a reserve enforceable but do not justify a postcode filter, then exposed the contradiction between admitting higher general-sale prices and denying that remaining buyers fund the preference.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): B won by forcing the debate onto the only residual dispute after shared ground (pre-surge reserves, ID-locks, resale caps, ballots) and showing the postcode filter is pure exclusion: it does no anti-scalping work, reaches opt-in local fans rather than aggrieved neighbors, and shifts cost onto distant buyers. A’s legitimacy case stayed live but never repaired the mechanism gap or incidence collision. B’s R2 clip was a real cost yet did not erase the cleaner comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s [B2.1–B2.2] turn: PRO’s anti-arbitrage bolt-ons are B’s instruments and make locality redundant, leaving only exclusion.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won by narrowing the entire debate to a single comparative question—"what does the postcode add?"—and showing that every legitimate goal PRO named (anti-scalping, affordability, reserved access before surge pricing) could be achieved by identity-linked, resale-capped, open ballots without geographic exclusion. CON demonstrated that PRO's anti-arbitrage safeguards made the locality filter redundant rather than necessary, and that the social-license mechanism failed to reach aggrieved non-fan neighbors because opt-in ballots self-select fans who already live nearby. PRO's responses on incidence were contradictory (conceding the dynamic pool clears higher while claiming the organizer "absorbs" the cost), and PRO's concession that the live obligation is residency—while member presales already happen voluntarily—stripped the motion down to a mandatory postcode filter. CON's closing crystallized that this filter changes nothing but who is shut out, leaving PRO without a compelling reason to prefer address-based sorting over need or devotion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's sustained attack that PRO's own anti-scalping mechanisms (identity-linked entry, purchase limits, face-value transfer) render the geographic carve-out redundant, crystallized in the closing frame that the motion reduces to "a border drawn around a stadium." This shifted the burden onto PRO to justify why address should outrank devotion or need, and PRO never recovered.
