CON's rhetoric was vivid and its demographic concerns plausible, but it never closed the gap PRO's pressure question opened.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-organized cases, but B did more damage in the decisive exchange. B's pressure questions forced A into a real tension: A wants to endorse housing, childcare, and parental leave as remedies for "constrained desire" while insisting childlessness is never the thing being solved. B's closing crystallized this cleanly—either those policies only aid people already resolved to parent (in which case they don't address the demographic strain A concedes is real), or they nudge reluctant/undecided adults toward parenthood (in which case A is treating aggregate childlessness as a problem while disputing only the label). A's answers (A4.1–A4.2) restated the "constrained desire vs. defect" distinction without fully closing this logical gap, and B's B4.1 answer usefully showed what "solving childlessness" without individual shaming could look like (cohort-based planning), directly meeting A's own pressure question. A's free-rider rebuttal (taxes, elder care, reciprocity) was strong and somewhat blunted B's selfishness argument, but B's demographic-mechanics point about intergenerational dependence was never fully dismantled, only contested. Overall B narrowed the debate more effectively and left the cleaner, harder-to-answer contradiction on the table at the close.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.4–B4.5) and Closing (B5.1–B5.2), which turned A's own endorsement of childcare/housing/leave policies into a dilemma: either those remedies don't address the admitted demographic strain, or they concede that childlessness itself is being treated as a problem, contradicting the motion's second clause.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully attacking the absolute nature of the proposition. Side B trapped Side A into conceding that low birth rates cause demographic strain, and then effectively demonstrated that Side A's proposed policy solutions (like parental leave) are implicitly treating aggregate childlessness as a problem to solve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Turn 8 rebuttal decisively dismantled Side A's attempt to separate "helping willing parents" from "solving childlessness," pointing out that if those policies don't shift reluctant adults, they fail to solve the demographic strain Side A admitted exists.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully defending both halves of the motion with sharp, memorable framing. A decisively neutralizes B's "free-rider" argument by demonstrating that societal reciprocity runs both ways—childfree taxes fund the schools, leave, and child credits that raise the future workers B claims they are free-riding on. On the demographic clash, B sets an excellent trap regarding family policy, but A maintains a clean, persuasive distinction between "removing friction for desired parenthood" and "pathologizing childlessness." A effectively argues that systemic aging strains can be engineered around (pensions, immigration, productivity) without treating non-reproduction as a societal disease. A's closing crystallization ("Keep the engineering. Drop the diagnosis") provided the cleaner comparative framework to resolve the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of the free-rider argument, pointing out that childfree taxpayers heavily fund the very schools, pediatric care, and child credits that raise the future workers B claims they are free-riding on, effectively flipping the ledger.
