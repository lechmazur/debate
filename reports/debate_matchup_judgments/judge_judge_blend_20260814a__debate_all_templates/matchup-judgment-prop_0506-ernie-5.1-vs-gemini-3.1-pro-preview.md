### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing and exploiting PRO's admitted incentive trap: refusing verification avoids liability, turning the rule into a blueprint for willful blindness rather than safety. CON also better distinguished objective mechanisms (CSAM, DMCA) from subjective AI judgments and showed how the standard would drive mass over-removal or corporate ignorance while fraudsters adapt. PRO kept a clean trigger narrative but never neutralized the contradiction or the uniqueness of subjective verification pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's A1/A2 answers in rebuttal 2 plus closing, where CON turned PRO's own "escape hatch" language into decisive proof of perverse incentives.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B won by exposing a structural flaw in PRO's liability mechanism: because liability only attaches after verified reports, platforms can avoid liability entirely by refusing to verify—a perverse incentive PRO repeatedly treated as a feature. B also successfully distinguished AI impersonation from CSAM/copyright verification, showed that litigation would target individual missed variants, and demonstrated that the standard would incentivize either mass censorship or corporate blindness. CON sharpened the clash while PRO's closing doubled down on the concession that sank their case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing turn on PRO's escape-hatch logic, showing that making liability contingent on verification creates a rational incentive for platforms to dismantle verification teams entirely, converting PRO's proposed safety mechanism into a reward for willful blindness.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON wins by decisively exposing a fatal perverse incentive in PRO's model. PRO repeatedly argued that platforms could avoid liability by simply refusing to verify reports, explicitly calling non-verification an insulating "escape hatch." CON correctly identified that this concession completely undermines the proposition's goal, as it legally rewards corporate blindness and incentivizes platforms to dismantle verification systems rather than address scams. CON also effectively distinguished AI verification from PRO's CSAM/copyright analogies by highlighting the lack of objective hash databases for synthetic media, making subjective context analysis legally risky and prone to over-removal. PRO never adequately resolved how their policy prevents platforms from choosing willful ignorance, leaving CON with a clear, unneutralized route to victory on both incentives and collateral damage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's response in A4.3-A4.4 and closing A5.1-A5.3, which seized on PRO's admission that refusing to verify insulates platforms from liability. CON turned PRO's "escape hatch" framing into a devastating proof that the policy structurally incentivizes the exact willful blindness it claims to solve, collapsing PRO's core mechanism.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.3** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): B wins by directly answering pressure questions on verification and monetization, establishing a workable third-party notice threshold while showing CON's over-moderation fears apply only to actual scams; A's responses remained vague on the standard and failed to neutralize the chokepoint incentive argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 directly answering Q1/Q2 on third-party verification and chokepoint compensation.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won by defending a narrow, workable duty of care: platforms that ignore repeated, concrete third-party proof of synthetic financial fraud should face civil liability. CON raised real worries about vague standards and over-moderation, but largely asserted that "verified" is undefinable rather than engaging PRO's model of competent third-party verification paired with a high threshold. PRO's market-failure and accessible-chokepoint framing stayed live after the exchange, while CON's alternative of better criminal enforcement was asserted, not developed. A's rebuttals directly answered B's pressure questions, whereas B's answers to A begged the question by using existing ambiguity to reject the proposed standard.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal (A4), answering CON's pressure questions by clarifying that "verified" reports come from competent third parties and that platforms are liable precisely because they are the accessible chokepoint when criminal enforcement fails, neutralizing CON's "litigation nightmare" and "shell game" attacks.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by successfully defending the workability of the "repeated, verified reports" threshold and effectively reframing the purpose of civil liability. While Side A (CON) consistently attacked the ambiguity of "verified" and warned of over-moderation, PRO's clarification that verification comes from competent third parties (banks, AGs) pre-litigation, combined with the explicit distinction between civil compensation/deterrence and criminal apprehension, neutralized CON's core efficacy and feasibility attacks. PRO also better mitigated the chilling effect concern by emphasizing the motion's narrow focus on financial fraud and high evidentiary bar. CON's case, though coherent, relied heavily on a slippery slope that PRO's strict threshold adequately addressed, and CON's closing largely repeated earlier points without resolving the clash on PRO's terms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 directly answers CON's pressure questions by clarifying that third parties verify reports before litigation and explicitly conceding that civil liability doesn't catch criminals but instead compensates victims and forces platforms to close scam storefronts. This reframing cleanly severed CON's link between platform liability and criminal enforcement, securing PRO's route to victory.
