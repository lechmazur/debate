B dismissed this as a straw man but never engaged with the substance. On slippery slope, B's Q2 was genuinely sharp, but A answered it with alcohol (comparably massive death toll), and B never returned to contest that answer in closing. B's closing was short, repetitive, and did not sharpen any of the live clashes. A's enforcement and New Zealand Indigenous concerns were never fully answered either. B had a compelling narrative about cyclical age-restriction failure and tobacco's unique lethality, but the technical execution of rebuttals was too thin to close the gap opened by A's stronger comparative analysis.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 response to B's Q1 (A4.1–A4.2): A correctly identified that the banned cohort actually grows as post-cutoff births age into adulthood, providing a stable and expanding illicit customer base for 60+ years, directly dismantling B's core claim that the black market starves as the legal market contracts. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO built and maintained a structurally coherent case across the full exchange: the generational ban is the only policy that shrinks the legal supplier pool every year, whereas conventional age limits permanently add new adult suppliers with each generation. That mechanism was clearly articulated in the opening, defended in rebuttals, and crystallized in the closing without meaningful degradation. CON's three main lines each had vulnerabilities that PRO exploited. First, CON's repeated invocation of New Zealand as proof of failure was damaged early and never repaired: PRO correctly identified the reversal as a governing-coalition change, not a policy-effectiveness finding, and CON continued citing it without addressing that rebuttal. Second, CON's slippery-slope argument collapsed when PRO drew a defensible harm-threshold distinction—no other common legal product is intentionally engineered to be addictive and kills half its long-term users as intended—and CON's rebuttal essentially conceded no other product fits the profile before retreating to abstract autonomy concerns, which weakened rather than strengthened the argument. Third, CON's best original move—that retail constriction is upstream of social supply, so targeting the 20% retail channel indirectly cuts the 80% social channel—was introduced in Rebuttal 2 (A4.1) but underdeveloped and unsupported compared to the Singapore anecdote that carried too much weight. Singapore shows a ceiling, not a mechanism that directly rebuts the generational-ban logic. PRO's pressure questions were sharper and exposed CON's 80/20 tension more cleanly. CON answered their own questions adequately (A4 used correct labeling), but the answers did not recover the NZ problem or fully neutralize the structural supply-shrinkage argument. PRO's path to victory—only this policy eliminates intergenerational social supply structurally—remained live after the exchange; CON's alternatives were plausible but not decisive rebuttals to that structural claim.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's early identification that New Zealand's smokefree generation reversal was a governing-coalition political choice rather than evidence of policy failure (B2.2) was the most decisive rebuttal exchange. CON cited New Zealand repeatedly across multiple speeches as its primary real-world proof of unworkability, but PRO's explanation was accurate to background knowledge of events (the Luxon-led coalition reversed it on ideological grounds, not failed impact evidence), and CON never directly answered this. Returning to the same example without addressing the rebuttal was an execution cost that weakened CON's empirical grounding for its central enforcement claim throughout the rest of the debate.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built the stronger case by repeatedly showing that the generational ban leaves the dominant 80% social supply channel intact for decades while Singapore-style retail enforcement delivers immediate reductions without the arbitrary birth-year inequality; PRO never neutralized this timeline and effectiveness gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2.1/A4.1 directly exposed that every living adult remains a supplier for 30-50 years, collapsing PRO's shrinking-pool mechanism on its own timeframe.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO wins because it established that conventional age restrictions permanently replenish the pool of legal adult suppliers feeding the 80% social supply chain, while a generational ban structurally eliminates that vector over time. CON's alternative of strict retail enforcement was shown to reach only the 20% direct retail leakage without a convincing mechanism to dismantle social sharing from legal adults, and CON's reliance on New Zealand as proof of unworkability was successfully undermined by PRO's observation that the policy was reversed for political reasons before full implementation. CON's autonomy and slippery-slope objections lost comparative force against PRO's tightly defended framing of tobacco as a uniquely harmful, intentionally addictive product with zero safe use.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's rebuttal demonstrating that CON's preferred retail-ID strategy only targets the 20% store-based supply while leaving the 80% social supply chain intact, whereas the generational ban shrinks the legal supplier pool over time and automates enforcement.
