### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON built the stronger cumulative case across all five rounds. Three moves proved decisive. First, the knife/firearm dissection (A2.3–A2.5) showed concretely that "plain" words like "firearms and knives" still generate the same edge-case problems PRO attributed only to technical jargon, directly undermining PRO's central conflation argument. Second, CON's safe-harbor alternative (precise operative statute plus reliance-protected plain-language guidance) absorbed PRO's best argument—the "pamphlet trap"—without conceding the motion; PRO never dismantled this model, only restating that the map is not the territory without explaining why a reliance-protected safe harbor is insufficient. Third, the "margin is the law" reframe (A4.3) was the debate's most decisive analytical move: PRO conceded that uncertainty at the fringes was acceptable, but CON showed that legal coercion happens precisely at those fringes, so calling the sacrifice "minor" does not neutralize it. PRO's strongest moment was the pamphlet-trap argument (B2.1–B2.2), which was genuinely effective and forced CON to develop the safe-harbor response, but that response held up under pressure. PRO also partially retreated from the motion by insisting plain language is not the same as vague language—which raised the question of what precision was actually being sacrificed, and CON exploited this consistently. Both pressure question rounds were pointed, but CON's Q2 (who supplies the missing precision?) was sharper, and PRO's answer in B4.2—courts apply ordinary public understanding—effectively conceded CON's democratic-accountability concern by admitting that unelected judges fill the gaps. Both sides had clipped speeches, so neither is penalized asymmetrically for that. On balance, CON offered a cleaner through-line, a more concrete counter-model, and progressively narrowed the clash in its favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.3 ("for that person, the margin is the law") was the most decisive single exchange. It directly answered PRO's A1 concession that uncertainty at "fringe scenarios" is acceptable, showing that legal coercion falls exactly at those margins, making the concession fatal rather than minor. PRO never recovered from this reframe in the closing.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by establishing that precision defines enforceable boundaries limiting government power, using the knife/firearm edge cases and margin pressure to show sacrifices hit exactly where coercion occurs; A directly answered B's questions on safe harbors and notice without evasion, offering workable alternatives like guides while B's responses on judicial ordinary-meaning fallback and "minor" uncertainty failed to neutralize the democratic cost of post-legislative discretion or rebut the map-versus-territory distinction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's rebuttal answers to B's pressure questions on safe harbors (A4.1) and determinate notice (A4.2), plus the margin-is-the-law point, which directly damaged B's core accessibility-over-precision framing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully exposed that PRO's case retreated from the motion's core trade-off by demonstrating that legal coercion operates at the margins where precision matters most. PRO's rebuttals were strong but never fully recovered from the concrete demonstration that binding plain language invites the very uncertainty and elite litigation it claims to prevent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal (B2), which charged PRO with retreating from the motion's required sacrifice and deployed the firearm/knife example to show that contested edge cases are not "pedantic" distractions but the actual boundaries of state coercion.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the stronger path to victory across the full exchange. The most decisive move came in Rebuttal 2: PRO correctly identified that "14 days' written notice via certified mail"—CON's central example—is already plain language, which defused CON's best illustration of the precision/clarity tradeoff and showed the real tradeoff is only between marginal edge-case refinement and core intelligibility, not between functional bright lines and fuzzy standards. CON never recovered from this parry; subsequent speeches kept insisting PRO "ducked hard cases" without demonstrating a concrete case where necessary precision was actually incompatible with accessibility. PRO's timing argument—that law should guide conduct before harm, not merely arm lawyers after—was never squarely answered; CON's response ("appellate parsing is how citizens defeat arbitrary state action") effectively conceded that CON's model is remedial rather than preventive. PRO also won the power-shift clash by showing that when citizens cannot read statutes, discretion shifts earlier and less visibly to lawyers and administrators, meaning CON's world doesn't actually protect citizens better, it just relocates interpretive power. CON's source-code analogy was memorable and original, but PRO successfully distinguished private software from public coercive commands, undermining it. CON's "false sense of security" concern was genuine but unresolved against PRO's claim that bounded standards with examples and factors preserve meaningful predictability at the center. On weighing, PRO consistently explained why front-end knowability matters more than back-end precision for the majority of governed people; CON never answered the comparative weighing question about how many ordinary compliance cases improve versus how many edge cases are harmed. CON's execution was clean and its source-code framing was rhetorically effective, but it lost the decisive clashes on the specific example, the timing/front-end question, and the power-shift comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2: showing that "14 days via certified mail" is already plain language and would be preserved under plain-language drafting, thereby collapsing CON's central illustration of the precision-versus-clarity tradeoff and reframing the real sacrifice as only marginal edge-case refinement—a move CON never fully answered.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built the stronger overall case by framing precision as the citizen's objective restraint against arbitrary power and showing how the mandated sacrifice creates worse power shifts to unelected officials via new ambiguity, consistently neutralized PRO's secondary-materials rebuttal and false-security risks. PRO advanced a credible timing and knowability emphasis but could not overcome the resolution's explicit precision trade-off or CON's sharper closing contrast of shields versus blurry maps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The A rebuttals reversing the power-shift argument plus B's A1/A2 answers distinguishing marginal precision from obscurity, which sharpened the core clash on timing and citizen protection.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO successfully reframed the tradeoff: the sacrifice is not bright-line exactitude but marginal edge-case refinement that obscures the central duty for ordinary citizens. CON’s "source code" and "blurred map" analogies were rhetorically sharp, but PRO showed that law is public coercion rather than private software, and that unreadable precision shifts power to interpreters and agencies before citizens ever reach a courtroom. CON never proved that specialized domains inherently require inaccessible prose, and its safe-harbor alternative conceded that citizens cannot read the binding law itself. PRO’s timing argument—maximizing knowability before harm rather than precision after it—provided the cleaner comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 distinction between technical concepts and technical obscurity, showing that bright-line rules like "14 days" are already plain while the real cost is incomprehensible density, decisively blunted CON’s ambiguity critique.
