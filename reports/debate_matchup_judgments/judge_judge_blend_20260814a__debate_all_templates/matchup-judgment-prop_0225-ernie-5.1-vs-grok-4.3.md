### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but Side A (CON) built a slightly stronger path to victory across the full exchange. The decisive contributions were: (1) the "false binary" attack in Rebuttal 2 [A4.1-A4.2]—PRO's only concrete mechanism was a cap, and CON named plausible alternatives (career services, employer partnerships, curriculum reform), forcing PRO to either defend caps as uniquely necessary or concede the instrument is blunt. PRO's closing rebuttal—"alternatives still leave enrollment mismatch intact"—was abstract and never engaged with why those tools had failed specifically; (2) the teachers-social-workers-community-organizers example [A4.4], raised in the opening and sharpened in Rebuttal 2, identified a concrete category of graduates whose careers show up as "low employment in major" but represent genuine social value; PRO never directly answered this, and it materially undermined PRO's assumption that the employment metric is reliable enough to justify caps; (3) the equity argument [A1.3, A2.4] retained residual force because PRO's reframe—that steering students toward higher-employment tracks is the real equity—does not address students who deliberately choose teaching or social work, where the market systematically underprices the contribution. PRO's strongest moments were the well-targeted pressure questions (especially Q1, which exposed that CON's alternative mechanisms are generic) and the clean framing that the motion restricts expansion rather than eliminating departments. But PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 was evasive on cross-subsidies, and PRO never overcame the specific counterexample of community-service careers being mislabeled as low-yield. On net, CON left more live arguments on the table after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's [A4.1-A4.2] "false binary" attack in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive single move: CON named concrete alternatives to caps (internships, employer partnerships, curriculum reform), labeled PRO's inability to engage those alternatives as "surrender," and thereby shifted the burden back to PRO to explain why caps specifically were necessary. PRO's closing response—"alternatives leave enrollment mismatch intact"—was too abstract to neutralize the specific tools CON listed, leaving a visible gap in PRO's instrumental case.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate centered on the motion: finite public subsidies, persistent low-employment outcomes, and the student harm of debt and underemployment. A's alternatives like internships and curriculum reform were plausible but underdeveloped and did not fully answer the funding tradeoff or national shortage comparison.

That directly undercut A's central claim that caps harm the very students they claim to protect.
