### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO wins on razor-thin margin. PRO's strongest live arguments at debate's end were (1) the ideological pattern—Pulitzer winners and Holocaust memoirs appearing on the same challenge lists as genuinely explicit YA content, which CON never satisfactorily explained away with "review committees handle it" because the point was about why those books were challenged at all; and (2) the chilling effect on librarians buying diverse titles, which CON attempted to reframe as mere "accountability" but never directly argued why that deterrent stops at explicitly sexual content rather than extending to any politically inconvenient book. CON won several exchanges clearly: the challenge/removal analytical distinction was genuinely important and PRO never squared with it, the "doesn't need to stop every grab" admission was turned into a damaging "acceptable collateral damage" framing, and the content-filter comparison in closing was clean and effective. CON also correctly noted PRO was conflating challenges with removals throughout. The reason PRO still holds the edge is that the motion explicitly covers both challenges and removals, so the ideological breadth of the challenge culture—CON's own concession that review committees must reject some challenges because they are inappropriate—is itself evidence the system has "gone too far." PRO's pre-emptive concession in the opening also set a durable framing the proposition never abandoned. B's clipped Rebuttal 1 is a minor execution cost. On balance, PRO's combination of vivid stakes-framing and two live unrebutted threads (pattern motivation, chilling scope) narrowly outweighs CON's sharper analytical rebuttal work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's second rebuttal turning PRO's own statement—"doesn't need to stop every grab"—into a "staggering failure of safeguarding" and "acceptable collateral damage" framing. This was the debate's sharpest single exchange: it used PRO's literal concession to recast PRO's entire alternative (professional guidance, better shelving) as structurally inadequate rather than merely suboptimal, forcing PRO onto the defensive about whether younger students are genuinely protected under its preferred system.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): B won by making the cleanest comparative distinction: a challenge list is not the same as a removal, and curated school spaces need not provide unrestricted access to mature material. A had a compelling overbreadth and ideology case, but leaned heavily on asserted patterns and did not sufficiently answer B's practical safeguard point. A's final turn also overclaimed that removal is pointless because material exists elsewhere, which B effectively exposed as a weak standard for school guardrails.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive rebuttal was that A blurred challenges with removals: review committees can reject challenges to classics while still removing genuinely explicit material from age-bracketed spaces. A never fully neutralized that distinction.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully distinguishing between initial challenges and actual removals, framing the administrative review process as a functional democratic safeguard rather than an ideological purge. CON effectively weaponized PRO’s admission that librarian guidance “doesn’t stop every grab” to win the practical safeguarding clash, arguing that in captive, shared school environments, removal or strict restriction is a proportional duty to younger students. While PRO (Side B) articulated strong equity and chilling-effect arguments, CON’s clearer institutional framing, direct answers to pressure questions, and effective closing analogy (comparing library curation to school Wi-Fi filters) provided a cleaner, more decisive path to victory. The minor clip in CON’s first rebuttal was noted but did not materially derail their case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2, which sharply distinguished challenges from removals, directly answered both pressure questions, and turned PRO’s concession about guidance failing to “stop every grab” into a decisive argument on institutional safeguarding duties.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO won primarily through two live routes that CON never adequately neutralized. First, PRO correctly exploited the B2.7 concession, where CON explicitly stated parents "do not get the right to prevent every other family's child from accessing" a book—a principle that mass removals and library-wide restrictions violate on their face. CON's Rebuttal 2 walk-back was thin, claiming the statement was only about parental opt-outs rather than institutional policy, but the contradiction remained live. Second, PRO's chilling-effect argument—that preemptive self-censorship and de facto removal during lengthy administrative reviews represent real harm not captured in final-verdict statistics—was never honestly answered. CON dismissed it as "speculative" without engaging the mechanism, while PRO grounded it in plausible institutional behavior. CON's core defense, the repeatedly invoked sub-5% removal figure, suffered from being presented with brittle precision in a closed-book setting and from the structural limitation PRO identified: it measures only permanent formal bans, missing the procedural harm. The "Trojan horse" framing—that activists bundle a few explicit titles with civil-rights histories to trigger sweeping administrative freezes—was sharpened across rounds and never neutralized, especially after CON conceded in B2.2 that such bundling occurs. CON's clipped opening is a minor additional execution cost. PRO's proper A1/A2 labeling and disciplined adaptation across rounds gave it the cleaner path to victory on the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exploitation of B2.7 was the most decisive exchange. CON voluntarily laid down a principle—parents cannot dictate what other families' children access—that directly contradicts the practice of mass removals PRO was challenging. PRO's Q1 sharpened this into a pointed contradiction, and CON's Rebuttal 2 response failed to resolve it, instead restating that relocation is not a ban, which sidesteps the point that mass challenges prevent the very cross-family access CON itself had endorsed as a protected right.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by keeping the motion focused on whether the current wave has gone too far overall, not whether any age-based curation is legitimate. Side B’s best argument was the low permanent-removal statistic, but it was an unsupported precise named claim and did not answer A’s process-based harm: temporary removals, bundled challenges, chilling effects, and public-library access barriers. A also exposed B’s tension between saying parents cannot block access for other families and defending broad institutional restrictions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was turning B’s concession that parents should not prevent other families’ access, then pairing it with B’s admission that challenge lists bundle explicit books with civil-rights titles. That made B’s “measured curation” frame look underinclusive and evasive.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully shifting the decisive metric from final removal statistics to procedural overreach and institutional chilling effects. While CON anchored its case on a low permanent removal rate to argue that curation remains measured, PRO effectively demonstrated that administrative limbo, bundled challenge lists, and preemptive self-censorship constitute the real overreach, directly engaging the proposition's concession that some titles are genuinely age-inappropriate. PRO also capitalized on a structural contradiction in CON's framing of parental rights versus community-wide restrictions, using sharp pressure questions and consistent comparative weighing to neutralize CON's outcome-based metric. CON's repeated reliance on a single unverified statistic and its dismissive treatment of process harms left its case vulnerable to PRO's cleaner, more comprehensive causal story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing turned CON's admission about bundled challenge lists and parental rights into a decisive clash on procedural harm. By framing months-long administrative reviews and preemptive ordering freezes as de facto removals, PRO completely neutralized CON's reliance on final removal rates and established a clearer, more responsive standard for what "gone too far" means under the motion.
