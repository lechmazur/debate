### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around the experience of 'most users' and the structural incentives of the platforms. While Side A effectively proved that relationships formed on apps can be durable, Side B convincingly argued that these successes represent outliers rather than the typical user experience, which is defined by continuous churn, evaluative labor, and gamified dissatisfaction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 decisively reframed Side A's 'more activity creates more chances' argument by comparing it to treating a costly emotional investment like a lottery ticket, successfully challenging Side A's assumption that volume equals relationship-building progress.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A built a stronger structural case around misaligned incentives and the telling absence of success metrics, which B never fully rebutted despite countering with access benefits.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answering pressure questions and highlighting B's concession on metrics.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): CON wins by grounding the debate in observable outcomes—rising app-origin relationships and comparable durability—against PRO's structural inference of systemic harm. CON successfully reframed engagement optimization as the necessary engine for scale and algorithmic filtering, neutralizing PRO's claim that retention incentives sabotage most users. PRO never squarely rebutted the comparative baseline that, for many users, the pre-app alternative was near-zero access, and relying on the absence of app-published success metrics proved too thin to overturn CON's outcome-based evidence. The clash remained sharp throughout, but CON's closing crystallized the decisive weighing: actual relationship formation at scale defeats theoretical structural sabotage.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 (B2) framing the misalignment between engagement metrics and relationship outcomes—particularly the "counts meetings, not outcomes" and "fifty first dates" lines—which forced CON to spend the rest of the debate defending whether volume translates into value.
