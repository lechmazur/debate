### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side A won because Side B repeatedly treated carrier claims as an automatic trigger for blank checks, which the proposition does not require. A defended targeted, competitive, technology-neutral subsidies with ceilings and accountability, while B ultimately endorsed subsidized wireless, direct public funding, and public capital to connect rural households. That conceded the proposition's substance. B's opportunity-cost pressure was meaningful, but its central trigger argument attacked an unnecessarily narrow mechanism rather than rural broadband subsidy itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's decisive response was that commercial non-viability begins the public inquiry rather than determining the recipient or subsidy size. Side B's own plan then used public support after private returns failed, undermining its claimed distinction between connection and subsidy.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully defending the principle of the motion and catching Side A in a strategic contradiction. Side A argued brilliantly against writing blank checks to pessimistic carriers, but eventually admitted their own solution relied on "subsidized wireless" and direct funding, thereby conceding the core proposition to subsidize rural broadband expansion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing speech cleanly capitalized on the Q&A exchange, pointing out that Side A's alternative literally included public subsidies for satellites and schools, reducing A's entire case to a dispute over "the label on the cheque."
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): PRO held the proposition as written – commercial non-viability diagnoses market failure, it shouldn't end inquiry. CON's perverse-incentive and cost-tail attack was answered by verified build-out, open eligibility including co-ops, clawbacks, tech neutrality and competitive procurement. CON then conceded the core in B4.1 by endorsing repayable public capital for co-ops and "subsidized satellite" for scattered homes – a subsidy even when private carriers say unviable – and its ceiling critique was recast by PRO as cost discipline, not restoration of a pure-profit veto.
