### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by pairing a concrete access benefit with a regulated-camera model and by repeatedly exposing Side A's alternatives as inaccessible to ordinary citizens. Its claim that exceptions defeat a proposition about most trials was also logically overstated. B's empirical claims were sometimes broader and more precise than warranted, keeping the result competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered both pressure questions, explained how automatic exclusions and sealed motions avoid forcing vulnerable witnesses to self-identify, and reframed Side A's preferred access friction as a class barrier. Side A preserved the irreversibility concern but did not show why tailored safeguards could not protect exceptional cases while allowing cameras in most proceedings.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won a competitive debate by keeping a cleaner live path: existing public-trial exposure plus controlled pool feeds, categorical exemptions, and sealed opt-outs make cameras a modernization of openness rather than a new circus. Net: PRO answered the decisive pressure more directly and left the better comparative reason to adopt the proposition as written.

By arguing that witness exposure already exists via live-tweeting and courtroom reporters, and that a regulated pool feed actually gives judges more control (delays, fixed angles, rebroadcast bans) than the current free-for-all, Side A neutralizes Side B's core harm. Side A also provides crisp, structurally sound answers to Side B's pressure questions regarding witness opt-outs (categorical exemptions and sealed in-chambers motions), dismantling the Catch-22.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2), where they directly answer Side B's pressure questions by explaining how categorical off-ramps and sealed in-chambers motions eliminate the burden on vulnerable witnesses to publicly request privacy, while pointing out that a pool feed gives the judge more control than unregulated live-tweeting.
