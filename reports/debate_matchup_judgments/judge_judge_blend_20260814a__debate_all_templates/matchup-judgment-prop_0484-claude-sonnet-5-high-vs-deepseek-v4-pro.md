### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): A won narrowly because its implementation objection remained decisive: weighted votes cannot protect islands if major emitters can reject, bypass, or leave the resulting regime. B compellingly defended the fairness and administrability of risk weighting, but never established how additional formal votes translate into emissions cuts or enforceable compliance. A’s repeated clipped turns were a meaningful execution cost that kept the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was its response to B’s claim that the status quo already provides zero enforcement. A correctly refocused the comparison on whether reweighting improves cooperation: B identified other incentives for emitters to participate, but did not show that those incentives would make them accept adverse votes or implement the resulting commitments.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by systematically neutralizing Side A's two main objections: the 'lawyer's feast' of definitions and the walkout threat. Side B provided highly effective practical mechanisms, whereas Side A suffered from repeated clipping due to poor word management and failed to overcome B's external constraint arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal decisively answered the pressure questions by comparing the proposed voting bands to existing IMF/EU quota systems and introducing carbon tariffs as a reason emitters wouldn't walk out.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built the stronger path to victory by demonstrating that the current GDP/population weighting has already failed to produce enforceable climate commitments, effectively neutralizing Side B's claim that reducing emitter weight would cause fatal walkouts. A's tiered-formula response to B's pressure questions showed existential risk could be operationalized using existing indices, while A's closing crystallized the core asymmetry: the choice is between a system held hostage by emitters and one that weights survival as a formal stake. Side B suffered significant execution costs from three clipped speeches and repeatedly begged the question that keeping major emitters comfortable equals legitimacy without adequately addressing why moral authority has failed to secure binding outcomes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer to the walkout threat in Rebuttal 2 (A4.1-A4.2), which argued that zero enforceable commitments is already the status quo under GDP/population weighting and that emitters face trade, reputational, and technology costs that keep them engaged beyond raw vote share, defused Side B's central strategic objection and reframed the "cooperation" defense as hostage-taking.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by separating the injustice of current representation from whether this specific reform would improve outcomes. Its capacity and defection argument remained live: Side B offered stigma, legitimacy, and soft power, but did not establish that weighted votes create leverage beyond pressures already operating today. Side A also showed that proportional existential-risk weighting requires disputed models, horizons, and eligibility boundaries. Side B made a strong moral case for amplified voice, but relied too heavily on asserting that greater legitimacy would materially change emitter behavior. Repeated clipping also modestly hurt Side B’s execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s answer to Side B’s claimed enforcement mechanism was decisive: reputational stigma and moral condemnation already accompany emitter withdrawal, while weighted voting adds no control over emissions, finance, or technology. Side B’s claim that reweighting would intensify those costs remained asserted rather than causally demonstrated.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by effectively demonstrating that rewriting voting power based on a highly contested metric would consume institutional capital without changing the underlying capacity deficit of major emitters. Side A neutralized Side B's "moral stigma" mechanism by showing it already exists and does not prevent defection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly dismantled Side B's attempt to distinguish SIDS from Bangladesh, proving that the proposed metric would invite endless procedural border disputes.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully framed the burden: showing the status quo is unfair is insufficient; PRO must prove risk-weighted votes produce material improvements. CON demonstrated that the moral stigma and soft power PRO invoked already operate without vote reform, while the proposed metric introduces operational paralysis, arbitrary boundaries (Bangladesh), and potential acceleration of emitter defection without altering the capacity bottleneck. PRO never bridged the gap between symbolic voice and actual emitter behavior, whereas CON's rebuttals consistently turned PRO's analogies and concessions into liabilities.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 dismantling of the triage analogy and the exposure that PRO's mechanism relies on moral costs that already exist, crystallized in the line that PRO offers only "a louder microphone in a room the pharmacy can still walk out of" and "a better seat at your own funeral."
