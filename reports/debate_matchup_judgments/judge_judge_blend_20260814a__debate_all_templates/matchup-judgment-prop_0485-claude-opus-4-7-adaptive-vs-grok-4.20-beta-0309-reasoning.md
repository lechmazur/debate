### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): Side A won by making the motion turn on “full root access” rather than general right-to-repair. A conceded the strongest PRO benefits—diagnostics, competition, battery longevity, reduced waste—but argued they could be achieved through mandated read access, certified authenticated write tools, fair licensing, and antitrust enforcement without giving broad kernel-level authority over safety-critical BMS firmware. Side B had a plausible monopoly and repair-cost case, but repeatedly conflated diagnostics with root write authority and never convincingly answered the safety/cyber asymmetry or the lack of a real licensing gate in the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 were decisive: A separated “read cell-level drift” from “rewrite BMS firmware,” then showed that PRO’s aviation analogy actually required the certification regime missing from the motion, while PRO mostly restated that anything short of root was a leash.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B secured a decisive win by precisely parsing the motion's demand for "full root access." By conceding the need for diagnostic read access and fair repair, B isolated A to defending unrestricted kernel write authority. A's attempt to use aviation as an analogy backfired when B exposed the lack of equivalent FAA-style licensing in the motion, rendering A's reliance on state business registrations and after-the-fact tort liability highly unpersuasive against fleet-scale cyber and thermal runaway risks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal cleanly exposed A's conflation of diagnostic read access and kernel write access, while devastatingly turning A's aviation analogy by pointing out the motion lacks any equivalent licensing gate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON wins by showing the motion overshoots its own justification. PRO never proves why regulating authenticated access cannot break the monopoly, only asserting it preserves a "leash," while CON offers specific structural remedies. CON decisively turns PRO's aviation analogy against them by noting such deep access requires rigorous federal licensing absent from the motion, and PRO's reliance on generic state business registration fails to supply that safety gate. The incremental authority the motion demands adds unique thermal-runaway and fleet-scale cyber risks that outweigh its unproven necessity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's separation of diagnostic read access from kernel write authority, exposing that PRO bundles a safe, necessary capability with a dangerous, unnecessary one, and turning the aviation analogy against PRO by showing it requires licensing gates the motion omits.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully isolating the motion's demand for "full root access" as a dangerous overshoot.
