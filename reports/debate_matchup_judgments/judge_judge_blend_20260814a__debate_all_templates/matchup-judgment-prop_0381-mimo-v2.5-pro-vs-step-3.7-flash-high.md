Ultimately PRO maintained the better framing of the proposition throughout—"far more" as incremental design reform rather than unrestricted access—and CON spent too much energy attacking the extreme version. Multiple clippings mildly disadvantage PRO's visible record but not enough to reverse the outcome.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) sustained the stronger path to victory on three live claims that CON never fully neutralized. First, the deterrence math: PRO argued that people deterred from shelters create a larger effective capacity loss than small accommodations would; CON's only counter was that peak emergencies max out capacity, but this implicitly assumes away the deterrence problem CON itself acknowledged. Second, the couples-and-DV argument: PRO's connected-needs framing (survivor needs partner, animal, and documents simultaneously) remained live throughout; CON's response—better information management and hospital-triage analogies—was too thin to answer the specific claim that separation deters vulnerable people from entering in the first place. Third, the essential-possessions reframe: PRO recharacterized "personal possessions" as medication, documents, and tools rather than discretionary luggage, which CON never directly refuted, instead relying on the strawman that someone refused a backpack would choose a flood zone. CON's sharpest move was the PETS Act pivot—correctly showing that the reform PRO cited established separate parallel facilities, not co-sheltering—and the "targeted scalpel vs. sweeping sledgehammer" framing was genuinely strong. But CON never demonstrated that the parallel infrastructure is reliably implemented, and PRO's patchiness response was enough to keep that route alive. CON also absorbed two clipped turns (opening and closing), with the closing cut before its conclusion, costing CON crystallization at the final moment when PRO's closing ran clean. On balance PRO won by a clear but competitive margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's PETS Act pivot (A2.1–A2.2): correctly identifying that the very reform PRO cited established separate pet facilities rather than on-site co-sheltering, turning PRO's own evidence against the proposition. This forced PRO onto the defensive about patchiness and inconsistency of implementation. PRO partially answered with the connected-needs argument and implementation reliability critique, but never fully closed the gap, making this the most genuinely damaging exchange of the debate—though ultimately insufficient because CON left the couples-deterrence and essential-possessions flanks exposed.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): A won by conceding the strongest PRO example—pet-related evacuation deterrence—while reframing it as a case for targeted, parallel infrastructure rather than a broad redesign of human shelters around couples, pets, and possessions. A’s capacity, safety, and operational-simplicity weighing remained more comparative and disciplined.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer that B’s capacity math assumed slack shelters, while the real test is peak disaster demand where every partition, pet zone, and storage system competes with setup speed and human capacity, was the most decisive exchange.

CON mounted a strong operational case centered on triage, deployment speed, and peak capacity, and effectively isolated the pet deterrent as already addressed by the PETS Act. However, CON never fully resolved the comparative weighing on net lives saved versus operational complexity, and two clipped turns incurred a minor execution cost per the protocol. PRO’s consistent focus on the deterrence-capacity tradeoff, sharper answers to pressure, and cleaner resolution of the central clash secures the win.
