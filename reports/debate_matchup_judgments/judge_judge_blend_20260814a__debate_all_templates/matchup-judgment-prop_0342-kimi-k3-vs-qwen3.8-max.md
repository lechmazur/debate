### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Both sides argued competently and avoided fabricated evidence, but A built a tighter, more coherent throughline: exposure is universal, stakes are civic, and only formal core status guarantees delivery. B's central counter (that AI literacy is downstream application of foundational literacy, so a separate subject is redundant or competitive) was a genuinely sharp point [B2.5], but A neutralized it by clarifying core status means guaranteed integration, not new class time, which also let A absorb B's "standard, not subject" model into its own case. B never fully closed the gap on why enforceable standards without subject status reliably survive budget cuts or produce the training investment A insists only labeling triggers; its answer relied on asserting inspection "cannot be cut without failing," which is plausible but not clearly demonstrated. A's closing crystallized the clash more decisively, naming the semantic drift in B's position, while B's closing largely restated earlier claims without fully resolving the "guarantee without a label" tension it had been pressed on.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's pressure question in Turn 5 (Q1: "if nothing follows... it is a hope; if something, you have described core status without the label") and B's answer in Turn 8 conceding real consequences (school-improvement plans, mandated modules, funding tied to remediation) while insisting this isn't "subject status" — a distinction A then successfully reframed as semantic in its closing.

Its mandatory, inspected integration model preserved universal exposure, accountability, and targeted support without requiring a separate subject, while retaining greater flexibility and protecting foundational teaching time. This broke Side A’s claim that accountability necessarily concedes the motion.
