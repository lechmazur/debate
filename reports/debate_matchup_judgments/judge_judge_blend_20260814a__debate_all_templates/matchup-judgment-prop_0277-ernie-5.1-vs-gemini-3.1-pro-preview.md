### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but CON (Side A) edges out for two reasons that compound across the later rounds. First, the purchasing-power rebuttal in A4.3 was the most analytically sharp move in the debate: CON correctly identified that displacement is driven by transient capital, not physical headcount, which means PRO's "load per area drops" logic doesn't neutralize the gentrification risk even at lower tourist densities. PRO never directly answered this; they re-asserted guardrails, which CON had already attacked with the meta-argument that if cities could legislate away tourism economics, overtourism wouldn't be a global crisis. PRO's only live counter was the Barcelona example, but CON never needed to fully rebut it because PRO failed to show it was generalizable rather than an outlier. Second, PRO's closing was clipped before completion—a real execution cost in the round where crystallization mattered most. CON's closing was complete, concise, and returned cleanly to the purchasing-power frame and the guardrails critique. PRO's strongest points—informal overflow, causal reversal on containment, load-per-area—remained live but were incrementally weakened by CON's consistent pressure on what type of investment follows tourists and whether policy discipline is achievable in practice. The match is clear but genuinely competitive: PRO's opening was richer in examples and the firewall-leaks argument never disappeared entirely.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.3 purchasing-power distinction: tourists displace residents not by physical weight but by unequal capital, so even a fraction of global tourist traffic injected into a modest neighborhood can flip local amenities into Airbnbs. This directly neutralized PRO's core "load-per-area" defense and PRO offered no clean answer beyond appealing to guardrails, which CON had already framed as fantasy policy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by showing that containment, despite minor leakage, is more pragmatic than managed dispersal. It successfully undermined PRO's core mechanism by distinguishing between physical crowd load and economic displacement driven by tourist purchasing power, demonstrating that even a fraction of redirected visitors can gentrify residential neighborhoods. CON also coherently resolved the tension between tourism revenue and the off-season, while PRO relied on asserted policy guardrails and a clipped closing that could not recover from the damage to its causal story.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 response distinguishing between physical crowd load and economic purchasing power as the true driver of displacement, which gutted PRO's argument that spreading tourists would reduce harm by lowering "load per area."
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON wins by decisively shifting the clash from physical crowding to economic displacement. While PRO argued that dispersal reduces physical load and paired it with policy guardrails, CON effectively countered that tourist displacement is driven by unequal purchasing power and short-term rental conversions, which official dispersal would spread city-wide. CON's defense of containment as a pragmatic firewall that quarantines commercial disruption to optimized zones, combined with a strong rebuttal framing PRO's regulatory solutions as unrealistic, provided a clearer path to preserving residential livability. PRO's clipped closing also incurs a minor execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's [A4.3] dismantling PRO's load-management analogy by proving displacement is economic, not physical. CON showed that even a fraction of tourist traffic brings enough transient capital to flip local amenities into Airbnbs, neutralizing PRO's core mechanism for why dispersal protects neighborhoods.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built a stronger cumulative case and adapted more effectively across rounds. Three moves were decisive. First, PRO's seasonal dispersal argument—that flattening peak demand maximizes existing infrastructure and creates year-round employment—was introduced early and never neutralized. CON's counter that "peaks can't be flattened" was directly undercut by PRO's correct observation that the Colosseum doesn't migrate south for winter and that only marginal shifts are needed, not wholesale behavior change. Second, PRO's closing "shift the margins" concession was strategically smart: it surrendered the maximalist version of the claim (you can't override school calendars) while preserving the substantive win (even modest redistribution breaks bottlenecks). Third, CON's sharpest reframe—"I don't concentrate anything, the market does"—was clever but self-undermining, because it converted CON's affirmative case from "concentration is better" into "the status quo is inevitable," which reads as capitulation rather than a policy argument. CON made real points (Airbnb saturation spreading, behavioral inflexibility, fiscal logic of taxed concentration zones), and the "density is the product" framing was memorable, but CON never fully explained why letting concentration destroy city centers is preferable to managed distribution. PRO's regulatory mechanism answers (capped permits, transit routing) were adequate responses to enforcement pressure, even if not airtight. The internal tension in CON's case—defending organic concentration while warning that artificial dispersal causes saturation—left PRO's load-management argument the cleaner path to a decision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to CON's behavioral-inflexibility argument was the decisive exchange. CON insisted tourist behavior is not a software patch and that cold January cities won't lure families away from sunny Barcelona. PRO answered in B4.4 that monuments are open 365 days and then crystallized in B5.4 that the goal is not to empty the Colosseum in July but to shift the margins enough to break bottlenecks. This was direct, proportionate, and required no faith in urban-planning miracles—it met CON's strongest objection on its own terms while preserving the substantive case for dispersal.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B won by more effectively defending the proposition's dual mandate—geographic and seasonal dispersal—while exposing Side A's position as resigned acceptance of permanent urban overload. B's load-management framework and direct, substantive answers to pressure questions about enforcement and equity provided a clearer policy path. A's core agglomeration argument was rhetorically forceful but conceded the "overloaded" problem identified in the motion, and A never fully recovered from B's demonstration that spreading volume mathematically reduces per-neighborhood burden rather than replicating saturation everywhere.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal (Turn 7), where B directly answered Side A's pressure questions on enforcement and equity, reframed A's stance as fatalistic surrender, and crystallized the clash as "living ecosystem versus commercial product."
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully grounding the debate in market realities and structural tourist behavior drivers (weather, school calendars, iconic pull) that Side B's planning mechanisms struggled to overcome. While B offered a strong load-management frame and smartly lowered the burden to shifting margins, A consistently exposed the implementation gap, arguing that dispersal policies historically spread saturation rather than cure it, and that treating tourist demand as a software problem ignores hard constraints. A's rebuttals were sharper, directly answering pressure questions while turning B's regulatory reliance into a liability, and A's closing crystallized the clash around fiscal honesty and market respect versus planning idealism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which directly answered B's pressure questions by dismantling the feasibility of off-peak incentives (open does not mean visited) and framing B's regulatory mechanisms as unproven miracles against market forces, effectively turning B's own saturation warnings against them.
