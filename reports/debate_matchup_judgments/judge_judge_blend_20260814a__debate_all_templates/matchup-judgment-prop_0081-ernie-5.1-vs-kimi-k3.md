- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON built the more disciplined and strategically sharper case. ID verification") effectively conceded this framing. The most decisive moment was PRO's own line in closing—"a false badge is no worse than no badge"—which CON turned into the crux of its final speech, arguing persuasively that counterfeit trust is strictly worse than no trust. PRO's case had real merit (equity, business incentives) but suffered from repeated format violations (two clipped speeches, including the closing) and never fully neutralized CON's layered-pathway dilemma or funding-mechanism pressure. CON's closing crystallized the clash cleanly without new evidence, while PRO's was cut off mid-argument, compounding the execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 turning PRO's "layered pathways" defense into an inescapable dilemma (either the strong check excludes vulnerable users, or a weak alternative lets scammers route around it), combined with its closing exploitation of PRO's "false badge is no worse than no badge" concession, was the decisive exchange.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won a competitive debate by showing that the disputed stronger verification creates real funding, privacy, exclusion, and adversarial-design tradeoffs that Side B mostly answered through assertions about existing revenue and deliberate design. B was also clipped twice and leaned on unsupported platform-specific claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s decisive exchange combined the layered-versus-alternative verification dilemma with Side B’s closing claim that a false badge is no worse than no badge. - **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A systematically dismantled Side B's mechanisms, trapping B in a dilemma on verification pathways and capitalizing brilliantly on B's claim that a false badge is no worse than no badge. Side B suffered from logical inconsistencies and execution errors, including being clipped twice.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A built a coherent, principled case (allocation-by-wallet, collective-action logic, perverse incentives) and used memorable analogies (seatbelts, oxygen masks, a nurse not checking credit cards) that gave the debate a clean throughline. Crucially, A's rebuttals exploited B's own concessions: B admitted free basic reporting/blocking already exist without collapsing into a "wasteland" (undercutting B's funding-crisis warning), and in the pressure-question exchange B conceded that "the real fraud barrier is the verification process... not the payment" — a self-defeating admission that guts B's central claim that paywalls function as a safety filter. A seized on this decisively in closing, while B's closing never addressed or repaired that concession, instead re-asserting the funding-fragility argument without a concrete mechanism for why basic tools survive but advanced ones supposedly can't. B's "friction filters intent" argument was also directly refuted (a scammer can absorb a small fee; a victim may not) and never adequately answered. B kept a competent, business-realist frame throughout, but execution weaknesses — an unresolved self-defeating concession and thinner mechanism for its funding claims — left its route to victory damaged by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing highlight of B's pressure-question concession ("the real fraud barrier is the verification process... not the payment," B4.3) was the decisive exchange: it collapsed B's paywall-as-filter argument and went unanswered in B's final speech.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by showing that payment neither verifies identity nor stops motivated scammers, while predictably excluding vulnerable users. Side A retained a plausible sustainability concern, but mostly asserted that dedicated safety revenue is uniquely necessary without establishing that general subscription and advertising revenue could not fund core protections. Its abuse argument also blurred blocking, reporting, and verification, then conceded that verification itself—not payment—is the real fraud barrier. Side B combined this concession with stronger access, network-effect, and incentive comparisons.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B decisively exploited Side A's admission that document and biometric checks, not the fee, create the fraud barrier. The line that a paywall filters wallets rather than malice directly defeated Side A's friction argument and clarified why scammers can absorb the fee while poorer victims cannot.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by decisively dismantling Side A's 'friction filters intent' argument. Side B used pressure questions to force Side A to concede that a paywall does not stop a motivated scammer, and that the verification process itself is the real barrier. Side B then leveraged this concession in the closing to prove that a paywall only prices out broke victims without deterring funded predators. Side B also convincingly framed safety as core infrastructure that prevents churn, effectively neutralizing Side A's funding concerns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal and closing brilliantly capitalized on Side A's concession that the verification process, not the fee, is what stops fraud, neutralizing Side A's defense of the paywall as a protective barrier.
