### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): Side A built the stronger cumulative case on the three decisive clashes. First, on the "limiting principle" question: CON conceded the sex marker is an updateable legal proxy, then argued revision must stay binary—but CON's only answer was circular (B4.1: the binary is the standard because it serves the binary). Side A exposed this loop sharply in both Rebuttal 2 and the closing and CON never closed it. Second, on administrative catastrophe: Side A pointed to Canada, Australia, Germany, New Zealand, and US X passports as live evidence that the predicted legal unraveling never materialized. CON's response—that most global systems are still binary—recast an empirical failure as a mere transition lag, which is a much weaker defense of the original claim. Third, and most decisively, Side A's Rebuttal 2 turned CON's "case-by-case is an own-goal" back on CON: binary IDs already require bespoke assessment for trans and intersex individuals. Prison placement for a trans woman holding a male-coded ID is the paradigm CON refused to answer. CON demanded IDs be self-contained policy resolvers, but that standard condemns the binary system too. CON's strongest moment was the framing in closing ("state utility vs. personal affirmation"), which was rhetorically crisp but artificially binary—Side A's argument was also grounded in state accuracy and practical harm reduction, not merely affirmation. The clipped speeches hurt Side A marginally but the core argumentative moves were clearly visible in what survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 A1 answer: pointing out that a trans woman holding a male-coded ID already requires case-by-case prison placement assessment, so CON's standard that IDs must eliminate bespoke judgment was never true under the binary system either. This directly neutralized CON's best offensive turn and reframed the entire "standardization" argument against CON.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by successfully confronting Side A's theoretical objections with empirical reality. While Side A argued eloquently that 'X' markers would destroy the utilitarian standardization of IDs, Side B pointed out that many jurisdictions already use them without administrative collapse. Furthermore, Side B effectively exposed contradictions in Side A's framework, notably demonstrating that binary IDs already require case-by-case assessments in edge cases and that forcing intersex individuals into a binary relies on legal convention rather than strict physical reality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's response to the 'case-by-case assessment' pressure question was the decisive moment. By pointing out that binary IDs don't automatically resolve prison placements for trans individuals either, Side B proved that Side A was holding the 'X' marker to an impossible standard that the status quo doesn't meet.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON)): PRO won by dismantling CON's claim that sex markers record objective biological reality, showing instead that they are mutable legal proxies already revised for gender transitions.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's second rebuttal turning CON's "case-by-case assessment" objection by showing binary IDs already fail to eliminate downstream discretion for trans and intersex individuals, thereby neutralizing CON's administrative argument while reinforcing that accurate identification—not automated policy resolution—is the ID's proper function.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO won on the decisive practical clash: how ID checks actually work. CON's core claim that sex markers record "biological sex" as an externally verifiable characteristic never recovered from PRO's accurate observation that no clerk at a counter verifies chromosomes or anatomy — verification works through photo and presentation matching. CON's height analogy was interesting but PRO correctly identified that height also goes unverified in practice, and the comparison ultimately helped PRO rather than CON. On the friction argument, PRO's distinction between confusion prompting a neutral question versus a mismatched binary marker triggering an active fraud accusation was the sharpest clash in the debate, and CON's response that X markers can cause equal friction in foreign systems, while real, never matched the asymmetry PRO identified between an unfamiliar document and one that appears actively falsified. CON's strongest live argument — international interoperability — was partially answered by the ICAO X-marker permissibility point, and while CON correctly noted that permission differs from seamless acceptance, PRO reframed the comparison as minor novel friction versus severe fraud-suspicion friction, which held. CON's clipped Rebuttal 2 was a real execution cost, leaving its closing argument about PRO's concessions to carry more weight than the clipped speech itself supported. PRO properly formatted A1/A2 responses and maintained consistent pressure on the practical safety line throughout. The margin is clear but competitive because CON's document-integrity and international-infrastructure arguments were substantive and not fully resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 distinction between "confusion resolved by asking a question" versus "a mismatch triggering suspicion of identity fraud" [A2.3–A2.4] was the most decisive single exchange. CON's response in Rebuttal 1 that the comparison was rigged by matching worst-case binary against best-case X [B2.3] was the best counter, but PRO held the comparison in Rebuttal 2 by noting that confusion is a request for information while a presentation-marker mismatch is effectively an accusation of a crime — an asymmetry CON never squarely neutralized.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Turn 7 effectively neutralized the strategic evasion argument by clarifying how selective service actually operates, and delivered the decisive weighing between a curious clerk and an accusation of a crime.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by showing that the practical function of an ID—matching the bearer to the document in everyday encounters—is undermined when the state forces a binary marker that visibly contradicts a nonbinary person's presentation. While Side B offered a coherent framework about verifiable external referents and global interoperability, they never fully answered A's core challenge that clerks do not verify biological sex, making the mismatch problem real and dangerous. A blunted B's interoperability fears with existing X passports and ICAO standards, while B's clipped final rebuttal and closing overstated A's concessions without proving that X markers make documents globally useless. A crystallized the clash more cleanly as practical safety versus abstract dogma.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, where they answered B's pressure questions by likening self-attestation to legal name changes, distinguishing a clerk's mundane question about an X marker from an identity-fraud accusation, and noting that bartenders do not verify home addresses—thereby defusing B's "external referent" theory of document integrity.
