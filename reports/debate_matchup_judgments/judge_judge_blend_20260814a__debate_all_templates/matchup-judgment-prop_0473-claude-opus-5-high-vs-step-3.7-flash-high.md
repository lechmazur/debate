### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that equal terms among co-location buyers do not answer unfairness toward non-buying counterparties, and by consistently distinguishing legitimate hosting costs from selling a relative latency edge. Side B retained plausible liquidity and off-site migration concerns, but repeatedly mischaracterized A as alleging secret tiers and never comparatively established that those benefits outweighed the conflict of interest. Its clipped rebuttal and nonexistent closing references also imposed execution costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A forced Side B to identify the purchased product as reliable, low-jitter access unavailable ten kilometres away. A then showed that this conceded the relative advantage B had denied, while B's simultaneous claims that eliminating differentials would not reduce revenue but would alienate customers created a damaging contradiction.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A delivered a dominant performance by repeatedly clarifying that the latency advantage being sold is the gap between the co-location hall and the outside world. Side B repeatedly missed this distinction, incorrectly claiming A was arguing about a secret tier inside the hall. Side A's Pressure Questions flawlessly trapped Side B into admitting both that co-location provides a distinct latency advantage over outside members and that eliminating this advantage would alienate customers, completely collapsing Side B's core claim that no advantage is being sold.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Closing brilliantly capitalized on Side B's Rebuttal 2, pointing out that Side B spent four speeches denying an advantage existed, only to explicitly define that exact advantage (low-variance access vs. 10km jitter) when pressed.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won the core clash on what is sold. CON insisted co-location is not a sale of position because cables are equal and the slot is open to all. PRO used that equality as a confession that proximity advantage is illegitimate, then forced CON in pressure to name the product: low-variance direct LAN access that 10km away firms must live without [B4.1]. Closing exposed the contradiction that CON called that edge both worthless and customer-alienating to remove. PRO's auction-house analogy handled uniform-price fairness, and the referee-selling-yards vs geography distinction defused the off-site arms race and cost-recovery claims. CON never answered the revenue-if-differentials-zero question or named a voluntary speed bump, and repeated liquidity benefits without answering manufactured-need rebuttal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 trap and crystallization in A5 — forcing B to admit in B4.1 that buyers pay for consistent low-jitter access over distant members, then showing that admission contradicts B's four-round denial that any advantage exists and collapses the uniform-price fairness defense.
