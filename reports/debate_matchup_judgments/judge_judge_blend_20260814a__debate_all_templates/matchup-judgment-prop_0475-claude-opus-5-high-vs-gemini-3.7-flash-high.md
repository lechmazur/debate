### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by controlling the decisive comparisons: subsidies at the margin of a mature industry mostly inflate land prices rather than grocery affordability; public money at the base of a steep cost curve buys the learning curve; and error costs favor redirection with transition support over another generation locking in high land/methane footprints. CON raised real worries about energy, pure sugar feedstocks, isolates vs whole foods, and transition shocks, but repeatedly overstated the motion as overnight stripping/bankruptcy and never cleanly answered industrial grain-fed vs marginal-grassland subsidy share or the capitalization mechanism. PRO’s answers on diversification of failure modes and the self-sealing “too expensive so don’t fund” trap resolved the live clash more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO flipping CON’s feedstock objection (animals already consume far more crop calories for metabolism/methane) and later land-price capitalization answers to the affordability pressure.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by reframing the shift as a phased redirection with transition payments rather than a sudden abolition, and by showing that CON’s core objections were self-sealing: dismissing fermentation because it is currently expensive and small-scale is exactly why public support at the base of the cost curve is needed. CON never squarely answered PRO’s pressure on the subsidy-to-retail-price mechanism or rebutted the diversification argument that adding tank-based protein creates resilience through different failure modes rather than novel fragility. PRO’s closing crystallized the asymmetric error costs effectively, while CON’s closing largely repeated prior assertions without resolving the live subsidy-mechanism and infant-industry clashes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s rebuttal exposing the self-sealing logic in CON’s case—arguing that precision fermentation must not be funded because it is currently unproven and costly, which guarantees it stays that way—and showing that true food resilience means diversifying across failure modes rather than defending a single decentralized but disease-vulnerable system.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) won the core efficiency and subsidy-purpose clash. CON's strongest lines – marginal-land upcycling and food-security insurance – were directly undercut by B's feedstock math (animals demand far more grain/sugar than fermenters), capitalization argument (support inflates land not retail prices), and resilience asymmetry (a ruined batch = days vs culled herds = years, grid dependence affects both). B's decade-long shift with transition payments and error-cost weighing crystallized why reallocation buys the cost curve; CON's R2 reply that "vast majority on pasture" and "toxic waste" overstated and never answered what share of subsidised system is actually grain-fed industrial.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.2-B2.5 and B4.1-B4.4 – the self-defeating feedstock comparison plus the capitalization-to-land and diversification-asymmetric-failure-mode rebuttal, which left CON's price-shock and centralization vulnerability claims without comparative weight.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the live decision rule clearer: subsidy dollars must deliver real abatement, not hoped-for thermodynamics. PRO’s case repeatedly put benefits behind an unproven “once scale/price parity arrives” premise, and never supplied a non-circular leakage mechanism or a coherent financing path for hubs, transitions, and stewardship from a pot it had already cut. CON’s geography-of-emissions, ingredients/joint-product, and certain-tonnes-now comparisons survived the exchange and made the proposition easier to reject.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s A4.5/A5.1–A5.3 exposure of PRO’s circular leakage answer: price parity is both the unfunded premise and the alleged proof that herds will not relocate.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by exposing that Side B's entire case rested on an unfunded, speculative chain (scale → price parity → global displacement) while failing to answer carbon leakage mechanics, the joint-product nature of dairy economics, and the budget impossibility of cutting incumbent subsidies while simultaneously funding transitions. Side A's comparative weighing—certain methane abatement now versus speculative systemic transformation later—provided a cleaner decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's joint-product turn (A4.3) and circularity critique (A4.5), which showed that undercutting dairy ingredients would cheapen co-products and that Side B answered leakage only by assuming the conclusion it needed to prove.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won the central mechanism clash. PRO's case rested on "once parity, then global displacement" to solve leakage and land, but never showed how a rich-country subsidy cut reduces the global herd. A exposed this circularity, added the joint-product rebound (cheap whey = cheap butter/beef = more consumption) and the certain-vs-speculative asymmetry for methane. A answered pressure Qs directly; B's A1/A2 re-asserted parity as the solution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON R2 A1/A2 and closing on 'once' — dismantled leakage/displacement as premise-as-conclusion and introduced joint-product rebound that PRO never squarely answered.
