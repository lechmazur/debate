### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A gave a strong autonomy-based case that mandatory always-on coaching is intrinsically intrusive and not merely a privacy problem, and A correctly resisted some overbroad safety-tool examples. But B did more comparative work: it showed that mandates can be protective, that optional adoption may fail where consistency matters, that many harms can be addressed through design and data-use regulation, and especially that A relied on auditability for a ban while denying auditability for narrower safeguards. B overstated the motion at times by treating it as banning anything that monitors, despite the “productivity companion” wording, but its regulation-versus-prohibition and quota/assistance comparisons remained more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange was the enforcement asymmetry: A said enterprise deployments are easy to audit for prohibition, and B turned that into a reason inspectors could also audit prompt caps, settings, and data-firewalls, undercutting A’s claim that regulation is uniquely futile.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A systematically disassembled Side B's case by trapping them in two major contradictions. First, A forced B to concede that 'safety' AI tools should be allowed, demonstrating that B actually supports functional regulation rather than the blanket ban the motion requires. Second, A exposed B's contradictory stance on enforcement, pointing out that if enterprise software is easily auditable for a ban, its settings are equally auditable for regulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Turn 8, where Side A uses B's pressure question answers to prove that B has adopted A's regulatory framework and contradicted their own logic on auditability.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A won by forcing B to concede functional line-drawing that equals regulation (fatigue monitors survive via design distinctions) and by exposing the enforcement asymmetry B could not resolve. A's rebuttals directly neutralized B's autonomy claims and produced the cleaner comparative outcome: governed tools vs. raw quotas. B's pressure answers and closings left the motion's text and its own carve-outs unreconciled.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly dismantled B's Q answers by showing PRO's safety carve-out and audit claims collapse into CON's framework.
