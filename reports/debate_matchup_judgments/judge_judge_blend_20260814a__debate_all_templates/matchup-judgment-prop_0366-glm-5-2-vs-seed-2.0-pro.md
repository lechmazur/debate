- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that simultaneous home releases cannibalize box office revenue, because studios "cannot means-test convenience."   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's final crystallization that a home release "does not arrive labeled 'for those who physically cannot attend'" cleanly dismantled Side B's entire economic defense.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.2), which argued that excluded viewers represent additive, not cannibalized, revenue, directly deflated CON's central economic objection and shifted the burden CON could not recover.

A argued that simultaneous home releases would only attract viewers who could never attend theaters, thus generating pure new revenue without cannibalizing box office sales. B grounded this in observable industry behavior, noting that studios tested day-and-date, saw cannibalization, and walked it back, directly contradicting A's claim of purely additive revenue. B's compromise of shorter holdbacks also substantially mitigated A's accessibility concerns without dismantling the funding model for ambitious filmmaking, making B's comparative weighing cleaner, more realistic, and harder to refute.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's takedown of A's "pure additional revenue" claim, highlighting that home releases cannot be surgically targeted only to locked-out viewers and that the marginal couch-versus-multiplex viewer will inevitably be pulled away, a reality confirmed by the market's rejection of the model.
