### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side B made the strongest urgency case: coal is a major emissions source, voluntary frameworks have underperformed, and enforcement addresses free-riding. But Side A won the comparative mechanism clash. A repeatedly forced the question of whether penalties actually build replacement grids, storage, and fiscal capacity, and B never fully answered except by narrowing the penalty design with late guardrails and optimistic assumptions about green-market access. A also effectively undercut B's transition-finance answer by distinguishing pledged finance from deployed infrastructure and by arguing that tariffs on coal-intensive exports can weaken the tax base needed for decarbonization. B's case leaned on several brittle precise claims and late additions, while A stayed more disciplined and gave the cleaner reason to reject the motion as counterproductive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's response that JETP-style pledges do not equal deployed grid capacity, and that trade penalties can damage the industrial revenue needed for the transition, was the decisive exchange. B's later answer relied on ad hoc targeting and green-export upside rather than squarely resolving the physical-readiness problem.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): CON wins by successfully arguing that economic coercion cannot overcome physical and fiscal constraints. CON effectively neutralizes PRO's primary empirical defense (the South Africa JETP) by pointing out that it is currently stalled by the very debt and grid constraints CON warned about, a point PRO drops in the second half of the debate. CON's powerful framing that 'you cannot tariff a grid into existence' and the need to sequence grid build-outs before plant retirements squarely defeats PRO's reliance on rigid deadlines and penalties.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal directly flipping PRO's South Africa JETP example, demonstrating that pledged transition finance is bottlenecked by sovereign debt and grid constraints rather than seamlessly replacing coal.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): CON won by showing that penalties trigger fiscal damage, market fragmentation, and political backlash while failing to address infrastructure timelines, directly damaging PRO's enforcement and fairness claims. PRO repeated climate urgency points without neutralizing those practical risks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.3 on tariffs crushing industrial revenue bases that fund transitions, left largely unanswered despite A4.2's guardrail claim.
