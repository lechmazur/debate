### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B won by exposing the proposition's categorical overbreadth. Side A made a compelling case for regulating commercial exploitation and malicious impersonation, but repeatedly treated a spouse or next of kin as equivalent to the estate. Its admission that a widow could need court permission from a hostile executor confirmed B's central objection. B also correctly showed that accepting licenses for public commercial sales does not concede prior estate consent for every private or hosted simulation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was its response to A's claimed commercial concession: targeted licensing for firms publicly marketing a stranger's identity is narrower than requiring estate permission before anyone simulates that person. This preserved B's distinction between abusive commercialization and private mourning while revealing that A had not justified the motion's full scope.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by rigidly enforcing the exact text of the motion, proving that requiring 'estate' consent for 'any' simulation would lock private mourning behind probate courts. Side B fought back valiantly by pointing out the dangers of a consentless baseline (the stalker/commercial exploitation), but Side A effectively neutralized this by showing that authorization (to privately mourn) and refusal (to stop harassment) do not have to be controlled by the same legal key.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal distinguishing 'authorization' from 'refusal' effectively answered Side B's pressure regarding the stalker, proving we do not need a blanket estate veto to stop abuse.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): CON wins on overbreadth. PRO's core (scraper/stalker, commercial market) is real, but B forced a costly admission at A4.1 that a widow who holds the voicemails is forbidden until she petitions a hostile executor – turning private mourning into a licensed act. B's alternative – holder may use own archive, targeted harassment/commercial-impersonation bans stop abusers – answers the stalker case without estate veto, while PRO concedes the private offline spouse case is covered and cannot name another workable gatekeeper. That makes the motion as written the greater cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Q1 about hostile executor vs widow holding voicemails, answered at A4.1 with Yes – widow must petition – which exposed that PRO's rule locks the kitchen table and undercut PRO's claim that estate = widow protecting mourners.
