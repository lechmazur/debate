PRO’s distinction between legitimate ongoing services and static hardware, combined with sharp pressure questions that exposed CON’s lack of concrete examples, left the core argument unrebutted. CON’s failure to answer why calibration logic applies to heated seats, and its inability to name a case where subscriptions reduced upfront costs, sealed the outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4’s rebuttal to the pressure questions, which exposed the asymmetry between PRO’s documented fact of double-paying for already-installed hardware and CON’s unverified predictions of future market changes, was the most decisive exchange.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) kept the proposition narrow to already-installed, zero-marginal-cost hardware and conceded ongoing services are different, while CON (A) relied on hypothetical financing effects. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully weaponizing Side A's concession regarding 'service contracts' to show that ongoing software calibration for embedded hardware functions essentially operates as a subscription. Side B also maintained a decisive, unanswered economic argument that Side A's rule would force manufacturers to eliminate budget trims or raise base prices.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which systematically dismantled Side A's distinction between "external" service contracts and "internal" hardware by pointing out that liability recalibration and driver-assist tuning directly touch the installed hardware's functionality.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON demonstrated that PRO's distinction between banned subscriptions and permissible service contracts collapses, since ongoing calibration, safety patching, and liability recalibration are intrinsic to installed hardware functions rather than merely external add-ons. PRO never squarely answered why the resolution as written would not either ban necessary recurring costs or reintroduce them through rebranded service contracts. CON also showed the economic tradeoff—eliminating subscription unlocking likely strips shared-SKU hardware from budget trims or raises base prices—that PRO dismissed without engaging its mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and closing crystallized that PRO's "service contract" carve-out functionally permits the exact recurring-payment model the resolution forbids, exposing the proposition as either unworkably broad or a mere labeling exercise.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): CON won by sustaining two live routes: (1) shared-SKU efficiency lets budget buyers pay less, and a permanent-access mandate means stripped hardware or higher prices – PRO never answered beyond "clear sticker". (2) PRO conceded service contracts for safety/calibration, which A showed is still a recurring payment gating installed hardware, collapsing PRO's blanket ban into labeling. PRO repeated ownership intuition without resolving that contradiction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1/R2 collapse – PRO's admission that "service contracts" can charge to keep installed modules calibrated is fatal, because that is a subscription gating hardware, meaning PRO re-authorizes what the resolution bans under a new name.
