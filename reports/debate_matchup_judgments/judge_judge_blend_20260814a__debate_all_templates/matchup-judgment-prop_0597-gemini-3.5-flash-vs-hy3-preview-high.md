- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by making the conditional nature of the motion matter: once serious safety thresholds are met, B’s residual-risk and consent objections looked too absolute compared with the concrete certainty of serious monogenic disease. B was competitive on future autonomy, alternatives, and the limits of multigenerational proof, but often asserted a right to an unmanipulated genome without showing why that should outweigh preventing devastating disease under safe conditions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s answer to the consent argument was decisive: future people cannot consent to disease either, and parents already make major medical choices for children. B’s active-versus-passive reply was clear but did not sufficiently explain why passive transmission of a preventable lethal mutation is morally preferable once safety is met.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's direct A1/A2 answers in Rebuttal 2 that reframed safety thresholds and regulatory realism against CON's absolute-risk standard.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by successfully problematizing the motion's conditional premise and offering a more robust regulatory comparison. While Side B (PRO) effectively argued that inaction carries moral weight and that the motion stipulates met safety thresholds, Side A sharply distinguished between passive disease inheritance and active multigenerational genome editing, noting that short-term safety checks cannot epistemically guarantee centuries of human germline stability. Side B's case was competent but relied on broader regulatory generalizations that Side A concretely answered.

This directly undercut PRO's pragmatic justification for a permissive framework and forced PRO onto the defensive regarding enforceability and legitimization.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): B won by making the safety-threshold premise the decisive clash. A argued compassionately for narrow, regulated use, but never fully solved B's point that heritable safety cannot be proven before first human lineages are created, and A's own model seemed to require either unproven release or reproductive monitoring of descendants. B also sharply undercut A's natural-pregnancy examples by noting embryo editing requires IVF too, so PGD covers most cases and the remaining benefit is mainly genetic relatedness versus donor gametes or adoption. A kept a live benefit in rare PGD-impossible cases, but B weighed it more convincingly against irreversible germline and autonomy risks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was exposing that A's phased human trial model cannot both prove multigenerational safety before use and avoid controlling the reproduction of the first edited cohort.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A prevailed by exposing that PRO's safety thresholds require either releasing unproven edits into the gene pool or imposing dystopian lifelong monitoring on edited children, while showing PGD plus alternatives already suffice without those costs. PRO's focus on present suffering was weakened by failing to neutralize the ethical impossibility of true pre-approval multi-generational safety.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly dismantled PRO's regulatory model by labeling required descendant monitoring as an unacceptable violation of reproductive liberty.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by decisively exploiting a structural flaw in PRO’s safety model. When PRO argued that multi-generational safety requires monitoring edited individuals and their offspring before broader authorization, CON correctly identified this as either a logical impossibility (you cannot verify descendants’ safety before the first birth) or an ethical violation (state-gating the reproductive liberty of edited children). CON also neutralized PRO’s necessity argument by pointing out that germline editing requires IVF anyway, making PGD available in the exact same clinical pathway. PRO’s closing defense relied on standard trial consent norms, which failed to address the unique, non-consenting status of future generations or the IVF equivalence point. CON’s case progressed sharply across rounds, turning PRO’s own regulatory mechanism into the decisive reason to reject the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which dismantled PRO’s safety threshold by showing it forces a choice between releasing unproven edits into the gene pool or legally restricting the reproductive freedom of edited children. This turned PRO’s core safeguard into an ethical liability and crystallized the debate.
