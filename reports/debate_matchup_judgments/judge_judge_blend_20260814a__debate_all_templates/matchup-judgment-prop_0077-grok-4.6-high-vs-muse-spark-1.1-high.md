### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, competing causal stories—PRO on trust-driven reporting, CON on the concrete release of already-identified persons—and each answered pressure questions in proper A1/A2 format. The pivotal exchange came when PRO tried to shift blame for any "manhunt" onto ICE's failure to seek a warrant, claiming sanctuary policies "honor" warrants and offer a "lobby handover while in custody." CON's Turn 8 response sharply exposed the gap: sanctuary policies announce a fixed release time and open the door regardless of whether ICE has obtained a warrant by then, so the handover is not actually controlled once release occurs. PRO's closing repeated the "ICE's fault" framing without directly rebutting this timing distinction, leaving CON's mechanism critique as the more decisive, unanswered point. PRO's rebuttal of the Steinle anecdote and its point that detainers often target non-violent, unconvicted individuals were strong and prevented a larger CON win, but CON's narrower, better-defended mechanism argument gave it the edge in the decisive late clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Turn 8 response ("A warrant honored after release is not a jail transfer... Sanctuary refuses that short window, announces a release time, and opens the door") directly dismantled PRO's "ICE's fault" framing from Rebuttal 2, and PRO never squarely answered this timing distinction in its closing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO won by leveraging CON's concession that a prompt judicial check could fix Fourth Amendment concerns, then showing sanctuary policies already honor judicial warrants—making the post-release manhunt ICE's operational failure, not a city-caused harm. CON's enforcement loss therefore collapsed into either an unconstitutional shortcut or federal paperwork avoidance, while CON never offered a concrete constitutional alternative. On trust, CON's dismissal of witness and victim silence as unmeasured fear proved too thin against PRO's specific, plausible mechanisms, and CON's counter that hurt people call anyway never resolved the predator-exploitation dynamic. PRO's closing crystallized the weighing cleanly: certain community safety versus a rights-evading shortcut.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's capture of CON's B2.5 concession that a prompt judicial check or short lawful window would fix the constitutional problem, using it to reframe the entire enforcement harm as self-inflicted by ICE rather than caused by sanctuary policy.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by effectively dismantling Side A's core "manhunt" and "walk-out" arguments. PRO successfully demonstrated that the release of flagged individuals is a result of federal inaction on judicial warrants rather than city policy, trapping CON in a position where they appeared to blame cities for adhering to the Fourth Amendment. PRO also provided a cleaner comparative weighing of the tangible community trust benefits against the mere loss of an unconstitutional enforcement shortcut. CON had strong rhetoric and pressed well on concrete vs. speculative harms, but struggled to resolve the warrant clash without implicitly advocating for unlawful holds or relying on unverified quantitative assumptions to dismiss PRO's trust metrics.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they crystallized the clash over the judicial warrant mechanism, showing that CON's "manhunt" is caused by ICE's refusal to obtain lawful paperwork before release, thereby collapsing CON's primary enforcement loss argument.
