### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won by showing that preventing deaths among even a reachable subset still reduces citywide mortality, while exposing Side B's false choice between supervised sites and outreach or treatment. Side B raised credible coverage and neighborhood-capacity concerns, but repeatedly treated permission as reliance on one exclusive fixed site and never established that alternatives protect solitary users or must displace supervised care.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest answer was that a site need not reach every user to lower total deaths: moving high-risk use from an unwitnessed location to guaranteed observation changes outcomes. This directly weakened Side B's coverage objection, especially because peer naloxone does not solve genuinely solitary use.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won by decisively defeating Side B's alternative on overdose deaths. Side A successfully pointed out that distributed naloxone fails the solitary user who cannot self-administer when unconscious, whereas a supervised site guarantees a witness. Side B's response—that outreach aims to make use 'less solitary'—was too weak and long-term to beat Side A's immediate life-saving mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's attack in Rebuttal 1 and 2 exposing the fatal flaw in Side B's outreach alternative: naloxone in a pocket cannot be self-administered by an unconscious solitary user, making supervised observation objectively superior for that specific high-risk event.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won by keeping a cleaner live route: deaths prevented under observation subtract from the city total, and regulated indoor use contains disorder that already exists on streets. CON raised real coverage, doorstep-market, and capacity worries, but never neutralized the solitary-user problem or the false treatment-vs-supervision split as cleanly as PRO answered them. PRO’s pressure and closing crystallized the clash; CON’s net-citywide standard stayed partly abstract.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s Q1/A1 exchange on who administers naloxone to the solitary 2 a.m. user, forcing CON’s model to assume a witness it cannot guarantee.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): A won by defending the motion's marginal, comparative burden: supervised sites need not solve all citywide overdoses to save reachable users and reduce visible consumption. Its complement-not-substitute framing weakened B's speculative resource tradeoff, while B never established that mobile teams can reliably supervise the moment of use or that exterior concentration outweighs indoor removal and sanitation. B's falsifiability and chokepoint arguments were sharp, but depended heavily on asserted displacement, weak governance, and raw city totals rather than the relevant counterfactual.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was A's response that B turned “reduce” into “solve alone.” B effectively pressed A on flat citywide totals, but A preserved a credible counterfactual benefit and showed that outreach and supervised sites can operate together rather than compete as mutually exclusive alternatives.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully shifting the metric to city-wide outcomes and exploiting Side A's admission that city totals might remain flat. Side B's argument that this makes Side A's case unfalsifiable was a decisive blow. Furthermore, Side B effectively differentiated the Latin American context, arguing that fixed sites would become taxable chokepoints in areas lacking state capacity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which pointed out that Side A's defense of a flat city-wide death rate ("it can hide prevented deaths when the drug supply worsens") makes the policy's efficacy unfalsifiable.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by keeping the motion on citywide reduction of deaths and disorder, not indoor zeros or unfalsifiable “hidden lives saved” counters. B’s scale/selection critique (tiny daytime queues vs nocturnal solitary use), magnet/chokepoint disorder story, and Latin America capacity/extortion comparison consistently damaged A’s live path; A’s ambulance analogy and “complement not substitute” framing were clean but never fully answered that metric and concentration pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 2 / closing strike that A’s “flat totals can hide lives if supply worsened” makes “reduce” unfalsifiable, paired with the mobile-naloxone-without-a-fixed-queue alternative.
