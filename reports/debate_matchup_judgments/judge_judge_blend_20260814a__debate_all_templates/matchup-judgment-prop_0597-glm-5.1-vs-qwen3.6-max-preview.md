### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): PRO wins a competitive but clear debate. Three live advantages survive the full exchange. First, on PGD: CON's best alternative is genuinely undermined by PRO's dual-carrier scenario, and CON's response—dismissing these families as "statistical edge cases"—is evasive rather than dispositive. These are precisely the families the motion exists for, and telling them to use donor gametes or adopt does not constitute a neutral medical alternative. Second, on oversight: PRO's argument that prohibition removes accountability rather than preventing the practice is never fully neutralized. CON's supply-chain-fragmentation counter is weaker because the underlying logic—permitting editing under rules creates enforcement infrastructure—remains standing. Third, PRO's closing reframe is effective and unaddressed: the disease itself is already irreversible; prohibition merely preserves that irreversibility. CON's "you cannot trial unborn descendants" argument is real but is blunted by PRO correctly noting that medicine routinely manages heritable-implication uncertainty under thresholds rather than demanding infinite certainty. CON's strongest ground is multigenerational propagation, which is genuinely distinct from routine drug approval, and the scope-creep concern lands some pressure. But CON never supplies a mechanism explaining why biotech-specific market pressure defeats guardrails when analogous domains—organ donation, controlled substances, somatic gene therapy—maintain categorical limits under commercial pressure. CON's closing is notably brief and leaves the moral burden question essentially unanswered. PRO's definitional-creep response (treaty-codified disease list, amendment friction) is imperfect but more concrete than CON's counter. The clip on PRO's Rebuttal 1 is a real but minor execution cost that does not change the verdict. On balance, PRO built the cleaner path: the families most burdened by serious monogenic disease deserve a regulated route, and CON never adequately justified denying them that route once safety is established.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing inversion of the irreversibility frame—"Huntington's disease is irreversible; prohibition guarantees the disease's irreversible harm continues unabated"—is the sharpest single exchange. CON spent the entire debate weaponizing irreversibility against editing but never answered the direct counter that the disease propagates through lineages without any intervention at all. This reframe transformed CON's strongest rhetorical weapon into a liability and went unanswered in CON's brief closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's analogies and mechanisms. When B argued that multigenerational risks are routinely managed in medicine (like vaccines), A decisively countered with biological reality: standard medical interventions do not create self-replicating, compounding genetic changes through meiosis. A also effectively neutralized B's black-market argument by explaining how a ban suppresses the highly specialized IVF infrastructure required for editing, whereas legalization industrializes it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4.4) devastatingly dismantled B's comparison of germline editing to maternal vaccines by pointing out that standard interventions do not create self-replicating mutations that compound through meiosis and recombination.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (CON)): PRO wins by defending the narrow, conditional terms of the motion more effectively. The core decisive move is the closing turn on irreversibility: if safety thresholds are met, the remaining certainty is the disease itself, which is irreversible and propagates naturally. PRO successfully shows that PGD is not universally available or effective (dual-carrier families, cost, physical barriers), and that prohibition does not prevent harm but guarantees its continuation for those families. CON's strongest objections—multigenerational uncertainty and market creep—are blunted by the motion's built-in safety condition, and CON never fully justifies why even a safe, narrow intervention should be denied to the families who have no alternative. CON's dismissal of those families as statistical edge cases proves rhetorically and ethically costly, while PRO crystallizes the clash cleanly in the final speech. The debate is close, but PRO's path to victory survives the exchange more intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The irreversibility turn in PRO's closing (A5.2-A5.3), which reframes CON's central objection by showing that heritable disease is already irreversible and that prohibition preserves that harm rather than preventing it. This directly neutralizes CON's multigenerational risk framing and sharpens the moral burden in PRO's favor.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins a clear but competitive debate by working inside the motion's own conditional framing more consistently. The motion says "once safety thresholds are met," and PRO built its case around what rigorous, indication-locked thresholds would actually look like, framing the debate as managed risk versus guaranteed suffering. CON's most powerful move—arguing that intergenerational safety is epistemically unreachable—is philosophically coherent but amounts to a permanent veto regardless of preclinical advances. PRO's "functional veto" reframe in the pressure-questions round and Rebuttal 2 effectively exposed this as an absolute prohibition dressed as a precaution, and CON's own closing confirmed it when Side A said the impossibility of the standard "isn't caution paralyzing medicine; it is medicine recognizing its own limits." That concession helps PRO more than CON. CON's regulatory-failure arguments (stem cells, organ trafficking, jurisdictional shopping) are grounded in real-world examples and genuinely strong, but PRO's own organ-trafficking analogy did not clearly backfire because CON never exploited it directly. CON's clipped opening is a real execution cost in the foundational speech, even though subsequent rounds recovered most of the content. PRO's indication-locked enforcement argument is somewhat assertoric, but CON never demonstrated why it would uniquely fail beyond asserting that comparable lines always have—an equally assertoric claim at that level of generality. On the moral weighing, PRO's argument that denying biologically related children to families facing Huntington's or Tay-Sachs is "institutionalized neglect" remained live through the close, while CON's unconsenting-future-generations framing, though morally weighty, was partially undercut by the fact that those future generations would not exist without the edit in the first place.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's functional-veto reframe across Rebuttal 2 and the closing was the most decisive exchange. By showing that CON's demand for multi-generational human data cannot be satisfied before deployment—making prohibition permanent regardless of preclinical advances—PRO converted CON's cautionary standard into an ethical liability. CON's closing response ("this isn't caution paralyzing medicine; it is medicine recognizing its own limits") was honest but effectively confirmed PRO's characterization: CON's position does not permit the technology under any conceivable safety regime, which the motion explicitly contemplates permitting once thresholds are crossed.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that the motion's prerequisite ("once safety thresholds are met") is epistemically impossible to fulfill for an irreversible, intergenerational intervention. Side A also effectively dismantled Side B's regulatory enforcement mechanisms by pointing to real-world jurisdictional shopping.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's brilliant turn of Side B's "paradox" accusation in closing: "It is no paradox; it is the argument," perfectly crystallizing why preclinical models can never satisfy the precautionary burden for germline editing.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.6 Max Preview (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): PRO won by showing that CON's demand for multi-generational human data sets an epistemically unreachable bar that functionally vetoes the motion's conditional framework, forcing present families into preventable suffering. PRO grounded its case in concrete disease examples, turned irreversibility into a therapeutic benefit, and offered a plausible indication-locked regulatory architecture with international auditing that directly answered CON's creep and jurisdictional-shopping concerns. CON's clipped opening and reliance on generalized regulatory pessimism left its counter-case comparatively abstract and less able to overcome PRO's moral urgency.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's functional-veto rebuttal across Rebuttal 1 and the Closing, which exposed that CON's safety standard would permanently ban the technology regardless of preclinical advances, shifting the burden from managed risk to impossible certainty and making CON's position feel like institutionalized neglect.
