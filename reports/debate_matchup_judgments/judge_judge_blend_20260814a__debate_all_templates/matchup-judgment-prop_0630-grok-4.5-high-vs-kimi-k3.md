### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): CON built a disciplined "fork" strategy early (B2.6): either PRO's promised checks are genuine, in which case the regime converges on CON's proportional framework and the motion is empty, or the checks are thin enough to preserve speed, in which case unintended integrations slip through. PRO's pressure-round answers (A4.1, A4.2) walked directly into this trap, conceding independent re-sequencing of every finished crop for foreign DNA, rearrangements, and composition — precisely the "one framework" CON had been proposing. CON's Rebuttal 2 and closing exploited this concession forcefully ("That is my framework wearing their label"), while also landing two under-rebutted structural objections: that "known pathway" triggers cannot catch the unknown risks regulation exists to find, and that a paper-trail border system catches only honest shippers, not the unlabeled violator. PRO's closing reasserted that checks are "shorter and narrower" but never explained the mechanism distinguishing this from CON's proposal, leaving the concession largely unresolved. PRO's case was coherent and well-argued in isolation, but CON's cross-examination and follow-through were sharper and more decisive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's fork in B2.6, cashed in when PRO's pressure-question answers (A4.1) conceded independent regulators re-sequencing every finished crop — CON's B4 and closing then used this admission to argue PRO had adopted CON's framework while keeping only its unsafe gaps (capped triggers, invisible paperwork-based border checks).

When Side B clarified that it would only screen 'known' pathways and rely on paper trails, Side A effectively weaponized those details to prove Side B's framework was dangerously blind to unexpected hazards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which seamlessly used Side B's answers from the Pressure Questions to prove that Side B had adopted Side A's architecture but fatally added unsafe 'capped scrutiny' and 'invisible violations'.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more coherent case around a single decisive incoherence: unregulated random mutagenesis versus heavily regulated precise edits with identical or better safety characterization. A's pressure questions (the twin wheat hypothetical and the "your own fallback needs the same line" point) forced B into a corner. B's answer in Rebuttal 2 — "if the plants and drought-tolerance phenotype match after full verification, the same rules apply" — effectively conceded A's core principle that risk-equivalent plants deserve rule-equivalent treatment, and A's closing correctly seized on this as "That is my case." B's attempt in closing to redraw a distinction between "same rules" and "streamlined exemption" came across as backpedaling rather than a clean rebuttal. B maintained a respectable trait-based framework and raised real concerns about multiplex edits and enforcement of "limited," but never resolved why the historical mutagenesis exemption isn't itself evidence for A's parity argument, repeatedly falling back on "historical accident" without engagement. A's progression across rounds was sharper: the mutagenesis contradiction, the precedent count, and the access/moat argument were reinforced and never squarely neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Pressure Question on the twin drought-tolerant wheat plants (one mutagenized, one edited) forced B to answer in Rebuttal 2 that identical verified plants get identical rules — a concession A then used decisively in closing to claim B had adopted A's own framework.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B decisively won the debate by exposing a contradiction in Side A's stance on process vs. trait regulation. Side B's concrete answers in Rebuttal 2 cleanly neutralized Side A's pressure on traceability and line-drawing, while Side A struggled to answer the "identical plants" hypothetical without either contradicting its trait-based standard or demanding an unrealistic overhaul of all existing mutagenesis rules.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's A2 in Rebuttal 2, which pointed out that Side A's fallback—relying on "ordinary variety rules" for natural-looking changes—requires drawing the exact same boundary Side A just claimed was impossible to draw.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): PRO won the central trait-vs-process clash. CON claimed to be trait-based while defending full GMO review for precise single-letter edits and excusing random mutagenesis as historical accident. PRO's twin-wheat pressure exposed it, and CON conceded in B4.2 that identical plants should have identical rules - which is PRO's product-based streamlined model. PRO also answered monitoring via seed-lot traceability and gave workable bright lines, while CON offered only slippery-slope warnings and no harms from a decade of exemptions in six jurisdictions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The twin-wheat identical plant exchange. A's Q2 in Pressure forced B to admit identical phenotype = same rules in B4.2, which A then crystallized in closing as concession that conventional-like edits deserve conventional rules with added molecular docs.
