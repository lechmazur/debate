This directly undermined CON's earlier claims that corporate credits adequately serve public-interest research and that merit panels are more censorious than corporate gatekeepers. PRO's closing capitalized on this effectively, quoting CON's own words as the structural lynchpin of the whole debate. PRO's CERN and Human Genome Project analogies for treaty-bound coordination were well-chosen and grounded, and the transparency/FOIA argument in A4 gave a more concrete answer to the gatekeeping worry than CON provided for its analogous corporate-credentialing concern. The clipping of multiple PRO turns imposed some execution cost but didn't erase the decisive comparative advantage PRO built across the middle and closing rounds. CON's closing failed to address the self-undermining B4 concession and instead recycled earlier efficiency arguments, leaving the gate-control comparison firmly in PRO's favor.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): B's most decisive analytical move was the API-vs-compute distinction [B2.1, B3.1]: A's flagship example (firms restricting misinformation auditors in 2023) was about access to closed model APIs, not about underlying compute, so a public GPU pool would not have helped those researchers. A's A1 response — red-teaming leaked weights and training open-weight benchmarks — was creative but partial; it conceded the closed-model auditing problem and pivoted to a narrower use case. B then offered a credible, targeted alternative throughout: a statutory right-to-audit with mandatory non-discriminatory API access, enforceable by an independent regulator, that directly solves the stated lock-out without building government infrastructure. A never adequately neutralized this alternative — pointing out that firms could "throttle compute for auditors" under B's regime is a real concern, but B's proposal specifically covered non-discriminatory compute access, which A glossed over. On efficiency, B consistently maintained that competitive cloud markets deliver higher utilization and elastic capacity than centralized pools, and A's counter (corporate overprovisioning that could be reallocated) was asserted rather than demonstrated. A's broader claims — early-career researchers, Global South science, fragmented lab waste — were real and B never fully addressed their breadth, which is why the margin stays competitive rather than dominant. B's multiple clipped speeches are a real execution cost reducing the margin, but the visible content sustained the core analytical advantage throughout the exchange. B's closing crystallized the bottleneck mismatch cleanly: the proposition builds infrastructure to solve a model-access problem it cannot touch.
