- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won by keeping the debate centered on the proposition: programs where most students do not complete create debt, lost time, and weak labor-market signals, while shorter pathways reduce time at risk and deliver tangible credentials. B had a real challenge on partial college value, transfers, and vocational attrition, but leaned on overbroad claims that unfinished degrees often outperform completed technical credentials and never fully answered A's opportunity-cost framing. B was also hurt by clipped turns and some exaggerated late claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was answering B's partial-value argument by asking who actually receives those benefits in low-completion programs, then contrasting completed technical credentials with debt and no degree. Side B effectively neutralized Side A's claims by arguing that a ladder most students fall off of is a liability, and cleanly explained the mechanical advantage of shorter programs reducing the "time at risk."
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 decisively answered the automation and ceiling arguments by contrasting the "floor" an indebted dropout faces against the "foothold" a completed technical credential provides.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A built the stronger case by keeping the focus on the proposition's core metric (completion) and directly answering pressure questions with structural comparisons on time-at-risk and credential signaling.
