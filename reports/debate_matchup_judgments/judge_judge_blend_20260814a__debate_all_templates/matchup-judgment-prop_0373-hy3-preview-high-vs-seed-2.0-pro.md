### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued cleanly and followed format well, but PRO built the more decisive comparative case. PRO's concrete disproportion argument — that a single complaint triggering total removal for all ages and patrons, with no formal review, is the actual "too far" line — was never squarely answered by CON, who repeatedly retreated to "this is just a tradeoff, no baseline was breached" without engaging why a narrow, unproven risk to young children justifies erasing access for older teens and adults. CON's strongest moves were procedural: exposing that PRO's "never reinstated" statistic rested on contested advocacy-group methodology, and correctly catching PRO's pressure question mischaracterizing a "concession" that was never made (a strong, well-labeled A1/A2 answer). But CON's own claim that these organizations "openly admit" inflating counts was itself an unverified, quasi-invented factual assertion, weakening its grounding. CON's feasibility argument (age-gating is "soft guidance," so full removal is the only enforceable tool) explains why removal happens but does not rebut PRO's proportionality complaint about why a single complaint should trigger the maximal remedy without review. PRO's canonical-works comparison and concrete examples (Pulitzer memoir, LGBTQ+ memoirs pulled district-wide) gave content to what "too far" means, which CON's abstract "messy negotiation" framing never fully neutralized.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The clash over "single complaint triggers full, unreviewed removal" — PRO's disproportion example went substantively unanswered by CON's "it's just a tradeoff, no line was crossed" framing, leaving PRO's core overreach claim live through the closing.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): PRO built the clearer route by repeatedly forcing the overbreadth clash: removals from high-school and adult collections when the conceded risk is only for the youngest. CON's operational-necessity and "messy negotiation" defenses were damaged by PRO's existing-infrastructure reply and by B's own concession that full removal does not protect better than soft sectioning. Pressure questions from A exposed the permanent-vs-temporary gap most cleanly; B never restored an objective boundary, leaving dislike-of-outcomes as its residual claim, which cannot defeat the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 (and follow-through in A4): if soft guidance already fails to stop a 7-year-old, full high-school removal adds no extra protection—collapsing CON's only-tool argument.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (CON)): PRO held the proposition's built-in concession and made the decisive comparison live: removing a title from high schools and adult public collections because it is unsuitable for 7-year-olds is overreach. CON's core operational defense collapsed when Q1 forced the admission that full high-school removal does not protect young children better than elementary-only restriction, and separate school buildings already provide age-gating. CON's statistical-inflation and prior-self-censorship points scored but did not answer that tradeoff, while PRO's misattribution in Q2 was corrected but did not outweigh the conceded central harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 pressing soft-guidance vs high-school removal, answered in B4.1 with "It does not protect them better" and retreat to only-enforceable-tool, which conceded PRO's overreach mechanism.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): B controlled the framing from the opening onward, distinguishing legitimate age-sorting from blanket, district-wide removal, and repeatedly forced A to answer for the actual practice rather than the idealized policy on paper. When pressed with specific pointed questions about rising removal rates and titles vanishing at every grade level, A largely deflected with recontextualization ("more engagement," "not absent, just not in this building") rather than directly refuting the empirical claims. B, by contrast, answered A's demand for a "too far" baseline with a concrete, workable standard (age-banding, transparent review, no total removal absent zero educational value) and then showed how the current process departs from that very standard A never disputed. A's final rebuttal was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, cutting off its closing argument and costing it needed follow-through. B's closing crystallized this asymmetry cleanly: A described how curation is supposed to work, not what is happening, and never rebutted that gap.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q1/Q2 on removal rate increases and titles vanishing at every grade level) combined with A's evasive, non-quantitative answers in Rebuttal 2 was the decisive exchange—it exposed that A had no direct rebuttal to B's core empirical claim and could only reassert generalities.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): A won by keeping the motion's "gone too far" frame live: even granting age-inappropriateness, current process (single-complaint total removals across ages, skipped reviews) abandoned a prior workable baseline of age-banding plus transparent review. B repeatedly described ideal curation, formal reviews, re-shelving, and external availability while treating those as the norm, but never squarely neutralized A's scale and practice claims or stopped strawmanning A as demanding unlimited access. B's clipped Rebuttal 2 further limited its cleanup. A's progression and weighing made the clash easier to decide.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 (A2.1-A2.6) exposing B's ideal-process description as non-responsive to actual total-pull practices
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO won by holding the proposition's concession: even if some books are age-inappropriate, practice has exceeded it. A established a clear baseline (age-banding, transparent review, no total removal) and hammered the rulebook-vs-reality clash. B never refuted the core factual claims — total removals across all grades after single informal complaints, tenfold spike, and challenges to non-sexual content — and relied on the "buy elsewhere" justification A sharply turned. B also incurred a clear execution cost by exceeding the word limit in Rebuttal 2, clipping its key defense.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 [A2.1-A2.6] — exposing B's ideal-world curation narrative as misdirection vs actual total removal, single-complaint veto, and library equity argument.
