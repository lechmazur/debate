### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): B won by keeping the focus on equal consumer-protection duties for actors with comparable reach and by successfully resisting A's attempt to define the motion as identical broadcast mechanics. A had a strong redundancy and overbreadth argument, but it leaned too much on a rigid version of parity that the proposition did not require and used somewhat asserted claims about current rules already solving the problem. B's explanation that equivalent strictness can be delivered through prominent platform-appropriate notice made the policy workable while preserving the main harm story: undisclosed paid persuasion at mass scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal was decisive: it answered A's strongest pressure by distinguishing strict equivalent notice from literal full-screen broadcast formalities, then offered cross-platform reach as a workable threshold, blunting A's authenticity and arbitrariness attacks.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B won by successfully forcing PRO into a strategic dilemma. B proved that if 'as strict as' means traditional broadcast mechanics, it ruins the medium, and when PRO retreated to 'practical adaptations' like spoken acknowledgments or hashtags, B smartly pointed out that PRO was simply describing the existing FTC status quo. PRO also committed a major unforced error in the Pressure Questions by asking CON to solve PRO's own burden of defining a 'large' audience threshold.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 decisively capitalized on PRO's pressure question blunder and cleanly crystallized the dilemma that PRO's 'strict parity' either crushes authenticity or just replicates existing rules.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): CON wins because PRO cannot defend "as strict as" traditional media without either demanding rigid broadcast formalities that destroy authentic influencer content—a burden PRO itself tries to avoid by allowing simple text or spoken disclosures—or conceding that context-appropriate notice suffices, which collapses strict parity into the tailored framework already in place. CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing crystallize this dilemma decisively, while PRO never resolves how cross-platform audience thresholds would work non-arbitrarily.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing demonstrating that PRO's proposed "simple adaptations" (spoken acknowledgments, on-screen text) are precisely the medium-sensitive disclosures current rules already require, proving PRO abandons strict parity while pretending to defend it.
