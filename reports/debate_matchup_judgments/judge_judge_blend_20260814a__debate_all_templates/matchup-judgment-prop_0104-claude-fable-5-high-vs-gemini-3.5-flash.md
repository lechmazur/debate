### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B won clearly. B built a strong mechanism linking unsecured home guns to youth suicide and accidental injury, then weighed that against a comparatively limited burden with lawful alternatives like carrying or quick-access storage. A’s self-defense burden was rhetorically forceful but remained mostly asserted: it relied on rare home-invasion scenarios, assumed storage equals disarmament, and never gave a convincing comparative reason that delayed access costs outweigh youth deaths prevented. B also exposed A’s tension between saying the laws are unenforceable and saying law-abiding owners will comply, then used A’s own compliance premise to support prevention. A’s later speeches repeated “functional disarmament” and “paper promise” without squarely defeating B’s core suicide-prevention mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s most decisive exchange was pressing A to name the actual legal requirement that disarms homeowners, then arguing that the burden is not confiscation but choosing among lawful storage options, while the prevention mechanism operates through parents’ compliance before a teen suicide impulse arises.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clear, largely conceded causal mechanism—youth gun deaths run through unsecured home firearms and impulsive teen suicide is uniquely lethal—and showing that the burden side lacked a coherent statutory foundation. A exposed decisive contradictions in B's case (laws cannot be simultaneously unenforceable and a severe daily burden; responsible owners cannot both already secure guns and be newly disarmed), while B never identified a specific legal requirement that prevents armed self-defense or overcame A's probability weighing: a marginal delay in a rare emergency versus a loaded gun available to a child every night. A's comparative framing held through the final speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's first rebuttal contradiction turn, which revealed that B's burden story was internally inconsistent—laws could not be both unenforceable paper promises and severe daily disarmament—and that if responsible owners already secured their guns, the law burdened only the careless minority whose weapons were causing the deaths.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A won by establishing a clearer causal mechanism (laws change parent storage behavior before a teen's impulsive crisis) and consistently winning the probability weighing. B's compliance paradox argument was competent but never overcame A's asymmetry frame: a marginal delay in a rare home invasion versus a loaded gun accessible to a suicidal youth every night. A's rebuttals were sharper, directly answered pressure, and crystallized the clash decisively in the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) dismantled B's efficacy and burden claims by clarifying that the law targets parent behavior, not teen deterrence, and by forcing B to concede that no statute actually bans the cited self-defense workarounds, reducing B's core burden to a preference rather than a legal mandate.

Close, but PRO's practical mechanism and burden comparison survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to the "two-second safe" dilemma was the key exchange: a private PIN can be fast for the owner while still blocking a child or teen, which weakened A's attempt to make effectiveness and low burden mutually exclusive.

The comparative ledger therefore favors CON.
