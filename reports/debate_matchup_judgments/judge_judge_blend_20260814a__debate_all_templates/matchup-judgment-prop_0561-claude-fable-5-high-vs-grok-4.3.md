### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): A won by narrowing the debate to the motion's distinctive feature: benefits tied to hours. B established a plausible need for mandatory gig-worker security, but A largely conceded that goal and showed why hours are a poor metric: multi-app double counting, idle-time exclusion, and incentives for algorithmic rationing. B's later concession to portable cash accounts weakened the contrast with A's alternative, and B's claim that A lacked compulsion was persuasively answered as a misread. B had a decent final push that payout-based levies can be deflated, but it came late and did not overcome A's cleaner metric comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 was decisive: it directly answered B's pressure questions, reframed the alternative as a mandated smooth payout levy, and turned B's portable-account concession into evidence that only the hours metric remained contested.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Side A systematically isolated and dismantled the specific mechanism of the proposition ("tied to hours worked"), successfully proving that hours are fundamentally incompatible with gig work and would lead to algorithmic rationing. Side B misread Side A's alternative as voluntary and failed to resolve the multi-apping dilemma or defend against the "cliffs" vulnerability they inadvertently admitted to.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal decisively won the debate by using Side B's own admission of "thresholds or coverage cliffs" to prove that tying benefits to hours inevitably leads to algorithmic rationing, while clarifying that Side A's alternative was always a mandated, continuous levy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON successfully narrowed the debate to the word "hours" and proved that metric creates unique pathologies—definitional ambiguity in multi-app gig work and threshold effects that invite algorithmic rationing and capping—while a payout-based levy into a portable account achieves the same worker protections without those failure modes. PRO conceded too much by accepting the portable-account architecture, leaving only the hours-tied mechanism to defend, and never resolved the threshold-gaming problem or showed hours to be more objective than payouts in gig contexts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4), which demonstrated that the only remaining dispute was the metric "hours," that PRO had effectively conceded the portable-account architecture, and that hours create step-function liabilities vulnerable to algorithmic capping while payout levies are smooth and continuous.
