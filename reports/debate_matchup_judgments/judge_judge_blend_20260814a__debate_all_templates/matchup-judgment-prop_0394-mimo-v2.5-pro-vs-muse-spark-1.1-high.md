- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides argued the motion as written and engaged directly with the "presumptively / strong pedagogical reason" language, but B built a tighter, more consistently exploited case. B also sustained a coherent quality-vs-quantity frame (accurate targeted captions vs. error-prone mass automation, e.g., garbled dosages) that A never fully neutralized beyond asserting that imperfect access beats none. A's burden-shifting frame and "weeks of waiting" example were strong and repeated effectively, but A's own alternative rebuttal to B's "individualized system has failed for decades" critique was never squarely answered by B either, leaving a mutual gap. What tips the balance is execution: A's Opening, Rebuttal 1, and Rebuttal 2 were all clipped for exceeding word limits, repeatedly cutting off A's argument at its concluding, weight-bearing sentences, which is a real and recurring cost per the judging criteria. B, by contrast, delivered complete, disciplined speeches that directly answered pressure questions with clear A1/A2 labels and consistently sharpened rather than widened the clash. The combination of sharper rebuttal work and cleaner execution gives B the edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's "publication with a password" rebuttal (B4.5) to A's claim that sensitive seminars could be recorded with "restricted access" — it exposed a real internal tension in A's case about what the pedagogical exception actually protects.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but A more effectively resolved the central clash it framed early: who should bear the risk of imperfection—the institution (justify withholding) or the student (prove need). A's answer to B's strongest point ("garbled auto-captions look authoritative and are dangerous") was sharp and turned the argument back on B by noting live lectures and student notes are equally imperfect yet never withheld, and by proposing a concrete labeling fix. B's rejoinder (institutional imprimatur differs from informal notes) was reasonable but didn't fully neutralize the point. In the Pressure Questions exchange, A's answers (A1/A2) were more direct and responsive—especially the clean "faculty remains judge, standard is exclusion vs. control" answer on the trauma-seminar question—whereas B's second answer largely reasserted its alternative without concretely refuting why case-by-case gatekeeping is less bureaucratic than default-on-with-opt-out. B also suffered real execution costs: both its opening and closing were clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning its final crystallizing point and part of its foundational case never fully landed. A's closing cleanly crystallized the burden-of-risk framing that ran through the debate, giving the clearer decision rule for a judge to apply. Minor grounding deduction to A for the unsupported "85% transcript" figure, but this didn't undermine the structural clarity of A's case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 turning B's "actively dangerous garbled transcript" argument back on itself by noting live lectures and student notes are equally imperfect yet never withheld, paired with the direct, standard-setting answer on who judges pedagogical exceptions in the trauma-seminar case.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by effectively framing the debate around who bears the burden of accessing learning and successfully defending the proposition's 'pedagogical exception' clause as sufficient to protect faculty. Side B's closing crystallization ("imperfect help now beats perfect help never") was highly persuasive. Side A also suffered minor execution costs from clipped opening and closing turns.
