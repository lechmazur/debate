B's food-security claims also never engaged A's point that diversification, not entrenchment, is the more resilient posture. A's repeated clipping for exceeding word limits is a real execution cost, but the visible portions were still the stronger comparative case. B's under-length speeches and repetitive closing phrases ("fragile moonshot," "laws of thermodynamics") diminished its sharpness without adding new weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 pressure question—asking whether marginal pastures are being used to shield the whole sector—was the most decisive exchange. B's response in B4.1 continued defending the entire sector with the same marginal-land examples rather than distinguishing which subsidy recipients those examples actually represent, thereby conceding A's core framing that the bulk of conventional agriculture support covers intensive, high-emission supply chains, not efficient smallholder grazing.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): PRO ran a coherent and grounded case centered on the thermodynamic ceiling of animal agriculture, climate vulnerability, and the subsidy-as-catalyst mechanism (the solar analogy). Those arguments retained real force throughout. CON's best move was consistently anchoring to the motion's high bar—"far more"—and showing that PRO never supplied a credible readiness threshold to justify that magnitude of shift. CON's pressure question Q1 was the sharpest single moment in the debate: demanding a concrete threshold exposed that PRO's answer ("pilot scale viability") is far too thin to justify redirecting the bulk of agricultural subsidy support. CON's Rebuttal 2 converted that answer into a clean summary: PRO wants reallocation first and proof later, which is backwards in a domain where failures become food-price crises. CON also landed a genuine dent in the solar analogy by distinguishing fungible electricity from food, where taste, nutrition, allergenicity, and consumer trust are non-trivial. CON's alternative path (milestone-linked R&D, pilot grants, reformed livestock subsidies) was credible and consistently presented, giving a live route to vote CON without endorsing the status quo. PRO's rebuttals on concentration (bioreactors can be local cooperatives) and the capital-source question were plausible in principle but remained at the level of "attractive hopes" rather than mechanisms. PRO's thermodynamic ceiling argument was never really defeated, but it supports "some shift" more cleanly than "far more," which is the motion as written. CON's three clipped speeches are a real execution cost, but the core arguments were visible and intact before the clips. On balance, CON built a cleaner comparative case on the decisive "far more" framing and managed the pressure round more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question Q1—demanding a concrete threshold for "far more" support—and PRO's answer that pilot-scale viability is sufficient. CON's Rebuttal 2 crystallized this: pilot viability proves a technology can work under controlled conditions but does not prove staple-scale affordability, contamination resilience, regulatory clearance, or consumer acceptance. This exchange exposed PRO's core burden gap and became the decisive leverage point for CON's closing summary that PRO wants reallocation first and proof later.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by effectively leveraging the exact wording of the motion ('shift far more') to force Side A to defend a massive reallocation of food base subsidies for a technology that A admitted was only at 'pilot scale.'   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's dismantling of the solar analogy by pointing out that electricity is a fungible commodity, whereas food involves taste, digestibility, and cultural acceptance, making a massive early reallocation much riskier.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON successfully framed the motion as a premature lopsided reallocation rather than prudent diversification. They distinguished food from fungible commodities like electricity, deflated PRO's solar analogy, and showed that milestone-based funding from reform and research budgets could support alternative proteins without hollowing out a functioning food system. PRO's admission that pilot-scale viability justified shifting far more agricultural support proved too high a burden to defend against CON's food-security risk analysis, and their conditional subsidy designs remained undemonstrated hopes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal to PRO's pressure questions showing that staged R&D, loan guarantees, and procurement trials already provide a subsidy bridge without draining conventional agriculture, paired with the exposure that pilot-scale viability is an insufficient threshold for far more reallocation.
