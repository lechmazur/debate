### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO wins by holding firm to the motion's conditional frame. The proposition is explicitly limited to cases "where revenue is transparently used," and PRO correctly and persistently identified that CON spent most of the debate disputing whether that condition is commonly met rather than whether the practice should be legal when it is. This is PRO's most durable live route to victory and it was never squarely neutralized. The decisive blow lands in closing when PRO exploits CON's B4.1 concession — "My position does not require criminalizing flawlessly managed concessions" — to show that CON effectively agrees with the motion's specific scope while arguing around it. CON's structural critique (that policy built on a rarely-met condition is institutional malpractice) is valid as far as it goes, but it substitutes a different question for the one on the table. CON's strongest individual arguments — the infanticide cascade mechanism and the one-time versus recurring revenue comparison — were real pressure, but PRO addressed them at the population level with the anti-poaching funding argument, which CON never concretely dismantled. PRO's geographic/marginal-land argument was imperfectly defended (PRO's A2 answer to CON's Pressure Question named vague categories rather than engaging CON's specific named ecosystems), which is a notable execution cost, but it was not overturned. CON performed well on biology and rhetoric, particularly the "protector versus profiteer" framing and the recurring-revenue comparison, but the cumulative weight of arguing outside the motion's frame, combined with the B4.1 self-undermining concession, gives PRO the cleaner path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's exploitation of CON's B4.1 concession in the closing is the most decisive exchange. When CON wrote "My position does not require criminalizing flawlessly managed concessions," PRO correctly identified this as conceding the motion's precise scope and crystallized it as the deciding reason to vote PRO. CON's recovery framing — that "flawlessly managed" is merely the exception the motion depends on — was too little too late and still leaves CON without a clear affirmative reason to ban the practice in the cases the motion actually covers.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): B won by keeping the debate anchored to the motion's conditional: places where revenue is transparently used for conservation and livelihoods. A made strong attacks on corruption, biology, and alternatives, but much of that challenged whether the condition usually holds rather than whether hunting should remain legal when it does. A's A4.1 admission that flawlessly managed concessions need not be criminalized seriously undercut the CON position. B also did better weighing on remote habitat, community incentives, and population-level conservation tradeoffs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's repeated rebuttal that A was debating mismanaged trophy hunting rather than the motion's transparent-use cases was decisive, especially when paired with the pressure question that forced A to concede that flawlessly managed concessions need not be banned.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON opened with sharp empirical critiques and biological arguments, but PRO successfully anchored the debate to the motion's explicit conditional scope: programs where revenue is actually transparent. CON's admission in Rebuttal 2 that their position "does not require criminalizing flawlessly managed concessions" [A4.1] proved strategically costly, as PRO crystallized this as fatal to CON's ability to oppose the motion as written. While CON's "liquidating the asset" and infanticide lines created real pressure, PRO's pragmatic habitat-protection framework, stronger geographic specificity, and effective weighing of community incentives vs. outsider moral discomfort provided a cleaner reason to affirm. PRO consistently narrowed the dispute to the text of the motion and punished CON for critiques that largely applied outside the stated condition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Turn 7 rebuttal [B4.1-B4.3] and Turn 9 closing [B5.1] exposing the tension in CON's stance on flawlessly managed concessions, using the "perfect enemy of the good" frame to neutralize CON's corruption critique by bracketing it outside the motion's explicit conditional.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO built a more durable case across the full exchange. Three live advantages survive the debate. First, the proposition's limiting conditions—transparent accounting, measurable conservation outcomes, direct community benefit—are genuine operative constraints that CON repeatedly treated as ornamental. CON's genetic-harm and demand-sustaining arguments assume a system with no regulatory backbone; PRO's repeated reminder that wildlife authorities set quotas against population models, not by client checkbook, was adequate rather than decisive, but CON never produced a compelling mechanism for why the safeguards in the proposition's text must fail rather than merely sometimes fail. Second, PRO's "demand interception" rebuttal is logically tight: the prestige demand already exists independently of legal trophy programs; banning the regulated channel does not extinguish the demand but removes the only traceable, reinvestment-linked version of it, concentrating all pressure into illegal markets. CON's counter—that legal exports validate the demand culturally—is real but was not developed into a full causal story that could outweigh PRO's mechanism. Third, and most importantly, PRO's challenge to CON's alternatives ("not at scale in arid, remote conservancies") was never concretely answered. CON's A1 pivot to Namibia being "driven by non-consumptive tourism" recontextualized the example rather than defeating it, and the Kenya 1977 reference was dropped in late without acknowledging that Kenya's wildlife numbers have been deeply troubled since that ban—a gap PRO could have exploited but didn't need to. CON's "GEF channels billions" claim was exactly the vague, overconfident broadening that the judging criteria discount. PRO's "scaling, not scaled" framing was simpler and better grounded. CON's biological paradox argument—that landscapes too poor for eco-tourism are also too poor for sustainable hunting—was the most original pressure point and created genuine tension, but PRO had already established Namibia as a real-world refutation rather than a theoretical one. On balance, PRO controlled the central clash more clearly: the proposition asks whether transparent, conservation-linked trophy hunting should remain legal where it already works, and CON never showed a mechanism that concretely replaces working programs rather than programs that might someday work.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2 in Rebuttal 2 was the most decisive exchange: "Regulated trophy programs don't invent that demand; they intercept a fraction of it and route the revenue back into protecting the populations the demand threatens. Banning legal trophies does not extinguish desire; it removes the traceable, auditable channel and concentrates all of that demand into illegal markets with zero conservation reinvestment." CON's follow-up—that legal exports sustain cultural prestige—was a reasonable reply but never came with a mechanism showing that eliminating the legal channel actually reduces total demand rather than merely shifting it underground, leaving PRO's version of the comparison standing.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): B won by keeping the debate tied to the proposition's conditional scope: transparent, conservation-linked hunting where it is already funding conservation and livelihoods. A raised serious biological and commodification objections, but often overstated them as universal and did not prove that non-lethal funding reliably replaces trophy revenue in the specific remote or low-density places at issue. B's pluralism frame, safeguards argument, and present-tense replacement challenge gave the cleaner reason to keep legality.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's answer to eco-tourism and alternative funding was the decisive exchange: B argued that tourism, grants, and credits may help but are not interchangeable or reliably scaled in remote arid conservancies, while A's Kenya and global-funding response did not prove replacement for the exact local revenue stream the motion concerns.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): CON won by demonstrating that trophy hunting causes inherent biological harm that transparency cannot fix, that the "post-prime" regulatory ideal collapses under market and financial pressure, and that PRO's claimed absence of alternatives is contradicted by existing large-scale non-lethal funding already operating in comparable arid contexts. PRO's case suffered from an unresolved paradox: arid lands allegedly too marginal for tourism were simultaneously claimed to produce sustainable hunting surpluses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn in Rebuttal 2 using Kenya's post-1977 hunting ban and international grant funding to directly answer PRO's pressure questions about where alternatives function at scale, undermining PRO's core "no present replacement" argument.
