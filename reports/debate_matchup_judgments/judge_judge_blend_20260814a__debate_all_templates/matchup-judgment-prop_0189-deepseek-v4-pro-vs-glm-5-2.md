PRO's strongest rebuttal—that no alternative achieves zero sewage discharge—was present but underdeveloped in later rounds, leaving CON's middle-path argument live at the close.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO built a cleaner and more durable path to victory across the exchange. The core physical claim—that removing human sewage from any discharge strictly lowers the contamination ceiling—was never actually overturned by CON. CON's best move was the chronic-versus-episodic reframe: combined systems treat light-rain runoff, separated systems discharge it untreated, and chronic low-level toxicity can be more ecologically damaging than intermittent sewage events. That is a legitimate and technically grounded argument. But PRO answered it directly in Rebuttal 2 by pointing out that separation is a prerequisite for deploying green infrastructure—bioswales, retention basins, permeable pavement—that treats stormwater at its source; combined systems structurally cannot route stormwater through these tools because it is funneled into the sanitary pipe. CON never adequately closed that gap in either subsequent speech. CON's closing repeats the chronic-pollution point without accounting for the green-infrastructure counter, leaving a live concession on the table. PRO's pressure question on tunnel incremental expansion also landed: CON's answer (relief tunnels, offline caverns) is plausible but does not escape the rigidity critique, and PRO correctly identified in closing that adding capacity to a bored tunnel still requires another major excavation. CON suffered three clipping penalties for exceeding word limits in Rebuttal 1, Rebuttal 2, and one more—a real execution cost that reduced visible argumentative density. CON's 80–90% overflow-reduction figure and "sharp increases in pathogen loads" assertion both carried some unsupported specificity. PRO's framing ("paying rent on a broken system") and the fire-suppression metaphor were rhetorically effective and well-integrated rather than decorative. On balance, PRO's case grew stronger as the debate narrowed; CON's case stalled at the chronic-exposure point without completing the rebuttal loop.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A4.1–A4.2 response to CON's Q1 was the most decisive exchange. CON's chronic-pollution pressure question assumed separated stormwater must run untreated into waterways. PRO answered directly that separation unlocks green infrastructure—bioswales, retention basins, permeable surfaces—that intercepts stormwater before it reaches the outfall, and that combined systems cannot deploy these tools because stormwater is co-mingled in the sanitary pipe. This answer shifted the comparison from "raw stormwater vs. treated combined flow" to "stormwater with treatment options vs. stormwater plus sewage with no separation possible," and CON never closed that gap in either subsequent speech.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): B won by making the key comparison about frequency, chronic runoff pollution, disruption, and opportunity cost rather than only what happens during overflow events. A had a strong intuitive case that removing raw sewage is a root-cause fix, but it overclaimed that separation is strictly cleaner and that green infrastructure is uniquely unavailable under combined-system management. B’s alternative of tunnels, controls, and incremental storage remained a plausible lower-cost path, and A never fully answered the private lateral, citywide excavation, and opportunity-cost burdens. B’s repeated clipping was an execution cost, but not enough to overcome the stronger comparative case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s episodic-versus-chronic runoff rebuttal was decisive: it directly undercut A’s “runoff plus sewage versus runoff alone” framing by arguing that combined systems treat first-flush runoff during light rains while separation can create untreated pollution every storm.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): PRO wins by decisively controlling the water quality and structural efficacy clash. CON raised a nuanced argument about chronic stormwater toxicity versus episodic sewage shocks, but PRO effectively neutralized it by demonstrating that separation structurally enables source-level green infrastructure (bioswales, permeable pavement) that combined systems cannot deploy because they funnel runoff into sanitary pipes. PRO's framing of a strictly lower "contamination ceiling" (runoff alone versus runoff plus raw human waste) was physically intuitive and consistently pressured CON's case. PRO also better leveraged the motion's explicit concession on upfront cost, framing separation as a permanent capital fix versus indefinite operating costs, and successfully rebutted CON's tunnel flexibility claims by highlighting the fixed-volume reality of bored infrastructure. CON's disruption and opportunity cost points were strong but ultimately secondary to the core environmental and adaptive capacity clash where PRO held the clearer comparative edge. Multiple clipped turns for CON also marginally weakened its late-stage execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 response to CON's chronic pollution claim, showing that separation does not mandate untreated runoff but instead unlocks distributed green infrastructure to treat stormwater at the source. This directly flipped CON's primary environmental offense into a structural advantage for separation, since combined systems cannot effectively use those tools while stormwater is routed into sanitary pipes.
