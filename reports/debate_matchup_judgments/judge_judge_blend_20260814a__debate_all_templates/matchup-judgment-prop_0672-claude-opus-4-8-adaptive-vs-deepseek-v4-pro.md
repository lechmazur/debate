### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won by effectively reframing the domestic manufacturing benefit as critical insurance against supply shocks, rather than just an eventual volume replacement.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal, which capitalized on Side B's concession that a dominant supplier can restrict supply, successfully painting Side B's reliance on 'certain' imports as a risky, unproven bet.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing PRO's contradiction on supply certainty after conceding resilience value and by showing PRO never quantifies a decisive slowdown, leaving the decisive-burden unmet.

- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully leveraging the motion's weighing burden ("outweigh"/"decisive") and exposing a critical contradiction in Side A's case. During the pressure exchange, Side A conceded that a dominant foreign supplier could restrict supply, undermining A's core premise that near-term imports are "certain" and that tariff harm is the only immediate risk. Side B masterfully turned this concession, framing A's alternative resilience proposals as an admission that resilience is worth a price bump—"haggling over instruments" rather than defeating the motion. Side B also effectively neutralized A's historical example (2018-19 tariffs) and highlighted A's inability to quantify the "decisive" slowdown without relying on the contested assumption of permanently friendly imports. Side A's "climate debt" argument was compelling but ultimately couldn't overcome Side B's cleaner burden management and strategic collapse of A's supply certainty premise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which capitalized on Side A's Pressure Question answers. B exposed that A's threshold for "decisive" harm relied on unproven assumptions about import stability, and weaponized A's concession that suppliers can cut off supply to dismantle A's certainty framing. The "three unproven bets vs. one true thing" weighing cleanly resolved the clash.
