### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO)): Side A (CON) built a cleaner and more durable path to victory across the full debate. Three live threads stayed unresolved for PRO. First, the fairness paradox: CON identified early that a local reserve can privilege a wealthy resident over a lower-income lifelong fan who lives outside the catchment zone. PRO's own A2 answer in Rebuttal 2 conceded they would not means-test and would prioritize residents on "stewardship" grounds — effectively confirming the paradox rather than escaping it. CON turned this concession sharply in [A4.3–A4.4] and again in the closing. Second, the "or members" inclusion: CON correctly noted that membership functions as a pay-to-enter insider lane, directly undermining PRO's equity framing. PRO never defended this element of the motion. Third, the proxy problem: CON argued throughout that local status is a crude proxy that does not actually screen out local scalpers, borrowed addresses, or member flipping, while better-targeted tools (purchase limits, lotteries, delayed transfer, face-value resale) address the real harms directly. PRO's counter — that a local window creates a "verified human barrier" — was plausibly challenged and never fully restored. PRO had genuine force on the community-burden and license-to-operate arguments, and the "dynamic pricing is already exclusion by wealth" reframe had real bite. But PRO's closing was more rhetorical than analytical, failed to re-engage the fairness paradox, and let CON's comparative frame — wrong proxy, wrong remedy, redistributes exclusion rather than fixing it — stand unanswered. CON's arguments evolved cleanly across rounds, narrowed the dispute, and used PRO's own concessions against them. That disciplined progression earns the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was CON's [A4.3–A4.4] in Rebuttal 2, where CON used PRO's own A2 answer against them. PRO had admitted they would not means-test and would prioritize residents on "stewardship" grounds. CON immediately exposed this as creating a protected class based on postcode rather than genuine fairness, cementing the core injustice argument that PRO had tried to deflect since the opening. This turn materialized the abstract fairness paradox into a concrete policy consequence PRO could not escape.
