### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins for three interlocking reasons. First, the core behavioral inertia argument is never neutralized: CON concedes that most households do not save voluntarily under the current system, yet claims expanded tax deductions will fix that by "rewarding active engagement" — a response that accepts the behavioral status quo PRO correctly identifies as the root problem. Second, PRO's regressivity critique — that tax deductions mainly subsidize asset-reshuffling among higher earners — goes largely unanswered. CON counters in Rebuttal 2 by introducing refundable credits and direct matches, but those features are not "increasing tax deductions for voluntary contributions" as the motion specifies; this drifts from the proposition and functions as an evasive redesign of the compared policy rather than a direct defense of it. Third, CON's most damaging move is the "zero preserves urgency" claim in A2 of Rebuttal 2 and the closing — the idea that zero savings is preferable to 3% because it leaves the problem visible. This is empirically implausible (inertial non-savers do not treat a zero balance as a clarifying alarm) and undermines CON's own framing that inadequacy, not non-participation, is the critical problem. PRO lands a clean counter in the closing by labeling this "wishful thinking." CON's best live argument — that 3% defaults institutionalize under-saving — was genuinely contested throughout and landed some pressure on PRO, but PRO answered it consistently: 3% plus decades of compounding and the ability to escalate beats zero, and the alternative policy produces zero for the inertial majority. PRO also correctly distinguished opt-out from early withdrawal, neutralizing the debt-trap concern. Overall, PRO made the decisional comparison clearer, sustained its core burden across all rounds, and inflicted more unanswered damage on CON's key mechanisms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive rebuttal exchange was PRO's B4.2/B5.1 response to CON's early-withdrawal debt-trap argument. PRO then turned the pressure back by noting that early withdrawal penalties also exist in voluntary plans, making CON's objection apply equally to the alternative policy. This cleanly neutralized one of CON's most rhetorically effective attacks and exposed a logical error in CON's case that was never corrected.
