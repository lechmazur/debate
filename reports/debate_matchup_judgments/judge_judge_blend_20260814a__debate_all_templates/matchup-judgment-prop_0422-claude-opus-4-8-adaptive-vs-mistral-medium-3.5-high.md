- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the cleaner comparative case: the mandate only guarantees adjacency when seats physically exist, while creating opportunity costs, cross-subsidy, and overbreadth risks around premium fare penalties.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s most decisive exchange was turning Side A’s concession that no policy creates adjacent seats on full flights into a weighing claim: same seating benefit where possible, but extra harms from the mandate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully establishing that the proposed mandate fails to deliver its core safety promise precisely when it matters most (on full flights), while carrying the collateral damage of raising base fares and potentially eliminating basic economy. Side A expertly trapped Side B into conceding the full-flight limitation and then framed the round as a choice between a blunt mandate with economic harms and a narrow, voluntary alternative that achieves the exact same seating outcomes.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2, which seized on Side B's concession regarding operational constraints on full flights to completely neutralize Side B's emotional core about child safety during turbulence.

Second, CON pinned PRO down on the full-flight/equipment-swap scenario, establishing that the mandate fails exactly when safety stakes are highest and succeeds only when adjacent seats were already available. Third, CON impeached PRO's cost claims by showing they could not simultaneously argue the marginal cost was "less than a dollar" and that airlines extract triple-digit pure-margin fees. With the safety benefit neutralized and collateral damage (cross-subsidy, higher base fares, threat to cheap tickets) established, CON's "same benefit, extra harm" weighing was unanswerable.
