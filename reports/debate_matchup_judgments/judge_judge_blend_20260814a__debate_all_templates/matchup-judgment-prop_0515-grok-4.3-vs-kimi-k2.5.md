### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side A built and defended a coherent three-part path to victory across every round: cognitive science grounds explicit phonics, equity demands a guaranteed floor rather than a lottery, and the catastrophic asymmetry between worst-case outcomes (slow pacing under structure vs. never learning the code under autonomy) settles the weighing. Most decisively, Side A weaponized B's own concession at [B2.5]—that skilled teachers already incorporate systematic phonics under autonomy—as proof that phonics is the correct destination and the only real debate is whether to mandate the map. This concession was introduced in Rebuttal 1, exploited in Pressure Questions, answered again in Rebuttal 2, and crystallized in the closing. Side B never escaped it. B's best arguments (implementation rigidity, pacing compliance, English learner flexibility) were genuine pressure points, but B never provided a reliable mechanism to ensure phonics instruction happens reliably under autonomy beyond asserting "professional development" and "local peer collaboration," which A correctly noted could also accompany mandated structure. B's Rebuttal 2 answers to A's pressure questions were the weakest moment: A1 essentially restated the concession rather than rebutting it, and A2 conceded that training is the mechanism while failing to explain why autonomy is a prerequisite for that training. Side A's closing did real comparative weighing—remediable slow pacing versus irremediable non-decoding—while Side B's closing largely recycled earlier points without sharpening the choice. Side A maintained epistemic discipline throughout, using cognitive science and plausible causal mechanisms without fake precision.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was Side A's exploitation of [B2.5]—CON's admission that skilled teachers already incorporate systematic phonics under autonomy. Side A turned this into a concession that phonics is the correct destination, reframing the whole debate as whether to mandate the map rather than trust the destination is reached. B never recovered a coherent answer to why, if phonics is what skilled teachers do anyway, mandating it for all teachers constitutes an unacceptable loss rather than a necessary guarantee.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): Side A won by making the central comparison clearer: structured policy raises a minimum floor of explicit decoding instruction, while autonomy leaves vulnerable students exposed to classrooms that may omit it. Side B had a real concern about rigid implementation and the need for adaptation for English learners and students with speech delays, but it repeatedly assumed mandates must become scripts and never gave a strong mechanism for ensuring autonomous teachers actually use systematic phonics rather than weaker guessing approaches. A's floor-versus-lottery framing carried the equity weighing, though some claims about permanence and brain science were overstated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's pressure question and second rebuttal were decisive: if good autonomous teachers already use phonics, why not mandate that baseline for everyone? Side B's answer of training and professional development did not adequately solve the risk of noncompliant or poorly informed classrooms.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a decisive risk-asymmetry frame: autonomy risks catastrophic failure (discredited guessing methods) for vulnerable students, while structured mandates risk only remediable pacing inefficiencies. Side B effectively turned Side A's concession that skilled teachers already use phonics into a compelling argument for mandating a baseline to protect students from instructional lotteries. Side A's reliance on teacher training and peer collaboration to guarantee quality under autonomy proved insufficient against B's structural guarantee. B's closing crystallized this weighing cleanly and answered the core policy burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 directly answered Side A's pressure questions by distinguishing curricular content from instructional pacing, then leveraged Side A's concession to argue that structure raises the floor while autonomy leaves foundational decoding to chance. This exchange neutralized CON's implementation fears and locked in PRO's risk-weighing advantage for the remainder of the debate.
