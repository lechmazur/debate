### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. B's best route was the dilemma that a prospective-only ban either has limited effect or mainly constrains greenfield planning, plus the claim that density bonuses deliver binding affordability. But A more effectively controlled the central clash: why single-family-only exclusion is needed at all. A showed that infrastructure, safety, and environmental concerns can be handled through codes, overlays, impact fees, and nonexclusive density caps, while B's defense often treated banning single-family-only districts as if it required unlimited density. A also kept the underproduction of discretionary bonus systems live, and B's examples of affordable-unit production did not sufficiently answer the scale problem. A's rhetoric and crystallization made the decision easier.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's most decisive exchange was pressing B to name a capacity or safety goal that only duplex prohibition achieves, then answering B's infrastructure-calibration response by arguing that cities can plan and cap density without making single-family use the exclusive legal form.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A built the stronger path to victory by forcing a live, unanswered burden on whether exclusion serves any function that codes and overlays cannot already handle, while consistently highlighting bonuses' decades-long underproduction; Side B's infrastructure calibration defense collapsed under direct comparison and its dilemma was neutralized without evasion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's closing dismantles B's final infrastructure answer by noting that capacity planning need not ban duplexes while allowing mansions, crystallizing the gatekeeping point.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A won by demonstrating that single-family-only zoning serves no legitimate capacity or safety goal that neutral density regulations cannot achieve, while exposing that density bonuses have systematically underproduced. A dismantled CON's infrastructure-calibration defense by showing that planned unit counts do not require banning duplexes while permitting larger mansions, proving the tool is mismatched to its stated purpose. CON's affordability critiques were blunted by A's land-cost division and vacancy-chain logic, and CON never squarely answered A's pressure for evidence of bonus programs producing middle housing at comparable scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over infrastructure calibration (A3.1, B4.1, A5.1-A5.2), where A exposed that CON's engineering defense collapsed because capacity planning regulates unit counts, not housing types, and a mansion can strain infrastructure more than a duplex—reducing the argument to aesthetic gatekeeping.
