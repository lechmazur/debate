### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by exposing an unresolved contradiction in A's mechanism. A wanted to preserve tracker discounts while preventing the untracked pool from becoming relatively costlier, but calling the ordinary rate a legal baseline did not change the adverse-selection arithmetic. B also directly answered both pressure questions with a concrete revocation-based compliance mechanism and aggregate regulatory review. A made strong privacy, dignity, and hidden-proxy arguments, and was more cautious epistemically, but its closing largely asserted that programs unable to survive its limits were involuntary rather than resolving B's pricing objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response to A's adverse-selection argument was decisive: A admitted that healthier customers opting in can make the untracked pool costlier, and B showed that mandatory opt-in alone does not stop that mechanism. A's legal-baseline answer changed the label without explaining how the premium arithmetic would be prevented.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won a competitive debate by converting A’s core adverse-selection story into CON’s strongest comparative: healthier trackers make the untracked pool costlier, so blocking wearable risk signals does not erase cost—it spreads it. A’s consent/dignity framing and “if sticky consent is required, it was never voluntary” crystallization were sharp and repeatedly recoverable, and B’s clipped opening was a real cost, but A never neutralized the real-vs-speculative harm comparison or produced a market example of the death spiral it needed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s Rebuttal 1 flip of [A2.2]: admitting opt-in worsens the untracked pool is textbook adverse selection and supports risk pricing, not a ban under “strict limits.”
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON won by turning PRO's adverse-selection mechanism against them and exposing that PRO's "legal baseline" fix was mere relabeling that left the underlying cost-shifting arithmetic intact.
