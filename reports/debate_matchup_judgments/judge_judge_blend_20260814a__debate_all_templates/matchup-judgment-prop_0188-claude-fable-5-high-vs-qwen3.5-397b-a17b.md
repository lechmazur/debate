### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): B wins clearly but not overwhelmingly. A built a plausible transparency case around p-hacking, null results, and result-blind review, but B more effectively narrowed the debate to the motion's overbreadth and implementation problems. B's attacks on exploratory social science, journal capacity, funding-journal coordination, and the distinction between mandatory registry deposit and journal conscription left A repeatedly clarifying or adding mechanisms late. A's answer that funding should hinge on Stage 1 acceptance helped, but it also strengthened B's claim that the defended policy had become a heavily amended registered-report model rather than the motion as written. A also never fully recovered the exploratory-research objection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was pressing the Stage 1/Stage 2 dilemma: if journals can reject protocols or later veto poor adherence, the guarantee is no longer simple publication regardless of outcome; if they cannot, the policy risks publishing junk and flooding journals.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON Rebuttal 1 and 2: forced contradiction on "regardless of outcome" vs. Stage 1/2 vetos and showed PRO's position converged on CON's alternative.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): CON demonstrated that PRO's mandate either collapses into contradiction or requires institutional amendments—funding-journal integration, Stage 1 and 2 vetoes—that go beyond the motion text and create gridlock and moral hazard. CON's framing of the clash—that the motion's defender ended up defending CON's own policy wearing extra bureaucracy—stuck through the closing and provided the cleaner comparative path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of the tension between "regardless of outcome" and quality control, showing that PRO's necessary Stage 1/Stage 2 review mechanisms reintroduced exactly the editorial discretion and file-drawer risks the motion claimed to abolish.
