### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won on the decisive clash: the C. elegans comparison. PRO consistently showed that IACUC review for a 302-neuron worm is already grounded in neural plasticity and functional capacity for altered experience—not organismal completeness, not nociceptors, not consciousness. CON's repeated counter—that C. elegans is a "complete organism with behavioral responsiveness" while an organoid is not—was a real distinction, but PRO correctly identified it as ad hoc: the existing framework itself does not formally require organismal wholeness as a trigger condition. CON never grounded the "organismal context" requirement in how IACUC actually operates; it was a criterion CON invented to draw the line where they needed it. The most decisive single exchange was PRO's handling of CON's AI pressure question in Rebuttal 2. CON raised AI learning as a reductio, expecting PRO to be forced into an absurd position. PRO turned it cleanly: AI is excluded because it lacks biological substrates—but organoids are living tissue, so that distinction runs in PRO's favor, not CON's. This exposed a fatal internal tension in CON's case: they needed biology to exclude AI, yet refused to let biology matter for organoids. CON's later speeches were also notably shorter (165 words in Rebuttal 2, 151 in Closing) and left key burdens underexplored, while PRO answered both pressure questions with required A1:/A2: labels and gave substantive, on-point answers. CON's "category error" argument had genuine initial force but was never developed into a positive account of where the principled line should fall within the framework's own logic, which allowed PRO's closing crystallization—"CON's own concessions say yes"—to land cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's AI question (B4.3–B4.4). CON introduced AI learning as a reductio to discredit PRO's functional-threshold argument. PRO answered that AI lacks biological substrates—and then immediately turned the point: CON must now concede biology matters, which is precisely what organoids (living human neurons) possess. This converted CON's attack into evidence for PRO's position and exposed an internal inconsistency CON never resolved.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by keeping the key standard on welfare capacity and organismal interests. Side B made a forceful precautionary case, but relied heavily on an under-supported C. elegans analogy and treated neural plasticity as if it were already the animal-review trigger. Side A answered that animals are regulated as integrated organisms with sensory and behavioral contexts, while organoids are disembodied cell clusters, and exposed the overbreadth problem with AI and other cultures.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: if functional learning alone triggers review, the logic overincludes AI; if biology alone triggers review, it overincludes all cell cultures. That pushed the debate back to welfare capacity, where Side B had not met its burden.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON)): Side A wins by successfully leveraging the C. elegans analogy to expose a tension in Side B’s “organismal welfare” standard. B’s category-error framing was coherent but grew repetitive and never fully resolved why minimal animal models trigger IACUC review while functionally similar organoids would not. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) won on two decisive turning points that PRO never adequately answered. First, CON took PRO's own C. elegans example—introduced to prove that minimal circuits support valuation—and pointed out that C. elegans is not subject to IACUC-level oversight despite those 302 neurons and approach/avoidance behavior. This is a direct, grounded counter drawn from PRO's own evidence, and PRO's Rebuttal 2 never squarely addressed it. Second, when PRO answered CON's Q2 by saying organoids could be added at the "appropriate tier" of the existing framework, CON immediately caught the concession: if organoids need a different tier, they do not fit under "the same ethical review frameworks" as the proposition literally requires. PRO's sliding from "same" to "similar/tiered" quietly abandoned the motion, and CON crystallized this cleanly in the closing. CON also maintained a sharper distinction throughout—mechanism shared (synaptic plasticity) does not equal ethical status shared—while PRO's core homology claim was asserted more than demonstrated. PRO's two clipped turns (Opening and Rebuttal 2) imposed additional execution costs, potentially cutting off responses that could have closed these gaps. PRO's precautionary principle and burden-shifting arguments had genuine force but were never developed into a complete answer to the proposition's exact wording. CON's rhetoric was consistently tighter: "mechanism is shared; ethical status is not" and "that's not precaution—it's paralysis" gave third-party readers clean comparative handles that PRO never matched.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's counter-use of the C. elegans example in Rebuttal 1 [A2.1–A2.2]: PRO introduced the organism as evidence that minimal circuits support valuation, but CON turned it immediately by noting that nematode research is not governed by IACUC-level protocols despite those 302 neurons. This undercut PRO's core threshold claim with PRO's own chosen example, and PRO never provided a satisfying answer in either subsequent speech.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by making the central distinction clearer: rudimentary learning and synaptic plasticity are not enough to show morally relevant suffering. Side B had a plausible precautionary route and improved it by stressing tiered review, but too much of its case rested on repeating that shared neural mechanisms imply potential suffering without bridging the gap to welfare, affect, or experience. Side A also effectively turned the C. elegans and mechanism arguments, and framed applying animal frameworks as a category error rather than proportionate precaution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's first rebuttal was decisive: it argued that C. elegans shows minimal valuation yet is not treated under full animal-research review, and that synaptic plasticity is necessary for suffering but not sufficient for it.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side B won by consistently exposing the categorical gap between synaptic plasticity and integrated suffering. PRO's core analogy collapsed when CON turned the C. elegans example to show that minimal neural valuation does not currently trigger animal-research frameworks. CON also effectively leveraged PRO's concession on tiered oversight to demonstrate a contradiction with the motion's demand for the "same" framework. PRO relied on speculative homology and an inverted burden of proof, while CON maintained a scientifically grounded distinction between disembodied cell clusters and sentient organisms, delivering sharper, more damaging rebuttals throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's turn on PRO's C. elegans evidence was decisive. PRO introduced the nematode to prove minimal circuits support valuation, but CON immediately noted that C. elegans research is not governed by IACUC protocols, devastating PRO's claim that learning inherently triggers animal-welfare frameworks. PRO's attempt to salvage this by calling existing frameworks arbitrary undermined their own normative appeal to those same frameworks.
