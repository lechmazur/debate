### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): A wins a close but real edge. B made a strong structural case that platforms profit from addictive design and that parental controls are weak, but A better exposed the ban’s unresolved costs: universal age checkpoints, privacy burdens, questionable enforceability, and harm to marginalized youth. A’s “audit the factory, not the individual” response gave a plausible regulatory alternative, while B’s reliance on non-algorithmic forums and chats looked like a retreat from a sweeping social-media account ban. B’s clipped turns and some brittle factual specificity also slightly hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest rebuttal was turning B’s “properly moderated, non-algorithmic services” into a concession that targeted design regulation can preserve useful connection without a blanket ban, while also pressing that B’s ban still requires state-backed age verification.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A built the stronger case by consistently exposing PRO's enforcement burdens on the same low-income parents cited as victims, neutralizing the lifeline retreat through direct responses on resilience-building, and showing design regulation can override profit motives via code audits without privacy costs or sudden exposure at 16. PRO's profit-incentive core remained compelling but was not squared with feasible alternatives to the ban's concrete drawbacks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 and 2 exposed PRO's inconsistency on parental burdens and lifeline (A2.1, A2.3, A3.1, A4.3), forcing clarifying retreats in B's Rebuttal 2 while advancing the regulation path.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by exposing critical contradictions in PRO's case and offering a cleaner enforcement story. PRO simultaneously argued that low-income parents are too exhausted to manage parental controls while proposing complex tokenized age-verification systems those same families would have to navigate [A2.1]. PRO also told marginalized teens to move support "offline" before retreating to "non-algorithmic online spaces," undermining their own credibility on lifeline harms [A2.3][A4.3]. CON's "audit the factory" framework—mandating chronological feeds and disabling autoplay without surveilling users—directly answered PRO's enforcement challenge [A4.1], while PRO never adequately explained why universal digital checkpoints would not become a privacy disaster. CON's digital-resilience and architectural-regulation arguments survived the exchange, whereas PRO's profit-motive critique, though forceful, could not overcome the self-inflicted wounds that made their ban look like an unworkable sledgehammer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal [A2.1] exposing PRO's contradiction on low-income parents being too exhausted for parental controls yet required to manage tokenized age-verification gateways, paired with [A2.4] capturing PRO's concession that properly moderated non-algorithmic services are the real answer—effectively proving CON's design-mandate model.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): B wins narrowly. A made strong privacy, overbreadth, and safer-design arguments, but B more clearly exposed that A's alternative still depends on age verification and can be evaded in similar ways. A's safer-default answer was creative, but it shifted late and did not fully solve borrowed-ID evasion or prove that design mandates can tame a business model B framed as inherently addictive. B's friction-not-perfection and network-effect weighing left the cleaner path for a ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure questions and second rebuttal on age-verification parity were the decisive exchange, turning A's strongest implementation attack back onto A's own alternative.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B won by making the decisive clash clearer: a fundamentally addictive business model cannot be safely regulated, and B's ban better solves collective action while neutralizing bypass concerns via network effects. B directly answered pressure on verification and definitions without evasion, exposing A's reliance on the same infrastructure A attacked. A's clipped turns and incomplete rebuttals on social reset weakened their case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 responses to A's pressure questions, combined with the contradiction attack on verification, dismantled A's framework more cleanly than any single exchange.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON’s “default safe mode” framework proved more coherent than PRO’s ban, answering the core harms of addictive design without incurring the privacy, circumvention, and community-loss costs that PRO’s ban could not escape. CON effectively turned PRO’s circumvention and verification critiques by showing that a universal non-addictive default disarms the algorithm regardless of age, whereas a ban drives determined teens into unregulated shadows. PRO failed to overcome the definitional problem of how to ban “social media” without destroying supportive communities on Discord, YouTube, and WhatsApp, and their attempt to equate the two sides’ age-verification burdens ignored CON’s burden-shifting distinction. While CON suffered real costs from clipped speeches, the visible arguments carried the decisive clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 turn on circumvention and age verification (A4.1–A4.4), where they showed that under their safety-default model a lying teen still lands in a non-addictive environment, whereas under PRO’s ban the same teen walks into the full algorithmic casino—while also distinguishing an adult-unlock gate from a universal minor registry.
