### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): Side B won by exposing a lethal circularity in CON's position: CON conceded that IP-protected devices already fill equipment graveyards across the Global South because they fail in tropical conditions, yet insisted on preserving the IP walls that legally block local engineers from ruggedizing or repairing those same machines. B turned this concession into a decisive comparative frame, showing that traditional pipelines produce scarcity and then criminalize the adaptation needed to relieve it. While CON's safety and incentive arguments remained live, they were blunted by B's rebuttal that open-source hardware can operate within the same certification frameworks while removing patent barriers, and that firms already neglect low-income markets regardless of IP. B's pressure questions sharpened this tension effectively, and B's closing crystallized the clash cleanly. CON's clipped opening and repetitive safety refrains without fully resolving the graveyard-adaptation paradox left A's route to victory narrower than B's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The most decisive exchange was B's rebuttal turning CON's equipment graveyards against them: B argued that those graveyards are filled with IP-protected devices that failed precisely because local engineers were legally prohibited from modifying them for tropical conditions, making IP itself a cause of preventable mortality. This reframing survived A's attempts to distinguish validated imports from unvetted forks, and it crystallized the central comparative question of the debate.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (CON); B = Kimi K2.5 Thinking (PRO)): PRO wins by successfully turning CON's safety and reliability arguments against the status quo. While CON effectively highlighted regulatory bottlenecks and the risks of unvetted modifications, PRO's "lethal circularity" frame proved decisive: proprietary devices already fill equipment graveyards because they cannot be legally adapted to austere environments, and IP walls actively block the repairs that would keep them functioning. PRO also cleanly weighed access against theoretical safety, noting that traditional pipelines lack market incentives to design for low-income settings anyway, leaving open-source as the only viable path to scalable, adaptable care. CON's reliance on cross-subsidization and structured pipelines failed to overcome the reality of exclusion and unfixable failures PRO documented.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 directly answering CON's pressure questions, particularly the turn that distinguished illegal desperation repairs from certified open-source production and exposed the contradiction in CON admitting proprietary devices fail in tropical conditions while defending the IP laws that forbid local ruggedization. This crystallized the lethal circularity clash and neutralized CON's safety advantage.
