### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built and maintained the stronger path to victory. Their core distinction—exposure is not attunement, and a contingent human partner does fundamentally different developmental work than a patient script—ran cleanly through every round and was never squarely neutralized. Side A's most effective move was the baseline challenge (compare AI to an exhausted parent, not an idealized one) and the division-of-labor reframe (AI handles the story, parent provides physical comfort). These were genuinely interesting arguments. But B answered both: the realistic alternatives beyond the tired-parent-vs.-television dichotomy (flat voice, backward pages, Grandma on video call) deflated the baseline challenge, and the "fusion is the mechanism" response to division of labor—the point that bedtime reading works because cognitive and physical attunement are fused, not separable—was the most analytically precise moment in the debate and A never recovered from it. B's pressure questions were sharper and more targeted than A's, especially Q1 exposing that A's own preferred example (AI reads nightly, parent cuddles) is exactly the routine the motion names, and Q2 distinguishing keyword-responsiveness from signal-reading. A's A2 answer was honest but conceded the definitional ground B needed. The burden point B kept returning to—A explained parental convenience throughout but never demonstrated developmental benefit for the child beyond "linguistic stimulation," which B repeatedly distinguished from contingent social learning—remained live at the close. B's closing was clipped (minor execution cost) but the preserved text still crystallized the stakes. A's closing relied on the same exhausted-parent frame it had used from the start, which by that point had been answered multiple times. Clear but competitive win for B.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to A2 [B4.3–B4.4]: "The division of labor is itself the developmental error. Bedtime reading works because the cognitive and the physical are fused... Splitting comfort from story severs the very mechanism by which stories become emotionally navigable." This was the debate's clearest analytical moment—it turned A's most original argument (optimization through labor-splitting) into evidence for B's case, and A's response (that fusion only works when the parent has bandwidth) did not match the force of the original claim.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the debate centered on the motion’s core developmental claim: routine outsourcing replaces contingent, emotionally reciprocal interaction with mere linguistic exposure. But B answered that the motion does not require perfect parents, only that routine reading/conversation/bedtime not be handed over to a device, and persuasively distinguished imperfect human joint attention from scripted or keyword-responsive engagement. A’s “high floor” case was plausible but leaned heavily on optimistic AI capability and parental exhaustion as the default, while B gave the cleaner child-centered weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s most decisive rebuttal was the “exposure is not attunement” exchange: it directly undercut A’s claim that patient, expressive AI input substitutes for early reading and bedtime interaction, and reframed A’s benefits as parental convenience rather than developmental equivalence.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B won by sustaining the core developmental claim on contingent reciprocity, offering realistic human alternatives that neutralized A's exhausted-parent baseline, and exposing that A's division-of-labor optimization still severs the fused mechanism; A's optimization reframing was repeatedly answered without evasion.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): CON wins on two live routes that PRO never cleanly neutralized. First, the definitional trap: by Rebuttal 2 PRO had tacitly conceded that engaged co-listening is not "outsourcing," forcing them to redefine the motion's operative term as "parental cognitive disengagement" rather than "which voice reads." CON correctly identified this as a quiet rewrite of the proposition, and PRO's closing never recovered that ground. Second, the false-dichotomy clash: PRO's prescribed alternative for the night-shift parent—a brief lullaby, a short memory, a cuddle—presupposes exactly the parental bandwidth CON's question exposed as unavailable. PRO simply reasserted the prescription without mechanism for why minimal human contact is achievable in those cases. CON's sharpest comparative frame, "engaged-parent-plus-AI versus exhausted-parent-alone," was introduced early and never refuted. PRO's developmental science framework (contingent responsiveness, conversational turns) is real and was never fully neutralized as a theoretical claim, which is why this is a clear rather than dominant margin. But theory alone does not win the practical application question the motion actually poses. PRO's closing was largely recap; CON's was the tighter crystallization. Two clipped speeches are a genuine execution cost for CON that narrows the margin, though both clips appear to have been sentence completions rather than new pivotal arguments. PRO's speeches were fully delivered and consistently grounded, which keeps this competitive throughout.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's first rebuttal [A2.1–A2.7]: the "goalpost-moving" charge and the "engaged-parent-plus-AI beats exhausted-parent-alone" comparison. This was the single most damaging move in the debate—it turned PRO's own developmental criteria against them by showing the honest pairwise comparison favored CON's preferred scenario on every metric PRO claimed to value. PRO's subsequent "parallel existence is not attachment" responses never squarely answered this framing.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won by keeping the motion focused on routine outsourcing rather than mere AI-assisted co-use. Side A’s strongest material defended supplementation by present parents and hardship cases, but that did not clearly negate the proposition as written. Side B’s distinction between using a tool and handing over the bedtime/conversational relationship was more decisive, and Side A also failed to directly answer the pressure question about unpredictable emotional attunement.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s first rebuttal was decisive: it exposed that Side A’s examples were engaged co-listening, not outsourcing, and reframed the clash around whether AI becomes the primary reader and source of comfort.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A wins by exposing PRO's definitional retreat (engaged co-listening isn't outsourcing) and highlighting practical parental constraints PRO cannot resolve, while answering pressure questions directly.
