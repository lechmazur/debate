### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): The decisive clash was over what "routinely" means and what the realistic counterfactual is for stretched families. A pressed a concrete, structurally-recurring scenario (a shift-worker parent using AI reading several nights a week, not as an emergency but as a patterned necessity) and forced B to either concede this counts as "routine" or narrow PRO's case to pure emergency use — B chose the latter, which weakened its own coverage of the motion's plain language and left A's strongest example effectively unrebutted. A also successfully quantified the tradeoff (partial-value AI nights plus full-value human nights vs. an exhausted household's inconsistent human nights), and when B introduced the "habit erosion" mechanism as its best new move, A directly challenged it as an unsupported folk assertion in Rebuttal 2 — a challenge B's closing never substantively answered, instead retreating to a recap of the routine/default distinction that ignored A's live math argument. B's opening and mechanism about responsive vs. generic speech were vivid and well-grounded, and its Pressure Questions exposed a real ambiguity in A's definitional move, but B's answers (A2 especially) softened A's toughest example rather than confronting it, and its closing did not sharpen the remaining clash. A's case suffered real execution costs from being clipped twice (Rebuttal 1 and Closing), but the substantive arguments landed before the cutoffs in both cases, so the damage was limited. On balance, A built the clearer, more adaptive path through the debate and left B's most important argument (habit erosion) hanging unanswered at the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 response to B's "habit erosion" claim — pointing out it was asserted as universal human nature with no mechanism and turning the causal question back on B ("why assume a parent using AI four nights a week is more likely to abandon reading than an equally exhausted parent with no tool at all") — was the most damaging exchange, and B's closing never returned to defend it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side A won by keeping the decisive clash focused on what the motion actually targets: making AI the planned, default venue for a child’s core early routines.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s Rebuttal 2, where it directly answered Side B’s pressure questions on habit erosion and baseline alternatives, then demonstrated that Side B had never actually defended routine default use as developmentally valuable—only as better than silence.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by successfully shifting the debate to the realistic baseline for stretched families. While Side B argued that "routine" means "default" and tried to carve out "emergency" use, Side A convincingly demonstrated that for a shift worker, regular AI use is both routine and necessary, making the alternative silence rather than ideal human interaction. Side B failed to answer this comparative math, relying instead on an unsubstantiated "habit erosion" argument.
