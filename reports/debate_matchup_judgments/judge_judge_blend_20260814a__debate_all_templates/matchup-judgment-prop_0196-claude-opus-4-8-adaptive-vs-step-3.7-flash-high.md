### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): B won by making the cleaner motion-specific comparison: a new-build mandate does not replace retrofits for existing stock, but prevents adding avoidable future retrofit burdens. A’s “fix the 99%” frame was rhetorically strong, but it depended on treating the motion as abandoning existing retrofits and on an asserted zero-sum funding tradeoff that B plausibly challenged. A exposed some overreach in B’s tailored implementation and B used some brittle late numbers, but B’s core prevention-versus-future-retrofit argument survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s first rebuttal that the 1% annual new stock compounds over decades and that failing to mandate it merely enlarges the future retrofit queue was the decisive exchange, because it directly undercut A’s central “new builds are too small to matter” route.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): Side A won by systematically dismantling Side B's false tradeoff and directly answering pressure questions, whereas Side B relied on inaccurate claims about what A had conceded or dropped. B's closing fell flat by claiming A never provided a mechanism for carbon reduction, when A had explicitly detailed two mechanisms in the preceding speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal (A4) and closing flawlessly exposed how B's alternative—a flexible standard that bans gas boilers but skips full readiness—still forces expensive future structural retrofits for insulation and airtightness.

PRO never supplied a convincing mechanism showing that a scarce dollar spent on new builds avoids more near-term tons than the same dollar spent retrofitting a leaking 1970s home, while CON repeatedly crystallized that PRO had softened the motion’s operative words—“all” construction became “core requirements minus add-ons,” and “rather than rely mainly on retrofits” became an admission that retrofits remain essential. That left PRO defending a diminished slogan while CON’s “fix the ninety-nine” frame stayed live.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over the 1% vs. 99% stock share and the “between now and 2040” timeline. CON turned PRO’s own “30% by 2055” projection against PRO to show existing stock still dominates emissions, then demonstrated that PRO never identified a mechanism by which scarce dollars spent on new builds avoid more immediate tons than dollars spent retrofitting standing leak buildings.
