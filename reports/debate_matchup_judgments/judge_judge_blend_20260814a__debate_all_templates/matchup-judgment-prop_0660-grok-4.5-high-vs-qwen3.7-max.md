### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won by distinguishing a protected right not to reply from a blanket communications ban, preserving voluntary flexibility and formal emergency coverage. Side A plausibly identified coordination delays, but repeatedly treated all rapid clarification as requiring coerced availability and asserted massive staffing costs without establishing their scale. B more convincingly weighed recovery, burnout, and hidden unpaid labor against manageable handoff friction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s answer that emergencies can use compensated on-call coverage while routine global work uses overlapping hours and deliberate handoffs most damaged A’s productivity case. Its further reframe—that some apparent productivity is merely labor cost externalized onto workers’ personal time—gave B the clearest comparative weighing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that delaying off-hours communication creates compounding errors overnight, turning routine clarifications into massive morning rework. This neutralized Side B's welfare claims by showing that anticipatory anxiety is driven by rational dread of this resulting chaos, not just fear of management retaliation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 in Rebuttal 2 decisively explained why a delayed message creates an avalanche rather than a standard morning workload, neatly reframing the resulting stress as a direct consequence of the law itself.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A built the stronger path to victory by establishing a clear causal mechanism—chronic connectivity erodes the cognitive recovery that knowledge work requires—and by reframing CON's productivity costs as merely the internalization of labor previously externalized onto unpaid personal time. Side B's argument that iterative coordination requires constant fluidity was plausible but never resolved the central dilemma exposed by Side A's pressure questions: if 8 PM messages are critical enough to prevent cascading failures, they should be handled by compensated on-call structures; if they are routine, they can wait for morning. Side B's answers conceded that formalizing this coverage would be prohibitively expensive, effectively admitting the current model relies on unpaid overtime. Side A capitalized on this in the closing by crystallizing the clash as exploitation masquerading as agility, while Side B's closing repeated earlier claims without neutralizing the cost-internalization frame. Side A also decisively answered the morning-avalanche worry by showing that anticipatory anxiety depends on the very coercive dynamics the law eliminates, and that a rested worker processes a morning backlog more efficiently than a fatigued worker handles midnight pings. On the comparative question, Side A demonstrated welfare gains are profound and absolute, while productivity losses are either illusory or represent fair cost accounting rather than economic destruction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they turned CON's productivity-damage claim into an argument for PRO by reframing it as the proper internalization of labor costs, and showed that anticipatory anxiety only persists under the coercive penalty structures the law dismantles. This neutralized the morning-avalanche argument and exposed the exploitation hidden in CON's "collaborative speed."
