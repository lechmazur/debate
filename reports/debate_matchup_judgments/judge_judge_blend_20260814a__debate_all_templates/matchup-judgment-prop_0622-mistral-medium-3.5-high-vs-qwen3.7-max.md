### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won by providing a more coherent mechanism for both economic decarbonization and distributional fairness. While Side A's political durability argument regarding concentrated benefits (factories) was rhetorically excellent, Side B decisively won the economic clash by pointing out that subsidizing green supply does nothing if builders still buy cheaper, dirty alternatives without a price signal. Furthermore, Side A faltered on the fairness clash by conceding Side B's point that weatherization subsidies fail to help renters.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 argument that subsidizing the supply of green cement does nothing if builders just buy the cheaper, polluting alternative, effectively exposing the demand-side flaw in pure subsidy regimes.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side A won by making the political durability clash decisive: concentrated local benefits from subsidies (IRA factories/jobs) proved more resilient than diffuse dividends, with stronger answers to pressure on timing and feasibility. B's efficiency case remained plausible but was neutralized on hard sectors and coalitions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 and Closing turned B's coalition argument by contrasting mobile corporate subsidies with immovable factory roots that voters defend locally.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner path to victory by establishing that an economy-wide carbon price is the indispensable demand-side engine for decarbonization, while equal per-capita dividends solve the political and distributional barriers that would otherwise block it. CON never squarely answered why subsidized green steel or cement would outsell cheaper dirty alternatives without a price signal forcing that choice; repeating the solar/wind subsidy precedent did not bridge the logic gap for process industries where dirty options remain far cheaper. While CON’s “factories stay, money moves” reframe and IRA example mounted a spirited challenge to PRO’s durability claim, PRO neutralized Australia’s carbon-tax collapse by showing it lacked the very universal dividend that creates a broad defensive coalition. PRO’s framework integrated efficiency, equity, and durability more coherently, whereas CON’s case remained more piecemeal across sectors and left the demand-side mechanism under-developed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s reframing of Australia’s carbon tax repeal (B2.2): by arguing the tax collapsed precisely because it lacked a visible, universal dividend, PRO neutralized CON’s key historical precedent and shifted the political-durability burden onto CON’s narrower subsidy coalition.
