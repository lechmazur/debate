- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): B won by exposing that PRO's examples and AI Act citations actually describe use/deployment triggers, forcing an admission of unbounded extraterritorial liability that CON effectively weaponized across rebuttals and closing. PRO never produced a concrete case their literal impact test catches that use misses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2.1–B2.3 and B4.3: CON repeatedly showed PRO's "impact" illustrations were use cases in disguise and turned the Lagos concession into decisive proof of overreach.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON)): CON won by showing PRO repeatedly conflated impact with the EU's existing use-and-deployment trigger, never identified a case the use-standard misses, and conceded that "use is merely a proxy for impact." PRO's evasion stories all ended in use on EU soil, proving the motion's literal wording unnecessary and unworkable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4) showed PRO's Swiss-intermediary example is already caught by the use-trigger, argued impact gives regulators a grievance but not a subpoenable defendant, and capitalized on PRO's admission that EU firms should be liable for downstream harms in Lagos—demonstrating the motion drowns compliant firms in boundless extraterritorial liability.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by successfully distinguishing between an enforceable "use/deployment" trigger and a vague, unbounded "impact" trigger. CON effectively demonstrated that PRO's own examples (the AI Act's "output used" clause, the Dublin mortgage, the Swiss intermediary) were all caught by a use-test, collapsing PRO's case. PRO's strategic error in embracing unbounded extraterritorial liability for the Lagos example played directly into CON's warnings about compliance anchors. CON's closing crystallized the clash perfectly by leveraging PRO's admission that "use is a proxy for impact," arguing convincingly that regulators should target the workable proxy rather than the diffuse concept. PRO failed to identify a single practical gap in the use-test that an impact-test would fill, leaving CON with the clearer, more workable path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, where they capitalized on PRO's Lagos answer and the "use is a proxy for impact" concession to frame the motion as regulating an unenforceable shadow instead of a concrete act, effectively neutralizing PRO's entire case.
