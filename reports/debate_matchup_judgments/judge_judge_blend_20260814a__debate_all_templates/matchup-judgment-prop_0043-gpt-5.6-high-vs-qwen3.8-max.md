- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won by demonstrating that formal reasoning provides an objective, durable standard (inference) that functions even when media literacy heuristics (provenance and source cues) fail or are tribalized. - **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): CON won because the motion requires formal training from primary school to beat every media-literacy curriculum, and CON kept that comparative live: a hybrid that embeds systematic logic/probability while adding platform terrain, pause-and-correct habits, and institutional norms is still media literacy and is more complete against identity-driven, emotion-engineered failure. PRO’s engine/hierarchy and provenance-vs-inference points were sharp and better crystallized durability and even-handedness, but they mostly showed that strong ML must borrow formal tools, not that a formal-centered curriculum does more than the richer package. PRO’s clipped closing also left the final crystallizing punch unfinished.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained answer to absorption (esp. B2/B4): embedding comprehensive formal tools in media contexts is integration that still leaves media literacy the fuller defense, not proof the formal curriculum does more.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON wins because the proposition’s absolute burden—“more than any media-literacy curriculum”—is fatal. CON shows that the strongest media-literacy curriculum can embed formal reasoning systematically while adding civic habits, platform awareness, and motivation that make those tools usable. PRO’s attempt to dismiss this as “annexation” or relabeling never fully overcomes the intuition that an engine with steering, brakes, and a map outperforms the engine alone. PRO’s compass-versus-map and provenance-versus-inference frames are powerful, but the closing was clipped for exceeding the word limit, leaving its crystallization incomplete. CON’s closing cleanly ties the debate back to the terrain where democratic failure actually happens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The second-rebuttal exchange over “annexation” is decisive: PRO argues that any media-literacy curriculum teaching formal tools comprehensively has simply imported PRO’s causal engine, while CON counters that this is integration—an engine with steering, brakes, and a map—making the package strictly superior and directly negating the “any” burden.
