  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (A2) masterfully bites the bullet on Side A's warning about platforms shutting down the app during slow periods, reframing it as a benefit that saves workers from burning gas for free.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger path by anchoring on deliberate platform control and oversupply cost-shifting, directly answering pressure questions with workable mechanisms (aggregate formulas), and forcing Side A into an unprincipled defense of externalizing minimum-wage costs. Side A repeated flexibility warnings without neutralizing the control distinction or answering the legal-principle burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answers to the pressure questions, which crystallized that fleet calibration is a confession rather than a refutation and exposed Side A's lack of any principle supporting unpaid labor reserves.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by successfully grounding the regulatory mechanism and winning the central clash over control. PRO's introduction of an aggregate utilization formula directly neutralized CON's strongest practical objection regarding multi-apping and invasive surveillance. When CON argued that algorithmic penalties were mere reputation signals, PRO effectively dismantled this by contrasting reputational downgrades with hard penalties like deactivation and zone restrictions, proving platforms exercise managerial control. Furthermore, PRO skillfully reframed CON's core economic prediction that platforms would cap fleets and gate slow hours not as a flexibility apocalypse, but as necessary market discipline that ends an unpaid subsidy. CON's case remained coherent but relied on a strained analogy between app algorithms and freelance market signals, and failed to overcome PRO's reframing of the economic trade-offs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response in Rebuttal 2 explaining how an aggregate fleet-wide utilization formula calculates pay without individual device monitoring. This directly defused CON's most damaging practical argument about multi-app accounting and shifted the debate back to the principled clash over who bears the cost of platform oversupply.
