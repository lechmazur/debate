### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A wins by building a sharper comparative case across the full exchange. The three live arguments that mattered most all resolved in CON's favour. First, the motivated reasoning problem—that numerate citizens use logical tools to rationalize tribal priors rather than escape them—was raised early and never squarely answered; PRO's "cognitive immunization" counter was plausible but speculative and lacked the force needed to neutralise it. Second, PRO's explicit concession in B4.3 that formal logic "doesn't detect the forgery—that's media literacy's role" was a genuine self-inflicted wound on the proposition, which requires showing logic does MORE. PRO tried to recover by framing logic as the foundational prior-probability check, but CON immediately countered that this very "plausibility" instinct is calibrated against by disinformation designed to feel persuasive to its target audience—so the Bayesian brake actually springs the trap rather than stopping it. Third, the internal-logic-versus-external-reality distinction CON introduced in A4.1 and sharpened in the closing held up: a fabricated article can be perfectly internally consistent, so logical coherence checks do not detect it. PRO's closing attempt to redefine lateral reading as "just logical evaluation" blurred rather than resolved this distinction and looked like evasive reframing. PRO's strongest live arguments—domain-independence, durability, developmental feasibility of age-appropriate probability games—were genuine and not fully neutralised, which is why the margin is competitive rather than dominant. But on the core comparative question—which skill does more against the actual mechanisms of democratic vulnerability—CON's case remained cleaner and more grounded after the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's exploitation of PRO's B4.3 concession in A4.2–A4.5 was the most decisive exchange. PRO admitted logic does not detect digital forgeries; CON correctly identified this as conceding that media literacy is the direct tool for the most acute modern threats. CON then added a sharp turn: the "prior probability" recovery PRO offered actually backfires, because disinformation is engineered to feel maximally plausible to its partisan target, meaning the Bayesian check rationalises rather than interrupts the deception. This combination—concession plus sharp counter to the attempted recovery—materially damaged PRO's live route to victory.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): B won by making the comparison more concrete: democratic vulnerability is driven by emotional, algorithmic, and source-based manipulation that formal logic alone does not detect. A had a plausible foundation argument, but weakened it by conceding that logic does not detect deepfakes and by trying to fold media-literacy habits into logic without proving a formal logic curriculum would teach them. B’s distinction between internal consistency and external verification was clearer and more responsive to the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it used A’s concession that logic “doesn’t detect the forgery” to argue that media literacy is the actual tool needed against modern disinformation, and it turned A’s plausibility-check argument by showing that motivated voters may find false claims plausible precisely because of their biases.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B won by establishing that modern threats are external/algorithmic and by exploiting Side A's explicit concession that logic cannot detect forgeries; Side A showed logic is foundational but never neutralized the practical-external distinction or the motivated-reasoning armor problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 pivot on the "doesn't detect the forgery" concession, forcing the comparison onto external verification that logic cannot supply.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the more coherent and sustainable case across all five rounds. Its core framing—that formal reasoning is evergreen while media-literacy curricula are perpetually obsolescent—was never decisively neutralized, and its most effective sub-argument, distinguishing formal mathematics from contested "critical-thinking" buzzwords, directly undercut CON's culture-war rebuttal. When CON pressed on partisan neutrality (A3.1), PRO answered cleanly and specifically: school boards do not protest fractions or probability the way they contest "critical thinking" syllabi. That answer held. PRO also landed the stronger move on institutional trust: CON's claim that media literacy teaches process rather than deference to specific outlets is reasonable, but PRO's rejoinder—that cross-checking in a fractured, echo-chamber ecosystem yields only more partisan content—was never adequately answered. CON's strongest argument was the fabricated-premises point (logic cannot help when the premise itself is forged). PRO partially addressed this by shifting to scale manipulation as the primary mechanism of populist harm, where statistical intuition does provide real defence. That pivot was imperfect—deepfakes remain a live counter-example—but it narrowed the gap rather than conceding it. CON also suffered a real execution cost from the clipped opening, losing the ability to fully develop its case from the start. CON's best material (the emotional-bypass argument in A4.3–A4.4) arrived late and was not built up sufficiently over earlier rounds to carry decisive weight. PRO's closing crystallized the single decisive question cleanly, while CON's closing relied on repeating the fabricated-premise point without new comparative weighing. Across the exchange PRO was sharper on the most contested sub-clashes and left more live routes to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B2.4–B2.5 turn on the culture-war clash: CON argued that logic curricula invite partisan attack while media literacy sidesteps it. PRO inverted this by pointing out that media literacy fundamentally requires designating which sources are trustworthy, which in a polarized society triggers exactly the institutional-bias backlash CON warned about. This was a genuine reversal, not a gloss, and it was reinforced by PRO's clean answer in B4.1 distinguishing contested "critical thinking" from objective mathematics—a distinction CON never adequately refuted.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): B wins because its central rebuttal exposed a major gap in A's model: formal logic and probability can evaluate reasoning, but they often cannot detect fabricated premises, deepfakes, or manipulated provenance. A had a strong live route on institutional distrust and media literacy's perishability, but B answered that media literacy is process-based and aimed at the information ecosystem rather than merely trusting approved outlets or checking artifacts. A's case was rhetorically sharp but over-relied on the idea that disinformation is usually statistically absurd, which left too many realistic manipulations untouched.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was the answer that a falsehood can be logically coherent if the premise is fabricated; media literacy teaches students to interrogate source, provenance, motive, and amplification before logic can even be applied.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): A built the stronger case by consistently showing that logic/stats provide an internal, institution-independent defense that survives zero trust and perfect AI forgery, while directly answering pressure questions and neutralizing B’s reactivity claims in rebuttals. B’s media-literacy defense weakened under scrutiny on source verification and obsolescence without fully resolving those burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s A4 rebuttal to B’s answers on Q1/Q2, exposing reliance on poisoned external ecosystems and AI-spoofable markers versus self-contained probability intuition.
