- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): PRO built the stronger path to victory by landing two arguments CON never fully neutralized: (1) that existing enforcement mechanisms cannot reliably catch language engineered to be non-falsifiable—"glow," "balance," "detox" evade substantiation rules by never actually asserting anything checkable—and (2) that labels operate at the moment of decision rather than after-the-fact, when the parasocial pitch has already landed. CON's response to the enforcement gap ("misleading implication rules can reach it in context") is an assertion requiring a reactive enforcement process that arrives too late, and CON never demonstrated how a rule against false claims catches language deliberately designed to be neither true nor false. CON's strongest counter—that "wellness" becomes a safe harbor laundering vague promises into a marketable official category—was genuine and the most original move in the debate, and PRO's definitional answer in B4 (wellness language "borrows the vocabulary of health to imply a bodily benefit it never quite states," distinguishing it from pure puffery) was adequate rather than decisive. CON's closing sharpened the "PRO invented a warning the label doesn't actually contain" attack effectively, and this is a real tension PRO left partly unresolved. However, CON was clipped twice (Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2), losing argument substance at critical moments, while PRO's closing was also clipped but the core framework was already established. CON's temporal concession—"truth-based rules can require specific disclaimers at the point of sale"—effectively adopted PRO's front-end logic while only disputing implementation, narrowing rather than defeating PRO's case. The margin is clear but competitive: PRO's enforcement gap and temporal arguments remain live routes; CON's labeling-as-safe-harbor argument was the most penetrating challenge but was not enough to overcome those unresolved gaps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B4 answer to CON's pressure question about non-falsifiable language was the most decisive exchange. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built the stronger path to victory by exploiting a structural contradiction in PRO's own case. PRO identified parasocial trust as the distinctive and decisive harm — the celebrity telling a fourteen-year-old "this fixed me" — and then, under pressure, conceded in A2 that "this changed my life" survives the labeling regime entirely. CON turned this concession into the debate's sharpest reversal: PRO's seatbelt analogy is self-defeating when the crash mechanism (parasocial persuasion) runs unimpeded after full compliance. The "organized ambiguity" framing — three labeled ambiguous promises rather than one, with the medical tag now lending credibility to adjacent wellness language by proximity — was never adequately neutralized. CON also made a plausible and unanswered institutional prediction: sophisticated legal teams will route contested terms toward the least regulated category, turning required distinctions into compliance shields for sellers rather than maps for buyers. PRO's cost-of-ambiguity closing is emotionally resonant and the motion's normative core (young audiences deserve clarity) has real pull, but CON answered it directly: labels don't shift cost from teenager to brand; they give the brand a legal defense while the persuasive engine runs on unchanged. PRO's repeated clipping penalty is a real execution cost that likely cut some of their best responses. The strongest surviving PRO line — that existing law doesn't reach the wellness gray zone of "detox," "barrier," "healing" — is genuine, but CON's reply (industry-wide enforcement of existing law, not a new one-class regime) is adequate and untouched.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's seatbelt reversal in Rebuttal 2: PRO introduced the seatbelt analogy to argue that labeling reduces a predictable harm mechanism. CON responded that PRO had already identified parasocial trust as the specific mechanism, then admitted "this changed my life" survives the labeling requirement. A seatbelt that does not address the crash mechanism is not a seatbelt; the analogy collapses on PRO's own concession. This exchange is decisive because it showed PRO's remedy is disconnected from PRO's own harm theory, and PRO never recovered a clean answer to it.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): CON exposed a persistent gap between the harm PRO identified (parasocial persuasion) and the remedy offered (claim categorization).
