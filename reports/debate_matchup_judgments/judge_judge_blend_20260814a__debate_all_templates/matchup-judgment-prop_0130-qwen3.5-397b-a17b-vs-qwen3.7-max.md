### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Both sides ran disciplined, well-structured cases, but PRO (Side B) built a slightly stronger cumulative path. Its three interlocking arguments — (1) theft does not transfer ownership regardless of the thief's vault quality; (2) the preservation gap is itself partly a product of circular colonial logic; and (3) the "artificial ceiling" on origin-country capacity building is perpetuated by retaining the very collections those countries need to anchor funding and expertise — formed a coherent moral and structural case that CON never fully dismantled. CON's strongest pillar, "survival is the prerequisite for justice," is genuine but operates almost entirely within PRO's conceded territory: it concedes the injustice and argues only for delay, not against return. CON's answer to the artificial-ceiling problem — partnerships, loans, technical funding — is reasonable but leaves intact PRO's central point that the decision over when "safety is assured" remains with the institution that committed the original wrong. CON's physical-analysis rebuttal (carbon dating, pigment composition) was the debate's sharpest narrow counter and genuinely answered PRO's digitization question, but it addresses only a subset of cases. PRO's "gilded cage" and "preserved corpse" language gave it memorable rhetorical anchors without sacrificing substance. CON's closing crystallized its core claim cleanly, but never resolved the structural asymmetry PRO kept pressing: a foreign museum deciding unilaterally when conditions are adequate enough for return is itself the continuation of colonial paternalism. The margin is real but not dominant — CON's survival argument is genuinely unrefuted for the hardest cases — making this a clear but competitive win for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 in the pressure round — asking how origin countries can ever build adequate facilities if Western institutions permanently retain the centerpiece collections needed to attract funding — combined with CON's answer about "partnerships and loans rather than permanent transfer," was the debate's most decisive exchange. PRO's follow-up in Rebuttal 2 correctly noted that this leaves the question of who decides when conditions are adequate entirely in the hands of the retaining institution, exposing the paternalism at the core of CON's position. CON never resolved this structural asymmetry.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won a close but clear debate by keeping the focus on looted ownership, cultural agency, and the paternalism of using superior museum resources as a permanent excuse for retention. Side A's survival prerequisite argument was powerful and memorable, but it often overstated the motion as conceding destruction rather than merely a preservation advantage, and it treated foreign custody as nearly guaranteed safety while giving thinner grounding for why return would mean serious loss. B's thief-with-a-better-vault framing and access/capacity-building responses better answered the central moral clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal was the decisive exchange: it turned Side A's preservation claim into a perverse incentive argument, saying the answer to infrastructure gaps is repatriation plus conservation support, not permanent hoarding, and it also undercut the encyclopedic museum access claim by pointing out that the current system mainly privileges the Global North.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B built the stronger case by consistently framing retention as ongoing theft that blocks local capacity and cultural agency, directly answering pressure questions on digitization and funding, and neutralizing CON's survival metric as paternalistic without conceding the motion's premise. Side A defended survival effectively but failed to fully rebut the perverse-incentive and agency arguments or explain why guaranteed current safety justifies permanent hoarding.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) that reframed CON's safety metric as artificially maintained deprivation and rejected the thief's vault excuse as fatal to CON's position.
