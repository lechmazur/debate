### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A wins because it made the burden clearer: legalization need not destroy cartels, only weaken them by removing or compressing a revenue stream that prohibition currently guarantees. B had strong arguments on diversification, state capture, and Uruguay being a weak analogy, but relied too heavily on asserting that cartels would fully replace losses or capture the legal market. A answered that adaptation is costly, revenue is not infinitely elastic, and even partial legal capture faces taxes, audits, lower margins, and legal competition. B’s clipping also slightly hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s most decisive rebuttal was that B’s adaptation argument proves too much: if criminals can always pivot, then no attack on any revenue stream would count as weakening. That reframed the debate around incremental weakening, where A’s revenue-diversion and margin-compression story was cleaner than B’s full-offset claim.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side B won by decisively winning the economic clash. While Side A effectively argued that cartels would attempt to capture the legal market and shift to synthetics, Side B's Rebuttal 2 successfully countered this by explaining that legal markets impose margin compression, and that synthetic markets are not infinitely elastic. Side A failed to overcome the logic that a cartel with compressed margins and lost revenue streams is definitionally weaker, and suffered execution costs from repeatedly clipping the word limit.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 systematically dismantled Side A's fallback arguments by introducing the concepts of margin compression in captured legal markets and the inelasticity/scaling costs of synthetic drug expansion.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A established a clearer net-weakening mechanism: legalization diverts cannabis revenue with costly adaptation and margin compression, directly countering B's capture and pivot claims in rebuttals and pressure answers. B's diversification and institutional-capture lines were repetitive and only partially addressed the resource costs A highlighted; clipped B turns added execution cost. A crystallized the revenue-removal vs status-quo subsidy choice more convincingly across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2 format) directly answered B's pressure questions on licensed-dispensary capture and synthetic scaling by showing compressed margins and resource limits, sharpening the live clash.
