### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO (Side B) built the stronger path to victory by anchoring the debate on a question CON never adequately answered: what happens between the ad and the consultation room? CON's central response—that informed consent downstream corrects for advertising upstream—was challenged early and never reinforced with a mechanism. CON's most interesting original point (individual biomarkers like AMH matter more than population averages) had real traction, and the "twice the rate of what?" fractional-framing attack was the debate's sharpest CON moment. But PRO neutralized both by Round 2: the "imperfect anchor is better than a fabricated one" reframe showed that the biomarker argument strengthens the case for a realistic statistical baseline rather than against it, and PRO's narrowing to a single directional sentence ("success rates vary significantly by age—ask about yours") foreclosed the data-dump objection. CON's answer to PRO's Q1—that having no age statistics is better than having imperfect ones—was the debate's weakest moment and was never convincingly defended. CON also repeatedly attacked an exaggerated version of PRO's position (full attrition chain on a billboard) rather than the modest regulatory floor PRO actually defended from Rebuttal 2 onward. CON's closing attempted to re-exploit the gap between PRO's minimal-sentence example and the proposition's language about "realistic age-based success rates," which was the strongest closing move, but it came too late and was undercut by PRO's earlier crystallization. Overall, PRO made the decisive clash clearer, adapted better across rounds, and left the more persuasive live argument standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's Q2/anchoring argument in Rebuttal 2 [B4.3–B4.4]: PRO reframed the status quo itself as producing the worst possible anchor—aspirational baby photos and "preserve your fertility" promises with no numerical grounding—so that CON's warning about misleading population averages applied more forcefully against current practice than against the proposed disclosure. The line "Individual biomarkers then refine that anchor rather than tear down a fantasy" effectively flipped CON's biomarker argument into support for PRO's position, and CON never recovered a clean counter.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won because it made the more convincing comparative case that advertising shapes expectations before informed consent can correct them, and that age is too central to egg-freezing outcomes to be hidden behind aspirational marketing. Side A landed a real objection that vague disclaimers or relative-risk soundbites may not satisfy the motion and could mislead, but B answered the bigger clash better: imperfect, standardized age-based context is more realistic than baby-photo optimism, and consultation can refine rather than replace that baseline.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's strongest exchange was against the consultation-room argument: ads are not neutral awareness tools, they create the expectations doctors later must undo, so downstream consent does not excuse upstream omission.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.7 Max (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by showing that advertising is structurally incapable of presenting nuanced age-based success rates without producing misleading soundbites, and that individualized biomarkers matter more than age cohorts in fertility medicine. PRO was unable to defend the actual proposition without retreating to vague disclaimers that did not satisfy the motion, while CON defended consultation-based informed consent as the proper venue and exposed the harms of decontextualized statistical anchors.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s first rebuttal dismantling of PRO’s proposed single-line statistic, demonstrating that relative-risk framing like “twice the live birth rate” obscures low absolute probabilities and would be weaponized in ad copy, undermining PRO’s claim that the mandate could be satisfied simply and honestly.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): PRO wins a clear but competitive debate. Three of CON's turns were clipped for exceeding the word limit—Opening, Rebuttal 1, and Pressure Questions—creating a real execution cost throughout. More substantively, PRO's core live routes were never fully neutralized. The "thaw survival vs. live-birth rate" example concretely showed clinics already use manipulative prominence choices, and CON never disputed this. PRO's pivot on the data problem was the debate's most effective move: rather than defending false precision, PRO argued that thin or incomplete data is itself the thing that must be prominently disclosed—turning CON's strongest argument into a reason for the mandate rather than against it. CON's best remaining counter—that proxy estimates from fresh IVF carry false regulatory authority—is genuine, but PRO explicitly addressed this by calling for ads to label estimates as estimates with acknowledged uncertainty, which is meaningfully better than the current "fertility insurance" framing. CON's claim that the consultation room already handles the problem was badly hurt by PRO's "point of sale" reframe, and CON's counter (women research online anyway) failed to fully rebut the asymmetry at first contact. CON's APR-standardization critique landed, but PRO's broader consumer-protection framework absorbed it. CON's closing crystallized the data argument well, but by that point PRO's case had more unresolved routes to victory than CON's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B2.5 response to CON's data-inadequacy argument: if outcome data is thin and confounded, that uncertainty is precisely what must be prominently disclosed. This judo move converted CON's strongest technical objection into an affirmative argument for the mandate, and CON's subsequent attempts to show that uncertainty disclaimers produce only boilerplate or false precision never fully recovered from PRO's explicit framing—"disclose that estimates are based on fresh IVF proxies and that long-term data is incomplete"—which is workably better than the status quo even if imperfect.
- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate. Side A’s strongest point was the workability objection: age-based frozen-egg live-birth data is incomplete and individualized, so mandated ad numbers could create false precision. But Side B more convincingly tied the motion to the advertising stage specifically, showing that existing informed consent and anti-fraud rules do not solve misleading first impressions or selective metrics like thaw survival. Because the proposition includes uncertainty, Side B had a plausible answer to the data objection: require disclosure of uncertainty and realistic limitations rather than let clinics market hope while hiding weak outcome information. Side A’s repeated clipping also slightly hurt execution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s first rebuttal was the most decisive: it answered existing regulation by distinguishing consultation-room consent from advertising prominence, answered infantilization with consumer-protection analogies, and turned Side A’s uncertainty objection into a reason for prominent uncertainty disclosure.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Qwen 3.7 Max (PRO)): PRO won by maintaining that prominently disclosing uncertainty—including explicit admissions that data is thin—materially improves on a status quo where clinics push misleading metrics like "thaw survival" while burying age-based realities. CON landed real damage on the proxy-estimate problem and the consultation-room checkpoint, but never fully overcame PRO's core asymmetry: advertising currently frames egg freezing as reliable fertility insurance, and even imperfect mandated disclosures would puncture that illusion before financial and emotional commitment. PRO's marketing-funnel framing survived the exchange more cleanly than CON's reliance on existing regulation and speculative claims about women's pre-consultation research.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's first rebuttal, which separated FTC fraud enforcement from prominence mandates, turned the "infantilization" charge back on CON, and recast thin data as a reason to mandate uncertainty disclosure rather than a reason to do nothing.
