### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V3.2 (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) built the stronger cumulative case. Its core argument—that the invasion destroyed a repressive but geographically contained equilibrium and replaced it with a power vacuum that incubated AQI/ISIS, enabled Iran's proxy expansion, and produced civil war on a scale that simple containment erosion would not have generated—was consistently developed, grounded in observable historical outcomes, and never squarely neutralized. The "cold suppression replaced by hot, self-reinforcing contagion" framing held up across all five turns. Side A (CON) raised genuine pressure: the execution-vs.-decision distinction regarding de-Baathification, the point that Iran's expansionism predated 2003, and the argument that a resurgent Saddam might have launched another interstate war worse than the civil war. However, CON's decisive counterfactual—that a future Saddam interstate war would dwarf actual instability—remained speculative throughout. After twelve years of post-Gulf War containment, asserting Saddam was poised for regional conquest required more grounding than CON supplied. PRO effectively answered this in B4.1-B4.2 by noting that even eroding containment would have kept the jihadist incubator from forming and prevented the sudden dissolution of Iraqi state structures. CON's "explosion now vs. explosion later" framing in A4.2 was CON's most interesting move, but PRO's counterpoint that the actual post-invasion scale (caliphate, regional proxy wars, hundreds of thousands dead) had no parallel under containment was not convincingly rebutted. PRO's pressure questions were sharper and more targeted than CON's, and PRO's answers to CON's questions were more direct. The closings crystallized the existing clash without new dumps, and PRO's crystallization was cleaner. The result is a clear but competitive win for PRO.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.2 answering CON's pressure questions: PRO directly explained why even eroding containment would not have provided the territorial incubator for AQI/ISIS (Saddam never allowed jihadists a base inside Iraq), and why coalition "resources" presided over de-Baathification and sectarian fracturing rather than stabilizing—materially damaging CON's claim that removal itself was sound and only execution was flawed.
