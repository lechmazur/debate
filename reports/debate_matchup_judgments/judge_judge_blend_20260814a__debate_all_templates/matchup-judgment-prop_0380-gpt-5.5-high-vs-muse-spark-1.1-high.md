### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent frameworks, but CON's distinction between voting as "one equal voice" and voting as aggregated power to bind others held up better under pressure than PRO's capacity-based inclusion standard. CON's horizontal-vs-vertical influence distinction (adults can lawfully exit bosses, spouses, landlords; sixteen-year-olds cannot lawfully exit school or guardianship) was a genuinely load-bearing point that PRO never neutralized with a concrete counterexample of comparably coercive adult dependency. The pressure-question exchange was decisive: CON's Q1 asking why the line stops at sixteen rather than fourteen exposed that PRO's "capacity plus membership" standard has no principled stopping point. PRO's answer (sixteen "tracks capacity better," "no line is metaphysically perfect") retreated into exactly the administrative-convenience justification PRO's own opening had explicitly disclaimed, and CON caught this contradiction cleanly in Rebuttal 2. PRO's closing tried to reframe this as CON's structures being self-justifying and circular, but never directly rebuilt a principled non-arbitrary stopping point at sixteen. PRO also suffered a real execution cost: Rebuttal 2 was clipped for exceeding the word limit, cutting off its final synthesis right as it pivoted to weigh the round. CON's closing was tighter, reasserted the unanswered slippery-slope pressure, and left the audience with a clearer decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's answer to its own pressure question (A1/A2 exchange) — pointing out that PRO's response to "why sixteen and not fourteen" collapsed into an admission of administrative preference rather than principle — was the single most damaging blow of the debate, since it undercut PRO's central claim that the line was principled rather than arbitrary.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON built the cleaner decisive case that the franchise is aggregated power to bind others and must track the legal shift to full civil majority, exit rights, and unmitigated liability; PRO’s membership-plus-capacity standard never cleanly answered why 16 rather than 14 or why non-exitable vertical dependence is harmless. PRO’s school-habit and vulnerability points were live but did not outweigh the responsibility/exit clash after A’s direct answers; B’s clipped Rebuttal 2 was an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 distinction between adult horizontal exit rights and a minor’s non-exitable vertical hierarchy (A4.2/A4.5) neutralized PRO’s secret-ballot cure and locked in the line-drawing pressure.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by drawing a cleaner principled distinction: voting should follow the age of full civil majority because binding others requires being fully bound oneself. PRO never squarely answered why sixteen rather than fourteen under their capacity-based standard, and their school-habit argument was turned effectively when CON showed that compulsory hierarchy creates vertical influence incompatible with independent judgment. CON’s rebuttals narrowed the clash to these decisive burdens and maintained them through the close.
