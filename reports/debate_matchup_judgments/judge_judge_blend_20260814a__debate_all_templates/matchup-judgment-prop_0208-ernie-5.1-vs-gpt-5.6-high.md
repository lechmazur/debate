### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully breaking Side A's causal link between a program's content and its low completion rate. Side B convincingly argued that completion rates in shorter pathways are higher largely because they mechanically expose students to fewer semesters of life interruptions, neutralizing Side A's core metric. Side A also incurred an execution penalty for introducing a highly specific new example in the closing speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's point that shorter technical pathways naturally require fewer semesters in which work, illness, or caregiving can interrupt enrollment, which elegantly explained away Side A's completion rate advantage.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON wins by exposing and sustaining a causal gap that PRO never closed: low completion does not prove degree program design is the cause of attrition, and PRO never showed that the same students would complete technical pathways or that those pathways offer superior long-term value. CON turned PRO’s structural explanations for technical completion into an argument for better support of existing degrees rather than cannibalizing them, and offered a concrete repair alternative. PRO’s late closing example and sweeping assertions about decades of failed reform could not bridge the missing causal link, while CON crystallized the decisive clash cleanly in the final speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s causal-identification critique and its turn that PRO’s own structural explanations for technical success—shorter duration and built-in supports—proved support matters, not that degree programs deserved funding cuts.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON won the causation and comparability clash. PRO's core arithmetic — low completion = waste, high completion = success — was shown to conflate outcome with cause, duration with quality, and population differences with program failure. CON answered pressure with specific repair mechanisms (guaranteed required seats, evening schedules, completion grants, admin consolidation) while PRO never bridged the same-student gap and introduced a prohibited new anecdote in closing about a nursing reallocation to substitute for evidence. Finite-budget pressure remains real, but CON left a live route: fix bottlenecks without narrowing options for low-income students.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3-A4.5 — CON shows registrar data measures outcome not cause, shorter programs mechanically have fewer interruption points, and crediting "built-in supports" proves support matters rather than that degree content should lose funding.
