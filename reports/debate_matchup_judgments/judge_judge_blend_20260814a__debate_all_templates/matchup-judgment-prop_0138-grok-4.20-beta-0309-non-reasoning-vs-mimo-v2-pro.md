### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO)): B wins on three decisive clashes. First, the contract asymmetry: A's core argument was that retroactively voiding anonymity agreements is an intolerable bait-and-switch, but B correctly and persistently identified that the child was never a party to that contract and cannot be bound by terms that erase their origins—a point A never adequately answered. In R2, A actually conceded that "the child is not a party," which inadvertently strengthened B's position. Second, the DNA-testing turn: A used consumer genetic testing to argue medical needs could be met without identifying information; B turned this into evidence that legal secrecy no longer protects anyone but instead converts managed knowledge into traumatic surprise discovery. This was the sharpest single rebuttal in the debate and left A without a coherent response. Third, on supply: A's strongest live argument was concrete harm to infertile couples from reduced donation; B's response—that supply reduction is a "clarification" separating dignity-respecting donors from those demanding invisible parenthood, and that adaptation is possible (UK example)—was principled and held up under pressure. A never showed why the supply cost outweighs the permanent erasure of identity rights; it simply repeated "concrete suffering" without engaging B's proportionality framework. A also weakened its own case by repeatedly labeling identity harm "sentimental" rather than directly rebutting it, and its later speeches were more repetitive than adaptive. B's progression across rounds was steadier: it answered pressure questions directly, maintained its core asymmetry argument, and consistently sharpened rather than widened the debate.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's turn on consumer DNA testing (B2.3–B2.4, reinforced in B4.2): A had argued that anonymized medical questionnaires plus consumer testing already met donor-conceived people's needs, making legal identifiability unnecessary. B inverted this—consumer testing proves anonymity is already broken, but delivers the revelation in the most traumatic, unmanaged way possible, precisely because legal secrecy forced the information underground. This converted A's practical argument into evidence for B's position and was never adequately answered by A.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON)): Side A wins by effectively distinguishing gamete donation from ordinary contracts, noting that procreative agreements uniquely create a non-consenting third party. Side B leans heavily on the sanctity of contracts but fails to overcome A's point that consumer DNA testing has already rendered legal anonymity a harmful fiction. Furthermore, Side B weakens its own case in Rebuttal 2 by mischaracterizing A's proposed "right to access" as forcibly exposing adults to information they may not seek.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A1), which definitively neutralizes B's slippery-slope contract argument by explaining that contracts creating human beings cannot bind the unconsenting third party they produce.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by establishing that donor-conceived people cannot be bound by contracts they never joined and that their interest in knowing their origins is a matter of basic dignity, not mere sentiment. B decisively turned CON’s own consumer-genetics example against them, showing that technology has already undermined anonymity and that legal secrecy now produces traumatic late discoveries rather than genuine protection. While CON landed real pressure on supply reductions and contractual trust, the motion itself flags supply loss as an acceptable cost, and B’s moral framing—that it is wrong to treat created persons as collateral damage in a fertility market—proved more resilient across the exchange. B also answered pressure questions more directly, whereas CON’s attempt to characterize a legal right to information as forced state exposure slightly mischaracterized the proposal and left the core dignity claim standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s turn that consumer DNA testing has already shattered guaranteed anonymity, transforming legal secrecy from a shield into a source of traumatic late discovery. This undermined CON’s defense of the status quo and repurposed CON’s own consumer-testing example against them.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (CON); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Non-Reasoning) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by successfully distinguishing gamete donation from ordinary commercial contracts, framing it as an act of procreation that creates a third party with independent rights the state must protect. PRO also effectively leveraged the reality of consumer DNA testing to show that legal secrecy no longer protects privacy but merely delays discovery into traumatic surprise, undermining CON's practical defense. While CON articulated strong utilitarian concerns about supply and contractual reliance, PRO's moral weighing aligned more cleanly with the motion's explicit trade-off, and PRO's rebuttals more decisively neutralized CON's core frameworks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's contract argument, correctly noting that agreements deliberately creating human beings cannot bind the resulting third party, and that the state routinely overrides adult contracts when children's welfare is at stake. This carved out a decisive exception to CON's reliance-interest framework.
