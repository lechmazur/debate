- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): B won by making the decisive clash clearer: the proposition requires showing "the" full practical cost before comparison, yet no such unique knowable number exists from search parameters alone (bags especially vary wildly for identical family-size data). A's anchoring critique and nutrition/MPG analogies were sharp but never neutralized that burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 [B4.3-B4.5]: toggle admits the mandated "full" cost is false for many and rewrites the prop into a hierarchy that hides the true base-fare unit.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by consistently proving that a regulator-mandated "full practical cost" would replace a real, achievable minimum price with a fictional average stereotype of a family, systematically distorting comparison rather than fixing it. B's closing crystallization—framing the clash as a true minimum ($79 is real) versus a state-mandated guess ($340 is wrong for millions)—was decisive. A's anchoring and comparison-stage harms were real but never fully overcame B's demonstration that the cure would bury legitimate cheap options and mislead search rankings, while existing checkout disclosure already addresses the harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal and closing demolition of A's "standard serving size" analogy by calling it "a nutrition label that pretends to be the grocery bill," coupled with the framing that a base fare is a true minimum while the mandated total is a false average that most users must hunt to undo.
