### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively resolving the core clash over age verification. Side B dismantled Side A's strongest argument (that both policies face the same verification hurdles) by showing that design duties can fail gracefully by defaulting to safety, while a bright-line ban demands exact verification and surveillance to avoid locking out adults.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, contrasting the "graceful failure" of design duties with the "catastrophic failure" of a ban under imperfect age verification.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): CON won by exposing a structural asymmetry that PRO never neutralized: under imperfect age verification, a ban either surveils the entire population or locks legitimate teenagers out of speech, whereas design duties can be structured as universal safe defaults that fail gracefully. PRO conceded verification is fuzzy at the fifteen-sixteen boundary but kept asserting the verification problem was symmetric, ignoring CON's "defaults for all" solution. CON also showed a ban buys compliance gates, not product reform, leaving the addictive machine intact, while PRO's counter that market loss would drive reform remained speculative and unsupported by the mechanism in the motion. PRO's aggregate harm framing was strong, but CON's cleaner failure-mode analysis and sharper crystallization of the tradeoff gave CON the clearer path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 [A4.3] and Closing [A5.2] framing that design duties "fail gracefully" under fuzzy age signals (cost: an adult gets a chronological feed) while a ban "fails catastrophically" (cost: a seventeen-year-old is locked out of public speech, requiring mass document collection), completely dissolving PRO's claim that both policies face the same verification problem.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON won the central enforcement and incentive clashes. PRO's pipeline-loss incentive fails because the motion's ban is keyed to a birthday, not safety, so cheap compliance is a gate, not a fix. On verification, PRO's symmetry claim was answered by CON's graceful vs catastrophic failure distinction: design duties can be applied as broad defaults with trivial adult cost, while a ban cannot tolerate the admitted fuzziness at 15-16 without population-wide ID/biometrics and speech lockouts. That left CON's route—improve the product even for evaders—alive while PRO's bright-line route was substantially undercut.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B R2 A1/A2 graceful vs catastrophic failure and inspectable defaults — dissolved PRO's verification symmetry and showed ban leaves the engine humming for the unsupervised kids it claims to protect.
