### Judge Notes

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): A won by maintaining a coherent enforcement mechanism and showing that the concrete harm of hidden evidence outweighs speculative deterrence. Bs definitional attack collapsed once A clarified the rule requires comprehensive disclosure of all trials, not just labeling results negative. B never proved that alternative sanctions like fines or audits would reliably compel disclosure, and its claim that cure periods contradict meaningful consequences mischaracterizes standard regulatory escalation ladders. While Bs patient collateral framing was rhetorically sharp and A never fully rebutted the optional trial deterrence point, A kept the cleaner comparative advantage by asking why immediate access to a product with a curated evidence base protects patients. Bs clipped opening also left its third argument underdeveloped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A Rebuttal 2 answering Bs Pressure Questions (A1/A2), where A explained that cure periods for minor errors and a credible market-access backstop for persistent refusal are complementary rather than contradictory, and that speculation about trials never designed cannot outweigh the concrete danger of burying completed evidence relevant to current treatment.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): PRO won by neutralizing the definitional attack - reframing to all trials with methods/outcomes - and by resolving the cure vs consequence contradiction as standard deterrence where the sponsor controls cost. CON never supplied a credible non-access sanction and its strongest "don't run the trial" harm remained speculative for optional studies, while PRO's concrete harm of hidden completed evidence affecting every prescription decision was directly responsive to market access privilege.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2.1/A2.3 and A4.1/A4.5 answers - that cure periods distinguish error from refusal and that rare use proves deterrence, not softness - directly undercut CON's central contradiction claim that made B2/B4 rely on.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by successfully trapping Side B in a practical dilemma regarding the enforcement mechanism: a credible market-access veto harms patients by delaying medicine access over compliance issues, while a rule with generous cure periods operates as the "soft obligation" Side B initially dismissed. Side A also landed a decisive blow by pointing out that the rule incentivizes sponsors to simply not run or register non-mandated trials (like comparative effectiveness and long-term safety studies), which Side B failed to adequately answer by focusing only on regulator-mandated pivotal trials. Side B's moral framing was strong, but Side A's practical weighing of the policy's real-world incentives and enforcement trade-offs was more convincing. Side A receives a minor execution penalty for a clipped opening, but its subsequent rebuttals were exceptionally sharp and controlled the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it dismantled Side B's "cure period" defense by showing that the rule's practical operation is soft while its credible threat harms patients, and effectively highlighted that the most damaging suppression occurs in non-mandated trials which the rule incentivizes sponsors to avoid entirely.
