That combination of surviving pressure better and controlling the final framing gives B a clear, if not overwhelming, edge.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON maintained a decisive structural dilemma that PRO never fully resolved: compensation high enough to increase supply inherently exploits financial desperation, while compensation too low fails to help patients. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but the decisive exchange centered on whether regulatory safeguards can actually neutralize the coercive force of poverty. CON's sharpest move was the "fatal dilemma" pressed in Rebuttal 2 and Closing: an advocate who defers financially desperate cases excludes exactly the people the policy claims to protect, while one who approves them after counseling has merely documented desperation rather than cured it. PRO's closing restated procedural safeguards (deferral, staged payments, withdrawal rights) without directly resolving this bind, effectively asserting both horns are acceptable rather than escaping the trap. CON's opening and Rebuttal 1 were clipped for length, which is a real execution cost, but the core substantive content in each speech had already been delivered before the cutoff, limiting the damage. On balance, CON's rebuttal work more precisely exposed an unresolved structural weakness in PRO's regulatory model, giving CON the cleaner path to victory in the final exchange.
