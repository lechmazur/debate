  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was answering A's enforcement and black-market pressure: ticket IDs and platform records make platform-side caps plausible, and fragmented unregulated sites are a much less convincing bulk outlet than major resale platforms.

PRO's rebuttals did more material damage to CON's live routes than vice versa.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4), where they answered CON's pressure questions by explaining embedded ticket metadata for trivial cap enforcement and demonstrating that bulk scalping requires major platforms' trusted traffic and escrow infrastructure—directly neutralizing CON's "forensic accounting" and "unregulated migration" objections.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (PRO)): Side B (PRO) wins by constructing a tighter causal chain and decisively turning Side A's key examples. While CON raised legitimate concerns about market migration and compliance, PRO effectively neutralized them by explaining why bulk scalping requires the liquidity, trust, and escrow of major platforms—conditions fragmented black markets lack. PRO's handling of the "parent recoupment" pressure was particularly damaging to CON: by pointing out that the above-face outlay CON worries about only exists because of the current unregulated markup system, PRO exposed the circularity in CON's harm scenario. PRO's closing crystallized the clash cleanly, leaving CON with no live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's response to CON's "parent stranded with losses" argument in Rebuttal 2 and Closing. CON claimed a parent who bought a ticket secondhand at $300 (for a $100 face value seat) couldn't recoup costs under a face-value cap. PRO sharply turned this by noting that the $300 premium is itself an artifact of the unregulated scalping market the motion seeks to fix; under the rule, that inflated purchase would never occur, and legitimate sellers could still recover face value plus verified fees. This dismantled CON's central equity objection and showcased superior weighing.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): A won by making the scope problem decisive: B’s strongest examples and harms centered on superstar sellouts and voluntary systems, while the motion covers most tickets across many mid-tier events. B had a plausible conflict-of-interest case, but its answers to empty seats, voluntary examples, and cost shifting were repetitive and sometimes evasive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s rebuttal that Glastonbury and Springsteen prove only that voluntary, bespoke systems can work for high-demand events, not that a mandatory rule for most tickets is justified, was the key exchange.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): CON successfully exposed a crippling scope mismatch in PRO's case: every example and causal mechanism PRO offered—superstar tours, Glastonbury, Springsteen—applied only to a narrow tier of guaranteed-sellout events, while the proposition mandates caps or face-value resale for most tickets. PRO never adequately answered why mid-tier concerts, local sports, and theater shows would not suffer the harms CON identified (empty seats, sellers unable to recoup fees, cost-shifting to primary sales). PRO's attempt to narrow the mandate to high-demand events felt like an evasion of the resolution's text, and while PRO's conflict-of-interest argument had force, it could not overcome the demonstrated collateral damage of a blanket policy. CON's alternative of targeted primary-market fixes remained more credible, and CON's closing crystallization of the scope problem provided the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's repeated drilling of the scope mismatch in Rebuttal 1 and the closing—showing that PRO's evidence addressed only top-tier sellouts while the mandate would cover mid-tier events where capped resale would leave seats empty and punish legitimate sellers—was the decisive exchange, as PRO never recovered from the charge that their solution was one-size-fits-all for a problem confined to a fraction of events.

  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 and Closing effectively turned PRO’s Glastonbury example against them by highlighting its voluntary, niche nature, while hammering the “scope mismatch” between superstar sellouts and the “most tickets” mandate. This directly neutralized PRO’s core evidence and established a decisive voting issue on collateral damage to mid-tier events and legitimate sellers.
