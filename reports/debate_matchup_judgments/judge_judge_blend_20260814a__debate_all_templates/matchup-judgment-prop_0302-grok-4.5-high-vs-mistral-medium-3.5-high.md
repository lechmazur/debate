### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): A won by establishing persistent male disadvantages across income levels, countries, discipline, graduation, and higher education, then explaining why a larger socioeconomic gap does not make this independent, compounding pattern marginal. B offered credible developmental and class-based alternatives, but repeatedly shifted from whether this is a major structural problem to whether it is the primary crisis requiring boy-exclusive policy. Its own account of developmental averages predictably interacting with common school rules also resembled a structural mismatch more than it refuted one. A overclaimed that cross-national uniformity and high-resource gaps conclusively prove its mechanism, and its opening was clipped, but B never adequately explained why the conceded breadth and consequences should remain merely marginal.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1 most decisively answered B's socioeconomic comparison: even conceding that class gaps are larger, a gender disadvantage recurring within every stratum remains an independent policy problem. B's response mainly reiterated that socioeconomic status matters more, which did not defeat the proposition's claim that male underperformance is a major problem rather than the single largest one.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully turning Side B's core defense into proof of the proposition. When Side B argued that the gaps are just the result of ordinary school timelines interacting with boys' developmental averages, Side A effectively demonstrated that a school timeline fundamentally mismatched to half the population's developmental traits is the very definition of a structural problem. Side A's example of the gap widening in high-resource nations like Finland perfectly sealed this argument.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal used the Finland/Sweden example to prove that if the problem were merely innate, more resources would shrink it; instead, higher-resourced enforcement of the existing structure widens it.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully showed that universal gender gaps are the expected signature of developmental averages interacting with ordinary school timing, not proof of structural anti-boy design. CON's rebuttal that high-resource nations show larger gaps because uniform standards make developmental differences more visible—not because of engineered hostility—neutralized PRO's strongest argument. CON also kept socioeconomic status as the steeper structural driver, showing male underperformance should remain a marginal, manageable concern addressed through gender-neutral targeting rather than a major structural reordering around boys.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's answer to PRO's "inversion in high-resource nations" argument: that larger gaps in countries like Finland reflect tighter measurement and uniform standards making developmental differences more visible, rather than proving schools are structurally mismatched against boys.
