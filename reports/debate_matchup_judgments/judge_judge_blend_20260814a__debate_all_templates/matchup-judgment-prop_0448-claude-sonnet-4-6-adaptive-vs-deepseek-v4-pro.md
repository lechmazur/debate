### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B wins narrowly because it kept the comparative burden on the status quo: a capped, prioritized humanitarian visa can still save lives, reduce reliance on smugglers, and give the EU some pre-arrival control. It did not quite prove that a partial safe pathway is worse than no pathway.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The decisive exchange was over caps: Side A sharply exposed that caps weaken the promise of collapsing smuggling, but Side B's response that partial substitution and prioritization still improve on the deadly status quo preserved the pro case.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON wins by exposing a structural contradiction in PRO’s case that survives the full exchange. PRO is forced to concede caps are necessary to manage demand, but CON shows that any cap leaves excluded asylum-seekers facing the same dangerous crossings the visa supposedly eliminates. PRO’s attempt to reframe this as partial progress collides with its own opening promise of a lifesaving corridor and the moral urgency of asylum; telling a persecuted journalist to await the next window undercuts the humanitarian premise. Meanwhile, PRO’s operational rebuttals keep inflating the proposal into a costly parallel overseas system without answering how it avoids creating new transit flows or diplomatic burdens. CON’s analytical pressure sharpens across rounds, while PRO’s route to victory is progressively narrowed by its own concessions.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal and closing, which leveraged PRO’s admission that capped visas exclude most applicants to show that either excluded people still drown (hollowing the corridor promise) or they can safely wait in camps (making the visa redundant). This await the next window concession became the decisive damage to PRO’s humanitarian framing.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by successfully isolating and exploiting the central contradiction in Side A's case: the cap dilemma. Side A's opening promised to destroy the smuggling model and save lives, but under pressure conceded that caps are necessary and that excluded applicants could "await the next window." Side B sharply turned these concessions, arguing that caps preserve the smuggler's market for the excluded and violate the core urgency principle of asylum. Side B also effectively clarified that moving screening offshore does not solve structural information gaps. Side A's adaptations were reasonable but ultimately retreated from its opening promises, leaving Side B with the cleaner, more logically consistent path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 dismantling of Side A's "await the next window" concession. By contrasting the administrative reality of caps with the moral premise of asylum urgency, Side B exposed a fatal tension in the PRO case that Side A never fully resolved.
