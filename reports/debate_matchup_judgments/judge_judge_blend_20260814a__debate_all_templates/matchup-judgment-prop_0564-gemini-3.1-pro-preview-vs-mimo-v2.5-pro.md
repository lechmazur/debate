### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): A won narrowly because the debate ultimately turned on whether major cities could safely replace a large, recurring farebox stream. B proved real equity and access benefits, but A kept the fiscal-scale objection alive: modest ridership gains do not justify surrendering a major operating revenue source, B's concrete fare-free example was not a major city, and broad taxation was not shown to be stable or progressive enough at scale. A had execution costs from clipping and an imperfect pressure-question answer, but still gave the cleaner reason to reject the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's most decisive rebuttal was the response to B's dedicated-tax answer: Dunkirk did not establish scalability for major cities, and B never fully answered how to replace a quarter-to-third of operating budgets permanently without weakening service or other city priorities.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B wins by better sustaining the core fiscal risk argument: fare revenue provides a self-regenerating dedicated stream that shields transit from annual budget competition, a point Side A never neutralized with scalable major-city mechanisms despite the Dunkirk example. Side A effectively highlighted collection inefficiencies and road analogies but left the long-term political vulnerability unaddressed after the pressure questions exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing and rebuttal 2 on fare revenue as a "political immune system" that regenerates with usage, directly answering the funding protection burden left open by Side A's small-scale examples and broad-based tax claims.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B wins because it dismantled Side A's core analogies and exposed the fiscal and scaling gaps in the fare-free proposal. CON deftly turned PRO's road-and-sidewalk comparison by noting that drivers already pay user-linked fees like gas taxes and tolls, undermining the claim that transit should be the sole free infrastructure. CON also successfully pressed that the motion's vague "broad-based taxation" offers no guarantee of progressive funding, while eliminating fare revenue means permanently losing a usage-linked stream that defends transit in budget cycles. PRO's best concrete example was Dunkirk, which CON rightly showed is orders of magnitude smaller than the major cities the motion targets, and PRO never quantified fare-collection savings in a way that could offset the substantial revenue loss. The efficiency and equity arguments from PRO remained plausible but were insufficiently grounded to overcome CON's sustained demonstration that the model is fiscally reckless at the required scale.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's rebuttal in Turn 8 [B4.1] turning PRO's road analogy by pointing out that drivers pay gas taxes, registration fees, and tolls—showing society already applies user-pays principles to infrastructure and reversing what PRO intended as a supportive comparison.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A won by making the central tradeoff clearer: fare-free transit risks sacrificing service quality, frequency, and reliability by removing a large net revenue stream. Side B had a strong equity case, but leaned too heavily on assertions that tax funding and fare-collection savings would replace the gap, without adequately answering A's point that fares net far more than they cost to collect. A also gave a plausible narrower alternative through targeted subsidies and automatic enrollment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal was decisive: it directly answered B's pressure on political funding and targeted-program gaps, then pressed the unresolved math problem that fare collection costs do not plausibly offset losing 20% to 40% of operating revenue.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): Side A wins: more effectively neutralized revenue and means-testing burdens via direct pressure answers, exposed enrollment gaps as systemic, and crystallized universal infrastructure framing. Side B's quality/service concerns stayed live but were undercut by evasion on political consistency and disorder costs. Clash progression favored A across rebuttals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 directly answered both pressure Qs, highlighted CON contradictions on taxation and enforcement security, and reframed costs to sharpen universal vs patchwork comparison.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON)): PRO wins by demonstrating that CON's targeted-subsidy alternative systematically fails to reach the very low-income riders it claims to protect, and CON's only response—vague promises to "streamline bureaucracy"—does not solve the enrollment gaps for gig workers, teenagers, or people in crisis. PRO's universal model closes those gaps while redirecting resources currently wasted on fare collection, enforcement, and means-testing infrastructure. CON's revenue-collapse warnings rest on unsupported assertions that farebox revenue vastly exceeds collection costs and on a politically self-contradicting claim that voters would choose fare-free transit yet reject the broad-based taxes to fund it. Across the exchange, PRO sharpens the clash while CON recycles assertions that PRO has already undermined.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over targeted low-income programs: PRO showed that means-tested subsidies consistently leave most eligible riders uncovered, and CON could only answer with hand-waving about future bureaucratic streamlining, fatally weakening CON's equity case.
