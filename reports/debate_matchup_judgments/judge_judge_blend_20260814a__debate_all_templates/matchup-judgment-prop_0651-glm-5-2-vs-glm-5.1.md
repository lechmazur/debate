### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Both sides were genuinely competitive. A landed strong and original framings throughout—"lease not commons," the drainage metaphor, the circular reform argument, and the Quechua epistemic-flattening point were all sharp and mostly unrebutted at the level of mechanism. However, the motion requires establishing net harm compared to a realistic alternative, and B consistently held A to that standard. B's "compared to what?" framing, the one-barrier-vs-many Botswana example, and the distinction between epistemic legitimacy and communicative reach (A2 in Rebuttal 2, which reframed the "English gate" as providing global visibility rather than certifying validity) were the most analytically decisive moves in the debate. A never defended a concrete feasible alternative—gesturing at "multilingual infrastructure with robust translation layers" while conceding it has never operated at scale. A's circular monopoly response was clever but did not close the gap because B correctly noted that local journals in Hindi, Portuguese, and Mandarin persist, and that researchers can and do publish locally alongside English. A's cafeteria metaphor overstated coercion and left B's revealed-preference argument partially alive. The governance-vs-use clash was genuine and B did not fully resolve it, which prevents a larger margin for B. But on the decisive comparative question the motion actually requires, B maintained the cleaner burden throughout the exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's A2 in Rebuttal 2: distinguishing epistemic legitimacy from communicative reach. A's strongest argument was that knowledge must pass through an English "gate" to count as legitimate science, implying structural epistemic harm. B's response—that Quechua botany is valid science for Quechua speakers regardless of English, and that English provides global visibility rather than certifying truth—directly undermined the most charged version of A's case. A never recovered a response to this distinction, leaving B's comparative framing intact for the close.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = GLM-5.1 (CON)): Side A won by effectively dismantling Side B's 'commons' and 'agency' arguments, distinguishing sharply between access to a system and governance of it. Side A's compelling explanation of 'epistemic filtering'—that translating knowledge into English forces it into Western conceptual frameworks—demonstrated a profound harm that Side B could not outweigh with its pragmatic 'communicative reach' arguments.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 directly answered Side B's pressure question about translation, explaining that passing knowledge through a single dominant language concentrates conceptual distortion by forcing all knowledge to conform to one framework (e.g., Linnaean vs. Quechua taxonomies).
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.1 (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side B sustained a sharper structural critique: English dominance operates as an epistemic filter and governance monopoly, not a neutral commons, and successfully turned Side A's "choice" and "co-authorship" narratives by exposing the coercive incentives beneath them. While Side A effectively pressed the feasibility of alternatives, Side B's access-versus-governance distinction and the toll-booth framing proved more durable across the full exchange.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which distinguished epistemic flattening from mere translation, recast researcher "choice" as coerced adaptation under monopoly incentives, and exposed the circularity of English-dominant reforms.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Both sides debated competently, but CON built the stronger path to victory on the decisive question the motion requires: net harm. PRO's three mechanisms were vivid and structurally clean, and the Yup'ik and gate-not-bridge framings were rhetorically effective throughout. However, PRO's case depended on an alternative ("asymmetric multilingualism with robust translation infrastructure") that CON successfully exposed as historically serving only European elite circuits, not Lagos, Mumbai, or Ouagadougou — the very communities PRO claimed to champion. PRO never recovered from that challenge. CON's B4 answers to the pressure questions were particularly decisive: the artemisinin and Toyota examples directly answered PRO's A3.2 demand for untranslated non-anglophone knowledge reshaping global science, and the "question is rigged by definition" reframe was sharp without being evasive. PRO's closing crystallized the gate metaphor well but made an overclaim — that pre-English multilingual networks preserved conceptual architecture "intact" — that CON had already undermined. PRO was rhetorically stronger round by round, but CON did more argumentative work, especially in demolishing the baseline comparison and fielding pressure questions honestly. The motion requires showing net harm, and CON's consistent answer — imperfect access beats exclusion, and no alternative PRO names delivers comparable reach without comparable or greater harm — remained live at the close. CON's clipped closing is a real execution cost, but what was delivered still landed the key comparison. Margin is clear but competitive because PRO's epistemic erasure argument has genuine force and was never fully extinguished.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 answer to A2: the artemisinin and Toyota examples directly counter PRO's pressure question demanding a non-anglophone knowledge system that reshaped global science on untranslated terms, exposing the framing as "designed to prove PRO's conclusion rather than measure reality." This was the sharpest single exchange — it neutralized PRO's most philosophically resonant claim while giving the debate a concrete, testable stake.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): Side B won by successfully exposing Side A's baseline alternative as historically flawed and by framing the practical benefits of global access as outweighing abstract epistemic losses.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Turn 8 response to Side A's demand for an "untranslated" knowledge system, pointing out the metric was rigged by definition, and Side B's Turn 10 framing of "philosophical purity over lived consequence" regarding Artemisinin.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) established that English dominance functions as a structural gate rather than a mutual bridge: non-anglophone knowledge systems must pay heavy asymmetric costs and strip their conceptual frameworks to participate, while anglophone mediocrity circulates freely. PRO successfully turned CON's own examples—Artemisinin and the Toyota system—into evidence that only decontextualized fragments cross, proving epistemic erasure. CON's clipped closing and "rigged question" defense on untranslated influence failed to fully answer the core asymmetry.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing turn of CON's Artemisinin and Toyota examples, showing that only isolated compounds or techniques crossed while the originating knowledge frameworks remained invisible, directly vindicated epistemic erasure and shattered CON's "contribution not extraction" narrative.
