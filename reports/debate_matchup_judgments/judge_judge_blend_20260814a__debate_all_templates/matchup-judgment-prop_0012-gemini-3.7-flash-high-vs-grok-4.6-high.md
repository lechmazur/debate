### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): A opened with a forceful moral case (consent, colonial theft, the "infinite delay" critique), but under B's pressure it began softening the motion's literal claim—conceding that "promptly" only bars institutional stalling, that verification of provenance is fine, and that communities may authorize continued study before reburial. B seized on this directly, repeatedly pointing out that the motion demands return "even when continued study would be valuable" with no carve-out for consent-based interim research or contested/unidentified remains, and that A's answers effectively rewrote the resolution into something narrower and more defensible. B's finite-window/documentation-then-return proposal, its distinction between identified graves and unidentified ancient populations, and its pressure questions about contested provenance exposed real gaps A never fully closed. A's closing repeated the dignity/authority framing without directly confronting the goalpost-shift charge, while B's closing sharpened the textual clash cleanly. B's case stayed closer to the resolution as written and did more damage in the exchanges.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 (B2.1–B2.3), pointing out that A's "communities can authorize research on their own terms" concession contradicts the motion's plain demand for prompt return "even when continued study would be valuable," was the pivotal moment—A never fully recovered from having its own case shown to be narrower than the resolution it was defending.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO established a decisive ethical framework around consent and colonial sovereignty that CON could not overcome. While CON effectively highlighted the irreplaceable nature of scientific evidence, their "finite study window" compromise collapsed under questioning when they could not clearly assign ultimate authority to descendant communities rather than institutions. PRO’s rebuttals cleanly separated consensual community-led study from unilateral institutional retention, and their closing crystallized the clash as a choice between bodily sovereignty and academic paternalism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO’s pressure question exposing who controls the "finite study window" timeline, which revealed that CON’s compromise preserved institutional veto power and undermined their claim to community-centered stewardship.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by strictly adhering to the text of the motion and exposing Side B's attempts to soften it. Side A effectively demonstrated that the proposition's mandate to return remains "promptly even when continued scientific study would be valuable" overrides not just institutional research but also the consent of descendant communities who might want the research completed. Side A's framing of reburial as "enforced amnesia" and the destruction of a unique physical archive provided a compelling, concrete impact that outweighed Side B's more abstract appeals to consent, especially since Side B struggled to reconcile their consent framework with the motion's absolute requirement for promptness.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they dismantled Side B's answers to the pressure questions by showing that Side B was rewriting the motion to allow for verification and community-authorized study, whereas the actual text forbids retaining remains simply because study is valuable, regardless of community wishes.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO built a tighter, more responsive case: the "value never expires" problem was raised in the opening and never actually neutralized by CON. CON's proposed fix — a "finite research window" policed by "independent IRBs" and the institution acting as "objective evidentiary trustee" — was exposed in PRO's Rebuttal 2 and Closing as merely renaming the same custody arrangement without an independent stopping point, since the institution that took the remains still decides when the window closes and still adjudicates contested affiliation. PRO also defused CON's core irreversibility argument by noting consensual sampling can occur before return and extracted data can still be used, meaning the only irreversible loss under PRO's framework is unfinished burial, not scientific knowledge. CON never squarely answered this consent-based alternative, instead repeating that reburial "extinguishes" the record and that unverified affiliation risks "misallocation," without explaining why institutional custody is more legitimate than community standing. PRO's pressure questions (what independently ends the window; who has standing if science never certifies a "true descendant") landed and were answered only with more institutional mechanism, not a genuine external check, which PRO capitalized on cleanly in the final rebuttal and closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 ("A1/A2... New names, same custody") in response to CON's IRB/"evidentiary trustee" answers was decisive: it reframed CON's entire proposed compromise as functionally identical to open-ended retention, and CON's closing failed to rebut this reframe.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A won by exposing that CON's "finite period" of study lacks an independent endpoint and defaults to the holding institution as both judge and beneficiary. A showed that contested affiliation under CON's model becomes a pretext for indefinite retention, while the harm of unfinished funerals is absolute. A answered CON's pressure questions cleanly, crystallized the clash effectively in closing, and demonstrated that prompt return ends the colonial exception of treating Indigenous dead as a research archive first and persons second.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal (Turn 7), which directly answered B's pressure questions, identified CON's "evidentiary trustee" framework as a recreation of institutional custody, and established that contested affiliation must not default to the collector.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Gemini 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core mechanism for delay. Side B effectively demonstrates that Side A's "finite research windows" and "kinship verification" are simply bureaucratic tools that allow the holding institution to retain custody indefinitely, directly violating the "promptly" mandate of the motion. Side B's framing of the power dynamic—the collector deciding who counts as family—was rhetorically powerful and logically unassailable. Side A made strong points about the irreversible loss of ancient DNA, but failed to overcome the structural flaw in their model: it leaves the final decision on return in the hands of the researchers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, where they answer the pressure questions by exposing Side A's trap: treating scientific testing as the only way to decide standing leaves the holding institution as the permanent judge and owner, and pointing out that shared claimants can bury together rather than waiting for a lab to crown a single "true" descendant.
