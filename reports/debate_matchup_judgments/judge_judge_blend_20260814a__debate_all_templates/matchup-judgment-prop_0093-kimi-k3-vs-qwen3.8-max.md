### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO (B) built the more resilient case by reframing the central dispute as "access vs. storage" and then relentlessly exploiting that frame: an all-day parked car blocks the plumber/nurse just as much as a bus lane would, while turnover zones actually guarantee short-stay access that CON's "first-come" parking cannot. CON (A) never fully answered this collapse—it kept asserting that dedicated lanes are riskier under weak enforcement without rebutting why storage itself isn't already a worse, unenforced failure mode. The pressure-question exchange was decisive: B's Q1 forced A to specify a threshold, and A's answer ("most spaces" as default, but converting where "ridership or delay is proven") effectively conceded the motion's core structure (more, not all). B's closing explicitly seized on this concession, while A's closing merely reasserted the "safer default" line without repairing the damage. B's equity reversal (car storage as a subsidy to car owners at the expense of poorer transit riders) was also never squarely rebutted, only counter-asserted with an outer-district hardship claim that B had already partially preempted with accessible-bay guarantees. Both sides stayed within reasonable epistemic bounds, but B's rebuttals did more real work exposing contradictions and turning CON's own examples against it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's [B2.2]/[B4.1] move—"a car parked from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. gives the plumber no access at all... turnover is service; storage is not"—combined with the pressure-question sequence where A's answer conceded that high-demand corridors should convert, which B's closing then used to argue CON had effectively adopted PRO's framework.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): Side A won by turning B’s central access argument: long-stay parked cars do not guarantee door-side access, while accessible bays, loading zones, and pickup areas can reserve it for actual users. A also weighed transit reliability, safety, deliveries, and public-space efficiency more clearly. B raised legitimate concerns about distributed access and enforcement, but never showed why preserving most curb space as first-come car storage solves those concerns better.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s strongest exchange was the response that the plumber, nurse, parent, and disabled traveler are short-stay access users, whereas an all-day parked car blocks them just as completely as another curb use. B’s reply that managed bays may be insufficient was plausible, but it did not overcome A’s comparison that ordinary parking guarantees neither proximity nor vacancy.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON)): PRO won the access vs storage clash. By showing a car parked 8am-6pm blocks the plumber/nurse/disabled resident as much as any bike lane, and that short-stay bays are a stronger guarantee than a first-come lottery, A neutralized CON's core access argument.
