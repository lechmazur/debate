- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (PRO)): Both sides argued with real sophistication and memorable framing, but CON (A) built the more durable path. Most decisively, when PRO offered levies and earmarked taxes as an alternative funding mechanism in Rebuttal 2, A countered that dedicated park levies also fail in downturns and still lock restrooms — a direct hit on PRO's central fallback — and PRO's closing never answered it, instead reasserting the "keep the center free" slogan. A's asymmetry argument (visible, contestable enclosure vs. invisible, total neglect) also went less rebutted than PRO's moral framing. The result is a close, high-quality debate where A's comparative case ultimately survived pressure better.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 point that dedicated park levies also fail and "lock restrooms" in downturns undercut PRO's proposed funding alternative, and PRO's closing never directly answered it, leaving A's funding-mechanism comparison the least damaged line in the debate.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Both sides argued competently, but B built a cleaner, more durable framework: a hard "floor" of free entry paired with flexible non-gating tools (parking fees, concessions, event permits), versus A's vaguer "necessity" ceiling. B's pressure question forced A to admit that peak use of the very signature assets the motion is about (major beaches/waterfronts) could be priced with "no permanent claim to stay unpriced," which let B's closing credibly claim A had conceded the motion's structure while still defending exactly the gate-pricing the motion opposes. A's counter that free rationing tools (timed entry, lotteries) also exclude the nurse by schedule was a fair point and kept the debate live, but A never rebutted B's core structural claim that price exclusion is systematically tied to wealth while rule-based rationing can be engineered for equity. B's tighter distinction between "floor" and "tools" made the debate easier to decide and better resolved the central clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Pressure Questions (Q2) forcing A to name any space that must always remain free, and A's answer conceding that only non-scarce/off-peak spaces qualify while signature peak assets could be priced — which B then used in closing to argue A had effectively conceded the motion while still defending gate-pricing of the exact spaces at issue.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): PRO won the definition fight: broadly free as a floor on entry, not a freeze on management. CON's opening tools — peak parking, reservable picnics, temporary concert fences — were compatible with that floor, leaving CON to defend a true entry gate. PRO showed the gate fails CON's own nurse test, while equity-engineered rules and non-gating revenues (concessions, permits, parking) answer scarcity without daily exclusion. CON's shift to waivers/free hours/off-peak free in R2 and closing sounded like PRO's model, and its ceiling — charge where load ruins — conceded most space remains free.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R1 reframe that peak parking, reserved picnics and concert fences already live inside a broadly free commons, so CON had offered no defense of the motion's actual target: the ticketed lawn / members-only beach as growing default.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Grok 4.6 (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully narrowing the clash to the entry fee itself, demonstrating that Side B's funding and capacity arguments could be solved by non-gating tools like concessions, parking, and capacity rules. Side A effectively trapped Side B into relying on waivers and free hours in the later rounds, which Side A correctly identified as a concession to the proposition's framework of a broadly free default with narrow exceptions. Side B's "enclosure by ruin" argument was strong, but Side A's distinction between rationing by rule versus rationing by wallet was more persuasive and better aligned with the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1, which exposed that Side B's opening merely defended non-gating tools (parking, concessions) that Side A already conceded, forcing the debate onto the narrower, more damaging clash over the entry fee itself.
