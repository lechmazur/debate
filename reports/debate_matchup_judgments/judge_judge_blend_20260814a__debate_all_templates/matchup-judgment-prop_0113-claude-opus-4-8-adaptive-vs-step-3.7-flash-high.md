### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by brilliantly isolating the exact mechanism of the motion (shifting the payment of the invoice) from the regulatory framework (targets, fines, reporting) that Side B relied upon.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 decisively capitalized on Side B's reliance on "escalating penalties" to answer the landfill question, pointing out that fines and targets are regulatory tools available without shifting the fundamental funding model to a privatized system.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A wins by crystallizing the live delta: all benefits PRO cites (targets, infrastructure, redesign) flow from regulation available without the cost shift; the motion uniquely adds a regressive till surcharge plus weaker democratic control. A maintained pressure across rebuttals and answers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A2/A4 repeatedly forcing PRO to defend invoice-shift itself rather than regulation, exposing rebate/regression concession and non-unique infrastructure claims.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 4.8 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won by isolating the motion's unique "delta." PRO showed EPR can work, but CON demonstrated that every benefit PRO cited—binding targets, infrastructure investment, public reporting, redesign—flowed from regulation and redistribution, both available without shifting the invoice to producers. By proving the motion uniquely added only a regressive flat checkout surcharge and weakened democratic accountability (replacing a vote on public councils with reporting from producer-run consortia), CON undercut PRO's fairness narrative. PRO never rebutted that the same regulation could drive outcomes under publicly run systems, and repeatedly relied on regulatory mechanisms while asserting they required producer payment. CON's sharp concession-catching on rebates, consistent separation of funding from regulation, and clearer weighing in closing gave it a live route to victory that PRO could not neutralize.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 and 2 sequence isolating the "delta": demonstrating that PRO's benefits (targets, infrastructure, penalties) were regulatory common ground, leaving the motion's unique contribution as a regressive till surcharge plus privatized control—sharpest in [A2.2]–[A2.8] and crystallized in [A4.5]–[A4.7].
