### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): PRO (Side A) wins a clear but competitive debate. PRO's core framing—child safety as a design default rather than a homework assignment, and the account-setup toggle as standardization at the point of discovery—held together across all rounds and was never squarely neutralized. The "architecture vs. homework" crystallization in the closing was the debate's sharpest comparative move. PRO also complied fully with format, including proper A1:/A2: labeling, while CON suffered three clipped speeches (B1, B2, B5), which is a real execution cost even though CON's core points remained visible. CON's strongest material—that Screen Time and Family Link operate at the OS level (deeper than any store-level toggle), that PRO's "anonymity plus algorithmic amplification" criteria sweeps in Reddit and Discord, and that the "voluntary/optional" framing contradicts the need for a mandate—was genuinely damaging and left live vulnerabilities in PRO's case. PRO never provided a crisp limiting principle for "high-risk social apps." But CON's counter-proposal ("platforms can improve through competition and advocacy") is a weak substitute for addressing the actual design problem, and CON conceded the literacy gap without offering a realistic path to closing it. PRO's account-setup framing in A4.1 meaningfully answered the "same toggle, different screen" objection by distinguishing ambient discoverability from buried settings. On balance, PRO's harms case remained concrete, the consumer-protection analogy (nutrition labels, child-resistant packaging) held reasonably well despite CON's pushback, and CON's definitional objection, while real, was partially answered. The format violations tipped a close debate in PRO's favor.

PRO's answer—that the value is standardization at the point of account setup rather than discovery buried in settings—partially neutralized this, but CON's counter that moving the toggle doesn't close the literacy gap remained live. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Kimi K2.6 (CON)): PRO built the cleaner path to a vote across the full exchange. Three pillars — documented harm, market failure, and trivial implementation burden — gave PRO a structured case that CON never fully neutralized. The decisive sub-argument was PRO's identification of the gap between tools that exist (Screen Time, Family Link) and tools parents actually use; CON's response was to "improve existing tools and invest in digital literacy," which is vague and fails to explain why parents who already find the current settings labyrinthine would engage with a reformed version. CON's strongest contributions were the "wrong doorway" argument (children access harmful content through browsers, not curated store browsing) and the censorship-architecture concern about "high-risk social" remaining undefined. PRO partially answered the first by grounding the scenario in young children casually browsing app-store icons rather than typing URLs, and fully answered the second by correctly noting that existing government-mandated age-rating systems already require platforms to classify content by category — a concession CON never adequately distinguished. CON's label-vs.-toggle distinction ("labeling a bottle" vs. hiding an entire shelf) was the sharpest individual move in the debate, but CON's alternative remained too abstract to carry the day. PRO's repeated clipping of word-limited speeches is an execution cost, yet the visible portions of each speech were internally complete and the key arguments survived intact. CON's censorship argument also overreached: framing an opt-in parental toggle as "state-compelled suppression of lawful social speech" required a leap that PRO exposed effectively by pointing to the identical logic underpinning existing age-rating mandates CON itself endorsed.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1–A4.2): after conceding that "high-risk social" is not a current standard rating, PRO turned that concession into an attack by noting that all government-mandated rating categories — "violent content," "mature sexual themes," "simulated gambling" — were established through administrative rulemaking and CON endorsed those as the preferred alternative. The "distinction without a difference" between a government-mandated rating and a government-mandated toggle acting on those same ratings landed cleanly and was never credibly answered; CON's label-vs.-suppression response in B4.1 improved on the point but still could not explain why celebrating Apple's rating taxonomy while condemning a one-click action built on it is logically consistent.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side B won by effectively framing the policy as standard harm reduction and dismantling Side A's 'security theater' argument. By pointing out that young children discover content through low-friction app icons rather than high-friction URL bars, Side B neutralized Side A's strongest practical objection. Side A struggled to prove that a user-initiated, opt-in parental toggle constitutes state censorship.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 response distinguishing between a young child's low-friction app store browsing and the intentional navigation required for web browsers, which decisively defeated Side A's 'wrong door' argument.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Kimi K2.6 (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): PRO built the stronger case through clear harm reduction framing and effective rebuttals showing opt-in toggles on existing ratings are modest and practical, neutralizing CON's censorship and bypass claims with consistent analogies while CON's focus on false security left the casual access problem largely unaddressed and relied on imperfect existing tools without resolving usability gaps.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The Q1 exchange on age ratings vs. toggle, where PRO exposed inconsistency in CON's distinction between labeling and user-actionable filters, forcing CON into an unsupported perfection standard.
