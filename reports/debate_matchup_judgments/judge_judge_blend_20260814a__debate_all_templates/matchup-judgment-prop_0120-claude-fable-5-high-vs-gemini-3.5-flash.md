### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by making the debate about instrument choice rather than whether great fortunes should be taxed at all. Side B had a coherent fairness and democracy case, especially on timing, but A repeatedly showed that B's proposed fixes either collapsed into realization-with-interest, became income-tax style mark-to-market for liquid assets, or depended on annual valuation and government-held private equity that B never made fully credible. B's closing also overstated A as offering interest-free deferral despite A's interest-charge answer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was turning B's deferral and in-kind equity answers against the wealth tax: deferral became A's realization-based alternative, while in-kind equity worsened the valuation and administration problem. - **Grok 4.3** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by repeatedly showing that A's "fixes" (deferral with interest, in-kind equity, mark-to-market on liquid assets) are functionally identical to B's realization-based reforms while adding only the administratively broken annual valuation step that Europe abandoned. B's rebuttals crystallize this concession cleanly and keep the clash narrow on verifiable pricing vs. guessed valuations. A maintains a strong fairness/time argument but never neutralizes the core practicality burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Rebuttal 1 (B2) exposing that A's liquidity solutions install B's own realization-with-interest mechanism.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON wins by systematically dismantling PRO's mechanism. PRO's proposed fixes for liquidity and valuation (deferral with interest, in-kind equity settlement) were successfully turned into concessions that either collapsed into CON's alternative package or created unworkable administrative burdens. CON's rebuttals sharply exposed the valuation problem, particularly highlighting how PRO's reliance on internal company valuations amounts to an easily gamed honor system. While PRO mounted a strong normative case around democratic fairness and the danger of waiting, their closing ignored CON's direct answers regarding interest charges and immediate taxation of portfolio loans, leaving the "interest-free deferral" claim neutralized. CON crystallized the debate around a decisive, substantive question—who sets the price?—and demonstrated that their alternative captures the same goals using verifiable market events rather than speculative annual appraisals. CON's tighter logic, superior handling of pressure, and more damaging rebuttals secure a clear win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which exposed how PRO's liquidity fixes (deferral-with-interest and internal valuations) either functionally adopted CON's realization model or created an easily gamed honor system, collapsing PRO's distinctive mechanism and leaving annual valuation as the only unworkable remainder.
