B conceded this failure was real and visible only in non-headline statistics, effectively proving A's point that GDP should not be primary. A also neutralized B's historical evidence by showing it arose from eras when GDP and well-being moved together, making it irrelevant to modern rich-country divergence. A cleanly answered B's subjectivity and gaming objections by exposing GDP's own revision lags, imputation problems, and documented record of political manipulation, while turning well-being gaming into a desirable incentive. B's rebuttals were repetitive, failed to engage A's sharpened means-versus-ends framing, and never produced a headline metric that would signal well-being collapse.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's sustained 'flash red' challenge and gaming rebuttal in Rebuttal 2, where A showed that manipulating national life satisfaction would require actually improving citizens' lives, whereas GDP already suffers from documented data inflation, provincial accounting revisions, and off-booking games.
