PRO’s case remained strong on equity and coordination, but CON’s pragmatic weighing and tighter causal grounding provided the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2, which directly answered PRO’s pressure questions by citing PRO’s own text on compression, dismantled the hidden-waste-as-blank-check assumption, and used concrete coverage examples to show why efficiency gains cannot fund a blanket paid-hours reduction. ### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A won by relentlessly pressing the practical mechanics of a 25% uniform cost shock on time-bound sectors. Side A successfully differentiated this specific policy from historical labor laws by emphasizing the uniquely steep financial incentive for employers to evade the mandate by converting full-time jobs to part-time. Side B had a very strong moral and historical framework, but failed to adequately overcome Side A's argument that the magnitude of the cost cliff would overwhelm existing enforcement tools and actively harm the vulnerable workers Side B aimed to protect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2 in Turn 8 directly neutralized Side B's historical analogy by pointing out that a 20% jump in unit labor cost creates a uniquely steep financial cliff that far exceeds the incremental pressure of normal minimum wage laws.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON wins because it maintained a focused, decisive clash: a legally mandated four-day week with no pay cut imposes a uniform cost shock on a non-uniform economy. CON showed that for millions of time-bound workers—cashiers, aides, cleaners, drivers—output cannot be compressed, so employers will rationally evade by splitting full-time roles into part-time slots, automating, or contracting out. PRO never squarely answered where the offsetting productivity gain comes from for these workers, and its fallback to phase-ins, sector supports, and stronger enforcement either conceded the rule was unworkable as written or relied on enforcement tools that CON showed already fail under weaker incentives. PRO’s elegant framing about productivity gains and social bargains never overcame this concrete, self-defeating dynamic for the very low-paid workers it claimed to help.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s second rebuttal and closing crystallized that PRO’s hard cases—retail, care, logistics—are not marginal exceptions but employ a large share of the full-time workforce, and that PRO’s proposed carve-outs and phase-ins collapse the promise of covering most employees, leaving only already-privileged desk workers. This turned PRO’s own framing against him and made the evasion mechanism the live route to victory.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): Side B wins by effectively anchoring the debate on the non-uniform nature of the economy and the steep incentive for employers to convert full-time roles to part-time, particularly in time-bound sectors. While Side A offered strong normative framing about updating the social bargain and externalized costs like burnout, Side B’s arithmetic on unit labor costs and the specific mechanism of evasion directly challenged PRO’s claim that the policy protects low-paid workers. Side B also successfully turned PRO’s concessions on phase-ins and enforcement into admissions that the uniform mandate as written is unworkable for a large share of the workforce. Side A’s responses relied heavily on normative appeals and faith in strengthened enforcement, which Side B credibly undermined by pointing out the dramatically steeper financial incentives for misclassification. Side B made the decisive clash clearer and provided a more grounded reason to reject the proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively dismantled PRO's enforcement and phase-in defenses by highlighting that the 20% cost shock creates a uniquely steep incentive for job-splitting that existing, already-struggling labor tools cannot contain, turning PRO's safeguards into concessions of the mandate's defectiveness for time-bound workers.
