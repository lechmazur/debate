- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (PRO)): PRO won by successfully narrowing the motion to the structure of the passenger charge and forcing an unavoidable distributional clash: if aviation demand must fall, whose flight disappears? PRO also neutralized CON's fuel-tax alternative by showing it can coexist with the levy, making CON's decarbonization arguments a change of subject rather than a rebuttal to the motion as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Pressure Question Q2 asking whose flight disappears first, and CON's damaging admission in A4.2 that the cleaner's holiday is the trip that vanishes under a flat tax, which PRO then weaponized in closing to prove CON rations mobility by wealth rather than by marginal value.

### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by locking the decisive clash on the tax base: atmosphere counts kerosene, not boardings. PRO’s continuous-itinerary definition, rural exemptions, and especially the claim that staff rotation is a “policy victory” fatally decoupled the levy from emissions. PRO later bolted the escalator onto distance/cabin duties, leaving the motion adding mainly a leaky person-registry. Equity-and-escalator rhetoric stayed lively but never neutralized that mechanism failure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4 on B4.2: treating roster rotation as victory while the seat and kerosene remain unchanged.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing that the proposed levy taxes the wrong metric—boardings instead of carbon—while landing fatal, unanswered evasion problems. CON demonstrated that two long-haul business trips register as "occasional" under the levy while numerous short regional hops climb the punitive bands, and that firms can simply rotate staff to dodge the escalator entirely, leaving kerosene burn unchanged. PRO's attempt to celebrate this rotation as a "policy victory" [B4.2] directly contradicted their closing claim that aggregate demand must fall [B5.5], a tension CON crystallized into a decisive reason to reject the motion. CON's alternative—per-tonne duties weighted by cabin and distance—tracked actual pollution from flight one, needed no national surveillance registry, and avoided the Brussels/foreign-carrier leakage that undermined the boarding-count model.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's demolition of PRO's corporate-rotation evasion in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, showing that changing whose passport occupies the seat does nothing to reduce the kerosene burned, reducing PRO's climate policy to a bookkeeping reshuffle of passports and exposing a direct contradiction with PRO's aggregate-demand theory.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): CON won the comparison of tax bases. B showed counting boardings misfires: long-haul business returns count as lightly-taxed while many short hops climb bands, and firms can rotate passengers. PRO's answers conceded the point — defining flight as itinerary [A2.2], exempting island routes, admitting the levy is a multiplier on duties already scaled by distance/cabin [A4.1], and calling rotation a "policy victory" [A4.2] even though the kerosene still burns. PRO's static-price dilemma was answered by B's revenue-recycling and per-tonne cabin weighting. B's clipping cost is noted, but its core clash — tax kerosene not boarding passes — survived.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B2/B4 exposure of rotation dodge — PRO admits changing passport is a victory while plane and emissions stay identical, conceding levy doesn't track pollution.
