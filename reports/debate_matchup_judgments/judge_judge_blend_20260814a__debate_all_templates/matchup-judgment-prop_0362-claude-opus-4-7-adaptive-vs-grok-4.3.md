### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by making the debate turn on the counterfactual flat price: if theaters cannot vary prices, access may not become stable and cheap, but instead uniformly higher with fewer low-entry opportunities. Side A had a plausible schedule-flexibility and streaming-friction case, but its answer to the flat-price burden was asserted rather than demonstrated. B's framing that mass habit is built by a low price floor was cleaner and better weighed, though somewhat overconfident about where the flat price would land.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange was pressing that A compared dynamic pricing to an imaginary low flat price. A answered that theaters would choose a moderate volume-maximizing price, but B effectively replied that fixed costs make that aspiration insufficient, preserving B's central access-floor argument.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully cornering Side A on the counterfactual flat price, mathematically demonstrating that a flat price would average upward to cover fixed costs and thereby price out the very families Side A aimed to protect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal and subsequent speeches ruthlessly pressing the "arithmetic" of flat pricing effectively neutralized Side A's core premise of consistent affordability.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Side A (CON) wins by decisively controlling the core economic clash. CON consistently demonstrated that PRO's case relies on an indefensible counterfactual: that mandating flat pricing would result in a low, accessible rate rather than a weighted average that lands at or above current peak prices. PRO's Rebuttal 2 defense (theaters would maximize volume and avoid averaging higher) failed to address fixed costs and was effectively dismantled by CON's arithmetic framing. CON also provided a cleaner mechanism for preserving mass habit (built at the floor, not the ceiling) by showing how dynamic pricing creates genuine low-cost entry points that compete with streaming, whereas flat pricing eliminates them. PRO's cultural arguments were coherent but ultimately rested on an economically implausible baseline, leaving CON with the stronger, more realistic path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 dismantling PRO's flat-pricing counterfactual. By exposing that PRO's model assumes theaters would willingly price below cost-recovery and ignoring the arithmetic of fixed costs across half-empty weekdays, CON collapsed PRO's core mechanism and crystallized the debate around the price floor versus averaged ceiling comparison.
