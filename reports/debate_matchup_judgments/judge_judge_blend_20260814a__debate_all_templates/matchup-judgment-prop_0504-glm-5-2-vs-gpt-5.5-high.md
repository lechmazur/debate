### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO's structural argument held up across the exchange: platform-specific checks create the identity-plus-context sensitive bundle by design, while a wallet is architecturally built to prevent it. The debate's most decisive moment was PRO's asymmetry point in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3)—CON demands near-perfect guarantees from wallets while accepting ordinary promises from thousands of platforms, even though both systems ultimately depend on law, audits, and technical rules. CON never cleanly neutralized that asymmetry; it distinguished failure modes by scale rather than showing wallets face a qualitatively higher standard. CON's blast radius framing was memorable and structurally sound, but PRO's closing reframe was effective: blast radius is not only the imagined catastrophic wallet failure but also the cumulative routine harm—identity tied to sensitive context scattered across thousands of platforms with uneven security. CON's best counter was the A1 answer (payment-card signals collect no identity at all, so wallets can concentrate at issuance what platform alternatives avoid entirely), which was genuinely clever but too narrow to override the structural case. CON's whiteboard-vs-reality critique was sustained but never quantified what would realistically go wrong in a multi-provider certified system versus what routinely goes wrong now. PRO also handled exclusion better: a common default can build accessibility routes once, whereas platform-specific checks force vulnerable users through different gauntlets everywhere. PRO's three clipped speeches are a mild execution cost but did not prevent core arguments from landing. On balance, PRO built the cleaner path to the motion's core question: which architecture better prevents overcollection by design rather than by promise.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's asymmetry argument in B4.3: CON demands near-perfect guarantees from wallets while accepting ordinary regulatory promises from thousands of platforms, even though identical tools—law, audits, technical standards—govern both. CON's response (blast radius distinguishes them) was coherent but applied a different evidentiary bar to the two systems without justifying why the catastrophic-failure lens applies to wallets while the routine-operation lens applies to platform checks.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A built the stronger case by sharpening the decisive clash on blast radius and failure asymmetry across rebuttals, directly answering pressure questions, and crystallizing why local vs systemic risk favors platform checks under realistic deployment.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 answers to Q1/Q2 on data volume and default pressure, then contrasting blast radii.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by exposing the gap between PRO's idealized cryptographic design and the political reality of EU infrastructure. While PRO effectively showed that platform-specific checks create repeated identity-plus-context exposure, CON maintained that a wallet default would centralize age verification into a high-value target vulnerable to government coercion and catastrophic breach. CON's blast radius framing, contrasting correctable local failures with systemic collapse, proved more durable, especially after PRO conceded that no system can guarantee against future bad laws. CON also capitalized on PRO's admission that default means a presumptive safe harbor, demonstrating regulatory flattening. Combined with PRO's repeated word-limit violations leaving speeches clipped, CON built the cleaner case for caution over lock-in.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2, where it answered PRO's pressure questions by showing issuers still hold permanent records, turned PRO's own safe-harbor definition of default into evidence of regulatory flattening, and crystallized the blast-radius asymmetry between local platform failures and systemic wallet compromise.
