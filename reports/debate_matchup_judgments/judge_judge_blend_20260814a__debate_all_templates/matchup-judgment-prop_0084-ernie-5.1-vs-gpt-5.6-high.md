- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent cases, but A's throughline—that verification data is another attack surface exploitable via breaches, subpoenas, and insider access, and is especially dangerous for survivors, LGBTQ+ users in hostile jurisdictions, and undocumented people—was never fully neutralized. B's mitigation (intermediary tokens, deletion, audits) was a clever technical proposal, but A's Rebuttal 2 landed a concrete counter (data persists in logs, backups, and processing pipelines regardless of stated policy), and B never got to answer this because its Closing was clipped before addressing the point, an explicit execution cost. A also sharpened the debate by conceding basic screening already exists and isolating the true motion question (the marginal escalation to "stronger" verification), shifting the burden onto B in a way B's closing did not squarely meet. B's fraud-cost-escalation argument remained reasonably strong and was not fully dismantled, keeping this competitive, but A's case was more consistently reinforced across rounds and ended with the cleaner, uncontested framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 point that even with intermediary/token verification, identifying data still exists transiently in logs, backups, and third-party caches — a practical rebuttal to B's "privacy-preserving" model that B's clipped Closing never fully answered.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner comparative path: privacy-preserving private accountability (intermediaries, tokens, deletion, public pseudonymity) raises retry costs and makes bans matter without requiring a permanent public ID vault, while CON's residual risks are governable failures rather than the intended design of endless identity cycling. CON's vulnerable-users and platform-mistrust lines stayed live and forceful but were repeatedly met by design distinctions CON never fully neutralized, leaving PRO's structural advantage intact after full exchange. Closing clip cost PRO polish but not the core clash resolution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 + false-choice comparison: no-retention tokens and separable lighter checks answer subpoena/audit pressure while showing CON supplies no substitute for stopping banned-scammer re-entry.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO successfully distinguished privacy-preserving verification (tokens, intermediaries, deletion) from the permanent identity vault CON attacked, keeping the structural problem of consequence-free impersonation live. CON's vulnerable-population argument remained potent but was partially neutralized by PRO's alternative routes and by the counter that weak verification also endangers survivors and LGBTQ+ users from repeat abusers. PRO's comparative framing—governable violation versus designed flaw—provided the cleaner route to victory, though PRO's clipped closing and idealized implementation model kept the margin competitive.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 separation of verification from storage using non-identifying tokens and technically separated verifiers, which blunted CON's breach and subpoena arguments and established a privacy-preserving middle ground between permanent identity vaults and effortless impersonation.
