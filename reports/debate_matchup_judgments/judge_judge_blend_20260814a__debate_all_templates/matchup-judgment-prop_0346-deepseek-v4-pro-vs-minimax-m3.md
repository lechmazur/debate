### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A won because it kept the motion centered on the real contrast between evaluating value created and constantly monitoring activity or presence signals. Side B had a strong concern about invisible coordination and gaming, but increasingly relied on redefining almost any observation of collaboration, feedback, or team impact as presence data. That overread made the motion impossible to evaluate and let Side A argue that outputs can include mentoring, incident resolution, customer outcomes, and peer-assessed contribution without collapsing into surveillance. Side A also answered the pressure questions directly with role-specific output metrics. Side B was competitive and rhetorically sharp, but its case attacked a cruder outputs-only system more than the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's most decisive rebuttal was that 360 feedback, team outcomes, and incident results are not merely login timestamps in disguise; they evaluate downstream value. This undercut Side B's central claim that PRO was smuggling presence back in and exposed CON's overbroad definition of presence.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully demonstrating the feasibility of output-oriented metrics across various roles, including real-time support. Side A's case ultimately relied on a definitional stretch, claiming that any peer-evaluated outcome (like 360 reviews) is actually 'presence data'. Side B effectively dismantled this by pointing out that equating human observation of outcomes with 'constant monitoring of presence signals' erases the fundamental distinction of the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2, which cleanly dispatched Side A's pressure question about on-call engineers by providing concrete, undeniably outcome-based KPIs (MTTR, CSAT, SLA) that measure resolution rather than mere responsiveness.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): PRO successfully defended that evaluating mainly on outputs aligns incentives with value creation and avoids performative busyness, while establishing that outcome metrics are meaningfully distinct from constant surveillance. CON’s critique that output metrics secretly rely on presence data was sharp but overreached by collapsing the distinction entirely; PRO’s reductio—that CON’s logic would make the motion meaningless—exposed that excess. However, PRO incurred real execution costs from repeatedly exceeding word limits, preventing a larger margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 1 (A2.2) reframing 360 feedback and team outcomes as "presence data with a different label," which forced PRO to spend the rest of the debate defending whether their output metrics were genuinely distinct from behavioral observation.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON)): A won a clear but competitive debate. B effectively attacked crude output metrics and made the strongest case that collaborative work needs real-time process awareness, but too often argued against output-only evaluation and defended humane activity awareness rather than the motion's constant monitoring of presence signals. A kept the comparison closer to the proposition, showing that outputs are inspectable while presence signals are gameable, proxy-like, and require surveillance escalation to verify. A's framing that managers should use judgment about work rather than launder judgment through status dots was the cleaner voting path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal most decisively answered B's gaming pressure by arguing that bad outputs can be reviewed, tested, and corrected, while fake presence often looks identical to real engagement unless it becomes invasive monitoring.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B won by cleanly dismantling Side A's defense of presence signals. Side B successfully argued that evaluating the "substantive engagement" Side A champions is actually evaluating the content (output) of work, whereas actual presence metrics fail in the dark and require invasive surveillance to verify.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing brilliantly contrasted the failure modes of both systems: output gaming fails in the open and is correctable, while presence gaming fails in the dark and corrupts the evaluation system itself.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B (PRO) won by establishing that output-based evaluation, while imperfect, fails in inspectable ways that allow for correction, whereas presence signals fail invisibly and collapse into either theatre or surveillance. B successfully framed A's case as demanding that presence signals do three incompatible jobs—capture connective value, resist gaming, and substitute for judgment—and showed they fail at all three. A's strongest points about real-time awareness and collaborative work were partially co-opted by B's argument that good outputs can include preventative and connective contributions, and A's concrete examples of valuable presence either reduced to observable outputs or admitted the need for surveillance that A claimed to oppose. B's rebuttals were sharper, the pressure questions more damaging, and the closing crystallization of "fails in the open" versus "fails in the dark" gave the cleaner comparative frame.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2 chain exposing that presence signals either cannot verify contribution without becoming surveillance (Q1/Q2) or are themselves unverifiable theatre, paired with the "inspectability" frame that output gaming is visible and correctable while presence gaming is invisible.
