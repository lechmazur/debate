- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): PRO built a rhetorically compelling "double standard" frame (conventional subsidies never had to prove net benefit, so regenerative shouldn't be held to a stricter bar), but never resolved the core logical tension CON repeatedly exposed: PRO simultaneously promised guaranteed "same-scale" spending and rigorous outcome-verification, without explaining what happens if verified opportunities fall short of that budget. CON named this contradiction in [B2.2] and again sharply in [B4.4], and PRO's answers in Rebuttal 2 (shifting funds to "extension support") effectively concede that true parity on regenerative outcomes cannot be guaranteed, undermining the very burden the motion sets. CON also kept firm pressure on the motion's explicit caveat ("even before long-term yield data is conclusive") by distinguishing income-replacement from food-replacement — transition payments smooth revenue but cannot manufacture missing grain — a point PRO answered only with selected farm-network anecdotes, which CON correctly noted cannot establish a national counterfactual given the motion's own admission of inconclusive data. Procedurally, PRO's Opening and Rebuttal 2 were both clipped for exceeding word limits, an explicit execution cost per the judging frame, while CON stayed within bounds and closed with a clean, decision-relevant frame ("promise merits support; proof determines scale"). CON's answers to pressure questions, while lacking a precise redirection number, were coherent and consistent with its position (staged, evidence-responsive expansion), whereas PRO's answers to CON's pointed questions about verified outcomes vs. guaranteed parity were evasive and left the contradiction standing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's repeated exposure of the tension between "guaranteed same-scale spending" and "rigorous verified-outcome conditions" ([B2.2] and [B4.4]), which PRO never cleanly resolved, was the most decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): B won by keeping the decisive clash on the proposition's core ("same scale" even before conclusive yield data) rather than allowing A to slide into mere reform of conventional subsidies or pilots. A's double-standard and urgency points were forceful and live, but never fully closed the justification gap for immediate parity over evidence-responsive scale; clippings further reduced execution. B answered pressures more cleanly and made the choice easier for a third-party voter.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.4–B4.5: parity-plus-rigorous-verification is self-undermining—if verified opportunities fall short of the precommitted total, standards must loosen, money must be spent on something else, or parity fails—locking in CON's adaptive sequencing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A wins by decisively dismantling the core mechanism of the proposition.
