### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B won by showing that understaffing, debt-loading, and neglect are conduct problems shared across ownership types, while an ownership cap is both evadable and poorly targeted. A never established that replacement capital would preserve capacity or that a cap would align investor incentives better than enforceable licensing and care standards.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's strongest exchange exposed the contradiction in A's enforcement case: if regulators can uncover beneficial ownership well enough to enforce a cap, they can directly enforce staffing and reinvestment conditions; if they cannot, layered structures also defeat the cap.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B won by decisively turning Side A's argument about corporate layers against them, showing that if ownership is easily hidden, a cap is unenforceable—and if it can be unmasked, conduct rules suffice. Side B also effectively defeated the replacement capital argument by noting alternative financing fails to scale even in markets where PE is absent.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's first rebuttal pointing out that if funds hide their true ownership to dodge penalties (as Side A claimed), an ownership cap enforced on paper is the easiest rule to evade.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): Side A won by consistently forcing the comparative: the motion's only distinctive tool is an ownership cap that is over-inclusive, under-inclusive, and easily gamed by the layered structures both sides acknowledge, while every remedy that actually targets understaffing or extraction is already available as conduct regulation for every owner. Side B established a plausible extraction story and market-failure frame, but never neutralized the capital-supply risk or showed why licensing strong enough to unmask owners could not simply impose staffing and reinvestment rules directly. A's later rounds narrowed cleanly to "beds lost now versus replacement that never appeared where margins were already thin," making the decision easier for a third-party reader.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (and parallel pressure answer) turning B's own corporate-layer admission against the enforceability of a cap while showing staffing remains the more observable, owner-neutral standard.
