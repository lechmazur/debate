### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that the proposed trigger risks both over-noticing and under-informing: a generic disclosure neither identifies whether automation was decisive nor supplies a contestable reason. Side A compellingly framed disclosure as a prerequisite to scrutiny, but its gatekeeper clarification effectively narrowed the broader “meaningfully influenced” standard and did not fully resolve ambiguous mixed human-software decisions or compliance costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s strongest answer was that category discovery can identify unnamed automated systems, while a bare notice still does not establish wrongdoing or distinguish decisive screening from incidental software use. This substantially weakened Side A’s claim that every meaningful challenge necessarily depends on prior disclosure.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side A won by effectively weaponizing the proposition's wording ("meaningfully influenced"). By showing that the mandate would result in boilerplate over-disclosure, A successfully neutralized B's core argument that disclosure gives workers actionable knowledge to file a lawsuit. B argued beautifully on the principle of transparency, but A won the pragmatic clash over what the specific mandate would actually achieve.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's second rebuttal and closing effectively dismantled B's central claim by arguing that a "label is not a theory"—proving that a generic boilerplate notice still leaves the worker guessing how the AI was used, thus failing to trigger the legal remedies B championed.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): CON wins the central clash: PRO admits line-drawing matters and retreats to a gatekeeper definition, conceding the proposition's "meaningfully influenced" is vague and overbroad. CON then shows a bare notice is compatible with both true screening and routine tooling, so it doesn't create the actionable theory PRO claims is a precondition, while category discovery plus retained records and targeted audits can. That leaves PRO with a symbolic label that produces noise, not knowledge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal-2 A1/A2 that category discovery solves unknown-tool problem and that a boilerplate "meaningfully influenced" notice is a label not a theory, directly neutralizing PRO's precondition argument.
