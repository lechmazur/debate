### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by effectively establishing that physical street design works independently of driver rationality and ongoing budget, whereas Side B's deterrence model requires both. Side A's argument that a speed bump forces even an impaired driver to slow down successfully dismantled Side B's main claim that infrastructure cannot address DUI or distracted driving.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's A2.2 and A2.3, which pointed out that deterrence requires rational calculation at the moment of risk, whereas physical engineering forces compliance whether a driver is drunk, enraged, or distracted.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): Side B built the stronger case by showing that design removes the need for rational calculation and delivers durable protection without ongoing budgets; Side A failed to neutralize the irrational-driver problem or the coverage limitation of penalties alone. B's pressure questions and closing sharpened the decisive durability/comprehensiveness comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 1 and answers to pressure questions (B4.1-B4.3) directly exposed A's deterrence reliance on rational actors and flipped Amsterdam to support design as decisive.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = MiniMax-M2.7 (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by exposing a scope gap that Side B (PRO) never closed. A showed that street redesign and lower limits address only speed, whereas the majority of fatal urban crashes involve DUI, distraction, and red-light running—behaviors that engineering does not stop. PRO’s physics and durability arguments were coherent, but PRO failed to neutralize the comparative point that penalties remove high-risk drivers from the road and deter the full spectrum of lethal behavior. PRO’s reliance on survival calculus assumed crashes are inevitable, while A demonstrated that penalties can prevent them entirely. Additionally, two of PRO’s rebuttals were clipped for exceeding word limits, creating a real execution cost that left PRO’s final weighing slightly truncated while A’s closing cleanly crystallized the unresolved scope problem.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s pressure question asking whether Amsterdam’s safety record would hold if fines were removed, and PRO’s answer that design makes compliance the path of least resistance. While this was PRO’s sharpest moment, CON’s broader scope argument—that redesign only mitigates speed while penalties cover all crash causes—ultimately outweighed the exchange.
