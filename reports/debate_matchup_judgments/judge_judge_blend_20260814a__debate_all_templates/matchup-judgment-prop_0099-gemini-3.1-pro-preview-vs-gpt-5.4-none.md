- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) carried a narrow but real edge by consistently exploiting the motion's own word "regardless." That framing created a concrete decision rule: if hosting produces any meaningful leverage, operational protections, or exposure, the proposition's absolute claim fails. PRO's counter—that human rights form a non-negotiable floor—was a principled slogan but never explained why "regardless" is the right standard rather than a claim that still needs defending. CON also landed the unanswered "alternative" argument: exclusion offers moral theater but no mechanism to help workers on the ground. PRO never squared that circle. PRO's pressure Q2 ("name one structural reform from existing Saudi sports engagement") was the sharpest single move in the debate and CON's answer—reframing to "exposure is not invisibility" rather than naming a concrete reform—was weaker than the question deserved. But CON had already hedged: it never claimed the World Cup would transform the Saudi state, only that engagement preserves tools while exclusion surrenders them. That narrower claim survived the exchange intact. PRO's strongest structural point—the uncontested bid surrendered leverage upfront—was addressed adequately though not decisively: CON correctly noted that post-award operational leverage (sponsor, broadcaster, and reputational pressure) is real and distinct from pre-award concessions. Neither side escaped cleanly, but CON's comparative frame (engagement with imperfect tools vs. exclusion with none) remained the cleaner path to a verdict after the full exchange. The clipped rebuttal was a small execution cost for CON that did not change the outcome.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Q2 ("can you point to a single structural human rights reform this existing sports scrutiny has actually achieved?") was the sharpest moment, forcing CON to concede that Saudi Arabia has not reformed under existing sports engagement. CON's pivot to "exposure is not invisibility" was a fair reframe but visibly weaker than the question demanded. However, CON's pre-existing engagement-vs.-exclusion comparison—which PRO never answered on the "what happens to workers if the bid is refused" dimension—ultimately absorbed the blow and preserved CON's live route to victory.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): CON won by exposing that PRO could not defend the proposition’s absolutist “regardless” clause. PRO delivered strong moral appeals on migrant labor and uncontested bids, but never established a principled, consistently applicable standard for when FIFA must refuse a host, and never justified ignoring all concrete benefits and leverage no matter their scale.   Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s response to PRO’s pressure questions and subsequent rebuttal demonstrating that operational leverage persists after an uncontested award (via sponsors, broadcasters, and delivery requirements) and that past sporting events in Saudi Arabia generated sustained visibility rather than invisibility, directly blunted PRO’s sportswashing narrative.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) won by maintaining the more decisive clash throughout: the World Cup is not a neutral logistics contract but a prestige and legitimacy asset, which is precisely why states compete ferociously for it. PRO's early framing that "investment is part of the mechanism, not a rebuttal" was never neutralized—CON kept responding with scrutiny benefits that, as PRO correctly noted, coexist with rather than cancel the legitimizing award. PRO's Q1 pressure question exposed the core contradiction in CON's "mere logistics" framing most effectively; CON's A1 response (Saudi Arabia seeks an "economic and cultural engine") tacitly conceded soft-power acquisition without acknowledging the implication. CON's best running argument—that Qatar 2022 scrutiny forced actual labor reforms—was partially double-edged: it confirmed scrutiny exists but did not rebut that the award was still the legitimizing act. CON's limiting principle (safety plus baseline monitoring) was correctly identified by PRO as emptying FIFA's human-rights commitments of substance. CON's "political purity" framing was rhetorically effective but was a genuine dodge; PRO's narrower test (whether the event's core political value lies in normalizing ongoing repression) held up better under pressure. PRO was penalized slightly for multiple clipped speeches—an execution cost across four of five turns—which kept CON competitive. But the surviving arguments were coherent and decisive enough that PRO still carries the core clash into the closing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's B2.4–B2.5 exchange in Rebuttal 1 ("scrutiny is temporary and fragmented; the legitimizing imagery is massive, coordinated, and remembered") directly answered CON's spotlight argument and reframed criticism as a side-effect of the reward rather than a cancellation of it. This point held through the rest of the debate and was never squarely answered; CON's repeated return to Qatar 2022 labor reforms showed scrutiny can happen but did not rebut the core claim that FIFA's endorsement is the primary asset being purchased.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): Side B successfully established that the World Cup is a prestige-conferring asset rather than a neutral logistical contract, and that awarding it to Saudi Arabia constitutes sportswashing because the event's political value substantially lies in normalizing an authoritarian regime. Side A's reliance on the "logistical responsibilities" framing collapsed under Side B's pressure question about why states fiercely compete for hosting rights, and their operational limiting principle (safety plus monitoring) effectively conceded that any well-run authoritarian state should be eligible. While Side A landed effective empirical blows with the Qatar 2022 scrutiny example, Side B convincingly argued that incidental criticism does not negate the core legitimizing function of the spectacle. Side B maintained the clearer route to victory by keeping the clash focused on what FIFA is actually rewarding, rather than allowing Side A to replace the motion with a question about engagement versus isolation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's rebuttal that scrutiny and sportswashing coexist—that incidental criticism riding alongside the event does not cancel the guaranteed legitimizing imagery and prestige—directly neutralized Side A's central "spotlight" defense and exposed that CON's operational standard would permit any regime to host so long as the tournament ran smoothly.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (CON); B = GPT-5.4 (no reasoning) (PRO)): PRO wins by decisively framing the World Cup as a prestige asset rather than a neutral logistical contract, directly answering the motion's "regardless of investment" clause by showing how infrastructure spending amplifies rather than offsets reputational laundering. PRO's rebuttal to the "spotlight effect" was particularly damaging: demonstrating that temporary scrutiny does not cancel the guaranteed legitimizing reward, and exposing CON's permissive limiting principle (safety + baseline monitoring) as functionally hollow. CON's reliance on Qatar 2022 and engagement theory was competent but never resolved the core tension between guaranteed prestige and conditional reform, leaving PRO's comparative weighing intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's dismantling of the "spotlight drives reform" argument, specifically the point that "criticism that rides along with the reward does not cancel the reward," paired with the university ethics prize analogy. This cleanly separated the act of conferring prestige from the incidental effects of scrutiny, neutralizing CON's central mechanism and forcing CON onto defensive, permissive ground.
