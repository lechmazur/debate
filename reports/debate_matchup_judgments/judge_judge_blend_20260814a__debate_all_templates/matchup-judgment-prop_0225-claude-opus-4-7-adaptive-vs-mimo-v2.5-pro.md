### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): B wins a clear but competitive debate. A made a plausible finite-resources/accountability case and answered some forecasting concerns by pointing to persistent shortages. But B more effectively controlled the decisive clash: the metric for “low-employment” remained contested, caps create immediate rationing and political-discretion risks, and A’s funding bridge from capped seats to actual shortage relief stayed weaker than A needed. B’s alternative of targeted bottleneck funding plus outcome transparency was not perfect on funding source, but it gave a cleaner reason to reject caps while preserving the goal of addressing shortages. A also suffered some execution cost from clipped later turns.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s most decisive exchange was pressing traceability: even if money is saved from capped programs, A had to show it reliably becomes nursing faculty, clinical placements, or preceptors. A’s fungibility answer helped somewhat, but B turned it into the claim that the cap’s harm is certain while the shortage benefit is speculative.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully breaking the link between Side A's proposed enrollment caps and the resolution's mandate to redirect funding to shortage fields. By forcing Side A to admit that budget savings are fungible and return to the general appropriation, Side B proved that the harms of the caps are guaranteed while the benefits are speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 weaponized Side A's admission that "money is fungible," decisively proving that a dollar saved from linguistics does not reliably become an ICU preceptor, thereby winning the core traceability clash.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully isolating the broken causal link in PRO's mechanism. When pressed on how capping a humanities seat actually funds a nursing preceptor, PRO admitted budget dollars are fungible and flow to general appropriations. Side B effectively weaponized this admission, framing the debate as a choice between a certain, immediate harm (rationed seats) and a speculative, untraceable benefit. Side B's alternative (targeted bottleneck funding plus demand-side transparency) adequately addressed the stated harms without the cap, and its closing crystallized the cost-benefit asymmetry decisively. Side A argued competently but could not overcome the traceability deficit or the structural reality of clinical bottlenecks.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 and Closing, which trapped PRO on budget fungibility and turned PRO's "money is fungible" defense into a fatal concession that severed the link between the enrollment cap and the claimed workforce benefit.
