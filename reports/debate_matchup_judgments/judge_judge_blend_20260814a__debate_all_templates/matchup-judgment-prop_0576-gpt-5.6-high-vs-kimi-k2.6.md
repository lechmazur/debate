### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but B ultimately controlled the decisive clash: the administrability of PRO's "primarily partisan vs. charitable" standard. When pressed for an objective threshold (B's Q1), A's answer (financial ratios, staff deployment, leadership energy) confirmed rather than refuted B's entanglement warning, and B explicitly and effectively exploited this in Rebuttal 2 ("a menu of discretionary considerations... PRO never supplies the line"). B also directly answered A's strongest structural point—that shared assets (staff, building, donor lists, "moral authority") let a firewall become "bookkeeping theater"—by distinguishing priceable tangible assets (reimbursable staff time, rentable buildings, chargeable mailing lists) from unpriceable intangibles like persuasive preaching, framing any attempt to tax the latter as taxing political speech itself. A never squarely dissolved that distinction, instead repeating the "shell" metaphor. A's proportionality/overinclusion vulnerability (innocent beneficiaries losing exemption for leadership's choices) was also never fully resolved beyond "self-inflicted," which B correctly noted doesn't address whether the penalty is proportionate. Both sides answered pressure questions properly with A1/A2 formatting, but B's answers landed more cleanly on the core administrability problem, giving CON the cleaner, more defensible path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 response to A's proposed "functional" threshold (B4.4), which turned A's own answer to the pressure question into proof of the vagueness B had warned against, was the most decisive exchange in the debate.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by cleanly dismantling Side A's two main arguments. B demonstrated that tangible subsidies (staff, buildings, lists) can be reimbursed via standard campaign finance firewalls, while intangible "moral authority" is not a government subsidy. Furthermore, B successfully distinguished religious entanglement from secular nonprofit regulation by pointing out that a sermon can be simultaneously spiritual and partisan, undermining A's "calculator and calendar" test.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal directly answered A's "bookkeeping theater" by distinguishing between reimbursable material assets (rent, staff time) and unsubsidized intangible assets (moral authority).
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K2.6 (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a more persuasive and memorable framework for the debate. A successfully frames the tax exemption as a public subsidy contingent on an organization's primary purpose, and devastatingly critiques Side B's proposed alternative (separate accounts and cost allocation) as "bookkeeping theater." While Side B raises strong, technically competent points about church-state entanglement and the difficulty of parsing the spiritual versus political nature of a sermon, A effectively neutralizes this by pointing to the functional, non-theological tests already applied to secular nonprofits. A's rhetoric is sharper, the progression across rounds is cleaner, and A makes the decisive clash—whether a second ledger can legitimize a fundamentally partisan institution—much easier for a third-party reader to resolve in the affirmative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's dismantling of Side B's "separate accounts" firewall, arguing that when an organization's primary purpose is partisan, isolating specific campaign expenditures fails to capture the holistic, un-invoiceable subsidies of institutional goodwill, infrastructure, and a captive congregation.
