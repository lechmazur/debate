### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): Side A won by effectively differentiating the unique, mobile, and unpredictable risks of apex predators from standard bounded environmental regulations. Side A also capitalized perfectly on Side B's answers to the pressure questions, framing B's reliance on necessity thresholds and scale-backs as a retreat from the motion itself.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's distinction between bounded property duties (like water rules) and releasing mobile predators, which systematically dismantled Side B's attempt to equate local objections to a generic veto over national environmental policy.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Grok 4.3 (PRO); B = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON)): B wins by exploiting A's narrowing concessions. A's second rebuttal and closing retreated to a narrow, technocratic exception—reintroduction only after alternatives fail, with compensation, monitoring, and scale-back—rather than defending the motion's general principle of overriding rural objection. B sharply flagged this as a rewrite of the proposition (B4.3, B5.2). B also kept alive a strong legitimacy/durability objection: apex predator policy requires daily rural cooperation, and imposed reintroduction breeds backlash, evasion, and collapsed trust. A's ecological case remained plausible but could not fully neutralize B's context-dependency challenge or solve the concentrated-burden problem, leaving B with the cleaner live route to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal (Turn 8, B4.3-B4.4), where B showed that A's answers to pressure questions transformed the motion from a general duty into a narrow last-resort exception, and noted that A's own "limit" required farmers to endure unmanageable losses before any scale-back.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GPT-5.5 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.3 (PRO)): CON wins by successfully framing the debate around practical durability, distributive risk, and the political reality of conservation. When PRO answered pressure questions by adding necessity thresholds, monitoring requirements, and scaling-back mechanisms, CON expertly turned those safeguards into a fatal concession: they effectively rewrote the motion's explicit mandate to proceed "even when" communities object. CON also complicated PRO's trophic cascade mechanism by highlighting underlying degradation drivers and demonstrating that predator reintroduction requires permanent conflict governance, not a "set-and-forget" natural fix. PRO's case was competent but relied on an idealized self-sustaining narrative that CON dismantled, and PRO never recovered from the charge that their model implicitly concedes the need for the very legitimacy the proposition overrides.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (A4) decisively turned PRO's attempt to answer pressure questions into a strategic vulnerability. By showing that PRO's added safeguards (necessity proof, monitoring, exclusion zones, scaling back) contradicted the motion's broad mandate and proved that imposed reintroduction is politically fragile, CON collapsed PRO's path to victory while sharpening the durability vs. imposition clash.
