### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built its case around a genuine logical dilemma—wide ranges preserve flexibility but do little to constrain discriminatory placement, while narrow ranges discipline offers but impose real matching friction—and pressed it consistently through pressure questions, rebuttals, and closing. PRO (B) never fully dissolved this tension; its answer that "flexibility and constraint are not opposites" and that ranges can be "credible and structured" without being rigid was asserted more than demonstrated, and CON's closing turned this answer into a concession ("the coexistence proves our point"). PRO's opening mechanism (information asymmetry, better sorting reducing failed negotiations) was solid, but its rebuttals mostly repeated "reduction not perfect prevention" without concretely rebutting CON's specific vectors (placement within band, raises, promotions, bonuses) that remain untouched by the mandate. PRO's Rebuttal 1 was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, cutting off its weighing argument mid-sentence—a real execution cost per the judging criteria. CON's closing crystallized the clash most cleanly with the "static posting versus dynamic matching" frame, giving the clearer decision rule.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 dilemma (wide range = weak equity effect; narrow range = real efficiency cost) was the pivotal move; PRO's Rebuttal 2 attempt to answer it ("reduction not perfect prevention," bands can be "credible" without rigidity) did not resolve the tension, and CON's closing exploited this by arguing PRO's own answer proved the dilemma rather than escaping it.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully dismantling Side A's core dilemma. B showed that even broad salary ranges provide a material equity benefit by establishing a floor and removing information asymmetry, while retaining enough flexibility to preserve matching efficiency. B also effectively reframed A's efficiency concerns as a defense of opportunistic bargaining.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized A's central dilemma by explaining that flexibility and constraint are not mutually exclusive, as even a broad range sets a visible floor and upper bound that limits the worst lowball offers.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON won by locking the debate on the wide/narrow dilemma and the proposition’s own “more than” test: ranges broad enough for dynamic tailoring leave ample room for bottom-of-band placement and leave later pay levers untouched, while ranges narrow enough to discipline pay impose recurring matching friction. PRO offered a coherent cumulative/compounding story and correctly reframed some secrecy as non-productive transfer, but never dissolved the tradeoff; its R1 clip and lighter weighing left the comparative claim under-delivered. CON’s answers to pressure stayed direct and converted flexibility concessions into equity dilution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s dilemma collapse in R1/R2 (flexibility preserves loopholes; discipline expands hiring friction) that forced PRO to defend coexistence without proving net pay-gap reduction.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON built a tighter, more analytically disciplined case around the broad-band/narrow-band dilemma: a mandate that governs only the advertised boundary does not guarantee equal placement within that boundary, and tightening bands enough to matter reintroduces real matching costs. CON's sharpest hit—that a posted range has two endpoints, not one baseline, so transparency can inform without equalizing outcomes, and that employer avoidance may reflect genuine uncertainty about fit rather than a plan to discriminate—directly undercut PRO's repeated equating of "lost flexibility" with "exploitative discretion." PRO's rebuttals and closing largely reasserted the secrecy-rent/exploitative-discretion framing rather than answering this circularity charge or supplying a metric for why systemic gains outweigh matching losses, which CON explicitly pressed for in Q2 and again in Rebuttal 2. PRO's Pressure Questions answers (A1/A2) were reasonable but did not neutralize the two-endpoint critique that followed. Both sides suffered clipped late speeches, but the substantive content of CON's core dilemma was delivered before the cut, while PRO's closing did not squarely resolve the unanswered rebuttal. This gives CON the cleaner, more sustained comparative path to the motion's burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 point that a range has two endpoints (so a poorly informed applicant may still anchor low while a well-connected one targets the ceiling) and that employer avoidance may reflect uncertainty about productive fit rather than discriminatory intent — this went substantively unanswered in PRO's closing.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A won by establishing a robust dilemma (broad bands fail to close the gap, narrow bands destroy productive matching) and successfully separating "productive discretion" (uncertainty about candidate level) from the "exploitative discretion" Side B relied upon.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, which cleanly dismantled Side B's visible baseline argument by pointing out that broad ranges have two endpoints, allowing uninformed candidates to still anchor to the floor while connected ones target the ceiling.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by forcing and holding the comparative burden of the motion. Its broad-versus-narrow dilemma and relative-gains critique stayed live: advertised bands need not equalize placement within-band, bonuses, or leveling, so gap reduction is narrow and substitutable, while matching frictions hit every covered vacancy. Side B (PRO) had a clean mechanism story (opacity, anchors, secrecy rent) and better closing crystallization, but repeatedly treated productive discovery as exploitative discretion and labeled avoidance “marginal” without a real metric. A’s clipped late turns cost polish, yet the decisive clash remained clearer on CON’s side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s relative-gains / broad-narrow replies (esp. A2–A4): a visible floor/ceiling need not shrink group disparities inside the band, and narrowing the band reintroduces false-negative matching costs PRO cannot dismiss as mere secrecy rent.
