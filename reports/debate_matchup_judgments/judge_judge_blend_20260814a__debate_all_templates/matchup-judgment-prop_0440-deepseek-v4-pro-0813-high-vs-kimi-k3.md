### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): A won the marginal-dollar comparison. It offered a plausible causal mechanism, concrete city examples, and a clear cost contrast, while B mainly established that focused deterrence needs some police capacity—not that expanding staffing produces greater violence reduction per dollar. B’s opportunity-cost critique was strong, but A directly counted specialist time and persuasively argued that a limited coordination burden remains cheaper and faster than recurring new-officer costs. A’s conflation of all targeted enforcement with focused deterrence and its erroneous closing references weakened an otherwise clear win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s first rebuttal most decisively flipped B’s complementarity argument: existing police can supply the enforcement backbone while the next dollar buys targeted coordination. B effectively replied that redeployment is not free, but never showed that those opportunity costs approach the cost of expanding staffing or erase A’s comparative advantage.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side A won by maintaining a sharper focus on the comparative metric of the prompt (cost-effectiveness of the marginal dollar). A successfully framed the debate as 'precision vs. presence,' co-opting B's defenses of targeted policing as proof that focus, rather than generalist headcount, is the active ingredient. B's argument that redeployment is zero-sum in strained departments was strong, but B failed to overcome A's math that backfilling specialist time remains vastly cheaper than hiring new generalist officers.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3) cleverly neutralized Side B's confounding variables by pointing out that 'gang-unit focus' and 'dedicated overtime' are themselves precision mechanisms, thereby forcing B to concede that targeting works better than raw headcount.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO won the central cost-effectiveness comparison. B established gun violence concentration -> precision leverage, priced the comparison as thin slice of specialist time vs recurring six-figure hires, and showed Boston/Oakland/Stockton declines on flat headcount with on-off decay tracking strategy, not staffing. A never priced expanding staffing, retreated to "false precision" and complementarity, and its own list of effective ingredients (gun corridors, overtime, gang-unit focus) conceded PRO's precision logic. B answered backfill costing directly while A left the key pressure unanswered.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's flip in Rebuttal 1 and 2 that CON's confounds (gang-unit focus, overtime, prosecution) are themselves precision mechanisms, plus the rounding-error costing of coordination vs a full recruit class, which turned CON's backbone-scarcity argument into PRO's case.
