### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by systematically exploiting the mechanical flaws in Side A's universal model. When Side A commits to a 25% military / 75% civil assignment split to avoid a lottery, Side B decisively frames this as an unequal compulsory labor scheme that destroys cohesion (by comparing a rifle platoon to a filing office) and wastes resources (by drafting youth to duplicate existing civilian labor). Side B also convincingly answers pressure on wartime scale by distinguishing peacetime conscription from wartime legal mobilization.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which weaponizes Side A's 100% assignment concession to prove the model would wreck social cohesion through unequal burdens while acting as a massive, inefficient labor scheme.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins by crystallizing the debate around PRO's own design concessions. CON forced PRO to admit that a realistic first-year rollout would place roughly three-quarters of conscripts in non-military civil roles, then exploited that 25/75 split to collapse both of PRO's advantages: it is too militarily thin to solve Europe's readiness gap and too visibly unequal to manufacture the solidarity PRO promises. CON's alternative—paying willing specialists, stockpiling equipment, and rehearsing existing civilian operators through reserves and exercises—survives as a cleaner, less coercive path to the same goals.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's absorption-to-exposure sequence in Rebuttal 1 and Rebuttal 2, which cornered PRO into specifying a ~25/75 military-to-civil split and then reframed it as a compulsory labour scheme whose unequal burden destroys the cohesion claim PRO entered the debate championing.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core model. When pressed during the pressure questions, Side A revealed that its "universal" system would only place 25% of a cohort in military roles and 75% in civil roles. Side B brilliantly weaponized this concession to destroy both of PRO's pillars: it showed the 75% civil placement was merely a compulsory labor scheme that added little actual defense depth, and it proved the assignment mechanism would breed intense resentment rather than social cohesion, as peers would face vastly different burdens. Side B also won the economic clash by arguing that since personnel and equipment are complements, Europe must prioritize the scarcer input (munitions and kit) rather than diverting funds to train masses of conscripts it cannot equip. Side A's case collapsed under the weight of its own design concessions, and Side B's closing crystallized the clash perfectly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's exploitation of Side A's 25% military / 75% civil concession in Rebuttal 2 and the Closing, framing it as a "compulsory labour scheme" that inherently destroys the social cohesion argument through visibly unequal burdens.
