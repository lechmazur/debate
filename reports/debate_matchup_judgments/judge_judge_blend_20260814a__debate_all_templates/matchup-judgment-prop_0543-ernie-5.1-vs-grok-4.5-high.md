### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): A's decisive move was the selection-effect argument: the adolescents most exposed to predatory contact and stranger amplification are precisely those who seek visibility and disable defaults, while the "majority who never touch settings" that B relies on for measurable harm reduction are largely the low-risk population to begin with. B's rebuttals (seatbelt/smoke-detector analogies, "perfection standard" framing) never squarely answered this mismatch between who stays protected and who is actually harmed—B kept asserting aggregate protection without engaging the marginal-population problem A raised. On censorship, A offered a concrete causal chain (mandatory age-sorting → liability exposure → platform over-restriction of messaging/follows/recommendations), which B dismissed as "speculation" but never displaced with an equally concrete reason why liability incentives wouldn't push platforms to over-comply. B's opening also leaned on a vague, unverifiable claim about "early compliance reports" showing sharp declines, a soft grounding weakness. A's case was tighter, more original, and more directly resolved the two-pronged burden the motion sets, while B's answers, though competent and well-formatted, largely restated the same aggregate-benefit framing without closing the gap A kept exposing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1/Rebuttal 2 point that the population protected by unchanged defaults is systematically not the same population driving predatory-contact harms, which B's inertia and "seatbelt" analogies never directly rebutted.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully dissecting Side A's causal link between default settings and harm reduction. Side B convincingly argued that the teens who face predatory harm are exactly the ones who will opt out for visibility, meaning the policy only protects low-risk users and fails to measurably reduce the harms that matter.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 dismantle of Side A's seatbelt analogy by pointing out that 'no one gains status by unbuckling,' whereas on social media, visibility is the exact good being chased.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (CON); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (PRO)): CON won by shifting the metric from aggregate inertia to durable impact on high-risk teens. A showed default privacy attracts opt-out from those seeking visibility, so protection pools in low-risk users and peer bullying remains untouched. PRO never resolved that selection effect and dismissed age-gating overreach as speculation, while A's mechanism linking minor-only enforcement to liability-driven throttling of messaging/follows made censorship structural, not hypothetical.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R1 reversal of the seatbelt analogy - defaults incentivize disabling because visibility is the good, so high-risk self-selects out and measurable reduction collapses to temporary theater - directly damaged PRO's core harm-reduction route.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, plausible causal stories, but CON (A) landed the more decisive distinguishing rebuttal: PRO's repeated analogy between mandated default privacy and "ordinary parental controls" or "locked adult profiles" was never squarely answered after A pointed out the category error—voluntary user/parent choice is not equivalent to a government-imposed default stripping the same choice from every minor. PRO's closing simply restated the analogy rather than engaging this distinction. CON also kept consistent pressure on PRO's own concession that grooming and self-harm content largely persist in private channels and closed groups untouched by discovery defaults, forcing PRO to fall back on "scale itself makes the reduction measurable"—an assertion rather than a resolved mechanism linking front-door restrictions to the specific harms named in the resolution. PRO's front-door/back-door framing and "audience remains reachable by choice" point were solid and kept the debate close, but CON's cleaner distinction between opt-in and coerced defaults, plus its unanswered evidentiary pressure, gives it the edge in the final comparison.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1), distinguishing voluntary parental/adult privacy choices from a government-mandated default imposed on all minors, which PRO's closing never directly rebutted despite repeating the parental-control analogy.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won both core metrics by maintaining a precise definition of 'defaults.' Side A successfully established a plausible causal mechanism for harm reduction—cutting the 'front door' vectors of unsolicited DMs and algorithmic discovery—which Side B failed to engage, instead repeating that harms happen in private channels. On censorship, Side A effectively proved that a starting posture does not strip user agency, defeating Side B's exaggerated characterization of defaults as 'compulsory invisibility.'
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's R1 and R2 explanation that defaults close the 'front door' to grooming pipelines, perfectly countering Side B's claim that defaults are useless because grooming happens in private DMs.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Grok 4.5 (high) (PRO)): PRO kept the winning path alive: private-by-default cuts front-door entry vectors (unsolicited stranger contact, public discovery/recommendation amplification) and at scale that is a plausible measurable reduction, without needing utopian erasure. CON stretched censorship to mean any discoverability loss, which collapsed under B's locked-profile/parental-control pressure, and CON's agency reply still treats opt-in defaults as compulsory invisibility. Migration claim remained speculative. Volume vs perfection framing gave PRO the cleaner reason to vote.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Q1 + B4.5 comparison that if default non-searchability for minors is 'censorship by architecture' then every parental control and locked adult profile is censorship — exposing CON's overbroad functional definition and forcing CON into a weak voluntary/coerced distinction that still leaves opt-in choice intact.
