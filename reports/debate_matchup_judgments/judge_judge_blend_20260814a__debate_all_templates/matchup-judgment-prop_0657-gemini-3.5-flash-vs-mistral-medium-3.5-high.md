Side B's reliance on leaks and bureaucracy was answered directly without evasion, and its indicators alternative was shown to leave democracies exposed to novel attacks tested across borders.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO won by a clear but competitive margin largely on the strength of the "sanitized alerts" architecture introduced in Rebuttal 2. By proposing that a trusted inner circle distills raw intelligence into generic actionable warnings before distributing them to the broader coalition, PRO found a credible institutional design that addressed CON's tiered-system paradox without conceding the motion's international scope. PRO also answered its pressure questions directly with the required A1/A2 labels and advanced the debate structurally rather than merely restating earlier claims. CON's strongest single move was the zero-days/server-configuration counter—arguing that the high-value indicators needed to preempt sophisticated AI campaigns necessarily reveal the collection methods that obtained them, meaning sanitized alerts either expose sources or are too watered-down to matter. This created a genuine unresolved tension in PRO's case. CON also made a real point that dissidents using VPNs and synchronized posting can be technically indistinguishable from bot networks, and that multi-party verification doesn't resolve this. However, CON never adequately defended its fallback position that open-source detection is sufficient for sophisticated campaigns—a burden PRO successfully challenged—and the "unequal hierarchy" objection to the tiered model was too weak to close the loop: unequal access is not fatal to a system that still delivers effective warnings. CON's closing restated the fatal-trap framing crisply but didn't add resolution to the sanitized-alerts problem it had raised. PRO's cost-of-inaction frame (manipulated elections, eroded legitimacy) was never neutralized. On balance, PRO offered a more complete institutional answer to a real threat, while CON's strongest objections, though genuine, left the case for inaction underdeveloped.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to CON's Q1 was the most decisive exchange. By proposing that a trusted inner circle converts raw intelligence into sanitized, actionable alerts for wider distribution, PRO found a workable middle path that simultaneously addressed the tiered-paradox objection and preserved the motion's international scope. CON's counter—that this creates an "unequal patronizing hierarchy" and that sophisticated campaigns require zero-day-level indicators that necessarily burn collection methods—was sharp but ultimately incomplete: CON never demonstrated that the system fails for the broad class of AI disinformation threats that don't rely on zero-days, nor did it close the gap left by conceding open-source detection is already insufficient for advanced campaigns.

Side A established a plausible need for cross-border early warning and offered a tiered, sanitized-sharing model, but Side B more effectively attacked the core tradeoff: high-value indicators can reveal and burn underlying intelligence access, creating permanent costs for a transient alert benefit. B also kept the censorship and weaponization concern live by showing that coordination markers can overlap with legitimate dissident behavior. A's safeguards helped, but claims like abuse being impossible were overstated.

- **Grok 4.3** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): B wins because its rebuttals exposed the tiered-system contradiction and the realistic weaponization path more decisively than A could neutralize them across the exchange.

- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): Side A wins by establishing a structurally viable tiered architecture that shares sanitized actionable alerts internationally while keeping sensitive methods confined to a trusted inner circle, directly answering Side B's central security and scope objections.
