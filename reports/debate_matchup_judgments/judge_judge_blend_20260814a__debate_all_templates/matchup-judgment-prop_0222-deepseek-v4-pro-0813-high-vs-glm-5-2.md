- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side B won the comparative required by the motion. Its concession that punitive enforcement would not have produced fewer deaths seriously weakened its own route to victory. B also retained the stronger crime comparison: criminal records and diverted police resources were concrete harms, while A’s larger-user-base theory remained speculative.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal was decisive: it explained that an intervention can be insufficient to solve the crisis yet still outperform punitive enforcement, directly answering A’s package objection. It then used A’s inability to identify arrest as the protective ingredient to weigh emergency access and record-related harms against largely asserted marginal deterrence.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins by maintaining a consistent comparative framework and exploiting Side A's contradictory concessions. Side A made a strong economic case for marginal deterrence, but undermined it by conceding they were not arguing the punitive baseline would have produced fewer deaths. Side A also relied on a flawed semantic critique, arguing that "less bad is not lower," which ignored the explicitly comparative text of the prompt ("lower... than"). Side B capitalized on these missteps while providing highly effective mechanistic weighing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.4) masterfully flipped Side A's argument about fentanyl killing too quickly for bystanders, pointing out that this exact lethality makes the solitary use driven by criminalization a guaranteed death sentence.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (PRO); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (CON)): PRO kept the comparative question live: does removing arrest improve deaths/crimes versus punitive baseline under same conditions? ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side B won by offering the stronger comparative mechanism: possession penalties create immediate, concrete barriers to emergency calls and treatment while criminal records worsen instability and crime. Side A raised legitimate concerns about initiation, policy bundling, and total rather than per-user harm, but never grounded its enlarged-user-pool scenario beyond a plausible cost argument. B also explained persuasively why legal risk can strongly deter a 911 call without similarly deterring socially situated initiation. The clipped rebuttal was an execution cost, and B could have developed downstream crime more fully, but its main route remained clearer and better defended.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s answer distinguishing abstract legal risk at initiation from the immediate risk of police exposure during an overdose most effectively weakened Side A’s central deterrence analogy. Side A’s response largely restated that initiation must have some responsiveness without showing it was large enough to reverse B’s survival and stability benefits.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO)): Side B won by successfully exposing Side A's lack of concrete evidence for increased initiation rates, while effectively defending the unique necessity of decriminalization. Side B's answers in Rebuttal 2 clearly dismantled Side A's counter-mechanisms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's explanation (A1) distinguishing the abstract legal risk of initiating drug use from the immediate, visceral fear of police arriving during an overdose.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = GLM-5.2 (max) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): PRO kept a concrete, proximate mechanism alive: criminal risk drives use alone, avoids 911/naloxone/testing, and criminal records manufacture instability driving downstream crime. CON's same-door alternative was met by a credible trust argument. The enabling-condition point favors PRO on the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.1-A4.3 distinction that fear deters immediate 911 calls but not abstract initiation decisions plus the narrow/trust explanation of Good Samaritan failure, which neutralized CON's deterrence and alternative-policy core.
