### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around a practical comparative: prediction will happen regardless, and banning AI merely shifts it from auditable models to unwritten manager hunches. Side B also effectively neutralized Side A's core "black box/trade secret" and "scale" arguments by pointing out that centralized models can be audited under legal protective orders and corrected company-wide, whereas thousands of individual manager biases cannot.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively flipped Side A's "scale" argument by pointing out that a flawed model can be audited and corrected once, whereas thousands of biased manager judgments cannot.
- **Grok 4.3** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): B wins by showing the ban is overbroad and drives prediction into less-reviewable human discretion while targeted rules address the real harms; A never neutralizes this core comparison despite strong pressure on bias and enforceability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 directly answers Q1/Q2 on lawful inputs and trade-secret reviewability, then weighs scale and auditability to reframe the choice as regulated models vs. undocumented hunches.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON); B = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO)): CON wins by reframing the choice as auditable systems versus unlogged manager discretion, showing a ban would push the same predictions underground where workers have less recourse. CON also successfully demonstrates overbreadth: productivity and quit forecasts have clear beneficial uses in coaching and retention, while discriminatory inputs and adverse uses can be targeted by narrower regulation without banning the entire category. PRO’s unionization and bias concerns are real but insufficient to justify a blanket ban on all three uses, especially when CON shows that logs and protective orders can create more accountability than the status quo of intuition and gossip.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s auditability turn—arguing that a flawed model can be audited and corrected once across a firm while thousands of biased manager judgments cannot, and that banning AI eliminates a paper trail rather than bias—materially undermines PRO’s scale and black-box arguments and frames the decisive comparative advantage for CON.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = ByteDance Seed2.0 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.4 (high) (CON)): Side B wins by consistently controlling the core comparative frame: banning AI does not stop employers from predicting worker behavior, it merely pushes those judgments from potentially logged, auditable systems into opaque managerial discretion and gossip. Side A built a strong initial case around baked-in historical bias, self-fulfilling prophecies, and trade-secret shielding, but relied heavily on worst-case assumptions about AI opacity. Side B directly answered these pressures by explaining how targeted input bans, mandatory decision logging, and court protective orders create a verifiable paper trail that human intuition fundamentally lacks. Side B also successfully flipped Side A's scale argument, noting that a single flawed model can be audited and corrected firm-wide, whereas thousands of biased human judgments cannot. Side A's repeated assertions about unenforceability and black-box harm never fully overcame Side B's clearer regulatory alternative or the practical reality that managers will keep predicting regardless of the ban. Side B's closing crystallized this clash sharply, framing the blanket ban as worker disarmament rather than protection.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 response to the trade-secret pressure question. By explaining that courts routinely review confidential business records under protective orders and that mandatory input/score logging actually generates the evidence workers need to prove discrimination, Side B neutralized Side A's central enforceability claim and reinforced the decisive comparative advantage of regulated, documented AI over undocumented human bias.
