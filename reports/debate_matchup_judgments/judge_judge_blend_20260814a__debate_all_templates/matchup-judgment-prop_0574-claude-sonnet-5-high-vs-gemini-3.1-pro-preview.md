A answered that old contracts were insufficiently specific, but did not resolve the contractual-autonomy objection.

CON introduced the Rogue One CGI case as proof that studios already generate new dead performances without permission, but PRO countered that the Cushing estate was actually consulted—a fact CON could only dismiss as a "PR courtesy." This blunted CON's technological-equivalence argument and reinforced PRO's claim that permission-seeking is already the responsible norm. More damagingly, CON explicitly conceded in Rebuttal 2 that a studio should proceed "despite a family's objection" if it holds bare legal rights, and PRO ruthlessly crystallized this in the closing as the core choice: exploitation-by-default versus consent-by-design. CON's contract-integrity framework was sophisticated, but PRO successfully distinguished distributing old works in new formats (streaming) from fabricating performances that never existed, and showed that nothing in PRO's model prevents future contracts from explicitly including AI rights. The "imperfect proxy beats no proxy" framing gave PRO a durable answer to CON's estate-criticism. While A's clipped opening was a real cost, the later speeches adapted sharply and resolved the clash in PRO's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 answer to the Rogue One pressure question: PRO demonstrated that CON's own example of CGI stand-ins actually relied on estate consultation, undermining the claim that legal ownership alone suffices, and recast the proposition as simply codifying a norm that reputable productions already follow.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): B won a clear but competitive debate by showing that the blanket permission rule creates an unconditional estate veto, while a regulated licensing system can protect identity yet preserve narrow exceptions and judicial review. A made a powerful dignity case but repeatedly portrayed B as supporting unrestricted studio use, despite B explicitly defending legal oversight. A also never established why restoration or other sympathetic uses must be barred rather than reviewed individually.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s second rebuttal decisively distinguished permission rules with exceptions from A’s absolute veto. The spare-key analogy answered A’s claimed contradiction and exposed that A invoked ordinary copyright dispute resolution while rejecting the safeguards and exceptions that make such systems workable.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): CON showed that PRO's blanket permission requirement creates an absolute estate veto that blocks legitimate restoration and tribute work when consent is impossible, and PRO never recovered from owning the position that such sympathetic projects should simply die. CON's distinction between a regulated licensing regime with judicial overrides and PRO's unconditional veto cleanly resolved the core clash in CON's favor.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 and Closing used the "spare key" analogy and PRO's own admission that restoration projects must die without consent to frame the proposition as an overbroad, unappealable estate veto rather than a genuine dignity safeguard.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Sonnet 5 (high) (CON)): PRO kept two live paths - dignity/identity theft not covered by copyright and market cannibalization of living artists. CON never answered the second, and CON conceded a permission-based update to publicity law is needed, shrinking the clash to absolute vs regulated permission. PRO's admission that sympathy projects should die if unconsented is stark but aligns with the proposition's wording and was framed as stewardship via chain of title, while CON's spare-key alternative admits consent matters. The unrebutted living-artist harm plus direct answer on the legal gap gives PRO the cleaner path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B Rebuttal 2 A1 distinction: regulated publicity license with time limits, compulsory-license, fair-use and orphan-work exceptions vs PRO's 'explicit permission or nothing' veto - reframing the lock as having a judicial spare key.
