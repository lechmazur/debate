### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): Side B won by consistently exposing the bluntness of the proposition's mandates. While Side A made a highly compelling political argument for why a hard gate and massive budget increase are needed to force politicians' hands, Side B successfully demonstrated that a blanket tripling could be absorbed by low-priority cosmetic work, and that the categorical ban would block genuinely new, life-saving safety megaprojects (like flood barriers). Side B's alternative metric (expected harm avoided per dollar) provided a cleaner, more rigorous decision rule that survived Side A's pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 systematically dismantled Side A's carve-out test for 'maintenance,' showing how it would still block crucial new safety projects while relying on a subjective 'prestige' distinction.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON); B = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO)): CON won by showing that PRO’s demand for a blanket tripling is arbitrary when governments do not know the condition of the stock, and that PRO’s gate against megaprojects either blocks legitimate safety projects or collapses into a vague, unenforceable standard. CON’s framework—targeted inspections for high-risk assets followed by expected-harm-per-dollar prioritization—provided a more coherent and disciplined path to actual safety.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal exposing that PRO’s carve-out for safety projects relies on an unenforceable subjective test of “restores rather than expands prestige capacity,” whereas CON’s expected-harm-per-dollar gate provides an objective standard that applies equally to repairs and new construction.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Qwen 3.8 Max (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4 Pro 0813 (high) (CON)): CON won the central gate clash. PRO established hidden corrosion and political bias toward ribbon-cutting, but CON showed blanket tripling is uncalibrated - it can fund low-risk work while critical assets stay uninspected - and that the categorical "no new megaprojects" veto blocks safety-improving replacements like hospitals, flood barriers, bypasses. PRO's attempt to reclassify urgent replacements as maintenance conceded the literal motion is overbroad, and its "surveys are maintenance" reply did not justify 3x over targeted high-risk inspections ranked by age, exposure, and failure history.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's R2 A1 - risk ranking via known proxies without a complete inventory, then buying targeted surveys, directly neutralized PRO's Q1 that expected-harm-per-dollar cannot work before tripling creates capacity.
