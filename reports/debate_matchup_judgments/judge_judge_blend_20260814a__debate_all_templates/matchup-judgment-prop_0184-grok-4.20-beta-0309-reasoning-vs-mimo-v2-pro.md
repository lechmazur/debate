PRO's core scientific premise—lead has no safe exposure level, so any policy that leaves pipes in place accepts permanent harm—was never rebutted with a counter-principle. CON responded only by calling it "absolutist" and pointing to the 90% reduction figure, but never engaged the non-linearity of lead's neurological harm at low doses. That premise therefore remained live through closing. PRO's "physics over governance" framing was clean and consistently maintained: once pipes are replaced the source is eliminated regardless of operator error, whereas chemical treatment demands flawless perpetual execution. CON's most powerful move was the temporal gap—children still drink lead during the replacement decades—and their pressure question on this was sharp. But PRO's response (phased prioritization of highest-risk pipes, block by block, with debt financing shortening the timeline) was at least partial and responsive. CON never satisfactorily answered the core "no safe level" bind: their 90% defense explicitly accepts ongoing measurable lead exposure in developing brains, and calling that "a massive immediate benefit" without addressing the scientific premise left the burden uncomfortably on CON's side. The equity argument also favored PRO: CON's debt-burden concern is real but addressed indirectly by the proposition's own framing (debt and rate hikes are the accepted cost). PRO held more of its ground under pressure and kept the decisive comparison cleaner. The margin is real but not dominant because CON's temporal-gap argument was never fully closed by PRO and represented genuine unresolved pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's pressure question Q1—"by your own logic, aren't you condemning children in those interim decades?"—was the sharpest moment in the debate and genuinely exposed PRO's unresolved temporal gap. However, because CON's own affirmative case required accepting ongoing lead exposure at the 10% residual level—a level PRO had established is harmful with no safe threshold—CON's pressure landed asymmetrically: it revealed a timing problem for PRO but did not rehabilitate CON's core position, which also accepted child lead exposure indefinitely.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO)): CON (Side A) wins by a clear but competitive margin on three live routes that PRO never squarely neutralized. First, the middle-path argument—targeted replacement of highest-risk lines plus optimized treatment—was introduced in CON's opening and maintained through every speech; PRO never explained why this hybrid approach is inadequate, which left CON holding a practical alternative that avoids the proposition's all-or-nothing demand. Second, the water poverty argument (rate hikes triggering shutoffs, unsafe alternatives, and deferred care for fixed-income households) was CON's sharpest pressure, and PRO's A1 answer ("ensuring water is inherently safe so families don't choose between a high bill and a poisoned tap") was a restatement of the goal rather than an answer to the affordability mechanism—it didn't engage the documented pathway from rate spikes to immediate health harm. Third, CON's Flint reframe—mismanagement during a source switch, not inherent treatment failure—held up across both rebuttal rounds; PRO's counter ("that's exactly the point, systems are vulnerable to human error") is real but circular, and CON's response about post-Flint redundancies and layered monitoring was a credible, if not definitive, reply. PRO's strongest asset was the philosophical clarity of "remove vs. manage" and the "infinite liability" framing, which was rhetorically effective and grounded the moral case well. But philosophy alone couldn't close the loop on affordability or the unanswered middle path. Both sides followed format correctly (Q1/Q2, A1/A2). PRO's opening was noticeably shorter than the cap and lighter on mechanism, ceding early ground CON never gave back.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's sustained deployment of the "targeted replacement plus treatment" middle path across all speeches was the most decisive rebuttal exchange. PRO repeatedly treated the choice as binary—manage the hazard forever or eliminate it—but CON repeatedly pointed out that evidence-driven selective replacement of highest-risk lines already addresses the most acute exposure without the fiscal shock the proposition demands. PRO's failure to explain why this hybrid is insufficient left the most practically credible answer to the motion's costs sitting squarely with CON.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2 Pro (PRO); B = Grok 4.20 0309 (Reasoning) (CON)): Side B won by successfully neutralizing Side A's core hazard claim and highlighting the immediate, tangible harms of Side A's fiscal requirements.
