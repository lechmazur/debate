### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B built the more coherent and adaptive case. Its decisive move was exposing a genuine internal contradiction in A's framework: A argued Iran's escalation is "bounded" because the regime prioritizes survival, yet also claimed a nuclear Iran would act with "absolute impunity"—a rational-enough-to-be-deterred-now-but-not-later inconsistency that A never resolved, even in closing. B also leveraged A's own concession that strikes only delay the program "by years" [A2.2] to reframe the cost-benefit calculus: a temporary setback bought at the price of expelled inspectors, lost visibility, and a plausible weaponization decision. B supported this with grounded, non-exotic historical analogies (Osirak, Stuxnet, North Korea, Pakistan) that functioned as reasonable background knowledge rather than fake precision. A's rebuttals largely restated its opening thesis (nuclear impunity, manageable escalation) without directly dismantling B's contradiction point or engaging with the "years vs. permanent problem" framing B kept returning to. A's answers to pressure questions ("permanently restored posture of credible deterrence," "intolerable costs") were more assertive than substantive, while B's answers directly cashed out historical deterrence logic and named a concrete alternative mechanism (JCPOA-style verified constraint). Both sides followed format correctly, but B's rebuttals did more comparative work and left A's live route to victory (deterrence-now vs. impunity-later) unresolved by the end.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 / Closing point exposing the contradiction between A's claim that Iran is deterred by regime-survival instincts now [A4.2] and that a nuclear Iran would act with "absolute impunity" later [A4.1/A5.2]—a self-refuting fork A never directly answered.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): Side A won by showing that strikes deliver only temporary delay while increasing motivation to weaponize, reducing inspection visibility, and creating a recurring escalation problem. Side B never adequately reconciled its claim that Iran is rationally deterred from retaliation with its claim that a nuclear Iran would enjoy absolute impunity, and it repeatedly asserted rather than demonstrated that escalation was manageable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's second rebuttal was decisive: it used Side B's regime-survival logic against it, arguing that an Iran rational enough to limit conventional escalation is also rational enough to face nuclear deterrence. It then showed that Side B's endgame amounted to indefinite repeated strikes, converting a temporary war risk into standing policy.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (PRO)): CON won by forcing PRO onto an unresolvable contradiction (regime-survival bounds escalation now yet yields "absolute impunity" with nukes) and by converting PRO's own "years" concession into a permanent-war endgame, while PRO's delay-vs-catastrophe path stayed rhetorically forceful but never cleaned the deterrence logic or showed why rolling strikes beat watched constraint.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's A4.2/A5.1 exposure of PRO's dual use of regime-survival rationality (safe ladder now; uncontainable nuclear later)

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON)): PRO built a tighter, more responsive case: it distinguished "bombing knowledge" from destroying material/capacity, inverted CON's "resolve" argument (Iran's resolve predated the strikes), and repeatedly forced CON to name an alternative mechanism to prevent a bomb. When CON's answer to the pressure question was essentially "reinforce monitoring and threaten force," PRO's "tripwire with no wall" rebuttal was devastating, since CON's own opening conceded that monitoring had already watched Iran enrich to 60%. PRO also used the actual post-strike outcome (muted retaliation, open Strait, quick ceasefire) as empirical leverage against CON's purely predictive spiral theory, and CON never squarely answered why that outcome doesn't undercut the "wider war" framing beyond redefining it as long-term structural risk—a redefinition PRO called out directly in Rebuttal 1 without an adequate CON response. CON's case (can't bomb knowledge, program goes underground, Russia/China alignment) was plausible but repetitive across rounds, never offered a concrete competing mechanism, and its closing/rebuttal 2 cited malformed reference IDs ([C1.5, C2.5]) that don't match the transcript's own tagging scheme, a small but real grounding lapse. Both sides followed the Q&A format correctly, but PRO's answers were more substantive and its closing crystallized the "which risk do you carry" framing more memorably than CON's closing, which mostly restated prior points.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 line that CON's "tripwire" monitoring was "a wire with no wall behind it" and "detection without a response is just a surveillance video of a breakout" — this directly neutralized CON's named alternative (continued IAEA monitoring plus deterrence) by pointing out it had already failed to stop 60% enrichment, and CON never recovered a distinct mechanism afterward.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): Side B won by making the cleaner comparative case: Iran already possessed intent and advanced enrichment, while the strikes reduced present capability during a favorable window and produced only bounded immediate retaliation. Side A compellingly argued that delay may be temporary and monitoring may be lost, but its containment alternative never clearly explained how it would prevent weaponization rather than merely detect or manage it. B occasionally overstated how fully the short aftermath disproved long-term escalation and asserted destruction too confidently, yet it consistently weighed a delayed, vulnerable program against the irreversible risks of a nuclear Iran. A also incurred execution costs through repeated nonexistent reference labels.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s decisive rebuttal was that monitoring had documented enrichment without stopping it, so visibility alone was not prevention. Under pressure, A proposed defenses, interdictions, and a credible threat of force, but did not show how those measures would reliably block a bomb; this strengthened B’s claim that CON ultimately offered containment rather than prevention.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Gemini 3.5 Flash (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): PRO built the cleaner path to victory by forcing a decisive risk comparison: bounded delay-plus-deterrence versus CON's monitored-but-failing path that still permits breakout. PRO adapted across rounds, falsified the spiral prediction with the actual muted aftermath, extracted that CON's alternative was containment accepting enrichment risk, and weighed Osirak-style rare strikes as cheaper than nuclear-era posture. CON's knowledge-loss and endless-cycle points stayed live but never neutralized PRO's outcome evidence or prevention burden.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's R2 answers (depth is not immunity; rare bounded strikes beat nuclear containment forever) plus pressure that forced CON to concede monitoring allowed 60% and offer only non-stopping "containment."
