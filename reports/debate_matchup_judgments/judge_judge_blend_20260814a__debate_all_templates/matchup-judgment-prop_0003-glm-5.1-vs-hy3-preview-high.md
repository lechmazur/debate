### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): A won by making the cleaner conditional case: when historic protections actually block needed density, they should not function as an absolute veto. B had strong points on heritage loss, luxury development, and alternatives, but relied too heavily on asserting that other land can solve the problem and that new housing would almost never help affordability. A directly pressed that those alternatives are also politically and practically blocked, answered the affordability objection with mixed density and possible affordability conditions, and weighed displacement and exclusion as irreversible harms comparable to or greater than building loss. A had some execution costs from clipped turns, but still made the decisive clash easier to judge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's strongest exchange was against B's alternative-land argument: A turned it from a solution into a delay, arguing that pointing to other politically blocked parcels does not justify preserving historic-district vetoes where density is needed.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON); B = GLM-5.1 (PRO)): Side B wins by successfully exposing the structural delays in Side A's alternative solutions. When Side A is forced to admit that alternative non-historic parcels are plagued by 'speculator hoarding, fragmented ownership, and lagging infrastructure,' Side B cleanly capitalizes by framing Side A's stance as a choice to stall housing while waiting for harder political and economic battles to be resolved.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing crystallization of Side A's Rebuttal 2 concession regarding fragmented ownership and speculator hoarding on alternative land.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = GLM-5.1 (PRO); B = Tencent Hy3 Preview (high) (CON)): PRO wins by successfully reframing irreversibility: displacement and exclusion destroy living communities just as permanently as demolition destroys façades. PRO directly answered CON's affordability and designation-reform pressure in Rebuttal 2, honestly accepted the motion's trade-offs, and exposed the political naivete of CON's "build elsewhere" alternative. CON repeated the luxury-housing and alternative-land claims without resolving PRO's core weighing on acute housing needs versus architectural preservation, leaving PRO with the cleaner, more decisive path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A1/A2) cleanly neutralized CON's affordability and reform pressure by clarifying that the status quo blocks all density types, that cities can pair relief with affordability mandates, and that exclusion itself causes permanent cultural erasure. This shifted the irreversibility burden back to CON and crystallized the decisive trade-off.
