### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (PRO); B = Grok 4.3 (CON)): PRO built the cleaner and more durable path to victory across the exchange. The most important analytical distinction in the debate — that project-specific environmental impact assessments differ structurally from regional, independently verified ecosystem baselines — was introduced early and never satisfactorily answered by CON, who repeatedly asserted that existing contractual surveys "establish workable reference conditions" without explaining how resource-focused exploration surveys substitute for the kind of before-state ecological baseline PRO demanded. PRO also cleanly answered CON's Q1 (the sharpest pressure in the debate): survey vessels with cameras and corers can gather species and sediment data without industrial-scale nodule extraction, directly defusing CON's attempt to show PRO's standard is self-defeating. CON's A1 to PRO's Q1 — that real-time sensors trigger automatic shutdown when readings hit pre-approved limits — inadvertently exposed a circularity: those thresholds must come from somewhere, and without robust baselines, their scientific grounding is exactly what PRO says is missing. CON's strongest surviving argument was the terrestrial harm comparison and the "delay locks in more land damage" flip of irreversibility, which is a genuine and live point. But it does not directly answer why approvals should proceed before baselines exist; it changes the subject to relative harm. PRO responded adequately that land recovers while abyssal ecosystems do not, and that the ISA's mandate is commons protection, not battery supply. Neither side used fake precision or invented citations, and both complied with format requirements. PRO's rhetoric was marginally more memorable ("you cannot retrofit a baseline onto a churned seabed"), and its argument progression across rounds narrowed the dispute more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A1 in Rebuttal 2 — explaining that survey vessels with cameras and corers establish ecosystem baselines without collector vehicles churning sediment — directly neutralized CON's Q1, which was designed to show that PRO's standard requires zero prior activity and is therefore self-defeating. The answer was crisp, accurate as general background knowledge, and showed that the distinction between exploration and commercial extraction is real and actionable, leaving CON's framing of the baseline requirement as impossibly circular without support.

B answered that baselines can be built through exploration and independent research without commercial extraction, and made the irreversibility and lost-baseline risk the clearer decision rule. A's climate and equity impacts remained live but were not proven enough to outweigh B's precautionary case.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's most decisive exchange was distinguishing project-level impact assessments and adaptive monitoring from independent regional baselines, then explaining that survey vessels and exploration sampling can generate data without industrial-scale mining. That undercut A's claim that extraction is the only workable path to knowledge.
