### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A won a clear but competitive debate. Side B had a strong structural case for tiered pricing, especially on recurring shortages, revenue, and baseline protection. But Side A made the more decisive comparison: in a physical shortage, pricing can license continued luxury use by the wealthy, while restrictions can directly prohibit the wasteful behavior. Side A also answered the main enforcement and vulnerable-family pressure by explaining escalating penalties and behavior-targeted restrictions, while Side B never fully solved the problem that revenue does not replace gallons during an acute drawdown.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's strongest exchange was turning Side B's "feature, not a bug" defense into a wealth-tiered commons argument, then answering the fine-enforcement objection with escalating penalties, public exposure, and shutoff mechanisms.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO); B = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON)): CON wins by crystallizing the moral stakes of shared scarcity and exposing the weakness in PRO's core equity claim. PRO's concession that wealthy users keeping green lawns is "a feature, not a bug" allowed CON to frame tiered pricing as wealth-tiered privatization of a commons. CON successfully showed that real restrictions target discretionary outdoor use rather than flat percentage cuts, meaning they avoid punishing low-income large families while delivering immediate, visible, equal cuts. PRO's structural revenue argument remained plausible but could not overcome the decisive clash on whether water access in a shortage should scale with wealth. CON's rebuttals were sharper, and their closing framed the choice with exceptional clarity.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8), where they answered PRO's pressure questions by demonstrating that restrictions are enforced through shutoffs and public accountability—not trivial fines—and that behavior-targeted bans on discretionary use avoid harming large families, directly neutralizing PRO's equity and privatization attacks while turning PRO's "feature, not a bug" quote into the central moral concession of the debate.
- **Qwen 3.6 Max Preview** (A = Claude Opus 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by controlling the frame, landing decisive rebuttals, and cleanly resolving the central clash. CON successfully anchored the debate in physical scarcity ("the reservoir is the clock"), forcing PRO to defend pricing on speed and certainty—terrain where billing cycles and low short-run elasticity are fatal weaknesses. CON's most damaging move was capturing PRO's "feature, not a bug" concession about wealthy users keeping green lawns, which CON relentlessly framed as privatizing a shared emergency. When PRO attacked CON on equity (large families, medical needs), CON's R2 clarification that real drought ordinances target discretionary outdoor behaviors (turf, pools, fountains) rather than raw per-capita gallons effectively neutralized the charge and exposed PRO's reliance on a strawman. PRO's responses leaned on idealized smart meters, pre-set pricing, and administrative household adjustments, which CON successfully turned into implementation burdens that erode pricing's supposed simplicity. CON's pressure questions were sharper, the A1/A2 answers in R2 were direct and damaging, and the closing crystallized the choice memorably ("The commons becomes a checkout lane"). PRO ran a competent economic case but never recovered from the equity concession or solved the acute-drawdown timeline problem within recurring droughts.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's R2 pivot on enforcement and targeting: dismantling PRO's claim that restrictions are just cheap $50 fines by explaining shutoffs, escalating penalties, and public shaming, then clarifying that blunt ordinances target discretionary outdoor use (~50% of urban demand) rather than survival gallons. This directly answered PRO's equity pressure, neutralized the large-family/medical-need attack, and restored the speed/certainty advantage without conceding ground.
