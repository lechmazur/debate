### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON's use of concrete, if hedged, historical examples (H-2A scale growth alongside persistent crossings, Spain-Morocco circular recruitment, Israeli barrier, Australian interdiction) gave its argument more traction than PRO's largely theoretical substitution mechanism. CON's sharpest moment was turning PRO's own Spain-Morocco example back on it in Rebuttal 2, showing a mature, well-designed circular program coexisting with heavy irregular flows from the same region — a direct hit on PRO's strongest concrete support. CON also pressed effectively on the unfalsifiable "insufficient scale" defense and on PRO's own admission that pathways must be large, cheap, and portable to work, forcing PRO into defending a program no one has built. PRO's best counter — that coexistence isn't a counterfactual, and that CON's headline enforcement wins (Israel, Australia) target different populations (asylum/maritime) than the seasonal workers at issue — was a real and only partially answered point, keeping PRO competitive throughout, especially in the pressure-questions exchange. But PRO never supplied its own comparative evidence to match CON's, relying on logic and mechanism rather than any grounded example of substitution working, while CON kept sharpening the empirical mismatch. Both closing/rebuttal speeches were clipped for length, a mutual execution flaw, but CON's overall rebuttal sequence did more damage to PRO's live route than PRO's rebuttals did to CON's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 turn of PRO's own Spain-Morocco example ([B4.3]) against PRO's substitution mechanism was the most decisive exchange, since it used PRO's chosen evidence to argue the mechanism is "empirically hollow" even under favorable conditions.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won by successfully framing the debate around empirical effectiveness versus theoretical promises. While Side A offered a very strong logical mechanism for substitution and correctly pointed out the flaws in measuring national totals (the "public transit" analogy), Side B capitalized on Side A's lack of real-world examples. Side B's structural critique—that irregular migrants lack the requirements for legal pathways—remained potent, and B effectively turned A's only concrete example (Spain-Morocco) against them.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's second rebuttal, where B defended the Israel example as targeting economic migrants and neatly turned A's Spain-Morocco example to point out that it coexists with a massive irregular Mediterranean route, exposing A's lack of empirical proof.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (PRO)): CON built the cleaner decisive case: existing seasonal/sectoral programs coexist with persistent irregular flows and provide no demonstrated substitution effect, while PRO’s design requirements (scale, accessibility, portability) collapse into an untested ideal rather than ordinary expansion; enforcement examples supply clearer before-after impact. PRO’s mixed-flow distinction and renewability mechanism were live and adaptive, but never overcame the burden of a real-world success instance or a non-unfalsifiable scale threshold after pressure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 1 on the unfalsifiable “too small” escape hatch plus design concessions amounting to open access, which forced PRO into theoretical reform rather than observed reduction.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent cases, but CON's methodological pressure proved harder for PRO to fully neutralize. CON's core objections—that a visa only reduces irregular migration if the recipient would otherwise have crossed illegally, that unauthorized population is a stock shaped by both entries and departures (so PRO's historical correlation doesn't prove entries rose), and that pathway compliance itself depends on enforcement for credibility—were pressed consistently and never fully closed by PRO. PRO's rebuttals (employer-driven substitution, "enforcement as infrastructure inside the pathway") were plausible but partly conceded CON's credibility point rather than refuting it, and PRO never squarely answered the stock-vs-flow critique of its central historical analogy. CON's causal mechanism ("an interception directly prevents that entry") was also more direct and required fewer assumptions than PRO's chain of substitution, employer behavior, and timely departure. Execution mattered too: PRO's rebuttal 1, rebuttal 2, and closing were all clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off key material (including its closing mid-sentence), while CON was clipped only once and delivered a clean, focused closing that crystallized the decisive causal gap. On balance, CON's tighter logical chain, sustained pressure on the substitution assumption, and cleaner execution give it a clear, though not overwhelming, edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's stock-vs-flow rebuttal to PRO's historical border-hardening evidence (B2.2/B4.1)—arguing that a rising unauthorized population reflects arrivals, departures, and duration together, not necessarily rising entries—undercut PRO's central empirical anchor and was never fully answered before PRO's speeches were clipped.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): Side B won by systematically dismantling Side A's two main pillars. First, B exposed the 'substitution' flaw in A's logic, demonstrating that issuing a visa only reduces irregular migration if it guarantees a 1:1 displacement of an illegal crossing and an off-the-books hire, which A failed to prove. Second, B successfully refuted A's historical argument by distinguishing between the 'stock' of unauthorized residents (which grew because circular migrants stayed) and the 'flow' of entries (which enforcement directly blocks).
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 effectively neutralized A's pressure questions by proving that an intercepted crossing definitively stops that attempt, whereas issuing a visa may just recruit a new migrant or allow an employer to expand without reducing off-the-books hiring.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = GPT-5.6 Sol (high) (CON)): CON wins on the substitution clash: PRO never proves each new visa displaces a would-be irregular entrant, replaces rather than supplements irregular labor, and results in timely departure. CON neutralizes PRO's best historical evidence with the stock-vs-flow correction, and shows enforcement's counterfactual is cleaner because an interception directly prevents that entry. PRO's circular-migration reframe is strong but clipped turns and unanswered overstay/recruitment risks leave its superiority claim unproven.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2: population stock does not measure entries so the 1990s-2000s trend does not show enforcement failure, plus interception stops that attempt while a visa only reduces irregular migration if the recipient would have migrated irregularly—directly exposing PRO's core counterfactual gap.
