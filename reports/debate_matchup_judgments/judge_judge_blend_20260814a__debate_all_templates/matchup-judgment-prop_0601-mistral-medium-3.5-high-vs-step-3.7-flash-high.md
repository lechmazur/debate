- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): Side A (CON) built the stronger path to victory by consistently pressing a single, analytically sharp distinction: that most targeted diesel buses serve peripheral routes with minimal private car ownership, meaning electrification cuts direct tailpipe emissions but leaves the largest source of urban transport emissions—private car trips—completely unaddressed. This argument was introduced in the opening, survived all rebuttal pressure, and was never squarely neutralized. Side B's best counter was the TransMilenio mode-shift claim, but Side A correctly identified that electrifying existing BRT corridors only preserves pre-existing modal shift rather than creating new car-to-transit diversion. Side B's Medellín feeder bus example was too vague and modest to close this gap against Side A's Bogotá metro projection. Side A also partially undermined Side B's core speed advantage by pointing to grid upgrade delays in São Paulo's peripheral zones, narrowing the timing differential without needing to eliminate it entirely. Side B's timing argument for "near-term" targets remained genuine and was never fully dismantled, keeping the debate competitive, but the mode-shift argument—that bus electrification treats a symptom rather than the structural cause—proved more durable across the exchange. Side A carries a real epistemic cost for overconfident precision on numbers like "18-month delays" and "1.2 million daily car trips," while Side B was generally more cautious with specific claims, explaining the ground_b advantage. Overall, Side A made the decisive clash clearer and answered the most important live pressure more effectively.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 1 distinguishing BRT corridor buses (where mode shift already exists and electrification merely preserves it) from the vast majority of targeted peripheral diesel buses (where car ownership is minimal and no new mode shift is created) was the most decisive exchange. This moved the debate from a simple speed/cost comparison to a strategic question about whether bus electrification actually addresses the right emissions source, and Side B never fully recovered from it.

- **Claude Sonnet 4.6 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (CON); B = Step 3.7 Flash (high) (PRO)): PRO (Side B) wins for three reinforcing reasons. Second, PRO's São Paulo counter-attack—that roughly 80% of metro riders were former bus users, not car commuters—directly gutted CON's central evidence for rail-enabled modal shift. CON never squarely answered this point, and without it, CON's Mexico City and São Paulo figures lose much of their argumentative force. PRO's equity case—buses already serve every neighborhood rail will never reach due to land tenure and budget barriers—was live through closing and never genuinely answered.
