### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): B built a tighter, more resilient case. Its core mechanisms—that visibility drives funding ("a benefit you cannot feature is a benefit you will not fund"), that egg freezing and leave serve genuinely different populations rather than sitting in a moral hierarchy, and that annual childcare/leave costs dwarf a one-time retrieval benefit—directly neutralized A's "distorted priorities" argument and were never squarely answered. B's pressure questions (on what counts as "strong" support, and on the same-company visible-vs-hidden comparison) exposed real weaknesses; A's answer to the "strong" standard question was vague ("common sense," "industry norms"), and B correctly hammered this as chilling and unworkable. B also landed a structurally damaging point that A never rebutted on the record: if flagship marketing is a "signal" of institutional priorities, flagship leave marketing signals an early-family timeline just as much, making A's asymmetric restriction inconsistent rather than neutral. A's conceptual core (flagship marketing as a "declaration of priorities," not a ban) was reasonable and consistently pressed, but A's rebuttals repeatedly failed to engage B's sharpest counters (the cost-multiple math, the signaling-symmetry point), and A's speeches were clipped for exceeding word limits in the opening, both rebuttals, and—most damagingly—the closing, which cuts off mid-sentence and undercuts A's ability to crystallize its case. That repeated execution failure, combined with unanswered live points, gives B a clear, though not overwhelming, win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's response combining [B2.4] (childcare costs recur and cost multiples more than one-time retrieval) with the signaling-symmetry point [B2.6-2.8] was decisive: it dismantled A's claim that choosing egg freezing over other benefits reveals a "distorted priority," and exposed an unaddressed inconsistency in A's signaling argument that A never rebutted in the visible transcript.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully collapsing Side A's distinction between "offering" and "marketing," demonstrating that in the real market, benefits rely on recruiting ROI. Side A's severe execution costs (clipping in four of five speeches) prevented them from fully defending their standard or their causal claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) decisively countered Side A's core defense by explaining that companies do not fund invisible perks, meaning a ban on flagship marketing effectively acts as a ban on the benefit itself.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (CON); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (PRO)): Side A (CON) won by demonstrating that the proposition’s flagship-marketing restriction would functionally erase egg-freezing benefits for many women—particularly at smaller employers where recruiting ROI determines whether a benefit survives—while imposing an undefined, paternalistic hierarchy that taxes the parent-later timeline. Side B (PRO) offered a sophisticated signaling framework about institutional priorities, but could not satisfactorily answer how a 29-year-old who wants to freeze is better off losing visible access today in exchange for speculative employer pressure tomorrow, nor could it specify what counts as strong enough support to avoid a chilling effect.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's recurring recruiting-ROI argument that benefits banished from careers pages die in practice (A2.1, A4.3), which converted PRO's narrow marketing-only framing into a concrete access loss and became the central live route that PRO never fully neutralized.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): A built a tight, consistent case: the motion only restricts flagship-status marketing, not the benefit's existence, and A repeatedly turned B's own concessions (that flagship visibility drives use, and that freezing is far cheaper than leave/childcare) into evidence for A's "substitution/moral license" theory rather than letting them sit as pure wins for B. A's pressure questions were sharp and directly answered in proper A1/A2 format, and A's closing crystallized the clash cleanly around the "cheap delay marketed loudly, expensive support funded not at all" asymmetry. B raised genuine points—paternalism, the "vast middle ground" of imperfect-but-real benefit packages, cost-asymmetry as triage—but never fully neutralized A's reframing that flagship status is prescriptive signaling, not mere information, and B's case suffered real execution costs: three separate turns (Opening, Rebuttal 2, Closing) were clipped for exceeding word limits, cutting off developing arguments (including the promising "middle ground employers" point and the final closing weighing) before the judge could see them fully realized. That repeated discipline failure, combined with A's cleaner exploitation of B's concessions, gives A the win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1-A4.6), which took B's concessions that "flagship marketing drives use" and freezing is "orders of magnitude cheaper" than leave/childcare and reframed them as proof of exactly the substitution the proposition targets, was the most decisive exchange—B never fully escaped this reframing in the remaining (partly clipped) speeches.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO); B = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON)): Side B won a narrow but clear victory by logically tracing the practical consequences of the proposition. While Side A used exceptional rhetoric and a clever pivot to frame the cost disparity as a cynical 'business model,' Side B effectively neutralized this by explaining it as necessary triage for resource-constrained employers. Side B also successfully argued that the proposition's high bar of 'strong' support would penalize a vast middle ground of imperfect employers and suppress a benefit women actively request.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's second rebuttal, which deftly countered Side A's 'business model' pivot by reframing the cost asymmetry as 'triage' rather than hypocrisy, proving that the rule punishes resource constraints rather than exposing a scam.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Xiaomi MiMo V2.5 Pro (CON); B = Muse Spark 1.1 (high) (PRO)): Side B wins because it successfully tied together Side A's twin concessions—that flagship visibility drives uptake and that egg freezing is orders of magnitude cheaper than comprehensive parental leave and childcare—into a cohesive "business model" argument. This showed the proposition targets a real structural substitution effect (cheap delay marketed prominently while costly present support is underfunded) rather than merely suppressing information. Side B also more effectively neutralized the paternalism charge by framing asymmetric flagship marketing as itself coercive within an employment dependency. Side A's scope objection (that "strong support" sweeps in employers with decent but imperfect policies) and triage defense were live burdens, but they were undermined by three clipped speeches and by Side B's cleaner closing crystallization of the clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's construction of the "business model" argument from Side A's concessions that flagship marketing drives use and freezing is vastly cheaper than leave/childcare, showing the proposition targets a structural incentive to substitute cheap delay for expensive present support.
