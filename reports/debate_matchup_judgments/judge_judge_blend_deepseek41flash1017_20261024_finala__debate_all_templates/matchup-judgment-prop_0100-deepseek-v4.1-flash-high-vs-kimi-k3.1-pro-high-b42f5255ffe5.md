### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Both sides argued cleanly around the core question of what "central" means, but CON (B) built the more durable framework: centrality as a functional role (the firm, scalable anchor that makes a high-renewable system workable) rather than a share of total generation or investment. This let CON absorb PRO's "portfolio" rebuttals by turning them back on PRO — pointing out that each named alternative (hydro, geothermal, interconnection, batteries) is endowment- or duration-limited, so the "divide the task" argument assumes availability it never demonstrates. CON's bridge/foundation analogies crystallized this distinction memorably and were never squarely dismantled; PRO's closing reasserted the "essential function vs indispensable technology" distinction but did not explain why CON's functional reading of "central" should be rejected, nor did it independently establish that most countries' constraints are mild enough for pure portfolios to suffice — it largely asserted this throughout. PRO also suffered a real execution cost: Rebuttal 1 was clipped before its concluding synthesis, weakening its momentum right when framing the "narrow clash" question. Both sides handled the Pressure Questions format correctly with A1/A2 labels, but CON's answers did more work engaging the opponent's exact phrasing and turning concessions (e.g., "may be central in genuinely land-scarce, isolated systems") into unresolved burdens for PRO. Overall CON gave the cleaner, more consistently reinforced reason to vote for its side.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 ([B4.1]-[B4.4]), which reframes "central" as a functional/system role rather than an energy-share metric, and explicitly turns PRO's "every tool has limits" admission into support for CON's necessity claim — directly defusing PRO's portfolio argument.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO won a close debate by holding the motion’s actual burden: not whether nuclear can help in hard cases, but whether it remains necessary as a central strategy in most countries. CON built a stronger technical story about firm seasonal capacity, endowment-limited alternatives, and “centrality at the margin,” with memorable bridge/foundation framing. But it never closed the many→most leap beyond structural assertion, and it under-answered PRO’s distinction between an essential system function and an indispensable principal investment pathway. PRO conceded exceptions cleanly, kept treating portfolios rather than single-tool failures as the reliability standard, and crystallized that gap-filling does not equal centrality. CON’s rhetoric was slightly sharper; PRO’s comparative path to the written motion was cleaner. Side B’s clipped R1 was a real cost but not enough to overturn the burden win.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5–B5.1: even necessary firm capacity at the margin is not automatically a central national decarbonization strategy; CON’s bridge analogy fails once material ≠ function, and hard cases still do not equal most countries.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO (B) sustained the winning distinction: firm capacity need does not equal nuclear centrality. CON showed nuclear is valuable as a scalable firm anchor in land-scarce, isolated, low-sun systems but never demonstrated that those conditions define most countries, a gap B pressed in Q2 and crystallized in R2/closing. B's portfolio logic that hydro, interconnection, storage, flexibility need not individually replace nuclear but can divide the task, plus delivery-risk and opportunity-cost weighing, left CON's bridge/foundation redefinition as asserted centrality rather than proven necessity for most national pathways.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.5-B4.6 – arguing that covering a marginal reliability gap does not make nuclear a central pillar and that CON moves from 'many' constrained cases to 'most' by accumulation rather than evidence, which directly neutralized CON's core necessity claim.
