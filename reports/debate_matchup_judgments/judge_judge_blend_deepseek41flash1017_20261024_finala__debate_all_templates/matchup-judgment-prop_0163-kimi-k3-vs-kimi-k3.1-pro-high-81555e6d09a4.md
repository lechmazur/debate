### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): PRO built a consistent comparative frame from the opening onward—certain, repeating, unaccountable malaria deaths versus bounded, staged, monitorable trial risk—and held that frame through every round. Its pressure question (what can be produced without an outdoor trial) forced CON into an answer ("non-drive field studies," contained tests, pre-designated regulators) that PRO then used decisively in closing to argue CON's own standard already concedes outdoor field work, collapsing the "block vs. permit" distinction CON needed to maintain. CON's irreversibility argument (self-limiting data doesn't validate persistent-drive reversibility, recall is impossible after dispersal) was the strongest material in the debate and was never fully neutralized, but CON's closing mostly re-asserted the distinction between "non-drive studies" and gene-drive release without squarely explaining why that distinction survives PRO's framing of self-limiting drives as the motion's legitimate first phase. CON's opening was also clipped before completing its burden statement, a real execution cost. PRO's answers to CON's pressure questions (naming Burkina Faso's regulator, Cartagena Protocol, insecticide/swamping countermeasures) were reasonably grounded and not overclaimed, giving PRO's "gating" mechanism more concrete texture than CON's abstract demand for "credible remediation." Overall PRO made the clash easier to decide and left the cleaner final reason to vote for it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A3.1's pressure question forcing CON to name what containment evidence could be produced without an outdoor trial, and CON's B4.1 answer invoking "non-drive field studies"—which PRO then weaponized in closing (A5.1) to argue CON had effectively conceded the motion's first phase.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3 (PRO)): B won a competitive debate by showing that phased, gated outdoor trials are necessary to learn what confined research cannot and do not entail automatic approval of persistent drives. A compellingly established that monitoring, liability, and stopping later phases cannot recall an escaped drive, but never made the ecological harm concrete enough to outweigh malaria’s certain burden and the value of regulated learning. A’s clipped opening and B’s cleaner status-quo comparison also mattered, though B overstated how directly blocking early trials causes present deaths.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s sequencing response was decisive: self-limiting trials need not prove persistent drives reversible; they can test dispersal, monitoring, and regulatory capacity before a later gate. A sharply replied that stopping escalation is not recalling escaped organisms, but did not show why that residual risk defeats all tightly regulated outdoor trials.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side A won by successfully framing the status quo as an irreversible, unaccountable catastrophe and exposing Side B's demand for pre-trial certainty as a permanent block. Side A's phased approach effectively neutralized the "recall" argument, and A's closing sharply capitalized on Side B's concession to "non-drive field studies" to show that the block was untenable. Side B's arguments on cross-border consent and lack of perfect retrieval were strong but failed to outweigh A's compelling comparative weighing of bounded risk against certain mass death.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.3-A4.4), which provided concrete regulatory and physical mechanisms for containment (Cartagena Protocol, swarming with non-drive males) and sharply contrasted the demand for hypothetical accountability with the total lack of accountability for the ongoing malaria deaths.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): B built a more disciplined and ultimately more damaging case. B's opening pre-empted PRO's framework by exposing the containment/recall problem and the capacity mismatch (the exact high-burden states named by the motion tend to have the weakest regulatory capacity), then sustained that pressure throughout. The decisive moment was B's pressure question forcing A to answer, in required A1/A2 format, "No guaranteed removal mechanism exists" — a major concession B then weaponized in every subsequent speech, arguing that all of PRO's safeguards (stopping criteria, surveillance, staged releases) govern the regulator but never the self-propagating organism itself. B also defused PRO's strongest rhetorical weapon — that CON's standard is an unfalsifiable permanent ban — by offering a concrete, falsifiable containment bar (self-exhausting/reversible drive designs validated in cage studies), directly answering A's own pressure question. A's closing attempt to spin this concession as vindicating PRO's "conditional permission" framework was cleverly met by B's closing, which pointed out that PRO's safeguards (regional consent, enforcement capacity, self-limiting design) appear nowhere in the motion as written and effectively concede that the proposition fails in its stated form. A's case was further weakened by two word-limit violations that clipped its opening and second rebuttal, an execution cost that lost potential content and signaled less disciplined delivery. Overall B answered the harder pressure more squarely, narrowed the dispute more effectively, and closed with the cleaner, more memorable framing.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's Rebuttal 2 answer to A's pressure question — offering self-exhausting/reversible drive designs as the evidence standard that would satisfy CON — simultaneously defused PRO's "unfalsifiable ban" charge and set up B's closing argument that today's self-propagating drives, the actual subject of the motion, still fail that bar.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): B won narrowly by showing that the motion supports conditional permission for qualifying trials, not mandatory release of every self-propagating design in every high-burden country. A powerfully established irreversibility and exposed weak stopping criteria, but ultimately conceded that limited designs, credible containment, and regional consent could make a trial acceptable. B correctly placed those safeguards within “tightly regulated,” while A’s claim that the motion necessarily concerns only today’s unrestricted self-propagating drives was not established. B’s two clipped turns reduced the margin.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s closing turn on A’s concession was decisive: if A would allow a self-limiting, credibly contained, regionally approved gene-drive trial, then A had justified strict qualification rather than the proposition’s blanket blocking alternative.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): Side B wins by decisively dismantling Side A's core safety mechanism. Side A relies on "tightly regulated" trials with "stopping criteria" to mitigate risk, but Side B lands a fatal logical blow: stopping criteria halt the regulator, not the mosquito. Side B further exploits Side A's concessions regarding the need for "regional approval" and "high regulatory capacity," pointing out that these conditions are neither in the motion nor characteristic of the "high-burden" countries the motion targets. Side B's distinction between self-propagating drives and self-exhausting designs cleanly answers Side A's pressure questions without conceding the motion. Side A's case ultimately collapses into a defense of a heavily amended proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's recurring argument that "stopping criteria stop the regulator, not the mosquito," which exposes the physical reality of gene drives against Side A's regulatory fiction, alongside the introduction of self-exhausting daisy-chain drives to answer the containment pressure question.
