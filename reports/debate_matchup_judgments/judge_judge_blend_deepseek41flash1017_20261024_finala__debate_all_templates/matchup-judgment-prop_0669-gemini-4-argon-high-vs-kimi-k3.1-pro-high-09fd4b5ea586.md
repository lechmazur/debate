CON (A) scored real points by showing PRO's distinction can collapse into mere relabeling ("plan" vs "statement"), and by stressing the motion's textual breadth, but its own alternative (narrow ban plus "independent review" and "transparent job-related criteria") was shown via PRO's pressure question and B2/B4 rebuttals to require the same discretionary line-drawing while opening a wider loophole for disguised ideological screening. CON's opening was also clipped for exceeding the word limit, costing it some final framing. Both answered pressure questions in proper A1/A2 format, but PRO's answers were tighter and more directly resolved the live tension, leaving PRO with the cleaner, more defensible path to the motion as written.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Both sides fought a genuinely sophisticated clash over whether "mission-defined work" and "ideological belief" can be separated. CON's strongest move was showing that mission-driven research and plans (health-disparities mentoring, climate adaptation) often embed contested normative premises, making PRO's "work vs. creed" line seem porous. But PRO's distinction—execute the task without swearing allegiance to its political rationale—survived the pressure exchange better: PRO's A1/A2 answers (B4) cleanly refined the line (refusal to submit necessary evidence of performance is disqualifying; refusal to personally endorse the rationale is not), and PRO's closing turned CON's own "conceptually defective" concession back on them to show the exception is narrow (scientific incoherence) rather than proof the whole distinction collapses. CON's final rebuttals and closing largely re-asserted "the distinction collapses" without substantially advancing beyond the earlier version of that claim, which reads as repetition rather than progression. CON also never fully answered PRO's capture-of-governance question (Q2) beyond gesturing at "pluralism." PRO's case is weakened somewhat by two clipped turns that cut off the end of its opening and first rebuttal, an execution cost that narrows the gap, but PRO's core framework proved more resilient under direct questioning and was defended with sharper, more decisive rhetoric in the final exchanges.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's closing (B5.2) turning its own "conceptually defective" concession into a narrow scholarly-fitness exception, directly rebutting CON's attempt (A5.1-A5.2) to use that concession as proof the work/ideology line is illusory, was the most decisive exchange of the debate.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A won by keeping a coherent decision rule live through the full exchange: public universities may define and assess the work (plans, competence, scholarly fit) without making personal agreement the price of opportunity. B’s strongest pressure—that plans inherently encode contested premises, so the line collapses and invites litigation—was real and well pressed, but never showed why inner alignment adds legitimate evaluative value once performance of the task remains fully assessable, nor a durable limiting principle beyond trusting the same majorities A warned about. A answered pressure questions directly, held the conduct/belief cut, and crystallized the graver injury as durable coercive machinery rather than hard boundary cases.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 answers (A1/A2) plus closing: excellent plan/service while rejecting political rationale stays eligible; denying the phenomenon under study is scholarly unfitness, not loyalty enforcement—exposing B’s equivocation without abandoning mission-driven evaluation.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO maintained a consistent work-vs-creed distinction: requiring evidence of how you will do the job vs requiring affirmation of belief. That directly answered CON's prospective-hiring and mission-coherence examples, and CON's own answers conceded it - A4.1 said no exclusion for politics unless it undermines the task, which is PRO's rule. CON's collapse claim that designing a plan equals adopting ideology was not sustained, and its litigation/paralysis warning was met by B4.3 review of wording, not mere accusation. B's pressure Q2 on majority relabeling orthodoxy as premise was never answered beyond pluralism.
