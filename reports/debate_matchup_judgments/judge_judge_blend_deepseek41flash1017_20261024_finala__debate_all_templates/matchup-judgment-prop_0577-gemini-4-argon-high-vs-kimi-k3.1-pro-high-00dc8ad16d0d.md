### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Both sides built coherent, plausible causal stories without fake sourcing, and both handled the Q&A format correctly with clear A1/A2 labeling. The decisive asymmetry was PRO's repeated point that border enforcement does nothing about people who are already inside the country irregularly—whether via overstay of existing legal entry or absconding workers—leaving an entire channel of irregular migration completely untouched, while pathways at least partially realign incentives for exactly that population through renewable lawful access. CON's rebuttal to this (that enforcement "tightens port-of-entry screening against overstay risks") was vague and never explained a mechanism, whereas CON's own strongest weapon against PRO—the wage-gap/overstay critique of seasonal visas—cuts both ways and was never fully neutralized because CON's own policy has no answer to the overstay problem either. PRO exploited this asymmetry explicitly (A4.2) and CON's closing still didn't resolve it, instead reasserting the "pricing out" mechanism without addressing inland demand. CON's network-effects/financing-relatives argument was a creative addition but remained speculative, and PRO correctly placed the burden back on CON to show it exceeds displaced crossings, which CON never did with more than assertion. This makes PRO's comparative framing ("greater net reduction, not abolition") the cleaner lens for resolving the motion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's point that border enforcement leaves the entire overstay/already-inside channel of irregular migration untouched (A1.5, reinforced at A4.2), which CON answered only vaguely via unexplained "port-of-entry screening" claims, versus CON's parallel critique that seasonal workers would overstay for better wages anyway—leaving both sides with an unresolved wage-gap problem, but only PRO's mechanism offers even a partial incentive-based answer to it.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by framing the motion as a marginal net comparison and consistently showing that pathways target the labor demand, repeat movement, and inland employment that border measures leave intact. Side A's deterrence case was plausible, but its decisive overstay, selection, and family-financing claims remained speculative, while its claim that pathways surrender the perimeter ignored that existing enforcement remains. B's clipped opening was an execution cost, but its later adaptation and weighing overcame it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B4.1-B4.5 was decisive: B directly answered both pressure questions, conceded some absconding without conceding the comparison, and exposed A's asymmetry of treating every visa imperfection as fatal while assuming enforcement adaptation and residual demand away.

PRO's substitution story was damaged by selection bias [A2.1] and the financing mechanism where legal wages loosen the cash constraint for relatives previously priced out [A4.4]. PRO conceded nothing physically stops absconding [B4.1] and relied on workplace penalties [B2.6], importing interior enforcement. Its re-entry incentive arithmetic (4 months vs 12 months informal work) never overcame CON's budget-constraint pricing-out argument, and B's clipped opening was an execution cost. Border deterrence covering the whole pool was the cleaner path to reduction.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's selection-bias + remittance-financed smuggling rebuttal (A2.1 / A4.4) that legal visas go to risk-averse workers who would not have crossed while funding new crossings, turning PRO's "possible spillover" defense into an unanswered net increase.
