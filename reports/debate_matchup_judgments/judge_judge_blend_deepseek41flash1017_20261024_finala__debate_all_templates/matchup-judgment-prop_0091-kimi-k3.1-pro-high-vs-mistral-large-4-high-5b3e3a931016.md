### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): B won by exposing A’s central unsupported leap from owning installed components to owning every functionality those components can support. B preserved protections against retroactive paywalls and dangerous disablement while showing that A’s broader rule could eliminate temporary activation choices or raise upfront costs. A was rhetorically forceful but repeatedly assumed that paying for physical hardware necessarily purchased premium activation.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the safety argument was decisive: prohibit billing-based interference with essential operation and require resilient local functionality, rather than treating the risk of bricking as justification for permanently unlocking every optional feature.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash clearer: the sale is of a disclosed configuration, not every latent use of installed parts. CON repeatedly forced PRO’s double-charging/property claim into a circular assumption that physical presence equals prepaid maximum functionality, while offering a narrower rule (no retroactive paywalling of included features; no bricking of essentials) that answered real harms without abolishing temporary activation or shared-hardware flexibility. PRO’s ownership rhetoric and house/toaster analogies stayed vivid but never crossed that gap, and the seasonal-user/choice cost of the motion remained under-answered. Clipped turns docked both sides slightly, but CON’s rebuttals and closings narrowed and resolved the dispute more cleanly.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s sustained capacity-vs-entitlement reply to “double-charging” (especially A2/A4), showing zero marginal activation cost and common hardware do not prove the buyer already purchased premium functionality, and that bricking fears justify bans on disablement rather than permanent unlock of every optional feature.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Mistral Large 4 (high) (PRO)): CON won the core clash on entitlement. PRO never overcame the configuration-vs-parts distinction: common hardware lowering build costs does not prove the base buyer paid for premium activation. CON's seasonal-user example and price-push argument showed a blanket mandate removes choice, while CON's essential-function isolation directly neutralized PRO's safety escalation. PRO repeated double-charging and unconscionability without answering that targeted bans on retroactive paywalling and bricking solve the harms.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Rebuttal 1 and 2 from CON: zero marginal activation does not equal already purchased activation, and safety risk is answered by banning billing-based disablement rather than mandating permanent unlock of every comfort feature.
