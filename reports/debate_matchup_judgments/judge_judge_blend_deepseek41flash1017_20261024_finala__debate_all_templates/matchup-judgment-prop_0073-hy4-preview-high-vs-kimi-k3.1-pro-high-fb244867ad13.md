### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a consistent, escalating case around measurability and fungibility, culminating in a sharp dilemma: any cap/review mechanism on revenue sharing either collapses into revisable public investment (CON's own position) or remains an open-ended, unmeasured lien. This dilemma was pressed in Pressure Questions and never fully dissolved by PRO, whose closing answer ("standing" distinguishes negotiated obligations from ordinary appropriations) asserted a distinction without demonstrating how it escapes the dilemma's horns. PRO (B) had strong moral framing — especially the "who bears the cost of uncertainty" reframe and the zero-vs-everything asymmetry point — and offered a flexible remedy menu (return/sharing/equivalent restitution) that honestly engaged CON's administrability worries. But PRO conceded ground by relying on "negotiated proxies" and "caps," which CON then weaponized into the central dilemma, and PRO's closing never cleanly closed that gap. PRO also suffered two clipped speeches (opening and Rebuttal 1), a real execution cost per the judging frame, even though the clipped portions appear to be near the end and likely summary material. On balance, CON's rebuttal chain was tighter and better sustained across rounds, and its final thesis ("restitution that cannot be measured or administered cannot be just") was never squarely defeated.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's cap dilemma in A2.2/A4.2 ("if revisable, it's public investment—CON's position; if not, it's a perpetual mortgage") was the most decisive exchange: B's attempted answer (community "standing" as the distinguishing feature, B4.3/B5.3) asserted a middle ground but never explained how a negotiated-but-enforceable obligation escapes collapsing into one of CON's two horns, leaving CON's framing as the operative lens for the rest of the debate.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by showing that uncertainty in valuation does not justify treating total retention as the neutral baseline. Its identifiable-community and demonstrable-benefit threshold, credit for later improvements, and flexible choice among return, bounded sharing, and equivalent restitution answered the strongest administrability and public-service concerns. Side B raised real fiscal tradeoffs but repeatedly assumed that benefits were unmeasurable and overstated a false choice between revocable aid and a perpetual mortgage. Side A’s clipped turns were an execution cost, but its core case remained clear.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest response was that capped, enforceable restitution can be bounded without becoming discretionary aid, while exact historical accounting is unnecessary where rents, resource income, or asset value remain demonstrable. This undercut Side B’s central dilemma and its claim that imperfect measurement requires zero restitution.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): Side A won by effectively neutralizing Side B's two primary arguments: measurement and displacement. Side A successfully argued that valuation difficulty should not default to 100% retention by the beneficiary, and correctly pointed out that Side B's alternative (present-need funding) is subject to the exact same fungibility and budget tradeoffs as restitution.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Turn 7 rebuttal neutralizing the displacement/fungibility argument by pointing out that CON's proposed tax-funded investments expose public budgets to the exact same fiscal tradeoffs, while leaving the community without standing.
