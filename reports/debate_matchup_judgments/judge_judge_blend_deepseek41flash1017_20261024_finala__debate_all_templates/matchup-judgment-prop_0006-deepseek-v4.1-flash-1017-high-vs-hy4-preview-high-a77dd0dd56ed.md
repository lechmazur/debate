### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): The debate turned on whether "active waiting time" could be defined narrowly enough to track genuine platform control without either (a) collapsing into existing on-call law (redundant) or (b) sweeping in free, hedgeable time (overbroad). PRO built a strong opening frame (control-based wage floor, the "$6-for-an-hour" courier) but in Rebuttal 1 and the Pressure Questions exchange was forced to concede that a courier who can freely multi-app or log off is NOT counted as active waiting — which gutted its own paradigm example. CON exploited this concession precisely in Rebuttal 2 and Closing, reframing the motivating case as a "mirage": either the courier is already restrained (and thus already covered by existing control-test law, making the motion redundant) or she is free to hedge (making the motion overbroad). PRO's counter — that compliance calculation must make the control standard explicit even if substantively it already exists — was a reasonable pivot but came late, narrowed PRO's original claim, and was cleanly answered by CON's "enforce it, don't rewrite the metric" line. CON also sustained a coherent "flexibility vs. control" throughline and used the final-speech advantage effectively to crystallize the dilemma. PRO never fully recovered the ground lost when it had to retreat from its own motivating example.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 (B4.1–B4.4), which used PRO's own Pressure-Question answer (A4.1, conceding that a courier able to hedge via a rival app is not "active waiting") to show the opening's signature $6/45-minute example falls outside PRO's own definition, making the proposed rule either redundant or overbroad — a trap PRO never fully escaped.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): CON won by forcing a live structural dilemma PRO never escaped: once “active waiting” requires genuine control/penalty, the $6-plus-45-minute selling case is either free choice (not compensable) or already covered by on-call/control rules (redundant); task-only arithmetic pressure was met by a coherent per-task-plus-exit-protection alternative. PRO kept a strong control/overrecruitment frame and fair pressure on hourly shortfalls, but narrowing the definition neutralized the motion’s distinctive fix without a stable middle between redundancy and overbreadth, while flexibility backfire remained a live cost PRO treated as the floor “working.”
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A’s Rebuttal 2 / closing on B4.1: concede free multi-app is not compensable, then show the motion’s emblem collapses into either existing control law or free speculative idle time.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (CON)): CON won by exposing a central tension in PRO's case: PRO's own definition of "active waiting" excluded their emblematic $6-courier example if she could freely multi-app or log off, forcing PRO to retreat to workers already covered by existing on-call law or separate abuse bans. PRO never established a distinct, large middle class that was both genuinely controlled and not already compensable. CON's alternative—per-task floors plus banning exit penalties—offered a cleaner remedy. While PRO's closing point that task-only accounting obscures control had force, CON's redundancy-or-overbreadth dilemma proved more decisive in showing the motion fixed no unique harm while trading flexibility for managed availability.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: In Rebuttal 2, CON demonstrated that PRO's central $6-delivery-plus-45-minute-wait scenario self-destructed under PRO's own definition: if the courier could freely open a rival app, PRO conceded the gap was not compensable, making the rule either redundant for already-restrained workers or overbroad for free ones. This dilemma gutted PRO's harm story and crystallized the debate around an unresolved definicial trap.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): A built a tighter, more sustained case: a concrete arithmetic example ($7.50/hr effective rate) anchored the stakes throughout, and A's narrowing of "active waiting" to platform-verifiable control signals (zone enforcement, penalties, response windows) directly answered CON's administrability and surveillance objections without abandoning the motion. In Pressure Questions, A's queries forced B into a key concession ("four hours of required readiness is control and must be paid as work"), and A's closing exploited this cleanly: if CON agrees standby control must be paid, the only live dispute is whether platforms must account for it by default (PRO) or workers must first win a status fight (CON) — framed as a "lawsuit tax" versus a floor. CON's strongest material was the labeling/self-definition critique and the multi-apping puzzle, both of which landed real hits, but CON's final move — reducing the motion to "this is just control law, so it's redundant or dangerous" — reads more as a reframe than a refutation, since it doesn't fully displace PRO's point that the motion shifts evidentiary burden onto platforms using telemetry they already hold. CON never fully closed the loop on why using existing dispatch/penalty records to test substance-over-label is unworkable, leaving PRO's mechanism as the more complete, if imperfect, answer.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Q1 pressure question forcing B's concession in [B4.3] that genuinely constrained standby time "is control and must be paid as work" — which A then weaponized in closing to reframe the entire debate as "PRO's default accounting rule vs. CON's worker-litigation burden."
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO)): CON won by making the decisive clash the motion’s trigger, not the moral claim that constrained standby can be unpaid. PRO’s fairness case on reserve labor and the $7.50 arithmetic stayed live and sharp, and pressure on transparency was strong. But once PRO narrowed “active waiting” to zones, penalties, and exclusive readiness, CON’s dilemma stuck: the rule either collapses into ordinary control/status law (and is largely redundant) or stays broader and hands platforms a gamed, access-rationing label. PRO’s burden-shift reply never fully escaped that structural fork, so CON gave the cleaner reason to reject the proposition as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s rebuttal 2 dilemma: PRO’s narrowed active-waiting either equals control law or recreates platform-defined queue/surveillance/rationing problems.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Tencent Hy4 Preview (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): PRO wins because CON conceded in Rebuttal 2 that genuinely controlled standby time must be paid as work, collapsing the dispute to mechanism. PRO successfully framed the motion as a practical default accounting rule that uses existing platform telemetry to shift the burden off workers, avoiding the lawsuit tax of case-by-case employment status litigation. PRO also blunted CON's key administrability objections with coherent substance-over-label and exclusivity tests, keeping the arithmetic unfairness of the core example live. CON's redundancy dilemma was sharp but could not overcome the concession on substance or PRO's burden-shifting closure.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 2 (A4) answering CON's pressure questions on relabeling and multi-apping with the "substance over labels" and "exclusivity" tests, which defanged CON's strongest administrability objections and preserved PRO's route to victory.
