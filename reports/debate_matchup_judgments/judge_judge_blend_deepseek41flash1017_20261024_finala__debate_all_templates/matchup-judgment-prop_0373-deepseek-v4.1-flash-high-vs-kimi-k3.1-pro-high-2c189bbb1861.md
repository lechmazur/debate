### Judge Notes

- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON (A) won a competitive burden fight: the motion is a verdict that the practice has gone too far, and PRO repeatedly fell back to least-restrictive review, relocation, and appeals—which A correctly framed as doing challenges well, not proof the institution is excessive. B landed the sharper moral frame (age fit ≠ community-wide erasure; hidden student losses; incentives skew to over-removal) and forced useful concessions, but never converted mechanism-plus-examples into a decisive pattern showing the normal request/review architecture routinely collapses. A's hospital/zoning and architecture-of-practice replies kept “some bad removals” from equaling “gone too far,” and B's clipped opening slightly blunted PRO's launch. Calibration and better process ended as shared ground that favors rejecting the motion more than affirming it.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's R2/closing burden flip: PRO's answers accept narrow removal and prescribe least-restrictive review, so they prove appeals and calibration—not that challenges/removals as a practice have gone too far.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): PRO won by keeping the proposition intact: age-inappropriateness justifies calibration (relocation/restriction/permission), not blanket erasure. CON defended an ideal review process it ultimately shares with PRO, conceded that removing an abuse memoir from every shelf is bad and that where older readers are served relocation is right, and never defended current incentive toward removal. Pressure Q2 forced that concession, while CON's demand for routine statistical proof misstates the burden in a closed-book debate where mechanism plus overbreadth counts. Clipped opening is a cost but did not erase PRO's live route.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 + Pressure Q2 on blanket removal vs relocation: A argued a book unsuitable at 12 but valuable at 17 has no legitimate reason for all-ages removal, forcing CON in R2 to agree blanket removal is wrong when older readers are served, conceding the core excess claim.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON)): Side A wins by effectively distinguishing between calibrated curation (relocation/restriction) and blanket erasure, successfully arguing that the current incentives and outcomes of book challenges have exceeded their legitimate purpose. Side B relied too heavily on an idealized, semantic definition of "challenges" as mere requests for review, which Side A sharply rebutted by pointing out that the real-world practice involves political pressure and over-removal. Although Side A's opening was clipped, their subsequent speeches were exceptionally strong and decisively won the clash over what "too far" means in practice.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where they turned Side B's advocacy for "better review" into Side A's own remedy, effectively collapsing Side B's defense by showing that careful calibration is the alternative to the current excessive practice, not a defense of it.
