- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): CON (Side A) built a cleaner, more cumulative case. The pressure questions were decisive: A's Q1 forced PRO to admit the motion does not mandate majority loss on thin-demand blocks, and PRO's refusal to specify an enforceable access minimum left the "few marked spaces will be enough" claim under-supported. A's final rebuttal and closing then pressed the resulting dilemma hard — once PRO concedes context-by-context application, the motion's only remaining content is an unfalsifiable "default," which either collapses into the targeted reallocation A already accepts or imposes a blanket harm A already rebutted. PRO's answers (A4.1-A4.3) gestured at a default/application distinction but never cashed it out concretely, and PRO's closing largely repeated its "garage" framing rather than resolving the trap. PRO's opening was also clipped by the word limit, a real execution cost. Overall CON better identified, pressed, and exploited the decisive clash, giving the cleaner path to a verdict.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1 (A2.1-A2.2), which caught PRO's own concession that policy should vary "this block, this hour" and converted it into the central dilemma — targeted reallocation vs. an unjustified blanket presumption — that PRO never fully escaped in later rounds.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by distinguishing context-sensitive allocation from a standing presumption that most curb must remain parking.
