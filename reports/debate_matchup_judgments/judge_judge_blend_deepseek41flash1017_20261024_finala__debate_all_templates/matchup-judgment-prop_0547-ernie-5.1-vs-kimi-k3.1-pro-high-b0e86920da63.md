### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): PRO built a specific, repeatedly refined causal mechanism—"pause as instruction"—distinguishing algorithmic optimization's use of implicit, passive engagement signals (dwell time, re-watching) from chronological feeds' indifference to such signals. This distinction survived CON's central counter that "anxious teens actively seek distressing content under any architecture," because PRO consistently pointed out that active search differs from the system's exploitation of involuntary behavioral cues between deliberate choices (A2.1, A4.6)—a point CON never squarely rebutted, instead reasserting that selection effects explain everything. CON's strongest asset was the "double standard" critique on unmeasurability (B3.2, B4.3), and PRO's answer (A4.3) was serviceable but not airtight, conceding no study exists while insisting the design is merely "testable." Still, since neither side could cite real studies under the rules, the comparative weight falls on mechanism plausibility and responsiveness to cross-examination, where PRO's architecture-specific story held up better than CON's reliance on "it could go either way" and a somewhat weak closing example (cooking tutorial vs. weight-loss search) that didn't clearly rebut the asymmetry point. PRO's case is weakened by repeated word-limit clipping across three speeches, an execution cost, but the substantive content before each clip remained coherent and complete.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's repeated distinction between a teen's active search (same under any architecture) and an algorithm's exploitation of passive/implicit signals to intensify a theme (A2.1, A4.6) was the most decisive exchange—CON's selection-effects rebuttal in B4.2 never directly answered this distinction, leaving it live through the closing.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON)): Side A won by maintaining the crucial distinction between pre-existing selection and algorithmic amplification. Side B showed that distress can exist under either feed and correctly pressed A on net average effects, but repeatedly treated deliberate searching as equivalent to automatic, user-specific reinforcement. A also correctly distinguished sleep as a mediator from sleep as a confound. A did not fully prove that harms outweigh possible benefits, and repeated clipping was an execution cost, but B’s demand for isolating evidence was applied inconsistently and did not neutralize A’s comparative mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s strongest exchange answered the selection objection: vulnerability may explain the initial pause or search, but it does not explain why an optimized feed automatically repeats that theme between deliberate choices. This directly weakened Side B’s claim that the same loop exists under chronology.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Baidu Ernie 5.1 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won by establishing a superior causal mechanism. While Side A relied heavily on selection effects (teens seek distress) and demanded an isolating RCT, Side B neatly separated active seeking from passive amplification, proving that optimization turns a momentary pause into a continuous, personalized feedback loop. Side B also effectively corrected Side A's methodological confusion regarding sleep as a mediator rather than a confound.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 1 effectively neutralized Side A's selection-effect argument by clarifying that while vulnerability explains why a teen pauses, optimization explains the subsequent architectural amplification.
