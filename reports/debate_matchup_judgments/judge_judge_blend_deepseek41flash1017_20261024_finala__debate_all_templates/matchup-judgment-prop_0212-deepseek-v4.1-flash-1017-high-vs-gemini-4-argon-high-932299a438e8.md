### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-structured cases, but CON (Side A) landed the more damaging and sustained blow: it forced PRO to concede that redaction cannot hide an informant's identity in intimate contexts ("yes, a family may recognize who was in the kitchen") and then relentlessly exploited that concession, arguing PRO's fallback of "contextual warnings," "right of reply," and "counseling" cannot plausibly prevent immediate retaliation. CON also raised, and PRO never fully resolved, the forensic-access paradox—that proving a handler fabricated an accusation via "quota patterns" and "corroborating files" requires reading other citizens' private dossiers, which contradicts a model of broad public/community access. PRO's framing (burden should fall on secrecy, gatekeeping should be visible/contestable rather than discretionary) was rhetorically strong and conceptually clean, and its pressure questions exposed a real tension in CON's reliance on magistrates, but CON's answers (extracts limited to verified state actions, unverified accusations sealed) were adequate rebuttals that blunted the "laundering" charge. In the final exchange, CON's closing crystallized the unresolved retaliation and verification problems more sharply than PRO's closing addressed them, giving CON the cleaner path to victory.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's repeated exploitation of PRO's concession in [B2.4] ("yes, a family may recognize who was in the kitchen") across [A2.3], [A4.4], and [A5.2] was the most decisive thread, because PRO's mitigations (counseling, right of reply, contextual warnings) never convincingly answered the immediate retaliation risk that concession created.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): A narrowly won by distinguishing broad access from indiscriminate publication and showing why visible, appealable withholding is preferable to CON’s confidential system of certified extracts. B powerfully established risks from false accusations and intimate surveillance, but largely generalized from the hardest secret-police files to all colonial and wartime archives, while insufficiently answering historians’ need to test official narratives or the danger of invisible omissions. B’s malformed closing references were also a modest execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s strongest exchange exposed that contextual warnings and later corrections may not prevent retaliation against falsely named informants. A nevertheless preserved its broader route by showing that both models require gatekeepers, while only its model makes withholding presumptively temporary, visible, and contestable.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): CON successfully demonstrated that colonial, wartime, and secret-police archives are weaponized records built on fabrication, blackmail, and intimate surveillance, where redaction fails and broad access detonates horizontal violence among survivors. PRO's concession that families may recognize informants even with redaction [B2.4], combined with their inadequate "public correction process" response [B4.3], left their harm-mitigation underdeveloped. CON's alternative of targeted forensic inquiry by independent bodies delivered the proposition's benefits (grave locations, verified facts) without the uniquely dangerous exposure of raw intelligence files, and effectively neutralized PRO's gatekeeping critique by showing both models rely on oversight bodies—differing only in whether filtering happens before or after release. After the full exchange, CON's route to victory on the distinctive toxicity of these archives remained the clearer reason to reject broad opening.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's exposure of the "forensic trap" and the perversity of PRO's "public correction process"—showing that disproving fabricated accusations would require exposing dozens of private neighbor files, and that forcing falsely accused individuals to publicly prove innocence before an angry community revictimizes them—materially undermined PRO's "distributed scrutiny" and "mediated disclosure" defenses.

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO)): Both sides built coherent, well-supported cases, but the decisive exchange centered on the Stasi example and the "who adjudicates" question.
