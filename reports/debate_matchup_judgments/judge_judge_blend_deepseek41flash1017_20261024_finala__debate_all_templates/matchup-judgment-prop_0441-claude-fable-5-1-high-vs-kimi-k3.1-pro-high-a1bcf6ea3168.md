### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully shifting the burden of proof regarding the 'without increasing violent crime' clause. A demonstrated that Side B's metric (city-wide rates) was structurally blind to the behavior of the relatively small pretrial population, rendering B's evidence of 'no surge' unpersuasive. A also won the mechanical clash by explaining how risk assessments release first-time violent offenders that cash bail would have detained.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 (A4.1/A4.2) brilliantly turned B's admission about lacking a clean violent-rearrest series into a structural defeat, arguing that 'we looked where it couldn't show up and saw nothing' does not satisfy the affirmative burden.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON)): PRO kept the motion's two-prong comparison live: New Jersey shows substantial pretrial jail cut without a corresponding violent-crime surge, and wealth-based detention does not reliably incapacitate the dangerous. CON conceded no clean series shows violent crime rose, leaving only a generic incapacitation mechanism that ignores that cash bail also releases rich dangerous defendants. That left PRO's comparative mechanism as the cleaner path.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's Rebuttal 1 & 2 dismantling CON's use of overall rearrest and hypothetical moderate-score defendants - noting missed court dates and non-violent rearrest are not violent crime rates, and cash bail detains by wealth not risk - which neutralized CON's only proxy for the claimed increase.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Claude Fable 5.1 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A built a stronger, more concrete case by identifying the exact marginal defendant (a first-time violent offender with a moderate risk score) who would be released under risk assessment but detained under cash bail. Side A also effectively dismantled Side B's reliance on city-wide crime rates by explaining why such macro-metrics are statistically blind to the specific pretrial population, shifting the burden back to PRO to prove their affirmative claim. Side B's defense relied too heavily on an idealized version of the policy and struggled to answer the mechanical realities of how risk-assessment tools actually score defendants.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2, where it sharply contrasted the mechanical reality of cash bail scaling with charge severity against risk tools scaling with prior records, perfectly illustrating why the marginal released defendant is more dangerous under reform.
