Its benefit-card response substituted a different pricing mechanism rather than answering that case. Side B's repeated clipping also imposed an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's answer that an open markdown is not equivalent to a targeted discount was decisive: repricing every buyer can make the additional sale unviable. Side B never squarely explained why the transparent lower-price version should be prohibited, leaving the blanket-ban burden unmet.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side A won the core clash by successfully demonstrating that profit-maximizing algorithms will inevitably use personalization to raise the baseline anchor, turning theoretical "discounts" into disguised price hikes. A also decisively won the enforcement debate by pointing out that policing B's standards requires a public benchmark, which personalization destroys. A's continuous word-count clipping was an execution flaw, but A's arguments were so structurally devastating that they secured a clear victory regardless.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 2 contrast on enforcement, noting that auditing a ban involves a checkable input (WTP signals) whereas auditing "fairness" under B's model requires a non-existent benchmark.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): CON won the decisive overbreadth clash: the motion bans beneficial forty-and-thirty discounts, not only abusive uplifts. ### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won a close, competitive debate by presenting a narrower posted-price and discount-only alternative that addressed secret surcharges while preserving targeted discounts. Side B compellingly defended transparency and challenged strategic baseline inflation, but never fully established why discount-only personalization caused enough residual harm to justify the broader ban. Side A also exposed tension between allowing published behavior-based offers and banning willingness-to-pay inference. Its repeated clipping and overclaim that price sensitivity usually tracks need kept the margin modest.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it answered the baseline and auditing questions directly, then turned Side B’s enforcement objection by arguing that policing prices against a posted baseline is simpler than determining whether a coupon arose from a prohibited inference. It also leveraged Side B’s concession that a genuine below-baseline discount does not overcharge its recipient.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): Side B won by introducing a robust alternative (a public price ceiling with personalized discounts below it) that neutralized Side A's primary harms of extraction and markups. Side B effectively used pressure questions to force Side A to concede that discounts below a genuine posted price do not overcharge anyone, reducing Side A's remaining case to complaints about opacity and paperwork.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly turned Side A's auditing arguments against them, showing that policing Side A's distinction between a 'published rule' and a 'covert model' for the exact same cart-abandonment coupon would require the very model reconstruction Side A claimed was impossible.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Claude Opus 5.5 (high) (CON)): CON won by making the decisive comparison clearer: after a posted-price-only-below safeguard removes the surcharge harms PRO named, the ban’s remaining effect is to shrink or eliminate targeted discounts that price-sensitive shoppers rely on. PRO kept a coherent principle about covert vulnerability pricing and the sensitivity-need gap, and answered pressure cleanly, but never showed why that residual opacity/assignment concern outweighs CON’s narrower alternative and net consumer cost once markups are gone.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s cart-abandonment / published-rule dilemma and the follow-through that PRO’s A4.2 concession leaves the ban stopping discounts, not overcharges.
