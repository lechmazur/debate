### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): B identified the central tension in PRO's case early—that "joint command" and "formal intelligence-sharing/sensor integration" are distinct claims—and pressed it through sharp pressure questions forcing A into a dilemma: either the joint commander has independent authority (sovereignty crisis) or it doesn't (no speed gain beyond technical interoperability). A's answer (A4.1) conceded the commander has "no authority for unilateral intercepts," which B then correctly exploited in Rebuttal 2 and Closing to show the motion's most distinctive element—joint command—was never shown necessary, while A kept repeating "seconds matter" and NATO analogies without resolving the fork it had created for itself. B's case was logically tighter, better sustained the same throughline across rounds, and ended with a cleaner, more decision-relevant frame than A's closing, which largely recycled earlier claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's pressure question Q1 ("does your joint commander have independent authority or not?") and A's resulting concession in A4.1, which B then used in Rebuttal 2 (B4.3) to show PRO's own mechanisms require no joint command at all—this exchange effectively dismantled PRO's distinctive case for "joint command" as opposed to mere technical cooperation.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side B won by successfully separating the benefits of intelligence-sharing from the necessity of a joint command. By forcing Side A to concede that a joint commander would rely on "pre-approved plans" rather than unilateral authority, Side B cleanly demonstrated that the speed benefits could be achieved through technical integration without the political risks of a merged command.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's Rebuttal 2 brilliantly weaponized Side A's answer on command authority, pointing out that if speed comes from pre-approved plans rather than unilateral command, the joint command structure itself is unnecessary.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = Mistral Medium 3.5 (high) (PRO)): CON won by cleanly separating sensor/data fusion and pre-authorized plans from joint command itself, then forcing PRO’s concession that speed comes from the former (not unilateral authority). PRO never showed why the motion’s decisive addition was necessary once those tools were granted, while CON neutralized the “seconds or death” frame and the interdependence-as-enforceable claim with the 2019 GSOMIA experience. PRO kept strong threat salience but lost the necessity comparison that decides the written proposition.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A4.3 / A5.1: CON locking PRO’s own A1 answer (pre-approved plans + integrated sensors, no unilateral intercept) and showing every speed mechanism is available without a binational commander.
