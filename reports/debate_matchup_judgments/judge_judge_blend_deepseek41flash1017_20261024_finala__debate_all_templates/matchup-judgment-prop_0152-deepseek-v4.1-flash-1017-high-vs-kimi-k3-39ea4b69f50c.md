### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): CON (A) built a structural argument that proved durable across rounds: the motion's permission has practical content only against the unwilling, since the genuinely willing already comply without needing legal "room." PRO (B) never squarely neutralized this point—its Q1 answer conceded that a willing teacher's contract adds nothing but "teeth" for the day she changes her mind, which CON exploited decisively in Rebuttal 2 and Closing ("a commitment that survives only because refusal is priced was never commitment"). CON also sustained a clean asymmetry frame (who bears the cost of an erroneous judgment, and who must litigate it after the harm), which PRO's "who can correct" reframe in Closing did not fully answer, since CON showed the correction mechanism is itself an after-the-fact burden on the weaker party. PRO's defense that "voluntary" and "realistic alternatives" are self-enforcing textual conditions, not amendments, was plausible but asserted rather than mechanistically defended, leaving CON's "interpreter retained after the harm" rebuttal live. Both sides handled the Pressure Questions format correctly, but CON's answers landed more damage on PRO's central example than PRO's answers did on CON's.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Q1 in Pressure Questions ("does your ban forbid the school from agreeing with a willing teacher?") and PRO's concession that it does not, which CON then used in Rebuttal 2/Closing to show the permission only adds punitive teeth for a future change of mind—never substantively rebutted by PRO.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by showing that Side A’s refusal-must-be-costless standard effectively treats every enforceable commitment as coercive. The proposition already denies room where choice or alternatives are unreal, while still covering willing adults who join distinctive institutions. Side A’s post-harm enforcement concern was powerful and kept the debate competitive, but it did not establish that conditional voluntariness is impossible or that all contractual consequences are coercion.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s closing response to A4.3 was decisive: equating any cost of refusal with coercion collapses the distinction between voluntary institutional commitment and compulsion, turning Side A’s purported safeguard into a blanket ban.
- **Qwen 3.7 Max** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Side A built a decisive analytical trap regarding the practical meaning of "legal room." By demonstrating that genuine willingness requires no legal enforcement, and that enforcement mechanisms are only used against the unwilling or those who change their minds, Side A exposed a fatal paradox in the proposition's "voluntary" condition. Side A also effectively neutralized Side B's attempts to read affirmative duties ("must accommodate") into the text, framing them as admissions that the motion itself is merely permission with a disclaimer. Side B's arguments were competent and rhetorically polished, but failed to overcome Side A's rigorous dismantling of the motion's core mechanism and the asymmetry of enforcement burdens.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 (Turn 8), specifically A1 and A2, where they crisply answered the pressure questions by showing that a willing teacher needs no penalty clause, and defining the threshold for voluntariness as "refusal is free," which elegantly collapsed Side B's defense of costly consent.
