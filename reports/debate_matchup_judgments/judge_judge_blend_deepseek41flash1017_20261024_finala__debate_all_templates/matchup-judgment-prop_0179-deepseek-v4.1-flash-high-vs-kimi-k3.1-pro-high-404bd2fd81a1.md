### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by offering a precautionary regulatory alternative that blocks unproven or harmful farms without assuming humane commercial confinement is impossible. Side B persuasively established serious risks, weak benefits, and bad commercial incentives, but conceded that enrichment at scale is not logically impossible and then largely defined sufficiently enriched operations as non-industrial. Side A directly answered both pressure questions, exposed that definitional move, and showed that mandatory welfare costs could either prevent production or remove the alleged intrinsic cruelty. Side B also overstated Side A’s conditional discussion as a concession that no current design qualifies.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s second rebuttal was decisive: it supplied concrete pre-licensing standards and then turned Side B’s concession that welfare is merely “antagonistic to the business model” into the point that regulation can make those costs compulsory. That left Side B proving risk and incentive, but not the claimed inevitability of indefensible cruelty.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully proving that Side B's case relied on an economic forecast rather than an inherent biological impossibility. By forcing Side B to concede that humane scaling is merely 'antagonistic to the business model,' Side A cleanly demonstrated that strict regulation could either force humane conditions or make the industry uneconomic, achieving the welfare goals without requiring a preemptive global ban.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's Rebuttal 2 cleanly seized on Side B's concession that scale makes enrichment 'antagonistic to the business model' to prove that regulation can simply make welfare costs compulsory, rendering cruelty non-intrinsic.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (PRO)): CON won the inherent-vs-bad-design clash. PRO conceded in B4 that commercial scale does not make enrichment logically impossible, only antagonistic to the business model, and that boutique enclosures may work. That undercuts the motion's claim that industrial confinement is inherently indefensible cruelty. CON provided the cleaner alternative: enforceable outcome thresholds (individual housing, space/shelter/enrichment, feeding failure/inactivity/injury/mortality) with staged pilots and burden on applicants, showing a ban shares enforcement problems while an illicit tank is even less visible. PRO's luxury weighing and race-to-bottom concern is real but does not prove unavoidable cruelty.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 2 A1/A2 - answered B's demand for a concrete enforceable standard with audit thresholds and staged pilots, and exposed PRO's concession that enrichment is not impossible but merely costly, reframing cruelty as a regulable cost rather than an intrinsic property.

### Judge Notes

- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B built the stronger comparative case: octopuses have demanding behavioral needs that conflict with industrial standardization, while the product is dispensable and the welfare error potentially severe. Side A effectively exposed the ban’s breadth, but its humane premium-farm scenario remained a plausible blueprint rather than proof of acceptable welfare outcomes. B also answered the enforcement objection well by distinguishing illegal evasion from lawful migration to weak jurisdictions. Repeated clipping was an execution cost for B, but the visible arguments still resolved the central clash.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s response to the black-market objection was decisive: a global ban removes lawful low-standard destinations, while illegal farms remain detectable, costly, and forced to compete with wild supply. This substantially weakened A’s claim that prohibition necessarily creates a worse shadow market.
- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side A won by successfully maintaining that the burden for a universal ban requires proving all potential farming models (including high-welfare, low-density ones) are unworkable, a standard Side B conceded it could not meet. Side B's repeated word count clipping also represented a significant execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A's argument in Rebuttal 1 and 2 that if high-welfare standards make farming economically unviable, the result is simply no farming—a success for regulation, rather than a reason to ban the compliant version globally.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON won the burden fight. PRO needed to show industrial confinement is structurally indefensible for a global ban, but its own concessions (B5.3 – ban does not need to prove no benign tank) left the universal quantifier unproven. CON's rebuttals that a low-density, enriched, monitored premium operation is banned by the motion yet not shown cruel [A2.1][A4.1][A5.1] and that licensing with pilots/shutdown handles uncertainty better than symbolism answered the core pressure and left PRO relying on a burden shift.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's turn 4/8-10 attack on the universal ban: that raising costs to create a small premium farm is a regulatory success not a reason to ban it, and that PRO's admission it need not prove impossibility of a benign enclosure is fatal to a global prohibition.
