### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Both sides executed the format cleanly and engaged the central clash: is a "core function vs. commercial activity" line more administrable and less theologically entangling than a "necessity and tailoring" standard? PRO built a concrete, role-based test (duties of worship, doctrine, pastoral care vs. support roles), placed the burden of proof on the institution, and barred aggregation of exempt and non-exempt roles — answering the pressure questions directly and turning CON's "theology-free" claim back on CON by showing that "substantial burden" and "centrality" analysis is at least as theological as asking what a role actually does. CON's critique that no line is fully non-theological was conceded rather than refuted, and its alternative (burden + necessity + tailoring) never clearly demonstrated superior objectivity; its strongest move — the commercial doctrinal-editor example — was largely absorbed by PRO's prior concession that function, not nonprofit status, governs core activity, so it did not land as a decisive counterexample. PRO's closing crystallized the comparison more sharply: its test has an observable stopping point (what does the role do?) while CON's has none. That gave PRO the cleaner, more defensible path to the resolution as written.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: PRO's A2.3/A4.1 sequence — arguing that CON's "substantial burden/centrality" test requires more theological judgment than PRO's role-based function test — was the pivotal exchange; CON's B4.1 reply ("there is no wholly non-theological line") effectively conceded the point rather than refuting it, which weakened CON's central objection for the rest of the debate.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (CON); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO)): Side B won by clarifying that the proposition does not make profit or institutional form decisive: a commercial publisher's doctrinal editor can qualify while its sales staff do not. That answer neutralized Side A's main underprotection example and supplied a clearer limiting rule based on actual duties, individual decisions, and institutional burden of proof. Side A's necessity-and-tailoring alternative was thoughtful, but it remained compatible with the proposition's necessary limit and did not establish a concrete religious function that B's test wrongly excluded. Its closing attack also treated core-function status as automatic immunity, although the proposition says only when and does not preclude further tailoring.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's decisive response was that paid or commercially situated work can still be a core religious function, while exemptions remain role-specific. This absorbed Side A's doctrinal-editor example and showed why it supported, rather than refuted, the proposition.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (PRO); B = MiniMax-M3 (CON)): Side A won by defending a clearer, more administrable standard that better avoids judicial theology while keeping ordinary commercial activity under equality law. A showed that CON's necessity-and-tailoring test invites the very broad commercial immunity the proposition warns against, forcing courts to weigh religious centrality. A's pressure question exposed that CON could not name a concrete non-core commercial role (like barista or clerk) that should be exempt; CON's doctrinal-editor example actually fit A's core-function test and proved that commercial form does not automatically defeat protection. A answered B's pressure questions directly, explained that each role is assessed by actual duties, and showed that bundling is blocked because every challenged decision must independently meet the functional test. B's closing mischaracterized A's test as granting automatic immunity and never overcame the comparative advantage on administrability and limiting enterprise-wide claims.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: The exchange over theological entanglement and the pressure-question response on commercial roles, where CON conceded there is no wholly non-theological bright line and offered a doctrinal-editor example that backfired by illustrating rather than undermining PRO's core-function test.

### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): Both sides engaged the core clash directly and executed the Q&A format correctly, but PRO maintained the more coherent and motion-consistent framework. PRO's function-specific test (clergy, worship, doctrine instruction vs. commercial tasks) gave clear, consistently applied examples (chaplain vs. cafeteria worker, editor vs. shelf-stocker) and repeatedly turned CON's own bookstore/thrift-shop hypotheticals against it, showing CON's "genuinely religious plus mission-connected plus notice" test would license exactly the broad commercial discrimination the motion seeks to prevent. CON's strongest moment was forcing the admission (A4.3) that a "cohesive community" rationale could justify broader exemptions, which CON correctly flagged as dissolving PRO's promised tightness (B4.4) — a real and damaging hit that PRO's closing did not fully neutralize beyond reasserting discipline. However, CON's own test required the same kind of line-drawing it accused PRO of (distinguishing "integrated communal practice" from "ordinary commercial exclusion"), effectively conceding the commercial/core distinction the motion demands, which undercuts CON's claim that the dichotomy is illegitimate rather than merely difficult. On balance PRO offered the more defensible, operational reading of the motion and more directly exposed the practical danger of CON's alternative, giving it a modest but clear edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's B4.4 catching PRO's concession that "cohesive community" ministry could justify broader exemptions (dissolving the "tight" line) was the single sharpest blow, though PRO's repeated turn of CON's own bookstore/thrift-shop hypotheticals into evidence that CON's test licenses broad commercial discrimination was the more sustained and ultimately decisive exchange.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON); B = MiniMax-M3 (PRO)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by preserving both sides of the motion: strong autonomy for genuinely religious functions and equal treatment in ordinary commerce. Side A compellingly exposed difficult boundary cases and risks of judicial entanglement, but its alternative remained too permissive. Genuine religious identity, mission connection, notice, and consistency do not explain why discriminatory commercial hiring becomes acceptable. B also showed that practice-specific review need not decide theological truth and can accommodate integrated ministry when a tailored necessity showing is made.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B’s most decisive response was that notice and consistency cannot transform discrimination into equality, while A’s mission-connection test could recast shelf-stocking, cafeteria work, and routine sales hiring as ministry. A’s claim that B’s cohesive-community concession dissolved the line was sharp, but B had already limited that possibility through direct connection, necessity, and tailoring.
- **Kimi K2.6** (A = MiniMax-M3 (PRO); B = DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) (CON)): PRO defended the tighter, more principled distinction by showing that anti-discrimination law should govern commercial activity while core religious functions receive narrow exemptions. CON’s strongest criticism—that functional line-drawing entangles courts in theology—was blunted because CON never explained why its own alternative of asking whether an institution is “genuinely religious” and a practice is “connected to mission” would avoid equally serious entanglement while not simply licensing broad commercial discrimination. PRO consistently exposed that CON’s test makes institutional identity decisive, allowing a religious label to sanitize ordinary hiring and sales decisions. Although CON landed memorable lines and pressed hard on hard cases like Catholic schools and hospital communities, PRO adequately answered that the inquiry targets the worker’s task and necessity to a legislatively defined function, not the truth of any doctrine. The debate remained competitive, but PRO left the cleaner comparative case intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s Rebuttal 2 (B4) reframed PRO’s answer that a “tailored rule” may be justified when community formation is the core ministry as a concession that the functional line dissolves into the very mission-based inquiry CON advocates. This was the sharpest tactical turn in the exchange, even if it slightly overstated the concession.
