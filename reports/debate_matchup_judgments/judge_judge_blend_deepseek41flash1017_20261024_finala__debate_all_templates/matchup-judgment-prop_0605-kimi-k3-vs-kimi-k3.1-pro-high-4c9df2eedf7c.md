### Judge Notes

- **Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): Side B won by exposing a critical flaw in Side A's "reformed sponsorship" alternative. While A argued that automatic transfers for wage theft would safely remove the employer's veto, B astutely pointed out that A's model still required a worker facing physical violence or threats to secure a replacement sponsor before leaving—a near impossibility while trapped. A's inability to resolve non-wage abuse under their framework made B's case for unconditional portability decisively stronger.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B's closing observation that Side A's "conditional release" preserves the exact leverage that makes kafala dangerous, as a worker facing violence cannot safely wait out a contract or interview for a new sponsor while in custody.
- **Grok 4.5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3 (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON built the cleaner path to decide the motion as written: after PRO conceded mobility is not a substitute for enforcement, CON forced a unique-benefit/unique-cost ledger—exit-without-veto is the only gain truly attributed to portability, while unattachment, capacity stretch for between-jobs workers, and admission contraction are distinct costs. PRO kept a strong rights core (who must say yes; sponsor conflict of interest) and a sharp “if you erase the veto you adopt the motion” strike, but never fully discharged the accountability gap for tens of millions of migrants or made the border-cost dismissal stick. CON’s automatic payroll-triggered transfer plus continuous named attachment offered a concrete alternative that captured the live harm without full replacement. Clipped PRO turns cost some crystallisation; CON’s progression and weighing made the net policy choice easier to reject.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON’s R1–R2 use of PRO’s “mobility is not a substitute for enforcement” concession to collapse portability’s unique fix to the veto alone, then re-score that against abandonment and admission costs—forcing PRO onto a narrow gain PRO could not outweigh.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3 (CON)): PRO kept the decisive clash on who must say yes to leave. CON's reformed model still requires contract expiry, proven theft, or a new sponsor before release, preserving a gatekeeper. PRO showed portability makes existing enforcement usable, answers the housing/repatriation gap with state-administered job-search period and enforceable wage claims, and exposes CON's admission-reduction claim as speculative economics versus certain present employer veto. CON's capacity and accountability arguments are serious but do not neutralize that core harm.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A R2 A1-A2: portability ends the consent requirement itself as an independent abuse; conditional automatic transfer still traps non-wage abuse and domestic workers, and if reform truly removes employer veto and puts status in worker's hands it adopts the motion.
