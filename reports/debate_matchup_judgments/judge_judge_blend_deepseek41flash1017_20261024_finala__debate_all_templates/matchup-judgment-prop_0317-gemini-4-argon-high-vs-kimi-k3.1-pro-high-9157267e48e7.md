B's case was also weakened procedurally: both its opening and first rebuttal were clipped for exceeding word limits, meaning key late content (affordability point, full barcode rebuttal) never reached the judge, a real execution cost per the judging frame. On balance, A's mechanism and moral framing were clearer and more consistently pressed, giving it a clear but competitive edge.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's answer to B's "nurse/lottery" pressure question (A4.2–A4.3), reframing the choice as a real chance at a $100 ticket versus guaranteed exclusion by a $600 markup, was the most decisive exchange—B's response (B4.1–B4.2) conceded the lottery was uncertain but never rebutted the core asymmetry A identified.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (PRO); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (CON)): Side A won the central comparison: caps remove the windfall driving speculative acquisition, while digital-ticket friction makes illicit side deals materially less scalable than official one-click resale. ### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): B built and sustained the decisive structural attack: the mandate's "cap or face-value channel" is either an optional add-on that nobody with a scarce ticket uses, or a binding cap that pushes the excess demand off-platform into bot-driven, unverified markets. A's cross-exam answer (A4.1) conceded the binding-cap branch, handing B the stronger horn of its own dilemma, and A's only reply—"near-total evasion" is needed to win—never explained why arbitrage incentives under a hard price ceiling wouldn't be severe, a point B kept pressing with the concrete "milliseconds to bot-snipe" mechanism. B also turned A's concession that dynamic primary pricing stays untouched into a clean "lose-lose" frame: fans still face scarcity rent upstream while losing verified secondary liquidity downstream. A's fairness/anti-extraction framing and consumer-protection points were reasonable but never neutralized this core shortage/evasion logic, and A's case suffered real execution costs, being clipped in the opening, first rebuttal, and closing, which blunted its ability to fully land rebuttals and crystallize the close. B's closing was tighter, unclipped, and reiterated the clearest, most memorable version of its winning mechanism.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: B's rebuttal 1 dilemma ("optional channel accomplishes nothing vs. mandatory cap causes black-market flight," B2.1-B2.2) combined with A's cross-exam concession in A4.1 that the cap does prohibit uncapped listings—effectively locking A into the more vulnerable horn of B's dilemma for the rest of the debate.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON)): Side B won a clear but competitive debate by exposing the proposition’s central fork: an optional face-value channel alongside uncapped resale has weak incremental effect, while an effective cap creates arbitrage incentives, rapid depletion, and off-platform migration. Side A persuasively argued that some honest sellers value secure cost recovery and that B overstated total evasion, but it never specified why its channel would materially outperform existing verified listings or prevent capped inventory from being captured for off-platform resale. B’s financing claims were more speculative, and its erroneous reference IDs warrant a deduction, but its core market-design objection remained live. Side A’s repeated clipping also imposed an execution cost.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side B’s response to Side A’s channel-versus-cap defense was decisive: coexistence leaves profitable resale untouched, whereas binding caps generate an arbitrage bounty and migration outside verified platforms. Side A answered that partial compliance still creates benefits, but did not fully resolve this design dilemma.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Gemini 4 Argon (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): CON won the decisive fork: if a face-value channel is optional beside uncapped resale, holders of $500-value tickets ignore it; if it is a hard cap, it creates a $400 arbitrage bounty that bots snipe and sellers evade off-platform. B's replies about secure transfers and sellers wanting only money back never solved supply flight or bot capture, and left the dynamic-primary-pricing contradiction intact.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: A's Rebuttal 1-2 fork that optional face-value listings do nothing while mandatory caps produce millisecond bot capture and off-platform migration via burner accounts/social media, directly undercutting B's safety claim in [B1.4].
