### Judge Notes

- **Claude Sonnet 5 (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Both sides fought hard over the same crux—whether the motion's "even if" clause can be satisfied without collapsing into either a toothless alternative (CON) or an unbounded freeze (PRO)—and both landed real hits. CON's Rebuttal 1 was the sharpest moment of the debate, correctly forcing PRO to admit its forest/peat-only framing was quietly rewriting the motion away from accepting real income/export losses. But PRO recovered well: it reversed the capacity argument (weak maps make continued licensing more dangerous, not less, which undercuts CON's "ring-fence without a map" solution) and, under direct pressure questioning, supplied a concrete mechanism—a secondary-forest concession pause—that squarely fits the motion's demand for added restraint beyond CON's narrow categories. CON's best counter (the pause is open-ended because it is tied to capacity that may never arrive) is a fair point, but PRO's closing partially answered it with proportionality/least-damaging-instrument language, while CON's own threshold ("don't tighten past the point lawful income/exports fall") effectively dissolves into opposing the motion's premise outright rather than defeating PRO's specific proposal. PRO's embrace of the tradeoff, plus the stronger handling of the capacity/uncertainty argument, gives it a narrow but real edge, despite execution costs from two clipped PRO speeches versus one clipped CON speech.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: CON's Rebuttal 1 ([B2.1]-[B2.3]), which caught PRO redefining "aggressive regulation" as only forest/peat/fire/traceability rules and forced PRO to either accept real income losses or abandon the motion as written, was the most decisive single exchange—though PRO's subsequent concrete secondary-forest-pause answer to the pressure questions partially neutralized it.
- **GPT-5.6 Sol (high)** (A = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO); B = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON)): Side A won a close, high-quality clash by defending the motion with a bounded proportionality standard: demonstrable harm, least-damaging instruments, mitigation for smallholders, and acceptance of some residual cost. Side B sharply exposed the risks of an open-ended secondary-forest pause, but its own threshold made virtually any decline in lawful planting or exports a stopping point. That left it without a persuasive answer when modest economic losses are necessary to prevent irreversible conversion, disputed concessions, or public environmental costs.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Side A’s threshold question forced Side B to state that regulation should stop once lawful planting or exports must fall. A then showed why this creates an implausible economic veto over additional safeguards, while offering proportionality and a temporary pause in unresolved concession areas as a concrete alternative. B’s reply that such a pause could become indefinite was strong, but did not fully overcome A’s option-value and irreversibility comparison.
- **Muse Spark 1.1 (high)** (A = Grok 4.7 (high) (CON); B = Kimi K3.1 Pro (high) (PRO)): PRO owned the motion's even-if clause instead of designing it away: accepted bounded losses to lawful income/exports, offered a proportionality test (demonstrable harm, least-damaging instrument), and named a concrete extra measure beyond CON's ring-fence – suspending new secondary-forest conversion until concession boundaries and community claims are reconciled. That answered A's Q2, turned weak maps/capacity into a reason for a precautionary pause preserving option value, and handled pressure honestly. CON's bright line – stop when any lawful planting must fall – protects households vividly but functions as an automatic veto on any additional safeguard, leaving irreversible clearing to be licensed while maps remain unreliable.
  Most decisive rebuttal noted: Exchange over B4.3 secondary-forest pause – B frames weak maps as raising option-value of a pause, A4.4-A4.6 frames same pause as open-ended tax on households who cannot wait, crystallizing the core irreversibility vs livelihood tradeoff.
