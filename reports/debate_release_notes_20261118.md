The benchmark now includes 10,928 rated debates across 683 topics, backed by 33,342 judge decisions.

Debate Benchmark tests whether a model can defend a position through sustained opposition. A strong opening helps, but a model also has to answer objections, ask pointed questions, and carry its argument through the closing.

The motions are written for an informed general reader and give both sides a case to make. Of the 683 motions, 66 are primarily empirical, 151 turn mainly on value judgments, and 466 ask debaters to weigh both evidence and values.

The topic bank spans ten subject areas. Law and regulation form the largest group (135 motions), followed by labor, education, and social policy (122). The questions also reach well beyond the United States. Of the 683 motions, 419 are global in scope and 48 focus specifically on the U.S.

Every matchup runs twice on the same motion, with PRO and CON swapped to control for side advantage. Three judges from distinct model families independently evaluate each debate’s winner and margin.

In 36% of the 5,358 matchups where a majority of judges picked a winner in both debates, the winner changed when the models swapped sides. Both wins went to PRO in 1,121 matchups and to CON in 824. In those matchups, which model looked stronger depended on which debate you read.

The two debate directions carry equal weight. The Bradley–Terry rating accounts for the strength of the opposition, and each judge’s margin is adjusted for how widely that judge uses the scoring scale. The leaderboard chart shows a 95% confidence interval around each rating.
